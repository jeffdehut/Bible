# Synchronological Chart

An open reconstruction of Sebastian C. Adams' *Synchronological Chart, or Map of History* (1871), which drew Bible history and world history as parallel streams along one time axis using Archbishop Ussher's chronology.

- **Upper streams:** Bible history after Ussher's chronology, as Adams used it. Lifetimes, reigns and events come from Theographic, which follows Floyd Nolen Jones's refinement of Ussher.
- **Synchronisms:** each event the Bible narrates that outside evidence also dates, drawn at its Ussher date (upper dot) and its modern date (lower dot). Red lines mark differences of more than five years. See [[connections/timeline/Synchronisms|Synchronisms]].
- **Lower streams:** the wider world at modern dates: Egypt, Sumer and Akkad, Babylon, Assyria, the Hittites and Lydia, Aram, Tyre, Persia, Greece, the Seleucids, Rome, Carthage, the Herods and Roman officials, India and China; from about AD 30 also the Byzantine Empire, Europe, Russia, the Islamic world, the Mongols, Japan, Africa, the Americas, Jewish history, the Church and the transmission of the Bible's text. Each stream shows eras (palest), then dynasties, then rulers. A lane of thinkers and writers and a lane of world events come last. Each item has a note in `connections/world/` (or its Bible person's note).
- **Two chronologies side by side:** the upper streams follow Ussher, as Adams did, and the lower ones follow modern dating. Before about 1000 BC the two diverge widely. For example, on modern dates Egypt's Old Kingdom and Sumer's city-states run straight through Ussher's date for the Flood (2348 BC). The chart shows both as they are dated and doesn't try to reconcile them.
- **Axis:** black lines mark centuries and pink lines decades, as on Adams' chart; years of the world (AM) run along the bottom.

Each era chart is wider and easier to read than the whole. Open an SVG in a web browser to zoom and to see details when you hover over a bar.
