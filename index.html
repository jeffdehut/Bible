                              About this project

                              Plain English Bible brings together a new, openly licensed translation of the whole Bible from the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek, with the original words beneath it, maps, a genealogy, a timeline of world history, and the web of connections that runs through Scripture: made to be read on a phone, free to everyone, and open for anyone to reuse.

                              Designed and orchestrated by Jeff Dehut. The translation and the tools were drafted with the help of AI (Claude) and are being reviewed; every source and license is credited where it is used.

                              About this translation

                              Every chapter is a first draft. The Open Plain English Translation was drafted with the help of AI (Claude), from the Hebrew and Aramaic of the Leningrad Codex and the Greek of the SBL Greek New Testament. No chapter has been reviewed yet. Until a chapter is reviewed, it carries a Draft label.

                              How a chapter is reviewed

                              • Draft: translated verse by verse from the Hebrew or Greek, with notes, and checked by computer against its source, so that no verse or note is missing.
                              • Checked: compared with the source and a lexicon for anything left out or added. Notes that cite ancient versions are verified against a critical edition.
                              • Reviewed: at least one reviewer who reads the original language has confirmed every verse, and is named.
                              • Approved: accepted for the published translation.

                              The aim

                              Say what the original says, in natural modern English that a reader around 12 to 14 years old can follow. Where the original can be read more than one way, or the manuscripts differ, a note says so. God's personal name, YHWH, is written "the LORD" in small capitals, as in most English Bibles.

                              Under each verse

                              Tap a verse to see it in Hebrew, Aramaic or Greek, word by word. The words, glosses and grammar come from STEPBible's tagged texts (TAHOT and TAGNT), and the definitions from its brief lexicons (TBESH and TBESG). STEPBible's glosses are its own, not this translation's.

                              People a verse names are listed under People: tap one to see their family in the genealogy. The names come from STEPBible's TIPNR, which marks each name in the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek.

                              Under Who is who, a verse shows who each “he”, “she”, “they” or unnamed subject refers to, followed back to the name or noun it stands for. This comes from MACULA Hebrew and MACULA Greek (Clear Bible, Biblica, CC BY 4.0). Under Word by word, a gold dot marks a word where the Greek editions or the Hebrew manuscripts differ; this comes from STEPBible's TAGNT and TAHOT (CC BY 4.0).

                              A word’s Full entry opens Brown, Driver and Briggs’s Hebrew lexicon (1906, public domain; Open Scriptures’ outline of it, CC BY 4.0) or Liddell, Scott and Jones’s Greek lexicon (1940, public domain; formatted by STEPBible, CC BY 4.0).

                              Under Related verses, a verse lists its cross-references from OpenBible.info (CC BY), those that at least three readers voted for, the most-voted first; most come from the public-domain Treasury of Scripture Knowledge. Tap one to read it in place.

                              Under Topics, a verse lists the topics of Nave’s Topical Bible (1896, public domain) that cite it, and under In the Bible dictionary, the entries of Easton’s Bible Dictionary (1897, public domain; linked edition from Theographic, CC BY-SA 4.0) that do. Both are also in the menu, under Topics and dictionary. Their verse numbers have been moved to this translation’s.

                              Under Parallel passages, a verse in the Gospels shows the section of A. T. Robertson’s Harmony of the Gospels (1922, public domain) it belongs to, with every Gospel that tells it, side by side; a verse in Samuel or Kings, the passages that tell it again in Chronicles or Isaiah, after Appendix 56 of the Companion Bible (public domain). Reading plans divide the chapters into days; the chronological plan follows Ussher’s dates as the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge gives them (via Theographic), with the chapters of each book kept in order. Your place in a plan is kept on this device, and in your account if you sign in.

                              Places a verse names are listed under On the maps: tap one to see it on the Bible map for that part of the story. Which verses name which places comes from OpenBible.info's Bible Geocoding Data (CC BY 4.0), kept only where this translation uses the name.
