About this book

Authors and dates as introductions to the Bible commonly give them; many are debated, and the ranges say so.

At a glance

    The words it uses most

    How often each word comes, for every 1,000 words of the letter in Greek, beside the Gospel of John and the rest of the New Testament. Tap a word to follow it through the letter below.

    Verse by verse

    Each column is a verse, each row a word; a dot is the word in that verse, larger where it comes more than once. Tap a column to read the verse.

    Side by side

    Words the letter sets against each other, verse by verse. A ring marks a verse where both stand together.

    The shape of its sentences

    Ways of speaking the letter comes back to, found by their words in the Greek. Tap one to read them together.

      Who it speaks of, and to

      The person of every verb and pronoun, verse by verse: “I”, “we”, “you”, and “he”, “she”, “they”. Above, the names the writer calls his readers.

      The people in it

      Everyone the letter names or speaks of, and the verses where they are.

      Beside the other letters of John

      Verses that share at least two words of substance with a verse of the other letters (the small words, such as “and” and “the”, aside). Tap a line to see the words and both verses.

      Counted from the SBL Greek New Testament with the Strong's numbers of STEPBible's tagged text (CC BY 4.0); the verses are the Open Plain English Translation, a draft. The words chosen for the charts are lists of Greek words, not interpretations: “love” counts agapē and agapaō, “know” ginōskō and oida, “truth” alētheia and its adjectives.
