    About this map

    A style study for the project's Bible maps, redrawing Eilers Sunday School Map No. 7, The Kingdoms Judah Israel (A. H. Eilers & Co., St. Louis), from open data. It keeps the Eilers look and aims to read more easily.

    What we kept from Eilers

    • Flat tints for each kingdom and people, with the seas left as paper
    • Water lines along the coasts, and hachured relief
    • Widely spaced capitals for the tribes, and red capitals for the kingdoms
    • A "?" after places whose location is uncertain
    • A grid with an index of places keyed to it

    What we changed

    • Labels never overlap. More appear as you zoom in.
    • Type is never smaller than 11 pixels on screen
    • Borders are drawn as lines as well as tints, so they don't depend on color

    How it was made

    • Land, water and relief come from elevation data. Lake Huleh and the Dead Sea are drawn at their old shorelines.
    • Places are the towns named in the Old Testament that OpenBible.info can locate. Places scored below 500 out of 1,000 are marked "?".
    • Tribal territories are approximate. Each piece of land is given to the tribe whose towns are nearest (the town lists of Joshua 13–19 and 21). Lakes cannot be crossed, and rivers are costly to cross, so borders tend to follow the rivers.
    • Names are spelled as in this project's Open Plain English Translation.

    Sources

    • Places: OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data, CC BY 4.0
    • Elevation: AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1)
    • Style: the Eilers Sunday School wall maps, published before 1931
