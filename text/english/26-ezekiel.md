## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the thirtieth year,[^1-1] in the fourth month, on the fifth day of the month, while I was among the exiles by the river Chebar,[^1-2] the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God.

**2** On the fifth day of the month, in the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin,[^1-3]

**3** the word of Yahweh[^1-4] came to Ezekiel son of Buzi, the priest,[^1-5] in the land of the Chaldeans,[^1-6] by the river Chebar, and the hand of Yahweh came on him there.[^1-7]

**4** I looked, and I saw a storm wind coming from the north: a great cloud, with fire flashing,[^1-8] and brightness all around it. In the middle of it, in the middle of the fire, was something like the gleam of amber.[^1-9]

**5** In the middle of it was the likeness of four living creatures.[^1-10] This was their appearance: they had a human form.

**6** Each had four faces, and each of them had four wings.

**7** Their legs were straight,[^1-11] and the soles of their feet were like the sole of a calf's foot. They gleamed like the gleam of polished bronze.[^1-12]

**8** Under their wings on their four sides they had human hands.[^1-13] As for the faces and the wings of the four of them,

**9** their wings touched one another. They did not turn as they went; each of them went straight ahead.

**10** The likeness of their faces was this: each had the face of a human being; the four of them had the face of a lion on the right side; the four of them had the face of an ox on the left side; and the four of them had the face of an eagle.[^1-14]

**11** Such were their faces. Their wings were spread out above. Each had two wings touching those of another, and two covering their bodies.

**12** Each of them went straight ahead. Wherever the spirit[^1-15] would go, they went. They did not turn as they went.

**13** As for the likeness of the living creatures, their appearance was like burning coals of fire, like the appearance of torches. Fire[^1-16] moved back and forth among the living creatures. The fire was bright, and lightning came out of the fire.

**14** The living creatures darted back and forth like the appearance of a flash of lightning.[^1-17]

**15** As I looked at the living creatures, I saw a wheel[^1-18] on the ground beside the living creatures, one for each of their four faces.[^1-19]

**16** The appearance of the wheels and the way they were made was like the gleam of beryl,[^1-20] and the four of them had the same likeness. Their appearance and the way they were made were like a wheel within a wheel.[^1-21]

**17** When they went, they went in any of their four directions;[^1-22] they did not turn as they went.

**18** As for their rims, they were high and awesome, and the rims of all four were full of eyes all around.[^1-23]

**19** When the living creatures went, the wheels went beside them, and when the living creatures rose from the ground, the wheels rose.

**20** Wherever the spirit would go, they went, wherever the spirit would go, and the wheels rose along with them, for the spirit of the living creatures[^1-24] was in the wheels.

**21** When they went, the wheels went; when they stood still, the wheels stood still; and when they rose from the ground, the wheels rose along with them, for the spirit of the living creatures was in the wheels.

**22** Over the heads of the living creatures there was the likeness of a dome,[^1-25] gleaming like awesome crystal,[^1-26] spread out over their heads above.

**23** Under the dome their wings were stretched out straight, one toward another. Each of them had two wings that covered it; each of them had two that covered their bodies.[^1-27]

**24** When they went, I heard the sound of their wings, like the sound of many waters, like the voice of the Almighty,[^1-28] a sound of tumult like the noise of an army camp. When they stood still, they let their wings drop.

**25** And there came a voice from above the dome that was over their heads. When they stood still, they let their wings drop.

**26** Above the dome that was over their heads was something that looked like lapis lazuli,[^1-29] in the likeness of a throne, and on the likeness of the throne, high above it, was a likeness with the appearance of a human being.

**27** I saw that from what looked like his waist upward he was like the gleam of amber, like the appearance of fire enclosed all around;[^1-30] and from what looked like his waist downward I saw something like the appearance of fire, and there was brightness all around him.

**28** Like the appearance of the rainbow[^1-31] in the clouds on a day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness all around. This was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of Yahweh.[^1-32] When I saw it, I fell on my face, and I heard the voice of someone speaking.

[^1-1]: The meaning of "the thirtieth year" is uncertain. It may be Ezekiel's age; priests began their service at thirty (Numbers 4:3).
[^1-2]: Probably a large canal near the city of Nippur in Babylonia, southeast of Babylon.
[^1-3]: In the summer of 593 BC (the fourth month, verse 1). King Jehoiachin of Judah had been taken to Babylon in 597 BC (2 Kings 24:8–15).
[^1-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-5]: The Hebrew could also be read as calling Buzi the priest. Verses 2–3 speak of Ezekiel in the third person; the rest of the book is told in the first person.
[^1-6]: That is, Babylonia. The Chaldeans were the people of southern Mesopotamia who ruled the Babylonian empire.
[^1-7]: A phrase Ezekiel uses for Yahweh's overpowering grip on him in his visions (3:14, 22; 8:1; 37:1; 40:1).
[^1-8]: Literally "fire taking hold of itself", perhaps fire flashing back and forth (the same phrase is used in Exodus 9:24).
[^1-9]: Hebrew *chashmal*, a word found only in Ezekiel (here, verse 27 and 8:2), of uncertain meaning. The Septuagint translates it *elektron*, which can mean amber or electrum, a shining alloy of gold and silver. Many translations have "gleaming metal". In modern Hebrew the word means "electricity".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *chayyot*, "living beings". In chapter 10 Ezekiel sees them again and recognizes them as cherubim (10:15, 20).
[^1-11]: Hebrew *regel* means both "foot" and "leg". Literally "their legs were a straight leg".
[^1-12]: Or "burnished bronze". The word *qalal* is found only here and in Daniel 10:6.
[^1-13]: The Hebrew text is written "and his hands were human" but read "and human hands".
[^1-14]: Or "vulture". Hebrew *nesher* can mean either.
[^1-15]: Or "the wind". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath". Also in verses 20–21.
[^1-16]: Literally "It", probably the fire.
[^1-17]: Hebrew *bazaq*, a word found only here, probably "flash of lightning". The Septuagint does not have this verse.
[^1-18]: Hebrew *ofan*, the ordinary word for a wheel (1 Kings 7:30–33). The wheels are the *ofannim*.
[^1-19]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "one wheel on the ground beside the living creatures, to his four faces".
[^1-20]: Hebrew *tarshish*, a precious stone whose identity is uncertain (Exodus 28:20).
[^1-21]: Perhaps two wheels set crosswise, so that they could go in any of four directions without turning (verse 17).
[^1-22]: Literally "on their four sides".
[^1-23]: Or "full of gleaming points". The Hebrew word for "eye" also means "gleam", as in "like the gleam of" in verses 4, 7, 16 and 22. The meaning of the first part of the verse is uncertain; literally "And their rims, and height was theirs, and fear was theirs, and their rims were full of eyes all around, the four of them."
[^1-24]: Or "the spirit of life". Hebrew *ruach ha-chayyah*, with "living creature" in the singular, used of the whole group; also in verses 21–22.
[^1-25]: Hebrew *raqia*, the word used for the dome of the sky in Genesis 1:6–8: something hammered or spread out. Traditionally "firmament".
[^1-26]: Or "ice". Hebrew *qerach* means both.
[^1-27]: The Hebrew repeats the clause: literally "to each, two covering them, and to each, two covering them, their bodies". Probably each creature covered its body with two wings, as in verse 11.
[^1-28]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^1-29]: Hebrew *sappir*, traditionally "sapphire". It was probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone (Exodus 24:10).
[^1-30]: Literally "like the appearance of fire, a house for it all around". The meaning is uncertain.
[^1-31]: Literally "the bow" (Genesis 9:13–16).
[^1-32]: Hebrew *kavod*, "weight, honor, glory": here the visible splendor of God's presence (Exodus 24:16–17; 40:34–35).

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** He said to me, "Son of man,[^2-1] stand on your feet, and I will speak with you."

**2** As he spoke to me, the Spirit[^2-2] came into me and set me on my feet, and I heard him speaking to me.

**3** He said to me, "Son of man, I am sending you to the Israelites, to rebellious nations[^2-3] that have rebelled against me. They and their ancestors have revolted against me to this very day.

**4** The children are hard-faced and stubborn-hearted.[^2-4] I am sending you to them, and you are to say to them, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh[^2-5] says.'

**5** And whether they listen or refuse to listen,[^2-6] for they are a rebellious house, they will know that a prophet has been among them.

**6** And you, son of man, do not be afraid of them or of their words, though briers and thorns[^2-7] are all around you and you live among scorpions. Do not be afraid of their words, and do not be terrified by their looks,[^2-8] for they are a rebellious house.

**7** You are to speak my words to them, whether they listen or refuse to listen, for they are rebellious.

**8** But you, son of man, listen to what I am saying to you. Do not be rebellious like that rebellious house. Open your mouth and eat what I am giving you."

**9** Then I looked, and I saw a hand stretched out to me, and in it was a scroll.[^2-9]

**10** He spread it out in front of me. It had writing on the front and on the back, and written on it were laments, mourning and woe.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal. Yahweh addresses Ezekiel this way more than ninety times in the book.
[^2-2]: Or "a spirit". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath", here without the article.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *goyim*, the word usually used for the nations other than Israel.
[^2-4]: Literally "hard of face and strong of heart".
[^2-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The Lord Yahweh* is Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD". Ezekiel names God this way more than two hundred times.
[^2-6]: Literally "or hold back". Also in verse 7.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *sarabim* and *sallonim*, rare words, probably "briers and thorns". Literally "are with you".
[^2-8]: Literally "by their faces".
[^2-9]: Literally "a scroll of a book".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** He said to me, "Son of man,[^3-1] eat what you find here. Eat this scroll, and go, speak to the house of Israel."

**2** So I opened my mouth, and he fed me the scroll.

**3** He said to me, "Son of man, feed your stomach and fill your belly with this scroll that I am giving you." So I ate it, and in my mouth it was as sweet as honey.

**4** He said to me, "Son of man, go to the house of Israel and speak my words to them.

**5** For you are not being sent to a people of strange speech and difficult language,[^3-2] but to the house of Israel,

**6** not to many peoples of strange speech and difficult language, whose words you cannot understand. Surely, if I sent you to them, they would listen to you.

**7** But the house of Israel will not be willing to listen to you, for they are not willing to listen to me. For the whole house of Israel is hard-headed and stubborn-hearted.[^3-3]

**8** Look, I have made your face as hard as their faces, and your forehead as hard as their foreheads.[^3-4]

**9** I have made your forehead like diamond,[^3-5] harder than flint. Do not be afraid of them, and do not be terrified by their looks,[^3-6] for they are a rebellious house."

**10** He said to me, "Son of man, take to heart all my words that I speak to you, and listen with your ears.

**11** Go to the exiles, to your own people, and speak to them. Say to them, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh[^3-7] says,' whether they listen or refuse to listen."[^3-8]

**12** Then the Spirit[^3-9] lifted me up, and I heard behind me the sound of a great rumbling:[^3-10] "Blessed be the glory of Yahweh from his place!"[^3-11]

**13** It was the sound of the wings of the living creatures brushing against one another, and the sound of the wheels beside them, the sound of a great rumbling.

**14** The Spirit lifted me up and took me away, and I went in bitterness, my spirit burning,[^3-12] and the hand of Yahweh was strong on me.

**15** I came to the exiles at Tel-abib,[^3-13] who lived by the river Chebar, and I sat where they were living.[^3-14] I sat there among them for seven days, overwhelmed.[^3-15]

**16** At the end of seven days the word of Yahweh came to me:

**17** "Son of man, I have made you a watchman[^3-16] for the house of Israel. Whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you are to warn them from me.

**18** When I say to the wicked, 'You will surely die,' and you do not warn him, and you do not speak to warn the wicked from his wicked way so as to save his life, that wicked person will die for his guilt, but I will hold you responsible for his blood.[^3-17]

**19** But if you warn the wicked, and he does not turn from his wickedness or from his wicked way, he will die for his guilt, but you will have saved your life.[^3-18]

**20** And when a righteous person turns from his righteousness and does wrong, and I put a stumbling block[^3-19] in front of him, he will die. Because you did not warn him, he will die for his sin, and the righteous deeds he has done will not be remembered, but I will hold you responsible for his blood.

**21** But if you warn the righteous person not to sin, and he does not sin, he will surely live, because he took the warning, and you will have saved your life."

**22** The hand of Yahweh was on me there, and he said to me, "Get up, go out to the plain,[^3-20] and there I will speak with you."

**23** So I got up and went out to the plain, and there was the glory of Yahweh standing there, like the glory I had seen by the river Chebar, and I fell on my face.

**24** Then the Spirit came into me and set me on my feet. He spoke with me and said to me, "Go, shut yourself inside your house.

**25** And you, son of man, look, they will put ropes on you and tie you up with them, so that you cannot go out among them.

**26** I will make your tongue stick to the roof of your mouth, so that you will be unable to speak and unable to rebuke them,[^3-21] for they are a rebellious house.

**27** But when I speak with you, I will open your mouth, and you are to say to them, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh says.' Whoever will listen, let him listen, and whoever refuses, let him refuse, for they are a rebellious house.

[^3-1]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^3-2]: Literally "deep of lip and heavy of tongue". Also in verse 6.
[^3-3]: Literally "strong of forehead and hard of heart".
[^3-4]: The name Ezekiel, Hebrew *Yechezqel*, means "God makes strong" or "God makes hard". *Hard* in verses 7–9 (*chazaq*) comes from the same root.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *shamir*, a very hard stone, perhaps emery or diamond (Jeremiah 17:1; Zechariah 7:12).
[^3-6]: Literally "by their faces".
[^3-7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The Lord Yahweh* is Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^3-8]: Literally "or hold back". Also in verse 27.
[^3-9]: Or "a spirit" or "a wind". Hebrew *ruach*. Also in verses 14 and 24.
[^3-10]: Or "a great earthquake". Also in verse 13.
[^3-11]: Some scholars read *berum*, "as the glory of Yahweh rose from its place", instead of *barukh*, "blessed", with a small change to the Hebrew (compare 10:4, 18–19).
[^3-12]: Literally "bitter, in the heat of my spirit".
[^3-13]: A settlement of the exiles in Babylonia. The name probably comes from Akkadian *til abubi*, "mound of the flood", that is, an ancient ruin mound. The modern city of Tel Aviv took its name from this verse.
[^3-14]: The Hebrew text is written "and where they were living" but read "and I sat where they were living".
[^3-15]: Or "appalled". Hebrew *mashmim*, "stunned, desolate".
[^3-16]: Hebrew *tsofeh*, a lookout posted on a city wall to warn of danger (2 Samuel 18:24–27). Verses 17–19 are repeated, with changes, in 33:7–9.
[^3-17]: Literally "his blood I will seek from your hand". Also in verse 20.
[^3-18]: Hebrew *nafshekha*, "your life, yourself". Also in verse 21.
[^3-19]: Hebrew *mikhshol*, "something to trip over", Ezekiel's word for what brings a person down into sin or into ruin (7:19; 14:3–4; 18:30; 44:12).
[^3-20]: Or "the valley". Hebrew *biq'ah*, a broad valley.
[^3-21]: Literally "you will not be a man who reproves them".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** "And you, son of man,[^4-1] take a brick[^4-2] and put it in front of you, and draw a city on it, Jerusalem.

**2** Lay siege to it: build a siege wall against it, pile up a ramp against it, set up camps against it, and place battering rams against it all around.

**3** Then take an iron griddle[^4-3] and set it up as an iron wall between you and the city. Set your face toward it, so that it is under siege, and besiege it. This is a sign for the house of Israel.

**4** Then lie on your left side, and lay the guilt[^4-4] of the house of Israel on it. For the number of days that you lie on it, you will bear their guilt.

**5** I have assigned you the years of their guilt as a number of days, 390 days.[^4-5] So you will bear the guilt of the house of Israel.

**6** When you have completed these, lie down a second time, on your right side, and bear the guilt of the house of Judah for 40 days. I have assigned you a day for each year, a day for each year.

**7** Set your face toward the siege of Jerusalem, with your arm bared, and prophesy against it.

**8** Look, I will put ropes on you, so that you cannot turn from one side to the other until you have completed the days of your siege.

**9** Take wheat and barley, beans and lentils, millet and spelt.[^4-6] Put them in one container and make bread for yourself from them. You are to eat it for the number of days that you lie on your side, 390 days.

**10** The food you eat is to be weighed out, twenty shekels[^4-7] a day; you are to eat it at set times.[^4-8]

**11** You are to drink water by measure, a sixth of a hin;[^4-9] you are to drink it at set times.

**12** You are to eat it as a barley cake, and bake it in their sight over human dung."[^4-10]

**13** Yahweh[^4-11] said, "This is how the Israelites will eat their bread, unclean, among the nations where I will drive them."

**14** Then I said, "Ah, Lord Yahweh![^4-12] I have never made myself unclean.[^4-13] From my youth until now I have never eaten anything that died by itself or was torn by wild animals, and no foul meat[^4-14] has ever come into my mouth."

**15** He said to me, "See, I will let you have cow dung instead of human dung. You may make your bread over it."

**16** He said to me, "Son of man, I am going to cut off the supply of bread[^4-15] in Jerusalem. They will eat bread by weight and with anxiety, and drink water by measure and in dismay,

**17** so that they will lack bread and water, and they will look at one another in dismay,[^4-16] and waste away in their guilt.

[^4-1]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal. Yahweh's speech, which began in 3:24, continues to the end of chapter 5.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *levenah*, a brick of sun-dried clay. In Babylonia clay was also used as a surface for writing and drawing.
[^4-3]: Hebrew *machavat*, a flat plate for baking (Leviticus 2:5).
[^4-4]: Hebrew *avon*, which means both wrongdoing and the punishment it brings. Also in verses 5–6 and 17.
[^4-5]: The Septuagint reads "190 days", here and in verse 9.
[^4-6]: Hebrew *dochan*, probably millet, and *kussemim*, a kind of wheat, perhaps spelt or emmer.
[^4-7]: Twenty shekels was about 8 ounces (230 grams).
[^4-8]: Literally "from time to time". Also in verse 11.
[^4-9]: A hin was about a gallon (4 liters), so a sixth of a hin was about 1⅓ pints (0.6 liter).
[^4-10]: Literally "with dung of human excrement". Dried dung was commonly burned as fuel (verse 15).
[^4-11]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-12]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^4-13]: Literally "Look, my *nephesh* has not been made unclean".
[^4-14]: Hebrew *piggul*, "foul, rotten": meat that has become unfit to eat (Leviticus 7:18). The law forbade priests to eat an animal that had died by itself or been torn by wild animals (Leviticus 22:8).
[^4-15]: Literally "break the staff of bread", as in Leviticus 26:26.
[^4-16]: Literally "they will be appalled, each man and his brother".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** "And you, son of man,[^5-1] take a sharp sword and use it as a barber's razor: pass it over your head and over your beard. Then take scales for weighing, and divide the hair.

**2** A third of it you are to burn in the fire in the middle of the city, when the days of the siege are completed. A third you are to take and strike with the sword all around the city. And a third you are to scatter to the wind, and I will draw a sword after them.

**3** Take a few hairs from there and tie them up in the hem of your robe.[^5-2]

**4** Take some of these again, throw them into the fire, and burn them in the fire. From there a fire will spread to the whole house of Israel.

**5** This is what the Lord Yahweh[^5-3] says: This is Jerusalem. I set her in the middle of the nations, with countries all around her.

**6** But she has rebelled against my rulings more wickedly than the nations, and against my decrees more than the countries around her. For they have rejected my rulings and have not walked in my decrees.

**7** Therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh says: Because you have been more unruly[^5-4] than the nations around you, and have not walked in my decrees or kept my rulings, and have not even acted according to the rulings of the nations around you,[^5-5]

**8** therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh says: Look, I, even I, am against you.[^5-6] I will carry out judgments in your midst in the sight of the nations.

**9** Because of all your detestable practices,[^5-7] I will do to you what I have never done before, and the like of which I will never do again.

**10** Therefore fathers will eat their children in your midst, and children will eat their fathers. I will carry out judgments on you, and I will scatter all who are left of you to every wind.

**11** Therefore, as I live, declares the Lord Yahweh,[^5-8] because you have defiled my sanctuary with all your vile idols[^5-9] and with all your detestable practices, I too will cut you down.[^5-10] My eye will not pity, and I will not spare.

**12** A third of you will die of plague or perish by famine in your midst; a third will fall by the sword all around you; and a third I will scatter to every wind, and I will draw a sword after them.

**13** Then my anger will be spent, and I will satisfy my fury on them,[^5-11] and I will be appeased.[^5-12] Then they will know that I, Yahweh, have spoken in my jealousy,[^5-13] when I have spent my fury on them.

**14** I will make you a ruin and a disgrace among the nations around you, in the sight of everyone who passes by.

**15** You will be[^5-14] a disgrace and a taunt, a warning and a horror to the nations around you, when I carry out judgments on you in anger and in fury and with furious punishments.[^5-15] I, Yahweh, have spoken.

**16** When I send against them the deadly arrows of famine, arrows for destruction, which I will send to destroy you, I will bring more and more famine on you, and I will cut off your supply of bread.[^5-16]

**17** I will send famine and wild animals against you, and they will leave you childless. Plague and bloodshed will sweep through you, and I will bring the sword against you. I, Yahweh, have spoken."

[^5-1]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^5-2]: Literally "in your wings", the corners or edges of a garment.
[^5-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The Lord Yahweh* is Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^5-4]: The meaning of the Hebrew word, *hamonkhem*, is uncertain. It may come from *hamah*, "be in turmoil".
[^5-5]: Compare 11:12, where they are said to have acted according to the rulings of the nations around them.
[^5-6]: *You* is feminine singular: Jerusalem is addressed. Also in verses 9–17.
[^5-7]: Hebrew *to'evot*, things that are abhorrent, especially in worship. Ezekiel uses the word more than forty times.
[^5-8]: Hebrew *ne'um Adonai YHWH*, "the declaration of the Lord Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement.
[^5-9]: Hebrew *shiqqutsim*, "detestable things", a contemptuous word for idols.
[^5-10]: Literally "I will diminish" or "I will clip"; the object is not stated, and the meaning is uncertain. The verb is used for a shaved beard in Jeremiah 48:37, which fits the sign of verses 1–4. Others translate "I will withdraw".
[^5-11]: Literally "make my fury rest on them". Also in 16:42; 21:22 (21:17 in most English Bibles); 24:13.
[^5-12]: Or "I will take comfort".
[^5-13]: Or "zeal". Hebrew *qin'ah*.
[^5-14]: Literally "She will be", speaking of Jerusalem in the third person.
[^5-15]: Literally "rebukes of fury".
[^5-16]: Literally "break the staff of bread for you" (4:16; Leviticus 26:26).

## Chapter 27

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of Yahweh[^27-1] came to me:

**2** "You, son of man,[^27-2] raise a lament[^27-3] over Tyre.

**3** Say to Tyre, who lives at the gateway to the sea,[^27-4] trader to the peoples of many coastlands, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh[^27-5] says: Tyre, you have said, "I am perfect in beauty."

**4** Your borders are in the heart of the seas; your builders made your beauty perfect.

**5** They built all your planks[^27-6] of juniper[^27-7] from Senir;[^27-8] they took a cedar from Lebanon to make a mast for you.

**6** They made your oars of oaks from Bashan; they made your deck[^27-9] of ivory set in cypress[^27-10] from the coastlands of Kittim.[^27-11]

**7** Your sail was fine linen with embroidery from Egypt, to serve as your banner. Your awning was blue and purple cloth[^27-12] from the coastlands of Elishah.[^27-13]

**8** The people of Sidon and Arvad[^27-14] were your rowers. Your own skilled men, Tyre, were in you; they were your helmsmen.[^27-15]

**9** The elders of Gebal[^27-16] and its skilled men were in you, repairing your seams.[^27-17] All the ships of the sea and their sailors were in you, to trade for your merchandise.

**10** Persia, Lud and Put[^27-18] were in your army, your men of war. They hung shield and helmet in you; they gave you your splendor.

**11** The men of Arvad and your army[^27-19] were on your walls all around, and the Gammadim[^27-20] were in your towers. They hung their shields[^27-21] on your walls all around; they made your beauty perfect.

**12** Tarshish[^27-22] traded with you because of your great wealth of every kind.[^27-23] They gave silver, iron, tin and lead for your wares.[^27-24]

**13** Javan, Tubal and Meshech[^27-25] were your merchants. They gave human beings[^27-26] and articles of bronze for your merchandise.

**14** From Beth-togarmah[^27-27] they gave horses, war horses[^27-28] and mules for your wares.

**15** The men of Dedan[^27-29] were your merchants. Many coastlands were your markets;[^27-30] they paid you with ivory tusks[^27-31] and ebony.

**16** Aram[^27-32] traded with you because of your many products. They gave turquoise,[^27-33] purple cloth, embroidered work, fine linen,[^27-34] coral and rubies[^27-35] for your wares.

**17** Judah and the land of Israel were your merchants. They gave wheat from Minnith,[^27-36] pannag,[^27-37] honey, oil and balm for your merchandise.

**18** Damascus traded with you because of your many products and your great wealth of every kind, in wine from Helbon[^27-38] and wool from Zahar.[^27-39]

**19** Vedan and Javan from Uzal[^27-40] paid for your wares: wrought iron, cassia and cane[^27-41] were among your merchandise.

**20** Dedan was your merchant in saddlecloths[^27-42] for riding.

**21** Arabia and all the princes of Kedar[^27-43] were your dealers.[^27-44] In lambs, rams and goats, in these they traded with you.

**22** The merchants of Sheba and Raamah[^27-45] were your merchants. They gave the finest of all spices, every kind of precious stone, and gold for your wares.

**23** Haran, Canneh and Eden,[^27-46] the merchants of Sheba, Asshur and Kilmad were your merchants.

**24** They were your merchants in fine garments,[^27-47] in cloaks of blue cloth and embroidered work, and in carpets of many colors, bound with cords and made secure, in your marketplace.[^27-48]

**25** The ships of Tarshish carried[^27-49] your merchandise. You were filled and heavily laden[^27-50] in the heart of the seas.

**26** Your rowers have brought you out into the mighty waters. The east wind has wrecked you in the heart of the seas.

**27** Your wealth, your wares and your merchandise, your sailors and your helmsmen, those who repair your seams and those who trade your merchandise, and all your men of war who are in you, with all the company that is within you, will fall into the heart of the seas on the day of your downfall.

**28** At the sound of the cry of your helmsmen the shorelands[^27-51] will shake.

**29** All who handle the oar will come down from their ships. The sailors and all the helmsmen of the sea will stand on the shore.

**30** They will raise their voices over you and cry out bitterly. They will throw dust on their heads and roll in the ashes.

**31** They will shave their heads bald for you and put on sackcloth.[^27-52] They will weep over you in bitterness of soul, with bitter mourning.

**32** In their wailing they will raise a lament for you and lament over you: "Who was like Tyre, like one silenced[^27-53] in the middle of the sea?

**33** When your wares went out from the seas, you satisfied many peoples. With your great wealth and your merchandise you made the kings of the earth rich.

**34** Now that you are wrecked by the seas, in the depths of the waters,[^27-54] your merchandise and all the company within you have gone down.

**35** All the inhabitants of the coastlands are appalled at you. Their kings shudder with horror; their faces are contorted.

**36** The traders among the peoples hiss at you.[^27-55] You have come to a horrible end,[^27-56] and you will be no more, forever."'"

[^27-4]: Literally "at the entrances of the sea": Tyre was an island city with harbors on the coast of Phoenicia.
[^27-5]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD".
[^27-6]: Hebrew *luchotayim*, a dual form: perhaps the planks of the ship's two sides.
[^27-7]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the juniper or the cypress.
[^27-8]: The Amorite name of Mount Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9).
[^27-9]: Literally "your boards", the word used for the frames of the tabernacle (Exodus 26:15).
[^27-10]: Literally "ivory, daughter of Ashurim". The same consonants can be read *bit'ashurim*, "in cypress" or "in boxwood" (Hebrew *te'ashur*, Isaiah 41:19).
[^27-11]: Cyprus, named after its city Kition. Later the name was used more widely for the lands and peoples of the west.
[^27-12]: Hebrew *tekhelet* and *argaman*: wool dyed violet-blue and red-purple (Exodus 25:4).
[^27-13]: Probably Cyprus, or part of it (Genesis 10:4).
[^27-14]: Sidon was a port city north of Tyre; Arvad was an island city farther north, off the coast of Syria.
[^27-15]: Hebrew *chovelim*, from *chevel*, "rope": the sailors who handled the rigging and steered the ship. Traditionally "pilots". Also in verses 27, 28 and 29.
[^27-16]: Byblos, a port city on the coast north of Sidon.
[^27-17]: Literally "strengthening your breaches": caulking the seams of the ship. Also in verse 27.
[^27-18]: *Lud* was probably Lydia, in western Asia Minor; *Put* was probably Libya.
[^27-19]: The same consonants can be read "and Helech", probably Cilicia in southeastern Asia Minor.
[^27-20]: A people or place not otherwise known.
[^27-21]: Hebrew *shelatim*, perhaps "shields" or "quivers".
[^27-22]: A distant port in the west, perhaps Tartessus in southern Spain.
[^27-23]: Literally "Tarshish was your trader from the abundance of all wealth". Also in verse 18.
[^27-24]: Hebrew *izbonim*, a word found only in this chapter, perhaps "wares" or "exports". Also in verses 14, 16, 19, 22, 27 and 33.
[^27-25]: *Javan* is Greece, or the Greeks of Ionia in western Asia Minor. *Tubal* and *Meshech* were peoples of central and eastern Asia Minor (Genesis 10:2).
[^27-26]: Literally "the *nephesh* of human beings": people sold as slaves.
[^27-27]: Literally "From the house of Togarmah", probably in eastern Asia Minor (Genesis 10:3).
[^27-28]: Or "horsemen".
[^27-29]: The Septuagint reads "the Rhodians", the people of the island of Rhodes. In Hebrew the letters *d* and *r* are easily confused. Dedan, in verse 20, was in northwestern Arabia.
[^27-30]: Literally "the market of your hand".
[^27-31]: Literally "horns of ivory".
[^27-32]: Aram is Syria. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac read "Edom", which differs by one similar letter.
[^27-33]: Hebrew *nofekh*, one of the stones of the high priest's breastpiece (Exodus 28:18). The identification is uncertain.
[^27-34]: Hebrew *buts*, a fine linen; a different word from *shesh* in verse 7.
[^27-35]: The identification of *ra'mot*, "coral", and *kadkod*, "rubies", is uncertain.
[^27-36]: A town in Ammon (Judges 11:33).
[^27-37]: Hebrew *pannag*, a word found only here, of unknown meaning: perhaps a kind of cake, or millet.
[^27-38]: A place in the hills north of Damascus, known for its wine.
[^27-39]: Or "white wool".
[^27-40]: The meaning is uncertain. *Vedan* can also be read "and Dan". *Javan* here may be a place in Arabia rather than Greece (verse 13), and *Uzal* was probably in Arabia (Genesis 10:27). The word read "from Uzal" (*me'uzzal*) can also mean "spun", as of yarn.
[^27-41]: Cassia and cane were aromatic spices (Exodus 30:23–24).
[^27-42]: Hebrew *bigde chofesh*, "garments of spreading", probably saddle blankets.
[^27-43]: A tribe of the Syrian–Arabian desert, known for its flocks (Isaiah 60:7; Jeremiah 49:28–29).
[^27-44]: Literally "the traders of your hand".
[^27-45]: Peoples of southern Arabia (Genesis 10:7).
[^27-46]: *Haran* was a city in northern Mesopotamia (Genesis 11:31), and *Eden* was probably Beth-eden on the Euphrates (Amos 1:5). *Canneh* and *Kilmad* are not otherwise known. *Asshur* is Assyria, or a place of that name.
[^27-47]: Literally "in perfect things".
[^27-48]: The meaning of several words in this verse is uncertain. *Ginze beromim*, "carpets of many colors", may be "chests of colored cloth"; *aruzim*, "made secure", may mean "made of cedar".
[^27-49]: Hebrew *sharotayikh*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "your caravans" or "your carriers".
[^27-50]: Or "very glorious". The Hebrew verb *kaved* means both "be heavy" and "be honored".
[^27-51]: Hebrew *migreshot*, usually the open land around a town; here perhaps the coastlands. The meaning is uncertain.
[^27-52]: Shaving the head and wearing sackcloth, a coarse cloth of goats' hair, were signs of mourning.
[^27-53]: Hebrew *kedumah*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "like one silenced" or "like one destroyed".
[^27-54]: Literally "At the time of being broken from the seas in the depths of the waters".

**1** The word of Yahweh[^28-1] came to me:

**2** "Son of man,[^28-2] say to the ruler of Tyre, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh[^28-3] says: Because your heart is proud, and you have said, "I am a god; I sit on the seat of the gods[^28-4] in the heart of the seas," though you are a human being and not a god, and you count your mind as the mind of a god,[^28-5]

**3** look, you are wiser than Daniel![^28-6] No secret is hidden from you.

**4** By your wisdom and your understanding you have gained wealth for yourself, and you have gathered gold and silver into your treasuries.

**5** By your great wisdom in trade you have increased your wealth, and your heart has grown proud because of your wealth.

**6** Therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh says: Because you count your mind as the mind of a god,

**7** I am going to bring foreigners against you, the most ruthless of the nations. They will draw their swords against the beauty of your wisdom and defile your splendor.

**8** They will bring you down to the Pit,[^28-7] and you will die the death of the slain in the heart of the seas.

**9** Will you still say, "I am a god," in the presence of the one who kills you? You are a human being and not a god, in the hands of those who strike you down.

**10** You will die the death of the uncircumcised[^28-8] by the hand of foreigners, for I have spoken, declares the Lord Yahweh.'"

**11** The word of Yahweh came to me:

**12** "Son of man, raise a lament over the king of Tyre and say to him, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh says: You were the seal of perfection,[^28-9] full of wisdom and perfect in beauty.

**13** You were in Eden, the garden of God. Every precious stone was your covering: carnelian, chrysolite and moonstone, beryl, onyx and jasper, lapis lazuli, turquoise and emerald,[^28-10] and gold. The workmanship of your settings and your mountings[^28-11] was in you; on the day you were created they were prepared.

**14** You were an anointed guardian cherub.[^28-12] I placed you there; you were on the holy mountain of God. You walked among the stones of fire.[^28-13]

**15** You were blameless in your ways from the day you were created, until wrongdoing was found in you.

**16** Through your great trade you were filled with violence within, and you sinned. So I banished you in disgrace[^28-14] from the mountain of God, and I destroyed you, guardian cherub, from among the stones of fire.[^28-15]

**17** Your heart grew proud because of your beauty; you corrupted your wisdom for the sake of your splendor. I threw you to the ground. I exposed you before kings, for them to gaze at you.

**18** By the great number of your iniquities, by the dishonesty of your trade, you defiled your sanctuaries. So I brought out fire from within you, and it consumed you. I turned you to ashes on the earth in the sight of all who saw you.

**19** All who knew you among the peoples are appalled at you. You have come to a horrible end,[^28-16] and you will be no more, forever.'"

**20** The word of Yahweh came to me:

**21** "Son of man, set your face toward Sidon[^28-17] and prophesy against it.

**22** Say, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh says: I am against you, Sidon, and I will display my glory within you. Then they will know that I am Yahweh, when I carry out judgments on her and show myself holy in her.

**23** I will send plague into her and bloodshed into her streets. The slain will fall within her, by the sword that is against her on every side. Then they will know that I am Yahweh.

**24** The house of Israel will no longer have a prickling brier or a painful thorn among all their neighbors who treat them with contempt. Then they will know that I am the Lord Yahweh.

**25** This is what the Lord Yahweh says: When I gather the house of Israel from the peoples among whom they have been scattered, I will show myself holy among them in the sight of the nations. They will live in their own land, which I gave to my servant Jacob.

[^28-3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD".
[^28-4]: Or "the seat of God". The Hebrew *elohim* can mean God or gods; so also "a god" at the end of the verse and in verse 6.
[^28-5]: Literally "you have set your heart like the heart of a god". The heart was thought of as the seat of understanding. Also in verse 6.
[^28-6]: The Hebrew text is written "Danel" but read "Daniel". The written form may refer to Danel, a wise and righteous king of ancient legend, known from texts found at Ugarit in Syria, rather than to the Daniel of the book of Daniel. Compare 14:14, 20.
[^28-7]: Hebrew *shachat*, the grave or the underworld.

## Chapter 29

**1** In the tenth year, in the tenth month, on the twelfth day of the month,[^29-1] the word of Yahweh[^29-2] came to me:

**2** "Son of man,[^29-3] set your face against Pharaoh king of Egypt, and prophesy against him and against all Egypt.

**3** Speak and say, 'This is what the Lord Yahweh[^29-4] says: I am against you, Pharaoh king of Egypt, the great monster[^29-5] lying in the middle of his streams,[^29-6] who has said, "My Nile is my own; I made it for myself."[^29-7]

**4** I will put hooks in your jaws and make the fish of your streams stick to your scales. I will haul you up out of your streams, with all the fish of your streams sticking to your scales.

**5** I will fling you into the wilderness, you and all the fish of your streams. You will fall on the open field; you will not be gathered up or collected.[^29-8] I have given you as food to the animals of the earth and the birds of the sky.

**6** Then all the inhabitants of Egypt will know that I am Yahweh. Because they have been a staff of reed[^29-9] for the house of Israel,

**7** when they grasped you by the hand, you splintered and tore open all their shoulders; and when they leaned on you, you broke and made all their backs go rigid.[^29-10]

**8** Therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh says: I am going to bring a sword against you, and I will cut off from you both human beings and animals.

**9** The land of Egypt will become a desolate waste. Then they will know that I am Yahweh. Because he said, "The Nile is mine; I made it,"

**10** therefore I am against you and against your streams. I will make the land of Egypt an utter ruin, a desolate waste, from Migdol to Syene, as far as the border of Cush.[^29-11]

**11** No human foot will pass through it, and no animal's foot will pass through it. It will be uninhabited for forty years.

**12** I will make the land of Egypt a desolation among desolate lands, and its cities will lie desolate among ruined cities for forty years. I will scatter the Egyptians among the nations and disperse them among the countries.

**13** For this is what the Lord Yahweh says: At the end of forty years I will gather the Egyptians from the peoples among whom they were scattered.

**14** I will restore the fortunes[^29-12] of Egypt and bring them back to the land of Pathros,[^29-13] the land of their origin. There they will be a lowly kingdom.

**15** It will be the lowliest of the kingdoms, and it will never again exalt itself above the nations. I will make them so few that they will not rule over the nations.

**16** Egypt will never again be the confidence of the house of Israel; it will only bring their iniquity to mind, when they turned to them for help.[^29-14] Then they will know that I am the Lord Yahweh.'"

**17** In the twenty-seventh year, in the first month, on the first day of the month,[^29-15] the word of Yahweh came to me:

**18** "Son of man, Nebuchadnezzar[^29-16] king of Babylon made his army labor hard against Tyre.[^29-17] Every head was rubbed bald, and every shoulder was rubbed raw.[^29-18] Yet he and his army got no wages from Tyre for the labor he had done against it.

**19** Therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh says: I am going to give the land of Egypt to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon. He will carry off its wealth,[^29-19] plunder its spoil and seize its loot, and it will be the wages for his army.

**20** As his payment for the labor he performed against it, I have given him the land of Egypt, because they did it for me, declares the Lord Yahweh.

**21** On that day I will make a horn[^29-20] sprout for the house of Israel, and I will open your mouth among them.[^29-21] Then they will know that I am Yahweh."
