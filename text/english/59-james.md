## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** James,[^1-1] a servant[^1-2] of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the Dispersion:[^1-3] Greetings.[^1-4]

**2** Consider it nothing but joy, my brothers and sisters,[^1-5] whenever you meet trials[^1-6] of many kinds,

**3** because you know that the testing of your faith produces endurance.

**4** And let endurance have its full effect,[^1-7] so that you may be mature and complete, lacking in nothing.

**5** If any of you lacks wisdom, let them ask God, who gives to all generously[^1-8] and without reproach, and it will be given to them.

**6** But they must ask in faith, without doubting, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.

**7** That person must not suppose that they will receive anything from the Lord.

**8** Such a person is double-minded,[^1-9] unstable in all their ways.

**9** Let the believer who is lowly[^1-10] boast in being lifted up,

**10** and the one who is rich in being brought low,[^1-11] because the rich will pass away like a flower in the grass.[^1-12]

**11** For the sun rises with its scorching heat[^1-13] and withers the grass; its flower falls, and the beauty of its appearance perishes. In the same way the rich will fade away in the midst of their pursuits.

**12** Blessed is the one who endures trial, because when they have stood the test they will receive the crown of life that God has promised[^1-14] to those who love him.

**13** When tempted, no one should say, "I am being tempted by God." For God cannot be tempted by evil,[^1-15] and he himself tempts no one.

**14** But each person is tempted when they are dragged away and enticed by their own desire.[^1-16]

**15** Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin, and when sin is fully grown, it gives birth to death.

**16** Do not be deceived, my dear brothers and sisters.

**17** Every good gift and every perfect present is from above,[^1-17] coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no change or shadow caused by turning.[^1-18]

**18** By his own choice he gave birth[^1-19] to us by the word of truth, so that we would be a kind of firstfruits[^1-20] of all he created.

**19** Know this,[^1-21] my dear brothers and sisters: everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to anger,

**20** for human anger[^1-22] does not produce the righteousness of God.

**21** So get rid of all filth and the evil that is everywhere,[^1-23] and humbly accept the word planted in you, which is able to save your souls.

**22** Be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.

**23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, they are like a man who looks at his own face[^1-24] in a mirror.

**24** He looks at himself, goes away, and at once forgets what he was like.

**25** But the one who looks closely into the perfect law, the law of freedom, and continues in it, not as a hearer who forgets but as a doer who acts, will be blessed in what they do.

**26** If anyone thinks they are religious but does not keep a tight rein[^1-25] on their tongue, they deceive their own heart, and their religion is worthless.

**27** Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress, and to keep oneself unstained by the world.

[^1-1]: Greek *Iakobos*, the same name as Jacob. Traditionally the writer is identified as James the brother of the Lord (Mark 6:3; Acts 15:13; Galatians 1:19).
[^1-2]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-3]: Greek *diaspora*, "scattering", the usual term for the Jews living outside the land of Israel. Here it may describe believers scattered among the nations (compare 1 Peter 1:1).
[^1-4]: Greek *chairein*, literally "rejoice", the ordinary greeting at the start of a letter (Acts 15:23). It leads into "joy" (*chara*) in verse 2.
[^1-5]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 16 and 19.
[^1-6]: Greek *peirasmos*, "trial, test" or "temptation"; also in verse 12. The related verb *peirazo* in verses 13–14 has the sense "tempt".
[^1-7]: Literally "a perfect work". Greek *teleios*, "complete, perfect, mature", the word translated "mature" later in the verse.
[^1-8]: Greek *haplos*, "simply": without reserve, with a single mind (compare "double-minded" in verse 8).
[^1-9]: Literally "a two-souled man".
[^1-10]: Literally "the lowly brother": the poor and of low standing.
[^1-11]: It is not clear whether the rich person is a believer, with "brother" understood from verse 9, or not.
[^1-12]: Compare Isaiah 40:6–8, "All people are grass … the grass withers, the flower fades". Literally "like a flower of the grass".
[^1-13]: Or "the scorching wind". Greek *kauson*, used of the hot east wind.
[^1-14]: Literally "that he promised". Many manuscripts read "that the Lord promised".
[^1-15]: Or "God has no experience of evil".
[^1-16]: The Greek words picture a fish or an animal drawn out and lured with bait.
[^1-17]: In Greek the first part of the verse forms a line of verse, perhaps a familiar saying.
[^1-18]: *The Father of lights* is the creator of the sun, moon and stars (Genesis 1:14–18). The terms *change* and *turning* may be drawn from the movements of the heavenly bodies, whose light varies and whose shadows shift; God does not change. The Greek of the last phrase is unusual, and its exact sense is uncertain.
[^1-19]: Greek *apokyeo*, the verb used of sin in verse 15.
[^1-20]: The first and best part of a harvest, which belonged to God (Leviticus 23:10).
[^1-21]: Or "You know this". Many manuscripts read "So then, my dear brothers and sisters, everyone should be …".
[^1-22]: Literally "the anger of a man".
[^1-23]: Literally "the abundance of evil".
[^1-24]: Literally "the face of his birth", his natural face.
[^1-25]: Greek *chalinagogeo*, "guide with a bridle" (compare 3:2–3).

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** My brothers and sisters,[^2-1] can you hold faith in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ[^2-2] and show favoritism?[^2-3]

**2** Suppose a man comes into your assembly[^2-4] wearing gold rings and fine clothes, and a poor man in dirty clothes also comes in,

**3** and you pay attention to the one wearing the fine clothes and say, "You sit here in a good place," but say to the poor man, "You stand, or sit there on the floor by my footstool."[^2-5]

**4** Have you not made distinctions among yourselves[^2-6] and become judges with evil thoughts?

**5** Listen, my dear brothers and sisters. Has not God chosen those who are poor in the eyes of the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom that he has promised to those who love him?

**6** But you have dishonored the poor. Is it not the rich who oppress you, and they who drag you into court?

**7** Is it not they who blaspheme the honorable name that was spoken over you?[^2-7]

**8** If you really fulfill the royal law[^2-8] according to the scripture, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself,"[^2-9] you are doing well.

**9** But if you show favoritism, you commit sin and are convicted by the law as lawbreakers.

**10** For whoever keeps the whole law but stumbles at one point has become guilty of breaking all of it.

**11** For he who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder."[^2-10] If you do not commit adultery but you do murder, you have become a lawbreaker.

**12** Speak and act as those who are going to be judged by the law of freedom.

**13** For judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment.

**14** What good is it, my brothers and sisters, if someone says they have faith but they do not have works?[^2-11] Can such faith save them?

**15** Suppose a brother or sister has no clothes[^2-12] and lacks daily food,

**16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace; keep warm and eat your fill," but you do not give them what the body needs. What good is that?

**17** So too faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.

**18** But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without works,[^2-13] and I will show you my faith by my works.[^2-14]

**19** You believe that God is one?[^2-15] You do well. Even the demons believe that, and they shudder.

**20** Do you want to be shown, you foolish person,[^2-16] that faith without works is useless?[^2-17]

**21** Was not Abraham our ancestor[^2-18] declared righteous[^2-19] by works when he offered his son Isaac on the altar?[^2-20]

**22** You see that faith was working together with his works, and faith was made complete by the works.

**23** And the scripture was fulfilled that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness,"[^2-21] and he was called God's friend.[^2-22]

**24** You see that a person is declared righteous by works and not by faith alone.

**25** In the same way, was not Rahab the prostitute also declared righteous by works when she welcomed the messengers and sent them out by another road?[^2-23]

**26** For just as the body without the spirit[^2-24] is dead, so faith without works is dead.

[^2-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 5 and 14.
[^2-2]: Literally "faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory": perhaps "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory", or "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Glory". *Faith in*: or "the faithfulness of".
[^2-3]: The SBLGNT punctuates the verse as a question expecting the answer "No". Many translations read it as a command: "do not hold the faith … while showing favoritism".
[^2-4]: Greek *synagoge*, "synagogue, gathering", used here of the believers' meeting.
[^2-5]: Literally "under my footstool".
[^2-6]: Or "been divided in your minds". Greek *diakrino*, the verb translated "doubt" in 1:6.
[^2-7]: Literally "the good name that was called upon you", the name of the one to whom they belong. Compare Amos 9:12, "the nations that are called by my name".
[^2-8]: Perhaps the law of the kingdom (verse 5), or the supreme law.
[^2-9]: Leviticus 19:18 (see Mark 12:31).
[^2-10]: Exodus 20:13–14; Deuteronomy 5:17–18 (see Mark 10:19).
[^2-11]: Greek *erga*, "works, deeds": what a person does. Also in verses 17–26.
[^2-12]: Literally "naked", that is, poorly clothed.
[^2-13]: Many manuscripts read "by your works". It is uncertain where the words of the "someone" end: after "I have works", as here, or at the end of verse 18, or later.
[^2-14]: The Greek has "my works" and "the faith"; many manuscripts read "my faith".
[^2-15]: Or "that there is one God". Compare Deuteronomy 6:4 (see Mark 12:29).
[^2-16]: Literally "empty man".
[^2-17]: Greek *arge*, "idle, useless", from *a-ergos*, "without work": a play on *erga*, "works". Many manuscripts read "dead".
[^2-18]: Literally "our father".
[^2-19]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justified"; also in verses 24 and 25. James may mean that God declared Abraham righteous on the basis of what he did, or that his actions showed him to be righteous (vindicated him). Paul uses the same verb and the same example of Abraham (Genesis 15:6) when he writes that "a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28; compare Romans 4:1–5; Galatians 2:16). Interpreters differ on how the two passages relate. Many hold that they use the words in different senses: Paul speaks of works of the law as a ground of standing with God, and James of the deeds that true faith produces; and Paul speaks of the beginning of a right relationship with God, James of its proof or of the final judgment. Others see a real difference between them, or James answering a misunderstanding of Paul's teaching.
[^2-20]: Genesis 22:9–12.
[^2-21]: Genesis 15:6, as in the Septuagint, which has "God" and the passive "it was credited". The Hebrew reads "Abram believed Yahweh, and he credited it to him as righteousness."
[^2-22]: Compare 2 Chronicles 20:7; Isaiah 41:8.
[^2-23]: Joshua 2:1–21. *The messengers*: the two spies Joshua sent.
[^2-24]: Or "breath".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers and sisters,[^3-1] for you know that we who teach will be judged more strictly.[^3-2]

**2** For we all stumble in many ways. If anyone does not stumble in what they say, they are a perfect person,[^3-3] able to keep the whole body in check as well.[^3-4]

**3** If we put bits into the mouths of horses to make them obey us, we guide their whole bodies as well.

**4** Look at ships too. Though they are so large and are driven by strong winds, they are steered by a very small rudder wherever the will of the pilot directs.

**5** So also the tongue is a small part of the body, yet it boasts of great things. See how great a forest is set ablaze by how small a fire![^3-5]

**6** And the tongue is a fire. The tongue is a world of wrongdoing set among the parts of our bodies. It stains the whole body, sets on fire the whole course of life,[^3-6] and is itself set on fire by hell.[^3-7]

**7** For every kind of animal and bird, of reptile and sea creature, can be tamed and has been tamed by humankind,

**8** but no human being can tame the tongue. It is a restless evil,[^3-8] full of deadly poison.

**9** With it we bless the Lord and Father,[^3-9] and with it we curse people, who have been made in the likeness of God.[^3-10]

**10** From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers and sisters, this ought not to be so.

**11** Does a spring pour out both fresh and bitter water from the same opening?

**12** My brothers and sisters, can a fig tree produce olives, or a grapevine figs? Neither can salt water produce fresh.[^3-11]

**13** Who among you is wise and understanding? Let them show it by their good way of life, by works done in the gentleness that comes from wisdom.

**14** But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast and do not lie against the truth.

**15** This is not the wisdom that comes down from above. It is earthly, unspiritual,[^3-12] demonic.

**16** For where there is jealousy and selfish ambition, there is disorder and every evil practice.

**17** But the wisdom from above is first of all pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial[^3-13] and sincere.[^3-14]

**18** And a harvest of righteousness[^3-15] is sown in peace by those who make peace.[^3-16]

[^3-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 10 and 12.
[^3-2]: Literally "we will receive greater judgment".
[^3-3]: Literally "a perfect man". Greek *teleios*, "complete, mature" (1:4).
[^3-4]: Literally "to bridle the whole body too" (compare 1:26).
[^3-5]: Or "how great a fire sets ablaze how great a forest". Greek *helikos* can mean "how great" or "how small".
[^3-6]: Literally "the wheel of becoming (or of birth)", perhaps the whole round of human existence. The Greek of this verse is difficult, and its parts can be joined in different ways.
[^3-7]: Greek *Gehenna* (see Mark 9:43).
[^3-8]: Many manuscripts read "an uncontrollable evil".
[^3-9]: Many manuscripts read "God and Father".
[^3-10]: Compare Genesis 1:26–27.
[^3-11]: Many manuscripts read "So no spring produces both salt water and fresh."
[^3-12]: Greek *psychikos*, "of the soul": merely natural or human, without God's Spirit (compare 1 Corinthians 2:14).
[^3-13]: Or "unwavering". Greek *adiakritos*, related to *diakrino*, "doubt" (1:6), "make distinctions" (2:4).
[^3-14]: Literally "without hypocrisy".
[^3-15]: Literally "the fruit of righteousness", which may mean the fruit that righteousness produces or the fruit that is righteousness.
[^3-16]: Or "for those who make peace".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Where do the wars and fights among you come from? Do they not come from this, from your desires for pleasure that are at war within you?[^4-1]

**2** You desire and do not have. You murder and covet, and you cannot obtain. You fight and wage war.[^4-2] You do not have because you do not ask.

**3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend what you get on your pleasures.

**4** You adulterous people![^4-3] Do you not know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? So whoever chooses to be a friend of the world becomes an enemy of God.

**5** Or do you think the scripture means nothing when it says, "He yearns jealously for the spirit he made to live in us"?[^4-4]

**6** But he gives greater grace. That is why it says, "God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble."[^4-5]

**7** So submit yourselves to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

**8** Come near to God, and he will come near to you. Wash your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded.[^4-6]

**9** Grieve, mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom.

**10** Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will lift you up.

**11** Do not speak against one another, brothers and sisters.[^4-7] Whoever speaks against a brother or sister or judges them speaks against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge of it.

**12** There is only one lawgiver and judge,[^4-8] the one who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor?

**13** Come now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will go to this or that city, spend a year there, do business and make a profit."

**14** You do not know what your life will be like tomorrow.[^4-9] You are a mist[^4-10] that appears for a little while and then vanishes.

**15** Instead, you ought to say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that."

**16** But as it is, you boast in your arrogance. All such boasting is evil.

**17** So whoever knows the right thing to do and does not do it, for them it is sin.

[^4-1]: Literally "in your members", the parts of your body (compare 3:6).
[^4-2]: Or, punctuated differently, "You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and wage war."
[^4-3]: Literally "Adulteresses!", the prophets' picture of God's people as an unfaithful wife (Hosea 3:1; Jeremiah 3:20). Many manuscripts read "Adulterers and adulteresses!"
[^4-4]: No passage of the Old Testament has these words. The Greek can be understood in several ways: "He (God) yearns jealously for the spirit he made to live in us" (compare Exodus 20:5; 34:14); "The spirit he made to live in us longs enviously", of the craving of the human spirit; or "The Spirit he made to live in us yearns jealously". Some punctuate it as a second question. Many manuscripts read "the spirit that lives in us".
[^4-5]: Proverbs 3:34, as in the Septuagint; also quoted in 1 Peter 5:5. The Hebrew reads "He mocks the mockers, but he gives favor to the humble."
[^4-6]: Literally "two-souled", as in 1:8.
[^4-7]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^4-8]: Many manuscripts leave out "and judge".
[^4-9]: Or "You do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life?"
[^4-10]: Or "a vapor", "a puff of smoke". Many manuscripts read "It is a mist", that is, your life.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Come now, you rich people, weep and wail over the miseries that are coming on you.

**2** Your wealth has rotted, and your clothes have become moth-eaten.

**3** Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion[^5-1] will be evidence against you, and it will eat your flesh. The treasure you have stored up in the last days is like fire.[^5-2]

**4** Look, the wages you withheld from the workers who mowed your fields are crying out, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of Armies.[^5-3]

**5** You have lived on the earth in luxury and self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.[^5-4]

**6** You have condemned and murdered the righteous one. Does he not oppose you?[^5-5]

**7** Be patient, then, brothers and sisters,[^5-6] until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for the precious crop from the land, being patient over it until it receives the early and the late rains.[^5-7]

**8** You too must be patient. Strengthen your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is near.

**9** Do not complain against one another, brothers and sisters, so that you will not be judged. Look, the Judge is standing at the door!

**10** As an example of suffering and patience, brothers and sisters, take the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.

**11** Look, we call blessed those who endured. You have heard of the endurance of Job, and you have seen the outcome the Lord brought about,[^5-8] for the Lord is full of compassion and mercy.[^5-9]

**12** Above all, my brothers and sisters, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or with any other oath. Let your "Yes" be yes and your "No" be no, so that you may not fall under judgment.[^5-10]

**13** Is anyone among you suffering? Let them pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let them sing praise.

**14** Is anyone among you sick? Let them call for the elders of the church, and let the elders pray over them, anointing them with oil in the name of the Lord.

**15** And the prayer offered in faith will save[^5-11] the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise them up. And if they have committed sins, they will be forgiven.

**16** So confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it works.[^5-12]

**17** Elijah was a human being just like us. He prayed earnestly[^5-13] that it would not rain, and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.[^5-14]

**18** Then he prayed again, and the sky gave rain, and the land produced its crops.[^5-15]

**19** My brothers and sisters, if anyone among you wanders from the truth and someone brings them back,

**20** let that person know that whoever brings a sinner back from the error of their way will save their soul from death[^5-16] and will cover a multitude of sins.[^5-17]

[^5-1]: Greek *ios*, "rust", the word translated "poison" in 3:8.
[^5-2]: Or, punctuated differently, "and it will eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up treasure in the last days."
[^5-3]: Greek *Kyrios Sabaoth*, from the Hebrew title *Yahweh tseva'ot*, "Yahweh of Armies", traditionally "the Lord of hosts". Compare Isaiah 5:9, "Yahweh of Armies has sworn in my hearing", and Deuteronomy 24:14–15.
[^5-4]: Many manuscripts read "as in a day of slaughter". Compare Jeremiah 12:3, "set them apart for the day of killing".
[^5-5]: The SBLGNT punctuates this as a question. *He* may be God, who "opposes the proud" (4:6, the same verb), or the righteous one, whose cry rises against them. Many translations read it as a statement: "who does not resist you".
[^5-6]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 9, 10, 12 and 19.
[^5-7]: Literally "the early and the late", the autumn and spring rains (Deuteronomy 11:14). Many manuscripts add "rain".
[^5-8]: Literally "the end of the Lord": the purpose he had and the good end he gave (Job 42:10–17).
[^5-9]: Compare Psalm 103:8, "Yahweh is compassionate and gracious".
[^5-10]: Many manuscripts read "fall into hypocrisy". Compare Matthew 5:34–37.
[^5-11]: Or "heal". Greek *sozo* means both.
[^5-12]: Or "The prayer of a righteous person, when it is put into action, can do much."
[^5-13]: Literally "he prayed with prayer", a Hebrew way of giving emphasis.
[^5-14]: 1 Kings 17:1; 18:1, where the rain comes "in the third year". Compare Luke 4:25, "three years and six months".
[^5-15]: 1 Kings 18:41–45.
[^5-16]: Literally "will save his soul from death": the sinner's soul, or perhaps the soul of the one who brings the sinner back. Many manuscripts read "will save a soul from death".
[^5-17]: Compare Proverbs 10:12, "love covers all offenses", and 1 Peter 4:8.
