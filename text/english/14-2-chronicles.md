## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Solomon son of David established himself firmly over his kingdom.[^1-1] Yahweh[^1-2] his God was with him and made him exceedingly great.

**2** Solomon spoke to all Israel: to the commanders of thousands and of hundreds, to the judges, and to every leader in all Israel, the heads of the ancestral families.[^1-3]

**3** Then Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place[^1-4] at Gibeon,[^1-5] because God's tent of meeting,[^1-6] which Moses the servant of Yahweh had made in the wilderness, was there.

**4** But David had brought up the ark of God from Kiriath-jearim to the place he had prepared for it,[^1-7] for he had pitched a tent for it in Jerusalem.

**5** The bronze altar that Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, had made was there[^1-8] in front of the tabernacle of Yahweh, and Solomon and the assembly sought it out.[^1-9]

**6** There Solomon went up to the bronze altar before Yahweh, which belonged to the tent of meeting, and he offered a thousand burnt offerings on it.

**7** That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him, "Ask for whatever you want me to give you."

**8** Solomon said to God, "You have shown great faithful love[^1-10] to my father David, and you have made me king in his place.

**9** Now, Yahweh God, let your word to my father David be confirmed, for you have made me king over a people as numerous as the dust of the earth.

**10** Now give me wisdom and knowledge, so that I may go out and come in before this people.[^1-11] For who can govern[^1-12] this great people of yours?"

**11** God said to Solomon, "Because this was in your heart, and you have not asked for riches, wealth or honor, or for the lives of those who hate you, nor have you asked for a long life,[^1-13] but you have asked for wisdom and knowledge for yourself, so that you may govern my people over whom I have made you king,

**12** wisdom and knowledge are granted to you. I will also give you riches, wealth and honor, such as the kings before you never had, and none after you will have."

**13** Then Solomon came from[^1-14] the high place at Gibeon, from before the tent of meeting, to Jerusalem, and he reigned over Israel.

**14** Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen.[^1-15] He had 1,400 chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, and he stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.

**15** The king made silver and gold[^1-16] as common in Jerusalem as stones, and he made cedar as plentiful as the sycamore-fig trees in the Shephelah.[^1-17]

**16** Solomon's horses were imported from Egypt and from Kue.[^1-18] The king's traders bought them from Kue at a price.

**17** They imported a chariot from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver,[^1-19] and a horse for 150. In the same way, through their agents,[^1-20] they exported them to all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

**18** Solomon resolved[^1-21] to build a house for the name of Yahweh, and a royal palace for himself.[^1-22]

[^1-1]: Literally "strengthened himself over his kingdom".
[^1-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-3]: Literally "the heads of the fathers".
[^1-4]: Hebrew *bamah*: a place of worship, often on a hilltop. Also in verse 13.
[^1-5]: Verses 3–13 parallel 1 Kings 3:4–15.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *ohel mo'ed*: the tent where Yahweh met with Moses and the people, that is, the tabernacle (Exodus 27:21; 40:34–35). Also in verses 6 and 13.
[^1-7]: Literally "in what David had prepared for it". See 1 Chronicles 13 and 15–16.
[^1-8]: The Hebrew vowel points read "he had placed". The same consonants can be read "there", as the Septuagint and the Vulgate understood them.
[^1-9]: Or "sought him", that is, Yahweh. The Hebrew verb *darash*, "seek, inquire of", is often used in Chronicles for turning to Yahweh in worship.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^1-11]: That is, lead them.
[^1-12]: Literally "judge". Also in verse 11.
[^1-13]: Literally "many days".
[^1-14]: The Hebrew reads "to"; the sense requires "from", as in the Septuagint and the Vulgate.
[^1-15]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*. Also later in the verse. Verses 14–17 parallel 1 Kings 10:26–29; compare 2 Chronicles 9:25–28.
[^1-16]: 1 Kings 10:27 has only "silver".
[^1-17]: The low hills between the hill country of Judah and the coastal plain.
[^1-18]: Hebrew *miqve'*, probably Kue, that is, Cilicia, in what is now southern Turkey. Also later in the verse. Some take the word as "in droves".
[^1-19]: Literally "six hundred of silver", that is, shekels. A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so six hundred shekels was about 15 pounds (6.9 kg), and 150 shekels about 3¾ pounds (1.7 kg).
[^1-20]: Literally "by their hand".
[^1-21]: Literally "said".
[^1-22]: Literally "a house for his kingdom". Verse 18 is 2:1 in most English Bibles.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Solomon assigned[^2-1] 70,000 men to carry loads and 80,000 men to cut stone in the hills, and 3,600 to supervise them.[^2-2]

**2** Solomon sent this message to Huram[^2-3] king of Tyre: "Do for me as you did for my father David, when you sent him cedars to build himself a house to live in.[^2-4]

**3** I am about to build a house for the name of Yahweh[^2-5] my God, to dedicate it to him, to burn fragrant incense before him, for the bread that is set out regularly,[^2-6] and for burnt offerings morning and evening, on the Sabbaths, the new moons and the appointed festivals of Yahweh our God. This is a duty for Israel forever.

**4** The house I am building will be great, for our God is greater than all gods.

**5** But who is able to build him a house, since the heavens, even the highest heavens,[^2-7] cannot contain him? Who am I to build him a house, except as a place to burn incense[^2-8] before him?

**6** So now send me a skilled man to work in gold, silver, bronze and iron, and in purple, crimson[^2-9] and blue yarn, and who knows how to engrave, to work with the skilled craftsmen who are with me in Judah and Jerusalem, whom my father David provided.

**7** Send me also cedar, cypress[^2-10] and algum[^2-11] logs from Lebanon, for I know that your servants know how to cut the timber of Lebanon. My servants will work with your servants

**8** to prepare timber for me in abundance, for the house I am building will be great and wonderful.

**9** I will give your servants, the woodcutters who cut the timber, twenty thousand kors[^2-12] of ground wheat,[^2-13] twenty thousand kors of barley, twenty thousand baths[^2-14] of wine and twenty thousand baths of oil."[^2-15]

**10** Huram king of Tyre answered in a letter that he sent to Solomon: "Because Yahweh loves his people, he has made you king over them."

**11** Huram also said, "Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Israel, who made the heavens and the earth! He has given King David a wise son, endowed with insight and understanding, who will build a house for Yahweh and a royal palace for himself.[^2-16]

**12** So now I am sending you a skilled man, endowed with understanding, Huram-abi.[^2-17]

**13** He is the son of a woman from the daughters of Dan,[^2-18] and his father was a man of Tyre. He knows how to work in gold and silver, bronze and iron, stone and wood, and in purple and blue yarn, fine linen[^2-19] and crimson. He knows how to do every kind of engraving and to carry out any design that may be given to him, with your skilled craftsmen and the skilled craftsmen of my lord David, your father.

**14** So now let my lord send his servants the wheat and the barley, the oil and the wine, that he has spoken of.

**15** We will cut all the timber you need from Lebanon and bring it to you as rafts by sea to Joppa, and you can take it up to Jerusalem."

**16** Solomon took a census of all the foreigners[^2-20] who were in the land of Israel, following the census that his father David had taken of them,[^2-21] and they were found to number 153,600.

**17** He made 70,000 of them carriers of loads and 80,000 stonecutters in the hills, and 3,600 overseers to make the people work.

[^2-1]: Literally "counted".
[^2-2]: Verses 1–17 are 2:2–18 in most English Bibles. They parallel 1 Kings 5:15–32 (English 5:1–18), which gives the number of overseers as 3,300 (1 Kings 5:30, English 5:16).
[^2-3]: Called Hiram in 1 Kings 5. Also in verses 10 and 11.
[^2-4]: Literally "As you did with David my father, and sent him cedars to build him a house to live in." The words "so do for me" are understood.
[^2-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-6]: Literally "and for the regular arrangement": the bread of the Presence, set out in rows on the table every Sabbath (Leviticus 24:5–8).
[^2-7]: Literally "the heavens and the heaven of heavens".
[^2-8]: Or "to burn offerings". The Hebrew verb *qatar* means to make something go up in smoke.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *karmil*, a word for crimson found only in Chronicles. Exodus uses *tola'at shani* (Exodus 25:4). Also in verse 13.
[^2-10]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the cypress or the juniper.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *algummim*, a costly wood that cannot be identified with certainty; perhaps red sandalwood. In 1 Kings 10:11–12 it is called *almuggim* and is brought from Ophir.
[^2-12]: A kor was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters).
[^2-13]: Hebrew *chittim makkot*, literally "wheat of blows", probably wheat that had been threshed or ground. 1 Kings 5:25 (English 5:11) has *makkolet*, "food".
[^2-14]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters).
[^2-15]: 1 Kings 5:25 (English 5:11) says Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand kors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty kors of pressed olive oil.
[^2-16]: Literally "a house for his kingdom".
[^2-17]: Hebrew *le-Huram avi*, "Huram my father": probably a name, or a title such as "my master craftsman". In 1 Kings 7:13 he is called Hiram.
[^2-18]: 1 Kings 7:14 says his mother was a widow from the tribe of Naphtali.
[^2-19]: Hebrew *buts*, a fine linen. Exodus uses *shesh* (Exodus 25:4).
[^2-20]: Hebrew *gerim*: foreigners living among the Israelites.
[^2-21]: See 1 Chronicles 22:2.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Solomon began to build the house of Yahweh[^3-1] in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah,[^3-2] where Yahweh had appeared to his father David,[^3-3] at the place that David had prepared,[^3-4] on the threshing floor of Ornan[^3-5] the Jebusite.

**2** He began to build on the second day of the second month,[^3-6] in the fourth year of his reign.

**3** These are the foundations Solomon laid for building the house of God:[^3-7] the length, in cubits of the old standard,[^3-8] was sixty cubits, and the width twenty cubits.

**4** The porch[^3-9] in front of the house was twenty cubits long, across the width of the house, and 120 high.[^3-10] He overlaid it on the inside with pure gold.

**5** He paneled the great house[^3-11] with cypress wood[^3-12] and overlaid it with fine gold, and he decorated it with palm trees and chains.

**6** He adorned the house with precious stones for beauty, and the gold was gold from Parvaim.[^3-13]

**7** He overlaid the house with gold: its beams, its thresholds, its walls and its doors. And he carved cherubim[^3-14] on the walls.

**8** He made the most holy place.[^3-15] Its length, across the width of the house, was twenty cubits, and its width was twenty cubits. He overlaid it with six hundred talents[^3-16] of fine gold.

**9** The weight of the nails was fifty shekels[^3-17] of gold. He also overlaid the upper rooms with gold.

**10** In the most holy place he made two cherubim of sculptured work,[^3-18] and they overlaid them with gold.

**11** The wings of the cherubim were twenty cubits long in all. One wing of the first cherub, five cubits long, touched the wall of the house, and its other wing, five cubits long, touched the wing of the other cherub.

**12** One wing of the other cherub, five cubits long, touched the wall of the house, and its other wing, five cubits long, was joined to the wing of the first cherub.[^3-19]

**13** The wings of these cherubim spread out twenty cubits. They stood on their feet, facing the house.[^3-20]

**14** He made the veil[^3-21] of blue, purple and crimson yarn and fine linen, and he worked cherubim into it.

**15** In front of the house he made two pillars, thirty-five cubits long,[^3-22] with a capital[^3-23] on top of each, five cubits high.

**16** He made chains in the inner sanctuary[^3-24] and put them on the tops of the pillars. He made a hundred pomegranates and put them on the chains.

**17** He set up the pillars in front of the main hall,[^3-25] one on the right and one on the left. He named the one on the right Jakin[^3-26] and the one on the left Boaz.[^3-27]

[^3-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple. This chapter parallels 1 Kings 6 and 7:15–22, in shorter form.
[^3-2]: The only other place the name Moriah occurs is Genesis 22:2.
[^3-3]: Literally "which was seen by David his father". See 1 Chronicles 21:15–22:1.
[^3-4]: Literally "which he prepared in the place of David".
[^3-5]: Called Araunah in 2 Samuel 24:16–25.
[^3-6]: Literally "in the second month, in the second".
[^3-7]: Literally "These Solomon founded to build the house of God".
[^3-8]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the house was about 90 feet long and 30 feet wide (27 by 9 m). The "old standard" was probably the older, shorter cubit, as against a longer one of about 21 inches (52 cm) (Ezekiel 40:5).
[^3-9]: Hebrew *ulam*: an entrance hall in front of the main hall; traditionally "porch" or "vestibule".
[^3-10]: About 180 feet (54 m). 1 Kings 6:3 does not give the height of the porch.
[^3-11]: That is, the main hall, the large room in front of the most holy place (1 Kings 6:17).
[^3-12]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the cypress or the juniper.
[^3-13]: A place not otherwise known.
[^3-14]: Hebrew *keruvim* (singular *keruv*, "cherub"): winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (Genesis 3:24; Exodus 25:18–22).
[^3-15]: Literally "the house of the holy of holies". Also in verse 10.
[^3-16]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so six hundred talents was about 45,000 pounds (20,400 kg).
[^3-17]: A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so fifty shekels was about 1¼ pounds (575 grams).
[^3-18]: Hebrew *tsa'tsu'im*, a word found only here, of uncertain meaning.
[^3-19]: Literally "And the wing of the one cherub … to the wing of the other cherub".
[^3-20]: Literally "and their faces toward the house", that is, toward the main hall.
[^3-21]: Hebrew *parokhet*: the hanging that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33). 1 Kings 6:31–32 speaks of doors of olive wood.
[^3-22]: 1 Kings 7:15 and Jeremiah 52:21 give the height of each pillar as eighteen cubits.
[^3-23]: Hebrew *tsefet*, found only here. Elsewhere the capitals are called *koterot* (4:12; 1 Kings 7:16).
[^3-24]: Hebrew *devir*: the innermost room, the most holy place (1 Kings 6:5, 16). The sense here is uncertain.
[^3-25]: Hebrew *heikhal*, "palace, temple": here the large main room of the house, in front of the inner sanctuary. Traditionally "the temple" or "the nave".
[^3-26]: *Jakin* probably means "he establishes".
[^3-27]: *Boaz* probably means "in him is strength".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** He made a bronze altar,[^4-1] twenty cubits[^4-2] long, twenty cubits wide and ten cubits high.

**2** He made the Sea[^4-3] of cast metal, ten cubits from rim to rim, round all around and five cubits high. A line of thirty cubits measured around it.

**3** Under it were figures of oxen[^4-4] encircling it all around, ten to a cubit, going all around the Sea. The oxen were in two rows, cast in one piece with it.

**4** It stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west,[^4-5] three facing south and three facing east. The Sea rested on top of them, and all their hindquarters were toward the center.

**5** It was a handbreadth[^4-6] thick, and its rim was made like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It could hold three thousand baths.[^4-7]

**6** He made ten basins[^4-8] and put five on the right and five on the left,[^4-9] for washing. In them they rinsed the things used for the burnt offering,[^4-10] but the Sea was for the priests to wash in.

**7** He made ten gold lampstands as prescribed for them[^4-11] and put them in the main hall,[^4-12] five on the right and five on the left.

**8** He made ten tables and placed them in the main hall, five on the right and five on the left. And he made a hundred gold bowls.[^4-13]

**9** He made the courtyard of the priests and the great court,[^4-14] and doors for the court, and he overlaid their doors with bronze.

**10** He put the Sea on the south side, toward the southeast.[^4-15]

**11** Huram[^4-16] made the pots,[^4-17] the shovels and the bowls. So Huram finished the work that he did for King Solomon in the house of God:

**12** the two pillars; the bowls[^4-18] and the capitals on top of the pillars, two of them; the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on top of the pillars;

**13** the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the pillars;[^4-19]

**14** he made the stands,[^4-20] and he made the basins on the stands;

**15** the one Sea, and the twelve oxen under it;

**16** and the pots, the shovels, the forks and all their utensils. Huram-abi[^4-21] made them for King Solomon for the house of Yahweh,[^4-22] of polished bronze.

**17** The king cast them in the plain of the Jordan, in the clay ground[^4-23] between Succoth and Zeredah.[^4-24]

**18** Solomon made all these utensils in such great quantities that the weight of the bronze was never determined.

**19** Solomon also made all the utensils that were in the house of God: the gold altar;[^4-25] the tables on which was the bread of the Presence;[^4-26]

**20** the lampstands and their lamps, to burn as prescribed in front of the inner sanctuary,[^4-27] of pure gold;[^4-28]

**21** the flowers,[^4-29] the lamps and the tongs, of gold, the finest gold;[^4-30]

**22** the wick trimmers, the bowls, the dishes[^4-31] and the firepans, of pure gold; and the entrance[^4-32] of the house, its inner doors to the most holy place[^4-33] and the doors of the house to the main hall, of gold.

[^4-3]: A huge basin of bronze, called "the Sea" for its size (verse 6).
[^4-4]: 1 Kings 7:24 has *peqa'im*, "gourds", where this verse has *beqarim*, "oxen", a word that looks similar in Hebrew.
[^4-5]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean.
[^4-6]: About 3 inches (7.5 cm).
[^4-7]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters), so the Sea held about 18,000 gallons (66,000 liters). 1 Kings 7:26 reads "two thousand baths".
[^4-8]: Hebrew *kiyyor*, the word used for the bronze basin of the tabernacle (Exodus 30:18). Also in verse 14.
[^4-9]: That is, on the south and on the north. Directions were given facing east, so the right side is the south. Also in verses 7 and 8.
[^4-10]: Literally "the work of the burnt offering".
[^4-11]: Hebrew *mishpat*, here "prescribed pattern". Also in verse 20.
[^4-12]: Hebrew *heikhal*, the large main room of the house, in front of the inner sanctuary (3:17). Also in verses 8 and 22.
[^4-13]: Hebrew *mizraqim*: bowls, probably for sprinkling the blood of the sacrifices (Exodus 27:3). Also in verses 11 and 22.
[^4-14]: Hebrew *azarah*, a word for a courtyard found in Chronicles and Ezekiel. Also later in the verse.
[^4-15]: Literally "from the right shoulder, eastward, opposite the south".
[^4-16]: The craftsman Huram-abi (2:12; verse 16). The Hebrew text is written "Hiram" in the second half of the verse but read "Huram".
[^4-17]: Hebrew *sirot*. 1 Kings 7:40 has *kiyyorot*, "basins". Also in verse 16.
[^4-18]: Hebrew *gullot*, "bowls": the rounded parts of the capitals. Also later in the verse and in verse 13.
[^4-19]: Literally "on the face of the pillars".
[^4-20]: Hebrew *mekhonot*: bronze stands on wheels, each carrying a basin (1 Kings 7:27–38).
[^4-21]: Hebrew *Huram aviv*, "Huram his father". Compare 2:12.
[^4-22]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-23]: Literally "in the thickness of the ground", probably clay ground suitable for molds.
[^4-24]: 1 Kings 7:46 has Zarethan.
[^4-25]: The altar of incense (Exodus 30:1–6; 39:38).
[^4-26]: Literally "bread of the face": bread set out in Yahweh's presence (Exodus 25:30; Leviticus 24:5–9).
[^4-27]: Hebrew *devir*: the innermost room, the most holy place (3:16).
[^4-28]: Hebrew *zahav sagur*, perhaps "refined gold" or "solid gold". Also in verse 22.
[^4-29]: Hebrew *perach*, "blossom": flower-shaped ornaments on the lampstands (Exodus 25:31).
[^4-30]: Literally "it was the perfection of gold". The meaning is uncertain.
[^4-31]: Hebrew *kappot*, "palms": small dishes, perhaps for incense (Exodus 25:29).
[^4-32]: 1 Kings 7:50 has "the sockets" (Hebrew *potot*), a word that looks similar to *petach*, "entrance".
[^4-33]: Literally "the holy of holies".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** So all the work that Solomon did for the house of Yahweh[^5-1] was finished. Then Solomon brought in the things his father David had dedicated,[^5-2] and he put the silver, the gold and all the utensils in the treasuries of the house of God.

**2** Then Solomon assembled the elders of Israel and all the heads of the tribes, the leaders of the ancestral families[^5-3] of the Israelites, in Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the covenant of Yahweh from the City of David, which is Zion.

**3** All the men of Israel assembled before the king at the festival in the seventh month.[^5-4]

**4** When all the elders of Israel had come, the Levites[^5-5] took up the ark.

**5** They brought up the ark, the tent of meeting[^5-6] and all the holy utensils that were in the tent. The Levitical priests[^5-7] brought them up.

**6** King Solomon and the whole community of Israel who had gathered to him were before the ark, sacrificing so many sheep and cattle that they could not be counted or numbered.

**7** Then the priests brought the ark of the covenant of Yahweh to its place, in the inner sanctuary[^5-8] of the house, in the most holy place,[^5-9] beneath the wings of the cherubim.[^5-10]

**8** The cherubim spread out their wings over the place of the ark, and the cherubim formed a covering above the ark and its poles.

**9** The poles were so long that their ends could be seen from the ark,[^5-11] in front of the inner sanctuary, but they could not be seen from outside. It is there to this day.[^5-12]

**10** There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets that Moses put there at Horeb,[^5-13] where Yahweh made a covenant[^5-14] with the Israelites when they came out of Egypt.

**11** Now the priests came out of the holy place.[^5-15] All the priests who were present had consecrated themselves, without keeping to their divisions.[^5-16]

**12** All the Levites who were singers, Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, with their sons and their relatives, dressed in fine linen, stood east of the altar with cymbals, harps and lyres. With them were 120 priests blowing trumpets.

**13** The trumpeters and the singers joined together to make one sound, praising and thanking Yahweh. As they raised the sound with trumpets, cymbals and other musical instruments, and praised Yahweh, "For he is good, for his faithful love[^5-17] endures forever,"[^5-18] the house, the house of Yahweh, was filled with a cloud.

**14** The priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the glory of Yahweh filled the house of God.

[^5-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple. Verses 1–14 parallel 1 Kings 7:51–8:11.
[^5-2]: Literally "the holy things of David his father".
[^5-3]: Literally "the chiefs of the fathers".
[^5-4]: Literally "at the festival; it is the seventh month". The festival is the Festival of Shelters, held in that month (Leviticus 23:34). 1 Kings 8:2 also gives the old name of the month, Ethanim.
[^5-5]: 1 Kings 8:3 has "the priests".
[^5-6]: Hebrew *ohel mo'ed*: the tent where Yahweh met with Moses and the people, that is, the tabernacle kept at Gibeon (1:3).
[^5-7]: Literally "the priests, the Levites". 1 Kings 8:4 has "the priests and the Levites".
[^5-8]: Hebrew *devir*: the innermost room of the temple. Also in verse 9.
[^5-9]: Literally "the holy of holies".
[^5-10]: Hebrew *keruvim* (singular *keruv*, "cherub"): winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (3:10–13; Exodus 25:18–22).
[^5-11]: 1 Kings 8:8 reads "from the holy place".
[^5-12]: Literally "And it is there to this day", probably the ark.
[^5-13]: Another name for Mount Sinai.
[^5-14]: Literally "where Yahweh cut", the usual idiom for making a covenant.
[^5-15]: The main hall in front of the inner sanctuary. 1 Kings 8:10 goes on at once: "the cloud filled the house of Yahweh". The account of the priests and the singers in verses 11b–13a is not in Kings.
[^5-16]: The priests normally served in turns, by divisions (8:14; 1 Chronicles 24).
[^5-17]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^5-18]: A refrain of worship found also in 7:3; 1 Chronicles 16:34; Psalms 106:1; 118:1; 136:1.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Solomon said, "Yahweh[^6-1] has said that he would live in thick darkness.[^6-2]

**2** But I have built you a lofty house, a place for you to live in forever."

**9** They taught in Judah, having with them the book of the law of Yahweh. They went around through all the cities of Judah and taught among the people.

**10** The terror of Yahweh fell on all the kingdoms of the lands around Judah, and they did not make war against Jehoshaphat.

**11** Some of the Philistines brought Jehoshaphat gifts and silver as tribute, and the Arabs brought him flocks: 7,700 rams and 7,700 male goats.

**12** Jehoshaphat grew steadily greater, and he built fortresses and storage cities in Judah.

**13** He carried out many works[^17-8] in the cities of Judah, and he had soldiers, mighty warriors, in Jerusalem.

**14** This was their enrollment by their ancestral families.[^17-9] From Judah, the commanders of thousands: Adnah the commander, and with him 300,000 mighty warriors;[^17-10]

**15** next to him Jehohanan the commander, and with him 280,000;

**16** next to him Amasiah son of Zichri, who volunteered for the service of Yahweh, and with him 200,000 mighty warriors.

**17** From Benjamin: Eliada, a mighty warrior, and with him 200,000 armed with bow and shield;

**18** next to him Jehozabad, and with him 180,000 equipped for war.

**19** These were the men who served the king, besides those the king had placed in the fortified cities throughout Judah.

[^17-1]: Or "governors". Hebrew *netsivim*.
[^17-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^17-3]: Literally "in the first ways of David his father". The Septuagint lacks "David", so that "his father" would be Asa.
[^17-4]: Plural of *Baal*, "lord, master": the Canaanite god Baal, worshiped in many local forms.
[^17-5]: Literally "His heart was high". The phrase usually means pride; here it means courage or devotion.
[^17-6]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops. Compare 20:33.

## Chapter 18

*Status: draft*

**1** Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor, and he made a marriage alliance with Ahab.[^18-1]

**2** Some years later he went down to Ahab in Samaria.[^18-2] Ahab slaughtered sheep and cattle in abundance for him and for the people who were with him, and he persuaded him to go up against Ramoth-gilead.

**3** Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Will you go with me to Ramoth-gilead?" He answered him, "I am as you are, and my people are as your people. We will be with you in the battle."

**4** But Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "First, please, seek the word of Yahweh."[^18-3]

**5** So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, four hundred men, and said to them, "Shall we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?" They said, "Go up, for God will hand it over to the king."

**6** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no other prophet of Yahweh here, so that we may consult him?"

**7** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man through whom we can consult Yahweh, but I hate him, for he never prophesies anything good about me, but always evil. He is Micaiah son of Imla." Jehoshaphat said, "The king should not say that."

**8** So the king of Israel called one of his officials[^18-4] and said, "Bring Micaiah son of Imla quickly."

**9** The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting each on his throne, dressed in their robes. They were sitting at the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.

**10** Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had made iron horns for himself, and he said, "This is what Yahweh says: With these you will gore the Arameans until they are destroyed."

**11** All the prophets were prophesying the same, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-gilead and triumph. Yahweh will hand it over to the king."

**12** The messenger who had gone to call Micaiah said to him, "Look, the prophets are all saying the same thing, good for the king.[^18-5] Let your word be like one of theirs, and speak favorably."

**13** But Micaiah said, "As Yahweh lives, whatever my God says, that is what I will speak."

**14** When he came to the king, the king said to him, "Micah,[^18-6] shall we go to war against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?" He said, "Go up and triumph. They will be handed over to you."

**15** The king said to him, "How many times must I make you swear to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of Yahweh?"

**16** Then he said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains like sheep that have no shepherd. And Yahweh said, 'These have no master. Let each of them go home in peace.'"

**17** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Didn't I tell you he would not prophesy anything good about me, but only evil?"

**18** Micaiah said, "Therefore hear the word of Yahweh. I saw Yahweh sitting on his throne, with all the host of heaven[^18-7] standing on his right and on his left.

**19** Yahweh said, 'Who will entice Ahab king of Israel to go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?' One said one thing, and another said another.

**20** Then a spirit[^18-8] came forward and stood before Yahweh and said, 'I will entice him.' Yahweh said to him, 'How?'

**21** He said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' Yahweh said, 'You will entice him, and you will succeed. Go out and do so.'

**22** So now, Yahweh has put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets of yours. Yahweh has decreed disaster for you."[^18-9]

**23** Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the Spirit of Yahweh go when he passed from me to speak to you?"

**24** Micaiah said, "You will see, on the day when you go into an inner room to hide."[^18-10]

**25** The king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and send him back to Amon the governor of the city and to Joash the king's son,

**26** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this man in prison and feed him with scant bread and scant water[^18-11] until I return safely.'"

**27** Micaiah said, "If you ever return safely, Yahweh has not spoken through me." And he said, "Listen, all you peoples!"[^18-12]

**28** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**29** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they went into battle.

**30** Now the king of Aram had commanded his chariot commanders, "Do not fight with anyone, small or great, but only with the king of Israel."

**31** When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "That is the king of Israel," and they turned to fight against him. But Jehoshaphat cried out, and Yahweh helped him. God drew them away from him.

**32** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.

**33** But a man drew his bow at random[^18-13] and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armor.[^18-14] He said to his chariot driver, "Turn around[^18-15] and take me out of the battle,[^18-16] for I am wounded."

**34** The battle grew fierce that day, and the king of Israel propped himself up in his chariot facing the Arameans until evening. At sunset he died.

[^18-4]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official. The word can also mean a eunuch.

[^18-7]: The heavenly beings who serve Yahweh.

[^18-10]: Literally "go into a room within a room to hide".
