## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia,[^1-1] to fulfill the word of Yahweh[^1-2] spoken by Jeremiah,[^1-3] Yahweh stirred the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, and he sent a proclamation throughout his whole kingdom and also put it in writing:[^1-4]

**2** "This is what Cyrus king of Persia says: Yahweh, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and he has appointed me[^1-5] to build him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

**3** Whoever among you belongs to any of his people, may his God be with him! Let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of Yahweh, the God of Israel. He is the God who is in Jerusalem.[^1-6]

**4** And every survivor,[^1-7] in whatever place he is living as a foreigner, is to be helped[^1-8] by the people of his place with silver and gold, with goods and with livestock, besides freewill offerings for the house of God in Jerusalem."

**5** Then the heads of the ancestral families[^1-9] of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests and the Levites, everyone whose spirit God had stirred, got ready to go up and build the house of Yahweh in Jerusalem.

**6** All their neighbors helped them[^1-10] with articles of silver, with gold, with goods, with livestock and with valuable gifts, besides everything that was given as a freewill offering.

**7** King Cyrus also brought out the articles of the house of Yahweh that Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem and put in the house of his gods.[^1-11]

**8** Cyrus king of Persia had them brought out by Mithredath the treasurer,[^1-12] who counted them out to Sheshbazzar, the leader of Judah.[^1-13]

**9** This was their number: 30 gold basins,[^1-14] 1,000 silver basins, 29 knives,[^1-15]

**10** 30 gold bowls, 410 silver bowls of a second kind[^1-16] and 1,000 other articles.

**11** All the articles of gold and of silver numbered 5,400.[^1-17] Sheshbazzar brought them all up when the exiles were brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem.

[^1-1]: Verses 1–3 repeat, with small differences, the last two verses of 2 Chronicles (36:22–23), which break off in the middle of verse 3 at "let him go up". Cyrus II of Persia conquered Babylon in 539 BC, so his first year as its king was 538 BC.
[^1-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-3]: Literally "from the mouth of Jeremiah". See Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10.
[^1-4]: Literally "and he made a voice pass through all his kingdom, and also in writing, saying".
[^1-5]: Or "charged me". Literally "he has appointed on me".
[^1-6]: Or "the God of Israel. He is God. It is in Jerusalem." 2 Chronicles 36:23 reads "may Yahweh his God be with him, and let him go up" and ends there.
[^1-7]: Literally "everyone who remains", that is, the people of Judah who survived the exile.
[^1-8]: Literally "the men of his place are to lift him up".
[^1-9]: Literally "the heads of the fathers", that is, of the fathers' houses: extended families descended from a common ancestor. The phrase recurs throughout Ezra and Nehemiah.
[^1-10]: Literally "all those around them strengthened their hands".
[^1-11]: Or "his god". See 2 Kings 24:13; 25:13–15; Daniel 1:2.
[^1-12]: Hebrew *gizbar*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *nasi*, "leader, prince". Sheshbazzar appears again in 5:14–16.
[^1-14]: The meaning of Hebrew *agartelim* is uncertain: some kind of basin or dish. Also later in the verse.
[^1-15]: The meaning of Hebrew *machalaphim* is uncertain: perhaps knives, or vessels of some other kind.
[^1-16]: The meaning of Hebrew *mishnim* is uncertain: perhaps "of second quality" or "matching".
[^1-17]: The items listed in verses 9–10 add up to 2,499.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** When the seventh month came,[^3-1] and the Israelites were in their towns, the people gathered as one man in Jerusalem.

**2** Then Jeshua son of Jozadak and his fellow priests,[^3-2] and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and his kinsmen, set to work and built the altar of the God of Israel, to offer burnt offerings[^3-3] on it, as it is written in the Law of Moses, the man of God.

**3** They set up the altar on its foundations, because they were afraid of the peoples of the lands,[^3-4] and they offered burnt offerings on it to Yahweh,[^3-5] burnt offerings morning and evening.[^3-6]

**4** They kept the Festival of Shelters,[^3-7] as it is written, and offered the daily burnt offerings by number, as prescribed for each day.[^3-8]

**5** After that they offered the regular burnt offering,[^3-9] the offerings for the new moons and for all the sacred appointed festivals of Yahweh, and the offerings of everyone who brought a freewill offering to Yahweh.

**6** From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings to Yahweh, but the foundation of the temple of Yahweh had not yet been laid.

**7** So they gave money to the stonecutters and the craftsmen, and food, drink and oil to the people of Sidon and Tyre, to bring cedar wood from Lebanon by sea to Joppa,[^3-10] as Cyrus king of Persia had authorized them.

**8** In the second month of the second year after they came to the house of God in Jerusalem, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, Jeshua son of Jozadak and the rest of their kinsmen, the priests and the Levites, and all who had come to Jerusalem from the captivity, made a start. They appointed the Levites who were twenty years old or more to supervise the work on the house of Yahweh.

**9** Jeshua,[^3-11] with his sons and his kinsmen, and Kadmiel and his sons, the descendants of Judah,[^3-12] together took charge of supervising those doing the work on the house of God, along with the descendants of Henadad, their sons and their kinsmen, the Levites.

**10** When the builders laid the foundation of the temple of Yahweh, the priests in their vestments[^3-13] were stationed with trumpets, and the Levites, the descendants of Asaph, with cymbals, to praise Yahweh as David king of Israel had directed.[^3-14]

**11** They sang responsively,[^3-15] praising and giving thanks to Yahweh: "For he is good; his faithful love[^3-16] toward Israel lasts forever." And all the people shouted with a great shout as they praised Yahweh, because the foundation of the house of Yahweh had been laid.

**12** But many of the priests and the Levites and the heads of the ancestral families, the old men who had seen the first house, wept aloud when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes,[^3-17] while many others shouted aloud for joy.

**13** The people could not tell the sound of the joyful shouting from the sound of the people's weeping, because the people were shouting with a great shout, and the sound was heard far away.

[^3-3]: Hebrew *olot*, from the verb "go up": offerings wholly burned on the altar (Leviticus 1).
[^3-4]: Or "even though they were afraid of the peoples of the lands". The Hebrew is difficult. *The peoples of the lands* are the other peoples living in and around Judah.
[^3-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-6]: The Hebrew text is written "he offered" but read "they offered".
[^3-7]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths, shelters". Traditionally "Tabernacles". See Leviticus 23:33–43; Numbers 29:12–38.
[^3-8]: Literally "according to the ruling, the matter of a day in its day".
[^3-9]: Hebrew *olat tamid*: the burnt offering made every morning and evening (Numbers 28:3–8).
[^3-10]: Literally "to the sea of Joppa".
[^3-11]: This Jeshua is probably the Levite of 2:40, not the priest of verse 8.
[^3-12]: Kadmiel is a Levite in 2:40, where he is listed with "the descendants of Hodaviah".
[^3-13]: Literally "clothed".
[^3-14]: Literally "according to the hands of David king of Israel". See 1 Chronicles 16:4–6; 25:1.
[^3-15]: Literally "They answered", perhaps one group answering another. The words that follow are a refrain found in Psalms 106:1; 107:1; 118:1; 136:1.
[^3-16]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^3-17]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "who had seen the first house in its founding, this house in their eyes".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** When the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles[^4-1] were building a temple for Yahweh,[^4-2] the God of Israel,

**2** they came to Zerubbabel and to the heads of the ancestral families and said to them, "Let us build with you, for like you we seek your God, and we have been sacrificing to him[^4-3] since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us here."[^4-4]

**3** But Zerubbabel, Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the ancestral families of Israel said to them, "It is not for you to build a house for our God with us.[^4-5] We alone will build it for Yahweh, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, the king of Persia, commanded us."

**4** Then the people of the land[^4-6] discouraged the people of Judah[^4-7] and made them afraid to build.

**5** They hired advisers against them to frustrate their plans throughout the reign of Cyrus king of Persia and until the reign of Darius king of Persia.[^4-8]

**6** In the reign of Ahasuerus,[^4-9] at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation[^4-10] against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

**7** And in the days of Artaxerxes,[^4-11] Bishlam,[^4-12] Mithredath, Tabeel and the rest of his associates[^4-13] wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The letter was written in Aramaic script and translated into Aramaic.[^4-14]

**8** Rehum the commander[^4-15] and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes, as follows:[^4-16]

**9** Then Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates, the judges, the envoys, the officials, the Persians,[^4-17] the people of Erech, the Babylonians, the people of Susa, the Dehaites,[^4-18] the Elamites,

**10** and the rest of the peoples whom the great and noble Osnappar[^4-19] deported and settled in the city of Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River.[^4-20] And now:[^4-21]

**11** This is a copy[^4-22] of the letter they sent to him: "To King Artaxerxes, from your servants, the men of the province Beyond the River. And now:

**12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have gone to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city. They are finishing the walls and repairing[^4-23] the foundations.

**13** Now let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, they will not pay tribute, tax or toll, and in the end it will bring loss to the kings.[^4-24]

**14** Now, since we eat the salt of the palace,[^4-25] and it is not right for us to see the king dishonored, we have sent to inform the king,

**15** so that a search may be made in the record books of your fathers. In the record books you will find and learn that this city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, and that revolt has been stirred up in it from long ago. That is why this city was laid waste.

**16** We are informing the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, you will then have no share in the province Beyond the River."

**17** The king sent this reply: "To Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River: Greetings.[^4-26] And now:

**18** The letter you sent to us has been read before me in translation.[^4-27]

**19** I issued a decree,[^4-28] and a search was made, and it was found that this city has risen up against kings from long ago, and that rebellion and revolt have been carried on in it.

**20** Powerful kings have ruled over Jerusalem and governed the whole province Beyond the River, and tribute, tax and toll were paid to them.

**21** Now issue a decree to make these men stop, so that this city is not rebuilt until a decree is issued by me.

**22** Take care not to be negligent in this matter. Why should the damage grow, to the harm of kings?"

**23** Then, as soon as the copy of King Artaxerxes' letter had been read before Rehum, Shimshai the scribe and their associates, they went in haste to the Jews in Jerusalem and made them stop by force of arms.[^4-29]

**24** Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped, and it remained stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.[^4-30]

**1** Now the prophets, Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo,[^5-1] prophesied to the Jews who were in Judah and Jerusalem, in the name of the God of Israel, who was over them.[^5-2]

**2** Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Jeshua son of Jozadak set to work and began to build the house of God in Jerusalem, and with them were the prophets of God, helping them.

**3** At that time Tattenai, the governor[^5-3] of the province Beyond the River,[^5-4] Shethar-bozenai and their associates came to them and said this to them: "Who issued you a decree[^5-5] to build this house and to finish this structure?"[^5-6]

**4** Then we said to them this:[^5-7] "What are the names of the men who are constructing this building?"

**5** But the eye of their God was on the elders of the Jews, and they did not make them stop until a report[^5-8] could go to Darius and a letter be sent back in reply.[^5-9]

**6** This is a copy of the letter that Tattenai, the governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and his associates, the officials[^5-10] in the province Beyond the River, sent to King Darius.

**7** They sent him a report, and this is what was written in it: "To King Darius: All peace!

**8** Let it be known to the king that we went to the province of Judah, to the house of the great God. It is being built with huge stones,[^5-11] and timber is being laid in the walls. This work is being done with diligence and is succeeding in their hands.

**9** Then we asked those elders, and we said this to them: 'Who issued you a decree to build this house and to finish this structure?'

**10** We also asked them their names, to inform you, so that we could write down the names of the men who are their leaders.[^5-12]

**11** This is the reply they gave us: 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and we are rebuilding the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and finished.

**12** But because our fathers angered the God of heaven, he gave them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, the Chaldean, who destroyed this house and deported the people to Babylon.

**13** However, in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, King Cyrus issued a decree to rebuild this house of God.

**14** King Cyrus also took out of the temple of Babylon the articles of gold and silver from the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem and brought to the temple of Babylon. They were given to a man named Sheshbazzar, whom he had appointed governor.

**15** He said to him, "Take these articles. Go and deposit them in the temple in Jerusalem, and let the house of God be rebuilt on its site."

**16** Then this Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundations of the house of God in Jerusalem. From then until now it has been under construction, and it is not yet finished.'

**17** Now, if it pleases the king, let a search be made in the royal treasury[^5-13] there in Babylon, to see whether a decree was in fact issued by King Cyrus to rebuild this house of God in Jerusalem. And let the king send us his decision on this matter."

[^5-1]: See Haggai 1:1 and Zechariah 1:1, where Zechariah is "son of Berechiah, son of Iddo". The text of 4:8–6:18 is in Aramaic.
[^5-2]: Or "in the name of the God of Israel, which was upon them".
[^5-3]: Aramaic *pechah*, "governor". Also in verses 6 and 14.
[^5-4]: The Persian province west of the Euphrates; see 4:10.
[^5-5]: Aramaic *te'em*, "order, decree". Also in verses 9, 13 and 17.
[^5-6]: The meaning of Aramaic *ushsharna* is uncertain: perhaps "structure", "woodwork" or "sanctuary". Also in verse 9.
[^5-7]: The Septuagint reads "Then they said to them", making this a second question asked by Tattenai and his associates.
[^5-8]: Aramaic *te'em*, here "report".
[^5-9]: Literally "and then they would return a letter about this".
[^5-10]: Aramaic *apharsekhaye*, a Persian word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "officials" or "inspectors".
[^5-11]: Literally "stones of rolling", stones so large they had to be rolled into place.
[^5-12]: Literally "who are at their head".
[^5-13]: Literally "the house of the treasures of the king".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Then King Darius issued a decree,[^6-1] and a search was made in the archives[^6-2] where the treasures were stored in Babylon.

**2** And in Ecbatana,[^6-3] the fortress in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and this was written in it: "Memorandum:

**3** In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus issued a decree: Concerning the house of God in Jerusalem, let the house be rebuilt as a place where sacrifices are offered, and let its foundations be firmly laid.[^6-4] Its height is to be sixty cubits[^6-5] and its width sixty cubits,

**4** with three layers of huge stones[^6-6] and one layer of new timber.[^6-7] The cost is to be paid from the royal treasury.[^6-8]

**5** Also, the articles of gold and silver from the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took out of the temple in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon, are to be returned and brought back to the temple in Jerusalem, each to its place. You are to deposit them in the house of God."

**6** "Now, Tattenai,[^6-9] governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and your associates, the officials in the province Beyond the River, keep away from there.

**7** Leave the work on this house of God alone. Let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews rebuild this house of God on its site.

**8** I also issue a decree about what you are to do for these elders of the Jews, for the building of this house of God: The cost is to be paid to these men in full from the royal revenue, from the tribute of the province Beyond the River, so that they are not stopped.[^6-10]

**9** And whatever is needed, young bulls, rams and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, and wheat, salt, wine and oil, as the priests in Jerusalem request, is to be given to them day by day without fail,

**10** so that they may offer sacrifices of pleasing aroma[^6-11] to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

**11** I also issue a decree that if anyone alters this edict, a beam is to be pulled out of his house, and he is to be lifted up and impaled on it,[^6-12] and his house is to be made a dunghill[^6-13] for this.

**12** May the God who has made his name dwell there overthrow any king or people who reaches out a hand to alter this, or to destroy this house of God in Jerusalem. I, Darius, have issued a decree. Let it be carried out with all diligence."

**13** Then Tattenai, the governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their associates did with all diligence just what King Darius had sent word to do.

**14** So the elders of the Jews built and prospered, through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo. They built and finished it by the decree of the God of Israel and by the decree of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia.[^6-14]

**15** This house was completed on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.[^6-15]

**16** And the Israelites, the priests and the Levites and the rest of the returned exiles,[^6-16] celebrated the dedication of this house of God with joy.

**17** For the dedication of this house of God they offered a hundred bulls, two hundred rams and four hundred lambs, and as a sin offering[^6-17] for all Israel, twelve male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

**18** And they installed the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their groups for the service of God in Jerusalem, as it is written in the book of Moses.[^6-18]

**19** On the fourteenth day of the first month the returned exiles kept the Passover.

**20** The priests and the Levites had purified themselves together, and all of them were clean. They slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their kinsmen the priests and for themselves.

**21** The Israelites who had returned from the exile ate it, together with everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land to join them, to seek Yahweh,[^6-19] the God of Israel.

**22** For seven days they kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread with joy, because Yahweh had made them joyful and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria[^6-20] toward them, so that he strengthened their hands in the work on the house of God, the God of Israel.

[^6-1]: Aramaic *te'em*, "order, decree". The text of 4:8–6:18 is in Aramaic.
[^6-2]: Literally "in the house of the books".
[^6-3]: Aramaic *Achmetha*: Ecbatana (modern Hamadan in Iran), a summer residence of the Persian kings.
[^6-4]: The meaning of the Aramaic is uncertain. Perhaps "let its foundations be retained" or "let burnt offerings be brought".
[^6-5]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so sixty cubits was about 90 feet (27 m). The length is not given.
[^6-6]: Literally "stones of rolling"; see 5:8.
[^6-7]: Or "one layer of timber".
[^6-8]: Literally "from the house of the king".
[^6-9]: Darius's reply to Tattenai begins here, after the copy of Cyrus's decree. The text does not mark the change.
[^6-10]: Or "without delay".
[^6-11]: Aramaic *nichochin*, related to the Hebrew phrase "pleasing aroma", used of sacrifices (Leviticus 1:9).
[^6-12]: Or "fastened to it".
[^6-13]: Or "a heap of rubble".
[^6-14]: Aramaic *te'em* is used for both the decree of God and the decree of the kings. Artaxerxes reigned after Darius; compare 7:15–24.
[^6-15]: Adar is the twelfth month, around February–March. The sixth year of Darius was 515 BC.
[^6-16]: Aramaic *bene galuta*, "the sons of the exile". In verses 19–20 the Hebrew has the same phrase, *bene ha-golah*.
[^6-17]: Or "purification offering". Aramaic *chatta'ah*, Hebrew *chatta't*: an offering to remove the effects of sin and impurity (Leviticus 4).
[^6-18]: The Aramaic section that began at 4:8 ends here. From verse 19 the text is in Hebrew again.
[^6-19]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6-20]: Here the Persian king, who now ruled the lands of the former Assyrian Empire.
