## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^1-1] called to Moses and spoke to him[^1-2] from the tent of meeting.[^1-3] He said,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When any of you brings an offering[^1-4] to the LORD, you are to bring your offering from the livestock, from the herd or from the flock.[^1-5]

**3** If his offering is a burnt offering[^1-6] from the herd, he is to offer a male without defect.[^1-7] He is to bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, so that he may be accepted[^1-8] before the LORD.

**4** He is to lay his hand[^1-9] on the head of the burnt offering, and it will be accepted on his behalf to make atonement[^1-10] for him.

**5** He is to slaughter the young bull[^1-11] before the LORD. Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to present the blood and splash the blood against all sides of the altar that is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**6** He is to skin the burnt offering and cut it into its pieces.

**7** The sons of Aaron the priest are to put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

**8** Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to arrange the pieces, with the head and the fat,[^1-12] on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**9** He is to wash its internal organs and its legs with water, and the priest is to burn[^1-13] all of it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire,[^1-14] a pleasing aroma[^1-15] to the LORD.

**10** "If his offering for a burnt offering is from the flock, from the sheep or from the goats, he is to offer a male without defect.

**11** He is to slaughter it on the north side of the altar before the LORD, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**12** He is to cut it into its pieces, with its head and its fat, and the priest is to arrange them on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**13** He is to wash the internal organs and the legs with water. Then the priest is to offer all of it and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**14** "If his offering to the LORD is a burnt offering of birds, he is to bring his offering from the turtledoves or from the young pigeons.[^1-16]

**15** The priest is to bring it to the altar, wring off its head[^1-17] and burn it on the altar. Its blood is to be drained out against the side of the altar.

**16** He is to remove its crop with its contents[^1-18] and throw it beside the altar, on the east side, in the place for the ashes.[^1-19]

**17** He is to tear it open by its wings without dividing it in two.[^1-20] Then the priest is to burn it on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

[^1-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-2]: Literally "And he called to Moses, and the LORD spoke to him". The first word, *vayyiqra*, "and he called", is the Hebrew name of the book.
[^1-3]: The tent where the LORD met with Moses (Exodus 25:22; 29:42–43), set up as described in Exodus 40. It is also called the tabernacle.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *qorban*, "something brought near", from the verb used throughout these chapters for bringing or offering a sacrifice.
[^1-5]: The herd is cattle; the flock is sheep and goats (verse 10).
[^1-6]: Hebrew *olah*, from the verb "go up": the whole animal went up in smoke on the altar, apart from the skin (7:8).
[^1-7]: Hebrew *tamim*, "whole, complete": without any blemish or defect (22:17–25).
[^1-8]: Or "so that it may be accepted".
[^1-9]: Literally "press his hand" or "lean his hand".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *kipper*, traditionally "make atonement". The word probably means "wipe clean, purge" or "pay a ransom": to remove the effects of sin or impurity.
[^1-11]: Literally "the son of the herd".
[^1-12]: Hebrew *peder*, found only in Leviticus (also verse 12 and 8:20): probably the hard fat around the internal organs.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *hiqtir*, "make go up in smoke", the word used for burning offerings on the altar. A different word (*saraf*) is used for burning things up outside the camp (4:12).
[^1-14]: Hebrew *ishsheh*, traditionally "an offering made by fire", as if from *esh*, "fire". Some think it means "gift" or "food offering" (compare 3:11).
[^1-15]: Literally "a smell of soothing": an offering that pleases the LORD. Traditionally "a sweet savor".
[^1-16]: Literally "sons of the dove": young pigeons or doves.
[^1-17]: Hebrew *malaq*, perhaps "pinch off" with the fingernail.
[^1-18]: Or "with its feathers". The meaning of the Hebrew word *notsah* here is uncertain.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *deshen*, the ashes soaked with fat from the sacrifices.
[^1-20]: Literally "he shall not divide (it)".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** "When anyone[^2-1] brings a grain offering[^2-2] to the LORD,[^2-3] his offering is to be of fine flour.[^2-4] He is to pour oil on it and put frankincense[^2-5] on it.

**2** He is to bring it to Aaron's sons, the priests, and the priest is to take from it a full handful of its fine flour and its oil, together with all its frankincense. Then the priest is to burn it on the altar as the memorial portion[^2-6] of the offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**3** The rest of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is most holy,[^2-7] a portion of the offerings by fire to the LORD.

**4** "When you bring a grain offering baked in an oven, it is to be of fine flour: cakes[^2-8] made without yeast and mixed with oil, or wafers made without yeast and spread with oil.

**5** "If your offering is a grain offering made on a griddle, it is to be of fine flour mixed with oil, made without yeast.

**6** Break it into pieces and pour oil on it. It is a grain offering.

**7** "If your offering is a grain offering made in a pan,[^2-9] it is to be made of fine flour with oil.

**8** You are to bring the grain offering made from these things to the LORD. It is to be presented to the priest, and he is to bring it to the altar.[^2-10]

**9** The priest is to take the memorial portion from the grain offering and burn it on the altar, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**10** The rest of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is most holy, a portion of the offerings by fire to the LORD.

**11** No grain offering that you bring to the LORD is to be made with yeast, for you must not burn any yeast[^2-11] or any honey[^2-12] as an offering by fire to the LORD.

**12** You may bring them to the LORD as an offering of the first produce,[^2-13] but they must not be offered up on the altar as a pleasing aroma.[^2-14]

**13** You must season every grain offering of yours with salt. Do not leave the salt of the covenant of your God out of your grain offering. You are to offer salt with all your offerings.

**14** "If you bring a grain offering of firstfruits to the LORD, you are to bring fresh ears of grain roasted in the fire, crushed kernels of new grain,[^2-15] as the grain offering of your firstfruits.

**15** Put oil on it and lay frankincense on it. It is a grain offering.

**16** The priest is to burn its memorial portion, some of its crushed grain and some of its oil, together with all its frankincense, as an offering by fire to the LORD.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "a person, a living being".
[^2-2]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute". In Leviticus it is an offering of flour or grain. Traditionally "meat offering", in the old sense of "food".
[^2-3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *solet*, finely ground flour, perhaps semolina.
[^2-5]: An aromatic resin from trees of southern Arabia and East Africa, burned as incense.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *azkarah*, "reminder, memorial": the part burned on the altar, standing for the whole offering. Or "token portion". Also in verses 9 and 16.
[^2-7]: Literally "a holy of holies": the highest degree of holiness. Only the priests could eat it, and only in a holy place (6:9–11; English 6:16–18).
[^2-8]: Hebrew *challot*, probably round cakes or loaves, perhaps pierced.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *marcheshet*, probably a deep pan for frying or boiling (compare 7:9).
[^2-10]: Literally "You shall bring the grain offering that is made from these to the LORD, and he shall present it to the priest, and he shall bring it near to the altar."
[^2-11]: Hebrew *se'or*, sourdough: a piece of fermented dough used to make bread rise.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *devash*: bees' honey, or a sweet syrup made from fruit such as dates or grapes.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *reshit*, "the first, the best": the first of the harvest given to the LORD (23:17).
[^2-14]: Literally "but they must not go up to the altar as a pleasing aroma".
[^2-15]: Hebrew *geres karmel*: freshly harvested grain, crushed or coarsely ground. The exact meaning of *karmel* is uncertain.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** "If his offering is a fellowship offering,[^3-1] and he offers an animal from the herd, whether male or female, he is to offer one without defect before the LORD.[^3-2]

**2** He is to lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it at the entrance of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash the blood against all sides of the altar.

**3** From the fellowship offering he is to present an offering by fire to the LORD: the fat[^3-3] that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**4** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins,[^3-4] and the long lobe of the liver,[^3-5] which he is to remove with the kidneys.[^3-6]

**5** Aaron's sons are to burn it on the altar on top of the burnt offering, which is on the wood on the fire, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**6** "If his offering for a fellowship offering to the LORD is from the flock, whether male or female, he is to offer one without defect.

**7** If he offers a lamb as his offering, he is to present it before the LORD.

**8** He is to lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**9** From the fellowship offering he is to present its fat as an offering by fire to the LORD: the whole fat tail,[^3-7] which he is to remove close to the backbone,[^3-8] the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**10** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**11** The priest is to burn it on the altar as food,[^3-9] an offering by fire to the LORD.

**12** "If his offering is a goat, he is to present it before the LORD.

**13** He is to lay his hand on its head and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**14** From it he is to present his offering as an offering by fire to the LORD: the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**15** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**16** The priest is to burn them on the altar as food, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma. All the fat belongs to the LORD.

**17** This is a permanent decree for all your generations, wherever you live: you must not eat any fat or any blood."

[^3-1]: Hebrew *zevach shelamim*, "a sacrifice of *shelamim*", traditionally "peace offering". *Shelamim* is related to *shalom*, "peace, well-being, wholeness", but its exact sense is uncertain. Most of the meat of this sacrifice was eaten by the worshiper and his household (7:11–21).
[^3-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-3]: Hebrew *chelev*: the layers of fat around the internal organs, not the fat mixed into the meat.
[^3-4]: Or "on the sinews". Hebrew *kesalim*, the muscles of the lower back.
[^3-5]: Literally "the appendage on the liver", probably the lobe that projects from it.
[^3-6]: Or "which he is to remove along with the kidneys". Literally "on the kidneys he shall remove it".
[^3-7]: Hebrew *alyah*: the broad tail of the fat-tailed sheep, a breed common in the Middle East.
[^3-8]: Hebrew *atseh*, found only here: probably the base of the spine.
[^3-9]: Literally "bread", a common word for food in general. Also in verse 16.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^4-1] said to Moses,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When anyone sins unintentionally[^4-2] by doing any one of the things that the LORD has commanded must not be done,

**3** if it is the anointed priest[^4-3] who sins, bringing guilt on the people, then for the sin he has committed he is to offer to the LORD a young bull without defect as a sin offering.[^4-4]

**4** He is to bring the bull to the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD, lay his hand on the bull's head and slaughter the bull before the LORD.

**5** Then the anointed priest is to take some of the bull's blood and bring it into the tent of meeting.

**6** The priest is to dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle some of the blood seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil of the sanctuary.[^4-5]

**7** The priest is to put some of the blood on the horns[^4-6] of the altar of fragrant incense that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting. He is to pour out all the bull's blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**8** He is to remove all the fat from the bull of the sin offering: the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**9** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys,

**10** just as these are removed from the ox of the fellowship offering. Then the priest is to burn them on the altar of burnt offering.

**11** But the bull's skin and all its flesh, with its head and its legs, its internal organs and its dung,

**12** the whole bull, he is to take outside the camp to a ritually clean[^4-7] place, where the ashes are poured out. There he is to burn it up on a wood fire. It is to be burned up where the ashes are poured out.

**13** "If the whole community of Israel goes astray unintentionally, and the matter escapes the notice of the assembly, and they do any one of the things that the LORD has commanded must not be done, and they realize their guilt,[^4-8]

**14** when the sin they have committed becomes known, the assembly is to offer a young bull as a sin offering. They are to bring it in front of the tent of meeting.

**15** The elders of the community are to lay their hands on the bull's head before the LORD, and the bull is to be slaughtered before the LORD.

**16** Then the anointed priest is to bring some of the bull's blood into the tent of meeting.

**17** The priest is to dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle it seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil.

**18** He is to put some of the blood on the horns of the altar that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting, and he is to pour out all the blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**19** He is to remove all its fat from it and burn it on the altar.

**20** He is to do with this bull just as he did with the bull of the sin offering. He is to do the same with it. So the priest will make atonement for them, and they will be forgiven.

**21** He is to take the bull outside the camp and burn it up, just as he burned up the first bull. It is the sin offering for the assembly.

**22** "When a leader[^4-9] sins, and unintentionally does any one of the things that the LORD his God has commanded must not be done, and he realizes his guilt,

**23** or the sin he has committed is made known to him, he is to bring as his offering a male goat without defect.

**24** He is to lay his hand on the goat's head and slaughter it in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered before the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**25** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out its blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering.

**26** He is to burn all its fat on the altar, like the fat of the fellowship offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for his sin, and he will be forgiven.

**27** "If one of the ordinary people[^4-10] sins unintentionally by doing any one of the things that the LORD has commanded must not be done, and he realizes his guilt,

**28** or the sin he has committed is made known to him, he is to bring as his offering a female goat without defect, for the sin he has committed.

**29** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering.

**30** The priest is to take some of its blood with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out all its blood at the base of the altar.

**31** He is to remove all its fat, just as the fat is removed from the fellowship offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar as a pleasing aroma to the LORD. So the priest will make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.

**32** "If he brings a lamb as his offering for a sin offering, he is to bring a female without defect.

**33** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter it as a sin offering in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered.

**34** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out all its blood at the base of the altar.

**35** He is to remove all its fat, just as the fat of the lamb is removed from the fellowship offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar on top of the offerings by fire to the LORD.[^4-11] So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven.

[^4-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *bishgagah*, "by mistake, inadvertently": through error or ignorance, not deliberately (compare Numbers 15:27–31). Also in verses 13, 22 and 27.
[^4-3]: That is, the high priest (8:12; 21:10). Also in verses 5 and 16.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *chatta't*, the same word as "sin" earlier in the verse. Many scholars translate it "purification offering", because its blood purified the sanctuary and the altar, and it was also required where no wrong had been done (12:6; 14:19; 15:15).
[^4-5]: Literally "the veil of the holy place". Hebrew *paroket*: the curtain that closed off the inner room of the tent, where the ark stood (Exodus 26:31–33).
[^4-6]: The projections at the four corners of the altar (Exodus 27:2; 30:2–3).
[^4-7]: Hebrew *tahor*: ritually clean, fit for contact with what is holy. Its opposite is *tamei*, "unclean".
[^4-8]: Or "and they become guilty". Hebrew *ashem*, which can mean both being guilty and feeling or recognizing guilt. Also in verses 22 and 27.
[^4-9]: Hebrew *nasi*, "one lifted up": a chief or leader of a tribe or clan.
[^4-10]: Literally "of the people of the land".
[^4-11]: Or "along with the offerings by fire to the LORD".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** "If anyone sins in this way: he hears a public call for witnesses,[^5-1] and he is a witness, because he has seen or knows about the matter, but he does not speak up, he will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.[^5-2]

**2** Or if anyone touches anything unclean,[^5-3] whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, or the carcass of unclean livestock, or the carcass of an unclean swarming creature,[^5-4] and it escapes his notice that he has become unclean, and then he realizes his guilt;[^5-5]

**3** or if he touches human uncleanness, any kind of uncleanness by which a person becomes unclean, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt;

**4** or if anyone thoughtlessly swears an oath with his lips to do harm or to do good, in any matter about which people swear thoughtlessly, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt in any of these matters;

**5** when he realizes his guilt in any one of these matters, he must confess the sin he has committed.

**6** He must bring to the LORD[^5-6] his penalty[^5-7] for the sin he has committed: a female from the flock, a lamb or a female goat, as a sin offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for his sin.

**7** "If he cannot afford[^5-8] a sheep or a goat, he is to bring to the LORD, as the penalty for the sin he has committed, two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.

**8** He is to bring them to the priest, who is to offer first the one for the sin offering. He is to wring its head at the back of its neck, without severing it.

**9** He is to sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood is to be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

**10** The second he is to offer as a burnt offering, according to the regulation.[^5-9] So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven.

**11** "But if he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he is to bring as his offering for the sin he has committed a tenth of an ephah[^5-10] of fine flour as a sin offering. He must not put oil on it or put frankincense on it, because it is a sin offering.

**12** He is to bring it to the priest, and the priest is to take a full handful of it as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar on top of the offerings by fire to the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**13** So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed in any one of these matters, and he will be forgiven. The rest is to belong to the priest, as with the grain offering."[^5-11]

**14** The LORD said to Moses,

**15** "When anyone acts unfaithfully[^5-12] and sins unintentionally in regard to any of the LORD's holy things,[^5-13] he is to bring to the LORD as his guilt offering[^5-14] a ram without defect from the flock, valued in silver shekels[^5-15] by the sanctuary shekel,[^5-16] as a guilt offering.

**16** He must make good what he has failed to give from the holy things,[^5-17] add a fifth of its value to it, and give it to the priest. The priest will make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he will be forgiven.

**17** "If anyone sins by doing any one of the things that the LORD has commanded must not be done, even though he does not know it, he is guilty[^5-18] and will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.

**18** He is to bring to the priest a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for the error he committed unintentionally without knowing it, and he will be forgiven.

**19** It is a guilt offering. He was certainly guilty before the LORD."[^5-19]

**20** The LORD said to Moses,[^5-20]

**21** "When anyone sins and acts unfaithfully against the LORD by deceiving his neighbor about something entrusted to him or left in his care,[^5-21] or about something stolen, or if he has cheated his neighbor,

**22** or has found something that was lost and lied about it, and has sworn falsely about any one of the things that people do and so sin,

**23** then, when he has sinned and realizes his guilt, he must give back what he stole, or what he got by cheating, or what was entrusted to him, or the lost thing he found,

**24** or anything at all about which he swore falsely. He must repay it in full and add a fifth to it. He must give it to its owner on the day he presents his guilt offering.[^5-22]

**25** He is to bring to the priest, as his guilt offering to the LORD, a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering.

**26** So the priest will make atonement for him before the LORD, and he will be forgiven for any one of all the things he may have done that made him guilty."

[^5-1]: Literally "he hears the voice of a curse": a public demand, backed by a curse, that anyone who knows something about a matter must come forward and testify.
[^5-2]: Literally "he will bear his iniquity": he is answerable for it and liable to its penalty. Also in verse 17.
[^5-3]: Hebrew *tamei*, "unclean": in a state of ritual impurity that bars a person or thing from contact with what is holy. It is not a matter of hygiene, nor in itself of moral guilt.
[^5-4]: Hebrew *sherets*: small creatures that swarm or creep, such as insects, rodents and lizards (11:29–31, 41–42).
[^5-5]: Or "and he becomes guilty". Hebrew *ashem*, which can mean both being guilty and feeling or recognizing guilt. Also in verses 3, 4, 5 and 23.
[^5-6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-7]: Hebrew *asham*, the word used for the guilt offering in verses 15–26. Here it means the penalty for his guilt, which is a sin offering. Also in verse 7.
[^5-8]: Literally "if his hand cannot reach what is enough for". Also in verse 11.
[^5-9]: That is, as in 1:14–17.
[^5-10]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel), so a tenth of an ephah was about 2 liters (2 quarts).
[^5-11]: Literally "and it shall be for the priest like the grain offering" (2:3, 10).
[^5-12]: Hebrew *ma'al*: a breach of faith, especially by taking or misusing what belongs to God.
[^5-13]: That is, the things dedicated to the LORD, such as offerings, tithes and the priests' portions.
[^5-14]: Hebrew *asham*, "guilt" or "compensation". Many scholars translate it "reparation offering", because it was brought together with repayment for what had been wrongly taken or withheld (verse 16; verses 21–24).
[^5-15]: A shekel was a weight of silver, about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams). The Hebrew may mean "at least two shekels".
[^5-16]: Literally "by the shekel of the sanctuary": the standard weight used at the sanctuary, twenty gerahs (Exodus 30:13; Leviticus 27:25).
[^5-17]: Literally "And what he sinned from the holy thing he shall repay".
[^5-18]: Or "and he feels guilty".
[^5-19]: Literally "He was guilty, guilty to the LORD."
[^5-20]: Verses 20–26 are 6:1–7 in most English Bibles.
[^5-21]: Hebrew *tesumet yad*, "a putting of the hand": perhaps a pledge, a deposit or a business partnership.
[^5-22]: Literally "on the day of his guilt": the day he admits his guilt and brings his guilt offering.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^6-1] said to Moses,[^6-2]

**2** "Command Aaron and his sons: These are the instructions[^6-3] for the burnt offering. The burnt offering is to stay on its hearth on the altar all night until morning, and the fire of the altar is to be kept burning on it.

**3** The priest is to put on his linen garment,[^6-4] with linen undergarments[^6-5] next to his body. He is to remove the ashes of the burnt offering that the fire has consumed on the altar, and put them beside the altar.

**4** Then he is to take off his garments and put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp to a ritually clean place.

**5** The fire on the altar is to be kept burning on it. It must not go out. Every morning the priest is to burn wood on it, arrange the burnt offering on it, and burn the fat of the fellowship offerings on it.

**6** A fire is to be kept burning on the altar continually. It must not go out.

**7** "These are the instructions for the grain offering. Aaron's sons are to present it before the LORD, in front of the altar.

**8** One of them is to take from it a handful of the fine flour of the grain offering and of its oil, with all the frankincense that is on the grain offering, and burn it on the altar as a pleasing aroma, its memorial portion for the LORD.

**9** Aaron and his sons are to eat what is left of it. It is to be eaten without yeast in a holy place. They are to eat it in the courtyard of the tent of meeting.

**10** It must not be baked with yeast. I have given it to them as their share of my offerings by fire. It is most holy, like the sin offering and the guilt offering.

**11** Any male among Aaron's descendants may eat it. It is their permanent share,[^6-6] for all your generations, from the offerings by fire to the LORD. Whatever touches them will become holy."[^6-7]

**12** The LORD said to Moses,

**10** You are to consecrate the fiftieth year and proclaim freedom[^25-6] throughout the land to all its inhabitants. It is to be a jubilee[^25-7] for you. Each of you is to return to his own property, and each of you is to return to his family.

**11** The fiftieth year is to be a jubilee for you. Do not sow, and do not reap what grows by itself, and do not gather the grapes of the untended vines.

**12** For it is a jubilee. It is to be holy to you. You are to eat what the fields produce.[^25-8]

**13** In this year of jubilee each of you is to return to his property.

**14** "If you sell anything to your neighbor or buy anything from your neighbor, do not cheat one another.

**15** You are to buy from your neighbor on the basis of the number of years since the jubilee, and he is to sell to you on the basis of the number of years of harvests that remain.

**16** If there are many years, you are to increase the price, and if there are few years, you are to reduce the price, because what he is selling you is a number of harvests.

**17** Do not cheat one another, but fear your God, for I am the LORD your God.

**18** "Carry out my decrees, and keep my rulings and do them, and you will live securely in the land.

**19** The land will yield its fruit, and you will eat your fill and live there securely.

**20** If you ask, 'What will we eat in the seventh year, if we may not sow or gather in our produce?'

**21** I will send my blessing on you in the sixth year, so that the land will produce enough for three years.

**22** When you sow in the eighth year, you will still be eating from the old produce. Until the ninth year, until its produce comes in, you will eat the old.

**23** "The land must not be sold permanently, because the land is mine. You are foreigners and temporary residents with me.

**24** Throughout the land you hold as property, you must allow the land to be redeemed.[^25-9]

**25** "If your brother[^25-10] becomes poor and sells some of his property, his nearest redeemer[^25-11] is to come and redeem what his brother has sold.

**26** If a man has no one to redeem it, but he himself prospers and finds enough to redeem it,

**27** he is to calculate the years since he sold it and refund the balance to the man he sold it to. Then he may return to his property.

**28** But if he does not find enough to pay him back, what he sold will remain in the hands of the buyer until the year of jubilee. In the jubilee it is to be released, and he may return to his property.

**29** "If a man sells a house to live in, in a walled city, he has the right to redeem it until a full year after its sale. His right of redemption lasts a year.[^25-12]

**30** If it is not redeemed before a full year has passed, the house in the walled city[^25-13] will belong permanently to the buyer and his descendants. It is not to be released in the jubilee.

**31** But houses in villages that have no walls around them are to be counted as open country. They may be redeemed, and they are to be released in the jubilee.

**32** As for the cities of the Levites, the houses in the cities they hold as property, the Levites always have the right to redeem them.

**33** Whatever is redeemed from the Levites, a house sold in a city that they hold as property, is to be released in the jubilee,[^25-14] because the houses in the cities of the Levites are their property among the Israelites.

**34** But the pastureland around their cities must not be sold, for it is their permanent property.

**35** "If your brother becomes poor and cannot support himself among you,[^25-15] you are to help him as you would a foreigner or a temporary resident,[^25-16] so that he may continue to live among you.

**36** Do not take interest or profit[^25-17] from him, but fear your God, so that your brother may continue to live among you.

**37** Do not lend him your money at interest or give him your food for profit.

**38** I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt to give you the land of Canaan and to be your God.

**39** "If your brother becomes poor among you and sells himself to you, do not make him work as a slave.

**40** He is to be with you like a hired worker or a temporary resident. He is to work for you until the year of jubilee.

**41** Then he and his children with him are to go free from you, and he is to return to his family and to the property of his ancestors.

**42** For they are my servants,[^25-18] whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. They must not be sold as slaves are sold.

**43** Do not rule over him harshly, but fear your God.

**44** "As for the male and female slaves you may have, you may buy male and female slaves from the nations around you.

**45** You may also buy them from the children of the temporary residents who live among you, and from their families who are with you, who were born in your land. They may become your property.

**46** You may leave them to your children after you to inherit as property. You may make them your slaves for life. But as for your brothers the Israelites, you must not rule over one another harshly.

**47** "If a foreigner or temporary resident among you becomes rich, and your brother becomes poor beside him and sells himself to the foreigner or temporary resident among you, or to a member of the foreigner's family,

**48** he has the right to be redeemed after he has sold himself. One of his brothers may redeem him,

**49** or his uncle or his uncle's son may redeem him, or any close relative from his family may redeem him. Or if he prospers, he may redeem himself.

**50** He and his buyer are to count the time from the year he sold himself to him until the year of jubilee. The price of his sale is to be based on the number of years, as if he had been with him for the time of a hired worker.

**51** If many years still remain, he must pay for his redemption a share of the price he was bought for, in proportion to them.

**52** If only a few years remain until the year of jubilee, he is to calculate it, and he must pay for his redemption in proportion to his years.

**53** He is to be with him as a worker hired from year to year. The buyer must not rule over him harshly in your sight.

**54** If he is not redeemed in any of these ways, he and his children with him are to go free in the year of jubilee.

**55** For the Israelites are my servants. They are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God.

[^25-8]: That is, what grows by itself (verse 11). Literally "From the field you shall eat its produce."

[^25-10]: Here a fellow Israelite. Also in verses 35, 36, 39, 46 and 47.

**1** "Do not make idols[^26-1] for yourselves, and do not set up a carved image or a standing stone[^26-2] for yourselves. Do not place a carved stone[^26-3] in your land to bow down to it, for I am the LORD[^26-4] your God.

**2** Keep my Sabbaths, and revere my sanctuary. I am the LORD.

**3** "If you follow my decrees and keep my commandments and do them,

**4** I will give you your rains at the right time, the land will yield its crops, and the trees of the field will yield their fruit.

**5** Your threshing will last until the grape harvest, and the grape harvest will last until sowing time. You will eat your fill of food and live securely in your land.

**6** I will give peace in the land, and you will lie down with no one to make you afraid. I will rid the land of dangerous animals,[^26-5] and the sword will not pass through your land.

**7** You will chase your enemies, and they will fall before you by the sword.

**8** Five of you will chase a hundred, and a hundred of you will chase ten thousand, and your enemies will fall before you by the sword.

**9** I will turn toward you and make you fruitful and multiply you, and I will uphold my covenant with you.

**10** You will still be eating last year's harvest[^26-6] when you have to clear out the old to make room for the new.

**11** I will put my dwelling place[^26-7] among you, and I will not reject you.[^26-8]

**12** I will walk among you and be your God, and you will be my people.

**13** I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt so that you would no longer be their slaves. I broke the bars of your yoke and made you walk upright.

**14** "But if you do not listen to me and do not carry out all these commandments,

**15** if you reject my decrees and loathe my rulings, so that you do not carry out all my commandments and you break my covenant,

**16** then I in turn will do this to you: I will bring sudden terror on you, wasting disease and fever that make the eyes fail and the life ebb away.[^26-9] You will sow your seed in vain, because your enemies will eat it.

**17** I will set my face against you, and you will be defeated by your enemies. Those who hate you will rule over you, and you will flee when no one is pursuing you.

**18** "And if after all this you still do not listen to me, I will go on to discipline you seven times over for your sins.

**19** I will break your proud strength. I will make your sky like iron and your land like bronze.

**20** Your strength will be spent in vain, because your land will not yield its crops, and the trees of the land will not yield their fruit.

**21** "If you continue to walk in opposition to me[^26-10] and refuse to listen to me, I will go on to strike you seven times over, as your sins deserve.

**22** I will send wild animals against you, and they will rob you of your children, destroy your livestock and make you few in number, so that your roads will be deserted.

**23** "If in spite of these things you do not accept my discipline, but continue to walk in opposition to me,

**24** then I too will walk in opposition to you, and I myself will strike you seven times over for your sins.

**25** I will bring a sword on you to take vengeance for the covenant.[^26-11] When you gather in your cities, I will send a plague among you, and you will be handed over to the enemy.

**26** When I cut off your supply of bread,[^26-12] ten women will bake your bread in a single oven, and they will dole out your bread by weight. You will eat, but you will not be satisfied.

**27** "If in spite of this you still do not listen to me, but continue to walk in opposition to me,

**28** then I will walk in opposition to you in fury, and I myself will discipline you seven times over for your sins.

**29** You will eat the flesh of your sons, and you will eat the flesh of your daughters.

**30** I will destroy your high places,[^26-13] cut down your incense altars[^26-14] and pile your corpses on the corpses of your idols,[^26-15] and I will reject you.

**31** I will turn your cities into ruins and make your sanctuaries desolate, and I will not take pleasure in your pleasing aromas.[^26-16]

**32** I myself will make the land desolate, so that your enemies who come to live in it will be appalled at it.

**33** I will scatter you among the nations, and I will draw the sword and pursue you. Your land will become desolate, and your cities will become ruins.

**34** Then the land will enjoy its Sabbaths[^26-17] all the time that it lies desolate and you are in the land of your enemies. Then the land will rest and enjoy its Sabbaths.

**35** All the time that it lies desolate it will have rest, the rest it did not have during your Sabbaths when you lived in it.

**36** "As for those of you who are left, I will bring fear into their hearts in the lands of their enemies. The sound of a windblown leaf will put them to flight. They will flee as one flees from the sword, and they will fall when no one is pursuing them.

**37** They will stumble over one another as if fleeing from the sword, though no one is pursuing. You will not be able to stand before your enemies.

**38** You will perish among the nations, and the land of your enemies will devour you.

**39** Those of you who are left will waste away in the lands of your enemies because of their wrongdoing. And because of the wrongdoing of their ancestors also, they will waste away like them.[^26-18]

**40** "But if they confess[^26-19] their wrongdoing and the wrongdoing of their ancestors, the unfaithfulness they committed against me, and also how they walked in opposition to me,

**41** so that I in turn walked in opposition to them and brought them into the land of their enemies, if then their uncircumcised hearts[^26-20] are humbled and they pay for their wrongdoing,

**42** then I will remember my covenant with Jacob. I will remember also my covenant with Isaac and also my covenant with Abraham, and I will remember the land.

**43** For the land will be left behind by them, and it will enjoy its Sabbaths while it lies desolate without them, and they will pay for their wrongdoing, because they rejected my rulings and loathed my decrees.

**44** Yet in spite of this, when they are in the land of their enemies, I will not reject them or loathe them so as to destroy them completely and break my covenant with them, for I am the LORD their God.

**45** For their sake I will remember the covenant with their ancestors,[^26-21] whom I brought out of the land of Egypt in the sight of the nations, to be their God. I am the LORD."

**46** These are the decrees, the rulings and the instructions that the LORD established between himself and the Israelites on Mount Sinai through Moses.[^26-22]

## Chapter 27

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^27-1] said to Moses,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When anyone makes a special vow to give the value of a person to the LORD,[^27-2]

**3** the value of a male from twenty to sixty years old is to be fifty shekels[^27-3] of silver, by the sanctuary shekel.

**4** If it is a female, the value is to be thirty shekels.

**5** If the person is from five to twenty years old, the value of a male is to be twenty shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**6** If the person is from a month to five years old, the value of a male is to be five shekels of silver, and the value of a female three shekels of silver.

**7** If the person is sixty years old or more, the value of a male is to be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**8** But if the one making the vow is too poor to pay the value,[^27-4] the person is to be presented to the priest, and the priest is to set a value for him. The priest is to set the value according to what the one making the vow can afford.

**9** "If the vow concerns an animal of a kind that may be presented as an offering to the LORD, every such animal that is given to the LORD becomes holy.

**10** He must not exchange it or substitute another for it, a good one for a bad one or a bad one for a good one. If he does substitute one animal for another, both it and its substitute will become holy.

**11** If the vow concerns any unclean animal, of a kind that may not be presented as an offering to the LORD, the animal is to be presented to the priest.

**12** The priest is to set its value, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets, so it is to be.

**13** If he wishes to redeem it, he must add a fifth to its value.

**14** "When anyone consecrates his house as holy to the LORD, the priest is to set its value, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets, so it will stand.

**15** If the one who consecrated it wishes to redeem his house, he must add a fifth to its value in silver, and it will be his again.

**16** "If anyone consecrates to the LORD part of the land that he holds as family property, its value is to be set according to the seed needed to sow it: fifty shekels of silver for a homer[^27-5] of barley seed.

**17** If he consecrates his field from the year of jubilee onward, it is to stand at the full value.

**18** But if he consecrates his field after the jubilee, the priest is to calculate the price for him according to the years that remain until the year of jubilee, and it is to be deducted from the value.

**19** If the one who consecrated the field wishes to redeem it, he must add a fifth to its value in silver, and it will remain his.

**20** But if he does not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to someone else, it can no longer be redeemed.

**21** When the field is released in the jubilee, it will be holy to the LORD, like a field that has been devoted.[^27-6] It will become the priest's property.

**22** If anyone consecrates to the LORD a field that he has bought, which is not part of the land he holds as family property,

**23** the priest is to calculate for him the amount of its value up to the year of jubilee, and he must pay its value on that day as a holy gift to the LORD.

**24** In the year of jubilee the field is to return to the person from whom he bought it, the one to whom the land belongs as family property.

**25** Every value is to be set by the sanctuary shekel, twenty gerahs[^27-7] to the shekel.

**26** "But no one may consecrate the firstborn of an animal, since as a firstborn it already belongs to the LORD. Whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's.

**27** If it is an unclean animal, he may buy it back at its value, adding a fifth to it. If it is not redeemed, it is to be sold at its value.

**28** "But nothing that anyone devotes[^27-8] to the LORD from all that he has, whether a person or an animal or family land, may be sold or redeemed. Everything devoted is most holy to the LORD.

**29** No person who has been devoted to destruction[^27-9] may be ransomed. He must be put to death.

**30** "A tenth[^27-10] of everything from the land, whether grain from the soil or fruit from the trees, belongs to the LORD. It is holy to the LORD.

**31** If anyone wishes to redeem any of his tenth, he must add a fifth to it.

**32** Every tenth of the herd and the flock, every tenth animal that passes under the shepherd's rod,[^27-11] is to be holy to the LORD.

**33** He must not look to see whether it is good or bad, and he must not substitute another for it. If he does substitute one, both it and its substitute will become holy. It cannot be redeemed."

**34** These are the commandments that the LORD gave Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai.
