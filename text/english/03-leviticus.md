## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Yahweh[^1-1] called to Moses and spoke to him[^1-2] from the tent of meeting.[^1-3] He said,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When any of you brings an offering[^1-4] to Yahweh, you are to bring your offering from the livestock, from the herd or from the flock.[^1-5]

**3** If his offering is a burnt offering[^1-6] from the herd, he is to offer a male without defect.[^1-7] He is to bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, so that he may be accepted[^1-8] before Yahweh.

**4** He is to lay his hand[^1-9] on the head of the burnt offering, and it will be accepted on his behalf to make atonement[^1-10] for him.

**5** He is to slaughter the young bull[^1-11] before Yahweh. Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to present the blood and splash the blood against all sides of the altar that is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**6** He is to skin the burnt offering and cut it into its pieces.

**7** The sons of Aaron the priest are to put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

**8** Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to arrange the pieces, with the head and the fat,[^1-12] on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**9** He is to wash its internal organs and its legs with water, and the priest is to burn[^1-13] all of it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire,[^1-14] a pleasing aroma[^1-15] to Yahweh.

**10** "If his offering for a burnt offering is from the flock, from the sheep or from the goats, he is to offer a male without defect.

**11** He is to slaughter it on the north side of the altar before Yahweh, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**12** He is to cut it into its pieces, with its head and its fat, and the priest is to arrange them on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**13** He is to wash the internal organs and the legs with water. Then the priest is to offer all of it and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to Yahweh.

**14** "If his offering to Yahweh is a burnt offering of birds, he is to bring his offering from the turtledoves or from the young pigeons.[^1-16]

**15** The priest is to bring it to the altar, wring off its head[^1-17] and burn it on the altar. Its blood is to be drained out against the side of the altar.

**16** He is to remove its crop with its contents[^1-18] and throw it beside the altar, on the east side, in the place for the ashes.[^1-19]

**17** He is to tear it open by its wings without dividing it in two.[^1-20] Then the priest is to burn it on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to Yahweh.

[^1-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-2]: Literally "And he called to Moses, and Yahweh spoke to him". The first word, *vayyiqra*, "and he called", is the Hebrew name of the book.
[^1-3]: The tent where Yahweh met with Moses (Exodus 25:22; 29:42–43), set up as described in Exodus 40. It is also called the tabernacle.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *qorban*, "something brought near", from the verb used throughout these chapters for bringing or offering a sacrifice.
[^1-5]: The herd is cattle; the flock is sheep and goats (verse 10).
[^1-6]: Hebrew *olah*, from the verb "go up": the whole animal went up in smoke on the altar, apart from the skin (7:8).
[^1-7]: Hebrew *tamim*, "whole, complete": without any blemish or defect (22:17–25).
[^1-8]: Or "so that it may be accepted".
[^1-9]: Literally "press his hand" or "lean his hand".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *kipper*, traditionally "make atonement". The word probably means "wipe clean, purge" or "pay a ransom": to remove the effects of sin or impurity.
[^1-11]: Literally "the son of the herd".
[^1-12]: Hebrew *peder*, found only in Leviticus (also verse 12 and 8:20): probably the hard fat around the internal organs.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *hiqtir*, "make go up in smoke", the word used for burning offerings on the altar. A different word (*saraf*) is used for burning things up outside the camp (4:12).
[^1-14]: Hebrew *ishsheh*, traditionally "an offering made by fire", as if from *esh*, "fire". Some think it means "gift" or "food offering" (compare 3:11).
[^1-15]: Literally "a smell of soothing": an offering that pleases Yahweh. Traditionally "a sweet savor".
[^1-16]: Literally "sons of the dove": young pigeons or doves.
[^1-17]: Hebrew *malaq*, perhaps "pinch off" with the fingernail.
[^1-18]: Or "with its feathers". The meaning of the Hebrew word *notsah* here is uncertain.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *deshen*, the ashes soaked with fat from the sacrifices.
[^1-20]: Literally "he shall not divide (it)".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** "When anyone[^2-1] brings a grain offering[^2-2] to Yahweh,[^2-3] his offering is to be of fine flour.[^2-4] He is to pour oil on it and put frankincense[^2-5] on it.

**2** He is to bring it to Aaron's sons, the priests, and the priest is to take from it a full handful of its fine flour and its oil, together with all its frankincense. Then the priest is to burn it on the altar as the memorial portion[^2-6] of the offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to Yahweh.

**3** The rest of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is most holy,[^2-7] a portion of the offerings by fire to Yahweh.

**4** "When you bring a grain offering baked in an oven, it is to be of fine flour: cakes[^2-8] made without yeast and mixed with oil, or wafers made without yeast and spread with oil.

**5** "If your offering is a grain offering made on a griddle, it is to be of fine flour mixed with oil, made without yeast.

**6** Break it into pieces and pour oil on it. It is a grain offering.

**7** "If your offering is a grain offering made in a pan,[^2-9] it is to be made of fine flour with oil.

**8** You are to bring the grain offering made from these things to Yahweh. It is to be presented to the priest, and he is to bring it to the altar.[^2-10]

**9** The priest is to take the memorial portion from the grain offering and burn it on the altar, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to Yahweh.

**10** The rest of the grain offering belongs to Aaron and his sons. It is most holy, a portion of the offerings by fire to Yahweh.

**11** No grain offering that you bring to Yahweh is to be made with yeast, for you must not burn any yeast[^2-11] or any honey[^2-12] as an offering by fire to Yahweh.

**12** You may bring them to Yahweh as an offering of the first produce,[^2-13] but they must not be offered up on the altar as a pleasing aroma.[^2-14]

**13** You must season every grain offering of yours with salt. Do not leave the salt of the covenant of your God out of your grain offering. You are to offer salt with all your offerings.

**14** "If you bring a grain offering of firstfruits to Yahweh, you are to bring fresh ears of grain roasted in the fire, crushed kernels of new grain,[^2-15] as the grain offering of your firstfruits.

**15** Put oil on it and lay frankincense on it. It is a grain offering.

**16** The priest is to burn its memorial portion, some of its crushed grain and some of its oil, together with all its frankincense, as an offering by fire to Yahweh.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "a person, a living being".
[^2-2]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute". In Leviticus it is an offering of flour or grain. Traditionally "meat offering", in the old sense of "food".
[^2-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *solet*, finely ground flour, perhaps semolina.
[^2-5]: An aromatic resin from trees of southern Arabia and East Africa, burned as incense.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *azkarah*, "reminder, memorial": the part burned on the altar, standing for the whole offering. Or "token portion". Also in verses 9 and 16.
[^2-7]: Literally "a holy of holies": the highest degree of holiness. Only the priests could eat it, and only in a holy place (6:9–11; English 6:16–18).
[^2-8]: Hebrew *challot*, probably round cakes or loaves, perhaps pierced.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *marcheshet*, probably a deep pan for frying or boiling (compare 7:9).
[^2-10]: Literally "You shall bring the grain offering that is made from these to Yahweh, and he shall present it to the priest, and he shall bring it near to the altar."
[^2-11]: Hebrew *se'or*, sourdough: a piece of fermented dough used to make bread rise.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *devash*: bees' honey, or a sweet syrup made from fruit such as dates or grapes.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *reshit*, "the first, the best": the first of the harvest given to Yahweh (23:17).
[^2-14]: Literally "but they must not go up to the altar as a pleasing aroma".
[^2-15]: Hebrew *geres karmel*: freshly harvested grain, crushed or coarsely ground. The exact meaning of *karmel* is uncertain.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** "If his offering is a fellowship offering,[^3-1] and he offers an animal from the herd, whether male or female, he is to offer one without defect before Yahweh.[^3-2]

**2** He is to lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it at the entrance of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash the blood against all sides of the altar.

**3** From the fellowship offering he is to present an offering by fire to Yahweh: the fat[^3-3] that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**4** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins,[^3-4] and the long lobe of the liver,[^3-5] which he is to remove with the kidneys.[^3-6]

**5** Aaron's sons are to burn it on the altar on top of the burnt offering, which is on the wood on the fire, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to Yahweh.

**6** "If his offering for a fellowship offering to Yahweh is from the flock, whether male or female, he is to offer one without defect.

**7** If he offers a lamb as his offering, he is to present it before Yahweh.

**8** He is to lay his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**9** From the fellowship offering he is to present its fat as an offering by fire to Yahweh: the whole fat tail,[^3-7] which he is to remove close to the backbone,[^3-8] the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**10** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**11** The priest is to burn it on the altar as food,[^3-9] an offering by fire to Yahweh.

**12** "If his offering is a goat, he is to present it before Yahweh.

**13** He is to lay his hand on its head and slaughter it in front of the tent of meeting. Then Aaron's sons are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**14** From it he is to present his offering as an offering by fire to Yahweh: the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**15** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys.

**16** The priest is to burn them on the altar as food, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma. All the fat belongs to Yahweh.

**17** This is a permanent rule for all your generations, wherever you live: you must not eat any fat or any blood."

[^3-1]: Hebrew *zevach shelamim*, "a sacrifice of *shelamim*", traditionally "peace offering". *Shelamim* is related to *shalom*, "peace, well-being, wholeness", but its exact sense is uncertain. Most of the meat of this sacrifice was eaten by the worshiper and his household (7:11–21).
[^3-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-3]: Hebrew *chelev*: the layers of fat around the internal organs, not the fat mixed into the meat.
[^3-4]: Or "on the sinews". Hebrew *kesalim*, the muscles of the lower back.
[^3-5]: Literally "the appendage on the liver", probably the lobe that projects from it.
[^3-6]: Or "which he is to remove along with the kidneys". Literally "on the kidneys he shall remove it".
[^3-7]: Hebrew *alyah*: the broad tail of the fat-tailed sheep, a breed common in the Middle East.
[^3-8]: Hebrew *atseh*, found only here: probably the base of the spine.
[^3-9]: Literally "bread", a common word for food in general. Also in verse 16.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Yahweh[^4-1] said to Moses,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When anyone sins unintentionally[^4-2] by doing any one of the things that Yahweh has commanded must not be done,

**3** if it is the anointed priest[^4-3] who sins, bringing guilt on the people, then for the sin he has committed he is to offer to Yahweh a young bull without defect as a sin offering.[^4-4]

**4** He is to bring the bull to the entrance of the tent of meeting before Yahweh, lay his hand on the bull's head and slaughter the bull before Yahweh.

**5** Then the anointed priest is to take some of the bull's blood and bring it into the tent of meeting.

**6** The priest is to dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle some of the blood seven times before Yahweh, in front of the veil of the sanctuary.[^4-5]

**7** The priest is to put some of the blood on the horns[^4-6] of the altar of fragrant incense that stands before Yahweh in the tent of meeting. He is to pour out all the bull's blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**8** He is to remove all the fat from the bull of the sin offering: the fat that covers the internal organs and all the fat that is on the internal organs,

**9** the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, by the loins, and the long lobe of the liver, which he is to remove with the kidneys,

**10** just as these are removed from the ox of the fellowship offering. Then the priest is to burn them on the altar of burnt offering.

**11** But the bull's skin and all its flesh, with its head and its legs, its internal organs and its dung,

**12** the whole bull, he is to take outside the camp to a ritually clean[^4-7] place, where the ashes are poured out. There he is to burn it up on a wood fire. It is to be burned up where the ashes are poured out.

**13** "If the whole community of Israel goes astray unintentionally, and the matter escapes the notice of the assembly, and they do any one of the things that Yahweh has commanded must not be done, and they realize their guilt,[^4-8]

**14** when the sin they have committed becomes known, the assembly is to offer a young bull as a sin offering. They are to bring it in front of the tent of meeting.

**15** The elders of the community are to lay their hands on the bull's head before Yahweh, and the bull is to be slaughtered before Yahweh.

**16** Then the anointed priest is to bring some of the bull's blood into the tent of meeting.

**17** The priest is to dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle it seven times before Yahweh, in front of the veil.

**18** He is to put some of the blood on the horns of the altar that stands before Yahweh in the tent of meeting, and he is to pour out all the blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering, which is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**19** He is to remove all its fat from it and burn it on the altar.

**20** He is to do with this bull just as he did with the bull of the sin offering. He is to do the same with it. So the priest will make atonement for them, and they will be forgiven.

**21** He is to take the bull outside the camp and burn it up, just as he burned up the first bull. It is the sin offering for the assembly.

**22** "When a leader[^4-9] sins, and unintentionally does any one of the things that Yahweh his God has commanded must not be done, and he realizes his guilt,

**23** or the sin he has committed is made known to him, he is to bring as his offering a male goat without defect.

**24** He is to lay his hand on the goat's head and slaughter it in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered before Yahweh. It is a sin offering.

**25** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out its blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering.

**26** He is to burn all its fat on the altar, like the fat of the fellowship offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for his sin, and he will be forgiven.

**27** "If one of the ordinary people[^4-10] sins unintentionally by doing any one of the things that Yahweh has commanded must not be done, and he realizes his guilt,

**28** or the sin he has committed is made known to him, he is to bring as his offering a female goat without defect, for the sin he has committed.

**29** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering.

**30** The priest is to take some of its blood with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out all its blood at the base of the altar.

**31** He is to remove all its fat, just as the fat is removed from the fellowship offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar as a pleasing aroma to Yahweh. So the priest will make atonement for him, and he will be forgiven.

**32** "If he brings a lamb as his offering for a sin offering, he is to bring a female without defect.

**33** He is to lay his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter it as a sin offering in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered.

**34** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and he is to pour out all its blood at the base of the altar.

**35** He is to remove all its fat, just as the fat of the lamb is removed from the fellowship offering, and the priest is to burn it on the altar on top of the offerings by fire to Yahweh.[^4-11] So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven.

[^4-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *bishgagah*, "by mistake, inadvertently": through error or ignorance, not deliberately (compare Numbers 15:27–31). Also in verses 13, 22 and 27.
[^4-3]: That is, the high priest (8:12; 21:10). Also in verses 5 and 16.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *chatta't*, the same word as "sin" earlier in the verse. Many scholars translate it "purification offering", because its blood purified the sanctuary and the altar, and it was also required where no wrong had been done (12:6; 14:19; 15:15).
[^4-5]: Literally "the veil of the holy place". Hebrew *paroket*: the curtain that closed off the inner room of the tent, where the ark stood (Exodus 26:31–33).
[^4-6]: The projections at the four corners of the altar (Exodus 27:2; 30:2–3).
[^4-7]: Hebrew *tahor*: ritually clean, fit for contact with what is holy. Its opposite is *tamei*, "unclean".
[^4-8]: Or "and they become guilty". Hebrew *ashem*, which can mean both being guilty and feeling or recognizing guilt. Also in verses 22 and 27.
[^4-9]: Hebrew *nasi*, "one lifted up": a chief or leader of a tribe or clan.
[^4-10]: Literally "of the people of the land".
[^4-11]: Or "along with the offerings by fire to Yahweh".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** "If anyone sins in this way: he hears a public call for witnesses,[^5-1] and he is a witness, because he has seen or knows about the matter, but he does not speak up, he will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.[^5-2]

**2** Or if anyone touches anything unclean,[^5-3] whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, or the carcass of unclean livestock, or the carcass of an unclean swarming creature,[^5-4] and it escapes his notice that he has become unclean, and then he realizes his guilt;[^5-5]

**3** or if he touches human uncleanness, any kind of uncleanness by which a person becomes unclean, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt;

**4** or if anyone thoughtlessly swears an oath with his lips to do harm or to do good, in any matter about which people swear thoughtlessly, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt in any of these matters;

**5** when he realizes his guilt in any one of these matters, he must confess the sin he has committed.

**6** He must bring to Yahweh[^5-6] his penalty[^5-7] for the sin he has committed: a female from the flock, a lamb or a female goat, as a sin offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for his sin.

**7** "If he cannot afford[^5-8] a sheep or a goat, he is to bring to Yahweh, as the penalty for the sin he has committed, two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.

**8** He is to bring them to the priest, who is to offer first the one for the sin offering. He is to wring its head at the back of its neck, without severing it.

**9** He is to sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood is to be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

**10** The second he is to offer as a burnt offering, according to the regulation.[^5-9] So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven.

**11** "But if he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he is to bring as his offering for the sin he has committed a tenth of an ephah[^5-10] of fine flour as a sin offering. He must not put oil on it or put frankincense on it, because it is a sin offering.

**12** He is to bring it to the priest, and the priest is to take a full handful of it as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar on top of the offerings by fire to Yahweh. It is a sin offering.

**13** So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed in any one of these matters, and he will be forgiven. The rest is to belong to the priest, as with the grain offering."[^5-11]

**14** Yahweh said to Moses,

**15** "When anyone acts unfaithfully[^5-12] and sins unintentionally in regard to any of Yahweh's holy things,[^5-13] he is to bring to Yahweh as his guilt offering[^5-14] a ram without defect from the flock, valued in silver shekels[^5-15] by the sanctuary shekel,[^5-16] as a guilt offering.

**16** He must make good what he has failed to give from the holy things,[^5-17] add a fifth of its value to it, and give it to the priest. The priest will make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he will be forgiven.

**17** "If anyone sins by doing any one of the things that Yahweh has commanded must not be done, even though he does not know it, he is guilty[^5-18] and will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.

**18** He is to bring to the priest a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for the error he committed unintentionally without knowing it, and he will be forgiven.

**19** It is a guilt offering. He was certainly guilty before Yahweh."[^5-19]

**20** Yahweh said to Moses,[^5-20]

**21** "When anyone sins and acts unfaithfully against Yahweh by deceiving his neighbor about something entrusted to him or left in his care,[^5-21] or about something stolen, or if he has cheated his neighbor,

**22** or has found something that was lost and lied about it, and has sworn falsely about any one of the things that people do and so sin,

**23** then, when he has sinned and realizes his guilt, he must give back what he stole, or what he got by cheating, or what was entrusted to him, or the lost thing he found,

**24** or anything at all about which he swore falsely. He must repay it in full and add a fifth to it. He must give it to its owner on the day he presents his guilt offering.[^5-22]

**25** He is to bring to the priest, as his guilt offering to Yahweh, a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering.

**26** So the priest will make atonement for him before Yahweh, and he will be forgiven for any one of all the things he may have done that made him guilty."

[^5-1]: Literally "he hears the voice of a curse": a public demand, backed by a curse, that anyone who knows something about a matter must come forward and testify.
[^5-2]: Literally "he will bear his iniquity": he is answerable for it and liable to its penalty. Also in verse 17.
[^5-3]: Hebrew *tamei*, "unclean": in a state of ritual impurity that bars a person or thing from contact with what is holy. It is not a matter of hygiene, nor in itself of moral guilt.
[^5-4]: Hebrew *sherets*: small creatures that swarm or creep, such as insects, rodents and lizards (11:29–31, 41–42).
[^5-5]: Or "and he becomes guilty". Hebrew *ashem*, which can mean both being guilty and feeling or recognizing guilt. Also in verses 3, 4, 5 and 23.
[^5-6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5-7]: Hebrew *asham*, the word used for the guilt offering in verses 15–26. Here it means the penalty for his guilt, which is a sin offering. Also in verse 7.
[^5-8]: Literally "if his hand cannot reach what is enough for". Also in verse 11.
[^5-9]: That is, as in 1:14–17.
[^5-10]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel), so a tenth of an ephah was about 2 liters (2 quarts).
[^5-11]: Literally "and it shall be for the priest like the grain offering" (2:3, 10).
[^5-12]: Hebrew *ma'al*: a breach of faith, especially by taking or misusing what belongs to God.
[^5-13]: That is, the things dedicated to Yahweh, such as offerings, tithes and the priests' portions.
[^5-14]: Hebrew *asham*, "guilt" or "compensation". Many scholars translate it "reparation offering", because it was brought together with repayment for what had been wrongly taken or withheld (verse 16; verses 21–24).
[^5-15]: A shekel was a weight of silver, about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams). The Hebrew may mean "at least two shekels".
[^5-16]: Literally "by the shekel of the sanctuary": the standard weight used at the sanctuary, twenty gerahs (Exodus 30:13; Leviticus 27:25).
[^5-17]: Literally "And what he sinned from the holy thing he shall repay".
[^5-18]: Or "and he feels guilty".
[^5-19]: Literally "He was guilty, guilty to Yahweh."
[^5-20]: Verses 20–26 are 6:1–7 in most English Bibles.
[^5-21]: Hebrew *tesumet yad*, "a putting of the hand": perhaps a pledge, a deposit or a business partnership.
[^5-22]: Literally "on the day of his guilt": the day he admits his guilt and brings his guilt offering.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Yahweh[^6-1] said to Moses,[^6-2]

**2** "Command Aaron and his sons: These are the instructions[^6-3] for the burnt offering. The burnt offering is to stay on its hearth on the altar all night until morning, and the fire of the altar is to be kept burning on it.

**3** The priest is to put on his linen garment,[^6-4] with linen undergarments[^6-5] next to his body. He is to remove the ashes of the burnt offering that the fire has consumed on the altar, and put them beside the altar.

**4** Then he is to take off his garments and put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp to a ritually clean place.

**5** The fire on the altar is to be kept burning on it. It must not go out. Every morning the priest is to burn wood on it, arrange the burnt offering on it, and burn the fat of the fellowship offerings on it.

**6** A fire is to be kept burning on the altar continually. It must not go out.

**7** "These are the instructions for the grain offering. Aaron's sons are to present it before Yahweh, in front of the altar.

**8** One of them is to take from it a handful of the fine flour of the grain offering and of its oil, with all the frankincense that is on the grain offering, and burn it on the altar as a pleasing aroma, its memorial portion for Yahweh.

**9** Aaron and his sons are to eat what is left of it. It is to be eaten without yeast in a holy place. They are to eat it in the courtyard of the tent of meeting.

**9** Any saddle on which the man with the discharge rides will be unclean.

**10** Whoever touches anything that was under him will be unclean until evening, and whoever carries such things must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**11** Anyone whom the man with the discharge touches without having rinsed his hands in water must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**12** A clay pot that the man with the discharge touches must be broken, and any wooden article must be rinsed in water.

**13** "When the man with the discharge becomes clean from his discharge, he is to count off seven days for his cleansing. Then he must wash his clothes and bathe his body in fresh water,[^15-3] and he will be clean.

**14** On the eighth day he is to take two turtledoves or two young pigeons and come before Yahweh at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and give them to the priest.

**15** The priest is to offer them, one as a sin offering[^15-4] and the other as a burnt offering. So the priest will make atonement for him before Yahweh because of his discharge.

**16** "When a man has an emission of semen, he must bathe his whole body in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**17** Any garment or any leather on which there is semen must be washed with water, and it will be unclean until evening.

**18** When a man lies with a woman and there is an emission of semen, they must both bathe in water, and they will be unclean until evening.

**19** "When a woman has a discharge, and the discharge from her body is blood, she will be in her menstrual impurity[^15-5] for seven days, and anyone who touches her will be unclean until evening.

**20** Anything she lies on during her menstrual impurity will be unclean, and anything she sits on will be unclean.

**21** Anyone who touches her bed must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**22** Anyone who touches anything she sits on must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**23** Whether it is on the bed or on anything on which she is sitting, when someone touches it, he will be unclean until evening.[^15-6]

**24** If a man lies with her, and her menstrual impurity comes on him, he will be unclean for seven days, and every bed he lies on will be unclean.

**25** "When a woman has a discharge of blood for many days, not at the time of her monthly period, or if she has a discharge that continues beyond her period, she will be unclean for all the days of her unclean discharge, just as during the days of her period. She is unclean.

**26** Any bed she lies on during all the days of her discharge will be to her like her bed during her period, and anything she sits on will be unclean, as in her menstrual impurity.

**27** Anyone who touches them will be unclean. He must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**28** When she becomes clean from her discharge, she is to count off seven days, and after that she will be clean.

**29** On the eighth day she is to take two turtledoves or two young pigeons and bring them to the priest at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**30** The priest is to offer one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering. So the priest will make atonement for her before Yahweh because of her unclean discharge.

**31** "In this way you are to keep the Israelites separate from their uncleanness, so that they do not die in their uncleanness by making my tabernacle,[^15-7] which is among them, unclean.

**32** These are the instructions for the man with a discharge, and for the man who has an emission of semen that makes him unclean,

**33** for the woman who is in her menstrual period, for anyone with a discharge, whether male or female, and for a man who lies with a woman who is unclean."

[^15-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^15-2]: Literally "from his flesh", here probably a polite way of referring to the genitals. Also in verses 3, 7 and 19.
[^15-3]: Literally "living water": water from a spring or a flowing stream.
[^15-4]: Hebrew *chatta't*. Here no wrongdoing is involved: the offering removes the impurity. See the note on 4:3. Also in verse 30.
[^15-5]: Hebrew *niddah*, "separation, impurity": here the state of a woman during her monthly period. Also in verses 20, 24–26 and 33.
[^15-6]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "And if it is on the bed, or on the article on which she is sitting, when he touches it he will be unclean until evening."
[^15-7]: Hebrew *mishkan*, "dwelling place": the portable sanctuary, also called the tent of meeting.
