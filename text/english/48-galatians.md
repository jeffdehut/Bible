## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, an apostle, not from human beings nor through a human being, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised him from the dead,

**2** and all the brothers[^1-1] who are with me, to the churches of Galatia:

**3** Grace to you and peace from God the Father and our Lord Jesus Christ,[^1-2]

**4** who gave himself for our sins to rescue us from the present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father,

**5** to whom be the glory forever and ever. Amen.

**6** I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting him who called you by the grace of Christ, and are turning to a different good news[^1-3] —

**7** not that there is another one; but there are some who are troubling you and want to distort the good news of Christ.

**8** But even if we, or an angel from heaven, should proclaim good news to you contrary to what we proclaimed to you, let him be accursed.[^1-4]

**9** As we have said before, so now I say again: if anyone is proclaiming good news to you contrary to what you received, let him be accursed.

**10** For am I now trying to win the approval of people, or of God? Or am I seeking to please people? If I were still pleasing people, I would not be a servant[^1-5] of Christ.

**11** For[^1-6] I want you to know, brothers and sisters,[^1-7] that the good news that was proclaimed by me is not of human origin.[^1-8]

**12** For I did not receive it from any human being, nor was I taught it, but I received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ.[^1-9]

**13** For you have heard of my former way of life in Judaism, how I persecuted the church of God beyond measure and tried to destroy it.

**14** I was advancing in Judaism beyond many of my own age among my people, so extremely zealous was I for the traditions of my ancestors.

**15** But when he who had set me apart from my mother's womb,[^1-10] and called me through his grace, was pleased

**16** to reveal his Son in me,[^1-11] so that I might proclaim him as good news among the Gentiles,[^1-12] I did not immediately consult with any human being,[^1-13]

**17** nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me. Instead I went away into Arabia,[^1-14] and returned again to Damascus.

**18** Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to get to know Cephas,[^1-15] and I stayed with him fifteen days.

**19** But I saw none of the other apostles except James, the Lord's brother.[^1-16]

**20** In what I am writing to you, I assure you before God that I am not lying.[^1-17]

**21** Then I went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia.

**22** I was still unknown by sight to the churches of Judea that are in Christ.

**23** They only kept hearing, "The one who once persecuted us is now proclaiming the good news of the faith he once tried to destroy."

**24** And they glorified God because of me.

[^1-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", here Paul's companions; the word can include women.
[^1-2]: Some manuscripts read "from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ".
[^1-3]: Greek *euangelion*, "good news", traditionally "gospel"; also in verses 7 and 11. *Proclaim good news* in verses 8–9, 11, 16 and 23 is the related verb, *euangelizo*.
[^1-4]: Greek *anathema*, something devoted to God for destruction, and so under a curse; also in verse 9.
[^1-5]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-6]: Many manuscripts read "Now".
[^1-7]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-8]: Literally "is not according to a human being".
[^1-9]: That is, a revelation from Jesus Christ, or a revelation of who Jesus Christ is.
[^1-10]: Compare Isaiah 49:1; Jeremiah 1:5. Many manuscripts read "when God, who had set me apart". The SBLGNT leaves out "God", following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort.
[^1-11]: Or "to me".
[^1-12]: Or "nations". Greek *ethne* means both.
[^1-13]: Literally "flesh and blood".
[^1-14]: Probably the kingdom of the Nabateans, to the south and east of Damascus.
[^1-15]: *Cephas* is the Aramaic for "rock", the name translated in Greek as Peter (John 1:42). Many manuscripts read "Peter".
[^1-16]: Or "I saw no other apostle; I saw only James, the Lord's brother".
[^1-17]: Literally "What I am writing to you, look, before God, I am not lying".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along also.

**2** I went up because of a revelation, and I laid before them the good news that I proclaim among the Gentiles, but privately, before those who were regarded as leaders,[^2-1] for fear that I might be running, or had run, in vain.

**3** But not even Titus, who was with me, was compelled to be circumcised, though he was a Greek.

**4** But because of the false brothers[^2-2] secretly brought in, who slipped in to spy on the freedom that we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might enslave us —

**5** we did not give in to them and submit even for a moment, so that the truth of the good news might remain with you.

**6** And from those who were regarded as important — what they once were makes no difference to me; God shows no partiality[^2-3] — for those leaders added nothing to my message.[^2-4]

**7** On the contrary, they saw that I had been entrusted with the good news for the uncircumcised, just as Peter had been with the good news for the circumcised

**8** (for he who was at work in Peter for his apostleship to the circumcised was also at work in me for the Gentiles).

**9** And when James and Cephas[^2-5] and John, who were regarded as pillars, recognized the grace that had been given to me, they gave me and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship, agreeing that we should go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised.

**10** Only, we were to remember the poor, the very thing I was also eager to do.

**11** But when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned.

**12** For before certain people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles. But when they came,[^2-6] he drew back and separated himself, because he was afraid of the circumcision group.[^2-7]

**13** And the rest of the Jews joined him in this hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy.

**14** But when I saw that they were not walking in step with the truth of the good news, I said to Cephas in front of them all, "If you, though you are a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how can you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?"[^2-8]

**15** We ourselves are Jews by birth and not Gentile sinners;

**16** yet we know that a person is not declared righteous[^2-9] by works of the Law but[^2-10] through faith in Jesus Christ.[^2-11] So we too have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be declared righteous by faith in Christ and not by works of the Law, because by works of the Law no human being[^2-12] will be declared righteous.

**17** But if, in seeking to be declared righteous in Christ, we ourselves have also been found to be sinners, is Christ then a servant of sin? By no means![^2-13]

**18** For if I build up again the very things I tore down, I show myself to be a lawbreaker.

**19** For through the Law I died to the Law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ.

**20** It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh[^2-14] I live by faith in the Son of God,[^2-15] who loved me and gave himself for me.

**21** I do not set aside the grace of God; for if righteousness comes through the Law, then Christ died for nothing.

[^2-1]: Literally "those who seemed", that is, those regarded as important; also in verses 6 and 9.
[^2-2]: Or "false believers". Greek *pseudadelphoi*.
[^2-3]: Literally "God does not receive the face of a human being". Compare Deuteronomy 10:17.
[^2-4]: Literally "for to me those who seemed added nothing". Or "imposed nothing more on me".
[^2-5]: The Aramaic name of Peter (see 1:18); also in verses 11 and 14. Many manuscripts read "Peter" in verses 11 and 14.
[^2-6]: Some manuscripts read "when he came".
[^2-7]: Literally "those of circumcision", probably Jewish believers who insisted that Gentile believers be circumcised; or Jews in general.
[^2-8]: Greek *ioudaizo*, "live as a Jew", adopting Jewish customs. Paul's words to Cephas may end here, or may continue to the end of verse 21.
[^2-9]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify"; also in verse 17.
[^2-10]: Greek *ean me*, usually "except", here generally understood as "but only".
[^2-11]: Or "through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ". Likewise "by faith in Christ" later in the verse may be "by the faithfulness of Christ". Literally "through faith of Jesus Christ" and "from faith of Christ".
[^2-12]: Literally "no flesh". Compare Psalm 143:2.
[^2-13]: Greek *me genoito*, "may it not be", a strong denial.
[^2-14]: That is, in this bodily life. Greek *sarx*, "flesh", here not in Paul's moral sense.
[^2-15]: Or "by the faithfulness of the Son of God". Some manuscripts read "by faith in God and Christ".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** You foolish Galatians! Who has bewitched you? Before your eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified.[^3-1]

**2** I want to learn only this from you: did you receive the Spirit by works of the Law, or by hearing with faith?[^3-2]

**3** Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being made complete by the flesh?[^3-3]

**4** Did you suffer[^3-4] so many things for nothing, if indeed it was for nothing?

**5** So does he who supplies the Spirit to you and works miracles among you do it by works of the Law, or by hearing with faith?

**6** It is just as with Abraham: he believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.[^3-5]

**7** Know then that it is those who are of faith who are the sons[^3-6] of Abraham.

**8** And the scripture, foreseeing that God would declare the Gentiles righteous[^3-7] by faith, proclaimed the good news beforehand to Abraham, saying, "In you all the nations will be blessed."[^3-8]

**9** So those who are of faith are blessed along with Abraham, the man of faith.[^3-9]

**10** For all who rely on works of the Law are under a curse. For it is written, "Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all the things written in the book of the Law, to do them."[^3-10]

**11** Now it is clear that no one is declared righteous before God by the Law, because "the righteous will live by faith."[^3-11]

**12** But the Law does not rest on faith; on the contrary, "The person who does them will live by them."[^3-12]

**13** Christ redeemed us from the curse of the Law by becoming a curse for us, for it is written, "Cursed is everyone who is hung on a tree,"[^3-13]

**14** so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

**15** Brothers and sisters,[^3-14] I am speaking in human terms: even with a human covenant,[^3-15] once it has been ratified, no one sets it aside or adds to it.

**16** Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed.[^3-16] It does not say, "And to seeds," as referring to many, but as referring to one, "And to your seed,"[^3-17] who is Christ.

**17** This is what I mean: the Law, which came 430 years later,[^3-18] does not cancel a covenant previously ratified by God,[^3-19] so as to nullify the promise.

**18** For if the inheritance comes from the Law, it no longer comes from the promise; but God gave it freely to Abraham through a promise.

**19** Why then the Law? It was added because of transgressions,[^3-20] until the seed would come to whom the promise had been made, and it was put in place through angels by the hand of a mediator.[^3-21]

**20** Now a mediator is not of one party; but God is one.[^3-22]

**21** Is the Law then against the promises of God? By no means! For if a law had been given that could give life, then righteousness would indeed come from the Law.

**22** But the scripture has shut up all things under sin, so that the promise might be given, by faith in Jesus Christ,[^3-23] to those who believe.

**23** Before faith came, we were held in custody under the Law, shut up until the faith that was to come would be revealed.

**24** So the Law has been our guardian[^3-24] until Christ came,[^3-25] so that we might be declared righteous by faith.

**25** But now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian.

**26** For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.[^3-26]

**27** For all of you who were baptized into Christ have put on Christ.

**28** There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female,[^3-27] for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

**29** And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to the promise.

[^3-1]: Many manuscripts read "Who has bewitched you, so that you do not obey the truth? Before your eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed among you as crucified."
[^3-2]: Or "by believing what you heard", or "by the message that brings faith". Literally "from hearing of faith"; also in verse 5.
[^3-3]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God; here perhaps also with a glance at circumcision, a rite performed on the flesh.
[^3-4]: Or "experience".
[^3-5]: Genesis 15:6, as in the Septuagint (see Romans 4:3). The Hebrew has "Yahweh", God's personal name, where Paul has "God".
[^3-6]: Greek *huioi*, "sons". Paul includes women and men (verses 26–28).
[^3-7]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify"; also in verses 11 and 24. *Gentiles* and *nations* later in the verse translate the same Greek word, *ethne*.
[^3-8]: Genesis 12:3, with "all the nations" from 18:18 (compare 22:18).
[^3-9]: Or "faithful Abraham". Greek *pistos*, "believing, faithful".
[^3-10]: Deuteronomy 27:26, in a form close to the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "Cursed is anyone who does not uphold the words of this law by doing them".
[^3-11]: Habakkuk 2:4 (see Romans 1:17). Or "the one who is righteous by faith will live". The Hebrew has "by his faithfulness", and the Septuagint "by my faithfulness", that is, God's.
[^3-12]: Leviticus 18:5 (compare Romans 10:5).
[^3-13]: Deuteronomy 21:23, which has "cursed by God".
[^3-14]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^3-15]: Greek *diatheke*, "covenant", also used of a will; also in verse 17.
[^3-16]: Greek *sperma*, "seed", that is, offspring; also in verses 19 and 29. Like Hebrew *zera*, which this translation renders "offspring" in Genesis, it is a singular word that can mean one descendant or many. Paul's argument turns on the singular; in verse 29 he applies the word to all who belong to Christ.
[^3-17]: Genesis 12:7; 13:15; 17:8; 24:7.
[^3-18]: Compare Exodus 12:40.
[^3-19]: Many manuscripts add "in Christ" or "for Christ".
[^3-20]: Or "for the sake of transgressions", perhaps meaning to show sin up as transgression.
[^3-21]: Compare Deuteronomy 33:2 in the Septuagint; Acts 7:53.
[^3-22]: The meaning is uncertain. Perhaps "a mediator stands between two parties, but God acts alone". Compare Deuteronomy 6:4.
[^3-23]: Or "by the faithfulness of Jesus Christ". Literally "from faith of Jesus Christ".
[^3-24]: Greek *paidagogos*, a slave who supervised a boy's conduct and took him to school; also in verse 25.
[^3-25]: Or "to lead us to Christ". Literally "toward Christ".
[^3-26]: Or "through faith, in Christ Jesus". *Sons*: see verse 7.
[^3-27]: Literally "there is not male and female", echoing Genesis 1:27.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** What I mean is this: as long as the heir is a child, he is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything.

**2** He is under guardians and managers until the time set by his father.

**3** So also we, when we were children, were enslaved under the elemental forces[^4-1] of the world.

**4** But when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the Law,

**5** to redeem those who were under the Law, so that we might receive adoption as sons.[^4-2]

**6** And because you are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts,[^4-3] crying, "Abba,[^4-4] Father!"

**7** So you are no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, then also an heir through God.[^4-5]

**8** Formerly, when you did not know God, you were slaves to beings that by nature are not gods.

**9** But now that you have come to know God, or rather to be known by God, how can you turn back again to the weak and worthless elemental forces? Do you want to be enslaved to them all over again?

**10** You observe days and months and seasons and years!

**11** I am afraid for you, that perhaps I have labored over you in vain.

**12** I beg you, brothers and sisters,[^4-6] become as I am, for I also have become as you are. You did me no wrong.

**13** You know that it was because of a bodily illness[^4-7] that I proclaimed the good news to you the first time.

**14** And though my condition was a trial to you, you did not despise or reject me,[^4-8] but you welcomed me as an angel of God,[^4-9] as Christ Jesus.

**15** Where then is your blessedness?[^4-10] For I testify that, if it had been possible, you would have torn out your eyes and given them to me.

**16** Have I then become your enemy by telling you the truth?

**17** They court you eagerly, but not for good. They want to shut you out, so that you will court them.

**18** It is good to be courted eagerly for a good purpose at all times, and not only when I am present with you,

**19** my children,[^4-11] for whom I am again in the pains of childbirth until Christ is formed in you.

**20** I wish I could be present with you now and change my tone, for I am perplexed about you.

**21** Tell me, you who want to be under the Law, do you not listen to the Law?

**22** For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the slave woman and one by the free woman.[^4-12]

**23** But the son of the slave woman was born according to the flesh,[^4-13] and the son of the free woman through a promise.

**24** These things are spoken as an allegory. These women are two covenants. One is from Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery; she is Hagar.

**25** Now Hagar[^4-14] is Mount Sinai in Arabia; she corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she is in slavery with her children.

**26** But the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother.[^4-15]

**27** For it is written: "Rejoice, barren woman, you who have not given birth! Break into song and shout, you who have not been in labor! For the children of the desolate woman are more than the children of the married woman."[^4-16]

**28** Now you,[^4-17] brothers and sisters, like Isaac, are children of promise.

**29** But just as at that time the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one born according to the Spirit, so it is now also.[^4-18]

**30** But what does the scripture say? "Drive out the slave woman and her son, for the son of the slave woman will never share the inheritance with the son of the free woman."[^4-19]

**31** Therefore, brothers and sisters, we are not children of the slave woman but of the free woman.

[^4-1]: Greek *stoicheia*, "elements": the basic principles of the world, or the elemental spirits thought to rule it; also in verse 9.
[^4-2]: Greek *huiothesia*, "adoption as sons", the legal act that made someone a son and heir (see Romans 8:15). *Sons* in verses 6–7 includes women and men (3:26–28).
[^4-3]: Many manuscripts read "your hearts".
[^4-4]: Aramaic for "father". Compare Mark 14:36; Romans 8:15.
[^4-5]: Many manuscripts read "an heir of God through Christ".
[^4-6]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 28 and 31.
[^4-7]: Literally "weakness of the flesh".
[^4-8]: Literally "your trial in my flesh you did not despise or spit out". Many manuscripts read "my trial in my flesh".
[^4-9]: Or "a messenger of God". Greek *angelos* means both.
[^4-10]: Or "your blessing of me". Greek *makarismos*, as in Romans 4:6, 9. Many manuscripts read "What then was your blessedness?"
[^4-11]: Many manuscripts read "my little children".
[^4-12]: Genesis 16:15; 21:2. *Slave woman*: Greek *paidiske*, a female slave; also in verses 23 and 30–31.
[^4-13]: That is, in the ordinary human way; also in verse 29.
[^4-14]: Literally "the Hagar", perhaps meaning the name Hagar. Many manuscripts read "For Hagar".
[^4-15]: Many manuscripts read "the mother of us all".
[^4-16]: Isaiah 54:1, as in the Septuagint, which has "Rejoice" where the Hebrew has "Sing". The Hebrew adds "says Yahweh".
[^4-17]: Many manuscripts read "Now we … are".
[^4-18]: Compare Genesis 21:9.
[^4-19]: Genesis 21:10, where Sarah says "with my son, with Isaac".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** For freedom Christ has set us free. So stand firm, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery.[^5-1]

**2** Listen! I, Paul, tell you that if you have yourselves circumcised, Christ will be of no benefit to you.

**3** And I testify again to every man who has himself circumcised that he is obligated to keep the whole Law.

**4** You who are seeking to be declared righteous[^5-2] by the Law have been cut off from Christ; you have fallen away from grace.

**5** For through the Spirit, by faith, we eagerly wait for the hope of righteousness.[^5-3]

**6** For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision has any value, but faith working through love.

**7** You were running well. Who hindered you from obeying the truth?

**8** This persuasion does not come from him who calls you.

**9** A little yeast leavens the whole batch of dough.

**10** I am confident in the Lord about you, that you will take no other view. But the one who is troubling you will bear his judgment, whoever he may be.

**11** But as for me, brothers and sisters,[^5-4] if I am still preaching circumcision, why am I still being persecuted? In that case the offense[^5-5] of the cross has been done away with.

**12** I wish those who are unsettling you would even castrate themselves![^5-6]

**13** For you were called to freedom, brothers and sisters. Only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh,[^5-7] but through love serve[^5-8] one another.

**14** For the whole Law is fulfilled in one word: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."[^5-9]

**15** But if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another.

**16** But I say, live[^5-10] by the Spirit, and you will not carry out the desire of the flesh.

**17** For the flesh desires what is against the Spirit, and the Spirit what is against the flesh; for these are opposed to each other, so that you do not do the things you want.

**18** But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the Law.

**19** Now the works of the flesh are evident:[^5-11] sexual immorality, impurity, debauchery,

**20** idolatry, sorcery, hostility, strife, jealousy, fits of rage, selfish ambitions, dissensions, factions,

**21** envy,[^5-12] drunkenness, carousing, and things like these. I warn you, as I warned you before, that those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God.

**22** But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness,[^5-13]

**23** gentleness, self-control. Against such things there is no law.

**24** And those who belong to Christ[^5-14] have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.

**25** If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit.

**26** Let us not become conceited, provoking one another, envying one another.

[^5-1]: Many manuscripts read "So stand firm in the freedom with which Christ has set us free, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery."
[^5-2]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^5-3]: That is, the righteousness for which we hope. Or "the hope that righteousness brings".
[^5-4]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 13.
[^5-5]: Greek *skandalon*, "stumbling block, cause of offense".
[^5-6]: Literally "would even cut themselves off". Paul's sarcasm turns on circumcision.
[^5-7]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God; also in verses 16–17, 19 and 24.
[^5-8]: Greek *douleuo*, "serve as a slave".
[^5-9]: Leviticus 19:18.
[^5-10]: Literally "walk".
[^5-11]: Many manuscripts add "adultery".
[^5-12]: Many manuscripts add "murders".
[^5-13]: Or "faith". Greek *pistis*.
[^5-14]: Many modern editions read "Christ Jesus". The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Brothers and sisters,[^6-1] if anyone is caught in some wrongdoing, you who are spiritual should restore such a person in a spirit of gentleness. Watch yourself, so that you are not tempted too.

**2** Carry one another's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill[^6-2] the law of Christ.

**3** For if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself.

**4** But let each one test his own work, and then he will have reason to boast in himself alone, and not in comparison with someone else.

**5** For each one will carry his own load.

**6** Let the one who is taught the word share all good things with the one who teaches.

**7** Do not be deceived: God is not mocked. For whatever a person sows, that he will also reap.

**8** For the one who sows to his own flesh[^6-3] will reap destruction from the flesh, but the one who sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit.

**9** And let us not grow weary of doing good, for at the proper time we will reap, if we do not give up.

**10** So then, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, and especially to those who belong to the household of faith.

**11** See with what large letters I am writing to you with my own hand.

**12** Those who want to make a good showing in the flesh[^6-4] are the ones who are trying to compel you to be circumcised, only so that they may not be persecuted for the cross of Christ.[^6-5]

**13** For even those who are circumcised[^6-6] do not themselves keep the Law, but they want you to be circumcised so that they may boast in your flesh.

**14** But may I never boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which[^6-7] the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.

**15** For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is anything, but a new creation.[^6-8]

**16** And as for all who follow this rule, peace be on them, and mercy, and on the Israel of God.[^6-9]

**17** From now on let no one cause me trouble, for I bear on my body the marks[^6-10] of Jesus.[^6-11]

**18** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers and sisters. Amen.

[^6-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 18.
[^6-2]: Many manuscripts read "and in this way fulfill".
[^6-3]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God.
[^6-4]: That is, in outward things, here circumcision; also "your flesh" in verse 13.
[^6-5]: Some manuscripts read "Christ Jesus".
[^6-6]: Many manuscripts read "those who have been circumcised".
[^6-7]: Or "through whom".
[^6-8]: Many manuscripts read "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision has any value, but a new creation" (compare 5:6).
