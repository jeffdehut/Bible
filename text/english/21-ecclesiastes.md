## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The words of the Teacher,[^1-1] the son of David, king in Jerusalem.

**2** "Vapor of vapors,"[^1-2] says the Teacher, "vapor of vapors! Everything is vapor."

**3** What gain[^1-3] do people have from all their toil, all their toiling under the sun?[^1-4]

**4** A generation goes and a generation comes, but the earth stands forever.

**5** The sun rises and the sun goes down, and it hurries, panting,[^1-5] back to its place, and there it rises again.

**6** It goes to the south and turns to the north; round and round goes the wind, and on its rounds the wind returns.

**7** All the streams flow to the sea, yet the sea is never full. To the place where the streams flow, there they keep on flowing.[^1-6]

**8** All things are weary,[^1-7] more than anyone can say. The eye is never satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

**9** What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done. There is nothing new under the sun.

**10** There is a thing of which people say, "Look, this is new!" It was here already, in the ages before us.

**11** There is no remembrance of those who came before,[^1-8] and of those who are yet to come there will be no remembrance either, among those who come after them.

**12** I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**13** I set my heart[^1-9] to seek and to explore by wisdom all that is done under heaven.[^1-10] It is an unhappy task[^1-11] that God has given human beings to keep them busy.[^1-12]

**14** I have seen all the deeds that are done under the sun, and look, everything is vapor and chasing the wind.[^1-13]

**15** What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is missing cannot be counted.

**16** I said to myself,[^1-14] "Look, I have grown great and gained more wisdom than all who ruled Jerusalem before me,[^1-15] and my heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge."

**17** So I set my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly. I learned that this too is chasing the wind.[^1-16]

**18** For with much wisdom comes much grief, and whoever increases knowledge increases pain.

[^1-1]: Hebrew *Qohelet*, a title or pen name, from the verb *qahal*, "to gather, assemble": one who gathers or addresses an assembly, or perhaps one who gathers sayings (12:9). The Greek title of the book, *Ekklesiastes*, means a member or speaker of an assembly; the traditional English rendering is "the Preacher". The word is feminine in form, perhaps as the name of an office. It is "the Teacher" throughout (1:2, 12; 7:27; 12:8–10). The book does not name Solomon, though the description fits him.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *havel havalim*. *Hevel* means "breath" or "vapor": something real but without substance, which cannot be grasped and soon vanishes. It is also Abel's name (Genesis 4:2). Ecclesiastes uses the word thirty-eight times for what is fleeting, futile, empty or beyond understanding. Traditionally it is translated "vanity", and in some modern versions "meaningless"; here it is "vapor" every time, so that readers can follow the pattern. "Vapor of vapors" is a superlative, like "Song of Songs": the merest vapor, utterly fleeting. The book ends with the same words (12:8).
[^1-3]: Hebrew *yitron*, "gain, profit, what is left over", a word found only in Ecclesiastes. It is "gain" or "advantage" throughout.
[^1-4]: "Under the sun" is the book's phrase for this world and human life in it, as people experience it. It occurs twenty-nine times, always translated the same way.
[^1-5]: Hebrew *sho'ef*, "panting, gasping", or "hurrying": the sun is pictured as a weary runner.
[^1-6]: Literally "there they return to go". Or "from there they go back again".
[^1-7]: Or "All words are weary". Hebrew *devarim* means both "things" and "words".
[^1-8]: Or "of the things that came before … of the things yet to come".
[^1-9]: Literally "I gave my heart", that is, I applied my mind. In Hebrew the heart is the seat of thought and will. Also in verse 17.
[^1-10]: Literally "under the heavens", a variation on "under the sun" (verse 3). Also in 2:3 and 3:1.
[^1-11]: Hebrew *inyan ra*, "a bad business, an evil occupation".
[^1-12]: Literally "to be busied with it". Or "to be afflicted with". *Inyan*, "task", and the verb here come from the same root.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *re'ut ruach*, "pursuit of wind", or perhaps "shepherding the wind" (from *ra'ah*, "to tend, to herd"), something that can never be caught or held. Traditionally "vexation of spirit". The phrase occurs seven times, and a related form, *ra'yon ruach* (verse 17; 4:16), twice; both are "chasing the wind" throughout.
[^1-14]: Literally "I spoke, I, with my heart".
[^1-15]: Literally "all who were before me over Jerusalem".
[^1-16]: Hebrew *ra'yon ruach*, a form of the phrase in verse 14.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** I said to myself,[^2-1] "Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy what is good."[^2-2] But look, this too was vapor.[^2-3]

**2** Of laughter I said, "It is madness," and of pleasure, "What does it accomplish?"

**3** I explored with my heart how to cheer my body with wine,[^2-4] while my heart still guided me with wisdom, and how to take hold of folly, until I could see what is good for human beings to do under heaven during the few days of their life.[^2-5]

**4** I did great things. I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.

**5** I made myself gardens and parks,[^2-6] and I planted every kind of fruit tree in them.

**6** I made myself pools of water, to water from them a forest of growing trees.

**7** I bought male and female slaves, and I had slaves born in my house.[^2-7] I also had great herds and flocks, more than all who were in Jerusalem before me.

**8** I also gathered for myself silver and gold, and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got myself male and female singers, and the delights of human beings: many concubines.[^2-8]

**9** So I became great, greater than all who were in Jerusalem before me. My wisdom also stayed with me.

**10** Whatever my eyes asked for, I did not keep from them. I did not hold back my heart from any pleasure, for my heart took pleasure in all my toil, and this was my portion[^2-9] from all my toil.

**11** Then I turned to look at all the works my hands had done, and at the toil I had toiled to do, and look, everything was vapor and chasing the wind.[^2-10] There was no gain under the sun.

**12** Then I turned to consider wisdom, and madness and folly. For what can the one do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done.[^2-11]

**13** I saw that wisdom has an advantage[^2-12] over folly, as light has an advantage over darkness.

**14** The wise have eyes in their heads, but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I also knew that one fate[^2-13] befalls them all.

**15** So I said to myself,[^2-14] "What befalls the fool will befall me too. Why then have I been so very wise?" And I said to myself that this too was vapor.

**16** For there is no lasting remembrance of the wise, any more than of the fool, since in the days to come everything will already be forgotten. How the wise die, just like the fool!

**17** So I hated life, because the work that is done under the sun was grievous to me; for everything is vapor and chasing the wind.

**18** I hated all my toil at which I toil under the sun, because I must leave it to the one who will come after me.

**19** And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all my toil, all I toiled at and was wise in under the sun. This too is vapor.

**20** So I turned and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil at which I had toiled under the sun.

**21** For there is a person whose toil is done with wisdom, knowledge and skill, and he must give it as a portion to someone who did not toil for it. This too is vapor, and a great evil.

**22** For what does a person get from all his toil and the striving of his heart[^2-15] at which he toils under the sun?

**23** For all his days are pain, and his task is grief; even at night his heart does not rest. This too is vapor.

**24** There is nothing better for a person than to eat and drink and let himself enjoy his toil.[^2-16] This too, I saw, is from the hand of God.

**25** For who can eat, and who can find enjoyment,[^2-17] apart from me?[^2-18]

**26** For to the person who pleases him[^2-19] God gives wisdom, knowledge and joy, but to the sinner he gives the task of gathering and collecting, only to give it to the one who pleases God. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.

[^2-1]: Literally "I said, I, in my heart".
[^2-2]: Literally "and see good".
[^2-3]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": what is fleeting, futile or beyond understanding; see 1:2.
[^2-4]: Literally "how to draw my flesh with wine". The meaning of the verb is uncertain: perhaps "to cheer", "to stimulate" or "to indulge".
[^2-5]: Literally "the number of the days of their life".
[^2-6]: Hebrew *pardesim*, a Persian loanword for an enclosed park. It is the source of the English word "paradise".
[^2-7]: Literally "sons of the house".
[^2-8]: Hebrew *shiddah ve-shiddot*, words found only here, of uncertain meaning. They are usually understood as "a concubine, and concubines" (perhaps from *shad*, "breast"); others take them as "a chest, and chests" of treasure. The singular and plural together express a great number.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *cheleq*, "portion, share": what is allotted to someone. A key word in Ecclesiastes, always "portion".
[^2-10]: See 1:14.
[^2-11]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "For what the man who will come after the king, what they have already done". Or "For what is the man who comes after the king? He is the one whom they made king long ago."
[^2-12]: Hebrew *yitron*, the word translated "gain" in verse 11; see 1:3.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *miqreh*, "what happens, what befalls someone": here death.
[^2-14]: Literally "in my heart". Also later in the verse.
[^2-15]: Hebrew *ra'yon libbo*. *Ra'yon* is the word in "chasing the wind" (*ra'yon ruach*) in 1:17.
[^2-16]: Literally "There is no good in a person that he should eat and drink and make his soul see good in his toil". Most take it to mean "nothing better than", as in 3:12, 22 and 8:15.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *yachush*, a word of uncertain meaning: perhaps "enjoy", or "worry".
[^2-18]: Or "more than I". The Septuagint reads "apart from him", that is, apart from God.
[^2-19]: Literally "who is good before him". Also later in the verse.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter[^3-1] under heaven:[^3-2]

**2** a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted;

**3** a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to break down and a time to build up;

**4** a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance;

**5** a time to throw stones away and a time to gather stones,[^3-3] a time to embrace and a time to refrain from embracing;

**6** a time to seek and a time to lose, a time to keep and a time to throw away;

**7** a time to tear and a time to sew, a time to be silent and a time to speak;

**8** a time to love and a time to hate, a time for war and a time for peace.

**9** What gain[^3-4] does the worker have from his toil?

**10** I have seen the task that God has given human beings to keep them busy.

**11** He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also set eternity[^3-5] in their hearts, yet so that[^3-6] no one can find out the work that God has done from beginning to end.

**12** I know that there is nothing better for them than to be glad and to do good[^3-7] while they live.

**13** And also, when anyone eats and drinks and enjoys[^3-8] all his toil, that is the gift of God.

**14** I know that whatever God does will last forever. Nothing can be added to it, and nothing taken from it. God has done it so that people will fear him.[^3-9]

**15** What has been is already here, and what is to be has already been, and God seeks out what has passed by.[^3-10]

**16** And I saw something else under the sun: in the place of justice, there was wickedness, and in the place of righteousness, there was wickedness.

**17** I said to myself,[^3-11] "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every matter and for every deed there."[^3-12]

**18** I said to myself about human beings, "God is testing them,[^3-13] to show them that they are animals, nothing more."[^3-14]

**19** For the fate[^3-15] of human beings and the fate of animals is one and the same: as one dies, so dies the other. They all have the same breath,[^3-16] and humans have no advantage over animals, for everything is vapor.[^3-17]

**20** All go to one place. All came from the dust, and all return to the dust.

**21** Who knows whether the spirit of human beings goes upward, and whether the spirit of animals goes down into the earth?[^3-18]

**22** So I saw that there is nothing better than for people to rejoice in their works, for that is their portion. For who can bring them to see what will happen after them?

[^3-1]: Hebrew *chefets*, "delight, desire", and in later Hebrew "matter, affair". Also in verse 17.
[^3-2]: See 1:13.
[^3-3]: The meaning of the stones is uncertain: perhaps clearing a field and gathering stones for building, or ruining an enemy's field with stones (2 Kings 3:19, 25).
[^3-4]: Hebrew *yitron*; see 1:3.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *ha-olam*, "eternity", the distant past and future. Others suggest "a sense of the ages", "the world", or, from a root meaning "to hide", "darkness, ignorance".
[^3-6]: Or "without which".
[^3-7]: Or "to enjoy themselves". Literally "to do good in his life".
[^3-8]: Literally "sees good in".
[^3-9]: Literally "fear before him".
[^3-10]: Literally "God seeks what is pursued". The sense may be that God brings back what has gone by, or that God looks after those who are pursued.
[^3-11]: Literally "I said, I, in my heart". Also in verse 18.
[^3-12]: The sense of "there" is uncertain: perhaps with God, or at the judgment.
[^3-13]: Or "God has set them apart".
[^3-14]: Literally "that they are animals, they themselves".
[^3-15]: Hebrew *miqreh*, "what befalls"; see 2:14.
[^3-16]: Hebrew *ruach*, "breath, spirit, wind". Also in verse 21, where it is "spirit".
[^3-17]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^3-18]: Or "Who knows the spirit of human beings, which goes upward, and the spirit of animals, which goes down into the earth?" The Hebrew vowel points read it this way. The same consonants can be read as questions, as followed here and in the Septuagint.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Again I saw all the oppression that is done under the sun. Look, the tears of the oppressed, and they have no one to comfort them! Power is on the side of their oppressors,[^4-1] and they have no one to comfort them.

**2** So I praised the dead, who have already died, more than the living, who are still alive.

**3** But better than both is the one who has not yet been, who has not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

**4** I saw that all toil and all skill in work comes from one person's envy of another.[^4-2] This too is vapor[^4-3] and chasing the wind.[^4-4]

**5** The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh.[^4-5]

**6** Better one handful with rest than two fistfuls with toil and chasing the wind.

**7** Again I saw vapor under the sun:

**8** There is someone who is alone, without a second;[^4-6] he has neither son nor brother. Yet there is no end to all his toil, and his eye[^4-7] is never satisfied with riches. "For whom am I toiling, and depriving myself of good things?"[^4-8] This too is vapor and an unhappy task.

**9** Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.

**10** For if they fall, one will lift up his companion. But pity the one who falls alone,[^4-9] with no second to lift him up!

**11** Again, if two lie down together, they keep warm; but how can one keep warm alone?

**12** And if someone overpowers one who is alone, two can stand against him.[^4-10] A three-stranded cord is not quickly broken.

**13** Better a poor but wise youth than an old but foolish king who no longer knows how to take advice.

**14** For he came out of prison[^4-11] to become king, though he was born poor in his kingdom.[^4-12]

**15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun following[^4-13] the youth, the second one,[^4-14] who would stand in his place.

**16** There was no end to all the people, to all whom he led,[^4-15] yet those who come later will not rejoice in him. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.[^4-16]

**17** Guard your steps[^4-17] when you go to the house of God. To draw near to listen[^4-18] is better than for fools to offer a sacrifice, for they do not know that they are doing evil.[^4-19]

[^4-1]: Literally "and from the hand of their oppressors, power".
[^4-2]: Literally "that it is the envy of a man from his neighbor". Or "the rivalry between one person and another".
[^4-3]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^4-4]: See 1:14.
[^4-5]: That is, he ruins himself through idleness.
[^4-6]: Literally "There is one, and there is no second".
[^4-7]: The Hebrew text is written "his eyes" but read "his eye".
[^4-8]: The Hebrew moves into the first person without saying who is speaking.
[^4-9]: Literally "But woe to him, the one who falls".
[^4-10]: Literally "And if someone overpowers him, the one, the two will stand against him".
[^4-11]: Literally "from the house of prisoners".
[^4-12]: The Hebrew does not make clear who "he" is in each part of this verse. It may be the youth, who rose from prison to the throne though born poor; "in his kingdom" may mean in the old king's kingdom. Or it may be the king, who came out of prison to reign, while the poor youth was born in his kingdom.
[^4-13]: Literally "with".
[^4-14]: Perhaps the youth of verse 13, who succeeds the old king, or another youth who takes his place in turn.
[^4-15]: Literally "to all before whom he was". Or "to all who were before them", that is, earlier generations.
[^4-16]: Hebrew *ra'yon ruach*; see 1:17.
[^4-17]: Literally "Guard your foot". The Hebrew text is written "your feet" but read "your foot". Verse 17 is 5:1 in most English Bibles.
[^4-18]: To listen often means to obey.
[^4-19]: Literally "for they do not know to do evil".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Do not be quick with your mouth, and do not let your heart hurry to bring out a word before God.[^5-1] For God is in heaven and you are on earth, so let your words be few.

**2** For a dream comes with much busyness,[^5-2] and the voice of a fool with many words.

**3** When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it, for he has no pleasure in fools. Fulfill what you vow.[^5-3]

**4** It is better not to vow than to vow and not fulfill it.

**5** Do not let your mouth make your flesh[^5-4] sin, and do not say before the messenger[^5-5] that it was a mistake.[^5-6] Why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?

**6** For when dreams are many, so are vapors[^5-7] and words.[^5-8] But fear God.

**7** If you see the poor oppressed, and justice and righteousness robbed in the province, do not be amazed at the matter, for one high official is watched by a higher one, and there are higher ones over them.[^5-9]

**8** But the gain[^5-10] of a land in every way is this: a king for the cultivated field.[^5-11]

**9** Whoever loves money[^5-12] will never be satisfied with money, and whoever loves wealth will never be satisfied with income. This too is vapor.

**10** When good things increase, those who eat them increase. And what advantage is there to their owner, except to see them with his eyes?

**11** Sweet is the sleep of the laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the abundance of the rich[^5-13] will not let him sleep.

**12** There is a grievous evil[^5-14] I have seen under the sun: riches kept by their owner to his harm.

**13** Those riches are lost in some bad venture,[^5-15] and he has fathered a son, but there is nothing in his hand.

**14** As he came from his mother's womb, so he will go again, naked as he came.[^5-16] He will take nothing for his toil that he can carry in his hand.

**15** This too is a grievous evil: exactly as he came, so he will go. And what gain does he have, who toils for the wind?

**16** All his days, too, he eats in darkness, with much grief, sickness and anger.[^5-17]

**17** Look, this is what I have seen to be good and fitting:[^5-18] to eat and drink and enjoy[^5-19] all the toil at which one toils under the sun during the few days of life[^5-20] that God has given him, for this is his portion.

**18** Also, whenever God gives anyone riches and possessions, and enables him to eat from them, to accept his portion and to rejoice in his toil, this is the gift of God.

**19** For he will not dwell much on the days of his life,[^5-21] because God keeps him busy[^5-22] with the joy of his heart.

[^5-4]: That is, yourself.
[^5-5]: Hebrew *mal'akh*, "messenger", perhaps a priest or temple official (compare Malachi 2:7). The Septuagint reads "before God".
[^5-6]: Hebrew *shegagah*, the word used for an unintentional sin in Leviticus 4 and Numbers 15.
[^5-7]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^5-8]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "For in a multitude of dreams and vapors and many words; for fear God."
[^5-9]: Literally "for a high one watches over a high one, and high ones are over them". This may mean that officials protect one another, or that each is watched by another above him.
[^5-10]: Hebrew *yitron*; see 1:3.
[^5-11]: The Hebrew is obscure. Literally "And the gain of a land in all, it is, a king for a field that is worked". Perhaps "a king who cares for the farmland", or "even a king is served by the field".
[^5-12]: Literally "silver". Also later in the verse.
[^5-13]: Or "the full stomach of the rich".
[^5-14]: Literally "a sick evil". Also in verse 15.
[^5-15]: Hebrew *inyan ra*, the phrase translated "an unhappy task" in 1:13 and 4:8.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** There is an evil I have seen under the sun, and it lies heavy on human beings:[^6-1]

**2** a man to whom God gives riches, possessions and honor, so that he lacks nothing he desires, yet God does not give him the power to eat from them, but a stranger eats them. This is vapor[^6-2] and a grievous sickness.[^6-3]

**3** If a man fathers a hundred children[^6-4] and lives many years, however many the days of his years may be, if he is not satisfied with good things,[^6-5] and he has no burial either,[^6-6] I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.

**4** For it comes in vapor and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered.

**5** It has not even seen the sun or known anything, yet it has more rest than he.

**6** Even if he lived a thousand years twice over, but did not enjoy[^6-7] good things, do not all go to one place?

**7** All the toil of human beings is for their mouth, yet the appetite[^6-8] is never filled.

**8** For what advantage does the wise have over the fool? What does the poor person gain by knowing how to conduct himself before the living?[^6-9]

**9** Better is what the eyes see than the wandering of desire.[^6-10] This too is vapor and chasing the wind.[^6-11]

**10** Whatever exists has already been named, and it is known what a human being is:[^6-12] he cannot contend with one who is stronger than he.[^6-13]

**11** The more words, the more vapor;[^6-14] and what advantage is that to anyone?

**12** For who knows what is good for a person in life, during the few days of his life of vapor,[^6-15] which he passes like a shadow? For who can tell anyone what will happen after him under the sun?

[^6-1]: Or "and it is common among human beings".
[^6-2]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^6-3]: Literally "an evil sickness".
[^6-4]: Literally "a hundred".
[^6-5]: Literally "if his soul is not satisfied with good".
[^6-6]: To lack a proper burial was a great disgrace.
[^6-7]: Literally "see".
[^6-8]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here "appetite"; also "life, self".
[^6-9]: Literally "What to the poor man who knows how to walk before the living?"
[^6-10]: Literally "than the going of the *nephesh*", the appetite that roams after what it does not have.
[^6-11]: See 1:14.
[^6-12]: Or "and it is known that he is human (*adam*)", perhaps recalling that the first human was named from the ground, *adamah* (Genesis 2:7).
[^6-13]: Perhaps God.
[^6-14]: Literally "For there are many words that increase vapor".
[^6-15]: Literally "the number of the days of the life of his vapor".

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** A good name is better than good oil,[^7-1] and the day of death than the day of one's birth.

**2** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of feasting, for that is the end of every person, and the living will take it to heart.

**3** Grief[^7-2] is better than laughter, for when the face is sad the heart may be glad.[^7-3]

**4** The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of pleasure.

**5** It is better to listen to the rebuke of the wise than to listen to the song of fools.

**6** For like the crackling of thorns under a pot,[^7-4] so is the laughter of the fool. This too is vapor.[^7-5]

**7** Surely oppression[^7-6] makes the wise foolish, and a bribe corrupts the heart.

**8** Better is the end of a thing than its beginning; better a patient spirit than a proud spirit.[^7-7]

**9** Do not be quick in your spirit to become angry,[^7-8] for anger lodges in the lap of fools.

**10** Do not say, "Why were the former days better than these?" For it is not from wisdom that you ask about this.

**11** Wisdom is good with an inheritance,[^7-9] and an advantage to those who see the sun.

**12** For to be in the shade of wisdom is to be in the shade of money,[^7-10] and the advantage of knowledge is this: wisdom gives life to those who have it.

**13** Consider the work of God: who can make straight what he has made crooked?

**14** In the day of prosperity, be glad,[^7-11] and in the day of adversity, consider: God has made the one as well as the other, so that no one can find out anything that will come after him.

**15** In my days of vapor[^7-12] I have seen everything: there is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked person who lives long in his wickedness.

**16** Do not be too righteous, and do not make yourself too wise. Why destroy yourself?

**17** Do not be too wicked, and do not be a fool. Why die before your time?

**18** It is good that you should take hold of the one and not let go of the other,[^7-13] for whoever fears God will come through with both.[^7-14]

**19** Wisdom makes the wise stronger than ten rulers in a city.

**20** Surely there is no one on earth so righteous that he does good and never sins.[^7-15]

**21** Also, do not take to heart all the things that people say, or you may hear your servant[^7-16] cursing you.

**22** For your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others.

**23** All this I tested by wisdom. I said, "I will be wise," but it was far from me.

**24** What exists is far off, and deep, very deep. Who can find it out?

**25** I turned, I and my heart, to know and to explore and to seek wisdom and the sum of things,[^7-17] and to know that wickedness is folly and that foolishness is madness.

**26** And I find more bitter than death the woman who is a snare, whose heart is a net and whose hands are chains. The one who pleases God[^7-18] escapes her, but the sinner is caught by her.[^7-19]

**27** "See, this is what I have found," says the Teacher,[^7-20] "adding one thing to another to find the sum,

**28** which my soul still seeks but I have not found: one man[^7-21] among a thousand I have found, but a woman among all these I have not found.

**29** Only see, this I have found: that God made human beings upright, but they have sought out many schemes."[^7-22]

[^7-6]: Or "extortion".
[^7-7]: Literally "better long of spirit than high of spirit".
[^7-8]: The verb is from *ka'as*, translated "grief" in verse 3.
[^7-9]: Or "Wisdom is as good as an inheritance".
[^7-10]: Literally "For in the shade of wisdom, in the shade of silver". Shade is a picture of protection.
[^7-11]: Literally "In the day of good, be in good".
[^7-12]: Literally "in the days of my vapor", that is, in my brief life.
[^7-13]: Literally "and also from this do not rest your hand".
[^7-14]: Literally "will go out with all of them". Or "will avoid all extremes".
[^7-15]: Compare 1 Kings 8:46.
[^7-16]: Or "slave".
[^7-17]: Hebrew *cheshbon*, "reckoning, account, explanation". Also in verse 27.
[^7-18]: Literally "who is good before God".
[^7-19]: Some see here the dangerous woman of Proverbs 5 and 7, or Folly pictured as a woman (Proverbs 9:13–18).
[^7-20]: Here, as in 1:2 and 12:8–10, the narrator speaks of the Teacher. In the Hebrew the verb "says" is feminine, perhaps matching the feminine form of *Qohelet* (see 1:1).
[^7-21]: Hebrew *adam*, usually "human being", here set against "woman".
[^7-22]: Hebrew *chishvonot*, a play on *cheshbon*, "sum", in verses 25 and 27.
