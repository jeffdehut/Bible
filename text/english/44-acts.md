## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In my first book,[^1-1] Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began to do and to teach,

**2** until the day he was taken up, after he had given instructions through the Holy Spirit to the apostles he had chosen.[^1-2]

**3** After his suffering he showed himself to them alive, with many convincing proofs. Over a period of forty days he appeared to them and spoke about the kingdom of God.

**4** While he was staying with them,[^1-3] he ordered them not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for what the Father had promised, which, he said,[^1-4] "you heard about from me.

**5** For John baptized with water, but not many days from now you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit."

**6** So when they had come together, they asked him, "Lord, is this the time when you will restore the kingdom to Israel?"

**7** He said to them, "It is not for you to know the times or seasons that the Father has set by his own authority.

**8** But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."[^1-5]

**9** When he had said this, he was lifted up while they were watching, and a cloud took him out of their sight.

**10** While they were staring into the sky[^1-6] as he went, suddenly two men dressed in white stood beside them.

**11** They said, "Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into the sky? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven."

**12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mountain called Olivet,[^1-7] which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath day's walk away.[^1-8]

**13** When they arrived, they went up to the upstairs room where they were staying: Peter and John, James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot,[^1-9] and Judas son of James.[^1-10]

**14** All of these were devoting themselves with one mind to prayer,[^1-11] together with some women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.

**15** In those days Peter stood up among the brothers and sisters[^1-12] (a crowd of about 120 people[^1-13] was gathered together) and said,

**16** "Brothers,[^1-14] the scripture had to be fulfilled that the Holy Spirit spoke long ago through the mouth of David about Judas, who became a guide for those who arrested Jesus.

**17** For he was counted as one of us, and he received his share[^1-15] in this ministry.

**18** (With the reward he got for his wickedness, this man acquired a field.[^1-16] Falling headlong,[^1-17] he burst open in the middle, and all his intestines spilled out.

**19** This became known to everyone living in Jerusalem, so that the field came to be called in their own language[^1-18] Hakeldamach, that is, Field of Blood.)

**20** For it is written in the book of Psalms: 'Let his camp be desolate, and let no one live in it,'[^1-19] and, 'May another take his office.'[^1-20]

**21** So one of the men who have been with us the whole time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,[^1-21]

**22** beginning from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up from us, one of these must become a witness with us to his resurrection."

**23** So they put forward two men: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also known as Justus, and Matthias.

**24** Then they prayed, "Lord, you know the hearts of all. Show which one of these two you have chosen

**25** to take the place[^1-22] in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place."

**26** Then they cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was counted with the eleven apostles.

[^1-1]: Literally "The first account I made". The Gospel of Luke is also addressed to Theophilus (Luke 1:3).
[^1-2]: Or "to the apostles whom he had chosen through the Holy Spirit".
[^1-3]: Or "While he was eating with them" or "As he was gathering them together". The meaning of the Greek word is uncertain.
[^1-4]: "He said" is supplied. The Greek moves from reported speech into Jesus' own words.
[^1-5]: Literally "to the end of the earth", the phrase used in Isaiah 49:6, which is quoted in 13:47.
[^1-6]: Greek *ouranos*, which means both "sky" and "heaven", as in verse 11.
[^1-7]: Greek *Elaion*, "olive grove": the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem.
[^1-8]: The distance Jewish teaching allowed a person to walk on the Sabbath, about 2,000 cubits, a little over half a mile (about 1 km).
[^1-9]: Greek *zelotes*, "zealous one". Mark 3:18 calls him "Simon the Cananaean", from an Aramaic word meaning "zealous".
[^1-10]: Literally "Judas of James", probably meaning "son of James" (also Luke 6:16).
[^1-11]: Many manuscripts add "and supplication".
[^1-12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers". The group included women (verse 14). Many manuscripts read "among the disciples".
[^1-13]: Literally "names".
[^1-14]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly.
[^1-15]: Literally "lot", the word used for casting lots in verse 26.
[^1-16]: Verses 18–19 are an aside by the narrator, set within Peter's speech. Matthew 27:3–10 tells of Judas' death differently: Judas hanged himself, and the chief priests bought a field with the money he had thrown into the temple.
[^1-17]: Literally "becoming prone". Some take the Greek word to mean "swelling up".
[^1-18]: Aramaic, the everyday language of Jerusalem. *Hakeldamach*: traditionally spelled "Akeldama".
[^1-19]: Psalm 69:26 (69:25 in most English Bibles), applied to one person: the psalm reads "their camp" and "their tents".
[^1-20]: Psalm 109:8. Greek *episkope*, "office, position of oversight", related to *episkopos*, "overseer".
[^1-21]: Literally "went in and went out over us", an idiom for living and working among people.
[^1-22]: Many manuscripts read "to take the share" (Greek *kleros*, "lot", as in verses 17 and 26).

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** When the day of Pentecost[^2-1] arrived, they were all together in one place.[^2-2]

**2** Suddenly a sound came from heaven like the rush of a violent wind,[^2-3] and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.

**3** Tongues like fire appeared to them, divided among them, and one came to rest on each of them.

**4** All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages,[^2-4] as the Spirit enabled them to speak out.

**5** Now there were Jews living in Jerusalem, devout men from every nation under heaven.

**6** When this sound[^2-5] came, the crowd gathered, and they were bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in his own language.

**7** They were amazed and astonished, and they said,[^2-6] "Look, aren't all these who are speaking Galileans?

**8** Then how is it that each of us hears them in our own native language?

**9** Parthians, Medes, and Elamites; people who live in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,[^2-7]

**10** Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene; and visitors from Rome,

**11** both Jews and converts to Judaism;[^2-8] Cretans and Arabs: we hear them speaking in our own languages[^2-9] about the mighty works of God!"

**12** They were all amazed and perplexed, and they said to one another, "What does this mean?"

**13** But others mocked them and said, "They are full of new wine!"

**14** Then Peter stood up with the Eleven, raised his voice, and declared to them, "Fellow Jews[^2-10] and all of you who live in Jerusalem, let this be known to you, and listen carefully to my words.

**15** These people are not drunk, as you suppose, for it is only nine in the morning.[^2-11]

**16** No, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:

**17** 'In the last days,[^2-12] God says, I will pour out my Spirit[^2-13] on all people.[^2-14] Your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your young men will see visions, and your old men will dream dreams.

**18** Even on my male and female servants[^2-15] in those days I will pour out my Spirit, and they will prophesy.

**19** I will show wonders in the heavens above and signs on the earth below:[^2-16] blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.

**20** The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and glorious day of the Lord comes.[^2-17]

**21** And everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.'

**22** Fellow Israelites,[^2-18] listen to these words. Jesus of Nazareth was a man commended to you by God through mighty works, wonders, and signs, which God did through him among you, as you yourselves know.

**23** This man was handed over by God's set plan and foreknowledge, and you killed him, nailing him to a cross[^2-19] by the hands of lawless men.

**24** But God raised him up, freeing him from the pains of death,[^2-20] because it was not possible for death to keep its hold on him.

**25** For David says about him: 'I saw the Lord[^2-21] always before me, for he is at my right hand, so that I will not be shaken.

**26** So my heart was glad, and my tongue rejoiced; my body too will live in hope.[^2-22]

**27** For you will not abandon my life[^2-23] to Hades;[^2-24] you will not let your faithful one[^2-25] see decay.[^2-26]

**28** You have made known to me the paths of life; you will fill me with joy in your presence.'[^2-27]

**29** Brothers,[^2-28] I can tell you confidently that the patriarch David died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this day.

**30** Since he was a prophet, then, and knew that God had sworn an oath to him that he would set one of his descendants on his throne,[^2-29]

**31** he looked ahead and spoke about the resurrection of the Messiah,[^2-30] that he was not abandoned to Hades,[^2-31] and his body did not see decay.

**32** God raised up this Jesus, and we are all witnesses of it.

**33** So he has been exalted to the right hand of God,[^2-32] and he has received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit and has poured out what you see and hear.

**34** For David did not go up into heaven, but he himself says, 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand

**35** until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet."'[^2-33]

**36** So let the whole house of Israel know for certain that God has made him both Lord and Messiah, this Jesus whom you crucified."

**37** When they heard this, they were cut to the heart, and they said to Peter and the rest of the apostles, "Brothers, what should we do?"

**38** Peter said to them, "Repent,[^2-34] and each of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

**39** For the promise is for you and for your children, and for all who are far away, as many as the Lord our God will call to himself."[^2-35]

**40** With many other words he testified and urged them, "Save yourselves[^2-36] from this crooked generation."

**41** So those who accepted his message[^2-37] were baptized, and about three thousand people[^2-38] were added that day.

**42** They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to the fellowship,[^2-39] to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.

**43** Awe came over everyone, and many wonders and signs were done through the apostles.

**44** All the believers were together and had everything in common.

**45** They sold their property and possessions and distributed the money to all, as anyone had need.

**46** Every day they continued to meet together in the temple with one mind. They broke bread in their homes and shared their food with glad and sincere hearts,

**47** praising God and having the goodwill of all the people. And every day the Lord added to their number[^2-40] those who were being saved.

[^2-1]: Greek *pentekoste*, "fiftieth": the Jewish Festival of Weeks, fifty days after Passover (Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12).
[^2-2]: Many manuscripts read "they were all together with one mind in one place".
[^2-3]: Greek *pnoe*, "wind, breath", a word related to *pneuma*, "spirit, wind".
[^2-4]: Literally "in other tongues". Greek *glossa* means both the tongue and a language; verse 3 uses it for the tongues like fire. In verses 6 and 8 the listeners call these languages their own *dialektos*, "language, speech". Verse 11 uses *glossa* again.
[^2-5]: Greek *phone*, "sound" or "voice": either the sound from heaven (verse 2) or the sound of their speaking.
[^2-6]: Some manuscripts read "They were all amazed"; many add "to one another".
[^2-7]: The Roman province of Asia, in the west of what is now Turkey.
[^2-8]: Greek *proselytoi*, traditionally "proselytes": Gentiles who had converted to Judaism.
[^2-9]: Greek *glossai*, "tongues", as in verse 4.
[^2-10]: Literally "Men, Jews". Greek *Ioudaioi* can also mean "Judeans", people of Judea.
[^2-11]: Literally "the third hour of the day", counting from sunrise.
[^2-12]: Joel 3:1–5 (2:28–32 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 17–21 mostly as in the Septuagint. Joel has "After this" where Acts has "In the last days, God says", and Joel puts the old men's dreams before the young men's visions.
[^2-13]: Literally "from my Spirit", the wording of the Septuagint; also in verse 18.
[^2-14]: Literally "all flesh".
[^2-15]: Or "slaves". Joel has "the male and female servants", without "my", and does not have "and they will prophesy".
[^2-16]: Joel has "in the heavens and on the earth"; "above", "signs" and "below" are added in Acts.
[^2-17]: Joel has "the great and terrible day". Acts follows the Septuagint, which reads *epiphanes*, "glorious, manifest". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" in verses 20–21 is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^2-18]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^2-19]: Literally "fastening (him)", that is, to a cross. *Lawless men*: or "men without the Law", that is, Gentiles.
[^2-20]: Greek *odines*, "pains", especially the pains of childbirth. The phrase "pains of death" is found in the Septuagint of Psalms 18:5 (18:4 in most English Bibles) and 116:3, where the Hebrew has "the cords of death".
[^2-21]: Verses 25–28 quote Psalm 16:8–11 from the Septuagint. Here the Hebrew reads "I have set the LORD always before me". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^2-22]: The Hebrew has "my whole being rejoices" (literally "my glory") and "will rest secure"; "my tongue" and "in hope" are the Septuagint's wording. *My body*: literally "my flesh", as in verse 31.
[^2-23]: Greek *psyche*, "soul, life", for Hebrew *nephesh*.
[^2-24]: Greek *hades*, the realm of the dead, like Sheol. The Hebrew psalm has *Sheol*.
[^2-25]: Greek *hosios*, "holy, devout", traditionally "your Holy One". The Hebrew has *chasid*, "faithful, loyal".
[^2-26]: Greek *diaphthora*, "decay, corruption", as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "the pit", a name for the grave.
[^2-27]: The Hebrew has "the path of life" and "in your presence there is fullness of joy". The psalm continues "at your right hand there are pleasures forever".
[^2-28]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly; also in verse 37.
[^2-29]: Literally "to seat (one) from the fruit of his loins on his throne". See Psalm 132:11; 2 Samuel 7:12–16. Many manuscripts read "that from the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up the Messiah to sit on his throne".
[^2-30]: Greek *christos*, "anointed one".
[^2-31]: Many manuscripts read "that his soul was not abandoned to Hades".
[^2-32]: Or "exalted by the right hand of God".
[^2-33]: Psalm 110:1. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name: "The LORD says to my lord".
[^2-34]: Greek *metanoeo*, to turn around in mind and life.
[^2-35]: Compare Isaiah 57:19 ("to the far and to the near") and Joel 3:5 (2:32 in most English Bibles: "those whom the LORD calls").
[^2-36]: Literally "Be saved". *Crooked generation*: compare Deuteronomy 32:5.
[^2-37]: Literally "his word". Many manuscripts read "those who gladly accepted his message".
[^2-38]: Literally "souls".
[^2-39]: Greek *koinonia*, "fellowship, sharing".
[^2-40]: Literally "added together". Many manuscripts read "added to the church".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three in the afternoon.[^3-1]

**2** A man who had been lame from birth was being carried there. Every day he was put at the gate of the temple called the Beautiful Gate, to beg for charity from those going into the temple.

**3** When he saw Peter and John about to go into the temple, he asked them for charity.

**4** Peter looked straight at him, as did John, and said, "Look at us."

**5** So the man gave them his attention, expecting to get something from them.

**6** But Peter said, "I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk!"[^3-2]

**7** Then he took him by the right hand and raised him up. At once the man's feet and ankles became strong.

**8** He jumped up, stood, and began to walk. Then he went with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God.

**9** All the people saw him walking and praising God.

**10** They recognized him as the man who used to sit begging at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.

**11** While he was holding on to Peter and John,[^3-3] all the people ran together to them in the colonnade called Solomon's,[^3-4] utterly astonished.

**12** When Peter saw this, he said to the people, "Fellow Israelites,[^3-5] why are you amazed at this? Why do you stare at us, as if we had made him walk by our own power or godliness?

**13** The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God of our ancestors,[^3-6] has glorified his servant[^3-7] Jesus. You handed him over and disowned him before Pilate, when Pilate had decided to release him.

**14** You disowned the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be released to you.

**15** You killed the Author[^3-8] of life, whom God raised from the dead. We are witnesses of this.

**16** It is his name, by faith in his name, that has made this man strong, whom you see and know.[^3-9] The faith that comes through him has given him this complete health in front of you all.

**17** Now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your rulers.

**18** But this is how God fulfilled what he had announced beforehand through the mouth of all the prophets, that his Messiah would suffer.

**19** So repent and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,

**20** so that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and that he may send the Messiah appointed for you, Jesus.

**21** Heaven must receive him until the times when all things are restored, which God spoke about long ago through the mouth of his holy prophets.

**22** Moses said,[^3-10] 'The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your own people.[^3-11] You must listen to him in everything he tells you.

**23** Everyone who does not listen to that prophet will be utterly cut off from the people.'[^3-12]

**24** And all the prophets who have spoken, from Samuel and those after him, also announced these days.

**25** You are the heirs[^3-13] of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with your ancestors, when he said to Abraham, 'And in your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed.'[^3-14]

**26** God raised up his servant and sent him to you first, to bless you by turning each of you from your wicked ways."[^3-15]

[^3-1]: Literally "the ninth hour", counting from sunrise. Many manuscripts begin the verse "Together", taking the word from the end of 2:47.
[^3-2]: Some manuscripts read "get up and walk".
[^3-3]: Many manuscripts read "While the lame man who had been healed was holding on to Peter and John".
[^3-4]: Greek *stoa*, a long porch with a roof held up by columns, along the edge of the temple courts.
[^3-5]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^3-6]: Literally "fathers". Exodus 3:6, 15. Some manuscripts read "The God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob", as in Exodus.
[^3-7]: Greek *pais*, "servant" or "child"; also in verse 26. *Has glorified his servant* echoes Isaiah 52:13 in the Septuagint ("my servant … will be glorified").
[^3-8]: Or "Leader", "Founder", "Pioneer". Greek *archegos*.
[^3-9]: The Greek of this sentence is awkward. Literally "And on the faith of his name, this one whom you see and know, his name made strong".
[^3-10]: Many manuscripts read "Moses said to the ancestors".
[^3-11]: Literally "from your brothers". Verses 22–23 quote Deuteronomy 18:15, 18–19, with words from Leviticus 23:29. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). Some manuscripts read "the Lord your God", as in Deuteronomy, and many read "the Lord our God".
[^3-12]: Greek *exolethreuo*, "destroy utterly". Deuteronomy 18:19 reads "I myself will hold him accountable"; the words "cut off from the people" are from Leviticus 23:29, where the Septuagint uses this verb.
[^3-13]: Literally "sons".
[^3-14]: Genesis 22:18; 26:4; 28:14 (compare 12:3). Genesis 22:18 and 26:4 have "nations"; 12:3 and 28:14 have "families".
[^3-15]: Or "to bless you as each of you turns from your wicked ways".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** While they were speaking to the people, the priests,[^4-1] the captain of the temple guard,[^4-2] and the Sadducees[^4-3] came up to them,

**2** greatly annoyed because they were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.[^4-4]

**3** They arrested them and put them in custody until the next day, because it was already evening.

**4** But many of those who had heard the message believed, and the number of the men came to about five thousand.

**5** The next day their rulers, elders, and scribes[^4-5] gathered in Jerusalem,

**6** with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas,[^4-6] John, Alexander, and all who were of the high priest's family.

**7** They made Peter and John stand in their midst and asked them, "By what power or by what name did you do this?"

**8** Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, "Rulers of the people and elders,[^4-7]

**9** if we are being questioned today about a good deed done to a sick man, and how this man has been made well,[^4-8]

**10** then let it be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel: by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead, by him this man stands before you healthy.

**11** He is the stone that you, the builders, treated with contempt, which has become the cornerstone.[^4-9]

**12** Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven[^4-10] given among people by which we must be saved."

**13** When they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and realized that they were uneducated, ordinary men,[^4-11] they were amazed. They recognized that they had been with Jesus.

**14** And seeing the man who had been healed standing with them, they had nothing to say in reply.

**15** So they ordered them to leave the council[^4-12] and conferred with one another.

**16** "What should we do with these men?" they said. "It is plain to everyone living in Jerusalem that a remarkable sign has been done through them, and we cannot deny it.

**17** But to keep it from spreading any further among the people, let us warn them to speak no more to anyone in this name."

**18** So they called them in and ordered them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus.

**19** But Peter and John answered them, "Judge for yourselves whether it is right in God's sight to listen to you rather than to God.

**20** For we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard."

**21** After threatening them further, they let them go. They could find no way to punish them, because of the people, for everyone was praising God for what had happened.

**22** For the man on whom this sign of healing had been done was more than forty years old.

**23** When they had been released, they went to their own people and reported all that the chief priests and the elders had said to them.

**24** When they heard it, they raised their voices to God with one mind and said, "Master,[^4-13] you made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them.[^4-14]

**25** You said by the Holy Spirit, through the mouth of our ancestor David, your servant,[^4-15] 'Why do the nations rage, and the peoples plot in vain?

**26** The kings of the earth take their stand, and the rulers gather together against the Lord and against his anointed one.'[^4-16]

**27** For truly in this city[^4-17] Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles[^4-18] and the peoples of Israel, gathered together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed,

**28** to do whatever your hand and your plan had decided beforehand should happen.

**29** And now, Lord, look at their threats, and enable your servants[^4-19] to speak your word with all boldness,

**30** while you stretch out your hand to heal, and signs and wonders are done through the name of your holy servant Jesus."

**31** When they had prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the word of God with boldness.

**32** The whole group of those who believed was one in heart and soul. No one claimed that any of his possessions was his own, but they had everything in common.

**33** With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was on them all.

**34** There was no one in need among them, for all who owned land or houses sold them, brought the money from what was sold,

**35** and laid it at the apostles' feet. It was distributed to each as anyone had need.

**36** Joseph,[^4-20] whom the apostles called Barnabas (which means "son of encouragement"),[^4-21] was a Levite, a native of Cyprus.

**37** He sold a field that he owned, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles' feet.

[^4-1]: Some manuscripts read "the chief priests".
[^4-2]: Literally "the captain of the temple", the priest in command of the temple police, second in rank to the high priest.
[^4-3]: A party of the leading priestly families, who did not believe in the resurrection (Mark 12:18).
[^4-4]: Or "proclaiming the resurrection from the dead in the case of Jesus".
[^4-5]: Experts in the Law of Moses.
[^4-6]: Annas had been high priest (AD 6–15) and was still honored with the title. Caiaphas, his son-in-law (John 18:13), was high priest at this time (about AD 18–36).
[^4-7]: Many manuscripts add "of Israel".
[^4-8]: Greek *sozo*, "save, heal", related to *soteria*, "salvation", in verse 12.
[^4-9]: Literally "the head of the corner". Psalm 118:22, applied to Peter's hearers. The Septuagint and Mark 12:10 have "rejected"; Acts has *exoutheneo*, "treat as nothing".
[^4-10]: Many manuscripts leave out "under heaven".
[^4-11]: Literally "unlettered men and laymen", that is, without formal training in the Law.
[^4-12]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^4-13]: Greek *despotes*, "master, sovereign ruler". Many manuscripts read "Master, you are the God who made".
[^4-14]: See Exodus 20:11; Psalm 146:6.
[^4-15]: The Greek of this sentence is difficult. Literally "who, of our father, through the Holy Spirit, of the mouth of David your servant, said". *Servant*: Greek *pais*, "servant" or "child", as in verses 27 and 30. Many manuscripts read "who said through the mouth of your servant David".
[^4-16]: Psalm 2:1–2, as in the Septuagint, which has "gather together" where the Hebrew has "conspire together". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). *His anointed one*: Greek *christos*, "anointed one, Messiah"; compare "whom you anointed" in verse 27.
[^4-17]: Many manuscripts leave out "in this city".
[^4-18]: Greek *ethne*, "nations" or "Gentiles", the word translated "nations" in verse 25.

**26** But I have nothing definite to write to my lord[^25-14] about him. So I have brought him before you all, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after this examination I may have something to write.

**1** Agrippa said to Paul, "You have permission to speak for yourself." Then Paul stretched out his hand and began his defense:

**4** All the Jews know the way I have lived from my youth, a life spent from the beginning among my own people and in Jerusalem.

**5** They have known me for a long time, and they could testify, if they were willing, that I lived as a Pharisee, according to the strictest party[^26-1] of our religion.

**6** And now I stand here on trial because of my hope in the promise God made to our ancestors,

**7** the promise that our twelve tribes hope to see fulfilled as they earnestly worship God night and day. It is because of this hope, Your Majesty, that I am accused by Jews!

**8** Why do any of you think it incredible that God raises the dead?

**9** I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things to oppose the name of Jesus of Nazareth.

**12** On one of these journeys I was traveling to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests.

**13** At midday, Your Majesty, as I was on the road, I saw a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those who were traveling with me.

**14** We all fell to the ground, and I heard a voice saying to me in Hebrew,[^26-5] 'Saul,[^26-6] Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'[^26-7]

**15** I said, 'Who are you, Lord?'[^26-8] And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**16** But get up and stand on your feet.[^26-9] For I have appeared to you for this purpose: to appoint you as a servant and a witness of what you have seen of me and of the ways in which I will appear to you.

**17** I will rescue you[^26-10] from your own people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you

**18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God,[^26-11] and so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are made holy by faith in me.'

**19** So then, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the vision from heaven.

**20** I declared first to those in Damascus, and then to those in Jerusalem and throughout the whole region of Judea, and to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God and do deeds that show their repentance.

**21** That is why the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.

**22** But I have had help from God to this very day, and so I stand here testifying to small and great alike. I say nothing beyond what the prophets and Moses said would happen:

**23** that the Messiah would suffer, and that, as the first to rise from the dead, he would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles."

**24** While Paul was saying this in his defense, Festus said in a loud voice, "You are out of your mind, Paul! Your great learning is driving you mad!"

**25** But Paul said, "I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus.[^26-12] What I am saying is true and sensible.

**26** For the king knows about these things, and I speak to him freely. I am sure that none of these things has escaped his notice, for this was not done in a corner.

**27** King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe."

**28** Agrippa said to Paul, "In so short a time are you persuading me to become a Christian?"[^26-13]

**29** Paul said, "Whether in a short time or a long time, I pray to God that not only you but also all who are listening to me today may become what I am, except for these chains."

**30** Then the king got up, and with him the governor and Bernice and those who were sitting with them.

**31** As they were leaving, they said to one another, "This man is doing nothing that deserves death or imprisonment."

**32** Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar."

## Chapter 27

**1** When it was decided that we[^27-1] would sail for Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion[^27-2] named Julius, of the Augustan Cohort.[^27-3]

**2** We boarded a ship from Adramyttium[^27-4] that was about to sail to the ports along the coast of Asia, and we put out to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.

**3** The next day we put in at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends and be cared for.

**4** From there we put out to sea and sailed under the lee of Cyprus,[^27-5] because the winds were against us.

**5** We sailed across the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia and came to Myra in Lycia.

**6** There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship[^27-6] sailing for Italy and put us on board.

**7** For a good many days we made slow headway, and with difficulty we arrived off Cnidus. Since the wind would not let us go any farther,[^27-7] we sailed under the lee of Crete, off Salmone.[^27-8]

**8** Sailing along the coast with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near the town of Lasea.

**9** Much time had now passed, and sailing had become dangerous, because by now even the Fast[^27-9] was over. So Paul gave them this advice:

**10** "Men, I can see that this voyage is going to end in disaster and heavy loss, not only of the cargo and the ship but also of our lives."

**11** But the centurion paid more attention to the pilot and to the owner of the ship[^27-10] than to what Paul said.

**12** Since the harbor was not suitable to spend the winter in, the majority decided to put out to sea from there, in the hope that they could somehow reach Phoenix and spend the winter there. Phoenix is a harbor of Crete, facing southwest and northwest.[^27-11]

**13** When a gentle south wind began to blow, they thought they had got what they wanted. So they weighed anchor and sailed along Crete, close to the shore.[^27-12]

**14** But before long a wind of hurricane force, called the Northeaster,[^27-13] rushed down from the island.[^27-14]

**15** The ship was caught by it and could not head into the wind,[^27-15] so we gave way to it and let ourselves be driven along.

**16** As we ran under the lee of a small island called Cauda,[^27-16] we were barely able to get the ship's boat[^27-17] under control.

**17** After the sailors had hoisted it aboard, they used supports to undergird the ship.[^27-18] They were afraid that they would run aground on the Syrtis,[^27-19] so they lowered the sea anchor[^27-20] and in this way were driven along.

**18** We were being battered so violently by the storm that the next day they began to throw the cargo overboard,[^27-21]

**19** and on the third day they threw the ship's gear[^27-22] overboard with their own hands.[^27-23]

**20** For many days neither sun nor stars appeared, and the storm continued to rage. At last all hope of our being saved was slipping away.

**21** They had gone a long time without food. Then Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me and not set sail from Crete. You would have been spared this disaster and loss.[^27-24]

**22** But now I urge you to take courage, for not one of you will lose his life; only the ship will be lost.

**23** For last night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood beside me

**24** and said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and God has granted you the lives of all who are sailing with you.'

**25** So take courage, men, for I trust God that it will turn out just as I have been told.

**26** But we must run aground on some island."

**27** On the fourteenth night, as we were being driven across the Adriatic Sea,[^27-25] about midnight the sailors suspected that they were nearing land.

**28** They took soundings and found that the water was twenty fathoms deep. A little farther on they took soundings again and found it was fifteen fathoms.[^27-26]

**29** Fearing that we might run aground somewhere on the rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for daylight to come.

**30** The sailors tried to escape from the ship. They lowered the boat into the sea, pretending that they were going to run out anchors from the bow.

**31** But Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved."

**32** Then the soldiers cut the ropes that held the boat and let it drift away.

**33** Just before daybreak, Paul urged them all to take some food. "Today is the fourteenth day," he said, "that you have been waiting in suspense and going without food, eating nothing at all.

**34** So I urge you to take some food. It will help you to survive, for not a hair from the head of any of you will be lost."[^27-27]

**35** When he had said this, he took bread, gave thanks to God in front of them all, broke it and began to eat.

**36** They were all encouraged and took some food themselves.

**37** There were 276[^27-28] of us on the ship in all.

**38** When they had eaten as much as they wanted, they lightened the ship by throwing the wheat[^27-29] into the sea.

**39** When day came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach, and they planned to run the ship ashore there if they could.

**40** So they cut loose the anchors and left them in the sea, and at the same time they loosened the ropes that tied up the steering oars.[^27-30] Then they hoisted the foresail[^27-31] to the wind and made for the beach.

**41** But they struck a place where two seas met,[^27-32] and ran the ship aground. The bow stuck fast and could not be moved, and the stern was being broken up by the pounding.[^27-33]

**42** The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners, so that none of them would swim away and escape.

**43** But the centurion wanted to save Paul, so he kept them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and get to land,

**44** and the rest to follow, some on planks and some on pieces of the ship.[^27-34] And so it was that everyone was brought safely to land.

**1** Once we were safely ashore, we learned that the island was called Malta.[^28-1]

**2** The local people[^28-2] showed us unusual kindness. They lit a fire and welcomed us all, because it had begun to rain and it was cold.

**3** Paul gathered a bundle of brushwood, and as he was putting it on the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and fastened itself on his hand.

**4** When the local people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "This man must be a murderer. He has escaped from the sea, but Justice[^28-3] has not allowed him to live."

**6** They were expecting him to swell up[^28-4] or suddenly fall down dead. But after they had waited a long time and seen that nothing unusual happened to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.

**7** In the area around that place there were estates belonging to the chief man of the island,[^28-5] whose name was Publius. He welcomed us and entertained us kindly for three days.

**8** Publius' father happened to be lying sick in bed with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to see him, prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him.

**9** After this had happened, the rest of the people on the island who had diseases also came and were cured.

**10** They honored us in many ways,[^28-6] and when we were about to sail, they put on board everything we needed.

**11** After three months we set sail in a ship that had spent the winter at the island, an Alexandrian ship with the Twin Brothers[^28-7] as its emblem.

**13** From there we cast off[^28-8] and reached Rhegium. After one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.
