## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** These are the names of the sons of Israel[^1-1] who came to Egypt. They came with Jacob, each man with his household:

**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah,

**3** Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin,

**4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**5** All the people descended from Jacob[^1-2] numbered seventy.[^1-3] Joseph was already in Egypt.

**6** Then Joseph died, and all his brothers, and that whole generation.

**7** But the Israelites were fruitful and swarmed;[^1-4] they multiplied and grew very, very strong, so that the land was filled with them.

**8** Then a new king came to power over Egypt, one who did not know about Joseph.

**9** He said to his people, "Look, the Israelite people are more numerous and stronger than we are.[^1-5]

**10** Come, let us deal shrewdly with them, or they will multiply even more. Then, if war breaks out, they too will join our enemies, fight against us, and go up from the land."[^1-6]

**11** So they put slave masters[^1-7] over them to oppress them with forced labor.[^1-8] They built storage cities for Pharaoh:[^1-9] Pithom and Rameses.

**12** But the more they oppressed them, the more they multiplied and spread, and the Egyptians came to dread the Israelites.

**13** So the Egyptians made the Israelites work ruthlessly.

**14** They made their lives bitter with hard labor, with mortar and bricks and with every kind of work in the fields. In all the work they made them do, they were ruthless.[^1-10]

**15** The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives,[^1-11] one of whom was named Shiphrah and the other Puah.

**16** He said, "When you help the Hebrew women give birth, look at the birthstool.[^1-12] If it is a son, kill him; but if it is a daughter, she may live."

**17** But the midwives feared God, and they did not do what the king of Egypt had told them. They let the boys live.

**18** So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this? Why have you let the boys live?"

**19** The midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women. They are vigorous,[^1-13] and they give birth before the midwife gets to them."

**20** So God was good to the midwives, and the people multiplied and grew very strong.

**21** Because the midwives feared God, he gave them families of their own.[^1-14]

**22** Then Pharaoh gave this order to all his people: "Every son that is born[^1-15] you must throw into the Nile,[^1-16] but let every daughter live."

[^1-1]: Hebrew *bene yisra'el*. Here it means Jacob's own sons; from verse 7 on, the same phrase means the Israelites, the people descended from them.
[^1-2]: Literally "All the persons who came out of Jacob's thigh". Hebrew *nephesh*, "person, living being".
[^1-3]: The Greek Septuagint and a Dead Sea Scroll of Exodus (4QExod-b) read "seventy-five", the number given in Acts 7:14. Compare Genesis 46:27.
[^1-4]: Or "teemed". The words *be fruitful*, *swarm*, *multiply* and *fill* echo God's blessing in Genesis 1:20–28 and 9:1, 7.
[^1-5]: Or "too numerous and too strong for us".
[^1-6]: Or "gain control of the land". The Hebrew phrase is unusual; it probably means "escape from the land".
[^1-7]: Literally "officers of forced labor".
[^1-8]: Literally "with their burdens".
[^1-9]: *Pharaoh*: the title of the king of Egypt, used in Exodus as if it were a name. The text does not say which king is meant.
[^1-10]: Verses 13–14 repeat the Hebrew root *avad*, "work, serve", five times.
[^1-11]: Or "the midwives to the Hebrew women". The Hebrew can be read either way, so it is not certain whether the midwives were Hebrews or Egyptians.
[^1-12]: Hebrew *ovnayim*, "the two stones", found only here and in Jeremiah 18:3, where it means a potter's wheel. It probably refers to the stones a woman sat or crouched on to give birth.
[^1-13]: Or "lively", "full of life". The word is related to *chayyah*, "animal", and may hint that they give birth as easily as animals do.
[^1-14]: Literally "he made houses for them". *He* is most likely God.
[^1-15]: The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint add "to the Hebrews".
[^1-16]: Hebrew *ye'or*, "the river", a word used for the Nile.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** A man from the house of Levi went and married a Levite woman.[^2-1]

**2** The woman became pregnant and gave birth to a son. When she saw that he was a fine child,[^2-2] she hid him for three months.

**3** When she could hide him no longer, she got a basket[^2-3] made of papyrus for him and coated it with tar and pitch. She put the child in it and set it among the reeds[^2-4] at the edge of the Nile.

**4** His sister stood some distance away to find out what would happen to him.

**5** Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe in the Nile, while her young women walked along the riverbank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her slave woman to get it.

**6** She opened it and saw the child: a baby boy, crying. She felt sorry for him and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

**7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call one of the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?"

**8** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." So the girl went and called the child's mother.

**9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**10** When the child grew older, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."[^2-5]

**11** In those days, after Moses had grown up, he went out to his own people[^2-6] and saw their forced labor. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.

**12** He looked this way and that, and when he saw that no one was there, he struck down[^2-7] the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**13** He went out the next day, and there were two Hebrew men fighting. He said to the one who was in the wrong, "Why are you hitting your fellow Hebrew?"[^2-8]

**14** The man said, "Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Are you planning to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid. He thought, "So the matter has become known."

**15** When Pharaoh heard about this, he tried to kill Moses. But Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian, and he sat down by a well.

**16** The priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

**17** Some shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses got up, came to their rescue, and watered their flock.

**18** When they came home to their father Reuel,[^2-9] he asked, "Why have you come back so early today?"

**19** They said, "An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds. He even drew water for us and watered the flock."

**20** He said to his daughters, "Where is he? Why did you leave the man behind? Invite him to have something to eat."[^2-10]

**21** Moses agreed to stay with the man, and he gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as his wife.

**22** She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, for he said, "I have become a foreigner in a foreign land."[^2-11]

**23** During that long time the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery and cried out, and their cry for help because of their slavery went up to God.

**24** God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob.

**25** God saw the Israelites, and God took notice.[^2-12]

[^2-1]: Literally "took a daughter of Levi".
[^2-2]: Literally "that he was good", the words used of God's creation in Genesis 1.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *tevah*, the word used for Noah's ark (Genesis 6–9). It occurs nowhere else.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *suph*, the word in *Yam Suph*, the name of the sea the Israelites later cross (13:18; 15:4).
[^2-5]: The Hebrew name *Mosheh* sounds like *mashah*, "draw out". The name may also be related to an Egyptian word meaning "born" or "son", found in names such as Thutmose.
[^2-6]: Literally "his brothers"; also later in the verse.
[^2-7]: The same Hebrew verb as "beating" in verse 11 and "hitting" in verse 13.
[^2-8]: Literally "your companion".
[^2-9]: Moses' father-in-law is called Reuel here and in Numbers 10:29, and Jethro in 3:1 and 18:1.
[^2-10]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^2-11]: The name *Gershom* sounds like *ger sham*, "a foreigner there".
[^2-12]: Literally "and God knew". The Greek Septuagint reads "and he made himself known to them".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Moses was tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro, the priest of Midian. He led the flock to the far side of the wilderness[^3-1] and came to Horeb,[^3-2] the mountain of God.

**2** There the angel[^3-3] of the LORD[^3-4] appeared to him in a flame of fire from the middle of a bush.[^3-5] Moses looked, and the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not burned up.

**3** So Moses said, "I will go over and look at this amazing sight. Why isn't the bush burning up?"

**4** When the LORD saw that he had gone over to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush: "Moses, Moses!" He answered, "Here I am."

**5** God said, "Do not come any closer. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy ground."

**6** He said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Then Moses hid his face, because he was afraid to look at God.

**7** The LORD said, "I have seen, I have truly seen, the misery of my people in Egypt. I have heard them crying out because of their slave drivers. I know how they are suffering.

**8** I have come down to rescue them from the power[^3-6] of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and spacious land, a land flowing with milk and honey,[^3-7] the home of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

**9** And now, the cry of the Israelites has reached me, and I have also seen how the Egyptians are oppressing them.

**10** So now, go. I am sending you to Pharaoh. Bring my people, the Israelites, out of Egypt."

**11** But Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"

**12** God said, "I will be with you.[^3-8] And this will be the sign for you that it is I who have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will all serve[^3-9] God on this mountain."

**13** Moses said to God, "Suppose I go to the Israelites and say to them, 'The God of your ancestors[^3-10] has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' What shall I tell them?"

**14** God said to Moses, "I am who I am."[^3-11] And he said, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'I Am[^3-12] has sent me to you.'"

**15** God also said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'The LORD,[^3-13] the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and this is how I am to be remembered from generation to generation.

**16** Go and gather the elders of Israel, and say to them, 'The LORD, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, appeared to me and said: I have paid close attention[^3-14] to you and to what has been done to you in Egypt.

**17** And I have promised that I will bring you up out of your misery in Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.'

**18** They will listen to you. Then you and the elders of Israel are to go to the king of Egypt and say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Now please let us go on a three-day journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD our God.'

**19** But I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless he is forced by a mighty hand.[^3-15]

**20** So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders that I will do among them. After that he will let you go.

**21** I will make the Egyptians look with favor on this people,[^3-16] so that when you go, you will not go empty-handed.

**22** Every woman is to ask her neighbor, and any woman living in her house, for objects of silver and gold and for clothing. You will put them on your sons and your daughters. So you will plunder[^3-17] the Egyptians."

[^3-1]: Or "to the west side of the wilderness". Literally "behind the wilderness".
[^3-2]: The mountain is called Horeb here and in 17:6, and Sinai in chapter 19.
[^3-3]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh*. In the next verses it is the LORD and God who speak from the bush.
[^3-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *seneh*, a thorny bush. The word sounds like *Sinai*.
[^3-6]: Literally "hand".
[^3-7]: A land rich in pasture for flocks and herds and in honey, either from bees or a syrup made from dates.
[^3-8]: Hebrew *ehyeh immakh*. *Ehyeh*, "I will be", is the same word that God uses of himself in verse 14.
[^3-9]: Or "worship". Hebrew *avad*, the verb also used for the Israelites' slave labor in Egypt (1:13–14).
[^3-10]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 15 and 16.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *ehyeh asher ehyeh*. *Ehyeh* is a form of the verb *hayah*, "be", that can mean "I am" or "I will be", and *asher* can mean "who", "what" or "that". So the phrase can also be translated "I will be who I will be" or "I will be what I will be". The Greek Septuagint reads "I am the one who is". It has been understood as a statement of God's own being, as a promise of his presence (compare "I will be with you" in verse 12), or as a refusal to be defined by any name. The text does not explain it.
[^3-12]: Hebrew *ehyeh*, "I am" or "I will be".
[^3-13]: *The LORD* stands for God's personal name, YHWH, which appears to come from the same verb, *hayah* (in an older form *hawah*), "be". In verse 14 God speaks of himself as *ehyeh*, "I am"; the name, probably pronounced *Yahweh*, is the matching form, "he is" or "he will be", by which others speak of him. Some scholars take it to mean "he causes to be". See the note on verse 2.
[^3-14]: Hebrew *paqod paqadti*, "I have surely visited" or "attended to", the words Joseph used in Genesis 50:24–25.
[^3-15]: Literally "and not by a mighty hand", which may mean "not even when he is forced by a mighty hand". The Greek Septuagint reads "unless by a mighty hand".
[^3-16]: Literally "I will give this people favor in the eyes of Egypt".
[^3-17]: The same Hebrew verb, *natsal*, as "rescue" in verse 8.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Moses answered, "But what if they do not believe me or listen to what I say? They may say, 'The LORD[^4-1] did not appear to you.'"

**2** The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A staff."

**3** He said, "Throw it on the ground." So he threw it on the ground, and it became a snake, and Moses ran away from it.

**4** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Reach out your hand and take hold of it by the tail." So he reached out his hand and grabbed it, and it became a staff in his hand.

**5** "This is so that they may believe that the LORD, the God of their ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you."

**6** The LORD also said to him, "Put your hand inside your cloak." So he put his hand inside his cloak, and when he took it out, his hand had a skin disease,[^4-2] white as snow.

**7** Then he said, "Put your hand back inside your cloak." So he put his hand back inside his cloak, and when he took it out of his cloak, it had become like the rest of his body again.

**8** "If they do not believe you or pay attention to the first sign,[^4-3] they may believe the second sign.

**9** And if they do not believe even these two signs or listen to you, take some water from the Nile and pour it on the dry ground. The water you take from the Nile will become blood on the dry ground."

**10** Moses said to the LORD, "Please, Lord, I have never been a man of words, not in the past[^4-4] and not since you have spoken to your servant. I am slow of speech and slow of tongue."[^4-5]

**11** The LORD said to him, "Who gave human beings their mouths? Who makes a person unable to speak or unable to hear, able to see or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?

**12** Now go. I will be[^4-6] with your mouth, and I will teach you what to say."

**13** But Moses said, "Please, Lord, send someone else."[^4-7]

**14** Then the LORD's anger burned against Moses, and he said, "What about your brother Aaron the Levite? I know that he can speak well. And look, he is already coming out to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad in his heart.

**15** You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you both what to do.

**16** He will speak to the people for you. He will be your mouth, and you will be like God to him.[^4-8]

**17** And take this staff in your hand, so that you can perform the signs with it."

**18** Then Moses went back to his father-in-law Jether[^4-9] and said to him, "Please let me go back to my own people[^4-10] in Egypt and see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace."

**19** The LORD said to Moses in Midian, "Go back to Egypt, for all the men who wanted to kill you are dead."[^4-11]

**20** So Moses took his wife and his sons, put them on a donkey, and went back to the land of Egypt. Moses took the staff of God in his hand.

**21** The LORD said to Moses, "When you go back to Egypt, see that you perform in front of Pharaoh all the wonders I have put in your power.[^4-12] But I will harden his heart,[^4-13] and he will not let the people go.

**22** Then say to Pharaoh, 'This is what the LORD says: Israel is my son, my firstborn.

**23** I told you, "Let my son go, so that he may serve[^4-14] me," but you refused to let him go. So now I will kill your son, your firstborn.'"

**24** On the way, at the place where they stopped for the night, the LORD met him and tried to kill him.[^4-15]

**25** But Zipporah took a flint knife, cut off her son's foreskin, and touched his feet[^4-16] with it. She said, "Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me."

**26** So he let him alone. At that time she said "a bridegroom of blood" because of the circumcision.[^4-17]

**27** The LORD said to Aaron, "Go into the wilderness to meet Moses." So he went and met him at the mountain of God, and he kissed him.

**28** Moses told Aaron everything that the LORD had sent him to say, and all the signs he had commanded him to perform.

**29** Then Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the Israelites.

**30** Aaron told them everything that the LORD had said to Moses, and he performed the signs in front of the people.

**31** The people believed. When they heard that the LORD had paid attention[^4-18] to the Israelites and had seen their misery, they bowed down and worshiped.

[^4-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *metsora'at*, traditionally "leprous". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy. *White as snow* may describe the color or the flaking of the skin.
[^4-3]: Literally "listen to the voice of the first sign". Likewise "believe the voice of the second sign".
[^4-4]: Literally "neither yesterday nor the day before".
[^4-5]: Literally "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue".
[^4-6]: Hebrew *ehyeh*, the word God uses in 3:12 and 3:14; also in verse 15.
[^4-7]: Literally "send by the hand of whomever you will send".
[^4-8]: Literally "you will be God to him": Moses will tell Aaron what to say, as God tells a prophet. Compare 7:1.
[^4-9]: Hebrew *Yeter*, a shorter form of *Yitro*, Jethro, the name used later in the verse.
[^4-10]: Literally "my brothers".
[^4-11]: Literally "all the men who were seeking your life are dead". Hebrew *nephesh*, "life".
[^4-12]: Literally "in your hand".
[^4-13]: Hebrew *chazaq*, "make strong, firm". Exodus describes Pharaoh's heart with three different verbs: *chazaq*, "be strong, firm" (in this translation "harden", "hard"); *kaved*, "be heavy" ("make stubborn", "stubborn"); and *qashah*, "be hard, harsh" ("make unyielding"). In Hebrew the heart is the center of thought and will, not only of feeling.
[^4-14]: Or "worship". Hebrew *avad*, the verb also used for Israel's slave labor (1:13–14).
[^4-15]: Literally "him … him". The Hebrew does not say whether Moses or his son is meant.
[^4-16]: The Hebrew does not say whose feet are meant: Moses', the boy's, or the LORD's. *Feet* may also be a polite way of referring to the genitals.
[^4-17]: Or "with regard to circumcision". The meaning of these verses is uncertain. Hebrew *chatan*, "bridegroom", is related to an Arabic word meaning "circumcise".
[^4-18]: Hebrew *paqad*; see 3:16.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Afterward Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, "This is what the LORD,[^5-1] the God of Israel, says: Let my people go, so that they may hold a festival for me in the wilderness."

**2** Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should listen to him and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and besides, I will not let Israel go."

**3** They said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us go on a three-day journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD our God, or he may strike us with plague or with the sword."

**4** But the king of Egypt said to them, "Moses and Aaron, why are you taking the people away from their work? Get back to your labor!"

**5** Pharaoh also said, "Look, the people of the land are now many,[^5-2] and you are making them stop their labor."

**6** That same day Pharaoh gave orders to the slave drivers over the people and to their foremen:[^5-3]

**7** "You are no longer to give the people straw for making bricks, as you did before.[^5-4] Let them go and gather straw for themselves.

**8** But require them to make the same number of bricks as they were making before. Do not reduce it. They are lazy. That is why they are crying out, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God.'

**9** Make the work heavier for the men, so that they keep at it and pay no attention to lies."

**10** So the people's slave drivers and their foremen went out and said to the people, "This is what Pharaoh says: I will not give you any straw.

**11** Go and get straw for yourselves wherever you can find it. But your work will not be reduced at all."

**12** So the people scattered throughout the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.

**13** The slave drivers kept pressing them: "Finish your work, the amount required each day, just as when there was straw."

**14** The Israelite foremen, whom Pharaoh's slave drivers had put over them, were beaten and asked, "Why didn't you finish your quota of bricks yesterday or today, as before?"

**15** Then the Israelite foremen went and cried out to Pharaoh, "Why do you treat your servants this way?

**16** No straw is given to your servants, yet we are told, 'Make bricks!' Now your servants are being beaten, but the fault is your own people's."[^5-5]

**17** He said, "You are lazy, lazy! That is why you are saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'

**18** Now go and work. No straw will be given to you, but you must still deliver the full quota of bricks."

**19** The Israelite foremen saw that they were in trouble when they were told, "You must not reduce your daily quota of bricks."

**20** As they came out from Pharaoh, they met Moses and Aaron, who were standing there waiting for them.

**21** They said to them, "May the LORD look on you and judge you! You have made us repulsive to Pharaoh and his officials,[^5-6] and you have put a sword in their hand to kill us."

**22** Moses went back to the LORD and said, "Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people? Why did you ever send me?

**23** Ever since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has brought trouble on this people, and you have not rescued your people at all."

[^5-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-2]: The Samaritan Pentateuch reads "Look, they are now more numerous than the people of the land".
[^5-3]: Hebrew *shoterim*, officials, perhaps record keepers. Verse 14 shows that they were Israelites placed over their own people.
[^5-4]: Literally "as yesterday and the day before"; also in verses 8 and 14.
[^5-5]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and your people sins" or "and the sin is your people's". The Greek Septuagint reads "so you will wrong your people".
[^5-6]: Literally "You have made our smell stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^6-1] said to Moses, "Now you will see what I will do to Pharaoh. Forced by a mighty hand,[^6-2] he will let them go; forced by a mighty hand, he will drive them out of his land."

**2** God spoke to Moses and said to him, "I am the LORD.

**3** I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob as God Almighty,[^6-3] but by my name the LORD I did not make myself known to them.[^6-4]

**4** I also established my covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land where they lived as foreigners.

**5** And I have heard the groaning of the Israelites, whom the Egyptians are holding in slavery, and I have remembered my covenant.

**6** So say to the Israelites: 'I am the LORD. I will bring you out from under the forced labor of the Egyptians. I will rescue you from slavery to them, and I will redeem[^6-5] you with an outstretched arm and with great acts of judgment.

**7** I will take you as my own people, and I will be your God. Then you will know that I am the LORD your God, who brought you out from under the forced labor of the Egyptians.

**8** I will bring you to the land that I swore[^6-6] to give to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, and I will give it to you as your possession. I am the LORD.'"

**9** Moses told this to the Israelites, but they did not listen to Moses, because they were so discouraged[^6-7] and their labor was so hard.

**10** Then the LORD said to Moses,

**11** "Go and tell Pharaoh king of Egypt to let the Israelites go out of his land."

**12** But Moses said to the LORD, "If the Israelites have not listened to me, how will Pharaoh listen to me, when I speak so poorly?"[^6-8]

**13** Then the LORD spoke to Moses and Aaron and gave them orders concerning the Israelites and Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the Israelites out of the land of Egypt.

**14** These are the heads of their ancestral families.[^6-9] The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron and Carmi. These were the clans of Reuben.

**15** The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul the son of a Canaanite woman. These were the clans of Simeon.

**16** These are the names of the sons of Levi according to their family records: Gershon, Kohath and Merari. Levi lived 137 years.

**17** The sons of Gershon: Libni and Shimei, by their clans.

**18** The sons of Kohath: Amram, Izhar, Hebron and Uzziel. Kohath lived 133 years.

**19** The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These were the clans of the Levites according to their family records.

**20** Amram married his father's sister[^6-10] Jochebed, and she bore him Aaron and Moses.[^6-11] Amram lived 137 years.

**21** The sons of Izhar: Korah, Nepheg and Zichri.

**1** If a thief is caught breaking in,[^22-1] and he is struck so that he dies, there is no bloodguilt for him.[^22-2]

**2** But if the sun has risen on him, there is bloodguilt for him. A thief must make full restitution. If he has nothing, he is to be sold to pay for what he stole.

**3** If the stolen animal is found alive in his possession, whether ox or donkey or sheep,[^22-3] he must pay back double.

**4** If a man lets his animals graze[^22-4] in a field or vineyard, and lets his livestock loose so that they graze in someone else's field, he must make restitution from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.

**5** If a fire breaks out and catches in thornbushes, so that stacked grain or standing grain or the field is burned up, the one who started the fire must make full restitution.

**6** If a man gives his neighbor money or goods to keep safe, and they are stolen from that man's house, then if the thief is caught, he must pay back double.

**7** If the thief is not caught, the owner of the house is to come before God[^22-5] to show that he has not laid his hand on his neighbor's property.

**8** In every case of a disputed wrong, whether it concerns an ox, a donkey, a sheep, a garment, or anything lost of which someone says, "This is mine," the case of both parties is to come before God. The one whom God declares guilty must pay back double to his neighbor.

**9** If a man gives his neighbor a donkey, an ox, a sheep or any other animal to keep safe, and it dies or is injured or is carried off while no one is looking,

**10** an oath before the LORD[^22-6] is to settle the matter between them, that he has not laid his hand on his neighbor's property. Its owner must accept this, and he does not have to make restitution.

**11** But if it was actually stolen from him, he must make restitution to its owner.

**12** If it was torn to pieces by a wild animal, he is to bring it as evidence. He does not have to make restitution for what was torn.

**13** If a man borrows an animal from his neighbor, and it is injured or dies while its owner is not with it, he must make full restitution.

**14** If its owner was with it, he does not have to make restitution. If it was hired, the hiring fee covers the loss.[^22-7]

**15** If a man seduces a virgin who is not engaged to be married and sleeps with her, he must pay the bride-price[^22-8] for her and make her his wife.

**16** If her father absolutely refuses to give her to him, he must still pay[^22-9] money equal to the bride-price for virgins.

**17** You must not let a sorceress live.

**18** Whoever has sexual relations with an animal must be put to death.

**19** Whoever sacrifices to any god other than the LORD alone must be destroyed.[^22-10]

**20** You must not mistreat or oppress a foreigner,[^22-11] for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

**21** You must not mistreat[^22-12] any widow or orphan.

**22** If you do mistreat them, and they cry out to me, I will certainly hear their cry.

**23** My anger will burn, and I will kill you with the sword. Then your wives will become widows and your children orphans.

**24** If you lend money to my people, to a poor person among you, do not act like a moneylender toward him. You must not charge him interest.

**25** If you ever take your neighbor's cloak as security for a debt, give it back to him before sunset,

**26** because it is his only covering.[^22-13] It is the cloak for his body. What else will he sleep in? When he cries out to me, I will listen, for I am gracious.

**27** You must not curse God,[^22-14] and you must not curse a leader of your people.

**28** You must not hold back the best of your harvest and of your wine and oil.[^22-15] You must give me the firstborn of your sons.

**29** You must do the same with your cattle and your sheep. The firstborn may stay with its mother seven days, but on the eighth day you must give it to me.

**30** You are to be holy people for me. You must not eat the meat of an animal torn by wild animals in the field. Throw it to the dogs.

[^22-2]: Literally "there is no blood for him": the one who killed him is not guilty of murder. In verse 2, "there is blood for him".
[^22-3]: Hebrew *seh*, a sheep or a goat; also in verses 8 and 9.
[^22-4]: Hebrew *ba'ar*, which can mean "graze" or "burn". Some understand verse 4 as "If a man burns off a field or vineyard and lets the fire spread …", to match verse 5.
[^22-5]: Or "the judges". Hebrew *ha-elohim*, "God"; also twice in verse 8. See the note on 21:6.
[^22-6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^22-7]: Literally "it came for its hire". Or "If he is a hired worker, the loss is covered by his wages".
[^22-8]: Hebrew *mohar*, the payment a man gave to the family of the woman he married.
[^22-9]: Literally "weigh out".
[^22-10]: Hebrew *charam*, "be devoted to destruction": given up to God by being destroyed.
[^22-11]: Hebrew *ger*, a foreigner who lives among the Israelites.
[^22-12]: The Hebrew verb *anah*, "mistreat", is the one used for Egypt's oppression of Israel (1:11–12); also in verse 22.

**29** I will not drive them out from before you in a single year, or the land would become a wasteland and the wild animals would become too many for you.

**30** Little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you have grown in number and can take possession of the land.

**31** I will set your borders from the Red Sea[^23-24] to the Sea of the Philistines,[^23-25] and from the wilderness to the River.[^23-26] For I will hand over to you the people who live in the land, and you will drive them out from before you.

**32** Do not make a covenant with them or with their gods.

**33** They must not live in your land, or they will make you sin against me. For if you serve their gods, it will be a trap for you.
