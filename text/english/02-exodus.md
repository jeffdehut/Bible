## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** These are the names of the sons of Israel[^1-1] who came to Egypt. They came with Jacob, each man with his household:

**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah,

**3** Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin,

**4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**5** All the people descended from Jacob[^1-2] numbered seventy.[^1-3] Joseph was already in Egypt.

**6** Then Joseph died, and all his brothers, and that whole generation.

**7** But the Israelites were fruitful and swarmed;[^1-4] they multiplied and grew very, very strong, so that the land was filled with them.

**8** Then a new king came to power over Egypt, one who did not know about Joseph.

**9** He said to his people, "Look, the Israelite people are more numerous and stronger than we are.[^1-5]

**10** Come, let us deal shrewdly with them, or they will multiply even more. Then, if war breaks out, they too will join our enemies, fight against us, and go up from the land."[^1-6]

**11** So they put slave masters[^1-7] over them to oppress them with forced labor.[^1-8] They built storage cities for Pharaoh:[^1-9] Pithom and Rameses.

**12** But the more they oppressed them, the more they multiplied and spread, and the Egyptians came to dread the Israelites.

**13** So the Egyptians made the Israelites work ruthlessly.

**14** They made their lives bitter with hard labor, with mortar and bricks and with every kind of work in the fields. In all the work they made them do, they were ruthless.[^1-10]

**15** The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives,[^1-11] one of whom was named Shiphrah and the other Puah.

**16** He said, "When you help the Hebrew women give birth, look at the birthstool.[^1-12] If it is a son, kill him; but if it is a daughter, she may live."

**17** But the midwives feared God, and they did not do what the king of Egypt had told them. They let the boys live.

**18** So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this? Why have you let the boys live?"

**19** The midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women. They are vigorous,[^1-13] and they give birth before the midwife gets to them."

**20** So God was good to the midwives, and the people multiplied and grew very strong.

**21** Because the midwives feared God, he gave them families of their own.[^1-14]

**22** Then Pharaoh gave this order to all his people: "Every son that is born[^1-15] you must throw into the Nile,[^1-16] but let every daughter live."

[^1-1]: Hebrew *bene yisra'el*. Here it means Jacob's own sons; from verse 7 on, the same phrase means the Israelites, the people descended from them.
[^1-2]: Literally "All the persons who came out of Jacob's thigh". Hebrew *nephesh*, "person, living being".
[^1-3]: The Greek Septuagint and a Dead Sea Scroll of Exodus (4QExod-b) read "seventy-five", the number given in Acts 7:14. Compare Genesis 46:27.
[^1-4]: Or "teemed". The words *be fruitful*, *swarm*, *multiply* and *fill* echo God's blessing in Genesis 1:20–28 and 9:1, 7.
[^1-5]: Or "too numerous and too strong for us".
[^1-6]: Or "gain control of the land". The Hebrew phrase is unusual; it probably means "escape from the land".
[^1-7]: Literally "officers of forced labor".
[^1-8]: Literally "with their burdens".
[^1-9]: *Pharaoh*: the title of the king of Egypt, used in Exodus as if it were a name. The text does not say which king is meant.
[^1-10]: Verses 13–14 repeat the Hebrew root *avad*, "work, serve", five times.
[^1-11]: Or "the midwives to the Hebrew women". The Hebrew can be read either way, so it is not certain whether the midwives were Hebrews or Egyptians.
[^1-12]: Hebrew *ovnayim*, "the two stones", found only here and in Jeremiah 18:3, where it means a potter's wheel. It probably refers to the stones a woman sat or crouched on to give birth.
[^1-13]: Or "lively", "full of life". The word is related to *chayyah*, "animal", and may hint that they give birth as easily as animals do.
[^1-14]: Literally "he made houses for them". *He* is most likely God.
[^1-15]: The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint add "to the Hebrews".
[^1-16]: Hebrew *ye'or*, "the river", a word used for the Nile.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** A man from the house of Levi went and married a Levite woman.[^2-1]

**2** The woman became pregnant and gave birth to a son. When she saw that he was a fine child,[^2-2] she hid him for three months.

**3** When she could hide him no longer, she got a basket[^2-3] made of papyrus for him and coated it with tar and pitch. She put the child in it and set it among the reeds[^2-4] at the edge of the Nile.

**4** His sister stood some distance away to find out what would happen to him.

**5** Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe in the Nile, while her young women walked along the riverbank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her slave woman to get it.

**6** She opened it and saw the child: a baby boy, crying. She felt sorry for him and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

**7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call one of the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?"

**8** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." So the girl went and called the child's mother.

**9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**10** When the child grew older, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."[^2-5]

**11** In those days, after Moses had grown up, he went out to his own people[^2-6] and saw their forced labor. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.

**12** He looked this way and that, and when he saw that no one was there, he struck down[^2-7] the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**13** He went out the next day, and there were two Hebrew men fighting. He said to the one who was in the wrong, "Why are you hitting your fellow Hebrew?"[^2-8]

**14** The man said, "Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Are you planning to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid. He thought, "So the matter has become known."

**15** When Pharaoh heard about this, he tried to kill Moses. But Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian, and he sat down by a well.

**16** The priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

**17** Some shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses got up, came to their rescue, and watered their flock.

**18** When they came home to their father Reuel,[^2-9] he asked, "Why have you come back so early today?"

**19** They said, "An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds. He even drew water for us and watered the flock."

**20** He said to his daughters, "Where is he? Why did you leave the man behind? Invite him to have something to eat."[^2-10]

**21** Moses agreed to stay with the man, and he gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as his wife.

**22** She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, for he said, "I have become a foreigner in a foreign land."[^2-11]

**23** During that long time the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery and cried out, and their cry for help because of their slavery went up to God.

**24** God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob.

**25** God saw the Israelites, and God took notice.[^2-12]

[^2-1]: Literally "took a daughter of Levi".
[^2-2]: Literally "that he was good", the words used of God's creation in Genesis 1.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *tevah*, the word used for Noah's ark (Genesis 6–9). It occurs nowhere else.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *suph*, the word in *Yam Suph*, the name of the sea the Israelites later cross (13:18; 15:4).
[^2-5]: The Hebrew name *Mosheh* sounds like *mashah*, "draw out". The name may also be related to an Egyptian word meaning "born" or "son", found in names such as Thutmose.
[^2-6]: Literally "his brothers"; also later in the verse.
[^2-7]: The same Hebrew verb as "beating" in verse 11 and "hitting" in verse 13.
[^2-8]: Literally "your companion".
[^2-9]: Moses' father-in-law is called Reuel here and in Numbers 10:29, and Jethro in 3:1 and 18:1.
[^2-10]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^2-11]: The name *Gershom* sounds like *ger sham*, "a foreigner there".
[^2-12]: Literally "and God knew". The Greek Septuagint reads "and he made himself known to them".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Moses was tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro, the priest of Midian. He led the flock to the far side of the wilderness[^3-1] and came to Horeb,[^3-2] the mountain of God.

**2** There the angel[^3-3] of Yahweh[^3-4] appeared to him in a flame of fire from the middle of a bush.[^3-5] Moses looked, and the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not burned up.

**3** So Moses said, "I will go over and look at this amazing sight. Why isn't the bush burning up?"

**4** When Yahweh saw that he had gone over to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush: "Moses, Moses!" He answered, "Here I am."

**5** God said, "Do not come any closer. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy ground."

**6** He said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Then Moses hid his face, because he was afraid to look at God.

**7** Yahweh said, "I have seen, I have truly seen, the misery of my people in Egypt. I have heard them crying out because of their slave drivers. I know how they are suffering.

**8** I have come down to rescue them from the power[^3-6] of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and spacious land, a land flowing with milk and honey,[^3-7] the home of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

**9** And now, the cry of the Israelites has reached me, and I have also seen how the Egyptians are oppressing them.

**10** So now, go. I am sending you to Pharaoh. Bring my people, the Israelites, out of Egypt."

**11** But Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"

**12** God said, "I will be with you.[^3-8] And this will be the sign for you that it is I who have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will all serve[^3-9] God on this mountain."

**13** Moses said to God, "Suppose I go to the Israelites and say to them, 'The God of your ancestors[^3-10] has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' What shall I tell them?"

**14** God said to Moses, "I am who I am."[^3-11] And he said, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'I Am[^3-12] has sent me to you.'"

**15** God also said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'Yahweh,[^3-13] the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and this is how I am to be remembered from generation to generation.

**16** Go and gather the elders of Israel, and say to them, 'Yahweh, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, appeared to me and said: I have paid close attention[^3-14] to you and to what has been done to you in Egypt.

**17** And I have promised that I will bring you up out of your misery in Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.'

**18** They will listen to you. Then you and the elders of Israel are to go to the king of Egypt and say to him, 'Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Now please let us go on a three-day journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to Yahweh our God.'

**19** But I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless he is forced by a mighty hand.[^3-15]

**20** So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders that I will do among them. After that he will let you go.

**21** I will make the Egyptians look with favor on this people,[^3-16] so that when you go, you will not go empty-handed.

**22** Every woman is to ask her neighbor, and any woman living in her house, for objects of silver and gold and for clothing. You will put them on your sons and your daughters. So you will plunder[^3-17] the Egyptians."

[^3-1]: Or "to the west side of the wilderness". Literally "behind the wilderness".
[^3-2]: The mountain is called Horeb here and in 17:6, and Sinai in chapter 19.
[^3-3]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh*. In the next verses it is Yahweh and God who speak from the bush.
[^3-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *seneh*, a thorny bush. The word sounds like *Sinai*.
[^3-6]: Literally "hand".
[^3-7]: A land rich in pasture for flocks and herds and in honey, either from bees or a syrup made from dates.
[^3-8]: Hebrew *ehyeh immakh*. *Ehyeh*, "I will be", is the same word that God uses of himself in verse 14.
[^3-9]: Or "worship". Hebrew *avad*, the verb also used for the Israelites' slave labor in Egypt (1:13–14).
[^3-10]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 15 and 16.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *ehyeh asher ehyeh*. *Ehyeh* is a form of the verb *hayah*, "be", that can mean "I am" or "I will be", and *asher* can mean "who", "what" or "that". So the phrase can also be translated "I will be who I will be" or "I will be what I will be". The Greek Septuagint reads "I am the one who is". It has been understood as a statement of God's own being, as a promise of his presence (compare "I will be with you" in verse 12), or as a refusal to be defined by any name. The text does not explain it.
[^3-12]: Hebrew *ehyeh*, "I am" or "I will be".
[^3-13]: The name YHWH sounds like a form of the same verb *hayah*, "be". In verse 14 God speaks of himself as *ehyeh*, "I am"; the name *Yahweh* is probably the matching form, "he is" or "he will be", by which others speak of him. Some scholars take it to mean "he causes to be". Traditionally "the LORD"; see the note on verse 2.
[^3-14]: Hebrew *paqod paqadti*, "I have surely visited" or "attended to", the words Joseph used in Genesis 50:24–25.
[^3-15]: Literally "and not by a mighty hand", which may mean "not even when he is forced by a mighty hand". The Greek Septuagint reads "unless by a mighty hand".
[^3-16]: Literally "I will give this people favor in the eyes of Egypt".
[^3-17]: The same Hebrew verb, *natsal*, as "rescue" in verse 8.

## Chapter 23

*Status: draft*

**1** Do not spread a false report. Do not join hands with a wicked person by being a malicious witness.[^23-1]

**2** Do not follow the crowd in doing wrong. When you give evidence in a lawsuit, do not side with the majority to twist justice.[^23-2]

**3** And do not show favor to a poor person in his lawsuit.

**4** If you come across your enemy's ox or donkey wandering off, you must take it back to him.

**5** If you see the donkey of someone who hates you lying down under its load, do not walk away and leave him with it. You must help him with it.[^23-3]

**6** Do not twist justice for the poor among you in their lawsuits.[^23-4]

**7** Keep far away from a false charge. Do not put an innocent or righteous person to death, for I will not acquit the guilty.

**8** Do not accept a bribe, for a bribe blinds those who see clearly and twists the words of those who are in the right.[^23-5]

**9** Do not oppress a foreigner.[^23-6] You yourselves know how a foreigner feels,[^23-7] because you were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

**10** For six years you are to sow your land and gather in its produce,

**11** but in the seventh year you must let it rest and leave it alone. Then the poor among your people may eat from it, and the wild animals may eat what they leave. Do the same with your vineyard and your olive grove.

**12** For six days you are to do your work, but on the seventh day you must stop,[^23-8] so that your ox and your donkey may rest, and the son of your slave woman and the foreigner may be refreshed.

**13** Pay attention to everything I have said to you. Do not call on the names of other gods. Do not even let them be heard on your lips.[^23-9]

**14** Three times[^23-10] a year you are to hold a festival for me.

**15** Keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread. For seven days you are to eat bread made without yeast, as I commanded you, at the appointed time in the month of Abib,[^23-11] for in that month you came out of Egypt. No one is to appear before me empty-handed.[^23-12]

**16** Keep the Festival of Harvest, with the first produce of your work, of what you sow in the field. And keep the Festival of Ingathering at the end of the year,[^23-13] when you gather in the produce of your work from the field.[^23-14]

**17** Three times a year all your males are to appear before the Lord Yahweh.[^23-15]

**18** Do not offer the blood of my sacrifice with anything that contains yeast. And the fat of my festival offering must not be left overnight until morning.

**19** Bring the best of the firstfruits of your soil to the house of Yahweh your God. Do not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**20** Look, I am sending an angel[^23-16] ahead of you to guard you on the way and to bring you to the place I have prepared.

**21** Pay attention to him and listen to his voice. Do not rebel against him,[^23-17] for he will not pardon your wrongdoing, because my name is in him.

**22** But if you listen carefully to his voice and do everything I say, I will be an enemy to your enemies and a foe to your foes.

**23** For my angel will go ahead of you and bring you to the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Canaanites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, and I will wipe them out.

**24** Do not bow down to their gods or serve them, and do not follow their practices.[^23-18] You must tear them down completely and smash their standing stones to pieces.[^23-19]

**25** Serve Yahweh your God, and he will bless your bread and your water.[^23-20] I will take sickness away from among you.

**26** No woman in your land will miscarry or be unable to have children. I will give you a full life span.[^23-21]

**27** I will send my terror ahead of you. I will throw into confusion every people you come against, and I will make all your enemies turn their backs and run from you.[^23-22]

**28** I will send the hornet[^23-23] ahead of you, and it will drive the Hivites, the Canaanites and the Hittites out of your way.

**29** I will not drive them out from before you in a single year, or the land would become a wasteland and the wild animals would become too many for you.

**30** Little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you have grown in number and can take possession of the land.

**31** I will set your borders from the Red Sea[^23-24] to the Sea of the Philistines,[^23-25] and from the wilderness to the River.[^23-26] For I will hand over to you the people who live in the land, and you will drive them out from before you.

**32** Do not make a covenant with them or with their gods.

**33** They must not live in your land, or they will make you sin against me. For if you serve their gods, it will be a trap for you.

[^23-1]: Literally "Do not put your hand with the wicked to be a witness of violence".
[^23-2]: Literally "Do not be after many for evil things, and do not answer in a dispute to turn aside after many, to turn aside."
[^23-3]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and you will refrain from leaving (it) to him; you must surely leave (it) with him." The verb *azav* usually means "leave". Here it is often taken as a similar word meaning "help" or "set free", so: "you must help him free it."
[^23-4]: Literally "Do not turn aside the justice of your poor in his lawsuit."
[^23-5]: Or "subverts the cause of the innocent".
[^23-6]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 12.
[^23-7]: Literally "you know the *nephesh* (life, soul) of the foreigner".
[^23-8]: Hebrew *tishbot*, "cease, rest", the verb related to *Shabbat*, "Sabbath".
[^23-9]: Literally "Do not cause the name of other gods to be remembered; let it not be heard on your mouth."
[^23-10]: Hebrew *regalim*, literally "feet", here "times". The same word came to mean the pilgrimage festivals.
[^23-11]: *Abib* means "new ears of grain": the first month of the year, around March–April.
[^23-12]: Literally "My face is not to be seen empty-handed."
[^23-13]: Literally "at the going out of the year".
[^23-14]: The Festival of Harvest is called the Festival of Weeks in 34:22, and the Festival of Ingathering is later called the Festival of Shelters (Leviticus 23:34).
[^23-15]: Hebrew *ha-adon YHWH*. *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^23-16]: Or "a messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. Also in verse 23.
[^23-17]: Or "Do not provoke him".
[^23-18]: Literally "do not do according to their works".
[^23-19]: Hebrew *matsevot*: standing stones set up as objects of worship.
[^23-20]: The Hebrew moves from "he" to "I" within the verse.
[^23-21]: Literally "I will fill the number of your days."
[^23-22]: Literally "I will give all your enemies to you, a back of the neck."
[^23-23]: Hebrew *tsir'ah*, of uncertain meaning. It is usually taken as "hornet" or "hornets", but some understand it as "panic" or "plague".
[^23-24]: Hebrew *Yam Suf*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it probably means the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^23-25]: The Mediterranean Sea.
[^23-26]: The Euphrates.

## Chapter 24

*Status: draft*

**1** Then he said to Moses,[^24-1] "Come up to Yahweh,[^24-2] you and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel. Bow down in worship at a distance.

**2** Moses alone is to come near to Yahweh. The others must not come near, and the people must not come up with him."

**3** Moses came and told the people all Yahweh's words and all the rulings.[^24-3] All the people answered with one voice, "We will do everything Yahweh has said."

**4** Then Moses wrote down all Yahweh's words. He got up early in the morning and built an altar at the foot of the mountain, and set up twelve standing stones[^24-4] for the twelve tribes of Israel.

**5** He sent some young men of the Israelites, and they offered burnt offerings and sacrificed bulls to Yahweh as fellowship offerings.[^24-5]

**6** Moses took half of the blood and put it in bowls, and the other half of the blood he splashed against the altar.

**7** Then he took the Book of the Covenant[^24-6] and read it aloud to the people.[^24-7] They said, "We will do everything Yahweh has said, and we will obey."[^24-8]

**8** Moses took the blood and splashed it on the people. He said, "This is the blood of the covenant that Yahweh has made with you on the basis of all these words."

**9** Then Moses and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel went up.

**10** They saw the God of Israel. Under his feet was something like a pavement made of lapis lazuli,[^24-9] as clear as the sky itself.[^24-10]

**11** But God did not lay a hand on these leaders of the Israelites.[^24-11] They saw God,[^24-12] and they ate and drank.

**12** Yahweh said to Moses, "Come up to me on the mountain and stay there. I will give you the stone tablets with the instruction and the commandment that I have written to teach them."[^24-13]

**13** So Moses set out with Joshua his assistant, and Moses went up the mountain of God.

**14** He said to the elders, "Wait here for us until we come back to you. Aaron and Hur are with you. Anyone who has a dispute can go to them."

**15** When Moses went up the mountain, the cloud covered the mountain.

**16** The glory of Yahweh settled[^24-14] on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it for six days. On the seventh day he called to Moses from inside the cloud.

**17** To the Israelites, the glory of Yahweh looked like a consuming fire on the top of the mountain.[^24-15]

**18** Moses went into the cloud and went up the mountain. He was on the mountain for forty days and forty nights.

[^24-1]: The speaker is Yahweh, though he speaks of himself in the third person in this verse.
[^24-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^24-3]: Hebrew *mishpatim*, "judgments, rulings": the laws of 21:1–23:19.
[^24-4]: Hebrew *matsevot*, as in 23:24, but here set up for the worship of Yahweh.
[^24-5]: Hebrew *zevachim shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers.
[^24-6]: Or "the scroll of the covenant".
[^24-7]: Literally "read it in the ears of the people".
[^24-8]: Literally "we will hear".
[^24-9]: Hebrew *sappir*, traditionally "sapphire". It was probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.
[^24-10]: Literally "like the very substance of the heavens for purity".
[^24-11]: Literally "against the nobles of the sons of Israel he did not send out his hand".
[^24-12]: The verb here (*chazah*) differs from the one in verse 10. It is often used of seeing in a vision.
[^24-13]: Or "the stone tablets, and the instruction and the commandment, which I have written to teach them." Hebrew *torah* here is "instruction" or "law".
[^24-14]: Hebrew *shakhan*, "dwell, settle", the verb from which *mishkan*, "tabernacle", comes.
[^24-15]: Literally "The appearance of the glory of Yahweh was like a consuming fire on the top of the mountain in the eyes of the sons of Israel."

## Chapter 25

*Status: draft*

**1** Yahweh[^25-1] said to Moses,

**2** "Tell the Israelites to bring me a contribution.[^25-2] You are to receive my contribution from everyone whose heart moves them to give.

**3** This is the contribution you are to receive from them: gold, silver and bronze;[^25-3]

**4** blue, purple and scarlet yarn;[^25-4] fine linen;[^25-5] goats' hair;

**5** rams' skins dyed red; fine leather;[^25-6] acacia wood;

**6** oil for the light; spices for the anointing oil and for the fragrant incense;

**7** onyx stones[^25-7] and other stones for setting, for the ephod[^25-8] and for the breastpiece.[^25-9]

**8** Have them make a sanctuary for me, so that I may live[^25-10] among them.

**9** Make it exactly like the pattern I am showing you, the pattern of the tabernacle[^25-11] and the pattern of all its furnishings.

**10** "They are to make an ark[^25-12] of acacia wood, two and a half cubits[^25-13] long, one and a half cubits wide and one and a half cubits high.

**11** Overlay it with pure gold. Overlay it inside and out, and make a gold molding[^25-14] all around it.

**12** Cast four gold rings for it and fasten them to its four feet,[^25-15] with two rings on one side and two rings on the other side.

**13** Make poles of acacia wood and overlay them with gold.

**14** Put the poles through the rings on the sides of the ark, so that the ark can be carried with them.

**15** The poles are to stay in the rings of the ark. They must not be removed from it.

**16** Put into the ark the testimony[^25-16] that I will give you.

**17** "Make an atonement cover[^25-17] of pure gold, two and a half cubits long and one and a half cubits wide.

**18** Make two cherubim[^25-18] of gold. Make them of hammered work[^25-19] at the two ends of the atonement cover.

**19** Make one cherub at one end and one cherub at the other end. Make the cherubim of one piece with the atonement cover, at its two ends.

**20** The cherubim are to have their wings spread out upward, sheltering the atonement cover with their wings. They are to face each other, with their faces turned toward the atonement cover.

**21** Put the atonement cover on top of the ark, and put into the ark the testimony that I will give you.

**22** There I will meet with you. From above the atonement cover, from between the two cherubim that are on the ark of the testimony, I will speak with you about everything I command you for the Israelites.

**23** "Make a table of acacia wood, two cubits long, one cubit wide and one and a half cubits high.

**24** Overlay it with pure gold, and make a gold molding all around it.

**25** Make a rim a handbreadth[^25-20] wide all around it, and make a gold molding all around its rim.

**26** Make four gold rings for it, and fasten the rings to the four corners, where its four legs are.

**27** The rings are to be close to the rim, to hold the poles for carrying the table.

**28** Make the poles of acacia wood and overlay them with gold. The table is to be carried with them.

**29** Make its plates and its dishes, and its jars and its bowls for pouring out drink offerings.[^25-21] Make them of pure gold.

**30** Put the bread of the Presence[^25-22] on the table, to be in front of me always.

**31** "Make a lampstand of pure gold. The lampstand is to be made of hammered work. Its base and its shaft,[^25-23] its cups, its buds[^25-24] and its blossoms are to be of one piece with it.

**32** Six branches are to come out from its sides: three branches of the lampstand from one side and three branches of the lampstand from the other side.

**33** On one branch there are to be three cups shaped like almond blossoms, each with a bud and a blossom, and on the next branch three cups shaped like almond blossoms, each with a bud and a blossom. It is to be the same for the six branches that come out from the lampstand.

**34** On the lampstand itself there are to be four cups shaped like almond blossoms, with its buds and its blossoms.

**35** There is to be a bud under the first pair of branches that come out from it, a bud under the second pair of branches, and a bud under the third pair of branches:[^25-25] so for the six branches that come out from the lampstand.

**36** Their buds and their branches are to be of one piece with it. The whole of it is to be a single piece of hammered work of pure gold.

**37** Make its seven lamps. Its lamps are to be set up[^25-26] so that they give light to the space in front of it.

**38** Its tongs and its trays[^25-27] are to be of pure gold.

**39** It is to be made from a talent[^25-28] of pure gold, together with all these utensils.

**40** See that you make them according to the pattern you are being shown on the mountain.

[^25-5]: Hebrew *shesh*, a fine linen, probably from Egypt.
[^25-6]: Hebrew *tachash*, of uncertain meaning. It may be the hide of a sea animal (such as the dugong), or leather of a special kind or color. Traditionally "badgers' skins" or "goatskins".
[^25-7]: Hebrew *shoham*. The identification of this stone is uncertain.
[^25-8]: Hebrew *ephod*, a priestly garment worn over the robe, described in 28:6–14.
[^25-9]: Hebrew *choshen*, a square of woven cloth worn on the high priest's chest, described in 28:15–30.
[^25-10]: Hebrew *shakhan*, "dwell", the verb from which *mishkan*, "tabernacle", comes.
[^25-11]: Hebrew *mishkan*, "dwelling place": the portable sanctuary described in chapters 25–31 and 35–40.
[^25-12]: Hebrew *aron*, "chest, box".
[^25-13]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the ark was about 45 inches long and 27 inches wide and high (113 by 68 by 68 cm).
[^25-14]: Or "rim", "border".

## Chapter 26

*Status: draft*

**1** "Make the tabernacle[^26-1] with ten curtains of fine twisted linen[^26-2] and blue, purple and scarlet yarn,[^26-3] with cherubim worked into them by a skilled designer.[^26-4]

**2** Each curtain is to be twenty-eight cubits[^26-5] long and four cubits wide. All the curtains are to be the same size.

**3** Five of the curtains are to be joined to one another, and the other five curtains are to be joined to one another.

**4** Make loops of blue yarn on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and do the same on the edge of the end curtain in the second set.

**5** Make fifty loops on the one curtain, and make fifty loops on the edge of the curtain at the end of the second set, with the loops opposite one another.

**6** Make fifty gold clasps, and join the curtains to one another with the clasps, so that the tabernacle becomes a single whole.

**7** "Make curtains of goats' hair for a tent over the tabernacle. Make eleven of these curtains.

**8** Each curtain is to be thirty cubits long and four cubits wide. All eleven curtains are to be the same size.

**9** Join five of the curtains together by themselves, and six of the curtains by themselves. Fold the sixth curtain double at the front of the tent.

**10** Make fifty loops on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain in the second set.

**11** Make fifty bronze clasps. Put the clasps into the loops and join the tent together, so that it becomes a single whole.

**12** As for the extra length that is left over in the curtains of the tent, the half curtain that is left over is to hang down over the back of the tabernacle.

**13** The cubit on one side and the cubit on the other side that is left over in the length of the curtains of the tent is to hang down over the sides of the tabernacle, on both sides, to cover it.

**14** Make a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and over that a covering of fine leather.[^26-6]

**15** "Make upright frames[^26-7] of acacia wood for the tabernacle.

**16** Each frame is to be ten cubits long and one and a half cubits wide.

**17** Each frame is to have two tenons,[^26-8] joined to each other.[^26-9] Do this for all the frames of the tabernacle.

**18** Make the frames for the tabernacle: twenty frames for the south side,

**19** and make forty silver bases[^26-10] to go under the twenty frames, two bases under one frame for its two tenons, and two bases under the next frame for its two tenons.

**20** For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, make twenty frames,

**21** with their forty silver bases, two bases under one frame and two bases under the next frame.

**22** For the back of the tabernacle, toward the west,[^26-11] make six frames.

**23** Make two frames for the corners of the tabernacle at the back.

**24** They are to be double at the bottom, and together they are to be complete up to the top, to the first ring.[^26-12] Both of them are to be made this way. They will form the two corners.

**25** So there will be eight frames, with their silver bases, sixteen bases: two bases under one frame and two bases under the next frame.

**26** "Make crossbars of acacia wood: five for the frames of one side of the tabernacle,

**27** five crossbars for the frames of the second side of the tabernacle, and five crossbars for the frames of the side of the tabernacle at the back, toward the west.

**28** The middle crossbar is to run halfway up the frames,[^26-13] from one end to the other.

**29** Overlay the frames with gold, and make their rings of gold to hold the crossbars. Overlay the crossbars with gold as well.

**30** Set up the tabernacle according to the plan[^26-14] you were shown on the mountain.

**31** "Make a veil[^26-15] of blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen, with cherubim worked into it by a skilled designer.

**32** Hang it on four posts of acacia wood overlaid with gold, with gold hooks, standing on four silver bases.

**33** Hang the veil under the clasps, and bring the ark of the testimony in there, behind the veil. The veil will separate the holy place from the most holy place[^26-16] for you.

**34** Put the atonement cover on the ark of the testimony in the most holy place.

**35** Place the table outside the veil, and the lampstand opposite the table on the south side of the tabernacle. Put the table on the north side.

**36** "For the entrance of the tent, make a screen[^26-17] of blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer.[^26-18]

**37** Make five posts of acacia wood for the screen and overlay them with gold, with gold hooks. And cast five bronze bases for them.

[^26-2]: Hebrew *shesh moshzar*, fine linen with its thread twisted from several strands.
[^26-3]: Literally "blue and purple and crimson worm": wools dyed violet-blue, red-purple and crimson. See 25:4.
[^26-4]: Literally "cherubim, the work of a designer". Also in verse 31.

[^26-6]: Hebrew *tachash*, of uncertain meaning. See 25:5.
[^26-7]: Hebrew *qerashim*, traditionally "boards". They may have been open frames rather than solid planks.
[^26-8]: Literally "two hands": projecting pegs at the bottom of each frame, which fitted into the bases.
[^26-9]: The meaning of the Hebrew word translated "joined" is uncertain.
[^26-10]: Hebrew *adanim*: bases with sockets for the tenons.
[^26-11]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean.

[^26-13]: Literally "in the middle of the frames".
[^26-14]: Hebrew *mishpat*, here "proper arrangement".
[^26-15]: Hebrew *paroket*: the curtain that closed off the most holy place.
[^26-16]: Literally "the holy of holies".
[^26-17]: Hebrew *masakh*, a curtain that screened an entrance.
