## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.[^1-1]

**2** The earth was formless and empty,[^1-2] and darkness covered the surface of the deep. The Spirit of God[^1-3] was hovering over the surface of the waters.

**3** Then God said, "Let there be light," and there was light.

**4** God saw that the light was good. Then God separated the light from the darkness.

**5** God called the light "day," and he called the darkness "night." There was evening, and there was morning: the first day.[^1-4]

**6** Then God said, "Let there be a dome[^1-5] in the middle of the waters, to separate water from water."

**7** So God made the dome and separated the water under the dome from the water above the dome. And it was so.

**8** God called the dome "sky."[^1-6] There was evening, and there was morning: the second day.

**9** Then God said, "Let the water under the sky be gathered into one place, and let dry ground appear." And it was so.

**10** God called the dry ground "land,"[^1-7] and he called the gathered waters "seas." And God saw that it was good.

**11** Then God said, "Let the land produce vegetation: plants that bear seed, and trees on the land that bear fruit with their seed in it, each according to its kind." And it was so.

**12** The land produced vegetation: plants bearing seed according to their kinds, and trees bearing fruit with their seed in it, according to their kinds. And God saw that it was good.

**13** There was evening, and there was morning: the third day.

**14** Then God said, "Let there be lights in the dome of the sky to separate the day from the night. Let them be signs to mark the appointed times,[^1-8] the days and the years.

**15** Let them be lights in the dome of the sky to give light on the earth." And it was so.

**16** God made the two great lights: the greater light to rule the day and the lesser light to rule the night. He also made the stars.

**17** God placed them in the dome of the sky to give light on the earth,

**18** to rule over the day and the night, and to separate the light from the darkness. And God saw that it was good.

**19** There was evening, and there was morning: the fourth day.

**20** Then God said, "Let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the dome of the sky."

**21** So God created the great sea creatures[^1-9] and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarm, according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**22** God blessed them and said, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters of the seas. And let the birds multiply on the earth."

**23** There was evening, and there was morning: the fifth day.

**24** Then God said, "Let the land produce living creatures according to their kinds: livestock, creatures that move along the ground, and wild animals, each according to its kind." And it was so.

**25** God made the wild animals according to their kinds, the livestock according to their kinds, and every creature that moves along the ground according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**26** Then God said, "Let us make human beings[^1-10] in our image, to be like us.[^1-11] Let them rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, the livestock, all the earth,[^1-12] and every creature that moves along the ground."

**27** So God created human beings in his image. In the image of God he created them;[^1-13] male and female he created them.

**28** God blessed them and said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply. Fill the earth and bring it under your control.[^1-14] Rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, and every living creature that moves on the earth."

**29** Then God said, "Look, I have given you every plant on the face of the whole earth that bears seed, and every tree that has fruit with seed in it. They will be your food.

**30** And to every wild animal of the earth, every bird of the sky, and every creature that moves on the earth, everything that has the breath of life in it,[^1-15] I have given every green plant for food." And it was so.

**31** God saw everything he had made, and it was very good. There was evening, and there was morning: the sixth day.

[^1-1]: Or "When God began to create the heavens and the earth, the earth was formless …". The Hebrew can be read as a heading for the whole account, as here, or as a time clause leading into verse 2. *The heavens and the earth* is a Hebrew way of saying "everything".
[^1-2]: Hebrew *tohu va-vohu*, a rhyming pair: a shapeless, empty waste.
[^1-3]: Or "a wind from God", "a mighty wind". Hebrew *ruach* means spirit, wind or breath.
[^1-4]: Literally "one day".
[^1-5]: Hebrew *raqia*, something hammered or spread out: the sky pictured as a solid vault. Traditionally "firmament"; also "expanse".
[^1-6]: Or "heavens", the same Hebrew word as in verse 1.
[^1-7]: Or "earth", the same Hebrew word as in verse 1.
[^1-8]: Hebrew *mo'adim*: fixed times, including seasons and festivals. Or "Let them serve as signs, and to mark seasons, days and years."
[^1-9]: Hebrew *tanninim*. Elsewhere the word also means sea monsters or serpents.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *adam*, "humankind". Here it is not yet the name Adam.
[^1-11]: Literally "in our image, according to our likeness". The plural "us" has been understood as God speaking to a heavenly court, as a plural of majesty, or as pointing to plurality within God. The text does not explain it.
[^1-12]: The Syriac translation reads "all the wild animals of the earth".
[^1-13]: Literally "him", referring to humankind as a whole. The next line has "them".
[^1-14]: Hebrew *kavash*, "subdue".
[^1-15]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, "a living being", the phrase used for the animals in verses 20 and 24.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** So the heavens and the earth were completed, with everything in them.[^2-1]

**2** On the seventh day God finished the work he had been doing,[^2-2] and on the seventh day he rested[^2-3] from all the work he had done.

**3** God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work he had done in creating.[^2-4]

**4** These are the generations[^2-5] of the heavens and the earth when they were created. When[^2-6] the LORD God[^2-7] made the earth and the heavens,

**5** no shrub of the field was yet on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprung up, for the LORD God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no human being to work the ground.[^2-8]

**6** But a stream[^2-9] would rise from the earth and water the whole surface of the ground.

**7** Then the LORD God formed the man[^2-10] from the dust of the ground. He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being.[^2-11]

**8** The LORD God planted a garden in Eden,[^2-12] in the east,[^2-13] and there he put the man he had formed.

**9** Out of the ground the LORD God made every tree grow that is pleasing to look at and good for food, with the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.[^2-14]

**10** A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden. From there it divided and became four branches.[^2-15]

**11** The name of the first is Pishon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold.

**12** The gold of that land is good. Bdellium[^2-16] and onyx stone[^2-17] are there too.

**13** The name of the second river is Gihon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Cush.[^2-18]

**14** The name of the third river is the Tigris.[^2-19] It is the one that flows east of Assyria.[^2-20] And the fourth river is the Euphrates.[^2-21]

**15** The LORD God took the man and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and take care of it.[^2-22]

**16** The LORD God commanded the man, "You may freely eat from every tree in the garden,

**17** but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. For on the day you eat from it, you will certainly die."[^2-23]

**18** Then the LORD God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper who is right for him."[^2-24]

**19** So out of the ground the LORD God formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky,[^2-25] and he brought them to the man to see what he would call them. Whatever the man called each living creature,[^2-26] that was its name.

**20** The man gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the sky, and to every wild animal. But for the man[^2-27] no helper who was right for him was found.

**21** So the LORD God made a deep sleep fall on the man, and he slept. Then he took one of his ribs[^2-28] and closed up the flesh in its place.

**22** The LORD God built the rib he had taken from the man into a woman, and he brought her to the man.

**23** The man said, "This one at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh! She will be called 'woman,'[^2-29] because she was taken out of man."

**24** That is why a man leaves his father and his mother and holds fast to his wife, and they become one flesh.

**25** The man and his wife were both naked,[^2-30] and they felt no shame.

[^2-1]: Literally "and all their host", that is, everything that fills them.
[^2-2]: Or "By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing". The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "On the sixth day".
[^2-3]: Hebrew *shavat*, "stopped, ceased", the root of the word *Sabbath*. Also in verse 3.
[^2-4]: Literally "from all his work which God created to do".
[^2-5]: Hebrew *toledot*, from the verb "to father, give birth": what came from someone, their descendants and their story. The formula "These are the generations of …" heads each main section of Genesis.
[^2-6]: Literally "On the day".
[^2-7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. In chapters 2–3 it is usually joined with "God" (Elohim): "the LORD God".
[^2-8]: Hebrew *adam*, "human being", sounds like *adamah*, "ground".
[^2-9]: Hebrew *ed*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps a spring or flow of water. Traditionally "mist".
[^2-10]: Hebrew *ha-adam*, "the human", the same word as in verse 5. In chapters 2–3 it is not yet a personal name.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase used for the animals in 1:20, 24 and 30.
[^2-12]: *Eden* sounds like a Hebrew word meaning "delight" or "abundance".
[^2-13]: Or "long ago".
[^2-14]: Or "the tree of knowing good and evil".
[^2-15]: Literally "four heads".
[^2-16]: An aromatic resin. Some think it is a pearl.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *shoham*, a precious stone that cannot be identified with certainty.
[^2-18]: Usually the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia); here perhaps a region in Mesopotamia (compare 10:8).
[^2-19]: Hebrew *Hiddeqel*.
[^2-20]: Hebrew *Ashur*. Or "in front of Assyria".
[^2-21]: Hebrew *Perat*.
[^2-22]: Or "to serve it and guard it".
[^2-23]: Literally "dying you will die", an emphatic form.
[^2-24]: Hebrew *ezer kenegdo*, literally "a helper corresponding to him" or "opposite him". *Ezer*, "helper", is often used of God (Psalm 33:20). Also in verse 20.
[^2-25]: Or "Now out of the ground the LORD God had formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky".
[^2-26]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase translated "living being" in verse 7.
[^2-27]: The Hebrew vowel points read "for Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "for the man", as elsewhere in the chapter.
[^2-28]: Or "one of his sides". Hebrew *tsela* usually means a side, as of a building or the ark.
[^2-29]: Hebrew *ishah*, "woman", sounds like *ish*, "man".
[^2-30]: Hebrew *arummim*. In 3:1 the snake is *arum*, "crafty", a play on words.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Now the snake[^3-1] was more crafty[^3-2] than any wild animal that the LORD God[^3-3] had made. It said to the woman, "Did God really say, 'You must not eat from any tree in the garden'?"

**2** The woman said to the snake, "We may eat fruit from the trees in the garden,

**3** but about the fruit of the tree that is in the middle of the garden, God said, 'You must not eat from it or touch it, or you will die.'"

**4** But the snake said to the woman, "You will certainly not die!

**5** For God knows that on the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God,[^3-4] knowing good and evil."

**6** The woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was desirable for gaining wisdom.[^3-5] So she took some of its fruit and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.

**7** Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked. They sewed fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths.

**8** They heard the sound[^3-6] of the LORD God walking in the garden in the breeze of the day,[^3-7] and the man and his wife hid from the LORD God among the trees of the garden.

**9** But the LORD God called to the man and said to him, "Where are you?"

**10** He said, "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid."

**11** He said, "Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?"

**12** The man said, "The woman you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate."

**13** Then the LORD God said to the woman, "What is this you have done?" The woman said, "The snake deceived me, and I ate."

**14** The LORD God said to the snake, "Because you have done this, you are cursed more than all the livestock and all the wild animals.[^3-8] You will crawl on your belly, and you will eat dust all the days of your life.

**15** I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your offspring[^3-9] and her offspring. He[^3-10] will strike[^3-11] your head, and you will strike his heel."

**16** To the woman he said, "I will make your pain in pregnancy very great.[^3-12] In pain you will give birth to children. Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you."

**17** To the man[^3-13] he said, "Because you listened to your wife and ate from the tree I commanded you about, saying, 'You must not eat from it,' the ground is cursed because of you. With painful toil you will eat from it all the days of your life.

**18** It will produce thorns and thistles for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**19** By the sweat of your brow[^3-14] you will eat bread until you return to the ground, for from it you were taken. You are dust, and to dust you will return."

**20** The man named his wife Eve,[^3-15] because she became the mother of everyone living.

**21** The LORD God made garments of skin for the man[^3-16] and his wife, and he clothed them.

**22** Then the LORD God said, "Look, the man has become like one of us, knowing good and evil. And now, he might reach out his hand and take from the tree of life too, and eat, and live forever!"[^3-17]

**23** So the LORD God sent him out of the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken.

**24** He drove the man out. And to the east of the garden of Eden[^3-18] he stationed the cherubim[^3-19] and a flaming sword that turned every way,[^3-20] to guard the way to the tree of life.

[^3-1]: Hebrew *nachash*, traditionally "serpent".
[^3-2]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, crafty", sounds like *arummim*, "naked" (2:25; 3:7).
[^3-3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-4]: Or "like gods", "like divine beings". Hebrew *elohim* can mean God or gods.
[^3-5]: Or "desirable to make one wise".
[^3-6]: Or "voice"; also in verse 10.
[^3-7]: That is, at the cool, breezy time of day, toward evening. Hebrew *ruach* means wind or breath.
[^3-8]: Or "you are cursed and set apart from all the livestock and all the wild animals".
[^3-9]: Literally "seed", here and in the next phrase. The Hebrew word is singular and can mean one descendant or many.
[^3-10]: Or "It", referring to the offspring.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *shuph*, used for both actions in this verse. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "crush", "bruise" or "strike at".
[^3-12]: Literally "I will greatly multiply your pain and your pregnancy".
[^3-13]: The Hebrew vowel points read "To Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "To the man", as elsewhere in chapters 2–3.
[^3-14]: Literally "face".
[^3-15]: Hebrew *Chavvah*, which sounds like the Hebrew word for "living".
[^3-16]: As in verse 17, the vowel points read "for Adam".
[^3-17]: The Hebrew sentence breaks off: "And now, so that he does not reach out his hand …".
[^3-18]: Or "in front of the garden of Eden".
[^3-19]: Winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (compare Exodus 25:18–22; Ezekiel 10).
[^3-20]: Literally "the flame of the sword that turned itself".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** The man had sexual relations with[^4-1] his wife Eve, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Cain.[^4-2] She said, "I have gotten a man with the help of the LORD."[^4-3]

**2** Then she gave birth again, to his brother Abel.[^4-4] Abel kept flocks, and Cain worked the ground.

**3** In the course of time[^4-5] Cain brought some of the produce of the ground as an offering[^4-6] to the LORD.

**4** And Abel, for his part, brought some of the firstborn of his flock and some of their fat portions. The LORD looked with favor on Abel and his offering,

**5** but on Cain and his offering he did not look with favor. So Cain was very angry, and his face fell.

**6** The LORD said to Cain, "Why are you angry? Why has your face fallen?

**7** If you do what is right, will you not be accepted?[^4-7] But if you do not do what is right, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, but you must rule over it."[^4-8]

**8** Cain spoke to his brother Abel.[^4-9] And while they were in the field, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.

**9** Then the LORD said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I don't know. Am I my brother's keeper?"

**10** Then he said, "What have you done? Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground![^4-10]

**11** Now you are cursed from the ground,[^4-11] which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**12** When you work the ground, it will no longer give you its strength. You will be a restless wanderer[^4-12] on the earth."

**13** Cain said to the LORD, "My punishment is too great to bear.[^4-13]

**14** Look, today you are driving me away from the face of the ground, and I will be hidden from your presence. I will be a restless wanderer on the earth, and whoever finds me will kill me."

**15** But the LORD said to him, "Therefore,[^4-14] whoever kills Cain will suffer vengeance seven times over." And the LORD put a mark on Cain,[^4-15] so that no one who found him would strike him down.

**16** So Cain went out from the LORD's presence and lived in the land of Nod,[^4-16] east of Eden.

**17** Cain had sexual relations with his wife, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Enoch. He was building a city, and he named the city after his son Enoch.

**18** Irad was born to Enoch. Irad fathered Mehujael, Mehujael[^4-17] fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**19** Lamech married two wives. The name of one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**20** Adah gave birth to Jabal. He was the father of those who live in tents and keep livestock.[^4-18]

**21** His brother's name was Jubal. He was the father of all who play the lyre and the pipe.[^4-19]

**22** Zillah also gave birth, to Tubal-cain, who forged every kind of tool of bronze and iron.[^4-20] Tubal-cain's sister was Naamah.

**23** Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, listen to my voice; wives of Lamech, hear what I say: I have killed a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me.

**24** If Cain is avenged seven times, then Lamech seventy-seven times."

**25** Adam[^4-21] had sexual relations with his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth.[^4-22] "For God has granted me another child[^4-23] in place of Abel," she said, "since Cain killed him."

**26** A son was born to Seth too, and he named him Enosh.[^4-24] At that time people began to call on the name of the LORD.[^4-25]

[^4-1]: Literally "knew"; also in verses 17 and 25.
[^4-2]: *Cain* sounds like the Hebrew *qaniti*, "I have gotten".
[^4-3]: Literally "with the LORD". *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *Hevel*, which means "breath" or "vapor".
[^4-5]: Literally "At the end of days".
[^4-6]: Hebrew *minchah*, a gift or tribute; also in verses 4 and 5.
[^4-7]: Literally "is there not a lifting up?", that is, of your face (compare verses 5–6), or of your offering.
[^4-8]: Or "and you will rule over it". The words are the same as those spoken to the woman in 3:16.
[^4-9]: Literally "Cain said to his brother Abel", with no words following. The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate add "Let us go out into the field."
[^4-10]: Literally "The voice of your brother's blood is crying out". The word "blood" is plural in the Hebrew here and in verse 11.
[^4-11]: Or "banished from the ground".
[^4-12]: Hebrew *na va-nad*; also in verse 14.
[^4-13]: Or "My guilt is too great to be forgiven". Hebrew *avon* means both guilt and its punishment.
[^4-14]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "Not so!"
[^4-15]: Literally "set a sign for Cain".
[^4-16]: *Nod* means "wandering" (compare verses 12 and 14).
[^4-17]: Hebrew *Mehijael*, a different spelling of the same name.
[^4-18]: *Father* here means the ancestor or founder of a way of life.
[^4-19]: Hebrew *kinnor*, a stringed instrument (traditionally "harp"), and *ugav*, a wind instrument (traditionally "flute").
[^4-20]: Literally "a hammerer of every cutting tool of bronze and iron". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^4-21]: Here, without the article, *adam* is used as a personal name.
[^4-22]: Hebrew *Shet*, which sounds like *shat*, "granted".
[^4-23]: Literally "another seed".
[^4-24]: *Enosh* means "human being".
[^4-25]: That is, to worship the LORD by calling on his name. Or "to proclaim the name of the LORD".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** This is the book of the generations[^5-1] of Adam. When God created humankind,[^5-2] he made them[^5-3] in the likeness of God.

**2** He created them male and female. He blessed them, and on the day they were created he named them "humankind."[^5-4]

**3** When Adam had lived 130 years,[^5-5] he fathered a son in his own likeness, according to his image, and he named him Seth.

**4** After he fathered Seth, Adam lived 800 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**5** Adam lived 930 years in all, and then he died.

**6** When Seth had lived 105 years, he fathered Enosh.

**7** After he fathered Enosh, Seth lived 807 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**8** Seth lived 912 years in all, and then he died.

**9** When Enosh had lived 90 years, he fathered Kenan.

**10** After he fathered Kenan, Enosh lived 815 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11** Enosh lived 905 years in all, and then he died.

**12** When Kenan had lived 70 years, he fathered Mahalalel.

**13** After he fathered Mahalalel, Kenan lived 840 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**14** Kenan lived 910 years in all, and then he died.

**15** When Mahalalel had lived 65 years, he fathered Jared.

**16** After he fathered Jared, Mahalalel lived 830 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**17** Mahalalel lived 895 years in all, and then he died.

**18** When Jared had lived 162 years, he fathered Enoch.

**19** After he fathered Enoch, Jared lived 800 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**20** Jared lived 962 years in all, and then he died.

**21** When Enoch had lived 65 years, he fathered Methuselah.

**22** After he fathered Methuselah, Enoch walked with God[^5-6] 300 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**23** Enoch lived 365 years in all.

**24** Enoch walked with God, and then he was no more, because God took him.

**25** When Methuselah had lived 187 years, he fathered Lamech.

**26** After he fathered Lamech, Methuselah lived 782 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**27** Methuselah lived 969 years in all, and then he died.

**28** When Lamech had lived 182 years, he fathered a son.

**29** He named him Noah,[^5-7] saying, "This one will comfort us in our work and in the painful toil of our hands, caused by the ground that the LORD[^5-8] has cursed."

**30** After he fathered Noah, Lamech lived 595 years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**31** Lamech lived 777 years in all, and then he died.

**32** When Noah was 500 years old, he fathered Shem, Ham and Japheth.

[^5-1]: Hebrew *toledot*; see 2:4. Here the formula begins "This is the book of …", that is, the written record.
[^5-2]: Hebrew *adam*, as in 1:26–27.
[^5-3]: Literally "him", referring to humankind as a whole, as in 1:27.
[^5-4]: Hebrew *adam*, which is also the name Adam.
[^5-5]: In this chapter the Hebrew states each age in full, for example "thirty and a hundred years". The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch give different figures for several of the ages; the Septuagint, for example, has Adam father Seth at 230 years and live 700 years afterward.
[^5-6]: That is, lived in close fellowship with God. Also in verse 24.
[^5-7]: *Noah* means "rest" and sounds like *yenachamenu*, "he will comfort us". The Septuagint reads "This one will give us rest".
[^5-8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** When human beings began to multiply on the face of the ground, and daughters were born to them,

**2** the sons of God[^6-1] saw that the daughters of humankind were beautiful,[^6-2] and they took as wives any of them they chose.

**3** Then the LORD[^6-3] said, "My spirit will not remain[^6-4] in human beings forever, for they are flesh.[^6-5] Their days will be 120 years."

**4** The Nephilim[^6-6] were on the earth in those days, and also afterward, when the sons of God slept with[^6-7] the daughters of humankind and they bore children to them. These were the mighty men of old, the famous men.[^6-8]

**5** The LORD saw that the wickedness of human beings on the earth was great, and that every inclination of the thoughts of their hearts was only evil all the time.

**6** The LORD regretted that he had made human beings on the earth, and he was grieved to his heart.

**7** So the LORD said, "I will wipe out from the face of the ground the human beings I have created: people together with livestock, creatures that move along the ground, and birds of the sky. For I regret that I made them."

**8** But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD.

**1** About that time Judah left his brothers and went down to stay[^38-1] with a man of Adullam named Hirah.

**2** There Judah saw the daughter of a Canaanite man named Shua. He married her and slept with her.[^38-2]

**3** She became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and he named him Er.

**4** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son, and she named him Onan.

**5** She gave birth to yet another son and named him Shelah. He was at Chezib when she gave birth to him.[^38-3]

**6** Judah took a wife for Er, his firstborn, and her name was Tamar.

**7** But Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the sight of the LORD,[^38-4] so the LORD put him to death.

**8** Then Judah said to Onan, "Sleep with your brother's wife and do your duty to her as her husband's brother.[^38-5] Raise up children[^38-6] for your brother."

**9** But Onan knew that the children would not be his. So whenever he slept with his brother's wife, he spilled his semen on the ground,[^38-7] so that he would not give children to his brother.

**10** What he did was evil in the sight of the LORD, so he put him to death too.

**11** Then Judah said to his daughter-in-law Tamar, "Live as a widow in your father's house until my son Shelah grows up." For he thought, "He too may die like his brothers." So Tamar went and lived in her father's house.

**12** A long time passed, and Shua's daughter, Judah's wife, died. When Judah had been comforted, he went up to Timnah to the men who were shearing his sheep, he and his friend Hirah the Adullamite.

**13** Tamar was told, "Your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his sheep."

**14** So she took off her widow's clothes, covered herself with a veil, and wrapped herself up. Then she sat down at the entrance to Enaim,[^38-8] which is on the road to Timnah. For she saw that Shelah had grown up, and she had not been given to him as his wife.

**15** When Judah saw her, he thought she was a prostitute, because she had covered her face.

**16** He turned aside to her by the road and said, "Come, let me sleep with you," for he did not know that she was his daughter-in-law. She said, "What will you give me for sleeping with me?"

**17** He said, "I will send you a young goat from the flock." She said, "Only if you give me a pledge until you send it."

**18** He said, "What pledge shall I give you?" She said, "Your seal[^38-9] and its cord, and the staff in your hand." So he gave them to her and slept with her, and she became pregnant by him.

**19** Then she got up and left. She took off her veil and put on her widow's clothes again.

**20** Judah sent the young goat by his friend the Adullamite to get the pledge back from the woman, but he did not find her.

**21** He asked the men of that place, "Where is the shrine prostitute[^38-10] who was by the road at Enaim?" They said, "There has been no shrine prostitute here."

**22** So he went back to Judah and said, "I did not find her. Also, the men of the place said, 'There has been no shrine prostitute here.'"

**23** Judah said, "Let her keep the things, or we will become a laughingstock. After all, I did send this young goat, but you could not find her."

**24** About three months later Judah was told, "Your daughter-in-law Tamar has acted as a prostitute, and now she is pregnant as a result of her prostitution." Judah said, "Bring her out, and let her be burned!"

**25** As she was being brought out, she sent word to her father-in-law: "I am pregnant by the man who owns these things." And she said, "Please look closely. Whose are these, the seal, the cord and the staff?"

**26** Judah recognized them and said, "She is more in the right than I am, because I did not give her to my son Shelah." And he did not sleep with her[^38-11] again.

**27** When the time came for her to give birth, there were twins in her womb.

**28** While she was giving birth, one of them put out a hand. The midwife took a scarlet thread and tied it on his hand, saying, "This one came out first."

**29** But as he drew back his hand, his brother came out. She said, "What a breach you have made for yourself!" So he was named Perez.[^38-12]

**30** Afterward his brother came out, with the scarlet thread on his hand, and he was named Zerah.[^38-13]

[^38-1]: Literally "turned aside".
[^38-2]: Literally "took her and went in to her". "Went in to" is a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations; also in verses 8, 9, 16 and 18.
[^38-3]: Or "It was at Chezib that she gave birth to him." The Septuagint reads "She was at Chezib".
[^38-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^38-5]: Hebrew *yabbem*: to marry the widow of a brother who had died without a son, so that a son born to her would carry on the dead brother's name (Deuteronomy 25:5–10).
[^38-6]: Literally "seed", meaning offspring. Also in verse 9.
[^38-7]: Literally "he spoiled it on the ground".
[^38-8]: Hebrew *petach Enayim*, "the entrance of Enaim". *Enaim* means "two springs" or "two eyes", so the phrase may also mean "in an open place". Also in verse 21.
[^38-9]: A small engraved stone or cylinder, worn on a cord, used to sign documents by pressing it into clay. Together with the staff it identified its owner.
[^38-10]: Hebrew *qedeshah*, literally "holy woman", a woman connected with a shrine. Verses 15 and 24 use the ordinary words for a prostitute (*zonah*) and prostitution. Whether a *qedeshah* was a prostitute who served at a shrine is debated. Also in verse 22.
[^38-11]: Literally "know her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^38-12]: *Perez* means "breach" or "bursting out".
[^38-13]: *Zerah* means "dawning" or "shining", perhaps recalling the bright scarlet thread.

**16** She kept his garment beside her until his master came home.

**23** The warden paid no attention to anything in Joseph's care, because the LORD was with him, and the LORD made whatever he did succeed.

[^39-2]: Literally "made everything he did succeed in his hand".
[^39-3]: Literally "he did not know anything with him except the bread he ate". This may mean that Potiphar paid attention only to his meals, or that food was the one thing Joseph did not handle, perhaps because of Egyptian rules about eating (compare 43:32).

## Chapter 40

**1** Some time later, the cupbearer[^40-1] and the baker of the king of Egypt offended their master, the king of Egypt.

**2** Pharaoh was angry with his two officials,[^40-2] the chief cupbearer and the chief baker,

**3** and he put them in custody in the house of the captain of the guard, in the prison where Joseph was being held.

**4** The captain of the guard assigned Joseph to them, and he attended them. They were in custody for some time.

**5** One night both of them, the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were being held in the prison, had a dream. Each had his own dream, and each dream had its own meaning.

**6** When Joseph came to them in the morning, he saw that they were upset.

**7** So he asked Pharaoh's officials who were in custody with him in his master's house, "Why do you look so troubled today?"[^40-3]

**8** They said to him, "We have each had a dream, and there is no one to interpret it." Joseph said to them, "Don't interpretations belong to God? Please tell me your dreams."

**9** So the chief cupbearer told Joseph his dream. He said to him, "In my dream there was a vine in front of me.

**10** On the vine were three branches. As soon as it budded, its blossoms came out, and its clusters ripened into grapes.

**11** Pharaoh's cup was in my hand. I took the grapes, squeezed them into Pharaoh's cup, and put the cup in Pharaoh's hand."

**12** Joseph said to him, "This is what it means: the three branches are three days.

**13** Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head[^40-4] and restore you to your position. You will put Pharaoh's cup in his hand, just as you used to do when you were his cupbearer.

**14** But remember me when things go well for you. Please show me faithful love:[^40-5] mention me to Pharaoh, and get me out of this place.

**15** For I was kidnapped from the land of the Hebrews, and even here I have done nothing to deserve being put in this dungeon."[^40-6]

**16** When the chief baker saw that the interpretation was favorable, he said to Joseph, "I too had a dream. There were three baskets of white bread[^40-7] on my head.

**17** In the top basket there were all kinds of baked food for Pharaoh, but the birds were eating it out of the basket on my head."

**18** Joseph answered, "This is what it means: the three baskets are three days.

**19** Within three days Pharaoh will lift up your head from you and hang you on a tree,[^40-8] and the birds will eat the flesh off you."

**20** The third day was Pharaoh's birthday, and he gave a feast for all his servants. In the presence of his servants he lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and the head of the chief baker.

**21** He restored the chief cupbearer to his position, and he put the cup in Pharaoh's hand again.

**22** But he hanged the chief baker, just as Joseph had interpreted to them.

**23** Yet the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph; he forgot him.

[^40-1]: Literally "the one who gives drink", the official who served the king his wine.
[^40-2]: Hebrew *sarisim*, court officials. The word can also mean "eunuchs". Also in verse 7.
[^40-3]: Literally "Why are your faces bad today?"
[^40-4]: "Lift up your head" means to honor or pardon someone. In verse 19 the same words are turned around to mean beheading, and in verse 20 they are used of both men.
[^40-5]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love, here the loyalty Joseph hopes for in return for his help.
[^40-6]: Hebrew *bor*, "pit, cistern", the word used of the pit Joseph's brothers threw him into (37:20–29).
[^40-7]: Or "three wicker baskets". The meaning of the Hebrew word *chori* is uncertain.
[^40-8]: Or "impale you on a pole". The hanging may have followed the beheading, with the body put on display.

## Chapter 41

**1** Two full years later, Pharaoh had a dream. He was standing by the Nile.[^41-1]

**2** Seven cows came up out of the Nile, sleek and fat,[^41-2] and they grazed among the reeds.

**3** After them seven other cows came up out of the Nile, ugly and thin, and they stood beside the other cows on the bank of the Nile.

**4** The ugly, thin cows ate up the seven sleek, fat cows. Then Pharaoh woke up.

**5** He fell asleep again and had a second dream. Seven heads of grain, plump and good, were growing on a single stalk.

**6** After them seven other heads of grain sprouted, thin and scorched by the east wind.

**7** The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven plump, full heads. Then Pharaoh woke up, and it had been a dream.

**8** In the morning his spirit was troubled, so he sent for all the magicians[^41-3] of Egypt and all its wise men. Pharaoh told them his dream, but there was no one who could interpret them for Pharaoh.[^41-4]

**9** Then the chief cupbearer spoke to Pharaoh. "Today I must mention my offenses," he said.

**10** "Pharaoh was angry with his servants, and he put me and the chief baker in custody in the house of the captain of the guard.

**11** One night we both had dreams, he and I, and each dream had its own meaning.

**12** A young Hebrew man was there with us, a servant of the captain of the guard. We told him our dreams, and he interpreted them for us, giving each of us the meaning of his own dream.

**13** And it turned out exactly as he interpreted it to us: I was restored to my position, and the baker was hanged."[^41-5]

**14** So Pharaoh sent for Joseph, and they quickly brought him out of the dungeon.[^41-6] He shaved, changed his clothes, and came in to Pharaoh.

**15** Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I have had a dream, and no one can interpret it. But I have heard it said of you that when you hear a dream, you can interpret it."

**16** Joseph answered Pharaoh, "It is not in me. God will give Pharaoh an answer for his good."[^41-7]

**17** Then Pharaoh said to Joseph, "In my dream I was standing on the bank of the Nile.

**18** Seven cows came up out of the Nile, fat and sleek, and they grazed among the reeds.

**19** After them seven other cows came up, scrawny, very ugly and thin. I have never seen such ugly cows in all the land of Egypt.

**20** The thin, ugly cows ate up the first seven fat cows.

**21** But when they had eaten them,[^41-8] no one could tell that they had eaten them; they looked just as ugly as before. Then I woke up.

**22** In my dream I also saw seven heads of grain, full and good, growing on a single stalk.

**23** After them seven other heads of grain sprouted, withered, thin and scorched by the east wind.

**24** The thin heads of grain swallowed up the seven good heads. I told this to the magicians, but no one could explain it to me."

**25** Then Joseph said to Pharaoh, "Pharaoh's dreams are one and the same.[^41-9] God has told Pharaoh what he is about to do.

**26** The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good heads of grain are seven years. It is one dream.

**27** The seven thin, ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and so are the seven empty heads of grain scorched by the east wind. They will be seven years of famine.

**28** It is just as I told Pharaoh: God has shown Pharaoh what he is about to do.

**29** Seven years of great plenty are coming throughout the land of Egypt.

**30** But after them will come seven years of famine. All the plenty in the land of Egypt will be forgotten, and the famine will exhaust the land.

**31** The plenty in the land will not be remembered because of the famine that follows, for it will be very severe.

**32** The dream was given to Pharaoh twice because the matter has been firmly decided by God, and God will soon make it happen.

**33** So now let Pharaoh look for a man who is discerning and wise and put him in charge of the land of Egypt.

**34** Let Pharaoh act and appoint overseers over the land, and let him take a fifth[^41-10] of the produce of the land of Egypt during the seven years of plenty.

**35** Let them gather all the food of these good years that are coming, and store up grain under Pharaoh's authority, to be kept as food in the cities.

**36** This food will be a reserve for the land for the seven years of famine that will come on the land of Egypt, so that the land will not be destroyed by the famine."

**37** The proposal pleased Pharaoh and all his servants.

**38** So Pharaoh said to his servants, "Can we find anyone like this, a man in whom is the spirit of God?"[^41-11]

**39** Then Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has made all this known to you, there is no one as discerning and wise as you.

**40** You will be in charge of my household, and all my people will submit to your orders.[^41-12] Only in the matter of the throne will I be greater than you."

**41** Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Look, I have put you in charge of the whole land of Egypt."

**42** Then Pharaoh took his signet ring off his hand and put it on Joseph's hand. He dressed him in robes of fine linen and put a gold chain around his neck.

**43** He had him ride in the chariot of his second-in-command,[^41-13] and they shouted before him, "Abrek!"[^41-14] So he put him in charge of the whole land of Egypt.

**44** Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh, but without your consent no one will lift a hand or foot in all the land of Egypt."

**45** Pharaoh gave Joseph the name Zaphenath-paneah,[^41-15] and he gave him Asenath daughter of Potiphera, priest of On,[^41-16] as his wife. And Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

**47** During the seven years of plenty the land produced abundant crops.[^41-18]

**48** Joseph gathered all the food of the seven years that came in the land of Egypt, and he stored the food in the cities. In each city he stored the food from the fields around it.

**49** Joseph stored up grain in great quantities, like the sand of the sea. There was so much that he stopped measuring it, because it was beyond measure.

**50** Before the years of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph, whom Asenath daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, bore to him.

**51** Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh,[^41-19] "because God has made me forget all my trouble and all my father's household."

**52** He named the second Ephraim,[^41-20] "because God has made me fruitful in the land of my suffering."

**53** The seven years of plenty in the land of Egypt came to an end,

**54** and the seven years of famine began, just as Joseph had said. There was famine in all the other lands, but in the whole land of Egypt there was food.

**55** When the whole land of Egypt began to go hungry, the people cried out to Pharaoh for food. Pharaoh told all the Egyptians, "Go to Joseph, and do whatever he tells you."

**57** People from all over the world[^41-22] came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph, because the famine was severe all over the world.

[^48-2]: Literally "Rachel died upon me". It may mean "to my sorrow" or "on my hands".
[^48-3]: Literally "from with his knees". The boys had been placed on, or between, Jacob's knees, a sign of adoption (compare 30:3; 50:23).

[^48-5]: Hebrew *go'el*, a relative who rescues or redeems a family member from trouble or slavery.

**1** Joseph threw himself on his father's face. He wept over him and kissed him.

**2** Then Joseph ordered his servants, the physicians, to embalm his father. So the physicians embalmed Israel.

**3** It took forty days, for that is the full time needed for embalming. And the Egyptians wept for him seventy days.

**4** When the days of weeping for him were over, Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household. "If I have found favor with you," he said, "please speak to Pharaoh for me[^50-1] and tell him:

**5** 'My father made me swear an oath. He said, "I am about to die. Bury me in the tomb I dug[^50-2] for myself in the land of Canaan." So now please let me go up and bury my father, and then I will come back.'"

**6** Pharaoh said, "Go up and bury your father, as he made you swear."

**7** So Joseph went up to bury his father. All Pharaoh's officials went up with him, the elders of his household and all the elders of the land of Egypt,

**8** together with all Joseph's household, his brothers and his father's household. Only their little children, their flocks and their cattle were left in the land of Goshen.

**9** Chariots and horsemen also went up with him. It was a very large company.

**10** When they came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, they lamented there with a very great and bitter lamentation. And Joseph observed seven days of mourning for his father.

**11** When the Canaanites who lived in the land saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad, they said, "This is a solemn mourning for the Egyptians." That is why the place beyond the Jordan was named Abel-mizraim.[^50-3]

**12** So Jacob's sons did for him as he had commanded them.

**13** His sons carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre, the field that Abraham had bought from Ephron the Hittite as a burial site of his own.

**14** After he had buried his father, Joseph returned to Egypt, he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father.

**15** When Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead, they said, "What if Joseph bears a grudge against us and pays us back in full for all the wrong we did to him?"

**16** So they sent word to Joseph: "Before your father died, he gave these instructions:

**17** 'This is what you are to say to Joseph: Please forgive the crime of your brothers and their sin, for they did you wrong.' So now please forgive the crime of the servants of the God of your father." Joseph wept when they spoke to him.

**18** Then his brothers also came and threw themselves down before him. They said, "We are your slaves."

**19** But Joseph said to them, "Don't be afraid. Am I in the place of God?

**20** You planned evil against me, but God planned it for good, to bring about what is happening today: to keep many people alive.

**21** So now, don't be afraid. I will provide for you and your little children." And he comforted them and spoke kindly to them.[^50-4]

**22** Joseph stayed in Egypt, he and his father's household. Joseph lived 110 years.

**23** Joseph saw Ephraim's children to the third generation. The children of Machir son of Manasseh were also born on Joseph's knees.[^50-5]

**24** Then Joseph said to his brothers, "I am about to die. But God will surely come to your aid[^50-6] and bring you up out of this land to the land he promised on oath to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob."

**25** Joseph made Israel's sons[^50-7] swear an oath. He said, "God will surely come to your aid, and then you must carry my bones up from here."

**26** So Joseph died at the age of 110. They embalmed him, and he was placed in a coffin in Egypt.
