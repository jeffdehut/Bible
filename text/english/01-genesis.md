## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.[^1-1]

**2** The earth was formless and empty,[^1-2] and darkness covered the surface of the deep. The Spirit of God[^1-3] was hovering over the surface of the waters.

**3** Then God said, "Let there be light," and there was light.

**4** God saw that the light was good. Then God separated the light from the darkness.

**5** God called the light "day," and he called the darkness "night." There was evening, and there was morning: the first day.[^1-4]

**6** Then God said, "Let there be a dome[^1-5] in the middle of the waters, to separate water from water."

**7** So God made the dome and separated the water under the dome from the water above the dome. And it was so.

**8** God called the dome "sky."[^1-6] There was evening, and there was morning: the second day.

**9** Then God said, "Let the water under the sky be gathered into one place, and let dry ground appear." And it was so.

**10** God called the dry ground "land,"[^1-7] and he called the gathered waters "seas." And God saw that it was good.

**11** Then God said, "Let the land produce vegetation: plants that bear seed, and trees on the land that bear fruit with their seed in it, each according to its kind." And it was so.

**12** The land produced vegetation: plants bearing seed according to their kinds, and trees bearing fruit with their seed in it, according to their kinds. And God saw that it was good.

**13** There was evening, and there was morning: the third day.

**14** Then God said, "Let there be lights in the dome of the sky to separate the day from the night. Let them be signs to mark the appointed times,[^1-8] the days and the years.

**15** Let them be lights in the dome of the sky to give light on the earth." And it was so.

**16** God made the two great lights: the greater light to rule the day and the lesser light to rule the night. He also made the stars.

**17** God placed them in the dome of the sky to give light on the earth,

**18** to rule over the day and the night, and to separate the light from the darkness. And God saw that it was good.

**19** There was evening, and there was morning: the fourth day.

**20** Then God said, "Let the waters swarm with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the dome of the sky."

**21** So God created the great sea creatures[^1-9] and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarm, according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**22** God blessed them and said, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters of the seas. And let the birds multiply on the earth."

**23** There was evening, and there was morning: the fifth day.

**24** Then God said, "Let the land produce living creatures according to their kinds: livestock, creatures that move along the ground, and wild animals, each according to its kind." And it was so.

**25** God made the wild animals according to their kinds, the livestock according to their kinds, and every creature that moves along the ground according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**26** Then God said, "Let us make human beings[^1-10] in our image, to be like us.[^1-11] Let them rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, the livestock, all the earth,[^1-12] and every creature that moves along the ground."

**27** So God created human beings in his image. In the image of God he created them;[^1-13] male and female he created them.

**28** God blessed them and said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply. Fill the earth and bring it under your control.[^1-14] Rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of the sky, and every living creature that moves on the earth."

**29** Then God said, "Look, I have given you every plant on the face of the whole earth that bears seed, and every tree that has fruit with seed in it. They will be your food.

**30** And to every wild animal of the earth, every bird of the sky, and every creature that moves on the earth, everything that has the breath of life in it,[^1-15] I have given every green plant for food." And it was so.

**31** God saw everything he had made, and it was very good. There was evening, and there was morning: the sixth day.

[^1-1]: Or "When God began to create the heavens and the earth, the earth was formless …". The Hebrew can be read as a heading for the whole account, as here, or as a time clause leading into verse 2. *The heavens and the earth* is a Hebrew way of saying "everything".
[^1-2]: Hebrew *tohu va-vohu*, a rhyming pair: a shapeless, empty waste.
[^1-3]: Or "a wind from God", "a mighty wind". Hebrew *ruach* means spirit, wind or breath.
[^1-4]: Literally "one day".
[^1-5]: Hebrew *raqia*, something hammered or spread out: the sky pictured as a solid vault. Traditionally "firmament"; also "expanse".
[^1-6]: Or "heavens", the same Hebrew word as in verse 1.
[^1-7]: Or "earth", the same Hebrew word as in verse 1.
[^1-8]: Hebrew *mo'adim*: fixed times, including seasons and festivals. Or "Let them serve as signs, and to mark seasons, days and years."
[^1-9]: Hebrew *tanninim*. Elsewhere the word also means sea monsters or serpents.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *adam*, "humankind". Here it is not yet the name Adam.
[^1-11]: Literally "in our image, according to our likeness". The plural "us" has been understood as God speaking to a heavenly court, as a plural of majesty, or as pointing to plurality within God. The text does not explain it.
[^1-12]: The Syriac translation reads "all the wild animals of the earth".
[^1-13]: Literally "him", referring to humankind as a whole. The next line has "them".
[^1-14]: Hebrew *kavash*, "subdue".
[^1-15]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, "a living being", the phrase used for the animals in verses 20 and 24.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** So the heavens and the earth were completed, with everything in them.[^2-1]

**2** On the seventh day God finished the work he had been doing,[^2-2] and on the seventh day he rested[^2-3] from all the work he had done.

**3** God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work he had done in creating.[^2-4]

**4** These are the generations[^2-5] of the heavens and the earth when they were created. When[^2-6] Yahweh God[^2-7] made the earth and the heavens,

**5** no shrub of the field was yet on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprung up, for Yahweh God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no human being to work the ground.[^2-8]

**6** But a stream[^2-9] would rise from the earth and water the whole surface of the ground.

**7** Then Yahweh God formed the man[^2-10] from the dust of the ground. He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being.[^2-11]

**8** Yahweh God planted a garden in Eden,[^2-12] in the east,[^2-13] and there he put the man he had formed.

**9** Out of the ground Yahweh God made every tree grow that is pleasing to look at and good for food, with the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.[^2-14]

**10** A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden. From there it divided and became four branches.[^2-15]

**11** The name of the first is Pishon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold.

**12** The gold of that land is good. Bdellium[^2-16] and onyx stone[^2-17] are there too.

**13** The name of the second river is Gihon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Cush.[^2-18]

**14** The name of the third river is the Tigris.[^2-19] It is the one that flows east of Assyria.[^2-20] And the fourth river is the Euphrates.[^2-21]

**15** Yahweh God took the man and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and take care of it.[^2-22]

**16** Yahweh God commanded the man, "You may freely eat from every tree in the garden,

**17** but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. For on the day you eat from it, you will certainly die."[^2-23]

**18** Then Yahweh God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper who is right for him."[^2-24]

**19** So out of the ground Yahweh God formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky,[^2-25] and he brought them to the man to see what he would call them. Whatever the man called each living creature,[^2-26] that was its name.

**20** The man gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the sky, and to every wild animal. But for the man[^2-27] no helper who was right for him was found.

**21** So Yahweh God made a deep sleep fall on the man, and he slept. Then he took one of his ribs[^2-28] and closed up the flesh in its place.

**22** Yahweh God built the rib he had taken from the man into a woman, and he brought her to the man.

**23** The man said, "This one at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh! She will be called 'woman,'[^2-29] because she was taken out of man."

**24** That is why a man leaves his father and his mother and holds fast to his wife, and they become one flesh.

**25** The man and his wife were both naked,[^2-30] and they felt no shame.

[^2-1]: Literally "and all their host", that is, everything that fills them.
[^2-2]: Or "By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing". The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "On the sixth day".
[^2-3]: Hebrew *shavat*, "stopped, ceased", the root of the word *Sabbath*. Also in verse 3.
[^2-4]: Literally "from all his work which God created to do".
[^2-5]: Hebrew *toledot*, from the verb "to father, give birth": what came from someone, their descendants and their story. The formula "These are the generations of …" heads each main section of Genesis.
[^2-6]: Literally "On the day".
[^2-7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. In chapters 2–3 it is usually joined with "God" (Elohim): "Yahweh God".
[^2-8]: Hebrew *adam*, "human being", sounds like *adamah*, "ground".
[^2-9]: Hebrew *ed*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps a spring or flow of water. Traditionally "mist".
[^2-10]: Hebrew *ha-adam*, "the human", the same word as in verse 5. In chapters 2–3 it is not yet a personal name.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase used for the animals in 1:20, 24 and 30.
[^2-12]: *Eden* sounds like a Hebrew word meaning "delight" or "abundance".
[^2-13]: Or "long ago".
[^2-14]: Or "the tree of knowing good and evil".
[^2-15]: Literally "four heads".
[^2-16]: An aromatic resin. Some think it is a pearl.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *shoham*, a precious stone that cannot be identified with certainty.
[^2-18]: Usually the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia); here perhaps a region in Mesopotamia (compare 10:8).
[^2-19]: Hebrew *Hiddeqel*.
[^2-20]: Hebrew *Ashur*. Or "in front of Assyria".
[^2-21]: Hebrew *Perat*.
[^2-22]: Or "to serve it and guard it".
[^2-23]: Literally "dying you will die", an emphatic form.
[^2-24]: Hebrew *ezer kenegdo*, literally "a helper corresponding to him" or "opposite him". *Ezer*, "helper", is often used of God (Psalm 33:20). Also in verse 20.
[^2-25]: Or "Now out of the ground Yahweh God had formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky".
[^2-26]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase translated "living being" in verse 7.
[^2-27]: The Hebrew vowel points read "for Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "for the man", as elsewhere in the chapter.
[^2-28]: Or "one of his sides". Hebrew *tsela* usually means a side, as of a building or the ark.
[^2-29]: Hebrew *ishah*, "woman", sounds like *ish*, "man".
[^2-30]: Hebrew *arummim*. In 3:1 the snake is *arum*, "crafty", a play on words.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Now the snake[^3-1] was more crafty[^3-2] than any wild animal that Yahweh God[^3-3] had made. It said to the woman, "Did God really say, 'You must not eat from any tree in the garden'?"

**2** The woman said to the snake, "We may eat fruit from the trees in the garden,

**3** but about the fruit of the tree that is in the middle of the garden, God said, 'You must not eat from it or touch it, or you will die.'"

**4** But the snake said to the woman, "You will certainly not die!

**5** For God knows that on the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God,[^3-4] knowing good and evil."

**6** The woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was desirable for gaining wisdom.[^3-5] So she took some of its fruit and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.

**7** Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked. They sewed fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths.

**8** They heard the sound[^3-6] of Yahweh God walking in the garden in the breeze of the day,[^3-7] and the man and his wife hid from Yahweh God among the trees of the garden.

**9** But Yahweh God called to the man and said to him, "Where are you?"

**10** He said, "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid."

**11** He said, "Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?"

**12** The man said, "The woman you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate."

**13** Then Yahweh God said to the woman, "What is this you have done?" The woman said, "The snake deceived me, and I ate."

**14** Yahweh God said to the snake, "Because you have done this, you are cursed more than all the livestock and all the wild animals.[^3-8] You will crawl on your belly, and you will eat dust all the days of your life.

**15** I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your offspring[^3-9] and her offspring. He[^3-10] will strike[^3-11] your head, and you will strike his heel."

**16** To the woman he said, "I will make your pain in pregnancy very great.[^3-12] In pain you will give birth to children. Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you."

**17** To the man[^3-13] he said, "Because you listened to your wife and ate from the tree I commanded you about, saying, 'You must not eat from it,' the ground is cursed because of you. With painful toil you will eat from it all the days of your life.

**18** It will produce thorns and thistles for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**19** By the sweat of your brow[^3-14] you will eat bread until you return to the ground, for from it you were taken. You are dust, and to dust you will return."

**20** The man named his wife Eve,[^3-15] because she became the mother of everyone living.

**21** Yahweh God made garments of skin for the man[^3-16] and his wife, and he clothed them.

**22** Then Yahweh God said, "Look, the man has become like one of us, knowing good and evil. And now, he might reach out his hand and take from the tree of life too, and eat, and live forever!"[^3-17]

**23** So Yahweh God sent him out of the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken.

**24** He drove the man out. And to the east of the garden of Eden[^3-18] he stationed the cherubim[^3-19] and a flaming sword that turned every way,[^3-20] to guard the way to the tree of life.

[^3-1]: Hebrew *nachash*, traditionally "serpent".
[^3-2]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, crafty", sounds like *arummim*, "naked" (2:25; 3:7).
[^3-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-4]: Or "like gods", "like divine beings". Hebrew *elohim* can mean God or gods.
[^3-5]: Or "desirable to make one wise".
[^3-6]: Or "voice"; also in verse 10.
[^3-7]: That is, at the cool, breezy time of day, toward evening. Hebrew *ruach* means wind or breath.
[^3-8]: Or "you are cursed and set apart from all the livestock and all the wild animals".
[^3-9]: Literally "seed", here and in the next phrase. The Hebrew word is singular and can mean one descendant or many.
[^3-10]: Or "It", referring to the offspring.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *shuph*, used for both actions in this verse. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "crush", "bruise" or "strike at".
[^3-12]: Literally "I will greatly multiply your pain and your pregnancy".
[^3-13]: The Hebrew vowel points read "To Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "To the man", as elsewhere in chapters 2–3.
[^3-14]: Literally "face".
[^3-15]: Hebrew *Chavvah*, which sounds like the Hebrew word for "living".
[^3-16]: As in verse 17, the vowel points read "for Adam".
[^3-17]: The Hebrew sentence breaks off: "And now, so that he does not reach out his hand …".
[^3-18]: Or "in front of the garden of Eden".
[^3-19]: Winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (compare Exodus 25:18–22; Ezekiel 10).
[^3-20]: Literally "the flame of the sword that turned itself".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** The man had sexual relations with[^4-1] his wife Eve, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Cain.[^4-2] She said, "I have gotten a man with the help of Yahweh."[^4-3]

**2** Then she gave birth again, to his brother Abel.[^4-4] Abel kept flocks, and Cain worked the ground.

**3** In the course of time[^4-5] Cain brought some of the produce of the ground as an offering[^4-6] to Yahweh.

**4** Abel too brought an offering: some of the firstborn of his flock and their fat portions. Yahweh looked with favor on Abel and his offering,

**5** but on Cain and his offering he did not look with favor. So Cain was very angry, and his face fell.

**6** Yahweh said to Cain, "Why are you angry? Why has your face fallen?

**7** If you do what is right, will you not be accepted?[^4-7] But if you do not do what is right, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, but you must rule over it."[^4-8]

**8** Cain spoke to his brother Abel.[^4-9] And while they were in the field, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.

**9** Then Yahweh said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I don't know. Am I my brother's keeper?"

**10** Yahweh said, "What have you done? Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground![^4-10]

**11** Now you are cursed from the ground,[^4-11] which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**12** When you work the ground, it will no longer give you its strength. You will be a restless wanderer[^4-12] on the earth."

**13** Cain said to Yahweh, "My punishment is too great to bear.[^4-13]

**14** Look, today you are driving me away from the face of the ground, and I will be hidden from your presence. I will be a restless wanderer on the earth, and whoever finds me will kill me."

**15** But Yahweh said to him, "Therefore,[^4-14] whoever kills Cain will suffer vengeance seven times over." And Yahweh put a mark on Cain,[^4-15] so that no one who found him would strike him down.

**16** So Cain went out from Yahweh's presence and lived in the land of Nod,[^4-16] east of Eden.

**17** Cain had sexual relations with his wife, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Enoch. He was building a city, and he named the city after his son Enoch.

**18** Irad was born to Enoch. Irad fathered Mehujael, Mehujael[^4-17] fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**19** Lamech married two wives. The name of one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**20** Adah gave birth to Jabal. He was the father of those who live in tents and keep livestock.[^4-18]

**21** His brother's name was Jubal. He was the father of all who play the lyre and the pipe.[^4-19]

**22** Zillah also gave birth, to Tubal-cain, who forged every kind of tool of bronze and iron.[^4-20] Tubal-cain's sister was Naamah.

**23** Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, listen to my voice; wives of Lamech, hear what I say: I have killed a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me.

**24** If Cain is avenged seven times, then Lamech seventy-seven times."

**25** Adam[^4-21] had sexual relations with his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth.[^4-22] "For God has granted me another child[^4-23] in place of Abel," she said, "since Cain killed him."

**26** A son was born to Seth too, and he named him Enosh.[^4-24] At that time people began to call on the name of Yahweh.[^4-25]

[^4-1]: Literally "knew"; also in verses 17 and 25.
[^4-2]: *Cain* sounds like the Hebrew *qaniti*, "I have gotten".
[^4-3]: Literally "with Yahweh". *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *Hevel*, which means "breath" or "vapor".
[^4-5]: Literally "At the end of days".
[^4-6]: Hebrew *minchah*, a gift or tribute; also in verses 4 and 5.
[^4-7]: Literally "is there not a lifting up?", that is, of your face (compare verses 5–6), or of your offering.
[^4-8]: Or "and you will rule over it". The words are the same as those spoken to the woman in 3:16.
[^4-9]: Literally "Cain said to his brother Abel", with no words following. The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate add "Let us go out into the field."
[^4-10]: Literally "The voice of your brother's blood is crying out". The word "blood" is plural in the Hebrew here and in verse 11.
[^4-11]: Or "banished from the ground".
[^4-12]: Hebrew *na va-nad*; also in verse 14.
[^4-13]: Or "My guilt is too great to be forgiven". Hebrew *avon* means both guilt and its punishment.
[^4-14]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "Not so!"
[^4-15]: Literally "set a sign for Cain".
[^4-16]: *Nod* means "wandering" (compare verses 12 and 14).
[^4-17]: Hebrew *Mehijael*, a different spelling of the same name.
[^4-18]: *Father* here means the ancestor or founder of a way of life.
[^4-19]: Hebrew *kinnor*, a stringed instrument (traditionally "harp"), and *ugav*, a wind instrument (traditionally "flute").
[^4-20]: Literally "a hammerer of every cutting tool of bronze and iron". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^4-21]: Here, without the article, *adam* is used as a personal name.
[^4-22]: Hebrew *Shet*, which sounds like *shat*, "granted".
[^4-23]: Literally "another seed".
[^4-24]: *Enosh* means "human being".
[^4-25]: That is, to worship Yahweh by calling on his name. Or "to proclaim the name of Yahweh".

## Chapter 29

*Status: draft*

**1** Jacob set out[^29-1] and went to the land of the people of the east.[^29-2]

**2** He looked, and there in the open country was a well, with three flocks of sheep[^29-3] lying beside it, because the flocks were watered from that well. The stone over the mouth of the well was large.

**3** When all the flocks had gathered there, they would roll the stone off the mouth of the well and water the sheep. Then they would put the stone back in its place over the mouth of the well.

**4** Jacob said to them, "My brothers,[^29-4] where are you from?" They said, "We are from Haran."

**5** He said to them, "Do you know Laban son of Nahor?"[^29-5] They said, "We know him."

**6** He said to them, "Is he well?"[^29-6] They said, "He is well. And look, here comes his daughter Rachel with the sheep."

**7** He said, "Look, it is still broad daylight.[^29-7] It is not time for the livestock to be gathered in. Water the sheep, and go and let them graze."

**8** They said, "We can't, until all the flocks are gathered[^29-8] and the stone is rolled off the mouth of the well. Then we water the sheep."

**9** While he was still speaking with them, Rachel came with her father's sheep, for she was a shepherd.

**10** When Jacob saw Rachel, the daughter of his mother's brother Laban, and the sheep of his mother's brother Laban, he went up, rolled the stone off the mouth of the well, and watered the sheep of his mother's brother Laban.

**11** Then Jacob kissed Rachel, and he wept aloud.[^29-9]

**12** Jacob told Rachel that he was her father's relative[^29-10] and that he was Rebekah's son. She ran and told her father.

**13** As soon as Laban heard the news about Jacob, his sister's son, he ran to meet him. He hugged him and kissed him and brought him to his house, and Jacob told Laban all these things.

**14** Laban said to him, "You really are my own flesh and blood."[^29-11] And Jacob stayed with him for a whole month.

**15** Then Laban said to Jacob, "Should you work for me for nothing just because you are my relative? Tell me what your wages should be."

**16** Now Laban had two daughters. The older one was named Leah, and the younger one was named Rachel.

**17** Leah's eyes were weak,[^29-12] but Rachel had a lovely figure and was beautiful to look at.

**18** Jacob loved Rachel, so he said, "I will work for you seven years for your younger daughter Rachel."

**19** Laban said, "It is better for me to give her to you than to give her to another man. Stay with me."

**20** So Jacob worked seven years for Rachel, and they seemed to him like only a few days, because he loved her.

**21** Then Jacob said to Laban, "Give me my wife, for my time is up, so that I may sleep with her."[^29-13]

**22** So Laban gathered all the people of the place and held a feast.

**23** But in the evening he took his daughter Leah and brought her to Jacob, and Jacob slept with her.

**24** Laban gave his servant[^29-14] Zilpah to his daughter Leah as her servant.

**25** In the morning, there was Leah! Jacob said to Laban, "What have you done to me? Didn't I work for you for Rachel? Why have you deceived me?"

**26** Laban said, "It is not done in our place to give the younger daughter in marriage before the firstborn.

**27** Finish this one's week,[^29-15] and we will give you the other one too, in return for working for me another seven years."

**28** Jacob did so. He finished Leah's week, and Laban gave him his daughter Rachel as his wife.

**29** Laban gave his servant Bilhah to his daughter Rachel as her servant.

**30** Jacob slept with Rachel too, and he loved Rachel more than Leah. And he worked for Laban another seven years.

**31** When Yahweh[^29-16] saw that Leah was unloved,[^29-17] he opened her womb. But Rachel was unable to have children.

**32** Leah became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and she named him Reuben, for she said, "Yahweh has seen my misery. Surely now my husband will love me."[^29-18]

**33** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "Yahweh heard that I was unloved, so he has given me this one too." So she named him Simeon.[^29-19]

**34** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "Now this time my husband will become attached to me, for I have borne him three sons." That is why he was named Levi.[^29-20]

**35** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "This time I will praise Yahweh." That is why she named him Judah.[^29-21] Then she stopped having children.

[^29-1]: Literally "lifted his feet".
[^29-2]: Literally "the sons of the east", the peoples living east of Canaan. Here it means the region of Haran in Aram.
[^29-3]: Hebrew *tson*, small livestock: sheep and goats together.
[^29-4]: A friendly way of addressing strangers.

**1** When Rachel saw that she had not borne Jacob any children, she became jealous of her sister. She said to Jacob, "Give me children, or I will die!"

**2** Jacob became angry with Rachel and said, "Am I in the place of God, who has kept you from having children?"[^30-1]

**3** She said, "Here is my servant[^30-2] Bilhah. Sleep with her,[^30-3] so that she can give birth on my knees,[^30-4] and I too can have children through her."[^30-5]

**4** So she gave him her servant Bilhah as a wife, and Jacob slept with her.

**5** Bilhah became pregnant and bore Jacob a son.

**6** Then Rachel said, "God has judged in my favor. He has also heard my voice and given me a son." That is why she named him Dan.[^30-6]

**7** Rachel's servant Bilhah became pregnant again and bore Jacob a second son.

**8** Then Rachel said, "I have wrestled mighty wrestlings[^30-7] with my sister, and I have won." So she named him Naphtali.[^30-8]

**9** When Leah saw that she had stopped having children, she took her servant Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife.

**10** Leah's servant Zilpah bore Jacob a son.

**11** Leah said, "Good fortune has come!"[^30-9] So she named him Gad.

**12** Leah's servant Zilpah bore Jacob a second son.

**13** Leah said, "How happy I am! For women[^30-10] will call me happy." So she named him Asher.[^30-11]

**14** During the wheat harvest Reuben went out and found some mandrakes[^30-12] in the field, and he brought them to his mother Leah. Rachel said to Leah, "Please give me some of your son's mandrakes."

**15** But Leah said to her, "Isn't it enough that you have taken my husband? Will you take my son's mandrakes too?" Rachel said, "All right, he can sleep with you tonight in exchange for your son's mandrakes."

**16** When Jacob came in from the field in the evening, Leah went out to meet him and said, "You must sleep with me,[^30-13] for I have paid for you with my son's mandrakes." So he slept with her that night.

**17** God listened to Leah, and she became pregnant and bore Jacob a fifth son.

**18** Leah said, "God has given me my wages, because I gave my servant to my husband." So she named him Issachar.[^30-14]

**19** Leah became pregnant again and bore Jacob a sixth son.

**20** Leah said, "God has given me a good gift. This time my husband will honor me,[^30-15] for I have borne him six sons." So she named him Zebulun.[^30-16]

**21** Afterward she gave birth to a daughter and named her Dinah.

**22** Then God remembered Rachel. God listened to her and opened her womb.

**23** She became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and she said, "God has taken away my disgrace."

**24** She named him Joseph, saying, "May Yahweh[^30-17] add another son for me."[^30-18]

**25** After Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, "Send me away, so that I can go to my own place and my own country.

**26** Give me my wives and my children, for whom I have worked for you, and let me go. You know how I have worked for you."

**27** But Laban said to him, "Please, if I have found favor with you …[^30-19] I have learned by divination[^30-20] that Yahweh has blessed me because of you."

**28** He went on, "Name your wages, and I will pay them."

**29** Jacob said to him, "You know how I have worked for you, and how your livestock have done in my care.

**30** The little you had before I came has grown into a great amount, and Yahweh has blessed you wherever I went.[^30-21] But now, when will I provide for my own household too?"

**31** Laban said, "What shall I give you?" Jacob said, "Don't give me anything. If you will do this one thing for me, I will go back to pasturing and taking care of your flock.

**32** Let me go through your whole flock today and remove from it every speckled or spotted animal,[^30-22] every dark-colored lamb, and every spotted or speckled goat. They will be my wages.

**33** My honesty[^30-23] will answer for me in the future, when you come to check the wages I have received. Any goat in my possession that is not speckled or spotted, or any lamb that is not dark-colored, will count as stolen."

**34** Laban said, "Good! Let it be as you have said."

**35** But that same day Laban removed the male goats that were streaked or spotted, and all the female goats that were speckled or spotted, every one that had white on it, and every dark-colored lamb, and he put them in the care of his sons.

**36** He put a distance of three days' journey between himself and Jacob, while Jacob was pasturing the rest of Laban's flock.

**37** Then Jacob took fresh branches of poplar,[^30-24] almond and plane trees, and he peeled white stripes in them, exposing the white wood of the branches.

**38** He set the branches he had peeled in the troughs, the watering places where the flocks came to drink, right in front of the flocks. And the animals mated when they came to drink.

**39** The flocks mated in front of the branches, and they gave birth to young that were streaked, speckled and spotted.

**40** Jacob separated out the lambs, and he made the flock face the streaked and all the dark-colored animals in Laban's flock. So he made separate flocks for himself and did not put them with Laban's flock.

**41** Whenever the stronger animals were mating, Jacob would put the branches in the troughs in front of the animals, so that they would mate beside the branches.

**42** But when the animals were weak, he would not put them there. So the weak ones went to Laban, and the strong ones to Jacob.

**43** So the man became extremely rich. He had large flocks, female and male servants, camels and donkeys.

## Chapter 31

**1** Jacob heard what Laban's sons were saying: "Jacob has taken everything that belonged to our father. He has made all this wealth[^31-1] from what belonged to our father."

**2** And Jacob saw from Laban's face that he was not as friendly toward him as before.[^31-2]

**3** Then Yahweh[^31-3] said to Jacob, "Go back to the land of your fathers and to your relatives, and I will be with you."

**4** So Jacob sent for Rachel and Leah and called them out to the field where his flock was.

**5** He said to them, "I can see from your father's face that he is not as friendly toward me as before. But the God of my father has been with me.

**6** You know that I have worked for your father with all my strength.

**7** Yet your father has cheated me and changed my wages ten times.[^31-4] But God did not let him harm me.

**8** If he said, 'The speckled ones will be your wages,' then the whole flock gave birth to speckled young. And if he said, 'The streaked ones will be your wages,' then the whole flock gave birth to streaked young.

**9** So God has taken away your father's livestock and given them to me.

**10** Once, in the season when the flock was mating, I looked up and saw in a dream that the male goats mating with the flock were streaked, speckled and mottled.

**11** The angel of God said to me in the dream, 'Jacob!' And I said, 'Here I am.'

**12** He said, 'Look up and see: all the male goats mating with the flock are streaked, speckled and mottled. For I have seen everything that Laban has been doing to you.

**37** You have felt through all my things. What have you found of all your household things? Put it here in front of my relatives and your relatives, and let them decide between the two of us.

**54** Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain and invited his relatives to eat a meal.[^31-25] They ate the meal and spent the night on the mountain.
