## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, a servant[^1-1] of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, set apart for the good news of God,

**2** which he promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy scriptures,

**3** concerning his Son, who was born a descendant of David according to the flesh[^1-2]

**4** and was appointed[^1-3] Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness[^1-4] by the resurrection from the dead:[^1-5] Jesus Christ our Lord.

**5** Through him we received grace and apostleship to bring about the obedience of faith[^1-6] among all the Gentiles[^1-7] for the sake of his name,

**6** and you too are among them, called to belong to Jesus Christ.[^1-8]

**7** To all who are in Rome, loved by God and called to be God's holy people:[^1-9] Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**8** First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is being proclaimed in the whole world.

**9** For God, whom I serve with my spirit in the good news of his Son,[^1-10] is my witness how constantly I remember you

**10** in my prayers at all times, asking that somehow, now at last, by the will of God I may succeed in coming to you.

**11** For I long to see you, so that I may share some spiritual gift with you to strengthen you,

**12** that is, so that we may be encouraged together while I am among you, each by the other's faith, both yours and mine.

**13** I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters,[^1-11] that I have often planned to come to you (but have been prevented until now), so that I might have some fruit among you too, just as among the rest of the Gentiles.

**14** I am under obligation both to Greeks and to non-Greeks,[^1-12] both to the wise and to the foolish.

**15** So, for my part, I am eager to proclaim the good news to you also who are in Rome.

**16** For I am not ashamed of the good news,[^1-13] because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

**17** For in it the righteousness of God[^1-14] is revealed from faith to faith,[^1-15] as it is written, "But the righteous will live by faith."[^1-16]

**18** For the wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the ungodliness and unrighteousness of people who by their unrighteousness suppress the truth,

**19** because what can be known about God is plain to them,[^1-17] for God has made it plain to them.

**20** For since the creation of the world his invisible qualities, his eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen, being understood through the things he has made, so that they are without excuse,

**21** because, although they knew God, they did not glorify him as God or give thanks to him. Instead, they became futile in their thinking, and their senseless hearts were darkened.

**22** Claiming to be wise, they became fools,

**23** and they exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images made to look like a mortal human being, and birds, and four-footed animals, and reptiles.[^1-18]

**24** Therefore God handed them over[^1-19] in the desires of their hearts to impurity, so that they dishonored their bodies among themselves.

**25** They exchanged the truth of God for the lie, and worshiped and served the creation rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever. Amen.

**26** For this reason God handed them over to dishonorable passions. For their women exchanged natural relations[^1-20] for those that are against nature,

**27** and in the same way the men also gave up natural relations with women and were inflamed with their desire for one another, men committing shameless acts with men and receiving in themselves the penalty their error deserved.

**28** And since they did not see fit to acknowledge God, God handed them over to an unfit mind,[^1-21] to do what ought not to be done.

**29** They are filled with every kind of unrighteousness,[^1-22] wickedness, greed and malice. They are full of envy, murder, strife, deceit and spite. They are gossips,

**30** slanderers, haters of God,[^1-23] insolent, arrogant, boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents,

**31** senseless, faithless, heartless,[^1-24] merciless.

**32** Although they know God's righteous decree, that those who practice such things deserve death, they not only do them but also approve of those who practice them.

[^1-1]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-2]: That is, by human descent. Literally "who came from the seed of David according to the flesh". Here *flesh* (Greek *sarx*) means human nature, not the flesh in Paul's moral sense (see 7:5). It stands in contrast to "according to the Spirit of holiness" in verse 4.
[^1-3]: Or "declared". Greek *horizo*, "mark out, appoint". *In power* may go with "Son of God" ("the Son of God in power") or with "appointed" ("powerfully appointed").
[^1-4]: That is, the Holy Spirit. Or "according to the spirit of holiness", his own holy nature.
[^1-5]: Literally "from the resurrection of the dead". *From* may mean "by" or "since".
[^1-6]: Literally "for obedience of faith". Or "the obedience that comes from faith", or "the obedience that is faith".
[^1-7]: Or "nations". Greek *ethne* means both; also in verse 13.
[^1-8]: Literally "called of Jesus Christ". Or "called by Jesus Christ".
[^1-9]: Literally "called holy ones"; traditionally "called to be saints".
[^1-10]: That is, in proclaiming the good news of his Son.
[^1-11]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-12]: Greek *barbaroi*, people who did not speak Greek.
[^1-13]: Many manuscripts read "the good news of Christ".
[^1-14]: Or "the righteousness that comes from God". Greek *dikaiosyne*, "righteousness, justice". The phrase can mean God's own righteousness, his justice and faithfulness, or the right standing he gives.
[^1-15]: Literally "from faith into faith". Or "by faith from first to last", or "from God's faithfulness to human faith". Greek *pistis* means both "faith" and "faithfulness".
[^1-16]: Habakkuk 2:4. Or "The one who is righteous by faith will live." The Hebrew has "by his faithfulness", and the Septuagint "by my faithfulness", that is, God's; Paul has neither "his" nor "my". Also quoted in Galatians 3:11.
[^1-17]: Literally "in them". Or "among them".
[^1-18]: Literally "for the likeness of an image of a mortal human being and of birds and of four-footed animals and of reptiles". Compare Psalm 106:20; Deuteronomy 4:16–18.
[^1-19]: Many manuscripts read "Therefore God also handed them over".
[^1-20]: Literally "the natural use", here of sexual relations; also in verse 27.
[^1-21]: *Did not see fit* and *unfit* translate related Greek words: *dokimazo*, "test, approve", and *adokimos*, "failing the test, worthless".
[^1-22]: Many manuscripts add "sexual immorality".
[^1-23]: Or "hated by God".
[^1-24]: Many manuscripts add "unforgiving".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Therefore you have no excuse, whoever you are,[^2-1] when you pass judgment. For in judging another, you condemn yourself, because you, the judge, do the same things.

**2** We know that God's judgment on those who do such things is in accordance with the truth.

**3** Do you suppose, you who judge those who do such things and yet do them yourself, that you will escape the judgment of God?

**4** Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is leading you to repentance?

**5** But because of your hardness and your unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath, when the righteous judgment of God is revealed.[^2-2]

**6** He will repay each person according to what he has done:[^2-3]

**7** to those who by perseverance in doing good seek glory and honor and immortality, eternal life;

**8** but for those who are self-seeking[^2-4] and disobey the truth but obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury.

**9** There will be trouble and distress for every human being[^2-5] who does evil, the Jew first and also the Greek,

**10** but glory and honor and peace for everyone who does good, the Jew first and also the Greek.

**11** For God shows no partiality.[^2-6]

**12** For all who have sinned without the Law will also perish without the Law, and all who have sinned under the Law[^2-7] will be judged by the Law.

**13** For it is not the hearers of the Law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the Law who will be declared righteous.[^2-8]

**14** For when Gentiles, who do not have the Law, do by nature what the Law requires,[^2-9] they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the Law.

**15** They show that the work of the Law[^2-10] is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their thoughts in turn accuse or even defend them,

**16** on the day when God judges the secrets of human beings according to my good news, through Christ Jesus.[^2-11]

**17** But if you call yourself a Jew,[^2-12] and rely on the Law, and boast in God,

**18** and know his will, and approve what is excellent[^2-13] because you are instructed from the Law,

**19** and are confident that you are a guide to the blind, a light to those in darkness,

**20** an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of children, because you have in the Law the embodiment of knowledge and truth —

**21** you, then, who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach that one must not steal, do you steal?

**22** You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples?

**23** You who boast in the Law, do you dishonor God by breaking the Law?

**24** For "the name of God is despised among the Gentiles because of you," as it is written.[^2-14]

**25** For circumcision is of value if you practice the Law; but if you break the Law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**26** So if an uncircumcised man keeps the requirements of the Law, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision?

**27** And the one who is physically uncircumcised but fulfills the Law will judge you, who, with the written code and circumcision, break the Law.[^2-15]

**28** For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is circumcision what is outward, in the flesh.[^2-16]

**29** No, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is circumcision of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the written code.[^2-17] That person's praise is not from people but from God.[^2-18]

[^2-1]: Literally "O man, everyone who judges". *O man* (Greek *o anthrope*) addresses an imagined listener, as in a debate; also in verse 3.
[^2-2]: Many manuscripts read "the day of wrath and of revelation and of God's righteous judgment".
[^2-3]: Psalm 62:13 (62:12 in most English Bibles); Proverbs 24:12. Literally "according to his works".
[^2-4]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^2-5]: Literally "every soul of a human being".
[^2-6]: Literally "there is no receiving of face with God", that is, no favoritism.
[^2-7]: Literally "in the Law", that is, having the Law.
[^2-8]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^2-9]: Or "For when Gentiles, who by nature do not have the Law, do what the Law requires". *By nature* may go with either part of the sentence.
[^2-10]: That is, what the Law requires. Compare "the works of the Law" in 3:20, and Jeremiah 31:33.
[^2-11]: Verse 16 may continue the thought of verses 12–13, with verses 14–15 as an aside.
[^2-12]: Many manuscripts read "Look, you call yourself a Jew".
[^2-13]: Or "discern what matters". Literally "test the things that differ".
[^2-14]: Isaiah 52:5, as in the Septuagint, which reads "Because of you my name is despised among the nations". The Hebrew has "all day long my name is continually despised". Compare Ezekiel 36:20–23. *Despised*: Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^2-15]: Literally "who through letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the Law". *Letter* (Greek *gramma*) means the Law as written; also in verse 29.
[^2-16]: That is, physical. Greek *sarx*, "flesh", here the body.
[^2-17]: Or "in spirit, not in letter". Compare Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6; Jeremiah 4:4.
[^2-18]: There is a play on words. The name Judah, from which "Jew" comes, is explained in Genesis 29:35 by the Hebrew for "I will praise".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Then what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the value of circumcision?

**2** Much, in every way. For first of all, they were entrusted with the words[^3-1] of God.

**3** What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify the faithfulness of God?[^3-2]

**4** By no means![^3-3] Let God be true, and every human being a liar,[^3-4] as it is written: "So that you may be proved right[^3-5] in your words, and prevail when you are judged."[^3-6]

**5** But if our unrighteousness shows God's righteousness, what shall we say? That God is unrighteous to inflict wrath? (I am speaking in a human way.)

**6** By no means! For then how could God judge the world?

**7** But[^3-7] if through my falsehood God's truthfulness overflows to his glory, why am I still being judged as a sinner?

**8** And why not say, as we are slanderously accused of saying, and as some claim that we say, "Let us do evil so that good may come"? Their condemnation is just.

**9** What then? Are we better off?[^3-8] Not at all. For we have already charged that all, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin,[^3-9]

**10** as it is written: "There is no one righteous, not even one.

**11** There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God.

**12** They have all turned aside; together they have become worthless. There is no one who does good, not even one.[^3-10]

**13** Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues.[^3-11] The venom of vipers is under their lips.[^3-12]

**14** Their mouth is full of curses and bitterness.[^3-13]

**15** Their feet are swift to shed blood;

**16** ruin and misery are in their paths,

**17** and the way of peace they have not known.[^3-14]

**18** There is no fear of God before their eyes."[^3-15]

**19** Now we know that whatever the Law says, it speaks to those who are under the Law,[^3-16] so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world may be held accountable to God.

**20** For by works of the Law[^3-17] no human being[^3-18] will be declared righteous in his sight, for through the Law comes the knowledge of sin.

**21** But now, apart from the Law, the righteousness of God has been made known, attested by the Law and the Prophets:

**22** the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ[^3-19] for all who believe.[^3-20] For there is no distinction,

**23** for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God,

**24** and they are declared righteous freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

**25** God put him forward as a place of atonement[^3-21] through faith, by his blood,[^3-22] to demonstrate his righteousness, because he had passed over the sins committed beforehand[^3-23]

**26** in his forbearance. It was to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that he would be righteous and would declare righteous the person who has faith in Jesus.[^3-24]

**27** Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law?[^3-25] By the law of works? No, but by the law of faith.

**28** For[^3-26] we hold that a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the Law.

**29** Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also,

**30** since God is one,[^3-27] and he will declare righteous the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.

**31** Do we then nullify the Law through faith? By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the Law.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** What then shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh,[^4-1] has found?[^4-2]

**2** For if Abraham was declared righteous[^4-3] by works, he has something to boast about, but not before God.

**3** For what does the scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness."[^4-4]

**4** Now to the one who works, the wages are not credited as a gift[^4-5] but as what is owed.

**5** But to the one who does not work but believes in him who declares the ungodly righteous, his faith is credited as righteousness,

**6** just as David also speaks of the happiness[^4-6] of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:

**7** "Happy are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.

**8** Happy is the person whose sin the Lord will never count."[^4-7]

**9** Is this happiness, then, for the circumcised, or also for the uncircumcised? For we say, "Faith was credited to Abraham as righteousness."

**10** How then was it credited? When he was circumcised, or when he was uncircumcised? Not when he was circumcised, but when he was uncircumcised.

**11** He received the sign of circumcision[^4-8] as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while still uncircumcised. This was so that he would be the father of all who believe without being circumcised, so that righteousness would be credited to them,[^4-9]

**12** and the father of the circumcised who are not only circumcised but who also follow in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham had while he was still uncircumcised.[^4-10]

**13** For the promise to Abraham or to his offspring[^4-11] that he would inherit the world did not come through the Law but through the righteousness of faith.

**14** For if those who rely on the Law[^4-12] are heirs, faith is made empty and the promise is nullified.

**15** For the Law brings about wrath, but[^4-13] where there is no law, there is no transgression either.

**16** That is why it depends on faith, so that it may rest on grace,[^4-14] and so that the promise may be guaranteed to all his offspring, not only to those who are of the Law but also to those who are of the faith of Abraham (who is the father of us all,

**17** as it is written, "I have made you the father of many nations"),[^4-15] in the presence of the God in whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls into being things that do not exist.[^4-16]

**18** Hoping against hope, he believed that he would become the father of many nations, according to what had been said: "So will your offspring be."[^4-17]

**19** Without weakening in faith, he considered[^4-18] his own body, which was as good as dead[^4-19] (for he was about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah's womb.

**20** He did not waver in unbelief concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in faith, giving glory to God,

**21** and was fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.

**22** That is why[^4-20] it was credited to him as righteousness.

**23** But the words "it was credited to him" were not written for his sake alone,

**24** but for ours also. It will be credited to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

**25** who was handed over for our trespasses and raised so that we would be declared righteous.[^4-21]

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Therefore, since we have been declared righteous[^5-1] by faith, we have peace[^5-2] with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.

**2** Through him we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we boast[^5-3] in the hope of the glory of God.

**3** And not only that, but we also boast in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance,

**4** and endurance produces proven character, and proven character produces hope.

**5** And hope does not put us to shame, because the love of God[^5-4] has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.

**6** For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.[^5-5]

**7** For one will scarcely die for a righteous person, though for a good person someone might perhaps even dare to die.

**8** But God shows his own love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

**9** Much more, then, since we have now been declared righteous by his blood, will we be saved through him from wrath.

**10** For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, now that we have been reconciled, will we be saved by his life.

**11** And not only that, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.

**12** Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all people, because all sinned[^5-6] —

**13** for sin was in the world before the Law,[^5-7] but sin is not counted when there is no law.

**14** Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam,[^5-8] who is a pattern[^5-9] of the one who was to come.

**15** But the gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died through the trespass of the one, much more have the grace of God and the gift given by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, overflowed to the many.

**16** And the gift is not like what came through the one who sinned. For the judgment following one trespass[^5-10] led to condemnation, but the gift following many trespasses led to being declared righteous.[^5-11]

**17** For if, by the trespass of the one, death reigned through that one, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ.

**18** So then, just as through one trespass condemnation came to all people, so also through one righteous act[^5-12] there comes to all people the declaration of righteousness that gives life.[^5-13]

**19** For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made[^5-14] sinners, so also through the obedience of the one the many will be made righteous.

**20** The Law came in alongside, so that the trespass would increase.[^5-15] But where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more,

**21** so that, just as sin reigned in death, so also grace would reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

[^5-4]: That is, God's love for us. Or "our love for God".
[^5-5]: Some manuscripts read "If indeed Christ, while we were still weak, at the right time died for the ungodly".
[^5-6]: Greek *eph' ho*, usually understood as "because". Others take it as "in whom all sinned", referring to Adam, as the Latin Vulgate has it, or as "with the result that all sinned".
[^5-7]: Literally "until the Law", that is, before it was given through Moses.

**12** Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa, who work hard in the Lord. Greet dear Persis, who has worked hard in the Lord.

**13** Greet Rufus,[^16-12] chosen in the Lord, and his mother, who has been a mother to me as well.[^16-13]

**14** Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermes, Patrobas, Hermas, and the brothers and sisters[^16-14] who are with them.

**15** Greet Philologus and Julia, Nereus and his sister, and Olympas, and all God's holy people who are with them.

**16** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the churches of Christ greet you.

**17** I urge you, brothers and sisters, to watch out for those who cause divisions and put obstacles in your way, contrary to the teaching that you have learned. Keep away from them.

**18** For such people do not serve our Lord Christ[^16-15] but their own appetites.[^16-16] By smooth talk and flattery they deceive the hearts of the unsuspecting.

**19** For everyone has heard of your obedience, so I rejoice over you. But I want you to be wise about what is good and innocent about what is evil.

**20** The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet.[^16-17] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ[^16-18] be with you.

**21** Timothy, my co-worker, greets you, and so do Lucius, Jason and Sosipater, my fellow Jews.

**22** I, Tertius, who wrote this letter, greet you in the Lord.[^16-19]

**23** Gaius, who is host to me and to the whole church, greets you. Erastus, the city treasurer, greets you, and so does our brother Quartus.[^16-20]

**24** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.[^16-21]

[^16-1]: Or "a deacon". Greek *diakonos*, used of an office in Philippians 1:1 and 1 Timothy 3:8–13.
[^16-2]: The eastern port of Corinth.
[^16-3]: Literally "worthily of the holy ones"; traditionally "saints"; also in verse 15.
[^16-4]: Greek *prostatis*, "patron, protector": someone who supports others with their means and influence.
[^16-5]: Called Priscilla in Acts 18:2, 18, 26.
[^16-6]: Literally "the firstfruits of Asia", the Roman province in the west of what is now Turkey. Many manuscripts read "Achaia".
[^16-7]: Many manuscripts read "for us".
[^16-8]: Greek *Iounian*. The SBLGNT, with Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and the Byzantine text, accents it as the woman's name Junia, a common Roman name. One edition in the apparatus (marked NA28 there) accents it as a man's name, Junias, which is not otherwise known; the accent decides the gender, and early manuscripts have no accents.
[^16-9]: Greek *syngeneis*, "relatives, kinsfolk", here probably fellow Jews; also in verses 11 and 21.
[^16-10]: Or "well known to the apostles". The Greek can mean that they were themselves outstanding apostles, or that the apostles held them in high regard.
[^16-11]: Literally "those of Aristobulus"; likewise "those of Narcissus" in verse 11.
[^16-12]: Perhaps the Rufus of Mark 15:21.
[^16-13]: Literally "his mother and mine".
[^16-14]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 17. *Our brother* in verse 23 is the singular, *adelphos*.
[^16-15]: Many manuscripts read "our Lord Jesus Christ".
[^16-16]: Literally "their own belly".
[^16-17]: Compare Genesis 3:15.
[^16-18]: Some manuscripts leave out "Christ".
[^16-19]: Or "I, Tertius, who wrote this letter in the Lord, greet you".
[^16-20]: Literally "Quartus the brother".
[^16-21]: Verse 24 is in the SBLGNT, following the Byzantine text; many modern editions, including Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and NA28, leave it out. In its place they end the letter with verses 25–27: "Now to him who is able to strengthen you according to my good news and the proclamation of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but is now disclosed, and through the prophetic scriptures, by the command of the eternal God, has been made known to all the Gentiles to bring about the obedience of faith: to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, be the glory forever. Amen." The Byzantine text has these words after 14:23 instead. The SBLGNT prints them only in its apparatus, so this translation has no verses 25–27.
