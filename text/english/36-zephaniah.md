## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of the LORD[^1-1] that came to Zephaniah[^1-2] son of Cushi, son of Gedaliah, son of Amariah, son of Hezekiah,[^1-3] in the days of Josiah son of Amon king of Judah.[^1-4]

**2** I will sweep away everything, completely,[^1-5] from the face of the earth, declares the LORD.[^1-6]

**3** I will sweep away human beings and animals; I will sweep away the birds of the sky and the fish of the sea, and the things that make the wicked stumble.[^1-7] I will cut off humankind from the face of the earth,[^1-8] declares the LORD.

**4** I will stretch out my hand against Judah and against all who live in Jerusalem. I will cut off from this place what is left of Baal, and the name of the idol priests[^1-9] along with the priests,

**5** those who bow down on the rooftops to the host of heaven,[^1-10] and those who bow down and swear allegiance to the LORD but also swear by Milcom,[^1-11]

**6** and those who turn back from following the LORD, who do not seek the LORD or inquire of him.

**7** Be silent before the Lord GOD,[^1-12] for the day of the LORD is near. The LORD has prepared a sacrifice; he has consecrated his guests.[^1-13]

**8** On the day of the LORD's sacrifice I will punish the officials and the king's sons, and all who dress in foreign clothing.[^1-14]

**9** On that day I will punish everyone who leaps over the threshold,[^1-15] who fill their master's house[^1-16] with violence and deceit.

**10** On that day, declares the LORD, a cry will be heard from the Fish Gate, wailing from the Second Quarter,[^1-17] and a great crash from the hills.

**11** Wail, you who live in the Mortar,[^1-18] for all the traders[^1-19] are destroyed; all who weigh out silver are cut off.

**12** At that time I will search Jerusalem with lamps, and I will punish the people who sit complacent, like wine left on its dregs,[^1-20] who say in their hearts, "The LORD will do nothing, good or bad."

**13** Their wealth will be plundered and their houses made a desolation. They will build houses but not live in them; they will plant vineyards but not drink their wine.[^1-21]

**14** The great day of the LORD is near, near and coming very quickly. Listen! The day of the LORD! There the warrior cries out bitterly.[^1-22]

**15** That day is a day of wrath, a day of distress and anguish, a day of ruin and devastation,[^1-23] a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness,[^1-24]

**16** a day of the ram's horn[^1-25] and the battle cry against the fortified cities and against the high corner towers.

**17** I will bring such distress on humankind that they will walk like the blind, because they have sinned against the LORD. Their blood will be poured out like dust, and their flesh[^1-26] like dung.

**18** Neither their silver nor their gold will be able to save them on the day of the LORD's wrath. In the fire of his jealousy the whole earth[^1-27] will be consumed, for he will make an end, a sudden and terrible end, of all who live on the earth.

[^1-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-2]: The name means "The LORD has hidden" or "The LORD protects".
[^1-3]: Perhaps King Hezekiah of Judah (about 715–686 BC), though the text does not call him king.
[^1-4]: Josiah reigned about 640–609 BC. In 622 BC he began a reform that removed the worship of other gods from Judah (2 Kings 22–23), and verses 4–6 may come from the years before it.
[^1-5]: Hebrew *asof asef*, "I will surely gather", here in the sense of sweeping away. The words may also play on *suf*, "come to an end". Also in verse 3.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 3 and 10.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *ha-makhshelot*, "the stumbling blocks", perhaps idols. The meaning of the phrase is uncertain. The list of living things reverses the order of their creation in Genesis 1.
[^1-8]: A play on words: *adam*, "humankind", and *adamah*, "ground, earth", as in Genesis 2:7.
[^1-9]: Hebrew *kemarim*, a word used only of priests of other gods (2 Kings 23:5; Hosea 10:5).
[^1-10]: The sun, the moon and the stars, worshiped on flat roofs (2 Kings 23:5, 12; Jeremiah 19:13).
[^1-11]: The Hebrew is pointed *malkam*, "their king". The same consonants can be read *Milkom*, Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5; 2 Kings 23:13).
[^1-12]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *Be silent* is Hebrew *has*, "Hush!", as in Habakkuk 2:20.
[^1-13]: Judah itself is the sacrifice, and the guests are the nations invited to share in it (Isaiah 34:6; Jeremiah 46:10).
[^1-14]: Clothing that marked those who followed foreign customs and the worship of other gods (compare 2 Kings 10:22).
[^1-15]: Perhaps a foreign custom of worship (1 Samuel 5:5), or rushing into houses to rob them.
[^1-16]: Or "the house of their lord", perhaps the king's palace or the temple of their god.
[^1-17]: The Fish Gate was in the north wall of Jerusalem (Nehemiah 3:3), and the Second Quarter, Hebrew *Mishneh*, a newer part of the city (2 Kings 22:14).
[^1-18]: Hebrew *makhtesh*, "mortar", a hollow, probably a market district in a valley of Jerusalem.
[^1-19]: Literally "the people of Canaan". *Canaanite* came to mean "trader" (Hosea 12:8; 12:7 in most English Bibles).
[^1-20]: Literally "who thicken on their dregs", like wine left too long on its sediment, which becomes thick and syrupy (Jeremiah 48:11).
[^1-21]: Compare Deuteronomy 28:30, 39; Amos 5:11.
[^1-22]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "The sound of the day of the LORD: bitter, crying out there is a warrior."
[^1-23]: Hebrew *sho'ah u-mesho'ah*, two words of similar sound.
[^1-24]: The same words are found in Joel 2:2. The Latin translation of verse 15, *Dies irae, dies illa*, "That day is a day of wrath", gave its name to a medieval hymn.
[^1-25]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^1-26]: Hebrew *lechum*, found elsewhere only in Job 20:23, probably "flesh" or "entrails".
[^1-27]: Or "the whole land". Also later in the verse.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Gather yourselves together, gather together,[^2-1] you shameless nation,[^2-2]

**2** before the decree takes effect[^2-3] and the day blows past like chaff, before the burning anger of the LORD[^2-4] comes on you, before the day of the LORD's anger comes on you.

**3** Seek the LORD, all you humble of the land, who do what he commands.[^2-5] Seek righteousness; seek humility. Perhaps you will be sheltered on the day of the LORD's anger.

**4** For Gaza will be abandoned, and Ashkelon will become a desolation. Ashdod will be driven out at noon, and Ekron will be uprooted.[^2-6]

**5** Woe to[^2-7] you who live by the seacoast, you nation of the Cherethites![^2-8] The word of the LORD is against you, Canaan, land of the Philistines: I will destroy you until no one is left living there.

**6** The seacoast will become pastures, with meadows[^2-9] for shepherds and pens for flocks.

**7** The coast will belong to the remnant of the house of Judah; they will graze their flocks there.[^2-10] In the houses of Ashkelon they will lie down in the evening, for the LORD their God will care for them and restore their fortunes.[^2-11]

**8** I have heard the taunts of Moab and the insults of the Ammonites, how they taunted my people and made boasts against their territory.[^2-12]

**9** Therefore, as I live, declares the LORD of Armies,[^2-13] the God of Israel, Moab will become like Sodom and the Ammonites like Gomorrah, a place overgrown with weeds,[^2-14] a salt pit and a desolation forever. The remnant of my people will plunder them, and the survivors of my nation[^2-15] will possess them.

**10** This is what they will get for their pride, because they taunted and made boasts against the people of the LORD of Armies.

**11** The LORD will be terrible against them, for he will starve[^2-16] all the gods of the earth. And all the coastlands of the nations[^2-17] will bow down to him, each in its own place.

**12** You too, Cushites,[^2-18] will be slain by my sword.[^2-19]

**13** And he will stretch out his hand against the north and destroy Assyria. He will make Nineveh a desolation, as dry as the desert.

**14** Flocks will lie down in her midst, all kinds of wild animals.[^2-20] Both the desert owl and the screech owl[^2-21] will roost on the tops of her pillars. A voice will hoot in the window; desolation[^2-22] will be on the threshold, for her cedar paneling has been stripped bare.

**15** This is the exultant city that lived in security, that said in her heart, "I am, and there is no one else."[^2-23] How she has become a desolation, a lair for wild animals! Everyone who passes by her hisses and shakes his fist.

[^2-1]: The meaning of the Hebrew verbs is uncertain. They may come from *qash*, "stubble": "gather yourselves like stubble".
[^2-2]: Literally "nation that is not ashamed". Or "nation that is not desired".
[^2-3]: Literally "before a decree gives birth". The Hebrew of the first half of the verse is difficult.
[^2-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-5]: Literally "who have done his justice".
[^2-6]: Two plays on words: *Azzah azuvah*, "Gaza will be abandoned", and *Eqron te'aqer*, "Ekron will be uprooted". *At noon* may mean "in broad daylight" or "in half a day". Gath, the fifth Philistine city, is not named (compare Amos 1:6–8).
[^2-7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^2-8]: A people closely linked with the Philistines, perhaps from Crete (1 Samuel 30:14; Ezekiel 25:16).
[^2-9]: Hebrew *kerot*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "meadows" or "caves". It sounds like *keretim*, "Cherethites" (verse 5).
[^2-10]: Literally "on them they will graze".
[^2-11]: Literally "turn their turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back their captives".
[^2-12]: Or "and enlarged themselves at the expense of their territory".
[^2-13]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. *The LORD of Armies* is also in verse 10.
[^2-14]: Literally "a possession of nettles". Moab and Ammon lay east of the Dead Sea, near the place where Sodom and Gomorrah once stood (Genesis 19:24–38).
[^2-15]: The Hebrew text is written "the survivors of the nation" but read "the survivors of my nation".
[^2-16]: Or "make lean, waste away", perhaps by depriving the gods of the offerings of their worshipers.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *iyyim*, "coasts, islands", the far-off lands across the sea.
[^2-18]: The people of Cush, on the upper Nile, whose kings ruled Egypt until about 663 BC. Cush may stand for Egypt here.
[^2-19]: Literally "they are the slain of my sword".
[^2-20]: Literally "all the animals of a nation". The meaning is uncertain.
[^2-21]: Hebrew *qa'at* and *qippod*, birds of uncertain kind (Isaiah 34:11).
[^2-22]: Hebrew *chorev*. The Septuagint reads "ravens".
[^2-23]: The words of a city that thinks it is like God. Babylon says the same in Isaiah 47:8, 10.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Woe to[^3-1] the rebellious and defiled one, the oppressing city![^3-2]

**2** She does not listen to the voice; she does not accept correction. She does not trust in the LORD;[^3-3] she does not draw near to her God.

**3** Her officials within her are roaring lions; her judges are wolves at evening, who leave nothing until the morning.[^3-4]

**4** Her prophets are reckless, treacherous men. Her priests profane what is holy; they do violence to the law.[^3-5]

**5** The LORD within her is righteous; he does no wrong. Morning by morning he brings his justice to light; he never fails. But the unjust know no shame.

**6** I have cut off nations; their corner towers are desolate. I have laid waste their streets, so that no one walks in them. Their cities are ruined, with no one in them, no one living there.

**7** I said, "Surely you will fear me; you will accept correction."[^3-6] Then her dwelling would not be cut off, in spite of all the punishment I have brought on her.[^3-7] But they rose early to make all their deeds corrupt.

**8** Therefore wait for me, declares the LORD,[^3-8] for the day when I rise up as a witness.[^3-9] For my decision is to gather nations, to assemble kingdoms, to pour out on them my indignation, all my burning anger. In the fire of my jealousy the whole earth will be consumed.

**9** For then I will change the speech of the peoples to a pure speech,[^3-10] so that all of them may call on the name of the LORD and serve him shoulder to shoulder.[^3-11]

**10** From beyond the rivers of Cush,[^3-12] my worshipers, my scattered people,[^3-13] will bring me offerings.

**11** On that day you will not be put to shame for all the deeds by which you rebelled against me, for then I will remove from your midst those who exult in their pride, and you will never again be haughty on my holy mountain.

**12** But I will leave in your midst a people humble and poor, and they will take refuge in the name of the LORD.

**13** The remnant of Israel will do no wrong and tell no lies, and no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouths. They will graze and lie down, with no one to make them afraid.[^3-14]

**14** Sing aloud, Daughter Zion![^3-15] Shout, Israel! Rejoice and exult with all your heart, Daughter Jerusalem!

**15** The LORD has taken away the judgments against you;[^3-16] he has turned back your enemy. The King of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst; you will never again fear disaster.

**16** On that day it will be said to Jerusalem: "Do not be afraid, Zion; do not let your hands hang limp.

**17** The LORD your God is in your midst, a warrior who saves. He will rejoice over you with gladness; he will be quiet in his love;[^3-17] he will delight in you with singing."

**18** I will gather those who grieve for the appointed festivals, who were away from you, a burden on her and a disgrace.[^3-18]

**19** Look, at that time I will deal with all who oppress you. I will save the lame and gather the outcast,[^3-19] and I will give them praise and renown in every land where they were put to shame.

**20** At that time I will bring you home, at the time when I gather you. For I will give you renown and praise among all the peoples of the earth, when I restore your fortunes[^3-20] before your eyes, says the LORD.

[^3-1]: Hebrew *hoy*. See 2:5.
[^3-2]: Jerusalem (verses 2–4). *Rebellious* may also mean "filthy". *Oppressing* (*ha-yonah*) is the same in sound as "the dove", perhaps a play on words (compare Hosea 7:11).
[^3-3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-4]: Literally "they do not gnaw until the morning". The meaning is uncertain. Compare Habakkuk 1:8.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law". Compare Ezekiel 22:26.
[^3-6]: *You* is Jerusalem. The Hebrew is feminine.
[^3-7]: Literally "all that I have appointed against her".
[^3-8]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^3-9]: The Hebrew is pointed *la'ad*, "for plunder". The same consonants can be read *le'ed*, "as a witness", as the Septuagint and the Syriac read.
[^3-10]: Literally "a purified lip". The Hebrew word *safah*, "lip", is the word for "language" in the story of Babel, where the one speech of humankind was confused (Genesis 11:1–9).
[^3-11]: Literally "with one shoulder".
[^3-12]: The land on the upper Nile, south of Egypt (2:12), at the edge of the known world.
[^3-13]: Literally "my suppliants, the daughter of my scattered ones". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^3-14]: Like sheep in a safe pasture (Micah 4:4; 7:14). Revelation 14:5 echoes the words "no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouths".
[^3-15]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. So also *Daughter Jerusalem*.
[^3-16]: Literally "your judgments", the punishments decreed against Jerusalem.
[^3-17]: Or "he will quiet you by his love". The Septuagint reads "he will renew you in his love".
[^3-18]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "Those grieving from the appointed time I have gathered; from you they were; a burden on her, a disgrace."
[^3-19]: The Hebrew is feminine singular, picturing the people as a lame ewe of the flock, as in Micah 4:6–7.
[^3-20]: Literally "when I turn your turnings", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "when I bring back your captives".
