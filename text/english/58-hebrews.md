## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Long ago God spoke to our ancestors through the prophets at many times and in many ways.[^1-1]

**2** But in these last days[^1-2] he has spoken to us through a Son,[^1-3] whom he appointed heir of all things, and through whom he also made the universe.[^1-4]

**3** The Son is the radiance of God's glory[^1-5] and the exact imprint of his being,[^1-6] and he sustains all things by his powerful word.[^1-7] After he had made purification for sins by himself,[^1-8] he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty[^1-9] on high.

**4** So he became as much greater than the angels as the name he has inherited is more excellent than theirs.

**5** For to which of the angels did God ever say, "You are my Son; today I have become your father"?[^1-10] Or again, "I will be his father, and he will be my Son"?[^1-11]

**6** And again, when he brings the firstborn into the world,[^1-12] he says, "Let all God's angels worship him."[^1-13]

**7** About the angels he says, "He makes his angels winds, and his servants flames of fire."[^1-14]

**8** But about the Son he says, "Your throne, God, is forever and ever,[^1-15] and the scepter of your kingdom is a scepter of justice.[^1-16]

**9** You have loved righteousness and hated wickedness; so God, your God, has anointed you[^1-17] with the oil of gladness above your companions."[^1-18]

**10** And, "In the beginning, Lord, you laid the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the work of your hands.[^1-19]

**11** They will perish, but you remain; they will all wear out like a garment.

**12** You will roll them up like a cloak, and like a garment they will be changed.[^1-20] But you are the same, and your years will have no end."[^1-21]

**13** But to which of the angels has he ever said, "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet"?[^1-22]

**14** Are they not all spirits in holy service,[^1-23] sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation?

[^1-1]: Literally "to the fathers in the prophets". *At many times and in many ways*: Greek *polymeros kai polytropos*, "in many parts and in many ways", two words beginning the letter with the same sound.
[^1-2]: Literally "at the last of these days".
[^1-3]: Literally "in a Son". The Greek has no article: God's last word came through one who is a Son. Or "through his Son".
[^1-4]: Literally "the ages". Greek *aiones*, the world and its ages of time.
[^1-5]: Literally "the radiance of the glory". Greek *apaugasma*, light shining out from its source; or "reflection".
[^1-6]: Greek *charakter*, the impression a seal or a die makes, and *hypostasis*, "real being, essence, nature".
[^1-7]: Literally "by the word of power". *Sustains*: Greek *pheron*, "carries, bears along".
[^1-8]: *By himself*: these words are in the SBLGNT, following many manuscripts; many modern editions leave them out and read "by the word of his power". Many manuscripts also read "our sins".
[^1-9]: A reverent way of referring to God.
[^1-10]: Psalm 2:7, also quoted in 5:5 and Acts 13:33. Literally "today I have begotten you".
[^1-11]: 2 Samuel 7:14 (also 1 Chronicles 17:13), God's promise to David about his son. Literally "I will be to him a father, and he will be to me a son."
[^1-12]: Or "And when he again brings the firstborn into the world". *The world*: Greek *oikoumene*, the inhabited earth (also in 2:5).
[^1-13]: Deuteronomy 32:43 in the Septuagint, which has a longer form of the verse than the Hebrew text; these words are not in the Hebrew (see the note there). A Dead Sea Scroll has "bow down to him, all you gods" in the verse. Similar words are in Psalm 97:7, "Bow down to him, all you gods!", where the Septuagint reads "all his angels".
[^1-14]: Psalm 104:4, as in the Septuagint. *Winds*: or "spirits". In the Hebrew the verse can be read "He makes the winds his messengers, flames of fire his servants", as this translation has it there: Hebrew *mal'akh* means "messenger" or "angel", and *ruach* "wind" or "spirit".
[^1-15]: Psalm 45:7–8 (45:6–7 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint, quoted in verses 8–9. In the psalm the words are addressed to the king. The Greek *ho theos*, "God", is here a form of address; it can also be read "God is your throne forever and ever".
[^1-16]: Or "of uprightness". Some manuscripts read "his kingdom".
[^1-17]: Or "so, God, your God has anointed you", continuing to address the Son. *Has anointed*: Greek *echrisen*, from the same root as *christos*, "anointed one, Messiah".
[^1-18]: Or "your partners", Greek *metochoi*, the word used in 3:1, 14.
[^1-19]: Psalm 102:26–28 (102:25–27 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 10–12 from the Septuagint, which adds "Lord". The Hebrew reads "Long ago you laid the foundation of the earth". In the psalm the words are addressed to the LORD, God's personal name (102:2, 13); Hebrews applies them to the Son.
[^1-20]: The Hebrew reads "You will change them like clothing, and they will pass away." *Roll them up*: Greek *helisseis*; compare Isaiah 34:4, "the heavens will be rolled up like a scroll". Many manuscripts leave out "like a garment" in this verse.
[^1-21]: Literally "your years will not fail".
[^1-22]: Psalm 110:1, where the LORD speaks to the king; also quoted in Mark 12:36. *A footstool for your feet*: a conquered enemy was put under the victor's feet.
[^1-23]: Greek *leitourgika pneumata*. *Leitourgia* is public and priestly service; compare "servants" (*leitourgoi*) in verse 7.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** That is why we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away.[^2-1]

**2** For if the message spoken through angels[^2-2] proved firm, and every violation and every act of disobedience received its just penalty,

**3** how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It was first announced through the Lord, and it was confirmed to us by those who heard him,

**4** while God added his testimony by signs and wonders, by various miracles,[^2-3] and by gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed[^2-4] according to his will.

**5** For it was not to angels that he subjected the world to come, which is what we are speaking about.

**6** Instead, someone has testified somewhere: "What is a human being, that you remember him, or a son of man,[^2-5] that you care for him?

**7** You made him for a little while lower than the angels;[^2-6] you crowned him with glory and honor.[^2-7]

**8** You put everything in subjection under his feet." For in putting everything in subjection,[^2-8] he left nothing that is not subject to him. But at present we do not yet see everything subject to him.

**9** What we do see is Jesus, who was made for a little while lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor because he suffered death,[^2-9] so that apart from God[^2-10] he might taste death[^2-11] for everyone.[^2-12]

**10** For it was fitting that he, for whom and through whom all things exist, in bringing many sons and daughters[^2-13] to glory, should make the pioneer[^2-14] of their salvation perfect[^2-15] through sufferings.

**11** For the one who makes people holy and those who are made holy all have one origin.[^2-16] That is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters.[^2-17]

**12** He says, "I will tell of your name to my brothers and sisters; in the midst of the assembly I will sing your praise."[^2-18]

**13** And again, "I will put my trust in him."[^2-19] And again, "Here am I, and the children God has given me."[^2-20]

**14** Since, then, the children share in flesh and blood,[^2-21] he himself likewise shared in the same things, so that through his death he might break the power[^2-22] of the one who holds the power of death, that is, the devil,

**15** and set free all those who were held in slavery all their lives by the fear of death.

**16** For surely it is not angels that he takes hold of to help,[^2-23] but the descendants[^2-24] of Abraham.

**17** So he had to be made like his brothers and sisters in every way, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest[^2-25] in the service of God, to make atonement[^2-26] for the sins of the people.

**18** Because he himself suffered when he was tested,[^2-27] he is able to help those who are being tested.

[^2-1]: Or "drift off course", like a boat carried past its landing place.
[^2-2]: The law given at Mount Sinai, which Jewish tradition held was given through angels (compare Acts 7:53; Galatians 3:19).
[^2-3]: Or "powerful deeds".
[^2-4]: Literally "and by distributions of the Holy Spirit".
[^2-5]: That is, a human being, a mortal. Psalm 8:5–7 (8:4–6 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 6–8 from the Septuagint. In verse 9 Hebrews applies the words to Jesus.
[^2-6]: *For a little while*: or "a little", also in verse 9. The Hebrew of the psalm reads "a little lower than God" (or "than the heavenly beings"); the Septuagint has "angels".
[^2-7]: Some manuscripts add "and you set him over the works of your hands", as in the psalm.
[^2-8]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions read "in putting everything in subjection to him". The SBLGNT leaves out "to him", following a minority reading.
[^2-9]: Literally "because of the suffering of death".
[^2-10]: The SBLGNT reads *choris theou*, "apart from God", here following a minority reading against the other editions. Many manuscripts and most modern editions read *chariti theou*, "by the grace of God". "Apart from God" may mean that Jesus died without God's help, or abandoned by God, or that "everything" in verse 8 does not include God.
[^2-11]: That is, experience death.
[^2-12]: Or "for everything".
[^2-13]: Literally "many sons". Greek *huioi*: the believers, men and women alike, who share the status of the Son (1:2).
[^2-14]: Greek *archegos*, "leader, founder, pioneer": one who goes first and opens the way for others (also 12:2). Or "to make perfect through sufferings the pioneer who brings many sons and daughters to glory".
[^2-15]: Greek *teleioo*, "make complete, bring to the goal", a key word in Hebrews (5:9; 7:28; 10:14).
[^2-16]: Literally "are all from one": from one Father, God, or from one ancestor.
[^2-17]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike; also in verses 12 and 17.
[^2-18]: Psalm 22:23 (22:22 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew reads "I will praise you". *Assembly*: Greek *ekklesia*, the word elsewhere translated "church".
[^2-19]: Isaiah 8:17, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "I will put my hope in him." Compare 2 Samuel 22:3 and Isaiah 12:2.
[^2-20]: Isaiah 8:18, where the prophet speaks of himself and his children. In the Hebrew, "God" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^2-21]: Literally "blood and flesh", that is, human nature.
[^2-22]: Greek *katargeo*, "make powerless, destroy".
[^2-23]: Literally "takes hold of", as one takes someone by the hand to help (8:9). It has also been understood as "takes on the nature of".
[^2-24]: Literally "seed".
[^2-25]: Greek *archiereus*, the chief priest of Israel, who alone entered the most holy place, once a year (Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:7).
[^2-26]: Greek *hilaskomai*, traditionally "make propitiation" or "make expiation". It is related to *hilasterion*, "atonement cover" (9:5).
[^2-27]: Or "tempted". The Greek word *peirazo* means both, also later in the verse (and in 4:15).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** So, holy brothers and sisters,[^3-1] you who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle[^3-2] and high priest of the faith we confess.[^3-3]

**2** He was faithful to the one who appointed him,[^3-4] just as Moses also was in his house.[^3-5]

**3** For he has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses, just as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself.

**4** For every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God.

**5** Moses was faithful in all his house as a servant,[^3-6] to testify to the things that would be spoken later,

**6** but Christ is faithful as a Son over his house. And we are that house, if we hold on to our confidence and the hope we take pride in.[^3-7]

**7** So, as the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear his voice,[^3-8]

**8** do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,[^3-9]

**9** where your ancestors put me to the test[^3-10] and saw my works

**10** for forty years.[^3-11] That is why I was angry with this generation,[^3-12] and I said, 'Their hearts are always going astray, and they have not known my ways.'[^3-13]

**11** So I swore in my anger, 'They shall never enter my rest.'"[^3-14]

**12** Brothers and sisters, see to it that none of you has an evil, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God.

**13** Instead, encourage one another every day, as long as it is called "Today", so that none of you is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

**14** For we have become partners of the Messiah,[^3-15] if we hold our original confidence[^3-16] firm to the end.

**15** As it is said,[^3-17] "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion."

**16** Who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who came out of Egypt, led by Moses?

**17** And with whom was he angry for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?[^3-18]

**18** And to whom did he swear that they would not enter his rest, if not to those who disobeyed?[^3-19]

**19** So we see that they could not enter because of unbelief.

[^3-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike; also in verse 12.
[^3-2]: Greek *apostolos*, "one who is sent", used of Jesus only here in the New Testament.
[^3-3]: Literally "of our confession". Many manuscripts add "Christ" to "Jesus".
[^3-4]: Literally "who made him".
[^3-5]: Numbers 12:7, where God says of Moses, "He is trusted in all my house"; *his house* is God's house. Greek *oikos*, "house", also means "household", the people of God. Many manuscripts and most modern editions read "in all his house", as in verse 5; the SBLGNT leaves out "all", following a minority reading.
[^3-6]: Greek *therapon*, the word used for Moses in the Septuagint of Numbers 12:7.
[^3-7]: Literally "the confidence and the boast of the hope". Many manuscripts add "firm to the end", as in verse 14.
[^3-8]: Psalm 95:7–11, quoted in verses 7–11 from the Septuagint; Hebrews returns to it in verse 15 and in 4:3–7. In the psalm God speaks from verse 8 on.
[^3-9]: The Hebrew of the psalm reads "as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness". The Septuagint translates the names, *Meribah* ("quarreling") as "the rebellion" and *Massah* as "testing" (Exodus 17:1–7; Numbers 20:1–13).
[^3-10]: Literally "tested in testing". Many manuscripts read "tested me, tried me".
[^3-11]: In the Hebrew of the psalm, "for forty years" begins the next sentence, "For forty years I loathed that generation." Hebrews adds "That is why", but in verse 17 it too joins the forty years to God's anger.
[^3-12]: Many manuscripts read "that generation".
[^3-13]: The Hebrew reads "They are a people whose hearts go astray"; the Septuagint has "always".
[^3-14]: Literally "If they will enter my rest", the form of an oath meaning "they will certainly not enter". *My rest*: in the psalm, the land God promised his people (Deuteronomy 12:9).
[^3-15]: Or "sharers in the Messiah". Greek *metochoi*, as in verse 1 and 1:9. *The Messiah*: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^3-16]: Literally "the beginning of the confidence". Greek *hypostasis*, here "confidence, assurance", the word translated "being" in 1:3 and "assurance" in 11:1.
[^3-17]: The words may go with what comes before ("as long as it is said", verses 13–14) or with what follows.
[^3-18]: Literally "whose limbs fell", their corpses. Compare Numbers 14:29, 32.
[^3-19]: Or "who refused to believe".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** So, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us take care[^4-1] that none of you is found to have fallen short of it.

**2** For we too have had the good news announced to us, just as they did. But the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not joined in faith with those who listened.[^4-2]

**3** For we who have believed do enter that rest,[^4-3] just as he has said, "So I swore in my anger, 'They shall never enter my rest'",[^4-4] even though his works had been finished since the foundation of the world.

**4** For somewhere he has spoken about the seventh day like this: "And on the seventh day God rested from all his works."[^4-5]

**5** And again in this passage: "They shall never enter my rest."

**6** Since, then, it still remains for some to enter it, and those who first had the good news announced to them did not enter because of their disobedience,[^4-6]

**7** God again sets a certain day, "Today", saying through David[^4-7] so long afterward, as has been said already, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts."

**8** For if Joshua[^4-8] had given them rest, God would not have spoken later about another day.

**9** So then, a Sabbath rest[^4-9] remains for the people of God.

**10** For whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from their own works, just as God did from his.[^4-10]

**11** So let us make every effort to enter that rest, so that no one falls by following the same example of disobedience.

**12** For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any double-edged sword.[^4-11] It penetrates even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of joints and marrow, and it judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

**13** No creature is hidden from his sight. Everything is naked and laid bare[^4-12] before the eyes of the one to whom we must give account.[^4-13]

**14** Since, then, we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold firmly to the faith we confess.[^4-14]

**15** For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who has been tested[^4-15] in every way as we are, yet without sin.

**16** So let us approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us at the time we need it.

[^4-1]: Literally "let us fear".
[^4-2]: Literally "not being mixed by faith with those who heard", perhaps those who listened with faith, such as Joshua and Caleb (Numbers 14:6–9). Some editions read "because it was not mixed with faith in those who heard", that is, the message was not met with faith.
[^4-3]: Literally "we enter into rest". Greek *katapausis*, the word for "rest" in the psalm.
[^4-4]: Psalm 95:11 (see 3:11).
[^4-5]: Genesis 2:2. *Rested*: Greek *katepausen*, the verb related to *katapausis*, "rest".
[^4-6]: Or "unbelief".
[^4-7]: Literally "in David", that is, in the Psalms. Psalm 95 has no title in the Hebrew; the Septuagint gives it one naming David.
[^4-8]: Greek *Iesous*, the Greek form of the name Joshua, and also of Jesus. Joshua led Israel into the promised land (Joshua 1:1–6; 21:44).
[^4-9]: Greek *sabbatismos*, "Sabbath-keeping", a different word from *katapausis*, "rest", in verses 1–8.
[^4-10]: Literally "For the one who has entered his rest has also himself rested from his works, as God did from his own."
[^4-11]: Literally "two-mouthed sword".
[^4-12]: Greek *tetrachelismena*, literally "with the neck bent back", exposed like a sacrificial animal or a defeated wrestler.
[^4-13]: Literally "to whom our word (is)". Greek *logos* plays on "the word of God" in verse 12; the phrase can also mean "with whom we have to do".
[^4-14]: Literally "the confession", as in 3:1.
[^4-15]: Or "tempted", as in 2:18.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Every high priest is chosen from among human beings and appointed to act on their behalf in matters relating to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins.

**2** He is able to deal gently[^5-1] with those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself is also subject to weakness.[^5-2]

**3** Because of this he must offer sacrifices for his own sins, just as he does for the people.

**4** And no one takes this honor for himself. He receives it when he is called by God, just as Aaron was.[^5-3]

**5** In the same way, the Messiah[^5-4] did not take for himself the glory of becoming high priest. It was given him by the one who said to him, "You are my Son; today I have become your father."[^5-5]

**6** As he also says in another place, "You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek."[^5-6]

**7** During his life on earth,[^5-7] he offered up prayers and pleas, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death,[^5-8] and he was heard because of his reverence.[^5-9]

**8** Son though he was, he learned obedience from what he suffered.[^5-10]

**9** And once he had been made perfect,[^5-11] he became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey him,

**10** since God designated him a high priest in the order of Melchizedek.

**11** We have much to say about this,[^5-12] and it is hard to explain, because you have become sluggish in listening.

**12** In fact, though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic elements of God's message.[^5-13] You have come to need milk, not solid food.

**13** Everyone who lives on milk is inexperienced in the message of righteousness,[^5-14] because they are still a baby.

**14** But solid food is for the mature, who through practice have trained their senses[^5-15] to distinguish good from evil.

[^5-1]: Greek *metriopatheo*, "keep one's feelings in check", and so "be patient, deal gently".
[^5-2]: Literally "is surrounded by weakness".
[^5-3]: Exodus 28:1.
[^5-4]: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^5-5]: Psalm 2:7 (see 1:5). Literally "today I have begotten you".
[^5-6]: Psalm 110:4, also quoted in verse 10; 6:20; 7:17, 21. The Hebrew reads "after the manner of Melchizedek". The Septuagint, quoted here, has *kata ten taxin*, "according to the order", that is, the rank or kind of priesthood. Melchizedek was king of Salem and "priest of God Most High" (Genesis 14:18–20); see chapter 7.
[^5-7]: Literally "in the days of his flesh".
[^5-8]: Or "out of death".
[^5-9]: Or "devotion", Greek *eulabeia*. Some take the phrase to mean "he was heard and set free from his fear".
[^5-10]: Greek *emathen … epathen*, "he learned … he suffered", a play on words.
[^5-11]: Greek *teleioo*, "make complete, bring to the goal" (see 2:10).
[^5-12]: Or "about him".
[^5-13]: Literally "the elements of the beginning of the oracles of God". Other editions accent the Greek differently: "you need to have someone teach you again what the basic elements … are".
[^5-14]: Or "unskilled in the word of righteousness", perhaps "unskilled in speaking about what is right".
[^5-15]: Literally "who because of habit have their faculties trained".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** So let us leave behind the elementary teaching about the Messiah[^6-1] and go on to maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works[^6-2] and of faith in God,

**2** instruction about washings[^6-3] and the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment.

**3** And this we will do, if God permits.[^6-4]

**4** For it is impossible, in the case of those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift and have come to share in the Holy Spirit,

**5** who have tasted the goodness of God's word and the powers of the age to come,

**6** and who have then fallen away,[^6-5] to renew them again to repentance, since they are crucifying the Son of God all over again for themselves[^6-6] and holding him up to public contempt.[^6-7]

**7** For land that drinks in the rain that often falls on it, and produces a crop useful to those for whom it is farmed, receives a blessing from God.

**8** But if it produces thorns and thistles,[^6-8] it is worthless and close to being cursed, and in the end it will be burned.

**9** Even though we speak like this, dear friends,[^6-9] we are convinced of better things in your case, things that belong to salvation.

**10** For God is not unjust. He will not forget your work and the love you have shown for his name, in serving God's holy people[^6-10] and in still serving them.[^6-11]

**11** We want each of you to show the same eagerness to the end, so that your hope may be fully assured.[^6-12]

**12** Then you will not become sluggish,[^6-13] but will imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

**13** When God made his promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater to swear by, he swore by himself,

**14** saying, "I will richly bless you, and I will greatly multiply you."[^6-14]

**15** And so, after waiting patiently, Abraham obtained what was promised.

**16** People swear by someone greater than themselves, and for them an oath gives confirmation and puts an end to every dispute.

**17** So when God wanted to show even more clearly to the heirs of the promise that his purpose could not change, he guaranteed it with an oath,[^6-15]

**18** so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold on to the hope set before us.

**19** We have this hope as an anchor for the soul, sure and firm, and it enters inside the veil,[^6-16]

**20** where Jesus has entered as a forerunner on our behalf, having become a high priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.[^6-17]

[^6-1]: Literally "the word of the beginning of the Messiah". *The Messiah*: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^6-2]: Probably acts that lead to death, or acts with no life in them (compare 9:14).
[^6-3]: Greek *baptismoi*, "washings", plural: ritual washings (as in 9:10), perhaps including baptism.
[^6-4]: Many manuscripts read "And let us do this, if God permits."
[^6-5]: Literally "and having fallen away", the last in a list of descriptions joined by "and". The King James Version's "if they shall fall away" reads it as a condition. Interpreters differ on whom verses 4–6 describe: true believers who can lose what they have received; people who have shared deeply in the life of the community without truly believing; or a case put as a warning, without saying that any of the readers is in it. They also differ on whether "impossible" means impossible for anyone or impossible for human teachers; the Greek does not say who would renew them.
[^6-6]: Greek *anastauroo*, which can mean "crucify again" or simply "crucify". *Since they are crucifying*: or "while they are crucifying", that is, for as long as they go on doing so. The Greek can give either the reason or the time.
[^6-7]: Greek *paradeigmatizo*, "make a public example of, expose to disgrace".
[^6-8]: Compare Genesis 3:17–18, where the ground is cursed and produces "thorns and thistles".
[^6-9]: Literally "beloved".
[^6-10]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^6-11]: Many manuscripts read "your work and the labor of love".
[^6-12]: Or "so as to have the full assurance of hope". Greek *plerophoria*, "full assurance, certainty".
[^6-13]: The same word as in 5:11.

**1** This Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High,[^7-1] met Abraham as he was returning from defeating the kings, and blessed him.[^7-2]

**2** Abraham gave him a tenth[^7-3] of everything. His name means first "king of righteousness", and then he is also king of Salem, which means "king of peace".[^7-4]

**3** He is without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life.[^7-5] Made like the Son of God, he remains a priest for all time.

**4** Consider how great this man was, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth of the best of the plunder.[^7-6]

**5** Those descendants of Levi who receive the priesthood are commanded by the law to collect a tenth from the people,[^7-7] that is, from their fellow Israelites,[^7-8] even though they too are descended from Abraham.[^7-9]

**6** But this man, who does not trace his descent from them, collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises.

**7** And without any dispute the lesser is blessed by the greater.

**8** In the one case, mortal men receive tithes. In the other, it is one who is declared to be alive.[^7-10]

**9** One might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid tithes through Abraham,

**10** for he was still in the body of his ancestor[^7-11] when Melchizedek met Abraham.

**11** Now if perfection could have been reached through the Levitical priesthood (for on the basis of it the people received the law),[^7-12] why was there still a need for another priest to arise in the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named in the order of Aaron?

**12** For when the priesthood is changed, there must also be a change of law.

**13** The one these things are said about belonged to another tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar.

**14** For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah,[^7-13] and Moses said nothing about priests in connection with that tribe.[^7-14]

**15** And it is even more evident if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,

**16** one who has become a priest not on the basis of a legal requirement about physical descent,[^7-15] but by the power of an indestructible life.

**17** For it is declared of him, "You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek."[^7-16]

**18** On the one hand, an earlier commandment is set aside because it was weak and useless

**19** (for the law made nothing perfect). On the other hand, a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God.

**20** And it was not without an oath. (Those others became priests without an oath,

**21** but he became a priest with an oath, through the one who said to him, "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind: 'You are a priest forever.'")[^7-17]

**22** By so much, then, Jesus has become the guarantee[^7-18] of a better covenant.[^7-19]

**23** There have been many of those priests, because death prevented them from continuing in office.

**24** But he holds his priesthood permanently,[^7-20] because he remains forever.

**25** So he is able to save completely[^7-21] those who come to God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them.

**26** It was fitting that we should have such a high priest: holy, innocent, undefiled, set apart from sinners, and raised higher than the heavens.

**27** He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices day after day, first for his own sins and then for those of the people.[^7-22] He did this once for all when he offered himself.

**28** For the law appoints as high priests men who are weak, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever.

**1** Faith is the assurance[^11-1] of what we hope for, the proof[^11-2] of what we cannot see.

**2** For by it the people of old[^11-3] received God's approval.

**3** By faith we understand that the universe[^11-4] was put in order by the word of God, so that what is seen did not come from things that are visible.[^11-5]

**4** By faith Abel offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did. Through it he was approved as righteous, for God approved his gifts. And through it,[^11-6] though he died, he still speaks.[^11-7]

**5** By faith Enoch was taken away so that he did not see death. "He was not found, because God had taken him away."[^11-8] For before he was taken away, he was approved as one who had pleased God.

**6** And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever comes to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.

**7** By faith Noah, when he was warned about things not yet seen, in reverent fear built an ark to save his household.[^11-9] By this he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith.

**8** By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place he was going to receive as an inheritance. He went out without knowing where he was going.[^11-10]

**9** By faith he lived as a foreigner[^11-11] in the land of the promise, as in a land not his own, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, who were heirs with him of the same promise.

**10** For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose architect and builder is God.

**11** By faith, together with Sarah herself, he received power to father a child,[^11-12] even though he was past the age for it, because he considered the one who had promised to be faithful.

**12** So from one man, and him as good as dead, there came descendants as many as the stars of the sky and as countless as the sand on the seashore.[^11-13]

**13** All these died in faith without having received the things that were promised. But they saw them and greeted them from a distance, and they acknowledged that they were strangers and foreigners on the earth.[^11-14]

**14** People who say such things make it clear that they are looking for a homeland.

**15** If they had been thinking of the land they had left, they would have had the opportunity to return.

**16** But as it is, they long for a better country, that is, a heavenly one. That is why God is not ashamed to be called their God,[^11-15] for he has prepared a city for them.

**17** By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac.[^11-16] He who had received the promises was ready to offer up his only son,[^11-17]

**18** the one of whom it had been said, "It is through Isaac that your offspring will be named."[^11-18]

**19** He reasoned that God was able even to raise people from the dead, and in a manner of speaking[^11-19] he did receive Isaac back from the dead.

**20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau with regard to things to come.[^11-20]

**21** By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and he worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff.[^11-21]

**22** By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, spoke of the exodus of the Israelites and gave instructions about his bones.[^11-22]

**23** By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents,[^11-23] because they saw that the child was beautiful,[^11-24] and they were not afraid of the king's edict.[^11-25]

**24** By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,[^11-26]

**25** choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin.

**26** He considered the reproach of the Messiah[^11-27] greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking ahead to the reward.

**27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the king's anger,[^11-28] for he persevered as one who sees him who is invisible.

**28** By faith he kept the Passover and the splashing of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them.[^11-29]

**29** By faith the people crossed the Red Sea[^11-30] as if on dry land, but when the Egyptians tried to do so, they were drowned.[^11-31]

**30** By faith the walls of Jericho fell after they had been circled for seven days.[^11-32]

**31** By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had welcomed the spies in peace.[^11-33]

**32** And what more shall I say? Time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson and Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets,

**33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, did what was right,[^11-34] obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,[^11-35]

**34** quenched the power of fire,[^11-36] escaped the edge of the sword,[^11-37] gained strength out of weakness, became mighty in war, and put foreign armies to flight.

**35** Women received back their dead by resurrection.[^11-38] Others were tortured,[^11-39] refusing to accept release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.

**36** Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and imprisonment.[^11-40]

**37** They were stoned,[^11-41] they were sawn in two,[^11-42] they were killed by the sword. They went about in sheepskins[^11-43] and goatskins, destitute, oppressed, mistreated.

**38** The world was not worthy of them. They wandered in deserts and mountains, in caves and holes in the ground.[^11-44]

**39** And all of them,[^11-45] though they were approved for their faith, did not receive what was promised,

**40** since God had foreseen something better for us, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us.

## Chapter 12

*Status: draft*

**1** Therefore, since we too are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses,[^12-1] let us also lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily entangles us,[^12-2] and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us,

**2** fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer[^12-3] and perfecter of faith. For the joy that was set before him[^12-4] he endured the cross, disregarding its shame, and he has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.

**3** Consider him who endured such hostility from sinners against themselves,[^12-5] so that you do not grow weary and lose heart.

**4** In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood.

**5** And you have forgotten the encouragement that speaks to you as sons:[^12-6] "My son, do not make light of the Lord's discipline, and do not lose heart when you are corrected by him,[^12-7]

**6** for the Lord disciplines the one he loves, and he punishes every son he accepts."[^12-8]

**7** Endure it as discipline.[^12-9] God is treating you as sons. For what son is there whom his father does not discipline?

**8** If you are left without discipline, which all children share, then you are illegitimate, and not sons.

**9** Besides, we had human fathers[^12-10] who disciplined us, and we respected them. Should we not much more submit to the Father of spirits[^12-11] and live?

**10** For they disciplined us for a short time, as seemed best to them, but he disciplines us for our good, so that we may share in his holiness.

**11** No discipline seems joyful at the time; it seems painful. But later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.

**12** So strengthen your drooping hands and your weak knees,[^12-12]

**13** and make straight paths for your feet,[^12-13] so that what is lame may not be put out of joint,[^12-14] but rather be healed.

**14** Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, without which no one will see the Lord.

**15** See to it that no one falls short of the grace of God, and that no bitter root springs up and causes trouble, and through it many become defiled.[^12-15]

**16** See that no one is sexually immoral, or godless like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal.[^12-16]

**17** For you know that afterward, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected. He found no opportunity for repentance, though he sought it with tears.[^12-17]

**18** For you have not come to something that can be touched,[^12-18] to a blazing fire, darkness, gloom and a whirlwind,[^12-19]

**19** to the sound of a trumpet and to a voice speaking words, so that those who heard it begged that no further word be spoken to them.[^12-20]

**20** For they could not bear the command, "If even an animal touches the mountain, it must be stoned."[^12-21]

**21** Indeed, so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, "I am terrified and trembling."[^12-22]

**22** But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to tens of thousands of angels in festal gathering,[^12-23]

**23** and to the church[^12-24] of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God the judge of all,[^12-25] and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect,

**24** and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant,[^12-26] and to the sprinkled blood that speaks better than the blood of Abel.[^12-27]

**25** See that you do not refuse the one who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused the one who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we turn away from the one who warns from heaven.

**26** At that time his voice shook the earth, but now he has promised, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heaven."[^12-28]

**27** The words "Yet once more" show that the things that can be shaken, as created things, will be removed, so that the things that cannot be shaken may remain.

**28** So, since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be grateful,[^12-29] and so worship God acceptably, with reverence and awe,

**29** for our God is a consuming fire.[^12-30]

## Chapter 13

**1** Let your love for one another as brothers and sisters continue.[^13-1]

**2** Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing so some have entertained angels without knowing it.[^13-2]

**3** Remember those in prison as though you were in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you too are in the body.

**4** Let marriage be held in honor by all,[^13-3] and the marriage bed kept undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.

**5** Keep your lives free from the love of money, and be content with what you have, for he himself has said, "I will never fail you or abandon you."[^13-4]

**6** So we can say with confidence, "The Lord is my helper; I will not be afraid. What can anyone do to me?"[^13-5]

**7** Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith.

**8** Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.

**9** Do not be carried away by all kinds of strange teachings. For it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods,[^13-6] which have not benefited those who live by them.

**10** We have an altar from which those who serve in the tabernacle have no right to eat.

**11** For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the sanctuary[^13-7] by the high priest as a sin offering[^13-8] are burned outside the camp.[^13-9]

**12** So Jesus also suffered outside the city gate,[^13-10] in order to make the people holy through his own blood.

**13** Let us, then, go out to him outside the camp, bearing the disgrace he bore.

**14** For here we have no lasting city, but we are looking for the city that is to come.

**15** Through him, then, let us continually offer up to God a sacrifice of praise, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name.[^13-11]

**16** Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for God is pleased with such sacrifices.

**17** Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they keep watch over your souls[^13-12] as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no benefit to you.

**18** Pray for us. We are sure that we have a clear conscience, and we want to live honorably in every way.

**20** Now may the God of peace, who brought back from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep[^13-13] by the blood of the eternal covenant,[^13-14] our Lord Jesus,

**22** I urge you, brothers and sisters,[^13-18] bear with my message of encouragement,[^13-19] for I have written to you briefly.

**23** You should know[^13-20] that our brother Timothy has been released. If he comes soon, I will see you with him.

**24** Greet all your leaders and all God's holy people.[^13-21] Those from Italy send you greetings.[^13-22]

**25** Grace be with you all.[^13-23]
