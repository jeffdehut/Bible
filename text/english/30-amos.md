## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The words of Amos,[^1-1] who was one of the sheep breeders[^1-2] of Tekoa,[^1-3] which he saw[^1-4] concerning Israel in the days of Uzziah king of Judah and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash king of Israel, two years before the earthquake.[^1-5]

**2** He said: The LORD[^1-6] roars from Zion and raises his voice[^1-7] from Jerusalem. The pastures of the shepherds mourn,[^1-8] and the top of Carmel[^1-9] withers.

**3** This is what the LORD says:[^1-10] For three crimes[^1-11] of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn it back,[^1-12] because they threshed Gilead with threshing sledges of iron.[^1-13]

**4** So I will send fire on the house of Hazael, and it will devour the fortresses[^1-14] of Ben-hadad.[^1-15]

**5** I will break the gate bar of Damascus. I will cut off the one who sits enthroned[^1-16] in the Valley of Aven, and the one who holds the scepter in Beth-eden.[^1-17] The people of Aram will go into exile to Kir,[^1-18] says the LORD.

**6** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Gaza, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they carried away whole communities[^1-19] into exile, to hand them over to Edom.

**7** So I will send fire on the wall of Gaza, and it will devour its fortresses.

**8** I will cut off the one who sits enthroned in Ashdod, and the one who holds the scepter in Ashkelon. I will turn my hand against Ekron, and the remnant of the Philistines will perish,[^1-20] says the Lord GOD.[^1-21]

**9** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Tyre, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they handed over whole communities to Edom and did not remember the covenant of brothers.[^1-22]

**10** So I will send fire on the wall of Tyre, and it will devour its fortresses.

**11** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Edom, and for four, I will not turn it back, because he pursued his brother[^1-23] with the sword and stifled all pity.[^1-24] His anger tore without end, and he kept his fury forever.[^1-25]

**12** So I will send fire on Teman, and it will devour the fortresses of Bozrah.[^1-26]

**13** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of the Ammonites, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they ripped open the pregnant women of Gilead to enlarge their territory.

**14** So I will kindle a fire on the wall of Rabbah,[^1-27] and it will devour its fortresses, with a battle cry on the day of battle, with a storm on the day of the whirlwind.

**15** Their king[^1-28] will go into exile, he and his officials together, says the LORD.

[^1-1]: A name from the verb "to carry a load".
[^1-2]: Hebrew *noqedim*, owners or keepers of sheep. The king of Moab is called a *noqed* in 2 Kings 3:4. Amos also calls himself a herdsman and a tender of sycamore figs (7:14).
[^1-3]: A town in the hills of Judah, about 10 miles (16 km) south of Jerusalem (2 Samuel 14:2).
[^1-4]: Hebrew *chazah*, "see", used of what a prophet receives in a vision.
[^1-5]: Uzziah reigned in Judah about 792–740 BC, and Jeroboam II in Israel about 793–753 BC. Amos probably spoke about 760 BC. The earthquake was remembered long afterward (Zechariah 14:5).
[^1-6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-7]: Literally "gives his voice". The same line is found in Joel 4:16 (3:16 in most English Bibles). The LORD roars like a lion (3:4, 8).
[^1-8]: Or "dry up". The Hebrew verb *aval* can mean either.
[^1-9]: The wooded ridge above the coast of northern Israel, one of the most fertile places in the land.
[^1-10]: In Amos's poetry, as in Isaiah, the words of the prophet and the words of the LORD alternate. The LORD's words in the poems are not put in quotation marks.
[^1-11]: Hebrew *pesha*, "rebellion, transgression", a deliberate breaking of what is right. Traditionally "transgressions". *For three … and for four*: a number saying (Proverbs 30:15–31). The crimes are many, and the last fills the measure. Each oracle in 1:3–2:16 then names one or more of them.
[^1-12]: Literally "I will not turn it back", that is, the punishment, or the word the LORD has spoken. Also in verses 6, 9, 11 and 13 and in 2:1, 4 and 6.
[^1-13]: Threshing sledges were heavy boards studded underneath with stones or iron spikes, dragged over grain. Gilead, east of the Jordan, belonged to Israel. Compare 2 Kings 10:32–33; 13:7.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *armenot*, the fortified palaces of a city. Also in verses 7, 10, 12 and 14.
[^1-15]: Hazael and his son Ben-hadad were kings of Aram, whose capital was Damascus (2 Kings 8:7–15; 13:3, 24).
[^1-16]: Or "the inhabitant". Also in verse 8.
[^1-17]: *Valley of Aven* means "valley of wickedness", perhaps the valley between the Lebanon ranges. *Beth-eden* means "house of delight", probably the Aramean state of Bit-adini on the Euphrates.
[^1-18]: The land the Arameans had come from (9:7). Compare 2 Kings 16:9.
[^1-19]: Literally "a complete exile". Also in verse 9. The people were sold as slaves.
[^1-20]: Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon and Ekron were four of the five chief cities of the Philistines. The fifth, Gath, is not named (compare 6:2).
[^1-21]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^1-22]: A treaty that bound the two sides as "brothers", perhaps with Israel (1 Kings 5:26, 5:12 in most English Bibles; 9:13).
[^1-23]: Israel. Edom was descended from Esau, the brother of Jacob (Genesis 25:24–26; 36:1).
[^1-24]: Literally "destroyed his compassion".
[^1-25]: Or "his fury kept watch forever". Many scholars read "he kept his anger forever" in the first line too, with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^1-26]: Teman was a region of Edom, and Bozrah one of its chief cities.
[^1-27]: The capital of the Ammonites, modern Amman in Jordan.
[^1-28]: Hebrew *malkam*, "their king". The same consonants can be read *Milkom*, Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5). A similar line in Jeremiah 49:3 adds "his priests".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** This is what the LORD[^2-1] says: For three crimes of Moab, and for four, I will not turn it back,[^2-2] because he burned the bones of the king of Edom to lime.[^2-3]

**2** So I will send fire on Moab, and it will devour the fortresses of Kerioth.[^2-4] Moab will die amid the uproar, with a battle cry and the sound of the ram's horn.[^2-5]

**3** I will cut off the ruler[^2-6] from within her and kill all her officials with him, says the LORD.

**4** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Judah, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they rejected the instruction[^2-7] of the LORD and did not keep his decrees. Their lies,[^2-8] which their ancestors followed, have led them astray.

**5** So I will send fire on Judah, and it will devour the fortresses of Jerusalem.

**6** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Israel, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they sell the innocent[^2-9] for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals.[^2-10]

**7** They trample the heads of the poor into the dust of the ground[^2-11] and push the humble out of the way.[^2-12] A man and his father go to the same young woman,[^2-13] and so they profane my holy name.

**8** They stretch out beside every altar on garments taken in pledge,[^2-14] and in the house of their God[^2-15] they drink wine taken from those they have fined.

**9** Yet it was I who destroyed the Amorite[^2-16] before them, though he was as tall as the cedars and as strong as the oaks. I destroyed his fruit above and his roots below.

**10** It was I who brought you up from the land of Egypt and led you forty years in the wilderness, to take possession of the land of the Amorite.

**11** I raised up some of your sons to be prophets, and some of your young men to be Nazirites.[^2-17] Is it not so, people of Israel? declares the LORD.[^2-18]

**12** But you made the Nazirites drink wine, and you commanded the prophets, "Do not prophesy."

**13** Look, I am going to crush you down where you stand,[^2-19] as a cart crushes when it is full of sheaves.

**14** The swift will have nowhere to flee;[^2-20] the strong will not hold on to his strength, and the warrior will not save his life.

**15** The one who handles the bow will not stand his ground, the swift of foot will not escape, and the one who rides the horse will not save his life.

**16** Even the bravest of the warriors[^2-21] will flee naked on that day, declares the LORD.

[^2-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-2]: See 1:3. Also in verses 4 and 6.
[^2-3]: Burning the bones of the dead was an outrage against them (compare 2 Kings 23:16).
[^2-4]: A city of Moab (Jeremiah 48:24). Or "the fortresses of its cities".
[^2-5]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *shofet*, "judge", here the one who governs.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^2-8]: Probably false gods.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*, "righteous", here one who is in the right, perhaps in a lawsuit.
[^2-10]: Perhaps for a debt as small as the price of sandals, or for a bribe. Compare 8:6.
[^2-11]: Literally "who pant for the dust of the earth on the head of the poor". The verb *sha'af* means "pant, long for", but here it may be a form of *shuf*, "trample". If it means "long for", they covet even the dust that the poor throw on their heads in grief.
[^2-12]: Literally "turn aside the way of the humble", that is, deny them justice.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *na'arah*, a girl or young woman, perhaps a servant in the household. Compare Leviticus 18:8, 15.
[^2-14]: The law required a cloak taken as a pledge for a debt to be given back before night (Exodus 22:25–26, 22:26–27 in most English Bibles; Deuteronomy 24:12–13).
[^2-15]: Or "their gods".
[^2-16]: Here the peoples who lived in Canaan before Israel (Genesis 15:16; Numbers 13:32–33).
[^2-17]: People dedicated to God by a vow, who drank no wine (Numbers 6:1–21).
[^2-18]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verse 16.
[^2-19]: Literally "I am pressing beneath you". The Hebrew verb *'uq* is found only here, and its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "press down", or "split open", as the ground splits under a heavy cart.
[^2-20]: Literally "flight will perish from the swift".
[^2-21]: Literally "the stout of heart among the warriors".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Hear this word that the LORD[^3-1] has spoken against you, people of Israel, against the whole clan that I brought up from the land of Egypt:

**2** You alone have I known[^3-2] of all the clans of the earth. Therefore I will punish you for all your wrongdoing.

**3** Do two walk together unless they have agreed to meet?

**4** Does a lion roar in the forest when it has no prey? Does a young lion growl from its den unless it has caught something?

**5** Does a bird fall into a trap on the ground when no snare[^3-3] is set for it? Does a trap spring up from the ground when it has caught nothing?

**6** If a ram's horn[^3-4] is blown in a city, are the people not afraid? If disaster comes to a city, has the LORD not done it?

**7** Surely the Lord GOD[^3-5] does nothing without revealing his plan[^3-6] to his servants the prophets.

**8** The lion has roared; who will not be afraid? The Lord GOD has spoken; who can do anything but prophesy?

**9** Proclaim it to the fortresses in Ashdod[^3-7] and to the fortresses in the land of Egypt, and say: "Gather on the mountains of Samaria, and see the great turmoil within her and the oppression in her midst."

**10** They do not know how to do what is right, declares the LORD,[^3-8] those who store up violence and destruction in their fortresses.

**11** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: An enemy will surround the land.[^3-9] He will strip you of your strength,[^3-10] and your fortresses will be plundered.

**12** This is what the LORD says: As a shepherd rescues from the lion's mouth two legs or a piece of an ear,[^3-11] so the people of Israel who live in Samaria will be rescued, with the corner of a couch and a piece of a bed.[^3-12]

**13** Hear, and testify against the house of Jacob, declares the Lord GOD, the God of Armies.[^3-13]

**14** On the day I punish Israel for its crimes,[^3-14] I will punish the altars of Bethel.[^3-15] The horns of the altar will be cut off and fall to the ground.

**15** I will strike the winter house together with the summer house. The houses of ivory[^3-16] will perish, and the great houses[^3-17] will come to an end, declares the LORD.

[^3-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-2]: That is, chosen and cared for as my own (Genesis 18:19; Hosea 13:5).
[^3-3]: Hebrew *moqesh*, perhaps the bait or the trigger of the trap.
[^3-4]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. Also in verses 8, 11 and 13.
[^3-6]: Hebrew *sod*, the private counsel of a ruler and his advisers (Jeremiah 23:18, 22).
[^3-7]: The Septuagint reads "Assyria".
[^3-8]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 13 and 15.
[^3-9]: Literally "An enemy, and around the land!" The Hebrew is abrupt.
[^3-10]: Literally "He will bring down your strength from you". The Hebrew addresses Samaria as "you" (feminine).
[^3-11]: A shepherd brought back such remains as proof that a wild animal had killed the sheep, so that he would not have to repay it (Exodus 22:12, 22:13 in most English Bibles; Genesis 31:39).
[^3-12]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "with the corner of a bed and with Damascus of a couch". *Damascus* may be a word for fine fabric, or a word for part of a couch. Or "so the people of Israel will be rescued, those who sit in Samaria on the corner of a bed and on the cushions of a couch".
[^3-13]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH Elohei ha-tseva'ot*, "the Lord GOD, the God of the Armies". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Compare "the LORD of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty".
[^3-14]: Literally "On the day I punish the crimes of Israel on him".
[^3-15]: The royal shrine of the northern kingdom (7:13; 1 Kings 12:28–33). The horns were the projections at the four corners of an altar. A person seeking refuge took hold of them (1 Kings 1:50).
[^3-16]: Houses decorated with carved ivory panels, like Ahab's palace in Samaria (1 Kings 22:39).
[^3-17]: Or "the many houses".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Hear this word, you cows of Bashan[^4-1] on the mountain of Samaria, who oppress the poor, who crush the needy, who say to your husbands,[^4-2] "Bring us something to drink!"

**2** The Lord GOD[^4-3] has sworn by his holiness: Look, the days are coming on you when you will be carried away with hooks, and the last of you with fishhooks.[^4-4]

**3** You will go out through the breaches in the wall, each woman straight ahead, and you will be thrown out toward Harmon,[^4-5] declares the LORD.[^4-6]

**4** Come to Bethel and rebel;[^4-7] go to Gilgal[^4-8] and rebel all the more! Bring your sacrifices every morning, your tithes every third day.[^4-9]

**5** Burn a thanksgiving offering of bread made with yeast,[^4-10] and proclaim your freewill offerings; announce them! For that is what you love to do, people of Israel, declares the Lord GOD.

**6** I gave you clean teeth[^4-11] in all your cities and a lack of bread in all your places, yet you did not return to me, declares the LORD.

**7** I also held back the rain from you when there were still three months to the harvest.[^4-12] I sent rain on one city and sent no rain on another city. One field had rain, and the field that had no rain dried up.

**8** Two or three cities staggered to one city to drink water, and could not drink their fill, yet you did not return to me, declares the LORD.

**9** I struck you with blight and mildew.[^4-13] Your many gardens and vineyards,[^4-14] your fig trees and your olive trees, the cutting locust[^4-15] devoured, yet you did not return to me, declares the LORD.

**10** I sent a plague among you as I did in Egypt.[^4-16] I killed your young men with the sword, and your horses were captured.[^4-17] I made the stench of your camps rise into your nostrils, yet you did not return to me, declares the LORD.

**11** I overthrew some of you, as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah,[^4-18] and you were like a burning stick snatched from the fire, yet you did not return to me, declares the LORD.

**12** Therefore this is what I will do to you, Israel. Because I will do this to you,[^4-19] prepare to meet your God, Israel!

**13** For look, he who forms the mountains and creates the wind,[^4-20] who tells human beings what his thoughts are,[^4-21] who turns the dawn into darkness,[^4-22] and treads on the heights of the earth: the LORD, the God of Armies,[^4-23] is his name.

[^4-1]: A fertile highland east of the Sea of Galilee, famous for its well-fed cattle (Deuteronomy 32:14; Psalm 22:13, 22:12 in most English Bibles). Here the phrase describes the wealthy women of Samaria.
[^4-2]: Literally "their lords".
[^4-3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The Lord GOD* is also in verse 5.
[^4-4]: The meaning of both words for hooks is uncertain. Or "in baskets … in fishing pots". *The last of you* may also mean "your descendants".
[^4-5]: The place is unknown. Perhaps Mount Hermon, in the north, is meant. The verb is active, literally "you will throw", perhaps "you will throw yourselves".
[^4-6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 5–11.
[^4-7]: The verb is related to *pesha*, "crime, rebellion" (1:3). The invitation is ironic: their worship at the shrines is itself rebellion.
[^4-8]: An ancient shrine near Jericho (Joshua 4:19–20; 1 Samuel 11:14–15; Hosea 4:15). Bethel, also a shrine, is about 11 miles (18 km) north of Jerusalem.
[^4-9]: Literally "for three days". Or "on the third day", perhaps of a pilgrimage. The law required tithes once a year, and a special tithe every third year (Deuteronomy 14:22–29).
[^4-10]: Bread made with yeast was eaten with a thanksgiving offering, but no yeast was to be burned on the altar (Leviticus 2:11; 7:13).
[^4-11]: That is, nothing to eat.
[^4-12]: The late rains of winter and early spring, which the grain needed before the harvest.
[^4-13]: Blight, scorching by the hot east wind, and mildew, a disease that turns grain yellow, are named together in Deuteronomy 28:22 and 1 Kings 8:37.
[^4-14]: Literally "the multitude of your gardens and your vineyards". Some scholars read "I laid waste your gardens and your vineyards", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^4-15]: Hebrew *gazam*, one of the four words for locusts in Joel 1:4. They may be different kinds of locust or stages in the insect's growth.
[^4-16]: Literally "in the way of Egypt", that is, like the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 9:3; Deuteronomy 28:27, 60). Or "on the road to Egypt".
[^4-17]: Literally "with the captivity of your horses".
[^4-18]: Genesis 19:24–25. The verb *overthrew* is the word used of their destruction.
[^4-19]: What the LORD will do is not stated.
[^4-20]: Or "spirit". Hebrew *ruach* can mean wind, breath or spirit.
[^4-21]: Or "their thoughts": the Hebrew may mean God's thoughts or human thoughts. The meaning of the word, *seach*, found only here, is uncertain.
[^4-22]: Or "who makes the dawn and the darkness". This verse is the first of three hymns in Amos that praise the LORD as Creator and end with his name (5:8–9; 9:5–6).
[^4-23]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, "the LORD, the God of Armies". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Compare "the LORD of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Hear this word, which I take up over you as a lament,[^5-1] house of Israel:

**2** Fallen, never to rise again, is the virgin Israel.[^5-2] She lies abandoned on her land, with no one to raise her up.

**3** For this is what the Lord GOD[^5-3] says: The city that marches out a thousand strong will have a hundred left, and the one that marches out a hundred strong will have ten left, for the house of Israel.

**4** For this is what the LORD says to the house of Israel: Seek me and live!

**5** But do not seek Bethel, do not go to Gilgal, and do not cross over to Beersheba.[^5-4] For Gilgal will surely go into exile, and Bethel will come to nothing.[^5-5]

**6** Seek the LORD and live, or he will break out like fire on the house of Joseph,[^5-6] and it will devour, with no one to put it out for Bethel.

**7** You who turn justice into wormwood[^5-7] and throw righteousness to the ground!

**8** He who made the Pleiades and Orion,[^5-8] who turns deep darkness[^5-9] into morning and darkens day into night, who calls for the waters of the sea and pours them out on the face of the earth: the LORD is his name.

**9** He makes destruction flash out[^5-10] against the strong, and destruction comes on the fortress.

**10** They hate the one who gives correction in the gate,[^5-11] and they detest the one who speaks with integrity.

**11** Therefore, because you trample on the poor[^5-12] and take from them a levy of grain, you have built houses of cut stone, but you will not live in them; you have planted pleasant vineyards, but you will not drink their wine.[^5-13]

**12** For I know how many your crimes are and how great your sins: you who harass the innocent, who take bribes,[^5-14] and who turn aside the needy in the gate.

**13** Therefore the prudent will keep silent at such a time, for it is an evil time.

**14** Seek good and not evil, so that you may live. Then the LORD, the God of Armies,[^5-15] will be with you, as you say he is.

**15** Hate evil, love good, and establish justice in the gate. Perhaps the LORD, the God of Armies, will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph.

**16** Therefore this is what the LORD, the God of Armies, the Lord,[^5-16] says: There will be wailing in all the squares, and in all the streets they will say, "Alas! Alas!"[^5-17] They will call the farmer to mourning, and those skilled in lament[^5-18] to wailing.

**17** In all the vineyards there will be wailing, for I will pass through your midst,[^5-19] says the LORD.

**18** Woe to[^5-20] you who long for the day of the LORD![^5-21] Why would you want the day of the LORD? It is darkness, and not light.

**19** It will be as when someone flees from a lion and a bear meets him, or goes into the house and leans his hand against the wall, and a snake bites him.

**20** Is not the day of the LORD darkness, and not light, gloom with no brightness in it?

**21** I hate, I reject your festivals; I take no delight in your solemn assemblies.[^5-22]

**22** Even though you offer me burnt offerings and your grain offerings, I will not accept them; I will not look at the fellowship offerings[^5-23] of your fattened animals.

**23** Take away from me the noise of your songs; I will not listen to the music of your harps.

**24** But let justice roll on like waters, and righteousness like a stream that never runs dry.[^5-24]

**25** Did you bring me sacrifices and grain offerings in the wilderness for forty years, house of Israel?

**26** You will carry Sikkuth your king and Kiyyun your images, the star of your god,[^5-25] which you made for yourselves,

**27** and I will send you into exile beyond Damascus,[^5-26] says the LORD, whose name is the God of Armies.

[^5-1]: Hebrew *qinah*, a funeral song. Verse 2 is in the rhythm of such a song, with a long line followed by a short one.
[^5-2]: Literally "the virgin of Israel", Israel pictured as a young woman who has died before her time.
[^5-3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-4]: An ancient shrine in the far south of Judah (Genesis 21:33; 26:23–25). On Bethel and Gilgal, see 4:4.
[^5-5]: Two plays on words. *Ha-gilgal galoh yigleh*, "Gilgal will surely go into exile". *Bethel*, "house of God", will become *aven*, "nothing" or "wickedness". Hosea uses Beth-aven, "house of wickedness", as a mocking name for Bethel (Hosea 4:15).
[^5-6]: The northern kingdom, whose chief tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, were descended from Joseph. Also in verse 15.
[^5-7]: A bitter plant. Compare 6:12.
[^5-8]: Hebrew *kimah* and *kesil*, two constellations, probably the Pleiades and Orion (Job 9:9; 38:31). This is the second of the three hymns in Amos (see 4:13).
[^5-9]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", sometimes understood as "the shadow of death" (Psalm 23:4).
[^5-10]: The Hebrew verb, found only here and in a few other places, is uncertain. Literally perhaps "makes destruction smile". Or "brings destruction suddenly".
[^5-11]: The open space by the city gate, where the elders heard cases and settled disputes. Also in verses 12 and 15.
[^5-12]: Hebrew *boshaskhem*, found only here. Perhaps "you levy taxes on the poor", which fits the next words.
[^5-13]: Compare Deuteronomy 28:30, 39; Zephaniah 1:13.
[^5-14]: Hebrew *kofer*, a payment that buys someone's release from the penalty they deserve (Numbers 35:31).
[^5-15]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, "the LORD, the God of Armies". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Compare "the LORD of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". Also in verses 15, 16 and 27.
[^5-16]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH.
[^5-17]: Hebrew *ho ho*, a cry of grief, related to *hoy* (verse 18).
[^5-18]: Professional mourners, often women (Jeremiah 9:16, 9:17 in most English Bibles).
[^5-19]: As the LORD passed through Egypt on the night of the Passover (Exodus 12:12).
[^5-20]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^5-21]: The day when the LORD would act in power. The people expected it to bring victory and light for Israel.
[^5-22]: Hebrew *atsarah*, a festive gathering for worship. Literally "I will not smell your solemn assemblies", the pleasing smell of their offerings (Leviticus 26:31).
[^5-23]: Hebrew *shelem*, the sacrifice shared in a meal by the worshipers (Leviticus 3; 7:11–21).
[^5-24]: Hebrew *nachal eitan*, "a lasting stream". Most streams in the land flow only in the rainy season.
[^5-25]: *Sikkuth* and *Kiyyun* are probably names of Mesopotamian gods connected with the planet Saturn, known from Assyrian texts as Sakkut and Kaiwan. The Hebrew vowels make them sound like *shiqquts*, "detestable thing". Or "the shrine of your king and the stand of your images". The Septuagint reads "You took up the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Rephan, their images", and Acts 7:42–43 quotes verses 25–27 in that form. *You will carry* may also be translated "You carried".
[^5-26]: Acts 7:43 reads "beyond Babylon".

## Chapter 6

**1** Woe to[^6-1] those who are at ease in Zion, and to those who feel secure on the mountain of Samaria, the notable men of the first of the nations, to whom the house of Israel comes!

**2** Cross over to Calneh and look; go from there to Hamath the great; then go down to Gath of the Philistines.[^6-2] Are they better than these kingdoms? Is their territory larger than yours?

**3** You push away the day of disaster, yet you bring near the reign of violence.[^6-3]

**4** They lie on beds of ivory and sprawl on their couches. They eat lambs from the flock and calves from the stall.

**5** They improvise[^6-4] to the sound of the harp; like David, they invent musical instruments for themselves.

**6** They drink wine by the bowlful[^6-5] and anoint themselves with the finest oils, but they do not grieve over the ruin of Joseph.

**7** Therefore they will now go into exile at the head of the exiles, and the feasting[^6-6] of those who sprawl will come to an end.

**8** The Lord GOD[^6-7] has sworn by himself,[^6-8] declares the LORD,[^6-9] the God of Armies:[^6-10] I loathe the pride of Jacob and hate his fortresses. I will hand over the city and everything in it.

**9** If ten men are left in one house, they will die.

**10** And when a relative, the one who burns the dead,[^6-11] picks up a body to carry the bones out of the house, he will say to someone in the back of the house, "Is anyone else with you?" He will say, "No one." Then he will say, "Hush! We must not mention the name of the LORD."[^6-12]

**11** For look, the LORD is giving the command, and he will strike the great house into fragments and the small house into splinters.

**12** Do horses run on rock? Does anyone plow there with oxen?[^6-13] Yet you have turned justice into poison[^6-14] and the fruit of righteousness into wormwood.

**13** You who rejoice over Lo-debar,[^6-15] who say, "Did we not take Karnaim for ourselves by our own strength?"

**14** For look, I am raising up a nation against you, house of Israel, declares the LORD, the God of Armies, and they will oppress you from Lebo-hamath to the Wadi of the Arabah.[^6-16]

[^6-1]: Hebrew *hoy*. See 5:18.
[^6-2]: Calneh and Hamath were cities in northern Syria, and Gath one of the five Philistine cities. All three had been conquered (Isaiah 10:9; 2 Kings 12:18, 12:17 in most English Bibles). The point may be that Israel is no safer than they were.
[^6-3]: Literally "the seat of violence", perhaps a court of injustice.
[^6-4]: Hebrew *paratim*, found only here. Perhaps "strum" or "sing idle songs".
[^6-5]: Literally "from bowls". The word is used for the large bowls in which the blood of sacrifices was collected (Exodus 27:3).
[^6-6]: Hebrew *marzeach*, a feast held by a group of wealthy men, sometimes in connection with mourning (Jeremiah 16:5). *Sprawl* is the word used in verse 4.
[^6-7]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^6-8]: Literally "by his *nephesh*", "by his life".
[^6-9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verse 14.
[^6-10]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*. See 4:13. Also in verse 14.
[^6-11]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "his uncle and the one who burns him". Burning the dead was unusual in Israel and may point to a plague. Or "the one who burns spices for him" in honor of the dead (Jeremiah 34:5).
[^6-12]: To call on the LORD, even in a blessing or a vow, might draw his attention and bring more death.
[^6-13]: Literally "Does one plow with the oxen?" The same letters, divided into words differently, can be read "Does one plow the sea with oxen?"
[^6-14]: Hebrew *rosh*, a poisonous plant. On wormwood, see 5:7.
[^6-15]: Hebrew *lo davar*, which means "nothing". Lo-debar and Karnaim, "horns", a picture of strength, were towns in Gilead, east of the Jordan, which Israel had recently taken back from Aram (2 Samuel 9:4; 2 Kings 14:25).
[^6-16]: Lebo-hamath, "the entrance to Hamath", was on the northern border of Israel, and the Wadi of the Arabah near the Dead Sea, in the south. These were the borders that Jeroboam II had restored (2 Kings 14:25).

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** This is what the Lord GOD[^7-1] showed me: he was forming a swarm of locusts[^7-2] when the late crop[^7-3] was beginning to come up. It was the late crop after the king's mowings.[^7-4]

**2** When they had finished eating the plants of the land, I said, "Lord GOD, forgive, please! How can Jacob survive?[^7-5] He is so small."

**3** The LORD relented[^7-6] concerning this. "It will not happen," said the LORD.

**4** This is what the Lord GOD showed me: the Lord GOD was calling for judgment by fire.[^7-7] It devoured the great deep[^7-8] and was devouring the land.[^7-9]

**5** Then I said, "Lord GOD, stop, please! How can Jacob survive? He is so small."

**6** The LORD relented concerning this. "This too will not happen," said the Lord GOD.

**7** This is what he showed me: the Lord was standing beside a wall built with a plumb line, with a plumb line in his hand.[^7-10]

**8** The LORD said to me, "What do you see, Amos?" I said, "A plumb line." Then the Lord said, "Look, I am setting a plumb line among my people Israel. I will not pass them by again.[^7-11]

**9** The high places[^7-12] of Isaac[^7-13] will be made desolate, and the sanctuaries of Israel will be laid waste. I will rise against the house of Jeroboam with the sword."

**10** Then Amaziah the priest of Bethel sent a message to Jeroboam king of Israel: "Amos has conspired against you in the very heart of the house of Israel. The land cannot bear all his words.

**11** For this is what Amos has said: 'Jeroboam will die by the sword, and Israel will surely go into exile away from its land.'"

**12** Amaziah said to Amos, "Seer,[^7-14] go! Run away to the land of Judah. Earn your living[^7-15] there, and prophesy there.

**13** But never prophesy at Bethel again, for it is the king's sanctuary and a royal temple."[^7-16]

**14** Amos answered Amaziah, "I am not a prophet, and I am not a prophet's son.[^7-17] I am a herdsman and a tender of sycamore figs.[^7-18]

**15** But the LORD took me from following the flock, and the LORD said to me, 'Go, prophesy to my people Israel.'

**16** So now hear the word of the LORD. You say, 'Do not prophesy against Israel, and do not preach[^7-19] against the house of Isaac.'

**17** Therefore this is what the LORD says: Your wife will become a prostitute in the city, and your sons and your daughters will fall by the sword. Your land will be divided up with a measuring line, and you yourself will die in an unclean land.[^7-20] And Israel will surely go into exile away from its land."

[^7-1]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Also in verses 2, 4, 5 and 6.
[^7-2]: Hebrew *govai*, a word for locusts not among the four in Joel 1:4 (compare Nahum 3:17).
[^7-3]: Hebrew *leqesh*, the later growth of grass and grain after the spring rains.
[^7-4]: Perhaps the first cutting of the grass, taken as a tax for the king's animals (1 Kings 18:5). After it, the late crop was all the people had.
[^7-5]: Literally "Who is Jacob, that he could stand?" Also in verse 5.
[^7-6]: Or "changed his mind". Also in verse 6.
[^7-7]: Literally "calling to contend by fire", as in a lawsuit. Some scholars read "calling for a shower of fire", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^7-8]: Hebrew *tehom rabbah*, the waters under the earth that fed the springs and rivers (Genesis 7:11).
[^7-9]: Literally "the portion", perhaps the land allotted to Israel.
[^7-10]: Hebrew *anakh*, found only in verses 7–8. The traditional meaning is "plumb line", a cord with a weight of lead, used to test whether a wall stands straight. The word may instead mean "tin", a metal used in making weapons: "standing on a wall of tin, with tin in his hand".
[^7-11]: That is, I will no longer pass over their sins and spare them. Also in 8:2.
[^7-12]: Hill shrines where sacrifices were offered.
[^7-13]: A poetic name for Israel, found only in Amos (also verse 16). Isaac was the father of Jacob.
[^7-14]: Hebrew *chozeh*, "one who sees visions", from the verb in 1:1.
[^7-15]: Literally "eat bread".
[^7-16]: Literally "a house of the kingdom".
[^7-17]: The Hebrew has no verb. Or "I was not a prophet …; I was a herdsman". A *prophet's son* is a member of a group of prophets (2 Kings 2:3, 5).
[^7-18]: The fruit of the sycamore-fig tree had to be slit or pinched to make it ripen. The tree does not grow at the height of Tekoa (1:1), so Amos may have worked in the lowlands.
[^7-19]: Literally "drip", as words flow from the mouth (Micah 2:6, 11).
[^7-20]: A foreign land, unclean because other gods are worshiped there.

## Chapter 8

*Status: draft*

**1** This is what the Lord GOD[^8-1] showed me: a basket of summer fruit.[^8-2]

**2** He said, "What do you see, Amos?" I said, "A basket of summer fruit." Then the LORD said to me, "The end[^8-3] has come for my people Israel. I will not pass them by again.[^8-4]

**3** On that day the songs of the temple[^8-5] will become wailing, declares the Lord GOD.[^8-6] So many corpses, thrown out everywhere! Hush!"[^8-7]

**4** Hear this, you who trample[^8-8] the needy and bring the poor of the land[^8-9] to an end,

**5** who say, "When will the new moon be over, so that we can sell grain, and the Sabbath, so that we can open the stores of wheat?[^8-10] We will make the ephah small and the shekel heavy[^8-11] and cheat with dishonest scales,

**6** buying the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals,[^8-12] and selling even the sweepings of the wheat."

**7** The LORD has sworn by the Pride of Jacob:[^8-13] I will never forget anything they have done.

**8** Will not the land tremble because of this, and everyone who lives in it mourn? All of it will rise like the Nile,[^8-14] be tossed about, and sink again like the river of Egypt.

**9** On that day, declares the Lord GOD, I will make the sun go down at noon and darken the earth in broad daylight.[^8-15]

**10** I will turn your festivals into mourning and all your songs into a lament. I will put sackcloth on every waist and shave every head bald.[^8-16] I will make it like the mourning for an only son, and its end like a bitter day.

**11** Look, the days are coming, declares the Lord GOD, when I will send a famine on the land: not a famine of bread or a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD.

**12** People will stagger from sea to sea[^8-17] and wander from north to east, running back and forth to seek the word of the LORD, but they will not find it.

**13** On that day the beautiful young women and the young men will faint from thirst.

**14** Those who swear by the guilt of Samaria,[^8-18] who say, "As your god lives, Dan," and "As the way to Beersheba lives,"[^8-19] will fall and never rise again.

[^8-1]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The Lord GOD* is also in verses 3, 9 and 11.
[^8-2]: Hebrew *kelub qayits*. *Qayits* is the fruit of late summer, chiefly figs, gathered at the end of the farming year.
[^8-3]: Hebrew *qets*, "end". The vision rests on a play on words: *qayits*, "summer fruit", sounds like *qets*, "end". The ripe fruit at the end of the year points to Israel's end.
[^8-4]: See 7:8.
[^8-5]: Or "palace". Some scholars read "the singing women of the palace", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^8-6]: Hebrew *ne'um Adonai YHWH*, "the declaration of the Lord GOD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 9 and 11.
[^8-7]: The Hebrew is abrupt. Literally "Many the corpse; in every place he has thrown; hush!" Compare 6:10.
[^8-8]: See 2:7.
[^8-9]: The Hebrew text is written "the humble of the land" but read "the poor of the land".
[^8-10]: Trading stopped on the new moon and the Sabbath (2 Kings 4:23; Nehemiah 10:32, 10:31 in most English Bibles). *Open the stores of wheat*: literally "open wheat".
[^8-11]: The ephah was the measure in which grain was sold, probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel). The shekel was the weight, about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams), used to weigh the buyer's silver. A small measure and a heavy weight cheated the buyer twice.
[^8-12]: Compare 2:6.
[^8-13]: Perhaps a title of God, like "the Glory of Israel" (1 Samuel 15:29). Or the LORD swears by the arrogance of Jacob (6:8), which is as sure not to change as his own oath.

[^8-16]: Literally "I will bring up sackcloth on all waists and baldness on every head". Wearing sackcloth and shaving the head were signs of mourning (Isaiah 15:2–3; Micah 1:16).
[^8-17]: From the Dead Sea in the east to the Mediterranean in the west, that is, the length and breadth of the land.

**1** I saw the Lord[^9-1] standing beside the altar,[^9-2] and he said: Strike the tops of the pillars so that the thresholds shake, and shatter them on the heads of all the people.[^9-3] Those who are left of them I will kill with the sword. Not one of them will get away; not one will escape.

**2** If they dig down into Sheol,[^9-4] from there my hand will take them. If they climb up to the heavens, from there I will bring them down.

**3** If they hide on the top of Carmel, from there I will search them out and take them. If they hide from my sight on the floor of the sea, from there I will command the serpent,[^9-5] and it will bite them.

**4** If they go into captivity before their enemies, from there I will command the sword, and it will kill them. I will set my eyes on them for harm and not for good.

**5** The Lord GOD of Armies[^9-6] touches the earth, and it melts, and all who live in it mourn. All of it rises like the Nile and sinks again like the river of Egypt.

**7** Are you not like the Cushites[^9-9] to me, people of Israel? declares the LORD.[^9-10] Did I not bring Israel up from the land of Egypt, and the Philistines from Caphtor,[^9-11] and the Arameans from Kir?

**8** Look, the eyes of the Lord GOD are on the sinful kingdom, and I will destroy it from the face of the earth. Yet I will not completely destroy the house of Jacob, declares the LORD.

**10** All the sinners among my people will die by the sword, those who say, "Disaster will not come near us or overtake us."

**11** On that day I will raise up the fallen shelter[^9-13] of David. I will repair its broken walls and raise up its ruins, and I will rebuild it as it was in days of old,

**13** Look, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when the one who plows will overtake the one who reaps, and the one who treads grapes the one who sows the seed.[^9-16] The mountains will drip sweet wine, and all the hills will flow with it.[^9-17]

**14** I will restore the fortunes[^9-18] of my people Israel. They will rebuild the ruined cities and live in them; they will plant vineyards and drink their wine; they will make gardens and eat their fruit.

**15** I will plant them on their land, and they will never again be uprooted from the land that I have given them, says the LORD your God.

[^9-8]: Hebrew *aguddah*, "a band, a bundle", here probably the dome of the sky, pictured as resting on the earth.
