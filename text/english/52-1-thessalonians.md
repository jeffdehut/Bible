## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, Silvanus and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:[^1-1] Grace to you and peace.[^1-2]

**2** We always give thanks to God for all of you, mentioning you in our prayers, constantly

**3** remembering before our God and Father your work that comes from faith, your labor prompted by love, and your endurance that comes from hope in our Lord Jesus Christ.[^1-3]

**4** For we know, brothers and sisters[^1-4] loved by God, that he has chosen you,[^1-5]

**5** because our good news came to you not in word only, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit and with full conviction. You know what kind of people we proved to be among you for your sake.

**6** And you became imitators of us and of the Lord, for you received the word in much affliction with the joy of the Holy Spirit,

**7** so that you became an example to all the believers in Macedonia and in Achaia.

**8** For from you the word of the Lord has sounded forth not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but your faith in God has gone out everywhere, so that we have no need to say anything.

**9** For they themselves report about us what kind of welcome we had among you,[^1-6] and how you turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God,

**10** and to wait for his Son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, Jesus, who rescues us from the coming wrath.

[^1-1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-2]: Many manuscripts add "from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ".
[^1-3]: Literally "your work of faith and labor of love and endurance of hope of our Lord Jesus Christ".
[^1-4]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-5]: Literally "knowing your election".
[^1-6]: Literally "what kind of entrance we had to you".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** For you yourselves know, brothers and sisters,[^2-1] that our visit to you was not in vain,

**2** but though we had already suffered and been shamefully treated at Philippi, as you know, we had the courage in our God to speak to you the good news of God in the face of much opposition.

**3** For our appeal does not come from error or impure motives, nor is it made with deceit,

**4** but just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the good news, so we speak, not to please people, but to please God, who tests our hearts.[^2-2]

**5** For we never came with words of flattery, as you know, nor with a pretext for greed (God is witness),

**6** nor did we seek glory from people, whether from you or from others,

**7** although we could have made our weight felt[^2-3] as apostles of Christ. Instead we were gentle[^2-4] among you, like a nursing mother caring for her own children.

**8** So, because we cared for you so much, we were glad to share with you not only the good news of God but also our own lives, because you had become dear to us.

**9** For you remember, brothers and sisters, our labor and toil. We worked night and day so as not to be a burden to any of you while we proclaimed to you the good news of God.

**10** You are witnesses, and God also, how holy and righteous and blameless was our conduct toward you who believe,

**11** just as you know how we treated each one of you, as a father treats his own children,[^2-5]

**12** encouraging you, comforting you, and urging you to live[^2-6] in a way worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory.

**13** And for this reason we also thank God constantly, that when you received the word of God that you heard from us,[^2-7] you accepted it not as a human word but as what it really is, the word of God, which is also at work in you who believe.

**14** For you, brothers and sisters, became imitators of the churches of God in Christ Jesus that are in Judea, because you suffered the same things from your own countrymen as they did from the Jews,[^2-8]

**15** who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets,[^2-9] and drove us out. They do not please God and are hostile to all people,

**16** hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles so that they may be saved. So they always fill up the measure of their sins.[^2-10] But wrath has come upon them at last.[^2-11]

**17** But we, brothers and sisters, when we were torn away[^2-12] from you for a short time, in person but not in heart, were all the more eager, with great longing, to see you face to face.

**18** For we wanted to come to you (I, Paul, more than once[^2-13]), but Satan[^2-14] hindered us.

**19** For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting (is it not you?) before our Lord Jesus at his coming?[^2-15]

**20** For you are our glory and our joy.

[^2-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 9, 14 and 17.
[^2-2]: Compare Jeremiah 11:20.
[^2-3]: Or "we could have been a burden to you" (compare verse 9). Literally "to be in weight". In some English Bibles these words are the end of verse 6.
[^2-4]: Greek *epioi*. Some manuscripts read *nepioi*, "infants".
[^2-5]: The Greek has no verb here; literally "how each one of you, as a father his own children, encouraging you …".
[^2-6]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^2-7]: Literally "having received from us a word of hearing of God".
[^2-8]: Greek *Ioudaioi*, "Jews" or "Judeans". The comparison is with the opposition that the churches in Judea faced from their own people.
[^2-9]: Or "who killed the Lord Jesus, and drove out the prophets and us". Many manuscripts read "their own prophets".
[^2-10]: Literally "so as always to fill up their sins".
[^2-11]: Or "completely", or "to the end". Greek *eis telos*.
[^2-12]: Greek *aporphanizo*, "make an orphan of".
[^2-13]: Literally "both once and twice".
[^2-14]: Greek *Satanas*, from Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, accuser".
[^2-15]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord Jesus. The word recurs in 3:13; 4:15; 5:23.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** So when we could bear it no longer, we thought it best to be left behind in Athens alone,

**2** and we sent Timothy, our brother and God's fellow worker[^3-1] in the good news of Christ, to strengthen you and encourage you in your faith,

**3** so that no one would be shaken by these afflictions. For you yourselves know that we are destined for this.

**4** For indeed, when we were with you, we kept telling you in advance that we were going to suffer affliction, and so it has happened, as you know.

**5** For this reason, when I could bear it no longer, I sent to find out about your faith, for fear that somehow the tempter[^3-2] had tempted you and our labor would be in vain.

**6** But now Timothy has come to us from you and has brought us the good news of your faith and love, and that you always remember us kindly and long to see us, just as we long to see you.

**7** For this reason, brothers and sisters,[^3-3] in all our distress and affliction we have been comforted about you through your faith.

**8** For now we live, if you stand firm in the Lord.

**9** For what thanksgiving can we return to God for you, for all the joy with which we rejoice because of you before our God,

**10** as we pray most earnestly night and day that we may see you face to face and supply what is lacking in your faith?

**11** Now may our God and Father himself and our Lord Jesus direct[^3-4] our way to you.

**12** And may the Lord make you increase and overflow in love for one another and for everyone, just as we do for you,

**13** so that he may strengthen your hearts, blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming[^3-5] of our Lord Jesus with all his holy ones.[^3-6]

[^3-1]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28. Other editions read "God's servant", and many manuscripts "God's servant and our fellow worker".
[^3-2]: Or "the one who tests". Greek *peirazo*, "test, tempt".
[^3-3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^3-4]: The Greek verb is singular, though it has two subjects.
[^3-5]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord Jesus (see 2:19).
[^3-6]: Greek *hagioi*, "holy ones", who may be angels (compare Zechariah 14:5) or God's holy people. Some manuscripts add "Amen".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Finally, then, brothers and sisters,[^4-1] we ask you and urge you in the Lord Jesus that, as you received from us how you ought to live[^4-2] and to please God, as in fact you are doing,[^4-3] you do so more and more.

**2** For you know what instructions we gave you through the Lord Jesus.

**3** For this is the will of God, your holiness:[^4-4] that you abstain from sexual immorality,

**4** that each of you know how to gain control over his own body[^4-5] in holiness and honor,

**5** not in lustful passion like the Gentiles who do not know God,

**6** and that no one wrong his brother or take advantage of him in this matter,[^4-6] because the Lord is an avenger in all these things, as we told you before and solemnly warned you.

**7** For God has not called us to impurity, but in holiness.

**8** So whoever rejects this rejects not a human being but God, who also gives his Holy Spirit to you.[^4-7]

**9** Now concerning love for one another as brothers and sisters,[^4-8] you have no need for anyone to write to you, for you yourselves are taught by God[^4-9] to love one another.

**10** And indeed you are doing it toward all the brothers and sisters throughout Macedonia. But we urge you, brothers and sisters, to do so more and more,

**11** and to make it your ambition to live quietly, to mind your own affairs, and to work with your hands, as we instructed you,

**12** so that you may live properly in the eyes of outsiders and be dependent on no one.[^4-10]

**13** We do not want you to be uninformed, brothers and sisters, about those who are asleep,[^4-11] so that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope.

**14** For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep through Jesus.[^4-12]

**15** For this we tell you by the word of the Lord: we who are alive, who are left until the coming[^4-13] of the Lord, will certainly not go ahead of those who have fallen asleep.

**16** For the Lord himself will come down from heaven with a cry of command, with the voice of an archangel and with the trumpet of God, and the dead in Christ will rise first.

**17** Then we who are alive, who are left, will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet[^4-14] the Lord in the air. And so we will always be with the Lord.

**18** So encourage one another with these words.

[^4-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 10 and 13.
[^4-2]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life; also in verse 12.
[^4-3]: Many manuscripts leave out "as in fact you are doing".
[^4-4]: Or "your sanctification"; also in verses 4 and 7. Greek *hagiasmos*, "being made holy".
[^4-5]: Literally "to acquire his own vessel". "Vessel" may mean a man's own body, or a wife (compare 1 Peter 3:7), so this may also mean "to take a wife for himself".
[^4-6]: Or "in business".
[^4-7]: Compare Ezekiel 36:27; 37:14.
[^4-8]: Greek *philadelphia*, "love of brothers".
[^4-9]: Greek *theodidaktoi*, "God-taught". Compare Isaiah 54:13.
[^4-10]: Or "lack nothing".
[^4-11]: That is, those who have died; also "fallen asleep" in verses 14 and 15.
[^4-12]: Or "God will bring with him, through Jesus, those who have fallen asleep".
[^4-13]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord (see 2:19).
[^4-14]: Greek *apantesis*, "meeting", a word used for going out to meet an arriving dignitary and escort him in.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Now concerning the times and the seasons, brothers and sisters,[^5-1] you have no need for anything to be written to you.

**2** For you yourselves know very well that the day of the Lord[^5-2] will come like a thief in the night.

**3** While people are saying, "Peace and security," sudden destruction will come on them, like labor pains on a pregnant woman, and they will not escape.

**4** But you, brothers and sisters, are not in darkness, so that the day would overtake you like a thief.[^5-3]

**5** For you are all children of light and children of the day.[^5-4] We do not belong to the night or to darkness.

**6** So then let us not sleep, as others do, but let us stay awake and be sober.

**7** For those who sleep, sleep at night, and those who get drunk are drunk at night.

**8** But since we belong to the day, let us be sober, putting on the breastplate of faith and love, and for a helmet the hope of salvation.[^5-5]

**9** For God did not appoint us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ,

**10** who died for us so that, whether we are awake or asleep,[^5-6] we may live together with him.

**11** So encourage one another and build one another up, just as you are doing.

**12** We ask you, brothers and sisters, to acknowledge[^5-7] those who labor among you and lead you[^5-8] in the Lord and admonish you,

**13** and to esteem them very highly in love because of their work. Be at peace among yourselves.[^5-9]

**14** And we urge you, brothers and sisters: warn the idle,[^5-10] encourage the fainthearted, help the weak, be patient with everyone.

**15** See that no one repays anyone evil for evil, but always pursue what is good for one another and for everyone.

**16** Always rejoice,

**17** pray constantly,

**18** give thanks in all circumstances; for this is God's will for you in Christ Jesus.

**19** Do not quench the Spirit.

**20** Do not despise prophecies,

**21** but test everything; hold fast to what is good.

**22** Keep away from every form of evil.[^5-11]

**23** May the God of peace himself make you completely holy, and may your whole spirit and soul and body be kept blameless at the coming[^5-12] of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**24** The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it.

**25** Brothers and sisters, pray for us.

**26** Greet all the brothers and sisters with a holy kiss.

**27** I make you swear by the Lord that this letter be read to all the brothers and sisters.[^5-13]

**28** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.[^5-14]

[^5-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 4, 12, 14, 25, 26 and 27.
[^5-2]: In the Old Testament, "the day of Yahweh" is the day of his coming in judgment (Amos 5:18–20; Joel 2:1–2).
[^5-3]: Some manuscripts read "would overtake you like thieves".
[^5-4]: Literally "sons of light and sons of day".
[^5-5]: Compare Isaiah 59:17.
[^5-6]: That is, alive or dead (compare 4:13–15); or, keeping the image of verses 6–7, watchful or not.
[^5-7]: Literally "to know". Or "to respect".
[^5-8]: Or "care for you". Greek *proistemi*, "stand before, lead, care for".
[^5-9]: Some manuscripts read "Be at peace with them".
[^5-10]: Or "the disorderly". Greek *ataktoi*, "out of order", used of soldiers out of line and of people who neglect their work (compare 4:11; 2 Thessalonians 3:6–11).
[^5-11]: Or "every kind of evil".
[^5-12]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord (see 2:19).
[^5-13]: Many manuscripts read "to all the holy brothers and sisters".
[^5-14]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
