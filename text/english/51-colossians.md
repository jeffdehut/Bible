## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus[^1-1] by the will of God, and Timothy our brother,

**2** to the holy and faithful brothers and sisters[^1-2] in Christ at Colossae: Grace to you and peace from God our Father.[^1-3]

**3** We always thank God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, when we pray for you,

**4** since we heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of the love you have for all God's holy people,[^1-4]

**5** because of the hope laid up for you in heaven. You heard of this hope before in the word of the truth, the good news

**6** that has come to you. Just as in the whole world it is bearing fruit and growing, so it has been among you also, from the day you heard it and came to know the grace of God in truth,

**7** just as you learned it from Epaphras, our beloved fellow servant,[^1-5] who is a faithful servant[^1-6] of Christ on our behalf,[^1-7]

**8** and who has told us of your love in the Spirit.

**9** For this reason, from the day we heard of it, we too have not stopped praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,

**10** so that you may live[^1-8] in a way worthy of the Lord, pleasing him in every way, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the knowledge of God,

**11** being strengthened with all power according to his glorious might,[^1-9] for all endurance and patience with joy,[^1-10]

**12** giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified you[^1-11] to share in the inheritance[^1-12] of God's holy people in the light.

**13** He has rescued us from the authority of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son he loves,[^1-13]

**14** in whom we have redemption,[^1-14] the forgiveness of sins.

**15** He is the image of the invisible God,[^1-15] the firstborn of all creation,[^1-16]

**16** for in him[^1-17] all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities. All things have been created through him and for him.

**17** He is before all things, and in him all things hold together.

**18** He is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning,[^1-18] the firstborn from the dead, so that in everything he himself might be first.

**19** For in him all the fullness was pleased to dwell,[^1-19]

**20** and through him to reconcile all things to himself,[^1-20] making peace through the blood of his cross, [through him,][^1-21] whether the things on earth or the things in heaven.

**21** And you, who once were alienated and hostile in mind, doing evil deeds[^1-22]—

**22** but now you have been reconciled[^1-23] in his body of flesh[^1-24] through his death—so as to present you holy and blameless and above reproach before him,

**23** if indeed you continue in the faith, established and firm, not shifting from the hope of the good news that you heard, which has been proclaimed in all creation[^1-25] under heaven, and of which I, Paul, became a servant.

**24** Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and in my flesh I am filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions[^1-26] for the sake of his body, which is the church.

**25** I became its servant according to the stewardship[^1-27] from God that was given to me for you, to make the word of God fully known,[^1-28]

**26** the mystery[^1-29] that has been hidden for ages and generations, but now has been revealed to his holy people.

**27** To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you,[^1-30] the hope of glory.

**28** Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, so that we may present everyone mature[^1-31] in Christ.

**29** For this I toil, struggling with all his energy, which he powerfully works within me.[^1-32]

[^1-1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike. *Holy*: or "to God's holy people and faithful brothers and sisters". Greek *hagioi*, "holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^1-3]: Many manuscripts add "and the Lord Jesus Christ".
[^1-4]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints"; also in verses 12 and 26.
[^1-5]: Greek *syndoulos*, "fellow slave".
[^1-6]: Greek *diakonos*, "servant", traditionally "minister"; also in verses 23 and 25.
[^1-7]: Many manuscripts read "on your behalf".
[^1-8]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^1-9]: Literally "according to the might of his glory".
[^1-10]: Or "… patience. With joy give thanks …", taking "with joy" with verse 12.
[^1-11]: Many manuscripts read "us".
[^1-12]: Literally "for the share of the lot", the language of the dividing of the land among the tribes of Israel (Joshua 14–19).
[^1-13]: Literally "the Son of his love".
[^1-14]: Greek *apolytrosis*, release, as of a slave or captive set free by a payment. The Textus Receptus (followed by the King James Version) adds "through his blood"; compare Ephesians 1:7.
[^1-15]: Verses 15–20 have the form of a hymn or poem in two parts: Christ and creation (verses 15–17) and Christ and the new creation (verses 18–20). Each part opens with "He is" and speaks of him as "firstborn".
[^1-16]: Greek *prototokos*, "firstborn". The word can mean first in time or first in rank, the one who holds a firstborn son's rights (compare Psalm 89:28, 89:27 in most English Bibles). *Of all creation*: or "over all creation".
[^1-17]: Or "by him".
[^1-18]: Greek *arche*, "beginning, origin, first place"; the same word is translated "rulers" in verse 16.
[^1-19]: Or "For God was pleased to have all the fullness dwell in him". The subject of "was pleased" is not stated, and may be "all the fullness" or God.
[^1-20]: Or "to him".
[^1-21]: The SBLGNT prints these words in brackets [ ], marking their place in the text as uncertain; this translation does the same. Some manuscripts leave them out.
[^1-22]: Literally "in evil works". The sentence breaks off here and starts again in verse 22.
[^1-23]: The SBLGNT here reads "you have been reconciled", following Holmes; other editions and many manuscripts read "he has now reconciled you".
[^1-24]: That is, his physical body. Greek *sarx*, "flesh", in its physical sense; also in verse 24.
[^1-25]: Or "to every creature".
[^1-26]: Literally "I fill up in turn the things lacking of the afflictions of the Christ in my flesh". The meaning is debated.
[^1-27]: Greek *oikonomia*, the task of a steward who manages what belongs to another.
[^1-28]: Literally "to fulfill the word of God".
[^1-29]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it; also in verse 27.
[^1-30]: Or "among you".
[^1-31]: Greek *teleios*, "mature, complete".
[^1-32]: Literally "according to his working, which works in me in power".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** For I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you and for those in Laodicea and for all who have not seen me face to face,[^2-1]

**2** so that their hearts may be encouraged, as they are united[^2-2] in love, and so that they may have all the riches of full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God's mystery,[^2-3] which is Christ.

**3** In him[^2-4] all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.

**4** I say this so that no one may deceive you with persuasive arguments.

**5** For though I am absent in body,[^2-5] yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ.

**6** So then, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord,[^2-6] live[^2-7] in him,

**7** rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, overflowing with thanksgiving.[^2-8]

**8** See to it that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elements of the world,[^2-9] and not according to Christ.

**9** For in him the whole fullness of deity[^2-10] dwells bodily,

**10** and you have been filled in him, who is the head of every ruler and authority.

**11** In him you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, by putting off the body of the flesh,[^2-11] in the circumcision of Christ,[^2-12]

**12** having been buried with him in baptism, in which[^2-13] you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead.

**13** And you, who were dead in your wrongdoings and in the uncircumcision of your flesh, he[^2-14] made alive together with him, having forgiven us all our wrongdoings,

**14** by canceling the record of debt[^2-15] that stood against us with its legal demands. He has taken it out of the way, nailing it to the cross.

**15** He disarmed[^2-16] the rulers and the authorities and made a public example of them, leading them in triumph[^2-17] in it.[^2-18]

**16** So let no one judge you in matters of food or drink, or with regard to a festival or a new moon or Sabbaths.

**17** These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.[^2-19]

**18** Let no one disqualify you,[^2-20] who delights in humility[^2-21] and the worship of angels,[^2-22] going on at length about what he has seen,[^2-23] puffed up without reason by his fleshly mind,[^2-24]

**19** and not holding fast to the head, from whom the whole body, supplied and held together by its joints and ligaments, grows with a growth that is from God.

**20** If with Christ you died to the elements of the world, why, as if you were still living in the world, do you submit to regulations,

**21** "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch"

**22** (things that all perish as they are used), according to human commands and teachings?[^2-25]

**23** These have indeed a reputation for wisdom, with their self-made religion and humility and severe treatment of the body, but they are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh.[^2-26]

[^2-1]: Literally "who have not seen my face in the flesh".
[^2-2]: Or "instructed". Greek *symbibazo*, "bring together, unite", also "teach"; the same verb is translated "held together" in verse 19.
[^2-3]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it. Many manuscripts read "the mystery of God and the Father and of Christ".
[^2-4]: Or "In it", that is, in the mystery.
[^2-5]: Literally "in the flesh".
[^2-6]: Or "as you received the Christ, Jesus the Lord".
[^2-7]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^2-8]: Many manuscripts read "overflowing in it with thanksgiving".
[^2-9]: Greek *stoicheia*, "elements": the basic components of the world, its elementary principles, or spiritual powers thought to be linked with them; also in verse 20.
[^2-10]: Greek *theotes*, "deity, divine nature".
[^2-11]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". "The body of the flesh" may mean the physical body (as in 1:22), or human nature in its weakness and opposition to God, "the flesh" in Paul's moral sense, as in verses 18 and 23. Many manuscripts read "the body of the sins of the flesh".
[^2-12]: That is, either the circumcision that Christ performs, or Christ's own death, pictured as a circumcision.
[^2-13]: Or "in whom", that is, in Christ.
[^2-14]: That is, God.
[^2-15]: Greek *cheirographon*, a handwritten document, especially a signed record of a debt.
[^2-16]: Or "He stripped". Greek *apekdyomai*, "take off, strip"; compare "putting off" in verse 11.
[^2-17]: Greek *thriambeuo*, used of a Roman general leading his defeated enemies in a triumphal procession.
[^2-18]: That is, in the cross (verse 14). Or "in him".
[^2-19]: Literally "but the body is of Christ".
[^2-20]: Greek *katabrabeuo*, "decide against, rob of a prize".
[^2-21]: Or "self-abasement"; also in verse 23. Greek *tapeinophrosyne*, translated "humility" in 3:12; here it may mean practices of self-denial such as fasting. *Who delights in*: literally "wishing in", which may also mean "insisting on".
[^2-22]: Or "the worship that angels offer".
[^2-23]: Greek *embateuo*, "enter, set foot on", also "investigate". Its sense here is uncertain. Many manuscripts read "what he has not seen".
[^2-24]: Literally "by the mind of his flesh".
[^2-25]: Compare Isaiah 29:13 in the Septuagint, which speaks of "the commands and teachings of people".
[^2-26]: The Greek of this clause is difficult. Literally "not in any honor toward the satisfying of the flesh". It may mean that these rules do nothing to restrain the flesh, or that they are of no value and serve only to satisfy the flesh.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.[^3-1]

**2** Set your minds on the things above, not on the things on earth,

**3** for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.

**4** When Christ, who is your life,[^3-2] is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory.

**5** So put to death what is earthly in you:[^3-3] sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry.

**6** Because of these things the wrath of God is coming on those who disobey.[^3-4]

**7** In these things[^3-5] you too once walked,[^3-6] when you were living in them.

**8** But now you too must put them all away: anger, rage, malice, slander, and obscene talk from your mouth.

**9** Do not lie to one another, since you have taken off the old self[^3-7] with its practices

**10** and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge according to the image of its Creator.[^3-8]

**11** Here there is no Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian,[^3-9] slave,[^3-10] free; but Christ is all and in all.

**12** So, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, put on a heart of compassion,[^3-11] kindness, humility, gentleness and patience,

**13** bearing with one another and forgiving each other, if anyone has a complaint against another. Just as the Lord[^3-12] has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.

**14** And over all these put on love, which is the bond of perfection.[^3-13]

**15** And let the peace of Christ[^3-14] rule[^3-15] in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.

**16** Let the word of Christ dwell in you[^3-16] richly, in all wisdom. Teach and admonish one another with psalms, hymns and spiritual songs, singing with gratitude[^3-17] in your hearts to God.[^3-18]

**17** And whatever you do, in word or in deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.

**18** Wives,[^3-19] submit[^3-20] to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord.

**19** Husbands, love your wives, and do not be harsh with them.

**20** Children, obey your parents in everything, for this pleases the Lord.

**21** Fathers,[^3-21] do not provoke your children, so that they do not lose heart.

**22** Slaves,[^3-22] obey your earthly masters[^3-23] in everything, not only when they are watching, to please people,[^3-24] but with sincerity of heart, fearing the Lord.[^3-25]

**23** Whatever you do, work at it wholeheartedly,[^3-26] as for the Lord and not for people,

**24** knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. Serve the Lord Christ.[^3-27]

**25** For the wrongdoer will be paid back for the wrong they have done, and there is no favoritism.

[^3-1]: Compare Psalm 110:1.
[^3-2]: Many manuscripts read "our life".
[^3-3]: Literally "the members that are on the earth".
[^3-4]: Literally "on the sons of disobedience". Some manuscripts leave out these words.
[^3-5]: Or "Among them".
[^3-6]: "Walked" is a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^3-7]: Literally "the old man"; also "the new" in verse 10.
[^3-8]: Literally "according to the image of the one who created him". Compare Genesis 1:26–27.
[^3-9]: *Barbarian*: Greek *barbaros*, someone who did not speak Greek. The Scythians, a people from north of the Black Sea, were thought of by Greeks as the least civilized of all.
[^3-10]: Greek *doulos*, "slave".
[^3-11]: Literally "the inward parts of compassion", the seat of the deepest feelings.
[^3-12]: Many manuscripts read "Christ".
[^3-13]: Or "the bond that makes perfect", "the bond of completeness".
[^3-14]: Many manuscripts read "the peace of God".
[^3-15]: Greek *brabeuo*, "act as umpire, decide".
[^3-16]: Or "among you".
[^3-17]: Or "with grace". Greek *charis*.
[^3-18]: Many manuscripts read "to the Lord".
[^3-19]: Verses 3:18–4:1 give instructions to the members of a household: wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters. Instructions of this kind are also found in Greek and Jewish writers of the time. Compare Ephesians 5:21–6:9.
[^3-20]: Greek *hypotasso*, "to place oneself under, to be subject to". Compare Ephesians 5:21–24.
[^3-21]: Greek *pateres*, "fathers", a word that can sometimes mean "parents".
[^3-22]: Greek *douloi*, "slaves". Slavery was part of the social order of the Roman world, and many members of the early churches were slaves or owned slaves. This passage addresses both (3:22–4:1).
[^3-23]: Literally "masters according to the flesh". Greek *kyrioi*, "lords, masters, owners", the same word as "Lord" later in this verse and in verses 23–24.
[^3-24]: Literally "not in eye-services, as people-pleasers".
[^3-25]: Many manuscripts read "fearing God".
[^3-26]: Literally "from the soul".
[^3-27]: Or "You are serving the Lord Christ". Many manuscripts read "for you serve the Lord Christ". *Serve*: Greek *douleuo*, "serve as a slave".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Masters,[^4-1] give your slaves what is right and fair,[^4-2] knowing that you also have a Master[^4-3] in heaven.

**2** Devote yourselves to prayer, staying alert in it with thanksgiving.

**3** At the same time pray for us too, that God may open a door for the word for us, to speak the mystery[^4-4] of Christ, for which I am in chains,

**4** so that I may make it clear, as I ought to speak.

**5** Live wisely toward outsiders,[^4-5] making the most of the time.[^4-6]

**6** Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.

**7** Tychicus, the beloved brother, faithful servant[^4-7] and fellow servant[^4-8] in the Lord, will tell you everything about my situation.

**8** I am sending[^4-9] him to you for this very purpose, so that you may know how we are[^4-10] and so that he may encourage your hearts,

**9** with Onesimus,[^4-11] the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will tell you everything that has happened here.

**10** Aristarchus my fellow prisoner greets you, as does Mark the cousin of Barnabas (about whom you have received instructions: if he comes to you, welcome him),

**11** and Jesus who is called Justus. These are the only ones of the circumcision[^4-12] among my fellow workers for the kingdom of God, and they have been a comfort to me.

**12** Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant[^4-13] of Christ,[^4-14] greets you. He is always struggling for you in his prayers, that you may stand mature and fully assured[^4-15] in all the will of God.

**13** For I testify about him that he has worked hard[^4-16] for you and for those in Laodicea and those in Hierapolis.

**14** Luke the beloved physician greets you, as does Demas.

**15** Greet the brothers and sisters[^4-17] in Laodicea, and Nympha and the church in her house.[^4-18]

**16** And when this letter has been read among you, have it read also in the church of the Laodiceans, and see that you also read the letter from Laodicea.

**17** And say to Archippus, "See that you complete the ministry[^4-19] you have received in the Lord."

**18** I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand.[^4-20] Remember my chains. Grace be with you.[^4-21]

[^4-1]: Greek *kyrioi*, "lords, masters, owners". This verse ends the instructions to households that begin in 3:18.
[^4-2]: Or "what is right and equal". Greek *isotes*, "equality, fairness".
[^4-3]: Or "Lord". Greek *kyrios*, the same word as "masters" earlier in the verse.
[^4-4]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it.
[^4-5]: Literally "Walk in wisdom toward those outside", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^4-6]: Literally "buying up the time".
[^4-7]: Greek *diakonos*, "servant", traditionally "minister".
[^4-8]: Greek *syndoulos*, "fellow slave".
[^4-9]: Literally "I sent", written from the point of view of the readers, who will receive the letter when Tychicus arrives.
[^4-10]: Many manuscripts read "so that he may know how you are".
[^4-11]: Compare Philemon 10–16, where Paul writes about a slave named Onesimus.
[^4-12]: That is, Jewish believers.
[^4-13]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^4-14]: The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "Christ Jesus".
[^4-15]: Many manuscripts read "complete".
[^4-16]: Literally "he has much toil". Many manuscripts read "he has great zeal".
[^4-17]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^4-18]: Many manuscripts read "Nymphas and the church in his house", with a man's name; some read "Nymphas and the church in their house".
[^4-19]: Greek *diakonia*, "service, ministry".
[^4-20]: Literally "The greeting with my own hand, of Paul". Paul usually dictated his letters to a scribe and wrote the last words himself (compare 2 Thessalonians 3:17).
[^4-21]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
