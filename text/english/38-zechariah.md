## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius,[^1-1] the word of the LORD[^1-2] came to Zechariah son of Berechiah, son of Iddo, the prophet:[^1-3]

**2** The LORD was very angry with your ancestors.[^1-4]

**3** So say to them: This is what the LORD of Armies[^1-5] says: Return to me, declares the LORD of Armies,[^1-6] and I will return to you, says the LORD of Armies.[^1-7]

**4** Do not be like your ancestors, to whom the earlier prophets[^1-8] proclaimed, "This is what the LORD of Armies says: Turn back from your evil ways and your evil deeds." But they did not listen or pay attention to me, declares the LORD.

**5** Your ancestors, where are they now? And the prophets, do they live forever?

**6** But my words and my decrees, which I commanded my servants the prophets, did they not overtake your ancestors? Then they repented and said, "The LORD of Armies has dealt with us as he planned to do, according to our ways and our deeds."

**7** On the twenty-fourth day of the eleventh month, the month of Shebat, in the second year of Darius,[^1-9] the word of the LORD came to Zechariah son of Berechiah, son of Iddo, the prophet.[^1-10]

**8** I saw in the night, and there was a man riding on a red horse! He was standing among the myrtle trees in the ravine,[^1-11] and behind him were red, sorrel[^1-12] and white horses.

**9** I asked, "What are these, my lord?"[^1-13] The angel who was speaking with me[^1-14] said to me, "I will show you what they are."

**10** Then the man who was standing among the myrtle trees answered, "These are the ones the LORD has sent to patrol the earth."[^1-15]

**11** They answered the angel of the LORD who was standing among the myrtle trees,[^1-16] "We have patrolled the earth, and the whole earth is still and at rest."[^1-17]

**12** Then the angel of the LORD said, "LORD of Armies, how long will you withhold your compassion from Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, with which you have been angry these seventy years?"[^1-18]

**13** The LORD answered the angel who was speaking with me with kind and comforting words.

**14** Then the angel who was speaking with me said to me, "Proclaim: 'This is what the LORD of Armies says: I am very jealous[^1-19] for Jerusalem and for Zion,

**15** and I am very angry with the nations that are at ease. For I was only a little angry, but they made the disaster worse.[^1-20]

**16** Therefore this is what the LORD says: I have returned to Jerusalem with compassion. My house will be rebuilt in it, declares the LORD of Armies, and a measuring line will be stretched out over Jerusalem.'[^1-21]

**17** Proclaim again: 'This is what the LORD of Armies says: My cities will again overflow with prosperity, and the LORD will again comfort Zion and again choose Jerusalem.'"[^1-22]

[^1-1]: October or November 520 BC, two months after Haggai's first message (Haggai 1:1). Darius I was king of Persia.
[^1-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-3]: *Zechariah* means "The LORD remembers". He prophesied together with Haggai (Ezra 5:1; 6:14), and was probably of the priestly family of Iddo, which returned from exile with Zerubbabel (Nehemiah 12:4, 16). Ezra calls him "son of Iddo", perhaps meaning "grandson".
[^1-4]: Literally "The LORD was angry with your ancestors with anger".
[^1-5]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. The title is used more than fifty times in Zechariah.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH tseva'ot*, "the declaration of the LORD of Armies", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^1-7]: Malachi 3:7 repeats the words "Return to me, and I will return to you".
[^1-8]: The prophets before the exile, such as Jeremiah (Jeremiah 25:4–5; 35:15).
[^1-9]: 15 February 519 BC. *Shebat* is the Babylonian name of the month, used after the exile.
[^1-10]: From here to 6:15 Zechariah tells of eight visions he saw in one night, each explained to him by an angel. Many of their details are uncertain.
[^1-11]: Hebrew *metsulah*, "deep place", perhaps a valley near Jerusalem, or the deep waters at the edge of the world.
[^1-12]: Hebrew *seruqqim*, a color of uncertain meaning, perhaps reddish brown or speckled.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *adoni*, a polite form of address, not the title of God.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *mal'akh*, "messenger, angel". This angel explains the visions throughout chapters 1–6.
[^1-15]: Literally "to walk about in the earth", like the patrols and messengers of the Persian king. Compare Job 1:7.
[^1-16]: The angel of the LORD is apparently the man of verses 8 and 10.
[^1-17]: The Persian empire was at peace after Darius had put down the revolts at the start of his reign, but Jerusalem still lay in ruins.
[^1-18]: Jeremiah had foretold seventy years of exile (Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10). The temple had been destroyed in 587 or 586 BC, nearly seventy years before.
[^1-19]: Literally "I am jealous with great jealousy", the passion of the LORD for his people. Compare 8:2.
[^1-20]: Literally "they helped for evil".
[^1-21]: A line for measuring the ground before building (Jeremiah 31:38–39).
[^1-22]: In most English Bibles chapter 1 has verses 18–21, which are 2:1–4 in the Hebrew.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Then I looked up and saw four horns.[^2-1]

**2** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "What are these?" He said to me, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, Israel and Jerusalem."

**3** Then the LORD[^2-2] showed me four craftsmen.[^2-3]

**4** I asked, "What are these coming to do?" He said, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, so that no one could lift up his head. But these craftsmen have come to terrify them, to throw down the horns of the nations that lifted up their horn against the land of Judah to scatter it."

**5** Then I looked up and saw a man with a measuring line in his hand.

**6** I asked, "Where are you going?" He said to me, "To measure Jerusalem, to see how wide it is and how long."

**7** Then the angel who was speaking with me came forward, and another angel came out to meet him.

**8** He said to him, "Run, tell that young man:[^2-4] 'Jerusalem will be inhabited like open villages without walls,[^2-5] because of the great number of people and animals in it.

**9** And I myself will be a wall of fire around it, declares the LORD,[^2-6] and I will be the glory within it.'"

**10** Come! Come![^2-7] Flee from the land of the north, declares the LORD, for I have scattered you like the four winds of the heavens,[^2-8] declares the LORD.

**11** Come, Zion! Escape, you who live with Daughter Babylon!

**12** For this is what the LORD of Armies[^2-9] says, he who sent me after glory[^2-10] to the nations that plundered you: Whoever touches you touches the pupil of his eye.[^2-11]

**13** For look, I am going to wave my hand against them,[^2-12] and they will become plunder for those who served them. Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me.

**14** Sing and rejoice, Daughter Zion,[^2-13] for look, I am coming, and I will live among you,[^2-14] declares the LORD.

**15** Many nations will join themselves to the LORD on that day and become my people. I will live among you, and you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you.

**16** The LORD will take possession of Judah as his portion in the holy land,[^2-15] and he will again choose Jerusalem.

**17** Be silent, all humankind,[^2-16] before the LORD, for he has roused himself from his holy dwelling.

[^2-1]: Verses 1–4 are 1:18–21 in most English Bibles, and verses 5–17 are 2:1–13. Horns are a picture of strength, here of the nations that conquered Israel and Judah.
[^2-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *charashim*, skilled workers in metal, wood or stone, who would cut the horns down.
[^2-4]: Probably the man with the measuring line (verse 5), or Zechariah himself.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *perazot*, "open country, unwalled villages". The city will be too large for walls.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 10 and 14.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *hoy*, usually "Woe to" (Amos 5:18). Here, and in verse 11, it is a call for attention. Verses 10–17 call the exiles home from Babylon, the "land of the north", from which invaders came to Israel.
[^2-8]: The Septuagint reads "I will gather you from the four winds of the heavens".
[^2-9]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 13 and 15.
[^2-10]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "after glory he sent me". Perhaps "for the sake of his glory", or "after the Glory sent me". The speaker may be the prophet or the angel.
[^2-11]: Literally "the gate of his eye", the pupil, which is guarded with great care (Deuteronomy 32:10). This is one of the *tiqqune sopherim*, the rabbinic "corrections of the scribes": the tradition says that the text once read "my eye" and was changed out of reverence for God.
[^2-12]: A gesture of threat (Isaiah 11:15; 19:16).
[^2-13]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. Compare Zephaniah 3:14.
[^2-14]: Hebrew *shakhan*, the verb used of the LORD's dwelling among his people in the tabernacle (Exodus 25:8). Also in verse 15.
[^2-15]: The only place in the Bible where the land is called "holy".
[^2-16]: Literally "all flesh". Compare Habakkuk 2:20; Zephaniah 1:7.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Then he showed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD,[^3-1] and the Accuser[^3-2] standing at his right hand to accuse him.

**2** The LORD said to the Accuser, "The LORD rebuke you, Accuser! The LORD, who has chosen Jerusalem, rebuke you![^3-3] Is not this man a burning stick snatched from the fire?"[^3-4]

**3** Now Joshua was dressed in filthy clothes[^3-5] as he stood before the angel.

**4** The angel said to those who were standing before him, "Take the filthy clothes off him." And to Joshua he said, "See, I have taken your guilt away from you, and I will clothe you in festal robes."

**5** Then I said, "Let them put a clean turban[^3-6] on his head." So they put a clean turban on his head and clothed him with garments, while the angel of the LORD stood by.

**6** Then the angel of the LORD solemnly charged Joshua:

**7** "This is what the LORD of Armies[^3-7] says: If you walk in my ways and keep my charge,[^3-8] then you will govern my house and have charge of my courts, and I will give you the right to come and go[^3-9] among these who are standing here.

**8** Listen, Joshua the high priest, you and your colleagues who sit before you, for they are men who are a sign:[^3-10] I am going to bring my servant the Branch.[^3-11]

**9** For look, the stone that I have set before Joshua: on one stone there are seven eyes.[^3-12] I am going to engrave an inscription on it, declares the LORD of Armies,[^3-13] and I will remove the guilt of this land in a single day.

**10** On that day, declares the LORD of Armies, each of you will invite his neighbor to sit under his vine and under his fig tree."[^3-14]

[^3-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-2]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan". *To accuse him* is *le-sitno*, from the same root. The accuser stood at the right hand of the accused in a court (Psalm 109:6). Also in verse 2.
[^3-3]: Jude 9 echoes these words: "The Lord rebuke you."
[^3-4]: Joshua and his people have been rescued from the fire of the exile (compare Amos 4:11).
[^3-5]: Hebrew *tso'im*, "soiled with excrement", the filthiest of words. Also in verse 4.
[^3-6]: Hebrew *tsanif*, a word related to *mitsnefet*, the turban of the high priest (Exodus 28:4, 36–39).
[^3-7]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^3-8]: Hebrew *mishmeret*, the duties of the priests in the sanctuary (Leviticus 8:35; Numbers 3:7).
[^3-9]: Literally "places to walk", perhaps access to the council of heaven (compare Jeremiah 23:18, 22).
[^3-10]: Hebrew *mofet*, "sign, portent", a sign of things to come (Isaiah 8:18).
[^3-11]: Hebrew *tsemach*, "sprout, growth", a title of a coming king of David's line (Jeremiah 23:5; 33:15; Zechariah 6:12). The Aramaic Targum reads "my servant the anointed one (Messiah)".
[^3-12]: The meaning of the stone is uncertain. It may be a stone for the temple, or a gem for the high priest's turban, which was engraved "Holy to the LORD" (Exodus 28:36–38). *Eyes* may mean "facets" or "springs". Compare 4:10.
[^3-13]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH tseva'ot*, "the declaration of the LORD of Armies", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verse 10.
[^3-14]: A picture of peace and safety (1 Kings 5:5; 4:25 in most English Bibles; Micah 4:4).

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Then the angel who was speaking with me came back and woke me, as someone is woken from sleep.

**2** He asked me, "What do you see?" I said,[^4-1] "I see a lampstand all of gold, with a bowl on top of it. It has seven lamps on it, with seven spouts for each of the lamps on top of it.[^4-2]

**3** There are two olive trees beside it, one on the right of the bowl and the other on its left."

**4** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "What are these, my lord?"

**5** The angel who was speaking with me answered, "Do you not know what these are?" I said, "No, my lord."

**6** Then he said to me, "This is the word of the LORD[^4-3] to Zerubbabel: Not by might and not by power, but by my Spirit,[^4-4] says the LORD of Armies.[^4-5]

**7** What are you, great mountain? Before Zerubbabel you will become a plain. He will bring out the top stone[^4-6] amid shouts of 'Grace, grace to it!'"[^4-7]

**8** Then the word of the LORD came to me:

**9** The hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house, and his hands will complete it. Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you.

**10** For who has despised the day of small things? They will rejoice when they see the plumb line[^4-8] in the hand of Zerubbabel. These seven are the eyes of the LORD, which range over the whole earth.[^4-9]

**11** Then I asked him, "What are these two olive trees on the right and on the left of the lampstand?"

**12** And a second time I asked him, "What are these two branches[^4-10] of the olive trees, beside the two gold pipes, which pour out the golden oil[^4-11] from themselves?"

**13** He said to me, "Do you not know what these are?" I said, "No, my lord."

**14** Then he said, "These are the two anointed ones[^4-12] who stand by the Lord of the whole earth."

[^4-1]: The Hebrew text is written "he said" but read "I said".
[^4-2]: Or "and seven spouts for the seven lamps". Literally "seven and seven spouts for the lamps". The lampstand recalls the seven-branched lampstand of the tabernacle (Exodus 25:31–40), but its form is different and uncertain.
[^4-3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath". *Might* is *chayil*, which can also mean "army".
[^4-5]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 9.
[^4-6]: Hebrew *ha-even ha-roshah*, "the head stone", perhaps the last stone set at the top of the building, or a stone from the first temple laid at the start of the work.
[^4-7]: Hebrew *chen chen lah*. Or "Beautiful, beautiful!"
[^4-8]: Literally "the stone of tin", probably the weight of a plumb line, used to see that a wall is straight. Or "the chosen stone".
[^4-9]: The seven eyes are the seven lamps of verse 2, and this answers Zechariah's question in verse 4. Verses 6–10 may join an oracle about Zerubbabel to the explanation of the vision. Compare 3:9; 2 Chronicles 16:9; Revelation 5:6.
[^4-10]: Literally "ears of grain", perhaps clusters of olives or branches heavy with fruit.
[^4-11]: Literally "the gold".
[^4-12]: Literally "the two sons of the fresh oil". The word is not *mashiach*, "anointed", but the picture is of anointing with oil. They are probably Zerubbabel, of the royal line, and Joshua the high priest. Revelation 11:4 speaks of two witnesses as "the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Again I looked up, and I saw a flying scroll.

**2** He asked me, "What do you see?" I said, "I see a flying scroll, twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide."[^5-1]

**3** He said to me, "This is the curse that is going out over the whole land. For according to what is written on one side, every thief will be swept away,[^5-2] and according to what is written on the other side, everyone who swears falsely[^5-3] will be swept away.

**4** I will send it out, declares the LORD of Armies,[^5-4] and it will enter the house of the thief and the house of the one who swears falsely by my name. It will stay inside his house and destroy it, both its timbers and its stones."[^5-5]

**5** Then the angel who was speaking with me came forward and said to me, "Look up and see what this is that is going out."

**6** I asked, "What is it?" He said, "This is the ephah basket[^5-6] that is going out." And he said, "This is their iniquity[^5-7] in all the land."

**7** Then a round cover of lead[^5-8] was lifted, and there was a woman sitting inside the basket.

**8** He said, "This is Wickedness." He thrust her back into the basket and pushed the lead weight down over its mouth.

**9** Then I looked up and saw two women coming out, with the wind[^5-9] in their wings. They had wings like the wings of a stork, and they lifted up the basket between earth and heaven.

**10** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "Where are they taking the basket?"

**11** He said to me, "To build a house[^5-10] for it in the land of Shinar.[^5-11] When the house is ready, the basket will be set down there on its stand."

[^5-1]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the scroll was about 30 feet (9 m) long and 15 feet (4.5 m) wide, the size of the porch of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:3).
[^5-2]: Or "has gone unpunished". The Hebrew verb *niqqah* can mean "be cleaned out" or "be held innocent". Also later in the verse.
[^5-3]: Literally "everyone who swears". *Falsely* is supplied from verse 4. Stealing and swearing falsely by the LORD's name break two of the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:7, 15).
[^5-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^5-5]: Compare the house struck by mold in Leviticus 14:45.
[^5-6]: Literally "the ephah", a dry measure of about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel), here a basket or barrel of that size. Also in verses 7–11.
[^5-7]: Hebrew *einam*, "their eye", perhaps "their appearance". The Septuagint and the Syriac read *avonam*, "their iniquity", which differs by one letter.
[^5-8]: Literally "a talent of lead", a round, flat piece of lead of a set weight.
[^5-9]: Hebrew *ruach*, "wind" or "spirit".
[^5-10]: That is, a temple, where the basket would be set on a stand, as an idol is.
[^5-11]: An old name for Babylonia (Genesis 11:2; Daniel 1:2), the place of exile and of the tower of Babel.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Again I looked up, and I saw four chariots coming out from between two mountains, and the mountains were mountains of bronze.[^6-1]

**2** The first chariot had red horses, the second chariot black horses,

**3** the third chariot white horses, and the fourth chariot dappled horses, all of them strong.[^6-2]

**4** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "What are these, my lord?"

**5** The angel answered me, "These are the four winds[^6-3] of heaven, going out after presenting themselves before the Lord of the whole earth.

**6** The chariot with the black horses is going out toward the land of the north, the white ones have gone out after them,[^6-4] and the dappled ones have gone out toward the land of the south."

**7** When the strong ones[^6-5] went out, they were eager to go and patrol the earth. He said, "Go, patrol the earth!" So they patrolled the earth.

**8** Then he called to me and said to me, "Look, those going out toward the land of the north have given my Spirit rest[^6-6] in the land of the north."

**9** The word of the LORD[^6-7] came to me:

**10** Take an offering from the exiles,[^6-8] from Heldai, Tobijah and Jedaiah, who have come from Babylon. Go that same day to the house of Josiah son of Zephaniah.

**11** Take silver and gold and make crowns,[^6-9] and set the crown on the head of Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest.

**12** Say to him: This is what the LORD of Armies[^6-10] says: Here is a man whose name is the Branch.[^6-11] He will branch out from his place, and he will build the temple of the LORD.

**13** It is he who will build the temple of the LORD. He will be clothed with majesty, and he will sit and rule on his throne. There will be a priest on his throne,[^6-12] and there will be a counsel of peace between the two of them.

**14** The crown will be a memorial in the temple of the LORD for Helem, Tobijah, Jedaiah and Hen son of Zephaniah.[^6-13]

**15** Those who are far away will come and help to build the temple of the LORD. Then you will know that the LORD of Armies has sent me to you. This will happen if you carefully obey the voice of the LORD your God.[^6-14]

[^6-1]: Perhaps the gateway of heaven, from which the chariots go out. In the ancient Near East the sun was pictured as rising between two mountains.
[^6-2]: Hebrew *amutsim*, "strong", perhaps a color: "piebald" or "gray". Compare the horses of 1:8.
[^6-3]: Or "spirits". Hebrew *ruchot* can mean either.
[^6-4]: Or "toward the west", with a small change to the Hebrew. The red horses are not mentioned.
[^6-5]: Probably the dappled horses, or all four teams. Some scholars read "the red ones", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^6-6]: That is, they have calmed my anger, or they have set my Spirit on the land of the north (compare Ezekiel 5:13). Hebrew *ruach* can mean spirit, wind or breath.
[^6-7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6-8]: Literally "Take from the exile". These men had brought gifts from the Jews in Babylon.
[^6-9]: Hebrew *atarot*, plural, perhaps one crown made of several circlets. Also in verse 14, where the verb is singular. Many scholars think the crown was meant for Zerubbabel, as the title "the Branch" suggests (3:8; Haggai 2:23), but no manuscript reads his name here.
[^6-10]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 15.
[^6-11]: Hebrew *tsemach*, "sprout, growth", a title of a coming king of David's line (3:8). *He will branch out* is *yitsmach*, from the same word. The Aramaic Targum reads "the anointed one (Messiah)".
[^6-12]: Or "He will be a priest on his throne". The Septuagint reads "and the priest will be at his right hand".
[^6-13]: *Helem* is probably Heldai (verse 10), and *Hen*, "favor", perhaps another name for Josiah (verse 10), or "for the kindness of the son of Zephaniah".
[^6-14]: The sentence is left unfinished in the Hebrew, as if to say, "This will happen, if …" Compare Deuteronomy 28:1.

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** In the fourth year of King Darius, the word of the LORD[^7-1] came to Zechariah on the fourth day of the ninth month, the month of Kislev.[^7-2]

**2** Now the people of Bethel had sent Sharezer and Regem-melech and their men[^7-3] to seek the favor of the LORD,[^7-4]

**3** and to ask the priests of the house of the LORD of Armies[^7-5] and the prophets, "Should I weep in the fifth month and abstain,[^7-6] as I have done for so many years?"

**4** Then the word of the LORD of Armies came to me:

**5** Say to all the people of the land and to the priests: When you fasted and mourned in the fifth month and in the seventh month[^7-7] for these seventy years, was it really for me that you fasted?

**6** And when you eat and when you drink, are you not eating and drinking for yourselves?

**7** Are not these the words that the LORD proclaimed through the earlier prophets,[^7-8] when Jerusalem was inhabited and at peace, with its towns around it, and when the Negev and the Shephelah[^7-9] were inhabited?

**8** The word of the LORD came to Zechariah:

**9** This is what the LORD of Armies says: Judge with true justice; show faithful love[^7-10] and compassion to one another.[^7-11]

**10** Do not oppress the widow or the fatherless, the foreigner or the poor, and do not plot evil against one another in your hearts.

**11** But they refused to pay attention. They turned a stubborn shoulder[^7-12] and stopped their ears so as not to hear.

**12** They made their hearts as hard as flint,[^7-13] so as not to hear the law[^7-14] and the words that the LORD of Armies had sent by his Spirit through the earlier prophets. So great anger came from the LORD of Armies.

**13** Just as I called[^7-15] and they would not listen, so they called and I would not listen, says the LORD of Armies.

**14** I scattered them with a storm among all the nations they had not known, and the land was left desolate behind them, with no one coming or going. So they made the pleasant land a desolation.

[^7-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7-2]: 7 December 518 BC. *Kislev* is the Babylonian name of the month.
[^7-3]: Literally "Bethel sent Sharezer and Regem-melech and his men". Or "Bethel-sharezer sent Regem-melech and his men". Bethel, about 11 miles (18 km) north of Jerusalem, was resettled after the exile (Ezra 2:28).
[^7-4]: Literally "to soften the face of the LORD", that is, to pray for his favor.
[^7-5]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.

**7** Awake, sword, against my shepherd, against the man who stands next to me,[^13-8] declares the LORD of Armies. Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered,[^13-9] and I will turn my hand against the little ones.

**8** In the whole land, declares the LORD, two thirds will be cut off and die, and a third will be left in it.

**9** I will bring this third into the fire. I will refine them as silver is refined and test them as gold is tested. They will call on my name, and I will answer them. I will say, "They are my people," and they will say, "The LORD is my God."[^13-10]

[^13-1]: Hebrew *niddah*, the impurity of menstruation, and the word used for the water that cleansed from impurity (Numbers 19:9).
[^13-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 7 and 8.
[^13-3]: The same verb, *daqar*, as *pierced* in 12:10. The law required a false prophet to be put to death, even by his own family (Deuteronomy 13:7–12; 13:6–11 in most English Bibles; 18:20).
[^13-4]: The garment worn by prophets such as Elijah (2 Kings 1:8; 2:13).
[^13-5]: The Hebrew is pointed *adam hiqnani*, "a man sold me" or "a man made me his servant". The same letters, divided into words differently, can be read *adamah qinyani*, "the land has been my livelihood". Compare Amos 7:14.
[^13-6]: Literally "between your hands", that is, on the chest or the back. Prophets of other gods sometimes cut themselves (1 Kings 18:28).
[^13-7]: Or "of those who love me".
[^13-8]: Literally "against the man who is my companion".
[^13-9]: Jesus quotes these words in Mark 14:27 and Matthew 26:31, of himself and his disciples, in the form "I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered."
[^13-10]: Compare Hosea 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles).

## Chapter 14

*Status: draft*

**1** Look, a day is coming for the LORD,[^14-1] when the plunder taken from you[^14-2] will be divided in your midst.

**2** For I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem for battle. The city will be captured, the houses plundered and the women raped.[^14-3] Half of the city will go into exile, but the rest of the people will not be cut off from the city.

**3** Then the LORD will go out and fight against those nations, as he fights on a day of battle.

**4** On that day his feet will stand on the Mount of Olives,[^14-4] which lies east of Jerusalem, and the Mount of Olives will be split in two from east to west by a very wide valley. Half of the mountain will move north, and half of it south.

**5** You will flee by the valley of my mountains,[^14-5] for the valley of the mountains will reach as far as Azel.[^14-6] You will flee as you fled from the earthquake in the days of Uzziah king of Judah.[^14-7] Then the LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with you.[^14-8]

**6** On that day there will be no light, neither brightness nor frost.[^14-9]

**7** It will be one unbroken day,[^14-10] known to the LORD, neither day nor night, and at evening time there will be light.

**8** On that day living water will flow out from Jerusalem, half of it to the eastern sea and half of it to the western sea.[^14-11] It will flow in summer and in winter.

**9** The LORD will be king over all the earth. On that day the LORD will be one, and his name one.[^14-12]

**10** The whole land will be changed into a plain like the Arabah,[^14-13] from Geba to Rimmon, south of Jerusalem.[^14-14] But Jerusalem will be raised up and will remain in its place, from the Benjamin Gate to the place of the First Gate and to the Corner Gate, and from the Tower of Hananel to the king's winepresses.[^14-15]

**11** People will live in it, and there will never again be a curse of destruction.[^14-16] Jerusalem will live in security.

**12** This will be the plague with which the LORD will strike all the peoples who have made war on Jerusalem: their flesh will rot while they are still standing on their feet, their eyes will rot in their sockets, and their tongues will rot in their mouths.

**13** On that day a great panic from the LORD will fall on them. Each will seize the hand of his neighbor, and they will raise their hands against each other.

**14** Judah too will fight at Jerusalem.[^14-17] The wealth of all the surrounding nations will be gathered: gold, silver and clothing in great abundance.

**15** A plague like this will also strike the horses, the mules, the camels, the donkeys and all the animals in those camps.

**16** Then all who are left of all the nations that came against Jerusalem will go up year after year to worship the King, the LORD of Armies,[^14-18] and to celebrate the Festival of Shelters.[^14-19]

**17** If any of the clans of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the LORD of Armies, there will be no rain on them.

**18** If the clan of Egypt does not go up and come in, they will have no rain.[^14-20] There will be the plague with which the LORD strikes the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Shelters.

**19** This will be the punishment[^14-21] of Egypt and the punishment of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Shelters.

**20** On that day the bells of the horses will bear the words "Holy to the LORD",[^14-22] and the cooking pots in the house of the LORD will be like the sprinkling bowls in front of the altar.

**21** Every cooking pot in Jerusalem and in Judah will be holy to the LORD of Armies, and all who offer sacrifices will come and take some of them and cook in them. And on that day there will no longer be a trader[^14-23] in the house of the LORD of Armies.

[^14-4]: The ridge east of Jerusalem, across the Kidron Valley. Only here in the Old Testament is it called by this name (compare 2 Samuel 15:30; Acts 1:11–12).
[^14-5]: The same consonants can be read "The valley of my mountains will be blocked", as the Septuagint reads. Also later in the verse.
[^14-6]: The place is unknown.
[^14-7]: The earthquake in the eighth century BC, which Amos 1:1 also mentions.
[^14-8]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "with him". *You* may be the LORD, addressed suddenly, or Jerusalem. The holy ones are the angels of the LORD's heavenly army (Deuteronomy 33:2–3; Psalm 89:6; 89:5 in most English Bibles). Compare 1 Thessalonians 3:13.
[^14-9]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "there will be no light, precious things and congealing". The Hebrew text is written "the precious things will congeal". The Septuagint reads "there will be no light, and cold and frost".
[^14-10]: Literally "one day", perhaps a day like no other (compare Genesis 1:5).
[^14-11]: The Dead Sea and the Mediterranean. Compare Ezekiel 47:1–12; Joel 4:18 (3:18 in most English Bibles).
[^14-12]: Compare Deuteronomy 6:4. This verse ends the *Aleinu* prayer of Jewish daily worship.
[^14-13]: The deep valley of the Jordan and the Dead Sea.
[^14-14]: Geba was at the northern border of Judah (2 Kings 23:8), and Rimmon in the south (Joshua 15:32).
[^14-15]: The gates and the tower were landmarks at the edges of the city (Jeremiah 31:38; 37:13; Nehemiah 3:1).
[^14-16]: Hebrew *cherem*, the ban by which a city was devoted to destruction (Joshua 6:17). Compare Revelation 22:3.
[^14-17]: Or "against Jerusalem".
[^14-18]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 17 and 21.
[^14-19]: The autumn festival at the end of the harvest, when the people lived in booths (Leviticus 23:33–43). It came just before the rainy season.
[^14-20]: Literally "and not on them". *Rain* is supplied from verse 17. Egypt depends on the Nile rather than on rain. The Septuagint and the Syriac do not have "not": "on them will come the plague".
[^14-21]: Hebrew *chatta't*, "sin", here the punishment for sin.
[^14-22]: The words engraved on the gold plate on the high priest's turban (Exodus 28:36).
[^14-23]: Literally "a Canaanite", a word also used for a merchant (Zephaniah 1:11; Proverbs 31:24). Compare John 2:16.
