## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan,[^1-1] in the wilderness, in the Arabah[^1-2] opposite Suph,[^1-3] between Paran and Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth and Di-zahab.

**2** It is eleven days' journey from Horeb[^1-4] to Kadesh-barnea by the Mount Seir road.

**3** In the fortieth year, on the first day of the eleventh month, Moses spoke to the Israelites, telling them everything the LORD[^1-5] had commanded him to tell them.

**4** This was after he had defeated Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon, and Og king of Bashan, who lived in Ashtaroth, at Edrei.[^1-6]

**5** East of the Jordan, in the land of Moab, Moses began to explain this law.[^1-7] He said:[^1-8]

**6** "The LORD our God spoke to us at Horeb. He said, 'You have stayed at this mountain long enough.

**7** Turn and set out. Go to the hill country of the Amorites and to all their neighbors in the Arabah, in the hill country, in the Shephelah,[^1-9] in the Negev[^1-10] and along the seacoast: the land of the Canaanites, and Lebanon, as far as the great river, the river Euphrates.

**8** See, I have set the land before you. Go in and take possession of the land that the LORD swore to give to your ancestors,[^1-11] to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, and to their descendants after them.'

**9** At that time I said to you, 'I cannot carry you by myself.

**10** The LORD your God has multiplied you, and here you are today, as many as the stars of the sky.

**11** May the LORD, the God of your ancestors, make you a thousand times as many as you are, and bless you as he has promised you.

**12** How can I bear by myself the weight of you, the burden of you and your quarrels?

**13** Choose[^1-12] wise, discerning and respected[^1-13] men from each of your tribes, and I will make them your leaders.'

**14** You answered me, 'What you have told us to do is good.'

**15** So I took the leaders of your tribes, wise and respected men, and made them leaders over you: commanders of thousands, commanders of hundreds, commanders of fifties and commanders of tens, and officials[^1-14] for your tribes.

**16** At that time I gave your judges this command: 'Hear the disputes between your fellow Israelites,[^1-15] and judge fairly between one person and another, whether a fellow Israelite or a foreigner who lives among you.[^1-16]

**17** Do not show partiality in judging.[^1-17] Hear the small and the great alike. Do not be afraid of anyone, for judgment belongs to God. Bring to me any case that is too hard for you, and I will hear it.'

**18** At that time I commanded you all the things you were to do.

**19** Then we set out from Horeb and went through all that great and terrible wilderness that you saw, on the way to the hill country of the Amorites, as the LORD our God had commanded us. And we came to Kadesh-barnea.

**20** I said to you, 'You have come to the hill country of the Amorites, which the LORD our God is giving us.

**21** See, the LORD your God has set the land before you.[^1-18] Go up and take possession of it, as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has told you. Do not be afraid or discouraged.'

**22** Then all of you came to me and said, 'Let us send men ahead of us to explore the land for us. Let them bring us back a report about the route we should take up and the cities we will come to.'

**23** The idea seemed good to me, so I took twelve men from among you, one man from each tribe.

**24** They turned and went up into the hill country and came to the Valley of Eshcol,[^1-19] and they spied out the land.

**25** They took some of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought it down to us. They brought us back a report and said, 'The land that the LORD our God is giving us is good.'

**26** But you were not willing to go up. You rebelled against the command[^1-20] of the LORD your God.

**27** You grumbled in your tents and said, 'The LORD hates us. That is why he brought us out of the land of Egypt, to hand us over to the Amorites to destroy us.

**28** Where can we go up to? Our brothers have made our hearts melt.[^1-21] They say, "The people are bigger and taller than we are. The cities are large, with walls up to the sky.[^1-22] And we even saw the Anakim[^1-23] there."'

**29** Then I said to you, 'Do not be terrified or afraid of them.

**30** The LORD your God, who is going ahead of you, will himself fight for you, just as he did for you in Egypt before your eyes,

**31** and in the wilderness. There you saw how the LORD your God carried you, as a man carries his son, all the way you went until you came to this place.'

**32** Yet in spite of this,[^1-24] you did not trust the LORD your God,

**33** who went ahead of you on the way to look for a place for you to camp, in fire by night to show you the way you should go, and in the cloud by day.

**34** When the LORD heard what you said,[^1-25] he was angry, and he swore,

**35** 'Not one of these men, this evil generation, will see the good land that I swore to give to your ancestors,

**36** except Caleb son of Jephunneh. He will see it, and I will give him and his children the land he has walked on, because he followed the LORD completely.'[^1-26]

**37** Because of you, the LORD was angry with me too. He said, 'You will not enter it either.

**38** Joshua son of Nun, who serves you,[^1-27] will enter it. Encourage him, for he will lead Israel to take possession of it.[^1-28]

**39** And your little ones, who you said would be taken as plunder, and your children, who today do not yet know good from bad, they will enter it. I will give it to them, and they will take possession of it.

**40** But as for you, turn around and set out for the wilderness, by the road to the Red Sea.'[^1-29]

**41** Then you answered me, 'We have sinned against the LORD. We will go up and fight, just as the LORD our God commanded us.' Each of you put on his weapons, and you thought it easy[^1-30] to go up into the hill country.

**42** But the LORD said to me, 'Tell them, "Do not go up and do not fight, for I am not with you.[^1-31] You will be defeated by your enemies."'

**43** So I told you, but you would not listen. You rebelled against the LORD's command, and in your arrogance you went up into the hill country.

**44** The Amorites who lived in that hill country came out against you and chased you like a swarm of bees. They struck you down in Seir as far as Hormah.

**45** You came back and wept before the LORD, but the LORD did not listen to your voice or pay any attention to you.

**46** So you stayed in Kadesh for a long time, all the time that you stayed there.[^1-32]

[^1-1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", seen from the land west of the river. Also in verse 5.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *aravah*: the deep rift valley of the Jordan and the Dead Sea, and its continuation south to the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^1-3]: *Suph* is otherwise unknown. It may be short for *Yam Suph*, the Sea of Reeds (verse 40).
[^1-4]: Deuteronomy calls the mountain of God Horeb, the name used for Sinai in Exodus 3:1 and 17:6.
[^1-5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-6]: Edrei is where Og was defeated (3:1). Compare Joshua 12:4, "who lived in Ashtaroth and in Edrei".
[^1-7]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, law". The verb *be'er*, "explain", means to make something plain (compare 27:8).
[^1-8]: Moses' first speech runs from verse 6 to 4:40.
[^1-9]: The low hills between the coastal plain and the hill country of Judah.
[^1-10]: The dry country in the south of the land.
[^1-11]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 11, 21 and 35.
[^1-12]: Literally "Give".
[^1-13]: Literally "known"; also in verse 15.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions.
[^1-15]: Literally "your brothers".
[^1-16]: Literally "between a man and his brother and his foreigner". Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^1-17]: Literally "Do not recognize faces in judgment".
[^1-18]: Here the Hebrew changes from "you" plural to "you" singular, addressing Israel as one person. Deuteronomy often moves between the two.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. *Eshcol* means "cluster" of grapes (Numbers 13:23–24).
[^1-20]: Literally "mouth"; also in verse 43.
[^1-21]: That is, they have made us lose courage.
[^1-22]: Literally "fortified up to the heavens".
[^1-23]: Literally "the sons of the Anakim", a people known for their great height (Numbers 13:28, 33).
[^1-24]: Or "in this matter". Literally "And in this word".
[^1-25]: Literally "the sound of your words".
[^1-26]: Literally "he has filled after the LORD".
[^1-27]: Literally "who stands before you".
[^1-28]: Or "he will give it to Israel as their inheritance". Literally "he will cause Israel to inherit it".
[^1-29]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it is probably the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^1-30]: The Hebrew verb occurs only here. It may mean "you thought it easy" or "you were eager".
[^1-31]: Literally "for I am not in your midst".
[^1-32]: Literally "according to the days that you stayed". The phrase probably means a long stay; it may also be read "as long as you did".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** "Then we turned and set out for the wilderness by the road to the Red Sea,[^2-1] as the LORD[^2-2] had told me. For a long time we went around the hill country of Seir.

**2** Then the LORD said to me,

**3** 'You have been going around this hill country long enough. Turn north.

**4** Give the people this command: "You are about to pass through the territory of your relatives,[^2-3] the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you, but be very careful.

**5** Do not start a fight with them, for I will not give you any of their land, not even enough to put your foot on.[^2-4] I have given the hill country of Seir to Esau as his possession.

**6** You are to buy food from them with money, so that you may eat, and you are also to buy water from them with money, so that you may drink.

**7** For the LORD your God has blessed you in all the work of your hands. He has watched over your journey[^2-5] through this great wilderness. For these forty years the LORD your God has been with you, and you have not lacked anything."'

**8** So we went on past our relatives, the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir, away from the Arabah road, away from Elath and Ezion-geber. Then we turned and went along the road to the wilderness of Moab.

**9** The LORD said to me, 'Do not harass Moab or start a war with them, for I will not give you any of their land as a possession. I have given Ar[^2-6] to the descendants of Lot[^2-7] as their possession.'

**10** (The Emim used to live there, a people great and numerous, and as tall as the Anakim.

**11** Like the Anakim, they too were counted as Rephaim,[^2-8] but the Moabites call them Emim.

**12** The Horites also used to live in Seir, but the descendants of Esau drove them out. They destroyed them and settled in their place, just as Israel did in the land that the LORD gave them as their possession.)

**13** 'Now get up and cross the Zered Valley.'[^2-9] So we crossed the Zered Valley.

**14** From the time we left Kadesh-barnea until we crossed the Zered Valley was thirty-eight years, until the whole generation of fighting men had died out from the camp, as the LORD had sworn to them.

**15** Indeed, the hand of the LORD was against them, to wipe them out from the camp until they were all gone.

**16** When the last of the fighting men among the people had died,

**17** the LORD said to me,

**18** 'Today you are to cross the border of Moab at Ar.

**19** When you come near the Ammonites,[^2-10] do not harass them or start a fight with them, for I will not give you any of the land of the Ammonites as a possession. I have given it to the descendants of Lot as their possession.'

**20** (That too was counted as a land of the Rephaim. Rephaim used to live there, but the Ammonites call them Zamzummim,

**21** a people great and numerous, and as tall as the Anakim. But the LORD destroyed them before the Ammonites,[^2-11] who drove them out and settled in their place.

**22** He had done the same for the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir, when he destroyed the Horites before them. They drove them out and have lived in their place to this day.

**23** And as for the Avvim, who lived in villages as far as Gaza, the Caphtorim, who came from Caphtor,[^2-12] destroyed them and settled in their place.)

**24** 'Get up, set out and cross the Arnon Valley. See, I have handed over to you Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land. Begin to take possession of it. Go to war against him.

**25** This very day I will begin to put the dread and fear of you on the peoples everywhere under heaven. When they hear the report of you, they will tremble and be in anguish because of you.'

**26** So I sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon king of Heshbon with an offer of peace.[^2-13] I said,

**27** 'Let me pass through your land. I will keep strictly to the road.[^2-14] I will not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**28** Sell me food for money, so that I may eat, and give me water for money, so that I may drink. Only let me pass through on foot,

**29** as the descendants of Esau who live in Seir and the Moabites who live in Ar did for me, until I cross the Jordan into the land that the LORD our God is giving us.'

**30** But Sihon king of Heshbon was not willing to let us pass through his land,[^2-15] for the LORD your God had made his spirit stubborn and his heart hard, to hand him over to you, as he has now done.[^2-16]

**31** The LORD said to me, 'See, I have begun to hand Sihon and his land over to you. Begin to take possession, so that you may possess his land.'

**32** Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Jahaz.

**33** The LORD our God handed him over to us,[^2-17] and we struck him down, together with his sons[^2-18] and all his people.

**34** At that time we captured all his towns and devoted every town to destruction,[^2-19] men, women and children. We left no survivors.

**35** We kept only the livestock as plunder for ourselves, along with the spoil of the towns we had captured.

**36** From Aroer, on the edge of the Arnon Valley, and from the town in the valley, as far as Gilead, there was not one town too strong for us.[^2-20] The LORD our God handed them all over to us.

**37** But you did not go near the land of the Ammonites: the whole bank of the Jabbok River, the towns of the hill country, or any place that the LORD our God had forbidden.[^2-21]

[^2-1]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it is probably the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^2-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Literally "your brothers"; also in verse 8. Esau, the ancestor of the Edomites, was Jacob's brother (Genesis 36:1, 8).
[^2-4]: Literally "not even as much as the treading of the sole of a foot".
[^2-5]: Literally "He has known your walking".
[^2-6]: A city or district of Moab, near the Arnon (Numbers 21:15, 28).
[^2-7]: Lot, Abraham's nephew, was the ancestor of Moab and Ammon (Genesis 19:36–38).
[^2-8]: An ancient people remembered for their great height (3:11).
[^2-9]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. Also in verses 14, 24, 36 and 37.
[^2-10]: Literally "the sons of Ammon"; also in verses 20, 21 and 37.
[^2-11]: Literally "before them".
[^2-12]: Caphtor is probably Crete. The Philistines came from there (Amos 9:7).
[^2-13]: Literally "words of peace".
[^2-14]: Literally "In the road, in the road I will go".
[^2-15]: Literally "to let us pass through him".
[^2-16]: Literally "as this day".
[^2-17]: Literally "gave him before us".
[^2-18]: The Hebrew text is written "his son" but read "his sons".
[^2-19]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^2-20]: Literally "too high for us".
[^2-21]: Literally "and all that the LORD our God had commanded".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** "Then we turned and went up the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Edrei.

**2** The LORD[^3-1] said to me, 'Do not be afraid of him, for I have handed him over to you, with all his people and his land. Do to him what you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon.'

**3** So the LORD our God also handed over to us Og king of Bashan and all his people. We struck him down until no survivor was left to him.

**4** At that time we captured all his towns. There was not a town that we did not take from them: sixty towns, the whole region[^3-2] of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

**5** All these were towns fortified with high walls, gates and bars, not counting a great many unwalled villages.[^3-3]

**6** We devoted them to destruction,[^3-4] as we had done to Sihon king of Heshbon, devoting every town to destruction, men, women and children.

**7** But all the livestock and the spoil of the towns we took as plunder for ourselves.

**8** So at that time we took the land from the two kings of the Amorites east of the Jordan,[^3-5] from the Arnon Valley to Mount Hermon.

**9** (The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir.)

**10** We took all the towns of the plateau,[^3-6] all Gilead and all Bashan as far as Salecah and Edrei, towns of Og's kingdom in Bashan.

**11** (Only Og king of Bashan was left of the remnant of the Rephaim. His bed[^3-7] was made of iron. Isn't it still in Rabbah of the Ammonites? It is nine cubits long and four cubits wide, by the standard cubit.[^3-8])

**12** So we took possession of this land at that time. I gave the Reubenites and the Gadites the land from Aroer, by the Arnon Valley, and half the hill country of Gilead with its towns.

**13** The rest of Gilead, and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og, I gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh. (The whole region of Argob, all that part of Bashan, is called the land of the Rephaim.

**14** Jair, a descendant of Manasseh,[^3-9] took the whole region of Argob as far as the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites. He named Bashan after himself, Havvoth-jair,[^3-10] as it is called to this day.)

**15** To Machir I gave Gilead.

**16** To the Reubenites and the Gadites I gave the territory from Gilead as far as the Arnon Valley, with the middle of the valley as the border, and as far as the Jabbok River,[^3-11] the border of the Ammonites.

**17** Their territory also included the Arabah, with the Jordan as its border, from Kinnereth[^3-12] to the Sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea,[^3-13] below the slopes of Pisgah on the east.

**18** At that time I commanded you, 'The LORD your God has given you this land to possess. All your fighting men[^3-14] are to cross over armed ahead of your fellow Israelites.[^3-15]

**19** Only your wives, your little ones and your livestock (I know you have a great deal of livestock) may stay in the towns I have given you,

**20** until the LORD gives your relatives rest as he has given you, and they too take possession of the land that the LORD your God is giving them on the other side of the Jordan.[^3-16] Then each of you may return to the possession I have given you.'

**21** At that time I commanded Joshua, 'Your own eyes have seen everything the LORD your God has done to these two kings. The LORD will do the same to all the kingdoms you are crossing over into.

**22** Do not be afraid of them, for the LORD your God is the one who fights for you.'

**23** At that time I pleaded with the LORD:

**24** 'Lord GOD,[^3-17] you have begun to show your servant your greatness and your mighty hand. What god is there in heaven or on earth who can do deeds and mighty acts like yours?

**25** Please let me cross over and see the good land on the other side of the Jordan, that good hill country, and Lebanon.'

**26** But the LORD was angry with me because of you, and he would not listen to me. The LORD said to me, 'That is enough![^3-18] Do not speak to me about this matter again.

**27** Go up to the top of Pisgah and look west, north, south and east.[^3-19] See it with your own eyes, for you will not cross this Jordan.

**28** Give Joshua his orders. Encourage him and strengthen him, for he will cross over at the head of this people, and he will lead them to take possession of the land that you will see.'

**29** So we stayed in the valley opposite Beth-peor.

[^3-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-2]: Hebrew *chevel*, literally "measuring line", here a measured-out region. Also in verses 13 and 14.
[^3-3]: Literally "towns of the open country".
[^3-4]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^3-5]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", seen from the land west of the river.
[^3-6]: Hebrew *mishor*, "level land": the high tableland north of the Arnon.
[^3-7]: Or "his coffin". Hebrew *eres*, "bed, couch". Some take it to mean a coffin made of a dark, hard stone like iron.
[^3-8]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the bed was about 13 feet long and 6 feet wide (4 by 1.8 meters). Literally "by the cubit of a man", that is, the ordinary cubit.
[^3-9]: Literally "son of Manasseh". In Hebrew "son" can mean any descendant.
[^3-10]: *Havvoth-jair* means "the tent villages of Jair".
[^3-11]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. Also earlier in the verse.
[^3-12]: The Sea of Galilee, which the Hebrew elsewhere calls the Sea of Kinnereth.
[^3-13]: The Dead Sea.
[^3-14]: Literally "all the sons of strength".
[^3-15]: Literally "your brothers, the sons of Israel". In verse 20 "your relatives" is literally "your brothers".
[^3-16]: Literally "beyond the Jordan". Here it means west of the river, seen from where Moses stands. Also in verse 25.
[^3-17]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^3-18]: Literally "Much for you", the words translated "long enough" in 1:6 and 2:3.
[^3-19]: Literally "lift up your eyes toward the sea and north and south and east". West is "toward the sea", the Mediterranean.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** "Now, Israel, listen to the decrees and the rulings[^4-1] that I am teaching you to follow, so that you may live, and go in and take possession of the land that the LORD,[^4-2] the God of your ancestors,[^4-3] is giving you.

**2** Do not add anything to what I command you, and do not take anything away from it. Keep the commands of the LORD your God that I am giving you.

**3** You saw with your own eyes what the LORD did at Baal-peor.[^4-4] The LORD your God destroyed from among you everyone who followed the Baal of Peor.

**4** But all of you who held fast to the LORD your God are alive today.

**5** See, I have taught you decrees and rulings, as the LORD my God commanded me, so that you may follow them in the land you are entering to take possession of.

**6** Keep them and follow them, for this will show your wisdom and understanding to the peoples.[^4-5] When they hear about all these decrees, they will say, 'Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.'

**7** For what great nation has a god[^4-6] so near to it as the LORD our God is to us whenever we call on him?

**8** And what great nation has decrees and rulings as righteous as this whole law that I am setting before you today?

**9** Only be careful, and watch yourselves closely,[^4-7] so that you do not forget the things your eyes have seen, and so that they do not slip from your heart[^4-8] as long as you live. Make them known to your children and your grandchildren:

**10** the day you stood before the LORD your God at Horeb, when the LORD said to me, 'Assemble the people for me, and I will let them hear my words, so that they may learn to fear me as long as they live on the earth, and may teach their children.'

**11** You came near and stood at the foot of the mountain, while the mountain blazed with fire up to the heart of the sky,[^4-9] with darkness, cloud and thick gloom.

**12** Then the LORD spoke to you out of the fire. You heard the sound of words, but you saw no form. There was only a voice.

**13** He declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to keep:[^4-10] the Ten Commandments.[^4-11] And he wrote them on two stone tablets.

**14** At that time the LORD commanded me to teach you decrees and rulings, for you to follow in the land you are crossing over into to take possession of.

**15** So watch yourselves very carefully.[^4-12] Since you saw no form of any kind on the day the LORD spoke to you at Horeb out of the fire,

**16** do not act corruptly by making for yourselves an idol[^4-13] in the form of any figure: the likeness of a male or a female,

**17** the likeness of any animal on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the sky,

**18** the likeness of anything that creeps on the ground, or the likeness of any fish in the waters below the earth.

**19** And when you look up to the sky and see the sun, the moon and the stars, the whole array of heaven,[^4-14] do not be drawn away to bow down to them and serve them. The LORD your God has allotted them to all the peoples everywhere under heaven.

**20** But you are the ones the LORD took and brought out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be the people of his inheritance,[^4-15] as you are today.

**21** The LORD was angry with me because of you,[^4-16] and he swore that I would not cross the Jordan or enter the good land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**22** For I am going to die in this land. I will not cross the Jordan. But you are going to cross over and take possession of this good land.

**23** Be careful not to forget the covenant that the LORD your God made with you. Do not make for yourselves an idol in the form of anything that the LORD your God has forbidden you.[^4-17]

**24** For the LORD your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God.[^4-18]

**25** When you have had children and grandchildren and have been in the land a long time, if you act corruptly by making an idol in the form of anything, and do what is evil in the eyes of the LORD your God, provoking him to anger,

**26** I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you today that you will soon perish completely from the land you are crossing the Jordan to take possession of. You will not live long on it, but will be utterly destroyed.

**27** The LORD will scatter you among the peoples, and only a few of you[^4-19] will be left among the nations where the LORD will drive you.

**28** There you will serve gods made by human hands, of wood and stone, which cannot see or hear or eat or smell.

**29** But from there you will seek the LORD your God, and you will find him, if you search for him with all your heart and with all your soul.[^4-20]

**30** When you are in distress and all these things have happened to you,[^4-21] in days to come,[^4-22] you will return to the LORD your God and listen to his voice.

**31** For the LORD your God is a compassionate God. He will not abandon you or destroy you, and he will not forget the covenant with your ancestors that he swore to them.

**32** Ask now about the former days, the days before you, from the day God created human beings[^4-23] on the earth. Ask from one end of heaven to the other: has anything as great as this ever happened, or has anything like it ever been heard of?

**33** Has any people ever heard the voice of God[^4-24] speaking out of fire, as you have heard it, and lived?

**1** When you go out to war against your enemies and see horses and chariots and an army larger than yours,[^20-1] do not be afraid of them, for the LORD[^20-2] your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt, is with you.

**2** When you are about to go into battle, the priest is to come forward and speak to the army.[^20-3]

**3** He is to say to them, "Hear, Israel. Today you are going into battle against your enemies. Do not lose heart.[^20-4] Do not be afraid, or alarmed, or terrified of them.

**4** For the LORD your God is the one who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies and to give you victory."[^20-5]

**5** Then the officials[^20-6] are to say to the army, "Has anyone built a new house and not yet dedicated it? Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else dedicate it.

**6** Has anyone planted a vineyard and not yet begun to enjoy its fruit?[^20-7] Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else enjoy it.

**7** Has anyone become engaged to a woman and not yet married her? Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else marry her."

**8** Then the officials are to speak further to the army and say, "Is anyone afraid or fainthearted? Let him go back to his house, so that he does not make his brothers lose heart as he has."[^20-8]

**9** When the officials have finished speaking to the army, commanders are to be appointed at the head of the troops.[^20-9]

**10** When you approach a town to attack it, first offer it terms of peace.

**11** If it accepts your terms[^20-10] and opens its gates to you, all the people found in it are to become forced laborers for you and serve you.

**12** But if it does not make peace with you and goes to war with you, then lay siege to it.

**13** When the LORD your God hands it over to you, put all its males to the sword.[^20-11]

**14** But the women, the children, the livestock and everything else in the town, all its plunder, you may take as spoil for yourselves. And you may enjoy[^20-12] the spoil of your enemies that the LORD your God has given you.

**15** This is what you are to do to all the towns that are very far away from you, which are not among the towns of these nations.

**16** But in the towns of these peoples that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, you must not leave alive anything that breathes.

**17** You must devote them completely to destruction:[^20-13] the Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, as the LORD your God has commanded you,

**18** so that they will not teach you to do all the detestable things they do for their gods, and so cause you to sin against the LORD your God.

**19** When you besiege a town for a long time, fighting against it to capture it, do not destroy its trees by swinging an axe against them. You may eat from them, but you must not cut them down. Are the trees of the field human beings, that you should besiege them?[^20-14]

**20** Only the trees that you know are not fruit trees may you destroy and cut down, to build siege works against the town that is at war with you, until it falls.

## Chapter 21

**1** If someone is found killed, lying in the open country in the land that the LORD[^21-1] your God is giving you to possess, and it is not known who killed him,

**2** your elders and your judges are to go out and measure the distance to the towns around the body.

**3** The elders of the town nearest to the body are to take a heifer that has never been worked and has never pulled in a yoke.

**4** The elders of that town are to bring the heifer down to a valley with a stream that never runs dry,[^21-2] which has not been plowed or sown, and there in the valley they are to break the heifer's neck.

**5** Then the priests, the sons of Levi, are to come forward, for the LORD your God has chosen them to serve him and to bless in the name of the LORD, and by their word every dispute and every case of assault[^21-3] is to be settled.

**6** All the elders of the town nearest to the body are to wash their hands over the heifer whose neck was broken in the valley,

**7** and they are to declare,[^21-4] "Our hands did not shed this blood, and our eyes did not see it.

**8** LORD, forgive[^21-5] your people Israel, whom you have redeemed, and do not hold the guilt of innocent blood against your people Israel."[^21-6] Then the bloodshed will be forgiven them.

**9** So you will purge[^21-7] the guilt of innocent blood from among you, for you will be doing what is right in the eyes of the LORD.

**10** When you go out to war against your enemies, and the LORD your God hands them over to you and you take some of them captive,

**11** you may see among the captives a beautiful woman, and desire her, and want to take her as your wife.

**12** Bring her into your house. She is to shave her head and trim her nails,

**13** and take off the clothes she was wearing when she was captured. She is to live in your house and mourn her father and her mother for a full month.[^21-8] After that you may go to her and be her husband, and she will be your wife.

**14** But if you are no longer pleased with her, let her go wherever she wishes.[^21-9] You must not sell her for money or treat her as a slave,[^21-10] because you have dishonored her.[^21-11]

**15** If a man has two wives, one loved and the other unloved,[^21-12] and both the loved wife and the unloved wife bear him sons, and the firstborn son is the son of the unloved wife,

**16** then on the day he divides his property among his sons as their inheritance, he may not treat the son of the loved wife as his firstborn in place of the son of the unloved wife, who is the firstborn.

**17** He must acknowledge the son of the unloved wife as the firstborn by giving him a double share[^21-13] of everything he has, for he is the first sign of his father's strength.[^21-14] The right of the firstborn belongs to him.

**18** If someone has a stubborn and rebellious son who will not obey his father or his mother, and will not listen to them even when they discipline him,

**19** his father and his mother are to take hold of him and bring him out to the elders of his town, at the gate of the place where he lives.

**20** They are to say to the elders of his town, "This son of ours is stubborn and rebellious. He will not obey us. He is a glutton and a drunkard."

**21** Then all the men of his town are to stone him to death. So you must purge the evil from among you, and all Israel will hear and be afraid.

**22** If someone has committed a sin that deserves the death penalty and has been put to death, and you hang his body on a tree,[^21-15]

**23** his body must not remain on the tree overnight. You must bury him that same day, for anyone who is hung on a tree is cursed by God.[^21-16] You must not defile the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

## Chapter 22

**1** If you see a fellow Israelite's[^22-1] ox or sheep wandering off, do not ignore it.[^22-2] You must take it back to him.

**2** If he does not live near you, or you do not know who he is, bring the animal home and keep it with you until he comes looking for it. Then give it back to him.

**3** Do the same with his donkey, and with his cloak, and with anything else he has lost that you find. You must not ignore it.

**4** If you see a fellow Israelite's donkey or ox fallen on the road, do not ignore it. You must help him get it back on its feet.

**5** A woman must not wear a man's things,[^22-3] and a man must not put on a woman's clothing, for anyone who does these things is detestable to the LORD[^22-4] your God.

**6** If you come across a bird's nest beside the road, in any tree or on the ground, with young birds or eggs, and the mother sitting on the young or on the eggs, do not take the mother along with the young.

**7** You must let the mother go, but you may take the young for yourself, so that it may go well with you and you may live long.

**8** When you build a new house, make a railing around your roof,[^22-5] so that you will not bring the guilt of bloodshed on your house if someone falls from it.

**9** Do not sow your vineyard with a second kind of seed, or the whole crop, both the seed you have sown and the fruit of the vineyard, will be forfeited.[^22-6]

**10** Do not plow with an ox and a donkey together.

**11** Do not wear clothing of mixed material,[^22-7] wool and linen together.

**12** Make tassels[^22-8] on the four corners of the cloak you wrap yourself in.

**13** Suppose a man marries a woman and sleeps with her,[^22-9] and then turns against her.[^22-10]

**14** He makes up charges against her[^22-11] and gives her a bad name, saying, "I married this woman, but when I came near her I did not find the evidence of her virginity."[^22-12]

**15** Then the young woman's[^22-13] father and mother are to take the evidence of her virginity and bring it out to the elders of the town at the gate.

**16** The young woman's father is to say to the elders, "I gave my daughter to this man as his wife, but he has turned against her.

**17** Now he has made up charges against her, saying, 'I did not find the evidence of your daughter's virginity.' But here is the evidence of my daughter's virginity." Then they are to spread out the cloth before the elders of the town.

**18** The elders of that town are to take the man and punish him.[^22-14]

**19** They are to fine him a hundred shekels of silver[^22-15] and give it to the young woman's father, because the man has given an Israelite virgin a bad name. She will remain his wife, and he may not divorce her[^22-16] as long as he lives.

**20** But if the charge is true, and no evidence of the young woman's virginity is found,

**21** they are to bring the young woman out to the door of her father's house, and the men of her town are to stone her to death. She has done a disgraceful thing in Israel by being promiscuous[^22-17] while in her father's house. So you must purge[^22-18] the evil from among you.

**22** If a man is found sleeping with another man's wife,[^22-19] both of them must die, the man who slept with the woman and the woman. So you must purge the evil from Israel.

**23** If a young woman who is a virgin is engaged to a man, and another man meets her in the town and sleeps with her,

**24** you are to take both of them out to the gate of that town and stone them to death: the young woman because she did not cry for help in the town, and the man because he violated[^22-20] his neighbor's wife. So you must purge the evil from among you.

**25** But if the man meets the engaged young woman in the open country, and seizes her and sleeps with her, only the man who slept with her must die.

**26** Do nothing to the young woman. She has committed no sin that deserves death. This case is like that of a man who attacks his neighbor and murders him.[^22-21]

**27** For he met her in the open country, and though the engaged young woman cried out, there was no one to rescue her.

**28** If a man meets a young woman who is a virgin and is not engaged, and seizes her and sleeps with her, and they are found out,

**29** the man who slept with her must give the young woman's father fifty shekels of silver,[^22-22] and she must become his wife. Because he has violated her, he may not divorce her as long as he lives.

**7** Never seek their peace or their prosperity, as long as you live.[^23-8]

**8** Do not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother. Do not abhor an Egyptian, because you were a foreigner[^23-9] in his land.

**20** Do not charge interest to your fellow Israelite,[^23-19] whether on money or on food or on anything else that can earn interest.

**21** You may charge interest to a foreigner,[^23-20] but not to your fellow Israelite, so that the LORD your God may bless you in everything you set your hand to in the land you are entering to take possession of.

**22** If you make a vow to the LORD your God, do not be slow to fulfill it, for the LORD your God will certainly require it of you, and you will be guilty of sin.

**24** Be careful to do what has passed your lips, just as you freely vowed to the LORD your God with your own mouth.[^23-21]

**25** When you go into your neighbor's vineyard, you may eat as many grapes as you want, until you are satisfied,[^23-22] but you must not put any in your basket.

**26** When you go into your neighbor's standing grain, you may pick the ears with your hand, but you must not put a sickle to your neighbor's standing grain.

## Chapter 25

*Status: draft*

**1** If there is a dispute between people, and they go to court and the judges decide between them, they are to acquit the innocent and condemn the guilty.[^25-1]

**2** If the guilty one deserves to be beaten,[^25-2] the judge is to make him lie down and have him beaten in his presence with the number of lashes his crime deserves.

**3** He may be given forty lashes, but no more. If he is beaten with many more lashes than these, your fellow Israelite[^25-3] will be degraded before your eyes.

**4** Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain.[^25-4]

**5** If brothers live together and one of them dies without a son, the dead man's widow must not marry outside the family, to a stranger. Her husband's brother is to go to her and take her as his wife, and do the duty of a husband's brother[^25-5] to her.

**6** The first son she bears is to carry on the name of the dead brother, so that his name will not be wiped out from Israel.

**7** But if the man does not want to marry his brother's widow, she is to go up to the elders at the gate and say, "My husband's brother refuses to keep his brother's name alive in Israel. He is not willing to do the duty of a husband's brother to me."

**8** Then the elders of his town are to summon him and speak to him. If he stands firm and says, "I do not want to marry her,"

**9** his brother's widow is to go up to him in the presence of the elders, pull his sandal off his foot, spit in his face, and declare,[^25-6] "This is what is done to the man who will not build up his brother's family."[^25-7]

**10** And his family will be known in Israel as "The house of the man whose sandal was pulled off."[^25-8]

**11** If two men are fighting each other, and the wife of one of them comes near to rescue her husband from the man who is hitting him, and she reaches out and seizes him by his private parts,

**12** you are to cut off her hand. Show her no pity.[^25-9]

**13** Do not have two different weights in your bag, one heavy and one light.[^25-10]

## Chapter 34

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Moses went up from the plains of Moab to Mount Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, opposite Jericho. And the LORD[^34-1] showed him the whole land: Gilead as far as Dan,

**2** all Naphtali, the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, all the land of Judah as far as the western sea,[^34-2]

**3** the Negev, and the plain of the valley of Jericho,[^34-3] the city of palm trees, as far as Zoar.

**4** Then the LORD said to him, "This is the land I swore to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, when I said, 'I will give it to your descendants.' I have let you see it with your own eyes, but you will not cross over into it."

**5** So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, as the LORD had said.[^34-4]

**6** He[^34-5] buried him in the valley in the land of Moab, opposite Beth-peor, and to this day no one knows where his grave is.

**7** Moses was 120 years old when he died. His eyesight was not dim, and his vigor had not left him.

**8** The Israelites wept for Moses in the plains of Moab for thirty days. Then the days of weeping and mourning for Moses came to an end.

**9** Joshua son of Nun was filled with the spirit of wisdom,[^34-6] because Moses had laid his hands on him. So the Israelites listened to him and did as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**10** Never since has a prophet arisen in Israel like Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face,

**11** in all the signs and wonders that the LORD sent him to do in the land of Egypt, to Pharaoh and to all his servants and to all his land,

**12** and in all the mighty power[^34-7] and all the great and terrifying deeds that Moses performed in the sight of all Israel.

[^34-3]: Hebrew *kikkar*, "circle": the round plain of the Jordan near Jericho.
[^34-4]: Literally "at the mouth of the LORD".
[^34-5]: The Hebrew does not name the subject. It is probably the LORD; or "he was buried". The Septuagint reads "they buried him".
[^34-6]: Hebrew *ruach*: spirit, breath.
