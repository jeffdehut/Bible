## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan,[^1-1] in the wilderness, in the Arabah[^1-2] opposite Suph,[^1-3] between Paran and Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth and Di-zahab.

**2** It is eleven days' journey from Horeb[^1-4] to Kadesh-barnea by the Mount Seir road.

**3** In the fortieth year, on the first day of the eleventh month, Moses spoke to the Israelites, telling them everything Yahweh[^1-5] had commanded him to tell them.

**4** This was after he had defeated Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon, and Og king of Bashan, who lived in Ashtaroth, at Edrei.[^1-6]

**5** East of the Jordan, in the land of Moab, Moses began to explain this law.[^1-7] He said:[^1-8]

**6** Yahweh our God spoke to us at Horeb. He said, "You have stayed at this mountain long enough.

**7** Turn and set out. Go to the hill country of the Amorites and to all their neighbors in the Arabah, in the hill country, in the western foothills,[^1-9] in the Negev[^1-10] and along the seacoast: the land of the Canaanites, and Lebanon, as far as the great river, the river Euphrates.

**8** See, I have set the land before you. Go in and take possession of the land that Yahweh swore to give to your ancestors,[^1-11] to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, and to their descendants after them."

**9** At that time I said to you, "I cannot carry you by myself.

**10** Yahweh your God has multiplied you, and here you are today, as many as the stars of the sky.

**11** May Yahweh, the God of your ancestors, make you a thousand times as many as you are, and bless you as he has promised you.

**12** How can I bear by myself the weight of you, the burden of you and your quarrels?

**13** Choose[^1-12] wise, discerning and respected[^1-13] men from each of your tribes, and I will make them your leaders."

**14** You answered me, "What you have told us to do is good."

**15** So I took the leaders of your tribes, wise and respected men, and made them leaders over you: commanders of thousands, commanders of hundreds, commanders of fifties and commanders of tens, and officials[^1-14] for your tribes.

**16** At that time I gave your judges this command: "Hear the disputes between your fellow Israelites,[^1-15] and judge fairly between one person and another, whether a fellow Israelite or a foreigner who lives among you.[^1-16]

**17** Do not show partiality in judging.[^1-17] Hear the small and the great alike. Do not be afraid of anyone, for judgment belongs to God. Bring to me any case that is too hard for you, and I will hear it."

**18** At that time I commanded you all the things you were to do.

**19** Then we set out from Horeb and went through all that great and terrible wilderness that you saw, on the way to the hill country of the Amorites, as Yahweh our God had commanded us. And we came to Kadesh-barnea.

**20** I said to you, "You have come to the hill country of the Amorites, which Yahweh our God is giving us.

**21** See, Yahweh your God has set the land before you.[^1-18] Go up and take possession of it, as Yahweh, the God of your ancestors, has told you. Do not be afraid or discouraged."

**22** Then all of you came to me and said, "Let us send men ahead of us to explore the land for us. Let them bring us back a report about the route we should take up and the cities we will come to."

**23** The idea seemed good to me, so I took twelve men from among you, one man from each tribe.

**24** They turned and went up into the hill country and came to the Valley of Eshcol,[^1-19] and they spied out the land.

**25** They took some of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought it down to us. They brought us back a report and said, "The land that Yahweh our God is giving us is good."

**26** But you were not willing to go up. You rebelled against the command[^1-20] of Yahweh your God.

**27** You grumbled in your tents and said, "Yahweh hates us. That is why he brought us out of the land of Egypt, to hand us over to the Amorites to destroy us.

**28** Where can we go up to? Our brothers have made our hearts melt.[^1-21] They say, 'The people are bigger and taller than we are. The cities are large, with walls up to the sky.[^1-22] And we even saw the Anakim[^1-23] there.'"

**29** Then I said to you, "Do not be terrified or afraid of them.

**30** Yahweh your God, who is going ahead of you, will himself fight for you, just as he did for you in Egypt before your eyes,

**31** and in the wilderness. There you saw how Yahweh your God carried you, as a man carries his son, all the way you went until you came to this place."

**32** Yet in spite of this,[^1-24] you did not trust Yahweh your God,

**33** who went ahead of you on the way to look for a place for you to camp, in fire by night to show you the way you should go, and in the cloud by day.

**34** When Yahweh heard what you said,[^1-25] he was angry, and he swore,

**35** "Not one of these men, this evil generation, will see the good land that I swore to give to your ancestors,

**36** except Caleb son of Jephunneh. He will see it, and I will give him and his children the land he has walked on, because he followed Yahweh completely."[^1-26]

**37** Because of you, Yahweh was angry with me too. He said, "You will not enter it either.

**38** Joshua son of Nun, who serves you,[^1-27] will enter it. Encourage him, for he will lead Israel to take possession of it.[^1-28]

**39** And your little ones, who you said would be taken as plunder, and your children, who today do not yet know good from bad, they will enter it. I will give it to them, and they will take possession of it.

**40** But as for you, turn around and set out for the wilderness, by the road to the Red Sea."[^1-29]

**41** Then you answered me, "We have sinned against Yahweh. We will go up and fight, just as Yahweh our God commanded us." Each of you put on his weapons, and you thought it easy[^1-30] to go up into the hill country.

**42** But Yahweh said to me, "Tell them, 'Do not go up and do not fight, for I am not with you.[^1-31] You will be defeated by your enemies.'"

**43** So I told you, but you would not listen. You rebelled against Yahweh's command, and in your arrogance you went up into the hill country.

**44** The Amorites who lived in that hill country came out against you and chased you like a swarm of bees. They struck you down in Seir as far as Hormah.

**45** You came back and wept before Yahweh, but Yahweh did not listen to your voice or pay any attention to you.

**46** So you stayed in Kadesh for a long time, all the time that you stayed there.[^1-32]

[^1-1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", seen from the land west of the river. Also in verse 5.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *aravah*: the deep rift valley of the Jordan and the Dead Sea, and its continuation south to the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^1-3]: *Suph* is otherwise unknown. It may be short for *Yam Suph*, the Sea of Reeds (verse 40).
[^1-4]: Deuteronomy calls the mountain of God Horeb, the name used for Sinai in Exodus 3:1 and 17:6.
[^1-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-6]: Edrei is where Og was defeated (3:1). Compare Joshua 12:4, "who lived in Ashtaroth and in Edrei".
[^1-7]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, law". The verb *be'er*, "explain", means to make something plain (compare 27:8).
[^1-8]: Moses' first speech runs from here to 4:40. As in many English Bibles, his long speeches in Deuteronomy are not put inside quotation marks.
[^1-9]: Hebrew *shephelah*: the low hills between the central hill country and the coastal plain.
[^1-10]: The dry country in the south of the land.
[^1-11]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 11, 21 and 35.
[^1-12]: Literally "Give".
[^1-13]: Literally "known"; also in verse 15.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions.
[^1-15]: Literally "your brothers".
[^1-16]: Literally "between a man and his brother and his foreigner". Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^1-17]: Literally "Do not recognize faces in judgment".
[^1-18]: Here the Hebrew changes from "you" plural to "you" singular, addressing Israel as one person. Deuteronomy often moves between the two.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. *Eshcol* means "cluster" of grapes (Numbers 13:23–24).
[^1-20]: Literally "mouth"; also in verse 43.
[^1-21]: That is, they have made us lose courage.
[^1-22]: Literally "fortified up to the heavens".
[^1-23]: Literally "the sons of the Anakim", a people known for their great height (Numbers 13:28, 33).
[^1-24]: Or "in this matter". Literally "And in this word".
[^1-25]: Literally "the sound of your words".
[^1-26]: Literally "he has filled after Yahweh".
[^1-27]: Literally "who stands before you".
[^1-28]: Or "he will give it to Israel as their inheritance". Literally "he will cause Israel to inherit it".
[^1-29]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it is probably the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^1-30]: The Hebrew verb occurs only here. It may mean "you thought it easy" or "you were eager".
[^1-31]: Literally "for I am not in your midst".
[^1-32]: Literally "according to the days that you stayed". The phrase probably means a long stay; it may also be read "as long as you did".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Then we turned and set out for the wilderness by the road to the Red Sea,[^2-1] as Yahweh[^2-2] had told me. For a long time we went around the hill country of Seir.

**2** Then Yahweh said to me,

**3** "You have been going around this hill country long enough. Turn north.

**4** Give the people this command: 'You are about to pass through the territory of your relatives,[^2-3] the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you, but be very careful.

**5** Do not start a fight with them, for I will not give you any of their land, not even enough to put your foot on.[^2-4] I have given the hill country of Seir to Esau as his possession.

**6** You are to buy food from them with money, so that you may eat, and you are also to buy water from them with money, so that you may drink.

**7** For Yahweh your God has blessed you in all the work of your hands. He has watched over your journey[^2-5] through this great wilderness. For these forty years Yahweh your God has been with you, and you have not lacked anything.'"

**8** So we went on past our relatives, the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir, away from the Arabah road, away from Elath and Ezion-geber. Then we turned and went along the road to the wilderness of Moab.

**9** Yahweh said to me, "Do not harass Moab or start a war with them, for I will not give you any of their land as a possession. I have given Ar[^2-6] to the descendants of Lot[^2-7] as their possession."

**10** (The Emim used to live there, a people great and numerous, and as tall as the Anakim.

**11** Like the Anakim, they too were counted as Rephaim,[^2-8] but the Moabites call them Emim.

**12** The Horites also used to live in Seir, but the descendants of Esau drove them out. They destroyed them and settled in their place, just as Israel did in the land that Yahweh gave them as their possession.)

**13** "Now get up and cross the Zered Valley."[^2-9] So we crossed the Zered Valley.

**14** From the time we left Kadesh-barnea until we crossed the Zered Valley was thirty-eight years, until the whole generation of fighting men had died out from the camp, as Yahweh had sworn to them.

**15** Indeed, the hand of Yahweh was against them, to wipe them out from the camp until they were all gone.

**16** When the last of the fighting men among the people had died,

**17** Yahweh said to me,

**18** "Today you are to cross the border of Moab at Ar.

**19** When you come near the Ammonites,[^2-10] do not harass them or start a fight with them, for I will not give you any of the land of the Ammonites as a possession. I have given it to the descendants of Lot as their possession."

**20** (That too was counted as a land of the Rephaim. Rephaim used to live there, but the Ammonites call them Zamzummim,

**21** a people great and numerous, and as tall as the Anakim. But Yahweh destroyed them before the Ammonites,[^2-11] who drove them out and settled in their place.

**22** He had done the same for the descendants of Esau, who live in Seir, when he destroyed the Horites before them. They drove them out and have lived in their place to this day.

**23** And as for the Avvim, who lived in villages as far as Gaza, the Caphtorim, who came from Caphtor,[^2-12] destroyed them and settled in their place.)

**24** "Get up, set out and cross the Arnon Valley. See, I have handed over to you Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land. Begin to take possession of it. Go to war against him.

**25** This very day I will begin to put the dread and fear of you on the peoples everywhere under heaven. When they hear the report of you, they will tremble and be in anguish because of you."

**26** So I sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon king of Heshbon with an offer of peace.[^2-13] I said,

**27** "Let me pass through your land. I will keep strictly to the road.[^2-14] I will not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**28** Sell me food for money, so that I may eat, and give me water for money, so that I may drink. Only let me pass through on foot,

**29** as the descendants of Esau who live in Seir and the Moabites who live in Ar did for me, until I cross the Jordan into the land that Yahweh our God is giving us."

**30** But Sihon king of Heshbon was not willing to let us pass through his land,[^2-15] for Yahweh your God had made his spirit stubborn and his heart hard, to hand him over to you, as he has now done.[^2-16]

**31** Yahweh said to me, "See, I have begun to hand Sihon and his land over to you. Begin to take possession, so that you may possess his land."

**32** Then Sihon came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Jahaz.

**33** Yahweh our God handed him over to us,[^2-17] and we struck him down, together with his sons[^2-18] and all his people.

**34** At that time we captured all his towns and devoted every town to destruction,[^2-19] men, women and children. We left no survivors.

**35** We kept only the livestock as plunder for ourselves, along with the spoil of the towns we had captured.

**36** From Aroer, on the edge of the Arnon Valley, and from the town in the valley, as far as Gilead, there was not one town too strong for us.[^2-20] Yahweh our God handed them all over to us.

**37** But you did not go near the land of the Ammonites: the whole bank of the Jabbok River, the towns of the hill country, or any place that Yahweh our God had forbidden.[^2-21]

[^2-1]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it is probably the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^2-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Literally "your brothers"; also in verse 8. Esau, the ancestor of the Edomites, was Jacob's brother (Genesis 36:1, 8).
[^2-4]: Literally "not even as much as the treading of the sole of a foot".
[^2-5]: Literally "He has known your walking".
[^2-6]: A city or district of Moab, near the Arnon (Numbers 21:15, 28).
[^2-7]: Lot, Abraham's nephew, was the ancestor of Moab and Ammon (Genesis 19:36–38).
[^2-8]: An ancient people remembered for their great height (3:11).
[^2-9]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. Also in verses 14, 24, 36 and 37.
[^2-10]: Literally "the sons of Ammon"; also in verses 20, 21 and 37.
[^2-11]: Literally "before them".
[^2-12]: Caphtor is probably Crete. The Philistines came from there (Amos 9:7).
[^2-13]: Literally "words of peace".
[^2-14]: Literally "In the road, in the road I will go".
[^2-15]: Literally "to let us pass through him".
[^2-16]: Literally "as this day".
[^2-17]: Literally "gave him before us".
[^2-18]: The Hebrew text is written "his son" but read "his sons".
[^2-19]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^2-20]: Literally "too high for us".
[^2-21]: Literally "and all that Yahweh our God had commanded".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Then we turned and went up the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to fight at Edrei.

**2** Yahweh[^3-1] said to me, "Do not be afraid of him, for I have handed him over to you, with all his people and his land. Do to him what you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon."

**3** So Yahweh our God also handed over to us Og king of Bashan and all his people. We struck him down until no survivor was left to him.

**4** At that time we captured all his towns. There was not a town that we did not take from them: sixty towns, the whole region[^3-2] of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.

**5** All these were towns fortified with high walls, gates and bars, not counting a great many unwalled villages.[^3-3]

**6** We devoted them to destruction,[^3-4] as we had done to Sihon king of Heshbon, devoting every town to destruction, men, women and children.

**7** But all the livestock and the spoil of the towns we took as plunder for ourselves.

**8** So at that time we took the land from the two kings of the Amorites east of the Jordan,[^3-5] from the Arnon Valley to Mount Hermon.

**9** (The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir.)

**10** We took all the towns of the plateau,[^3-6] all Gilead and all Bashan as far as Salecah and Edrei, towns of Og's kingdom in Bashan.

**11** (Only Og king of Bashan was left of the remnant of the Rephaim. His bed[^3-7] was made of iron. Isn't it still in Rabbah of the Ammonites? It is nine cubits long and four cubits wide, by the standard cubit.[^3-8])

**12** So we took possession of this land at that time. I gave the Reubenites and the Gadites the land from Aroer, by the Arnon Valley, and half the hill country of Gilead with its towns.

**13** The rest of Gilead, and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og, I gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh. (The whole region of Argob, all that part of Bashan, is called the land of the Rephaim.

**14** Jair, a descendant of Manasseh,[^3-9] took the whole region of Argob as far as the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites. He named Bashan after himself, Havvoth-jair,[^3-10] as it is called to this day.)

**15** To Machir I gave Gilead.

**16** To the Reubenites and the Gadites I gave the territory from Gilead as far as the Arnon Valley, with the middle of the valley as the border, and as far as the Jabbok River,[^3-11] the border of the Ammonites.

**17** Their territory also included the Arabah, with the Jordan as its border, from Kinnereth[^3-12] to the Sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea,[^3-13] below the slopes of Pisgah on the east.

**18** At that time I commanded you, "Yahweh your God has given you this land to possess. All your fighting men[^3-14] are to cross over armed ahead of your fellow Israelites.[^3-15]

**19** Only your wives, your little ones and your livestock (I know you have a great deal of livestock) may stay in the towns I have given you,

**20** until Yahweh gives your relatives rest as he has given you, and they too take possession of the land that Yahweh your God is giving them on the other side of the Jordan.[^3-16] Then each of you may return to the possession I have given you."

**21** At that time I commanded Joshua, "Your own eyes have seen everything Yahweh your God has done to these two kings. Yahweh will do the same to all the kingdoms you are crossing over into.

**22** Do not be afraid of them, for Yahweh your God is the one who fights for you."

**23** At that time I pleaded with Yahweh:

**24** "Lord Yahweh,[^3-17] you have begun to show your servant your greatness and your mighty hand. What god is there in heaven or on earth who can do deeds and mighty acts like yours?

**25** Please let me cross over and see the good land on the other side of the Jordan, that good hill country, and Lebanon."

**26** But Yahweh was angry with me because of you, and he would not listen to me. Yahweh said to me, "That is enough![^3-18] Do not speak to me about this matter again.

**27** Go up to the top of Pisgah and look west, north, south and east.[^3-19] See it with your own eyes, for you will not cross this Jordan.

**28** Give Joshua his orders. Encourage him and strengthen him, for he will cross over at the head of this people, and he will lead them to take possession of the land that you will see."

**29** So we stayed in the valley opposite Beth-peor.

[^3-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-2]: Hebrew *chevel*, literally "measuring line", here a measured-out region. Also in verses 13 and 14.
[^3-3]: Literally "towns of the open country".
[^3-4]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^3-5]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", seen from the land west of the river.
[^3-6]: Hebrew *mishor*, "level land": the high tableland north of the Arnon.
[^3-7]: Or "his coffin". Hebrew *eres*, "bed, couch". Some take it to mean a coffin made of a dark, hard stone like iron.
[^3-8]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the bed was about 13 feet long and 6 feet wide (4 by 1.8 meters). Literally "by the cubit of a man", that is, the ordinary cubit.
[^3-9]: Literally "son of Manasseh". In Hebrew "son" can mean any descendant.
[^3-10]: *Havvoth-jair* means "the tent villages of Jair".
[^3-11]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. Also earlier in the verse.
[^3-12]: The Sea of Galilee, which the Hebrew elsewhere calls the Sea of Kinnereth.
[^3-13]: The Dead Sea.
[^3-14]: Literally "all the sons of strength".
[^3-15]: Literally "your brothers, the sons of Israel". In verse 20 "your relatives" is literally "your brothers".
[^3-16]: Literally "beyond the Jordan". Here it means west of the river, seen from where Moses stands. Also in verse 25.
[^3-17]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^3-18]: Literally "Much for you", the words translated "long enough" in 1:6 and 2:3.
[^3-19]: Literally "lift up your eyes toward the sea and north and south and east". West is "toward the sea", the Mediterranean.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Now, Israel, listen to the statutes and the rulings[^4-1] that I am teaching you to follow, so that you may live, and go in and take possession of the land that Yahweh,[^4-2] the God of your ancestors,[^4-3] is giving you.

**2** Do not add anything to what I command you, and do not take anything away from it. Keep the commandments of Yahweh your God that I am giving you.

**3** You saw with your own eyes what Yahweh did at Baal-peor.[^4-4] Yahweh your God destroyed from among you everyone who followed the Baal of Peor.

**4** But all of you who held fast to Yahweh your God are alive today.

**5** See, I have taught you statutes and rulings, as Yahweh my God commanded me, so that you may follow them in the land you are entering to take possession of.

**6** Keep them and follow them, for this will show your wisdom and understanding to the peoples.[^4-5] When they hear about all these statutes, they will say, "Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people."

**7** For what great nation has a god[^4-6] so near to it as Yahweh our God is to us whenever we call on him?

**8** And what great nation has statutes and rulings as righteous as this whole law that I am setting before you today?

**9** Only be careful, and watch yourselves closely,[^4-7] so that you do not forget the things your eyes have seen, and so that they do not slip from your heart[^4-8] as long as you live. Make them known to your children and your grandchildren:

**10** the day you stood before Yahweh your God at Horeb, when Yahweh said to me, "Assemble the people for me, and I will let them hear my words, so that they may learn to fear me as long as they live on the earth, and may teach their children."

**11** You came near and stood at the foot of the mountain, while the mountain blazed with fire up to the heart of the sky,[^4-9] with darkness, cloud and thick gloom.

**12** Then Yahweh spoke to you out of the fire. You heard the sound of words, but you saw no form. There was only a voice.

**13** He declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to keep:[^4-10] the Ten Commandments.[^4-11] And he wrote them on two stone tablets.

**14** At that time Yahweh commanded me to teach you statutes and rulings, for you to follow in the land you are crossing over into to take possession of.

**15** So watch yourselves very carefully.[^4-12] Since you saw no form of any kind on the day Yahweh spoke to you at Horeb out of the fire,

**16** do not act corruptly by making for yourselves an idol[^4-13] in the form of any figure: the likeness of a male or a female,

**17** the likeness of any animal on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the sky,

**18** the likeness of anything that creeps on the ground, or the likeness of any fish in the waters below the earth.

**19** And when you look up to the sky and see the sun, the moon and the stars, the whole array of heaven,[^4-14] do not be drawn away to bow down to them and serve them. Yahweh your God has allotted them to all the peoples everywhere under heaven.

**20** But you are the ones Yahweh took and brought out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be the people of his inheritance,[^4-15] as you are today.

**21** Yahweh was angry with me because of you,[^4-16] and he swore that I would not cross the Jordan or enter the good land that Yahweh your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**22** For I am going to die in this land. I will not cross the Jordan. But you are going to cross over and take possession of this good land.

**23** Be careful not to forget the covenant that Yahweh your God made with you. Do not make for yourselves an idol in the form of anything that Yahweh your God has forbidden you.[^4-17]

**24** For Yahweh your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God.[^4-18]

**25** When you have had children and grandchildren and have been in the land a long time, if you act corruptly by making an idol in the form of anything, and do what is evil in the eyes of Yahweh your God, provoking him to anger,

**26** I call heaven and earth as witnesses against you today that you will soon perish completely from the land you are crossing the Jordan to take possession of. You will not live long on it, but will be utterly destroyed.

**27** Yahweh will scatter you among the peoples, and only a few of you[^4-19] will be left among the nations where Yahweh will drive you.

**28** There you will serve gods made by human hands, of wood and stone, which cannot see or hear or eat or smell.

**29** But from there you will seek Yahweh your God, and you will find him, if you search for him with all your heart and with all your soul.[^4-20]

**30** When you are in distress and all these things have happened to you,[^4-21] in days to come,[^4-22] you will return to Yahweh your God and listen to his voice.

**1** You are the children[^14-1] of Yahweh[^14-2] your God. Do not cut yourselves or shave the front of your heads[^14-3] for the dead.

**2** For you are a people holy to Yahweh your God. Yahweh has chosen you out of all the peoples on the face of the earth to be his people, his treasured possession.[^14-4]

**3** Do not eat anything detestable.[^14-5]

**4** These are the animals you may eat: the ox, the sheep, the goat,

**5** the deer, the gazelle, the roe deer, the wild goat, the ibex, the antelope and the mountain sheep.[^14-6]

**6** You may eat any animal that has a divided hoof, split in two, and that chews the cud.

**7** But of those that chew the cud or that have a divided, split hoof, you must not eat these: the camel, the hare and the hyrax.[^14-7] Although they chew the cud, they do not have a divided hoof. They are unclean for you.

**8** And the pig is unclean for you, because although it has a divided hoof, it does not chew the cud. You must not eat their meat or touch their carcasses.

**9** Of all the creatures that live in the water, you may eat these: you may eat anything that has fins and scales.

**10** But you must not eat anything that does not have fins and scales. It is unclean for you.

**11** You may eat any clean bird.

**12** But these are the ones you must not eat:[^14-8] the eagle, the bearded vulture, the black vulture,

**13** the red kite, the black kite, any kind of falcon,[^14-9]

**14** any kind of raven,

**15** the ostrich,[^14-10] the nighthawk, the seagull, any kind of hawk,

**16** the little owl, the great owl, the white owl,

**17** the desert owl, the carrion vulture, the cormorant,

**18** the stork, any kind of heron, the hoopoe and the bat.

**19** All winged swarming creatures[^14-11] are unclean for you. They must not be eaten.

**20** You may eat any clean winged creature.

**21** Do not eat anything that has died by itself. You may give it to the foreigner[^14-12] who lives in your towns,[^14-13] and he may eat it, or you may sell it to an outsider.[^14-14] For you are a people holy to Yahweh your God. Do not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**22** Each year you must set aside a tenth of all the produce of your seed that comes from the field.

**23** In the presence of Yahweh your God, at the place he will choose as a dwelling for his name,[^14-15] you are to eat the tithe of your grain, your new wine and your olive oil, and the firstborn of your herds and your flocks, so that you may learn to fear Yahweh your God always.

**24** But Yahweh your God may bless you so much that the journey is too long for you to carry the tithe, because the place where Yahweh your God will choose to put his name is too far from you.

**25** Then exchange it for money. Wrap up the money[^14-16] and take it with you, and go to the place that Yahweh your God will choose.

**26** Spend the money on whatever you want: cattle or sheep, wine or beer,[^14-17] or anything you wish.[^14-18] Eat it there in the presence of Yahweh your God, and rejoice, you and your household.

**27** And do not neglect the Levite who lives in your towns, for he has no share or inheritance among you.

**28** At the end of every three years, bring out the whole tithe of your produce for that year and store it in your towns.

**29** Then the Levite, who has no share or inheritance among you, and the foreigner, the fatherless[^14-19] and the widow who live in your towns, may come and eat and be satisfied, so that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work you do with your hands.

[^14-1]: Literally "sons".
[^14-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^14-3]: Literally "make a bald place between your eyes".
[^14-4]: Hebrew *segullah*, a king's or an owner's personal treasure.
[^14-5]: Hebrew *to'evah*: something abhorrent, especially in worship.
[^14-6]: The exact identification of several of these animals is uncertain.
[^14-7]: Hebrew *shaphan*, a small animal that lives among rocks, traditionally "rock badger" or "coney". The hare and the hyrax move their jaws as if chewing the cud.
[^14-8]: The identification of many of the birds in verses 12–18 is uncertain. Compare the list in Leviticus 11:13–19.
[^14-9]: The Hebrew has three similar names here, *ra'ah*, *ayyah* and *dayyah*. Leviticus 11:14 has two, *da'ah* and *ayyah*.
[^14-10]: Literally "the daughter of the *ya'anah*". Or "the eagle owl".
[^14-11]: That is, winged insects.
[^14-12]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 29.
[^14-13]: Literally "within your gates"; also in verses 27, 28 and 29.
[^14-14]: Hebrew *nokhri*: a foreigner from another land who has not settled among the Israelites.
[^14-15]: Literally "to make his name dwell there".
[^14-16]: Literally "bind up the silver in your hand".
[^14-17]: Hebrew *shekhar*, an intoxicating drink, probably beer made from grain.
[^14-18]: Literally "anything your *nephesh* (self) asks of you".
[^14-19]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan".

## Chapter 15

*Status: draft*

**1** At the end of every seven years you must grant a release.[^15-1]

**2** This is how the release is to be done: every creditor is to cancel the loan he has made to his neighbor.[^15-2] He must not press his neighbor, his fellow Israelite,[^15-3] for payment, because a release has been proclaimed for Yahweh.[^15-4]

**3** You may press a foreigner[^15-5] for payment, but you must cancel whatever your fellow Israelite owes you.[^15-6]

**4** However, there should be no poor among you,[^15-7] for Yahweh will richly bless you in the land that Yahweh your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess,

**5** if only you listen carefully to the voice of Yahweh your God and are careful to follow all this commandment that I am giving you today.

**6** For Yahweh your God will bless you, as he has promised you. You will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow. You will rule over many nations, but they will not rule over you.

**7** If there is a poor person among you, one of your fellow Israelites, in any of your towns[^15-8] in the land that Yahweh your God is giving you, do not be hardhearted or tightfisted toward your poor brother.[^15-9]

**8** Instead, open your hand to him freely, and lend him enough for whatever he needs.

**9** Be careful not to harbor this wicked thought:[^15-10] "The seventh year, the year of release, is near," and so look grudgingly[^15-11] on your poor brother and give him nothing. He may cry out to Yahweh against you, and you will be guilty of sin.

**10** Give to him generously, and do not give with a grudging heart,[^15-12] for because of this Yahweh your God will bless you in all your work and in everything you set your hand to.

**11** For there will never cease to be poor people in the land.[^15-13] That is why I command you: open your hand freely to your brother, to the needy and the poor in your land.

**12** If one of your own people, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you[^15-14] and serves you for six years, then in the seventh year you must set that person free.

**13** And when you set him free, do not send him away empty-handed.

**14** Supply him generously[^15-15] from your flock, your threshing floor and your winepress. Give to him as Yahweh your God has blessed you.

**15** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and Yahweh your God redeemed you. That is why I am giving you this command today.

**16** But if he says to you, "I do not want to leave you," because he loves you and your household and is well off with you,

**17** then take an awl and drive it through his ear into the door, and he will be your slave for life.[^15-16] Do the same for your female slave.

**18** Do not consider it a hardship to set him free, for his six years of service to you have been worth twice the wages of a hired worker.[^15-17] And Yahweh your God will bless you in everything you do.

**19** Set apart for Yahweh your God every firstborn male born in your herd and in your flock. Do not put the firstborn of your oxen to work, and do not shear the firstborn of your sheep.

**20** Each year you and your household are to eat them before Yahweh your God, in the place that Yahweh will choose.

**21** But if an animal has a defect, if it is lame or blind or has any serious defect, you must not sacrifice it to Yahweh your God.

**22** Eat it in your towns. Both the unclean and the clean[^15-18] may eat it, as they would a gazelle or a deer.

**23** But you must not eat its blood. Pour it out on the ground like water.

[^15-2]: Literally "every owner of a loan of his hand shall release what he lends to his neighbor".
[^15-3]: Literally "his brother"; also in verses 3, 7, 9 and 11.
[^15-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^15-5]: Hebrew *nokhri*: a foreigner from another land who has not settled among the Israelites.
[^15-6]: Literally "but what is yours with your brother, your hand shall release".
[^15-7]: Or "there will be no poor among you".
[^15-8]: Literally "gates"; also in verse 22.
[^15-9]: Literally "do not harden your heart or shut your hand against your poor brother".
[^15-10]: Literally "lest there be a word in your heart, a *beliyya'al* (worthless) one".
[^15-11]: Literally "and your eye is evil toward".
[^15-12]: Literally "and let your heart not be evil".
[^15-13]: Compare Mark 14:7.
[^15-14]: Or "sells himself to you". Literally "your brother, the Hebrew man or the Hebrew woman". Compare Exodus 21:2–11.
[^15-15]: Hebrew *ha'aneq ta'aniq*, from a word for "necklace": load him with gifts as if hanging them around his neck.
[^15-16]: Literally "a slave forever".
[^15-17]: Or "worth the wages of a hired worker". Literally "for double the hire of a hired worker he has served you six years".
[^15-18]: That is, people who are ritually unclean as well as people who are clean.

## Chapter 16

*Status: draft*

**1** Observe the month of Abib[^16-1] and keep the Passover[^16-2] for Yahweh[^16-3] your God, for in the month of Abib Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt by night.

**2** Sacrifice the Passover animal for Yahweh your God from your flock or your herd, at the place that Yahweh will choose as a dwelling for his name.[^16-4]

**3** Do not eat anything made with yeast with it. For seven days eat bread made without yeast with it, the bread of hardship, because you left the land of Egypt in haste. Do this so that all the days of your life you may remember the day you left the land of Egypt.

**4** No yeast is to be found among you anywhere in your territory for seven days, and none of the meat you sacrifice on the evening of the first day is to be left overnight until morning.

**5** You may not sacrifice the Passover in just any of the towns[^16-5] that Yahweh your God is giving you,

**6** but only at the place that Yahweh your God will choose as a dwelling for his name. There you are to sacrifice the Passover in the evening, at sunset, at the time[^16-6] when you came out of Egypt.

**7** Cook[^16-7] it and eat it at the place that Yahweh your God will choose. Then in the morning turn and go back to your tents.

**8** For six days eat bread made without yeast, and on the seventh day hold a solemn assembly[^16-8] for Yahweh your God. Do no work on that day.

**9** Count seven weeks. Begin to count the seven weeks from the time you first put the sickle to the standing grain.

**10** Then keep the Festival of Weeks for Yahweh your God with a freewill offering from your hand, given in proportion to[^16-9] how Yahweh your God has blessed you.

**11** Rejoice before Yahweh your God at the place that Yahweh your God will choose as a dwelling for his name, you and your son and your daughter, your male and female slaves, the Levite who lives in your towns, and the foreigner,[^16-10] the fatherless[^16-11] and the widow who live among you.

**12** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and be careful to follow these statutes.

**13** Keep the Festival of Shelters[^16-12] for seven days, when you have gathered in the produce of your threshing floor and your winepress.

**14** Rejoice at your festival, you and your son and your daughter, your male and female slaves, and the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow who live in your towns.

**15** For seven days keep the festival for Yahweh your God at the place that Yahweh will choose, for Yahweh your God will bless you in all your harvest and in all the work of your hands, and you will be full of joy.[^16-13]

**16** Three times a year all your males are to appear before Yahweh your God[^16-14] at the place he will choose: at the Festival of Unleavened Bread, at the Festival of Weeks and at the Festival of Shelters. No one is to appear before Yahweh empty-handed.

**17** Each of you is to give as he is able,[^16-15] according to the blessing that Yahweh your God has given you.

**18** Appoint judges and officials[^16-16] for your tribes in all the towns that Yahweh your God is giving you, and they are to judge the people with righteous judgment.

**19** Do not twist justice. Do not show partiality.[^16-17] Do not accept a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and twists the words of those who are in the right.[^16-18]

**20** Pursue justice, justice alone,[^16-19] so that you may live and possess the land that Yahweh your God is giving you.

**21** Do not plant any kind of tree as an Asherah pole[^16-20] beside the altar you build for Yahweh your God.

**22** And do not set up a standing stone,[^16-21] which Yahweh your God hates.

[^16-1]: *Abib* means "new ears of grain": the first month of the year, around March–April.
[^16-2]: Hebrew *pesach*: both the festival and the lamb or young goat sacrificed at it (Exodus 12). Also in verses 2, 5 and 6.
[^16-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^16-4]: Literally "to make his name dwell there"; also in verses 6 and 11.
[^16-5]: Literally "gates"; also in verses 11, 14 and 18.
[^16-6]: Hebrew *mo'ed*, "appointed time": either the time of day or the season of the year.
[^16-7]: Hebrew *bashal*, usually "boil". In Exodus 12:8–9 the Passover lamb is to be roasted and not boiled.
[^16-8]: Hebrew *atseret*, a closing assembly.
[^16-9]: Hebrew *missat*, a word found only here, probably meaning "the amount of".
[^16-10]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 14.
[^16-11]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan". Also in verse 14.
[^16-12]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths, shelters": huts made of branches (Leviticus 23:42–43). Traditionally "Tabernacles". Also in verse 16. Exodus 23:16 calls it the Festival of Ingathering.
[^16-13]: Literally "and you will be only joyful".
[^16-14]: Literally "be seen at the face of Yahweh your God"; also later in the verse. The same consonants could be read "see the face of Yahweh your God".
[^16-15]: Literally "according to the gift of his hand".
[^16-16]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions.
[^16-17]: Literally "Do not recognize faces".
[^16-18]: Compare Exodus 23:8.
[^16-19]: Literally "Righteousness, righteousness you shall pursue". Hebrew *tsedeq*.
[^16-20]: Hebrew *asherah*: a wooden pole or tree that was a symbol of the goddess Asherah.
[^16-21]: Hebrew *matsevah*: a standing stone set up as an object of worship.

## Chapter 17

*Status: draft*

**1** Do not sacrifice to Yahweh[^17-1] your God an ox or a sheep that has a defect or anything wrong with it, for that is detestable[^17-2] to Yahweh your God.

**2** Suppose a man or a woman is found among you, in one of the towns[^17-3] that Yahweh your God is giving you, who does what is evil in the eyes of Yahweh your God by breaking his covenant.

**3** That person has gone and served other gods and bowed down to them, or to the sun or the moon or any of the host of heaven,[^17-4] which I have forbidden.[^17-5]

**4** If you are told about it and hear of it, you must investigate thoroughly. If it is true and certain that this detestable thing has been done in Israel,

**5** you must bring out to your town gates the man or the woman who has done this evil thing, the man or the woman, and stone that person to death.[^17-6]

**6** The one who is to die must be put to death on the testimony[^17-7] of two witnesses or three witnesses. No one is to be put to death on the testimony of only one witness.

**7** The hands of the witnesses must be the first against him to put him to death, and then the hands of all the people. So you must purge[^17-8] the evil from among you.

**8** If a case comes before you that is too hard for you to judge,[^17-9] whether it concerns bloodshed, a legal claim or an assault,[^17-10] or any dispute in your towns, then get up and go to the place that Yahweh your God will choose.

**9** Go to the Levitical priests[^17-11] and to the judge who is in office at that time. Inquire of them, and they will declare the verdict to you.

**10** You must act according to the verdict they declare to you at the place that Yahweh will choose. Be careful to do everything they instruct you.

**11** Act according to the instruction[^17-12] they give you and the ruling they tell you. Do not turn aside from the verdict they declare to you, either to the right or to the left.

**12** Anyone who acts presumptuously and will not listen to the priest who stands there to serve Yahweh your God, or to the judge, must die. So you must purge the evil from Israel.

**13** Then all the people will hear and be afraid, and they will not act presumptuously again.

**14** When you come into the land that Yahweh your God is giving you, and have taken possession of it and settled in it, you may say, "Let me set a king over me, like all the nations around me."

**15** Then be sure to set over you a king whom Yahweh your God will choose. You are to set over you a king from among your own people.[^17-13] You may not put a foreigner over you, one who is not your brother.

**16** But he must not acquire many horses for himself, or make the people go back to Egypt to acquire many horses, for Yahweh has said to you, "You must never go back that way again."

**17** He must not take many wives for himself, or his heart will turn away. And he must not acquire great amounts of silver and gold for himself.

**18** When he sits on the throne of his kingdom, he is to write for himself on a scroll a copy of this law,[^17-14] taken from the one kept by the Levitical priests.[^17-15]

**19** It is to be with him, and he is to read from it all the days of his life, so that he may learn to fear Yahweh his God by keeping all the words of this law and these statutes and doing them,

**20** so that his heart will not be lifted up above his brothers, and so that he will not turn aside from the commandment, to the right or to the left. Then he and his sons will reign long over his kingdom in Israel.

[^17-2]: Hebrew *to'evah*: something abhorrent, especially in worship. Also in verse 4.
[^17-3]: Literally "gates"; also in verses 5 and 8.
[^17-4]: Literally "all the host of the heavens", the heavenly bodies pictured as an army.
[^17-5]: Literally "which I have not commanded". Moses speaks in Yahweh's own words.
[^17-6]: Literally "and stone them with stones, and they shall die".
[^17-7]: Literally "at the mouth"; also later in the verse.
[^17-8]: Literally "burn out"; also in verse 12.
[^17-9]: Literally "If a matter is too wonderful for you for judgment".
[^17-10]: Literally "between blood and blood, between claim and claim, and between stroke and stroke". *Stroke* (Hebrew *nega*) can also mean a skin disease, as in 24:8.
[^17-11]: Literally "the priests, the Levites"; also in verse 18.
[^17-12]: Hebrew *torah*.
[^17-13]: Literally "from among your brothers".

## Chapter 18

*Status: draft*

**1** The Levitical priests,[^18-1] the whole tribe of Levi, are to have no share or inheritance with Israel. They are to eat the food offerings for Yahweh,[^18-2] which are his inheritance.[^18-3]

**2** They will have no inheritance among their brothers.[^18-4] Yahweh is their inheritance, as he promised them.

**3** This is what is due to the priests from the people, from those who offer a sacrifice, whether an ox or a sheep: they are to give the priest the shoulder, the two cheeks and the stomach.

**4** You are to give him the first of your grain, your new wine and your olive oil, and the first wool from the shearing of your sheep.

**5** For Yahweh your God has chosen him and his sons out of all your tribes to stand and serve in the name of Yahweh for all time.

**6** If a Levite comes from one of your towns anywhere in Israel where he is living,[^18-5] he may come whenever he wishes[^18-6] to the place that Yahweh will choose,

**7** and serve in the name of Yahweh his God like all his fellow Levites who stand there before Yahweh.

**8** They are to have equal portions to eat, besides what he receives from the sale of his family property.[^18-7]

**9** When you come into the land that Yahweh your God is giving you, do not learn to imitate the detestable practices[^18-8] of those nations.

**10** There must not be found among you anyone who makes his son or his daughter pass through the fire,[^18-9] or who practices divination, or tells fortunes, or interprets omens, or practices sorcery,[^18-10]

**11** or casts spells, or consults ghosts or spirits,[^18-11] or inquires of the dead.

**12** For anyone who does these things is detestable to Yahweh, and it is because of these detestable practices that Yahweh your God is driving those nations out before you.

**13** You must be blameless[^18-12] before Yahweh your God.

**14** These nations you are about to dispossess listen to fortune-tellers and diviners. But as for you, Yahweh your God has not allowed you to do so.

**15** Yahweh your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you, from your own people.[^18-13] You must listen to him.

**16** This is just what you asked of Yahweh your God at Horeb on the day of the assembly, when you said, "Let me not hear the voice of Yahweh my God again, or see this great fire any more, or I will die."

**17** And Yahweh said to me, "What they have said is good.

**18** I will raise up for them a prophet like you from among their own people. I will put my words in his mouth, and he will tell them everything I command him.

**19** If anyone does not listen to my words that he speaks in my name, I myself will hold him accountable.[^18-14]

**20** But a prophet who presumes to speak in my name a word I have not commanded him to speak, or who speaks in the name of other gods, that prophet must die."

**21** You may say to yourself,[^18-15] "How can we know which word Yahweh has not spoken?"

**22** If a prophet speaks in the name of Yahweh and the thing does not happen or come true, that is a word Yahweh has not spoken. The prophet has spoken it presumptuously. Do not be afraid of him.
