## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to God's chosen people who live as foreigners[^1-1] in the Dispersion,[^1-2] in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia,[^1-3]

**2** chosen according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through the Spirit's work of making you holy, to obey Jesus Christ and to be sprinkled with his blood:[^1-4] May grace and peace be yours in abundance.[^1-5]

**3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! In his great mercy he has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

**4** and into an inheritance that can never perish, spoil or fade. It is kept in heaven for you,

**5** who through faith are guarded by God's power until the coming of the salvation that is ready to be revealed in the last time.

**6** In this you rejoice,[^1-6] though now for a little while, if it must be, you have been grieved by trials of many kinds,

**7** so that the genuineness of your faith, more precious than gold, which perishes even though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise and glory and honor when Jesus Christ is revealed.

**8** Though you have not seen him,[^1-7] you love him. Though you do not see him now, you believe in him and rejoice with a joy that is beyond words and full of glory,

**9** for you are receiving the goal of your faith, the salvation of your souls.

**10** Concerning this salvation, the prophets who prophesied about the grace that was to be yours searched and inquired carefully.

**11** They tried to find out what person or what time[^1-8] the Spirit of Christ[^1-9] within them was pointing to when it testified in advance to the sufferings destined for Christ and the glories that would follow.

**12** It was revealed to them that they were serving not themselves but you in the things that have now been announced to you by those who brought you the good news through the Holy Spirit sent from heaven. These are things that angels long to look into.

**13** So prepare your minds for action,[^1-10] keep sober, and set your hope completely[^1-11] on the grace that will be brought to you when Jesus Christ is revealed.

**14** As obedient children,[^1-12] do not be shaped by the desires you had in the past, in your ignorance.

**15** But just as the one who called you is holy, you too must be holy in all your conduct,

**16** for it is written, "You shall be holy, because I am holy."[^1-13]

**17** If you call on as Father the one who judges each person's work impartially, live in reverent fear during the time of your stay as foreigners.[^1-14]

**18** For you know that it was not with perishable things like silver or gold that you were ransomed from the empty way of life handed down from your ancestors,

**19** but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without defect or blemish.[^1-15]

**20** He was foreknown before the foundation of the world, but has been revealed at the end of the times for your sake.

**21** Through him you believe in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that your faith and hope are in God.

**22** Now that you have purified your souls by obeying the truth,[^1-16] so as to have sincere love for one another as brothers and sisters,[^1-17] love one another earnestly from the heart.[^1-18]

**23** For you have been born anew, not from perishable seed but from imperishable, through the living and enduring word of God.[^1-19]

**24** For "All people are like grass,[^1-20] and all their glory[^1-21] like the flower of grass. The grass withers, and the flower falls,

**25** but the word of the Lord endures forever."[^1-22] And this is the word that was proclaimed to you as good news.

[^1-1]: Greek *parepidemoi*, people staying for a time in a land not their own (also 2:11; compare Genesis 23:4).
[^1-2]: Greek *diaspora*, "scattering", the usual term for the Jews living outside the land of Israel, here used of the believers (compare James 1:1).
[^1-3]: Roman provinces in Asia Minor, in what is now Turkey.
[^1-4]: Literally "for obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ". Compare Exodus 24:7–8, where the people promise to obey and are sprinkled with the blood of the covenant.
[^1-5]: Literally "Grace to you and peace be multiplied".
[^1-6]: Or "Rejoice in this", a command. *In this*: or "In him", God (verse 3) or Christ.
[^1-7]: Many manuscripts read "Though you have not known him".
[^1-8]: Or "what time or what kind of time".
[^1-9]: Greek *Christos*, "the Anointed One", "the Messiah"; also later in the verse.
[^1-10]: Literally "girding up the loins of your mind", tucking up the long robe into the belt so as to be ready for work or travel (Exodus 12:11).
[^1-11]: Or "keep fully sober, and set your hope".
[^1-12]: Literally "children of obedience".
[^1-13]: Leviticus 11:44–45; 19:2; 20:7.
[^1-14]: Greek *paroikia*, the time of living as resident foreigners (compare 2:11).
[^1-15]: The Passover lamb had to be "without any defect" (Exodus 12:5), as did animals offered in sacrifice (Leviticus 22:18–20). *Without defect*: Greek *amomos* (compare Hebrews 9:14).
[^1-16]: Many manuscripts add "through the Spirit".
[^1-17]: Greek *philadelphia*, "love for brothers (and sisters)", the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-18]: Many manuscripts read "from a pure heart".
[^1-19]: Or "through the word of the living and enduring God". Many manuscripts add "forever".
[^1-20]: Literally "all flesh". Isaiah 40:6–8, quoted in verses 24–25 from the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "All people are grass, and all their faithfulness is like the flower of the field", and repeats "The grass withers, the flower fades".
[^1-21]: Many manuscripts read "all human glory".
[^1-22]: Isaiah reads "the word of our God will stand forever". In verse 25 Peter applies "the word" to the good news.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** So get rid of all malice and all deceit, hypocrisy, envy and all slander.

**2** Like newborn babies, long for the pure spiritual milk,[^2-1] so that by it you may grow up into salvation,[^2-2]

**3** since you have tasted that the Lord is good.[^2-3]

**4** As you come to him, a living stone, rejected by people but chosen and precious in God's sight,

**5** you yourselves also, like living stones, are being built[^2-4] into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

**6** For it stands in scripture: "Look, I am laying in Zion a stone, a chosen and precious cornerstone, and whoever believes in him[^2-5] will never be put to shame."[^2-6]

**7** So for you who believe, he is precious.[^2-7] But for those who do not believe,[^2-8] "The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone,"[^2-9]

**8** and, "A stone that makes people stumble and a rock that makes them fall."[^2-10] They stumble because they disobey the word, as they were destined to do.[^2-11]

**9** But you are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's own possession,[^2-12] so that you may proclaim the excellent deeds[^2-13] of the one who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.

**10** Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people. Once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy.[^2-14]

**11** Dear friends,[^2-15] I urge you, as foreigners and resident aliens,[^2-16] to abstain from the desires of the flesh,[^2-17] which wage war against the soul.

**12** Keep your conduct among the Gentiles good, so that, though they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good works and glorify God on the day he comes.[^2-18]

**13** For the Lord's sake submit to every human authority,[^2-19] whether to the emperor[^2-20] as supreme,

**14** or to governors, as those sent by him to punish those who do wrong and to praise those who do right.

**15** (For it is God's will that by doing good you should silence the ignorance of foolish people.)

**16** Live as free people, but do not use your freedom as a cover for evil; live as servants[^2-21] of God.

**17** Honor everyone. Love the family of believers.[^2-22] Fear God. Honor the emperor.

**18** Household slaves,[^2-23] submit to your masters with all respect,[^2-24] not only to those who are good and gentle but also to those who are harsh.

**19** For it is commendable[^2-25] if, because they are mindful of God, someone endures the pain of suffering unjustly.

**20** For what credit is it if you endure when you are beaten for doing wrong? But if you endure when you suffer for doing good, this is commendable before God.

**21** This is what you were called to, because Christ also suffered for you,[^2-26] leaving you an example,[^2-27] so that you should follow in his steps.

**22** He committed no sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth.[^2-28]

**23** When he was insulted, he did not insult in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but entrusted himself[^2-29] to the one who judges justly.

**24** He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree,[^2-30] so that we might die to sins[^2-31] and live for righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed.[^2-32]

**25** For you were going astray like sheep,[^2-33] but now you have returned to the shepherd and overseer[^2-34] of your souls.

[^2-1]: Greek *logikos*, "reasonable, spiritual", perhaps also "of the word" (*logos*, 1:23). *Pure*: Greek *adolos*, "without deceit", in contrast to "deceit" (*dolos*) in verse 1.
[^2-2]: Many manuscripts leave out "into salvation".
[^2-3]: Compare Psalm 34:9 (34:8 in most English Bibles), "Taste and see that Yahweh is good." In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^2-4]: Or "let yourselves be built", a command.
[^2-5]: Or "in it", the stone.
[^2-6]: Isaiah 28:16, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "a stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a firm foundation. Whoever trusts will not panic."
[^2-7]: Or "So the honor is yours who believe". Greek *time*, "honor, preciousness", from the same root as "precious" (*entimos*) in verses 4 and 6.
[^2-8]: Many manuscripts read "for those who disobey".
[^2-9]: Psalm 118:22 (see Mark 12:10). Literally "the head of the corner".
[^2-10]: Isaiah 8:14.
[^2-11]: Literally "to which they were also appointed". *Because they disobey the word*: or "they stumble at the word, because they disobey".
[^2-12]: Isaiah 43:20–21 and Exodus 19:5–6, in the wording of the Septuagint. The Hebrew of Exodus 19:6 reads "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation", and of 19:5 "my own treasured possession".
[^2-13]: Greek *aretai*, "excellences, praises", the Septuagint's word in Isaiah 43:21, where the Hebrew reads "so that they may declare my praise".
[^2-14]: Hosea 1:6, 9; 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles), where Hosea's children are named Lo-ammi, "not my people", and Lo-ruhamah, "not shown compassion".
[^2-15]: Literally "Beloved".
[^2-16]: Greek *paroikoi kai parepidemoi*, the words of Genesis 23:4 in the Septuagint, where Abraham calls himself "a foreigner and a resident alien".
[^2-17]: Literally "fleshly desires": the desires of human nature in its weakness.
[^2-18]: Literally "on the day of visitation", the day when God comes, in judgment or in mercy (compare Isaiah 10:3).
[^2-19]: Literally "every human creation": perhaps every human institution, or every human being.
[^2-20]: Literally "the king", the title used in the eastern provinces for the Roman emperor; also in verse 17.
[^2-21]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^2-22]: Literally "the brotherhood".
[^2-23]: Greek *oiketai*, slaves who served in a household.
[^2-24]: Literally "in all fear", perhaps the fear of God (verse 17).
[^2-25]: Literally "this is grace", what wins favor with God; also in verse 20.
[^2-26]: Many manuscripts read "for us".
[^2-27]: Greek *hypogrammos*, a pattern of letters for a child to trace in learning to write.
[^2-28]: Isaiah 53:9. The Hebrew reads "although he had done no violence, and there was no deceit in his mouth". Verses 22–25 echo Isaiah 53:4–12.
[^2-29]: Literally "he handed over", perhaps himself or his cause. Compare Isaiah 53:7.
[^2-30]: Literally "the wood", the cross (compare Deuteronomy 21:22–23; Acts 5:30). *Bore our sins*: compare Isaiah 53:11–12, "he will bear their wrongdoings … he bore the sin of many".
[^2-31]: Greek *apoginomai*, "be away from, have no part in", here "die to".
[^2-32]: Isaiah 53:5, "by his wounds we are healed".
[^2-33]: Isaiah 53:6, "We all, like sheep, have gone astray".
[^2-34]: Greek *episkopos*, "overseer, guardian".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** In the same way, wives, submit to your own husbands, so that even if some of them do not obey the word, they may be won over without a word by the way their wives live,

**2** when they see your pure and reverent conduct.[^3-1]

**3** Do not let your beauty be the outward kind, such as braided hair, gold jewelry or fine clothes.[^3-2]

**4** Instead, let it be the inner person of the heart, with the lasting beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is very precious in God's sight.

**5** For this is how the holy women of the past who put their hope in God used to make themselves beautiful. They submitted to their own husbands,

**6** as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord.[^3-3] You have become her daughters if you do what is right and do not give way to fear.[^3-4]

**7** Husbands, in the same way, live with your wives with understanding, showing them honor as the weaker partner[^3-5] and as heirs with you of the gracious gift of life, so that your prayers will not be hindered.

**8** Finally, all of you, be of one mind, sympathetic, loving one another as brothers and sisters, compassionate and humble.[^3-6]

**9** Do not repay evil for evil or insult for insult, but on the contrary, bless, for you were called to this, so that you may inherit a blessing.

**10** For "Whoever wants to love life and see good days must keep their tongue from evil and their lips from speaking deceit.[^3-7]

**11** They must turn away from evil and do good; they must seek peace and pursue it.

**12** For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer. But the face of the Lord is against those who do evil."[^3-8]

**13** Who is going to harm you if you are eager to do good?[^3-9]

**14** But even if you should suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear,[^3-10] and do not be troubled,

**15** but in your hearts regard Christ the Lord as holy.[^3-11] Always be ready to give an answer to anyone who asks you for the reason for the hope that is in you,

**16** yet do it with gentleness and respect,[^3-12] keeping a good conscience, so that when you are slandered,[^3-13] those who abuse your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.

**17** For it is better, if it is God's will, to suffer for doing good than for doing evil.

**18** For Christ also suffered[^3-14] for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you[^3-15] to God. He was put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit,[^3-16]

**19** in which[^3-17] he also went and made a proclamation to the spirits in prison,[^3-18]

**20** who had once disobeyed, when God waited patiently in the days of Noah while the ark was being built. In it a few people, eight in all,[^3-19] were brought safely through the water.[^3-20]

**21** And baptism, which corresponds to this,[^3-21] now saves you[^3-22] also, not as a removal of dirt from the body, but as the pledge of a good conscience toward God,[^3-23] through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

**22** who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God,[^3-24] with angels, authorities and powers made subject to him.

[^3-1]: Literally "your pure conduct in fear", perhaps the fear of God.
[^3-2]: Literally "Let yours be not the outward adorning of braiding hair, putting on gold or wearing clothes."
[^3-3]: Compare Genesis 18:12, where Sarah calls Abraham "my lord".
[^3-4]: Literally "and do not fear any terror". Compare Proverbs 3:25, "Do not be afraid of sudden terror".
[^3-5]: Literally "as with the weaker vessel, the female". Greek *skeuos*, "vessel, container", is used of the body or the person.
[^3-6]: Many manuscripts read "courteous".
[^3-7]: Psalm 34:13–17 (34:12–16 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 10–12 from the Septuagint, with the psalm's question ("Who is the one who delights in life …?") turned into a statement and its commands into the third person.
[^3-8]: In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh. Peter leaves out the end of the psalm verse, "to cut off the memory of them from the earth".
[^3-9]: Many manuscripts read "if you imitate what is good".
[^3-10]: Or "Do not be afraid of them", literally "do not fear their fear". Verses 14–15 echo Isaiah 8:12–13, in a form close to the Septuagint.
[^3-11]: Or "regard Christ as Lord and as holy". Isaiah 8:13 reads "Yahweh of Armies: he is the one you must regard as holy." Many manuscripts read "regard the Lord God as holy".
[^3-12]: Literally "fear", perhaps the fear of God (compare 2:17–18).
[^3-13]: Many manuscripts read "when they speak against you as evildoers" (compare 2:12).
[^3-14]: Some manuscripts read "died".
[^3-15]: Some manuscripts read "us".
[^3-16]: Or "by the Spirit". The contrast may be between two spheres of existence, the human and the spiritual, or between Jesus' body and the Spirit by whom he was raised.
[^3-17]: Or "in whom", that is, in the Spirit; or "and so".
[^3-18]: *Made a proclamation*: Greek *kerysso*, "proclaim, preach", which does not say what was proclaimed. This passage is one of the most difficult in the New Testament, and there are three main readings of it. (1) After his death or resurrection, Christ went and announced his victory, or God's judgment, to rebellious spirits held in prison since the days of Noah: the "sons of God" of Genesis 6:1–4, whom later Jewish writings (such as 1 Enoch) describe as fallen angels kept for judgment (compare 2 Peter 2:4–5; Jude 6). On this view he may have gone as he ascended (the same verb "went" is used in verse 22). (2) Between his death and resurrection, Christ went to the realm of the dead and preached to the spirits of those who had died, including those who perished in the flood (compare 4:6). This reading is linked with the creeds' words "he descended to the dead (or into hell)". (3) Christ, in the Spirit, preached through Noah (compare 1:11; 2 Peter 2:5) to the people of Noah's day, who refused to listen and are now spirits in prison.
[^3-19]: Literally "eight souls": Noah, his wife, his three sons and their wives (Genesis 7:13).
[^3-20]: Or "by means of water".
[^3-21]: Literally "which, as an antitype, now also saves you, baptism". The water of the flood corresponds to the water of baptism.
[^3-22]: Many manuscripts read "us".
[^3-23]: Greek *eperotema*, "request, appeal", or "pledge, answer" (in legal use, the answer given to a formal question). It may mean an appeal to God for a good conscience, or the pledge to God that comes from a good conscience.
[^3-24]: Compare Psalm 110:1.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Since Christ suffered in the flesh,[^4-1] arm yourselves also with the same way of thinking, because whoever has suffered in the flesh has finished with sin,[^4-2]

**2** so as to live the rest of your time in the flesh no longer for human desires but for the will of God.

**3** For you have spent enough time in the past doing what the Gentiles like to do, living in sensuality, lust, drunkenness, carousing, drinking parties and lawless idolatry.

**4** They are surprised that you no longer join them in the same flood of wild living, and they slander you.[^4-3]

**5** But they will give an account to the one who is ready to judge the living and the dead.

**6** This is why the good news was proclaimed even to the dead,[^4-4] so that, though they were judged in the flesh as human beings are, they might live in the spirit as God does.[^4-5]

**7** The end of all things is near. So be self-controlled and sober-minded for the sake of your prayers.

**8** Above all, keep your love for one another constant, because love covers a multitude of sins.[^4-6]

**9** Be hospitable to one another without complaining.

**10** As each of you has received a gift,[^4-7] use it to serve one another, as good stewards of the grace of God in its many forms.

**11** Whoever speaks should do so as one speaking the very words of God.[^4-8] Whoever serves should do so with the strength God supplies, so that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ. To him belong glory and power forever and ever.[^4-9] Amen.

**12** Dear friends,[^4-10] do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that has come upon you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you.

**13** But rejoice insofar as you share in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may also rejoice and be glad when his glory is revealed.

**14** If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory, the Spirit of God, rests on you.[^4-11]

**15** None of you should suffer as a murderer, a thief, a criminal or a meddler.[^4-12]

**16** But if anyone suffers as a Christian,[^4-13] they should not be ashamed but should glorify God in this name.[^4-14]

**17** For it is time for judgment to begin with the household of God.[^4-15] And if it begins with us, what will be the end of those who do not obey the good news of God?

**18** And if the righteous is scarcely saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?[^4-16]

**19** So then, those who suffer according to God's will should entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while continuing to do good.

[^4-1]: Many manuscripts add "for us".
[^4-2]: Or "has ceased from sin". The meaning is debated: whoever suffers in this way has broken with sin; or Christ, having suffered in the flesh, is done with sin.
[^4-3]: Or "and they blaspheme".
[^4-4]: *The dead* may be those who heard the good news while they were alive and have since died, or the dead in the realm of the dead (compare 3:19).
[^4-5]: Literally "according to human beings … according to God". *In the spirit*: or "by the Spirit" (compare 3:18).
[^4-6]: Compare Proverbs 10:12, "love covers all offenses", and James 5:20.
[^4-7]: Greek *charisma*, a gift of God's grace (*charis*).
[^4-8]: Literally "as oracles of God".
[^4-9]: *To him* may refer to God or to Jesus Christ.
[^4-10]: Literally "Beloved".
[^4-11]: Literally "the (Spirit) of glory and the Spirit of God". Compare Isaiah 11:2, "The Spirit of Yahweh will rest on him". Many manuscripts add "On their part he is blasphemed, but on your part he is glorified."
[^4-12]: Greek *allotriepiskopos*, a rare word, perhaps "one who meddles in other people's affairs", or "one who misuses what belongs to others".
[^4-13]: Greek *Christianos*, "a follower of Christ", a name first given to the disciples by others (Acts 11:26; 26:28).
[^4-14]: Many manuscripts read "in this matter".
[^4-15]: Compare Ezekiel 9:6, "Begin at my sanctuary".
[^4-16]: Proverbs 11:31, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "If the righteous are repaid on earth, how much more the wicked and the sinner!"

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** So I appeal to the elders among you, as a fellow elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and one who also shares in the glory that is going to be revealed:

**2** Shepherd God's flock that is among you, watching over it[^5-1] not because you must but willingly, as God wants;[^5-2] not for shameful gain but eagerly;

**3** not lording it over those entrusted to you,[^5-3] but being examples to the flock.

**4** And when the chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the crown of glory that will never fade.

**5** In the same way, you who are younger, submit to the elders.[^5-4] All of you, clothe yourselves with humility toward one another,[^5-5] because "God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble."[^5-6]

**6** So humble yourselves under God's mighty hand, so that he may lift you up at the right time,

**7** casting all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you.[^5-7]

**8** Be sober-minded; be watchful. Your adversary[^5-8] the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour.

**9** Resist him, firm in your faith, knowing that the same kinds of suffering are being laid on your family of believers[^5-9] throughout the world.

**10** And after you have suffered a little while, the God of all grace, who called you to his eternal glory in Christ, will himself restore you, strengthen you, make you firm and establish you.[^5-10]

**11** To him be the power[^5-11] forever and ever.[^5-12] Amen.

**12** Through Silvanus, whom I regard as a faithful brother, I have written to you briefly,[^5-13] to encourage you and to testify that this is the true grace of God. Stand firm in it.

**13** She who is in Babylon,[^5-14] chosen together with you, sends you greetings, and so does Mark, my son.[^5-15]

**14** Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace to all of you who are in Christ.[^5-16]

[^5-1]: Greek *episkopeo*, "watch over, oversee", the verb related to *episkopos*, "overseer" (2:25). Some manuscripts leave it out.
[^5-2]: Literally "according to God". Many manuscripts leave out these words.
[^5-3]: Literally "the lots", the people allotted to your care.
[^5-4]: Or "the older men", in contrast to "the younger".
[^5-5]: Greek *enkombomai*, "tie on", as a servant ties on an apron. Many manuscripts read "All of you, submitting to one another, clothe yourselves with humility".
[^5-6]: Proverbs 3:34, as in the Septuagint (see James 4:6). The Hebrew reads "He mocks the mockers, but he gives favor to the humble."
[^5-7]: Compare Psalm 55:23 (55:22 in most English Bibles), "Cast your burden on Yahweh", where the Septuagint reads "your care".
[^5-8]: Greek *antidikos*, an opponent in a lawsuit, an accuser.
[^5-9]: Literally "the brotherhood".
[^5-10]: Some manuscripts leave out "establish you".
[^5-11]: Many manuscripts read "the glory and the power".
[^5-12]: Some manuscripts read "forever".
[^5-13]: Silvanus (Silas) may have carried the letter, or helped to write it (compare Acts 15:22, 27; 2 Corinthians 1:19; 1 Thessalonians 1:1).
[^5-14]: Probably the church in the city from which the letter was written. *Babylon* was used as a name for Rome (compare Revelation 17–18); some take it as the city in Mesopotamia.
[^5-15]: Probably John Mark (Acts 12:12), a "son" in the faith.
[^5-16]: Many manuscripts read "in Christ Jesus. Amen."
