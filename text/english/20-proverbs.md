## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The proverbs[^1-1] of Solomon son of David, king of Israel,

**2** for learning wisdom[^1-2] and discipline,[^1-3] for grasping words of understanding,[^1-4]

**3** for receiving discipline in wise conduct, in righteousness, justice and fairness,

**4** for giving prudence[^1-5] to the simple,[^1-6] knowledge[^1-7] and discretion[^1-8] to the young.

**5** Let the wise listen and add to their learning; let the discerning gain guidance,[^1-9]

**6** for understanding a proverb and a figure of speech,[^1-10] the words of the wise and their riddles.

**7** The fear of Yahweh[^1-11] is the beginning[^1-12] of knowledge; fools[^1-13] despise wisdom and discipline.

**8** Listen, my son,[^1-14] to your father's discipline, and do not abandon your mother's instruction.[^1-15]

**9** For they are a graceful garland for your head and a necklace for your neck.

**10** My son, if sinners entice you, do not give in.

**11** If they say, "Come with us; let us lie in wait to shed blood;[^1-16] let us ambush the innocent for no reason;

**12** let us swallow them alive like Sheol,[^1-17] whole, like those who go down to the Pit;[^1-18]

**13** we will find all kinds of precious wealth; we will fill our houses with plunder.

**14** Throw in your lot with us; we will all have one purse,"

**15** my son, do not walk on the road with them; keep your foot from their path.

**16** For their feet run to evil, and they hurry to shed blood.[^1-19]

**17** For it is useless to spread a net in the sight of any bird.[^1-20]

**18** But these men lie in wait for their own blood; they ambush their own lives.

**19** Such are the ways of everyone who is greedy for gain: it takes away the life of those who possess it.

**20** Wisdom[^1-21] cries aloud in the street; in the squares she raises her voice.

**21** At the head of the bustling streets she calls out; at the entrances of the city gates she speaks her words:

**22** "How long, you simple ones, will you love being simple? How long will mockers[^1-22] delight in their mocking, and fools[^1-23] hate knowledge?

**23** Turn back at my correction![^1-24] Look, I will pour out my spirit[^1-25] to you; I will make my words known to you.

**24** Because I called and you refused, because I stretched out my hand and no one paid attention,

**25** because you ignored all my advice and would have none of my correction,

**26** I in turn will laugh at your disaster; I will mock when what you dread comes,

**27** when what you dread comes like a storm, and your disaster sweeps in like a whirlwind, when distress and anguish come upon you.

**28** Then they will call to me, but I will not answer; they will search hard for me, but they will not find me.

**29** Because they hated knowledge and did not choose the fear of Yahweh,

**30** because they would have none of my advice and spurned all my correction,

**31** they will eat the fruit of their own way and be stuffed full of their own schemes.

**32** For the waywardness of the simple will kill them, and the complacency of fools will destroy them.

**33** But whoever listens to me will live in safety and be at ease, with no dread of harm."

[^1-1]: Hebrew *mishle*, the plural of *mashal*: a proverb or short saying, often one that makes a comparison. *Mishle* is the book's name in Hebrew. Verses 1–7 introduce the whole book.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *chokhmah*: skill in living, wisdom that is both practical and moral. It is the key word of the book.
[^1-3]: Hebrew *musar*: training that forms character, through teaching, correction and, where needed, punishment. Traditionally "instruction". This translation uses "discipline" throughout. Also in verses 3, 7 and 8.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *binah*: the ability to discern, to see what matters and tell things apart. *Grasping* translates a verb from the same root.
[^1-5]: Hebrew *ormah*: shrewdness, good judgment. The related word *arum* describes the "crafty" snake in Genesis 3:1, but in Proverbs these words name a good quality.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *peta'im*, plural of *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led either way. Also in verses 22 and 32.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *da'at*: knowledge gained by learning and experience, especially knowledge of God and of how to live. Also in verses 7, 22 and 29.
[^1-8]: Hebrew *mezimmah*: the ability to think things through and make plans. The same word can mean a scheme in a bad sense (12:2; 14:17).
[^1-9]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, perhaps connected with steering a ship by its ropes: the skill of finding the right course.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *melitsah*, a rare word: perhaps an allusive saying or a figure whose meaning must be worked out (compare Habakkuk 2:6).
[^1-11]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The fear of Yahweh* is reverence for him that shows itself in obedience. It is a theme of the whole book (9:10; 15:33; 31:30).
[^1-12]: Or "the first principle", "the chief part". Hebrew *reshit*.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *ewilim*, plural of *ewil*. Proverbs uses several words for "fool". The *ewil* is morally perverse, stubborn and quick to quarrel. The *kesil* (verse 22), the most common word, is dull and self-satisfied, closed to learning. Both are translated "fool", and a note gives the Hebrew word at its first occurrence in each chapter.
[^1-14]: The teacher addresses his pupil as "my son", as a father would his son. The address recurs throughout chapters 1–7 and often later.
[^1-15]: Hebrew *torah*, "teaching, instruction".
[^1-16]: Literally "for blood".
[^1-17]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^1-18]: Hebrew *bor*, "pit, cistern", here the grave or the realm of the dead.
[^1-19]: Isaiah 59:7 has nearly the same words; Romans 3:15 quotes them.
[^1-20]: Literally "in the eyes of every owner of a wing". The point of the comparison is uncertain. Perhaps a bird that sees a trap is not caught by it, yet these men do not see the trap they are setting for themselves (verse 18). Or the word translated *useless* (Hebrew *chinnam*) means "for no reason", as in verse 11.
[^1-21]: Here wisdom is pictured as a woman who calls out in public, as also in 8:1–36 and 9:1–6. The Hebrew form, *chokhmot*, is unusual; it may be a plural that stresses her greatness (also 9:1).
[^1-22]: Hebrew *letsim*, plural of *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction. Proverbs treats the mocker as beyond teaching (9:7–8; 13:1).
[^1-23]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil*, the most common word for "fool" in Proverbs: someone dull and self-satisfied who does not want to learn. Also in verse 32. Compare the *ewil* in verse 7.
[^1-24]: Or "If you turn back at my correction, I will pour out …". Hebrew *tokhachat*, "correction, rebuke". Also in verses 25 and 30.
[^1-25]: Or "my thoughts". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, breath, mind". Literally "I will make my spirit gush out to you".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** My son, if you accept my words and store up my commands within you,

**2** turning your ear to wisdom and inclining your heart to insight,[^2-1]

**3** if you call out for understanding[^2-2] and raise your voice for insight,

**4** if you look for it as for silver and search for it as for hidden treasure,

**5** then you will understand the fear of Yahweh[^2-3] and find the knowledge[^2-4] of God.

**6** For Yahweh gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and insight.

**7** He stores up[^2-5] sound wisdom[^2-6] for the upright; he is a shield to those who walk in integrity,

**8** guarding the paths of justice and watching over the way of his faithful ones.[^2-7]

**9** Then you will understand righteousness and justice and fairness, every good path.

**10** For wisdom will come into your heart, and knowledge will be pleasant to your soul.

**11** Discretion[^2-8] will watch over you; insight will guard you,

**12** to rescue you from the way of evil, from men who speak perversely,

**13** who abandon the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness,

**14** who delight in doing evil and rejoice in the perversity of evil,

**15** whose paths are crooked and whose ways are devious;

**16** to rescue you from the strange woman,[^2-9] from the foreign woman[^2-10] with her smooth words,

**17** who abandons the companion of her youth[^2-11] and forgets the covenant of her God.[^2-12]

**18** For her house sinks down to death,[^2-13] and her paths lead to the dead.[^2-14]

**19** None who go to her come back, and they do not reach the paths of life.

**20** So you will walk in the way of good people and keep to the paths of the righteous.[^2-15]

**21** For the upright will live in the land,[^2-16] and the blameless will remain in it,

**22** but the wicked[^2-17] will be cut off from the land, and the treacherous will be torn out of it.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *tevunah*, from the same root as *binah*, "understanding" (verse 3). The two words are often paired; this translation uses "insight" for *tevunah* and "understanding" for *binah*. Also in verses 3, 6 and 11.
[^2-2]: Hebrew *binah*: the ability to discern, to see what matters and tell things apart.
[^2-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. On *the fear of Yahweh*, see 1:7.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *da'at*: knowledge gained by learning and experience. *The knowledge of God* means knowing him personally, not only knowing about him.
[^2-5]: The Hebrew text is written "And he has stored up" but read "He stores up".
[^2-6]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*: sound wisdom, and the success it brings.
[^2-7]: The Hebrew text is written "his faithful one" but read "his faithful ones". Hebrew *chasidim*, those who are loyal to him, a word related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^2-8]: Hebrew *mezimmah*: the ability to think things through and plan.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *ishah zarah*. *Zar* means "strange, outside, not one's own": someone outside one's family or circle. In chapters 2–7 the *strange woman* is a woman outside a man's marriage, usually another man's wife (verse 17; 6:26; 7:19). Other renderings are "the forbidden woman" and "the adulteress".
[^2-10]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman". The word can mean a woman from another people, or simply one who is a stranger to a man's household. It stands in parallel with *ishah zarah* in 5:20, 6:24 and 7:5.
[^2-11]: That is, her husband.
[^2-12]: Probably her marriage covenant, made before God (compare Malachi 2:14).
[^2-13]: Literally "For she sinks down to death, her house", or "For her house sinks down to death". The grammar of the Hebrew is unusual.
[^2-14]: Hebrew *rephaim*, the spirits of the dead (also 9:18; 21:16).
[^2-15]: Hebrew *tsaddiqim*, plural of *tsaddiq*: a righteous person, one who does what is right toward God and other people. The opposite is *rasha*, "wicked" (verse 22).
[^2-16]: Or "the earth". Also in verse 22.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: a wicked person, one who is guilty of wrong.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** My son, do not forget my instruction, and let your heart keep my commands,

**2** for they will add to your life many days and years, and peace.[^3-1]

**3** Do not let faithful love and truth[^3-2] leave you. Tie them around your neck; write them on the tablet of your heart.

**4** Then you will find favor and a good name[^3-3] in the sight of God and of people.

**5** Trust in Yahweh[^3-4] with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding.[^3-5]

**6** In all your ways acknowledge him,[^3-6] and he will make your paths straight.

**7** Do not be wise in your own eyes; fear Yahweh and turn away from evil.

**8** It will be healing for your body[^3-7] and refreshment for your bones.

**9** Honor Yahweh with your wealth and with the firstfruits of all your harvest.

**10** Then your barns will be filled with plenty, and your vats will overflow with new wine.

**11** My son, do not reject the discipline[^3-8] of Yahweh, and do not resent his correction,

**12** for Yahweh corrects the one he loves, as a father corrects the son he delights in.[^3-9]

**13** Happy[^3-10] is the one who finds wisdom, the one who gains insight.[^3-11]

**14** For she is more profitable than silver, and her yield is better than fine gold.

**15** She is more precious than coral;[^3-12] nothing you desire can compare with her.

**16** Long life is in her right hand; in her left hand are riches and honor.

**17** Her ways are pleasant ways, and all her paths are peace.

**18** She is a tree of life[^3-13] to those who take hold of her; those who hold on to her are happy.

**19** By wisdom Yahweh founded the earth; by insight he established the heavens.

**20** By his knowledge the deep waters burst open, and the clouds drop down dew.

**21** My son, do not let them out of your sight:[^3-14] keep sound wisdom[^3-15] and discretion,[^3-16]

**22** and they will be life for your soul and a lovely ornament for your neck.[^3-17]

**23** Then you will walk on your way in safety, and your foot will not stumble.

**24** When you lie down, you will not be afraid; when you lie down, your sleep will be sweet.

**25** Do not be afraid of sudden terror, or of the ruin of the wicked when it comes.

**26** For Yahweh will be your confidence[^3-18] and will keep your foot from being caught.

**27** Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to do it.

**28** Do not say to your neighbor,[^3-19] "Go away and come back; I will give it to you tomorrow," when you have it with you.

**29** Do not plot harm against your neighbor while he lives trustingly beside you.

**30** Do not quarrel with anyone for no reason, when he has done you no harm.

**31** Do not envy a violent man, and do not choose any of his ways.

**32** For the devious are detestable[^3-20] to Yahweh, but he takes the upright into his confidence.

**33** Yahweh's curse is on the house of the wicked, but he blesses the home of the righteous.

**34** He mocks the mockers,[^3-21] but he gives favor to the humble.[^3-22]

**35** The wise will inherit honor, but fools[^3-23] carry off disgrace.[^3-24]

[^3-1]: Literally "length of days and years of life and peace (*shalom*) they will add to you".
[^3-2]: Hebrew *chesed ve-emet*. *Chesed* is loyal, faithful love; *emet* means both "truth" and "faithfulness, reliability".
[^3-3]: Hebrew *sekhel tov*, literally "good sense", here perhaps the good name that good sense earns.
[^3-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *binah*, as in 1:2.
[^3-6]: Literally "know him".
[^3-7]: Literally "your navel". The Septuagint reads "your body".
[^3-8]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Verses 11–12 are quoted in Hebrews 12:5–6, following the Septuagint, which reads the last line of verse 12 as "and he punishes every son he accepts".
[^3-9]: Or "even as a father the son he delights in".
[^3-10]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed". *Happy* in verse 18 translates a verb from the same root.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *tevunah*, from the same root as *binah*, "understanding" (see 2:2). Also in verse 19.
[^3-12]: Hebrew *peninim*, probably red coral or pearls; traditionally "rubies".
[^3-13]: Compare Genesis 2:9; 3:22. Also in 11:30; 13:12; 15:4.
[^3-14]: Literally "do not let them slip from your eyes". *Them* are the things named in the next line.
[^3-15]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, as in 2:7.
[^3-16]: Hebrew *mezimmah*, as in 1:4.
[^3-17]: Literally "and grace for your neck", as in 1:9.
[^3-18]: Or "at your side". Hebrew *kesel* can mean "confidence" or "side, loins".
[^3-19]: The Hebrew text is written "to your neighbors" but read "to your neighbor".
[^3-20]: Hebrew *to'evah*, something loathsome, traditionally "an abomination". The phrase "detestable to Yahweh" is frequent in Proverbs.
[^3-21]: Hebrew *letsim*, as in 1:22: those who scorn wisdom and laugh at correction. The verb translated *mocks* is from the same root.
[^3-22]: The Hebrew text is written "to the poor" but read "to the humble". James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 quote this verse from the Septuagint: "God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble."
[^3-23]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil* (see 1:22).
[^3-24]: Literally "but fools lift up disgrace". The sense may be that disgrace is the only honor fools receive.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Listen, sons, to a father's discipline,[^4-1] and pay attention so that you may know understanding.[^4-2]

**2** For I am giving you good teaching; do not abandon my instruction.

**3** When I was a son to my father, tender, the only child in my mother's eyes,

**4** he taught me and said to me, "Let your heart hold on to my words; keep my commands, and live.[^4-3]

**5** Get wisdom, get understanding; do not forget the words of my mouth, and do not turn away from them.

**6** Do not abandon her, and she will guard you; love her, and she will watch over you.

**7** The beginning of wisdom is this: get wisdom. Whatever else you get, get understanding.[^4-4]

**8** Prize her, and she will lift you up; she will bring you honor if you embrace her.

**9** She will place a graceful garland on your head; she will present you with a beautiful crown."

**10** Listen, my son, and accept my words, and the years of your life will be many.

**11** I have taught you in the way of wisdom; I have led you along straight paths.

**12** When you walk, your steps will not be hampered, and if you run, you will not stumble.

**13** Hold on to discipline; do not let it go. Guard her,[^4-5] for she is your life.

**14** Do not set foot on the path of the wicked,[^4-6] and do not walk in the way of evildoers.

**15** Avoid it; do not travel on it. Turn away from it and pass on.

**16** For they cannot sleep unless they have done wrong; they are robbed of sleep unless they have made someone stumble.[^4-7]

**17** For they eat the bread of wickedness and drink the wine of violence.

**18** But the path of the righteous[^4-8] is like the light of dawn, shining brighter and brighter until full day.[^4-9]

**19** The way of the wicked is like deep darkness; they do not know what makes them stumble.

**20** My son, pay attention to my words; turn your ear to my sayings.

**21** Do not let them slip from your sight; keep them deep within your heart.

**22** For they are life to those who find them, and healing for their whole body.

**23** Above all else that you guard, guard your heart,[^4-10] for from it flow the springs of life.

**24** Put away from you a crooked mouth, and keep devious lips far from you.

**25** Let your eyes look straight ahead, and your gaze be fixed straight in front of you.

**26** Make level the path for your feet,[^4-11] and all your ways will be secure.

**27** Do not turn to the right or to the left; keep your foot away from evil.[^4-12]

[^4-1]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verse 13.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *binah*, as in 1:2. Also in verses 5 and 7.
[^4-3]: The father's quotation of his own father may end here, or at the end of verse 5 or of verse 9.
[^4-4]: Or "Wisdom is the first thing: get wisdom, and with all you have, get understanding". Literally "and with all your getting". The Septuagint does not have this verse.
[^4-5]: The pronoun is feminine, while the Hebrew word for *discipline* is masculine. *Her* is probably wisdom, as in verses 6–9.
[^4-6]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: a wicked person, one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22). Also in verse 19.
[^4-7]: The Hebrew text is written "unless they stumble" but read "unless they make someone stumble".
[^4-8]: Hebrew *tsaddiqim*, plural of *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^4-9]: Literally "until the day is established", perhaps noon.
[^4-10]: Or "With all watchfulness guard your heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the center of thought and will as well as feeling.
[^4-11]: Or "Watch the path of your feet". Hebrews 12:13 echoes this line in the wording of the Septuagint: "make straight paths for your feet".
[^4-12]: The Septuagint adds: "For God knows the ways on the right, but those on the left are crooked. He will make your paths straight and lead your journeys in peace."

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** My son, pay attention to my wisdom; turn your ear to my insight,[^5-1]

**2** so that you may keep discretion,[^5-2] and your lips may guard knowledge.

**3** For the lips of the strange woman[^5-3] drip honey, and her speech[^5-4] is smoother than oil.

**4** But in the end she is as bitter as wormwood,[^5-5] as sharp as a two-edged sword.

**5** Her feet go down to death; her steps lead straight to Sheol.[^5-6]

**6** She gives no thought to the path of life; her tracks wander, and she does not know it.[^5-7]

**7** So now, sons, listen to me, and do not turn away from the words of my mouth.

**8** Keep your way far from her, and do not go near the door of her house,

**9** or you will give your vigor to others and your years to someone cruel,

**10** or strangers will feast on your strength, and what you have toiled for will go to the house of a foreigner.

**11** At the end of your life you will groan, when your flesh and your body are worn out,

**12** and you will say, "How I hated discipline![^5-8] How my heart spurned correction!

**13** I did not listen to the voice of my teachers or turn my ear to my instructors.

**14** I have almost come to utter ruin[^5-9] in the middle of the assembly and the community."

**15** Drink water from your own cistern, flowing water from your own well.

**16** Should your springs overflow into the street, streams of water in the public squares?[^5-10]

**17** Let them be yours alone, not shared with strangers.

**18** Let your fountain be blessed, and rejoice in the wife of your youth,

**19** a loving doe, a graceful deer. Let her breasts satisfy you at all times; be intoxicated[^5-11] always with her love.

**20** Why should you be intoxicated, my son, with a strange woman, and embrace the breast of a foreign woman?[^5-12]

**21** For a person's ways are in full view of Yahweh's eyes,[^5-13] and he examines all their paths.

**22** The wicked man's own iniquities will capture him, and he will be held fast by the cords of his sin.

**23** He will die for lack of discipline, and in his great folly[^5-14] he will go astray.

[^5-1]: Hebrew *tevunah*, from the same root as *binah*, "understanding" (see 2:2).
[^5-2]: Hebrew *mezimmot*, plural of *mezimmah*, as in 1:4.
[^5-3]: Hebrew *zarah*, "strange woman", that is, a woman outside one's marriage (see 2:16). Also in verse 20.
[^5-4]: Literally "her palate".
[^5-5]: A bitter plant.
[^5-6]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^5-7]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "The path of life, lest she make it level (or weigh it); her tracks wander; she does not know". The verbs could also be "you": "so that you do not consider the path of life … you do not know it".
[^5-8]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verse 23.
[^5-9]: Literally "I was almost in all evil". Or "I almost did every kind of evil". The *assembly and the community* may be the gathering that judged wrongdoing.
[^5-10]: Or "Let your springs overflow …", meaning many children; but the question in verses 16–17 more likely warns against spending oneself on other women.
[^5-11]: Hebrew *shagah*, "stagger, go astray": here the dizziness of love. The same verb is translated *be intoxicated* in verse 20 and *go astray* in verse 23.
[^5-12]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16).
[^5-13]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5-14]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** My son, if you have put up security for your neighbor,[^6-1] if you have shaken hands on a pledge for a stranger,[^6-2]

**2** if you have been trapped by the words of your mouth, caught by the words of your mouth,

**3** then do this, my son, and free yourself, since you have fallen into your neighbor's power: go, humble yourself, and press your neighbor hard.

**4** Give no sleep to your eyes and no slumber to your eyelids.

**5** Free yourself like a gazelle from the hunter's hand,[^6-3] like a bird from the hand of the fowler.

**6** Go to the ant, you lazy person;[^6-4] watch her ways and be wise.

**7** She has no commander, no overseer or ruler,

**8** yet she prepares her food in summer and gathers her provisions at harvest.[^6-5]

**9** How long will you lie there, you lazy person? When will you get up from your sleep?

**10** A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest,

**11** and poverty will come on you like a prowler,[^6-6] and need like an armed man.[^6-7]

**12** A worthless person,[^6-8] a wicked man, goes about with a crooked mouth,

**13** winking with his eyes, signaling with his feet, pointing with his fingers,

**14** with perversity in his heart, always plotting evil, stirring up quarrels.

**15** So disaster will come on him suddenly; in an instant he will be broken, and there will be no healing.

**16** There are six things that Yahweh[^6-9] hates, seven that are detestable to him:[^6-10]

**17** haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood,

**18** a heart that devises wicked plans, feet that hurry to run to evil,

**19** a false witness who breathes out lies,[^6-11] and one who stirs up quarrels among brothers.

**20** My son, keep your father's command, and do not abandon your mother's instruction.

**21** Bind them on your heart always; tie them around your neck.

**22** When you walk, they[^6-12] will guide you; when you lie down, they will watch over you; and when you wake up, they will talk with you.

**23** For the command is a lamp, and the instruction is a light, and the corrections of discipline[^6-13] are the way of life,

**24** to keep you from the evil woman,[^6-14] from the smooth tongue of the foreign woman.[^6-15]

**25** Do not desire her beauty in your heart, and do not let her captivate you with her eyelashes.

**26** For a prostitute costs only a loaf of bread,[^6-16] but another man's wife hunts down a precious life.

**27** Can a man scoop fire into his lap without his clothes being burned?

**28** Can a man walk on hot coals without his feet being scorched?

**29** So it is with the one who goes in to his neighbor's wife: no one who touches her will go unpunished.

**30** People do not despise a thief if he steals to satisfy his hunger when he is starving.[^6-17]

**31** Yet if he is caught, he must pay back sevenfold; he must give up all the wealth of his house.

**1** Do not envy evil people, and do not desire to be with them,

**2** for their hearts plot violence, and their lips talk of trouble.

**3** By wisdom a house is built, and by insight[^24-1] it is established;

**4** by knowledge its rooms are filled with all kinds of precious and pleasant wealth.

**5** A wise man is strong,[^24-2] and a person of knowledge increases his strength,

**6** for by guidance[^24-3] you wage your war, and with many advisers there is victory.

**7** Wisdom[^24-4] is too high for a fool;[^24-5] in the gate he does not open his mouth.

**8** Whoever plans to do evil will be called a schemer.[^24-6]

**9** The scheming of folly[^24-7] is sin, and a mocker[^24-8] is detestable to people.

**10** If you lose heart in the day of trouble, your strength is small.[^24-9]

**11** Rescue those who are being taken away to death; hold back those who are staggering to the slaughter.

**12** If you say, "But we did not know this," does not the one who weighs hearts understand? Does not the one who guards your life know it? He will repay each person according to what he has done.[^24-10]

**13** Eat honey, my son, for it is good, and the honeycomb is sweet to your taste.

**14** Know that wisdom is like that for your soul; if you find it, there will be a future, and your hope will not be cut off.

**15** Do not lie in wait like a wicked man[^24-11] against the home of the righteous;[^24-12] do not destroy his resting place.

**16** For the righteous falls seven times and rises again, but the wicked stumble in times of disaster.

**17** Do not rejoice when your enemy[^24-13] falls, and do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles,

**18** or Yahweh[^24-14] will see it and be displeased, and turn his anger away from him.

**19** Do not fret because of evildoers or envy the wicked,

**20** for the evil person has no future; the lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out.

**21** My son, fear Yahweh and the king; do not associate with those who are given to change,[^24-15]

**22** for disaster from them will rise suddenly, and who knows the ruin the two of them can bring?[^24-16]

**23** These also are sayings of the wise.[^24-17] Showing partiality[^24-18] in judgment is not good.

**24** Whoever says to the wicked, "You are righteous," peoples will curse him, nations will denounce him.

**25** But those who rebuke the wicked will find delight, and a good blessing will come upon them.

**26** Whoever gives an honest answer kisses the lips.[^24-19]

**27** Put your outdoor work in order and get your fields ready; after that, build your house.[^24-20]

**28** Do not be a witness against your neighbor without cause; would you deceive with your lips?

**29** Do not say, "I will do to him as he has done to me; I will pay the man back for what he has done."

**30** I passed by the field of a lazy man,[^24-21] by the vineyard of a man who lacked sense,[^24-22]

**31** and look, it was all overgrown with thistles; its surface was covered with weeds, and its stone wall was broken down.

**32** I looked and took it to heart; I saw and received discipline:[^24-23]

**33** a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest,

**34** and poverty will come on you like a prowler, and need like an armed man.[^24-24]

## Chapter 25

**1** These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied.[^25-1]

**2** It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, and the glory of kings to search out a matter.

**3** As the heavens are high and the earth is deep, so the heart of kings is unsearchable.

**4** Remove the dross from silver, and a vessel comes out for the silversmith.

**5** Remove the wicked[^25-2] from the king's presence, and his throne will be established in righteousness.

**6** Do not put yourself forward before the king, and do not stand in the place of the great,

**7** for it is better to be told, "Come up here," than to be put lower in the presence of a noble whom your eyes have seen.[^25-3]

**8** Do not go out hastily to court, for what will you do in the end, when your neighbor puts you to shame?

**9** Argue your case with your neighbor, but do not reveal another's secret,

**10** or the one who hears it will disgrace you,[^25-4] and your bad name will never go away.

**11** Like apples of gold in settings of silver is a word spoken at the right time.[^25-5]

**12** Like a gold earring or an ornament of fine gold is a wise person who corrects a listening ear.

**13** Like the coolness of snow at harvest time is a faithful messenger to those who send him; he refreshes the life of his masters.

**14** Like clouds and wind without rain is a man who boasts of a gift he does not give.

**15** With patience a ruler can be persuaded, and a gentle tongue can break a bone.

**16** If you find honey, eat only what you need, or you will have too much and vomit it up.

**17** Seldom set foot in your neighbor's house, or he will have too much of you and hate you.

**18** Like a club, a sword or a sharp arrow is a man who gives false testimony against his neighbor.

**19** Like a broken tooth or a lame foot is trust in a treacherous person in a time of trouble.

**20** Like one who takes off a garment on a cold day, like vinegar on soda,[^25-6] is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.

**21** If your enemy[^25-7] is hungry, give him bread to eat; if he is thirsty, give him water to drink,

**22** for you will heap burning coals on his head, and Yahweh[^25-8] will reward you.[^25-9]

**23** The north wind brings forth rain, and a secret tongue, angry faces.

**24** Better to live in a corner of the roof than in a house shared with a quarrelsome wife.[^25-10]

**25** Like cold water to a weary soul is good news from a distant land.

**26** Like a muddied spring or a polluted fountain is a righteous person[^25-11] who gives way before the wicked.

**27** It is not good to eat much honey, nor is it glorious to seek one's own glory.[^25-12]

**28** Like a city broken into and left without walls is a person who has no control over his spirit.
