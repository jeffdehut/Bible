## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The record of the genealogy[^1-1] of Jesus the Messiah,[^1-2] the son of David, the son of Abraham.[^1-3]

**2** Abraham fathered Isaac, Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers.[^1-4]

**3** Judah fathered Perez and Zerah by Tamar, Perez fathered Hezron, and Hezron fathered Ram.[^1-5]

**4** Ram fathered Amminadab, Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon.

**5** Salmon fathered Boaz by Rahab,[^1-6] Boaz fathered Obed by Ruth, and Obed fathered Jesse.[^1-7]

**6** Jesse fathered King David. David[^1-8] fathered Solomon by the wife of Uriah.[^1-9]

**7** Solomon fathered Rehoboam, Rehoboam fathered Abijah, and Abijah fathered Asaph.[^1-10]

**8** Asaph fathered Jehoshaphat, Jehoshaphat fathered Joram, and Joram fathered Uzziah.[^1-11]

**9** Uzziah fathered Jotham, Jotham fathered Ahaz, and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah.

**10** Hezekiah fathered Manasseh, Manasseh fathered Amos,[^1-12] and Amos fathered Josiah.

**11** Josiah fathered Jeconiah[^1-13] and his brothers at the time of the exile to Babylon.[^1-14]

**12** After the exile to Babylon, Jeconiah fathered Shealtiel, and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel.[^1-15]

**13** Zerubbabel fathered Abiud,[^1-16] Abiud fathered Eliakim, and Eliakim fathered Azor.

**14** Azor fathered Zadok, Zadok fathered Achim, and Achim fathered Eliud.

**15** Eliud fathered Eleazar, Eleazar fathered Matthan, and Matthan fathered Jacob.

**16** Jacob fathered Joseph, the husband of Mary. From her[^1-17] was born Jesus, who is called the Messiah.

**17** So there were fourteen generations in all from Abraham to David, fourteen generations from David to the exile to Babylon, and fourteen generations from the exile to Babylon to the Messiah.[^1-18]

**18** This is how the birth of Jesus the Messiah[^1-19] came about. His mother Mary was engaged[^1-20] to Joseph, but before they came together, she was found to be pregnant by the Holy Spirit.

**19** Her husband Joseph was a righteous man and did not want to disgrace her publicly, so he decided to divorce her[^1-21] quietly.

**20** But when he had thought this over, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, "Joseph son of David, don't be afraid to take Mary home as your wife, because what has been conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.

**21** She will give birth to a son, and you are to name him Jesus,[^1-22] because he will save his people from their sins."

**22** All this took place so that what the Lord had said through the prophet would be fulfilled:

**23** "Look, the virgin will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel,"[^1-23] which means "God is with us."

**24** When Joseph woke up from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him and took his wife home.

**25** He did not have sexual relations with her[^1-24] until she had given birth to a son,[^1-25] and he named him Jesus.

[^1-1]: Literally "The book of the genesis". Greek *genesis* means origin or birth (as in verse 18). In the Greek Old Testament the same phrase introduces the record of Adam's descendants (Genesis 5:1; compare 2:4).
[^1-2]: Or "Jesus Christ". Greek *christos*, "anointed one".
[^1-3]: *Son* here means descendant.
[^1-4]: Verses 2–6 follow the line in Ruth 4:18–22 and 1 Chronicles 1:34; 2:1–15. The names in verses 2–16 are spelled as in this translation's Old Testament; Matthew gives them in their Greek forms, such as *Phares* (Perez), *Zara* (Zerah) and *Hesrom* (Hezron). The notes mention the forms that differ more widely.
[^1-5]: Greek *Aram* here and in verse 4. The Hebrew name is Ram (Ruth 4:19; 1 Chronicles 2:9–10).
[^1-6]: The Old Testament does not name Salmon's wife. Rahab is the name of the woman of Jericho who hid the Israelite spies (Joshua 2; 6:25). Salmon is called Salma in 1 Chronicles 2:11 and Salmah in Ruth 4:20.
[^1-7]: Greek *Boes* (Boaz) and *Iobed* (Obed).
[^1-8]: Many manuscripts read "King David".
[^1-9]: Literally "by her of Uriah", that is, Bathsheba (2 Samuel 11:3; 12:24).
[^1-10]: Many manuscripts read *Asa*, the king's name in 1 Kings 15:8–24 and 1 Chronicles 3:10; also in verse 8. Asaph is also the name of a psalm writer (Psalms 50; 73–83).
[^1-11]: Greek *Ozias*. Uzziah, also called Azariah (2 Kings 15:1–7; 1 Chronicles 3:12), was Joram's great-great-grandson: the list passes over Ahaziah, Joash and Amaziah (1 Chronicles 3:11–12).
[^1-12]: Many manuscripts read *Amon*, the king's name in 2 Kings 21:18–26 and 1 Chronicles 3:14; also later in the verse. Amos is also the name of a prophet.
[^1-13]: Greek *Iechonias*. Jeconiah, also called Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:6), was Josiah's grandson, the son of Jehoiakim (1 Chronicles 3:15–16).
[^1-14]: Greek *metoikesia*, "removal, deportation"; also in verses 12 and 17. The people of Judah were taken to Babylon in the days of Jehoiachin and Zedekiah (2 Kings 24:10–16; 25:8–21).
[^1-15]: Greek *Salathiel* and *Zorobabel*. In 1 Chronicles 3:17–19 Zerubbabel is the son of Pedaiah, Shealtiel's brother; elsewhere he is called the son of Shealtiel (Ezra 3:2; Haggai 1:1).
[^1-16]: The names from Abiud to Jacob (verses 13–16) are not found in the Old Testament, which lists other descendants of Zerubbabel (1 Chronicles 3:19–24).
[^1-17]: *Her* is feminine in the Greek. Here the pattern "X fathered Y" changes, and Jesus is said to be born of Mary (verses 18–25).
[^1-18]: Matthew arranges the genealogy in three sets of fourteen. As the names are listed, the first set has fourteen names (Abraham to David) and the second has fourteen (Solomon to Jeconiah, leaving out the kings mentioned in the note on verse 8). The third has thirteen (Shealtiel to Jesus) unless Jeconiah is counted again at its start. The letters of David's name in Hebrew (DWD) have the number values 4, 6 and 4, which add up to fourteen.
[^1-19]: Some manuscripts read "the birth of the Messiah".
[^1-20]: Or "betrothed", "pledged to be married". A betrothal was legally binding: Joseph is already called her husband (verse 19), and ending it required a divorce.
[^1-21]: Literally "release her", the word used for divorce (5:31–32).
[^1-22]: *Jesus* is the Greek form of the Hebrew name Joshua (*Yeshua*), which means "The LORD saves". The LORD is God's personal name.
[^1-23]: Isaiah 7:14. Matthew follows the Greek Septuagint, which has *parthenos*, "virgin", where the Hebrew has *almah*, "young woman", a girl old enough to marry. The Hebrew reads "she will call"; Matthew reads "they will call".
[^1-24]: Literally "he was not knowing her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^1-25]: Many manuscripts read "her firstborn son".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** After Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Judea, in the days of King Herod,[^2-1] magi[^2-2] from the east arrived in Jerusalem

**2** and asked, "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews? We saw his star at its rising[^2-3] and have come to worship him."[^2-4]

**3** When King Herod heard this, he was disturbed, and all Jerusalem with him.

**4** He called together all the chief priests and scribes of the people and asked them where the Messiah was to be born.

**5** They told him, "In Bethlehem in Judea, because this is what was written through the prophet:

**6** 'And you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah, for out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd my people Israel.'"[^2-5]

**7** Then Herod secretly called the magi and found out from them exactly when the star had appeared.

**8** He sent them to Bethlehem and said, "Go and search carefully for the child. When you find him, report to me, so that I too may come and worship him."

**9** After listening to the king, they set out. And there was the star they had seen at its rising, going ahead of them, until it came and stopped over the place where the child was.

**10** When they saw the star, they were overjoyed.[^2-6]

**11** They went into the house and saw the child with his mother Mary, and they fell down and worshiped him. Then they opened their treasure chests and offered him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh.[^2-7]

**12** And since they had been warned in a dream not to go back to Herod, they returned to their own country by another route.

**13** After they had gone, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said, "Get up, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt. Stay there until I tell you, because Herod is going to search for the child to kill him."

**14** So Joseph got up, took the child and his mother during the night, and left for Egypt.

**15** He stayed there until the death of Herod. This was so that what the Lord had said through the prophet would be fulfilled: "Out of Egypt I called my son."[^2-8]

**16** When Herod saw that he had been tricked by the magi, he was furious. He sent men to kill all the boys in Bethlehem and all its surrounding region who were two years old and under, according to the time he had found out exactly from the magi.

**17** Then what had been spoken through the prophet Jeremiah was fulfilled:

**18** "A voice was heard in Ramah,[^2-9] weeping and great mourning: Rachel weeping for her children,[^2-10] and she refused to be comforted, because they are no more."[^2-11]

**19** After Herod died, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt

**20** and said, "Get up, take the child and his mother, and go to the land of Israel, because those who were seeking the child's life are dead."

**21** So Joseph got up, took the child and his mother, and went into the land of Israel.

**22** But when he heard that Archelaus[^2-12] was reigning over Judea in place of his father Herod, he was afraid to go there. And having been warned in a dream, he went away to the region of Galilee.

**23** He went and settled in a town called Nazareth, so that what had been spoken through the prophets would be fulfilled: "He will be called a Nazarene."[^2-13]

[^2-1]: Herod the Great, who ruled Judea as king under the Romans from 37 to 4 BC.
[^2-2]: Greek *magoi*, experts in astrology and the interpretation of dreams, probably from Persia or Babylonia. Traditionally "wise men".
[^2-3]: Or "in the east"; also in verse 9.
[^2-4]: Or "pay him homage"; also in verses 8 and 11. Greek *proskyneo*, to bow down before someone, a king or God.
[^2-5]: Micah 5:1 (5:2 in most English Bibles), quoted freely, with words from 2 Samuel 5:2 ("You will shepherd my people Israel"). Micah's Hebrew reads "But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, too small to be among the clans of Judah, from you one will come out for me to be ruler in Israel."
[^2-6]: Literally "they rejoiced with very great joy".
[^2-7]: Frankincense and myrrh are fragrant resins from trees, used for incense, perfume and medicine.
[^2-8]: Hosea 11:1. Matthew follows the Hebrew; the Septuagint reads "out of Egypt I called his children".
[^2-9]: Many manuscripts add "lamentation,".
[^2-10]: Rachel, the mother of Joseph and Benjamin, was buried on the road to Bethlehem (Genesis 35:19).
[^2-11]: Jeremiah 31:15. Matthew's Greek is shorter than the Hebrew, which reads "lamentation and bitter weeping" and "she refuses to be comforted for her children".
[^2-12]: A son of Herod. He ruled Judea, Samaria and Idumea from 4 BC to AD 6, with the title of ethnarch ("ruler of a people"), not king.
[^2-13]: No passage in the Old Testament has these words. *Nazarene* may recall the Hebrew *netser*, "branch" (Isaiah 11:1), or *nazir*, "Nazirite", one set apart to God (Judges 13:5–7). Or "that he would be called a Nazarene".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** In those days John the Baptist appeared in the wilderness of Judea, proclaiming

**2** and saying, "Repent,[^3-1] for the kingdom of heaven[^3-2] has come near."

**3** This is the one the prophet Isaiah spoke about when he said: "A voice is shouting in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight.'"[^3-3]

**4** John wore clothing made of camel's hair, with a leather belt around his waist,[^3-4] and his food was locusts and wild honey.

**5** Then the people of Jerusalem, all Judea, and the whole region around the Jordan went out to him.

**6** They confessed their sins and were baptized by him in the Jordan River.

**7** But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees[^3-5] coming to his baptism, he said to them, "You brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the coming wrath?

**8** Produce fruit, then, that fits your repentance.

**9** And don't think you can say to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father.' For I tell you, God can raise up children for Abraham from these stones.

**10** The ax is already lying at the root of the trees. So every tree that does not produce good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.

**11** I baptize you with water for repentance, but the one who comes after me is more powerful than I am. I am not worthy to carry his sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.[^3-6]

**12** His winnowing fork[^3-7] is in his hand. He will clear his threshing floor and gather his wheat into the barn, but he will burn up the chaff with fire that never goes out."

**13** Then Jesus came from Galilee to John at the Jordan to be baptized by him.

**14** But John tried to stop him. "I need to be baptized by you," he said, "and do you come to me?"

**15** Jesus answered him, "Let it be so for now. It is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness[^3-8] in this way." Then John allowed him.

**16** When Jesus had been baptized, he came up from the water at once. Just then the heavens were opened,[^3-9] and he saw the Spirit of God coming down like a dove and coming on him.

**17** And a voice from the heavens said, "This is my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with him."[^3-10]

[^3-1]: Greek *metanoeo*, to change one's mind and way of life, to turn around. Also in verses 8 and 11 (*metanoia*, "repentance").
[^3-2]: Literally "the kingdom of the heavens", Matthew's usual phrase. Many Jews of the time spoke of "heaven" instead of naming God. Mark and Luke have "the kingdom of God".
[^3-3]: Isaiah 40:3. Matthew follows the Septuagint, which joins "in the wilderness" to the voice; the Hebrew reads "A voice is shouting: 'In the wilderness prepare the way for the LORD.'" In Isaiah's Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^3-4]: Elijah was dressed in the same way (2 Kings 1:8).
[^3-5]: The Pharisees were a movement of teachers and laypeople devoted to keeping the Law of Moses and the traditions that explained it. The Sadducees were a party of priests and leading families, who accepted only the written Law and did not believe in the resurrection (22:23).
[^3-6]: Many manuscripts leave out "and fire".
[^3-7]: A wooden fork for tossing threshed grain into the air, so that the wind would blow the chaff away.
[^3-8]: Or "to do everything that is right".
[^3-9]: Many manuscripts read "opened to him".
[^3-10]: Or "This is my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in him." Compare Psalm 2:7; Isaiah 42:1.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tested[^4-1] by the devil.

**2** After fasting forty days and forty nights, he was hungry.

**3** The tempter came and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread."

**4** Jesus answered, "It is written: 'People do not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.'"[^4-2]

**5** Then the devil took him to the holy city and set him on the highest point[^4-3] of the temple,

**6** and he said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. For it is written: 'He will command his angels concerning you, and they will lift you up in their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.'"[^4-4]

**7** Jesus said to him, "It is also written: 'Do not put the Lord your God to the test.'"[^4-5]

**8** Again the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory.

**9** And he said to him, "I will give you all these things if you will fall down and worship me."

**10** Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan![^4-6] For it is written: 'Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.'"[^4-7]

**11** Then the devil left him, and angels came and took care of him.

**12** When Jesus heard that John had been arrested,[^4-8] he withdrew to Galilee.

**13** He left Nazareth and went to live in Capernaum, which is by the sea in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali,

**14** so that what had been spoken through the prophet Isaiah would be fulfilled:

**15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations:[^4-9]

**16** the people sitting in darkness have seen a great light, and on those sitting in the land and shadow of death a light has dawned."[^4-10]

**17** From that time Jesus began to proclaim, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near."

**18** As Jesus was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon, who is called Peter, and his brother Andrew, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**19** He said to them, "Come, follow me, and I will make you fish for people."

**20** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**21** Going on from there, he saw two other brothers, James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in the boat with their father Zebedee, mending their nets, and he called them.

**22** At once they left the boat and their father and followed him.

**23** Jesus went throughout Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

**24** The news about him spread through all Syria, and people brought to him all who were ill, suffering from various diseases and pains: those possessed by demons, those who had seizures,[^4-11] and the paralyzed. And he healed them.

**25** Large crowds followed him from Galilee, the Decapolis,[^4-12] Jerusalem, Judea, and the region beyond the Jordan.

[^4-1]: Or "tempted". The Greek word means both. Also in verse 3, "the tempter".
[^4-2]: Deuteronomy 8:3. Matthew follows the Septuagint, which has "every word" and "God"; the Hebrew reads "everything that comes from the mouth of the LORD".
[^4-3]: Literally "the little wing", perhaps a high corner of the temple's outer wall.
[^4-4]: Psalm 91:11–12, leaving out "to guard you in all your ways".
[^4-5]: Deuteronomy 6:16. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"); also in verse 10.
[^4-6]: Many manuscripts read "Get behind me, Satan!"
[^4-7]: Deuteronomy 6:13. The Hebrew reads "Fear the LORD your God. Serve him"; Matthew has "worship" and "only", in a form close to the Septuagint.
[^4-8]: Literally "handed over".
[^4-9]: Or "of the Gentiles".
[^4-10]: Isaiah 8:23–9:1 (9:1–2 in most English Bibles), in a shortened form. Isaiah's Hebrew has "walked in darkness" and "the land of deep darkness" (traditionally "the shadow of death").
[^4-11]: Literally "those who were moonstruck", probably people with epilepsy.
[^4-12]: "The Ten Towns", a group of Greek cities east of the Jordan.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up on the mountain. After he sat down, his disciples came to him,

**2** and he began to teach them,[^5-1] saying:

**3** "Blessed[^5-2] are the poor in spirit, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs.

**4** Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted.[^5-3]

**5** Blessed are the gentle, for they will inherit the earth.[^5-4]

**6** Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness,[^5-5] for they will be filled.

**7** Blessed are the merciful, for they will be shown mercy.

**8** Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.[^5-6]

**9** Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God.[^5-7]

**10** Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs.

**11** "Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you, and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me.

**12** Rejoice and be glad, because your reward in heaven is great. For that is how they persecuted the prophets who were before you.

**13** "You are the salt of the earth. But if salt loses its taste,[^5-8] how can it be made salty again? It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled underfoot by people.

**14** "You are the light of the world. A town set on a hill cannot be hidden.

**15** People do not light a lamp and put it under a basket,[^5-9] but on a lampstand, and it gives light to everyone in the house.

**16** In the same way, let your light shine before people, so that they may see your good works and praise your Father in heaven.

**17** "Don't think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets.[^5-10] I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them.

**18** Truly I tell you, until heaven and earth pass away, not the smallest letter or the least stroke of a letter[^5-11] will pass from the Law until everything has taken place.

**19** So whoever breaks[^5-12] one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven. But whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.

**20** For I tell you, unless your righteousness goes far beyond that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.

**21** "You have heard that it was said to the people of long ago,[^5-13] 'You must not murder,' and 'Whoever murders will be liable to judgment.'[^5-14]

**22** But I tell you that everyone who is angry with their brother[^5-15] will be liable to judgment. Whoever says to their brother, 'Raca,'[^5-16] will be liable to the council.[^5-17] And whoever says, 'You fool!' will be liable to the fire of hell.[^5-18]

**23** "So if you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you,

**24** leave your gift there in front of the altar. First go and be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift.

**25** Come to terms quickly with your opponent while you are still with him on the way to court.[^5-19] Otherwise your opponent may hand you over to the judge, and the judge to the officer, and you will be thrown into prison.

**26** Truly I tell you, you will never get out of there until you have paid the last penny.[^5-20]

**27** "You have heard that it was said, 'You must not commit adultery.'[^5-21]

**28** But I tell you that anyone who looks at a woman[^5-22] with desire for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart.

**29** If your right eye causes you to stumble,[^5-23] tear it out and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to be thrown into hell.

**30** And if your right hand causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to go into hell.

**31** "It was also said, 'Whoever divorces his wife must give her a certificate of divorce.'[^5-24]

**32** But I tell you that anyone who divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality,[^5-25] causes her to commit adultery,[^5-26] and whoever marries a divorced woman commits adultery.

**33** "Again, you have heard that it was said to the people of long ago, 'Do not swear falsely, but carry out the oaths you have made to the Lord.'[^5-27]

**34** But I tell you, do not swear at all: not by heaven, because it is God's throne,

**35** or by the earth, because it is his footstool, or by Jerusalem, because it is the city of the great King.[^5-28]

**36** And do not swear by your head, because you cannot make one hair white or black.

**37** Let what you say be simply 'Yes, yes' or 'No, no'.[^5-29] Anything more than this comes from the evil one.[^5-30]

**38** "You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye and tooth for tooth.'[^5-31]

**39** But I tell you, do not resist an evil person.[^5-32] If anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn the other cheek to him as well.

**40** If anyone wants to take you to court and take your tunic, let him have your cloak as well.[^5-33]

**41** If anyone forces you to go one mile,[^5-34] go with him two.

**42** Give to the one who asks you, and do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you.

**43** "You have heard that it was said, 'Love your neighbor and hate your enemy.'[^5-35]

**44** But I tell you, love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you,[^5-36]

**45** so that you may be children[^5-37] of your Father in heaven. For he makes his sun rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the righteous and the unrighteous.

**46** If you love those who love you, what reward do you have? Don't even the tax collectors do the same?

**47** And if you greet only your brothers,[^5-38] what are you doing more than others? Don't even the Gentiles do the same?

**48** So be perfect,[^5-39] as your heavenly Father is perfect.

[^5-1]: Literally "opening his mouth, he taught them".
[^5-2]: Greek *makarioi*, "blessed, happy, fortunate": those whom God favors. Also in verses 4–11.
[^5-3]: Compare Isaiah 61:1–3, with its good news for the poor and comfort for all who mourn.
[^5-4]: Or "the meek", "the humble"; *the earth*: or "the land". Compare Psalm 37:11, "But the humble will inherit the land."
[^5-5]: Or "for justice", "for what is right".
[^5-6]: Compare Psalm 24:3–4.
[^5-7]: Literally "sons of God".
[^5-8]: The Greek verb also means "become foolish".
[^5-9]: Greek *modios*, a container for measuring grain, holding about 8 liters (2 gallons).
[^5-10]: *The Law or the Prophets*: the Hebrew scriptures.
[^5-11]: Literally "not one *iota* or one horn". The *iota* is the smallest letter of the Greek alphabet (compare the Hebrew *yod*), and the "horn" is a tiny stroke that tells one letter from another.
[^5-12]: Or "sets aside", "annuls".
[^5-13]: Or "by the people of long ago"; also in verse 33.
[^5-14]: Exodus 20:13; Deuteronomy 5:17. The second sentence sums up laws such as Exodus 21:12.
[^5-15]: Or "brother or sister", a fellow member of God's people; also in verses 23–24. Many manuscripts add "without cause".
[^5-16]: An Aramaic insult meaning "empty-headed".
[^5-17]: Greek *synedrion*, a council of judges; in Jerusalem, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^5-18]: Literally "the Gehenna of fire". Greek *Gehenna*, from the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, used as a name for the place of final judgment. Also in verses 29–30.
[^5-19]: Literally "on the way".
[^5-20]: Greek *kodrantes*, from the Latin *quadrans*, a small Roman coin worth 1/64 of a denarius.
[^5-21]: Exodus 20:14; Deuteronomy 5:18.
[^5-22]: Or "a married woman". Greek *gyne* means both "woman" and "wife".
[^5-23]: Or "to sin"; also in verse 30.
[^5-24]: Deuteronomy 24:1.

**5** The angel said to the women, "As for you, don't be afraid. I know that you are looking for Jesus, who was crucified.

**6** He is not here; he has been raised, just as he said. Come, see the place where he lay.[^28-3]

**7** Then go quickly and tell his disciples, 'He has been raised from the dead, and he is going ahead of you into Galilee. There you will see him.' Now I have told you."

**8** So they left the tomb quickly, afraid yet filled with great joy, and ran to tell his disciples.

**9** Suddenly[^28-4] Jesus met them and said, "Greetings!"[^28-5] They came to him, took hold of his feet, and worshiped him.

**10** Then Jesus said to them, "Don't be afraid. Go and tell my brothers to go to Galilee; there they will see me."

**11** While they were on their way, some of the guards went into the city and reported to the chief priests everything that had happened.

**12** After meeting with the elders and agreeing on a plan, the chief priests gave the soldiers a large sum of money,

**13** telling them, "You are to say, 'His disciples came during the night and stole him while we were asleep.'

**14** If this comes to the governor's ears, we will persuade him and keep you out of trouble."

**15** So they took the money and did as they were instructed. And this story has been spread among Jews to this day.

**16** The eleven disciples went to Galilee, to the mountain where Jesus had told them to go.

**17** When they saw him, they worshiped him, but some doubted.[^28-6]

**18** Jesus came to them and said, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.[^28-7]

**19** So go and make disciples of all nations,[^28-8] baptizing them in the name[^28-9] of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,

**20** teaching them to keep everything I have commanded you. And look, I am with you always,[^28-10] to the end of the age."[^28-11]

[^28-1]: Or "Late on the Sabbath".
[^28-2]: Many manuscripts add "from the entrance".
[^28-3]: Many manuscripts read "where the Lord lay".
[^28-4]: Many manuscripts begin the verse "As they were going to tell his disciples".
[^28-5]: Greek *chairete*, the ordinary greeting, literally "Rejoice!"
[^28-6]: Or "but they hesitated". Greek *distazo*, "doubt, hesitate". The Greek can mean that some of the Eleven doubted, or that they all did.
[^28-7]: Compare Daniel 7:14, "He was given authority".
[^28-8]: Or "all peoples". Greek *panta ta ethne*, the phrase used in 24:9, 14 and 25:32.
[^28-9]: Literally "into the name".
[^28-10]: Literally "all the days".
[^28-11]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
