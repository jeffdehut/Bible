## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of the LORD[^1-1] that came to Hosea[^1-2] son of Beeri in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, king of Israel.[^1-3]

**2** When the LORD first spoke through Hosea, the LORD said to Hosea, "Go, take for yourself a wife given to prostitution, and children of prostitution,[^1-4] for the land is committing flagrant prostitution by turning away from the LORD."

**3** So he went and married Gomer daughter of Diblaim, and she conceived and bore him a son.

**4** The LORD said to him, "Name him Jezreel,[^1-5] for in a little while I will punish the house of Jehu for the bloodshed of Jezreel, and I will put an end to the kingdom of the house of Israel.

**5** On that day I will break the bow of Israel in the Valley of Jezreel."

**6** She conceived again and bore a daughter. The LORD said to him,[^1-6] "Name her Lo-ruhamah,[^1-7] for I will no longer have compassion on the house of Israel, that I should forgive them at all.[^1-8]

**7** But I will have compassion on the house of Judah, and I will save them by the LORD their God. I will not save them by bow or sword or battle, by horses or horsemen."

**8** After she had weaned Lo-ruhamah, she conceived and bore a son.

**9** The LORD said, "Name him Lo-ammi,[^1-9] for you are not my people, and I will not be yours."[^1-10]

[^1-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *Hoshea*, "salvation" or "he has saved", the original name of Joshua (Numbers 13:16) and the name of the last king of Israel (2 Kings 17:1).
[^1-3]: Jeroboam II reigned from about 786 to 746 BC. Hosea's work as a prophet ran from the last years of Jeroboam's reign until shortly before the fall of Samaria in 722 BC.
[^1-4]: Literally "a wife of prostitutions and children of prostitutions". Hebrew *zenunim*, "prostitution, sexual unfaithfulness". It need not mean that she was a professional prostitute; it may mean that she was, or would become, unfaithful. Throughout the book the word pictures Israel's worship of other gods as a wife's unfaithfulness to her husband.
[^1-5]: Jezreel means "God sows". It was the royal city and valley where Jehu seized the throne by killing the family of Ahab (2 Kings 9–10). The name returns as a promise in 2:2, 24–25 (1:11; 2:22–23 in most English Bibles).
[^1-6]: Literally "He said to him". Also in verse 9.
[^1-7]: Lo-ruhamah means "not shown compassion" or "not loved". The Hebrew verb *racham* is used of the tender love of a parent.
[^1-8]: Or "but I will surely take them away". The Hebrew verb *nasa*, "lift up, carry", can mean "forgive" (bear away sin) or "take away".
[^1-9]: Lo-ammi means "not my people".
[^1-10]: Literally "and I, I will not be to you". Hebrew *lo-ehyeh*, "I will not be", probably echoes the name God gives himself in Exodus 3:14, *ehyeh*, "I am" or "I will be". Many translations, following the context and 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles), read "and I am not your God".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Yet the number of the Israelites will be like the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured or counted.[^2-1] And in the place where it was said to them, "You are not my people," it will be said to them, "Children of the living God."[^2-2]

**2** The people of Judah and the people of Israel will be gathered together. They will appoint for themselves one head, and they will go up from the land,[^2-3] for great will be the day of Jezreel.

**3** Say to your brothers, "Ammi," and to your sisters, "Ruhamah."[^2-4]

**4** Bring charges against your mother, bring charges, for she is not my wife, and I am not her husband. Let her remove her prostitution from her face and her adultery from between her breasts,

**5** or I will strip her naked and expose her as on the day she was born. I will make her like a wilderness, turn her into a parched land, and kill her with thirst.

**6** I will have no compassion on her children, because they are children of prostitution.

**7** For their mother has prostituted herself; she who conceived them has acted shamefully. For she said, "I will go after my lovers, who give me my bread and my water, my wool and my flax, my oil and my drink."

**8** Therefore I am going to hedge up your way with thorns,[^2-5] and I will build a wall against her, so that she cannot find her paths.

**9** She will pursue her lovers but not overtake them; she will look for them but not find them. Then she will say, "I will go back to my first husband, for it was better for me then than now."

**10** She did not know that it was I who gave her the grain, the new wine and the olive oil, and who lavished on her the silver and gold that they used for Baal.[^2-6]

**11** Therefore I will take back my grain in its time and my new wine in its season, and I will snatch away my wool and my flax, which were to cover her nakedness.

**12** Now I will uncover her shame[^2-7] before the eyes of her lovers, and no one will rescue her from my hand.

**13** I will put an end to all her celebrations: her festivals, her new moons, her Sabbaths and all her appointed feasts.

**14** I will lay waste her vines and her fig trees, of which she said, "These are my wages,[^2-8] which my lovers gave me." I will turn them into a thicket, and the wild animals will devour them.

**15** I will punish her for the feast days of the Baals,[^2-9] when she burned incense[^2-10] to them and decked herself with her nose ring and her jewelry, and went after her lovers, but me she forgot, declares the LORD.[^2-11]

**16** Therefore I am going to win her back.[^2-12] I will lead her into the wilderness and speak tenderly to her.[^2-13]

**17** There I will give her back her vineyards, and make the Valley of Achor[^2-14] a door of hope. There she will respond[^2-15] as in the days of her youth, as on the day she came up out of the land of Egypt.

**18** On that day, declares the LORD, you will call me "My husband," and you will no longer call me "My Baal."[^2-16]

**19** I will remove the names of the Baals from her mouth, and they will no longer be remembered by name.

**20** On that day I will make a covenant for them with the wild animals, the birds of the sky and the creatures that creep on the ground. I will break bow and sword and battle and banish them from the land, and I will let them lie down in safety.

**21** I will betroth you to myself forever. I will betroth you to myself in righteousness and justice, in faithful love[^2-17] and compassion.

**22** I will betroth you to myself in faithfulness, and you will know the LORD.

**23** On that day I will answer, declares the LORD. I will answer the heavens, and they will answer the earth.

**24** The earth will answer the grain, the new wine and the olive oil, and they will answer Jezreel.[^2-18]

**25** I will sow her for myself in the land. I will have compassion on Lo-ruhamah, and I will say to Lo-ammi, "You are my people," and he will say, "My God."[^2-19]

[^2-1]: Verses 1–2 are 1:10–11 in most English Bibles, and verses 3–25 are 2:1–23. Compare the promise to Abraham (Genesis 22:17; 32:13, 32:12 in most English Bibles).
[^2-2]: Quoted in Romans 9:26.
[^2-3]: Or "and they will spring up from the land", like a crop (see "Jezreel", "God sows", in 1:4), or "and they will take possession of the land". The meaning is uncertain.
[^2-4]: Ammi means "my people" and Ruhamah "shown compassion": the names of Hosea's children (1:6, 9) without "not".
[^2-5]: The Hebrew changes from "your" to "her".
[^2-6]: Or "which they made into Baal", that is, into images of Baal. Baal, "lord, master", was the Canaanite storm god, believed to give rain and fertility to the land.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *navlut*, a word found only here, perhaps "lewdness" or "genitals".
[^2-8]: Hebrew *etnah*, the payment given to a prostitute.
[^2-9]: The local forms of the god Baal worshiped at many shrines. Also in verse 19.
[^2-10]: Or "burned offerings". The Hebrew verb *qatar* means to make something go up in smoke.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-12]: Or "to persuade her", "to entice her".
[^2-13]: Literally "speak to her heart".
[^2-14]: Achor means "trouble" (Joshua 7:24–26).
[^2-15]: Or "she will sing". Hebrew *anah*, "answer, respond", the same verb as in verses 23–24.
[^2-16]: Hebrew *ishi*, "my husband" (literally "my man"), and *ba'ali*, "my master", also used for "my husband", and the name of the god Baal.
[^2-17]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^2-18]: Jezreel, "God sows", here becomes a name of promise. Compare 1:4–5; 2:2.
[^2-19]: Quoted in Romans 9:25 and 1 Peter 2:10.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^3-1] said to me, "Go again, love a woman who is loved by another man[^3-2] and is an adulteress, just as the LORD loves the Israelites, though they turn to other gods and love raisin cakes."[^3-3]

**2** So I bought her for myself for fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley.[^3-4]

**3** Then I said to her, "You are to live with me for many days. You must not be a prostitute or belong to any man, and I too will be faithful to you."[^3-5]

**4** For the Israelites will live for many days without king or official, without sacrifice or standing stone, without ephod or household gods.[^3-6]

**5** Afterward the Israelites will return and seek the LORD their God and David their king.[^3-7] They will come trembling to the LORD and to his goodness in the last days.[^3-8]

[^3-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-2]: Or "who is loved by her husband". Hebrew *rea*, "friend, companion", which can also mean a lover or a husband (Jeremiah 3:20).
[^3-3]: Cakes of pressed raisins were eaten at festivals (2 Samuel 6:19), here probably festivals of other gods.
[^3-4]: A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so fifteen shekels were about 6 ounces (170 grams). A homer was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters). A *lethech* was perhaps half a homer.
[^3-5]: Literally "and also I to you". Or "and I will not come to you either".
[^3-6]: *Standing stones* (Hebrew *matsevot*) were set up as objects of worship. The *ephod* was a priestly garment, sometimes an object used to seek guidance from God (Judges 8:27; 17:5). *Household gods* is Hebrew *teraphim*, small images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19).
[^3-7]: That is, a king from the family of David.
[^3-8]: Literally "at the end of the days".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Hear the word of the LORD,[^4-1] you Israelites, for the LORD has a case[^4-2] against those who live in the land. There is no faithfulness, no faithful love,[^4-3] no knowledge of God in the land.

**2** There is cursing, lying, murder, stealing and adultery.[^4-4] They break all bounds, and bloodshed follows bloodshed.[^4-5]

**3** Therefore the land mourns,[^4-6] and all who live in it waste away, along with the wild animals and the birds of the sky, and even the fish of the sea are swept away.

**4** Yet let no one bring a charge, and let no one accuse, for your people are like those who bring charges against a priest.[^4-7]

**5** You will stumble by day, and the prophet will stumble with you by night, and I will destroy your mother.[^4-8]

**6** My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge. Because you have rejected knowledge, I reject you from serving as my priest. Since you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.

**7** The more they increased, the more they sinned against me. I will change their glory into shame.[^4-9]

**8** They feed on the sin of my people,[^4-10] and they long for their iniquity.[^4-11]

**9** It will be like people, like priest. I will punish them for their ways and repay them for their deeds.[^4-12]

**10** They will eat but not be satisfied; they will prostitute themselves but not increase, for they have abandoned the LORD and stopped heeding him.[^4-13]

**11** Prostitution, wine and new wine take away understanding.[^4-14]

**12** My people consult their piece of wood, and their staff gives them answers.[^4-15] For a spirit of prostitution has led them astray, and they have prostituted themselves and left their God.[^4-16]

**13** They sacrifice on the tops of the mountains and burn incense on the hills, under oak, poplar and terebinth, because their shade is pleasant. That is why your daughters turn to prostitution and your daughters-in-law commit adultery.

**14** I will not punish your daughters when they turn to prostitution, or your daughters-in-law when they commit adultery, for the men themselves go off with prostitutes and sacrifice with shrine prostitutes.[^4-17] A people without understanding will come to ruin.

**15** Though you, Israel, play the prostitute, do not let Judah become guilty. Do not go to Gilgal, do not go up to Beth-aven,[^4-18] and do not swear, "As the LORD lives!"

**16** For Israel is stubborn, like a stubborn heifer. Can the LORD now pasture them like a lamb in a broad meadow?[^4-19]

**17** Ephraim[^4-20] is joined to idols; leave him alone!

**18** When their drinking is over, they give themselves to prostitution. Her rulers dearly love shame.[^4-21]

**19** A wind has wrapped her up in its wings,[^4-22] and they will be put to shame because of their sacrifices.[^4-23]

[^4-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *riv*, a lawsuit or legal dispute.
[^4-3]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^4-4]: The list recalls the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:7, 13–16; Deuteronomy 5:11, 17–20).
[^4-5]: Literally "bloods touch bloods".
[^4-6]: Or "dries up". The Hebrew verb can have either meaning.
[^4-7]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Some scholars read "for with you is my case, O priest", with small changes to the Hebrew.
[^4-8]: Probably the nation (compare 2:4, 2:2 in most English Bibles), or the priest's own family. *Destroy* (Hebrew *damah*) is the same verb as "are destroyed" in verse 6.
[^4-9]: According to an ancient Jewish scribal tradition (the "corrections of the scribes"), the original text read "They have exchanged my glory for shame" (compare Psalm 106:20; Jeremiah 2:11).
[^4-10]: Hebrew *chatta't* means both "sin" and "sin offering": the priests eat the people's sin offerings (Leviticus 6:19, 6:26 in most English Bibles), and so profit from their sin.
[^4-11]: Literally "they lift up his soul to their iniquity".
[^4-12]: Literally "I will punish him for his ways and repay him for his deeds".
[^4-13]: Literally "for they have abandoned the LORD to keep". Some join the first word of verse 11 to this verse: "they have abandoned the LORD to devote themselves to prostitution".
[^4-14]: Literally "take away the heart".
[^4-15]: A wooden idol and a diviner's rod, used to seek omens.
[^4-16]: Literally "they have prostituted themselves from under their God", as a wife leaves her husband's authority.
[^4-17]: Hebrew *qedeshot*, "consecrated women". See Genesis 38:21 and Deuteronomy 23:18 (23:17 in most English Bibles).
[^4-18]: Beth-aven, "house of wickedness", is a mocking name for Bethel, "house of God", where one of the royal shrines of the northern kingdom stood (1 Kings 12:28–33). Gilgal was another center of worship (9:15; 12:12, 12:11 in most English Bibles; Amos 4:4).
[^4-19]: Or "Now the LORD will pasture them like a lamb in a broad meadow."
[^4-20]: Ephraim, the largest tribe of the northern kingdom, is often used in Hosea as a name for the kingdom of Israel.
[^4-21]: The Hebrew of this verse is very uncertain. *Her rulers* is literally "her shields".
[^4-22]: Or "A spirit". Hebrew *ruach*. The wind will sweep them away (compare "a spirit of prostitution" in verse 12).
[^4-23]: The same consonants can be read "because of their altars", as the Septuagint does.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Hear this, you priests! Pay attention, house of Israel! Listen, house of the king! For the judgment is for you,[^5-1] because you have been a snare at Mizpah and a net spread out on Tabor.

**2** The rebels have gone deep into slaughter,[^5-2] but I will discipline them all.[^5-3]

**3** I know Ephraim, and Israel is not hidden from me. For now, Ephraim, you have prostituted yourself; Israel is defiled.

**4** Their deeds do not allow them to return to their God, for a spirit of prostitution is within them, and they do not know the LORD.[^5-4]

**5** Israel's pride testifies against him.[^5-5] Israel and Ephraim will stumble in their iniquity, and Judah also will stumble with them.

**6** With their flocks and their herds they will go to seek the LORD, but they will not find him; he has withdrawn from them.

**7** They have been unfaithful to the LORD, for they have borne children who are not his.[^5-6] Now the new moon[^5-7] will devour them along with their fields.

**8** Blow the horn in Gibeah, the trumpet in Ramah! Sound the alarm in Beth-aven![^5-8] Behind you, Benjamin![^5-9]

**9** Ephraim will become a desolation on the day of punishment. Among the tribes of Israel I make known what is certain.

**10** The officials of Judah have become like those who move a boundary stone.[^5-10] I will pour out my fury on them like water.

**11** Ephraim is oppressed, crushed in judgment,[^5-11] because he was determined to follow a command.[^5-12]

**12** I am like a moth[^5-13] to Ephraim, like rot to the house of Judah.

**13** When Ephraim saw his sickness and Judah his wound, Ephraim went to Assyria and sent to the great king.[^5-14] But he cannot heal you or cure you of your wound.

**14** For I will be like a lion to Ephraim, like a young lion to the house of Judah. I myself will tear them to pieces and go away; I will carry them off, and no one will rescue them.

**15** I will go back to my place until they admit their guilt[^5-15] and seek my face. In their distress they will earnestly seek me.

[^5-1]: Or "For you are responsible for justice". Hebrew *mishpat*.
[^5-2]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Some scholars read "they have dug deep the pit of Shittim", with small changes to the Hebrew, continuing the images of the snare and the net in verse 1.
[^5-3]: Literally "and I am discipline for all of them".
[^5-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-5]: Literally "answers in his face".
[^5-6]: Literally "foreign children".
[^5-7]: Or "a month", that is, very soon. The new moon was a festival (2:13, 2:11 in most English Bibles).
[^5-8]: See 4:15.
[^5-9]: Perhaps a battle cry (compare Judges 5:14). Gibeah, Ramah and Bethel lay on the road north from Jerusalem, in the land of Benjamin.
[^5-10]: Moving a neighbor's boundary stone was a serious crime (Deuteronomy 19:14; 27:17).
[^5-11]: Or "crushed, deprived of justice".
[^5-12]: Hebrew *tsav*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "command" or a mocking syllable (compare Isaiah 28:10). The Septuagint reads "worthless things" (Hebrew *shav*).
[^5-13]: Or "like pus". Hebrew *ash*.
[^5-14]: The Hebrew as pointed reads *melekh yarev*, "King Jareb" or "a king who contends". The same consonants, divided differently, can be read *malki rav*, "the great king", a title of the kings of Assyria. Also in 10:6.
[^5-15]: Or "until they bear their punishment".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** "Come, let us return to the LORD.[^6-1] For he has torn us, but he will heal us; he has struck us, but he will bind up our wounds.

**2** After two days he will revive us; on the third day he will raise us up, so that we may live before him.[^6-2]

**3** Let us know, let us press on to know the LORD. His coming is as sure as the dawn. He will come to us like the rain, like the spring rain that waters the earth."[^6-3]

**4** What shall I do with you, Ephraim? What shall I do with you, Judah? Your faithful love[^6-4] is like the morning mist, like the dew that goes away early.

**5** That is why I have cut them to pieces by the prophets; I have killed them by the words of my mouth, and my judgment goes out like light.[^6-5]

**6** For I desire faithful love, not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.[^6-6]

**7** But like Adam they have broken the covenant;[^6-7] there they have been unfaithful to me.

**8** Gilead is a city of evildoers, tracked with bloody footprints.

**9** As bands of robbers lie in wait for someone, so the company of priests murder on the road to Shechem.[^6-8] Indeed, they have committed shameful crimes.

**10** In the house of Israel I have seen a horrible thing. Ephraim's prostitution is there; Israel is defiled.

**11** For you also, Judah, a harvest is appointed, when I restore the fortunes of my people.[^6-9]

[^6-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Verses 1–3 give the words of the people who seek the LORD in their distress (5:15).
[^6-2]: That is, soon. Some Christian writers have connected this verse with the resurrection of Jesus "on the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:4).
[^6-3]: Hebrew *malqosh*, the spring rain of March and April, and *yoreh*, which can mean "the autumn rain" or "waters".
[^6-4]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love. Also in verse 6.
[^6-5]: The Hebrew as pointed reads "and your judgments, light goes out". The same consonants, divided differently, read "and my judgment goes out like light", as in the Septuagint.
[^6-6]: The first half of the verse is quoted by Jesus in Matthew 9:13 and 12:7, following the Septuagint: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice."
[^6-7]: Or "like human beings" or "at Adam". Adam was also a town in the Jordan valley (Joshua 3:16), which would explain "there".
[^6-8]: The Hebrew of the first part of the verse is uncertain.
[^6-9]: Many translations join this last clause to 7:1.

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** When I would heal Israel, the iniquity of Ephraim is exposed, and the wicked deeds of Samaria. For they practice deceit; a thief breaks in, and a band of raiders attacks in the street.

**2** But they do not consider[^7-1] that I remember all their wickedness. Now their deeds surround them; they are before my face.

**3** They make the king glad with their wickedness, and the officials with their lies.

**4** They are all adulterers. They are like an oven heated by a baker, who stops stirring the fire from the kneading of the dough until it is leavened.[^7-2]

**5** On the day of our king, the officials became sick with the heat of wine; he joined hands with mockers.[^7-3]

**6** For they draw near with their hearts like an oven in their plotting. All night their baker sleeps;[^7-4] in the morning it blazes like a flaming fire.

**7** All of them are hot as an oven, and they devour their rulers.[^7-5] All their kings have fallen,[^7-6] and none of them calls on me.

**8** Ephraim mixes himself with the peoples. Ephraim is a flat cake not turned over.[^7-7]

**9** Foreigners devour his strength, but he does not know it. Gray hair is sprinkled on him, but he does not know it.

**10** Israel's pride testifies against him,[^7-8] yet they do not return to the LORD[^7-9] their God or seek him in spite of all this.

**11** Ephraim is like a dove, easily fooled and without sense.[^7-10] They call to Egypt; they go to Assyria.

**12** As they go, I will spread my net over them; I will bring them down like the birds of the sky. I will discipline them according to the report to their assembly.[^7-11]

**13** Woe to them, for they have strayed from me! Destruction to them, for they have rebelled against me! I would redeem them, but they speak lies about me.

**14** They do not cry out to me from their hearts when they wail on their beds. For grain and new wine they gather together;[^7-12] they turn away from me.

**15** Though I trained and strengthened their arms, they plot evil against me.

**16** They turn, but not to the Most High;[^7-13] they are like a faulty bow. Their officials will fall by the sword because of the insolence of their tongues. This will make them a laughingstock in the land of Egypt.

[^7-1]: Literally "they do not say to their hearts".
[^7-2]: The Hebrew of verses 4–7 is difficult. The general picture is of conspirators whose passions smolder like a baker's oven until they flare up against their kings.
[^7-3]: Literally "he drew his hand with mockers".
[^7-4]: The same consonants can be read "their anger smolders".
[^7-5]: Literally "their judges".
[^7-6]: Four of the last six kings of Israel were assassinated (2 Kings 15:10, 14, 25, 30).
[^7-7]: That is, burned on one side and raw on the other.
[^7-8]: See 5:5.
[^7-9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7-10]: Literally "without heart".
[^7-11]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^7-12]: Or "they drag themselves about". The Septuagint reads "they gash themselves" (compare 1 Kings 18:28), a Hebrew reading that differs by one letter.
[^7-13]: Hebrew *lo al*, "not upward". *Al* may be a title of God, "the Most High" (compare 11:7). The meaning is uncertain.

## Chapter 8

*Status: draft*

**1** Put the horn to your lips![^8-1] An eagle is over the house of the LORD,[^8-2] because they have broken my covenant and rebelled against my law.

**2** They cry out to me, "My God, we, Israel, know you!"

**3** Israel has rejected what is good; an enemy will pursue him.

**4** They set up kings, but not through me; they appointed officials, but without my knowledge. With their silver and their gold they made idols for themselves, so that they will be cut off.[^8-3]

**5** He has rejected your calf, Samaria![^8-4] My anger burns against them. How long will they be incapable of innocence?

**6** For it is from Israel! A craftsman made it; it is not God. The calf of Samaria will be broken to pieces.

**7** For they sow the wind, and they will reap the whirlwind. The standing grain has no head; it will produce no flour.[^8-5] Even if it should produce, foreigners would swallow it up.

**8** Israel is swallowed up. Now they are among the nations like a worthless vessel.[^8-6]

**9** For they have gone up to Assyria, a wild donkey wandering alone.[^8-7] Ephraim has hired lovers.[^8-8]

**10** Even though they hire them among the nations, I will now gather them together. Soon they will writhe[^8-9] under the burden of the king of princes.[^8-10]

**11** Because Ephraim has multiplied altars for sinning, they have become to him altars for sinning.[^8-11]

**12** Though I write for him the many things of my law,[^8-12] they are regarded as something strange.

**13** They sacrifice meat as gifts to me[^8-13] and eat it, but the LORD does not accept them. Now he will remember their iniquity and punish their sins. They will return to Egypt.

**14** Israel has forgotten his Maker and built palaces,[^8-14] and Judah has multiplied fortified cities. But I will send fire on his cities, and it will devour their strongholds.[^8-15]
