## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel.[^1-1]

**2** Ahaziah[^1-2] had fallen through the lattice[^1-3] of his upper room in Samaria and was injured.[^1-4] So he sent messengers and told them, "Go and consult[^1-5] Baal-zebub,[^1-6] the god of Ekron,[^1-7] whether I will recover from this injury."

**3** But the angel[^1-8] of Yahweh[^1-9] said to Elijah the Tishbite, "Get up, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say to them, 'Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron?

**4** Therefore this is what Yahweh says: You will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die.'" So Elijah went.

**5** The messengers went back to the king,[^1-10] and he said to them, "Why have you come back?"

**6** They said to him, "A man came up to meet us and said to us, 'Go back to the king who sent you and say to him, "This is what Yahweh says: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? Therefore you will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die."'"

**7** He asked them, "What was the man like[^1-11] who came up to meet you and told you these things?"

**8** They said to him, "He was a hairy man,[^1-12] with a leather belt fastened around his waist." He said, "It was Elijah the Tishbite."

**9** Then the king sent a captain of fifty[^1-13] with his fifty men to Elijah. The captain went up to him, and there he was, sitting on top of the hill. He said to him, "Man of God, the king says, 'Come down!'"

**10** Elijah answered the captain of fifty, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty!"[^1-14] Then fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.

**11** Again the king sent another captain of fifty with his fifty men to him. He spoke to Elijah and said, "Man of God, this is what the king says: 'Come down at once!'"

**12** Elijah answered them, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty!" Then the fire of God[^1-15] came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.

**13** Again the king sent a third captain of fifty with his fifty men. The third captain of fifty went up, came and fell on his knees in front of Elijah, and begged him, "Man of God, please let my life and the lives of these fifty servants of yours be precious in your sight.

**14** Look, fire came down from heaven and consumed the first two captains of fifty and their fifties. But now let my life be precious in your sight."

**15** Then the angel of Yahweh said to Elijah, "Go down with him. Do not be afraid of him." So he got up and went down with him to the king.

**16** He said to the king, "This is what Yahweh says: You sent messengers to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, as if there were no God in Israel whose word you could consult.[^1-16] Therefore you will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die."

**17** So Ahaziah died, as Yahweh had said through Elijah.[^1-17] Because he had no son, Jehoram[^1-18] became king in his place, in the second year of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.[^1-19]

**18** As for the rest of the acts of Ahaziah, and what he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?[^1-20]

[^1-1]: Moab had been subject to Israel (3:4–5). In the Hebrew tradition 1 and 2 Kings are a single book, and the account of Ahaziah's reign begins in 1 Kings 22:52–54 (22:51–53 in most English Bibles).
[^1-2]: Ahaziah son of Ahab, king of Israel.
[^1-3]: Hebrew *sevakhah*, a latticework screen over a window or a balcony.
[^1-4]: Literally "became sick". The same word is used for his "injury" or "illness" later in the verse.
[^1-5]: Hebrew *darash be-*, "inquire of": to seek a god's answer through a prophet, priest or oracle. Also in verses 3, 6 and 16.
[^1-6]: The name means "lord of the flies". It is probably a mocking change of *Baal-zebul*, "Baal the prince", a title of the Canaanite god Baal.
[^1-7]: One of the five main Philistine cities, west of Judah.
[^1-8]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. It is the same word as the king's "messengers". Also in verse 15.
[^1-9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-10]: Literally "to him".
[^1-11]: Literally "What was the manner of the man".
[^1-12]: Literally "a man, a lord of hair". This may describe his hair or a garment of hair (compare Zechariah 13:4).
[^1-13]: An officer in command of fifty soldiers. Also in verses 10–14.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *ish*, "man", and *esh*, "fire", sound alike. Also in verse 12.
[^1-15]: Or "a tremendous fire".
[^1-16]: Literally "Because you sent messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, is it because there is no God in Israel to inquire of his word?"
[^1-17]: Literally "according to the word of Yahweh that Elijah had spoken".
[^1-18]: Ahaziah's brother, another son of Ahab (3:1). He is also called Joram (8:16; 9:14–24).
[^1-19]: The king of Judah with the same name is also called Joram (8:21–24). 3:1 dates the Israelite Jehoram's accession instead to the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat.
[^1-20]: Literally "the book of the matters of the days of the kings of Israel", an official royal record that has not survived. It is not the biblical book of Chronicles.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** When Yahweh[^2-1] was about to take Elijah up to heaven in a whirlwind, Elijah and Elisha were on their way from Gilgal.[^2-2]

**2** Elijah said to Elisha, "Please stay here, for Yahweh has sent me on to Bethel." But Elisha said, "As Yahweh lives, and as you yourself live,[^2-3] I will not leave you." So they went down to Bethel.

**3** The company of prophets[^2-4] who were at Bethel came out to Elisha and said to him, "Do you know that today Yahweh is going to take your master away from you?"[^2-5] He said, "Yes, I know. Be quiet."

**4** Elijah said to him, "Elisha, please stay here, for Yahweh has sent me on to Jericho." But he said, "As Yahweh lives, and as you yourself live, I will not leave you." So they came to Jericho.

**5** The company of prophets who were at Jericho came up to Elisha and said to him, "Do you know that today Yahweh is going to take your master away from you?" He said, "Yes, I know. Be quiet."

**6** Then Elijah said to him, "Please stay here, for Yahweh has sent me on to the Jordan." But he said, "As Yahweh lives, and as you yourself live, I will not leave you." So the two of them went on.

**7** Fifty men of the company of prophets went too, and they stood at a distance, facing them, while the two of them stood by the Jordan.

**8** Elijah took his cloak, rolled it up and struck the water. The water divided to one side and the other, and the two of them crossed over on dry ground.

**9** When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, "Tell me what I can do for you before I am taken from you." Elisha said, "Please let me have a double share[^2-6] of your spirit."

**10** Elijah said, "You have asked for something hard. If you see me as I am taken from you, it will be yours. But if not, it will not."

**11** As they were walking along and talking, suddenly a chariot of fire and horses of fire appeared and separated the two of them, and Elijah went up to heaven in the whirlwind.

**12** Elisha saw it, and he cried out, "My father, my father! The chariots of Israel and its horsemen!"[^2-7] And he saw him no more. Then he took hold of his own clothes and tore them in two.

**13** He picked up Elijah's cloak, which had fallen from him, and went back and stood on the bank of the Jordan.

**14** He took Elijah's cloak that had fallen from him and struck the water. He said, "Where is Yahweh, the God of Elijah?" When he too had struck the water,[^2-8] it divided to one side and the other, and Elisha crossed over.

**15** The company of prophets from Jericho, who were facing him, saw him and said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha." They came to meet him and bowed down to the ground before him.

**16** They said to him, "Look, your servants have fifty strong men with them. Please let them go and search for your master. Perhaps the Spirit of Yahweh[^2-9] has picked him up and thrown him down on one of the mountains or in one of the valleys." He said, "Do not send them."

**17** But they kept pressing him until he was embarrassed to refuse,[^2-10] and he said, "Send them." So they sent fifty men, and they searched for three days but did not find him.

**18** They came back to Elisha while he was staying at Jericho, and he said to them, "Didn't I tell you not to go?"

**19** The men of the city said to Elisha, "Look, the city is well situated, as my lord can see, but the water is bad and the land is unfruitful."[^2-11]

**20** He said, "Bring me a new bowl and put salt in it." So they brought it to him.

**21** He went out to the spring and threw the salt into it, and said, "This is what Yahweh says: I have healed this water. Never again will it cause death or unfruitfulness."[^2-12]

**22** And the water has stayed healed to this day, just as Elisha had said.

**23** From there Elisha went up to Bethel. As he was going up the road, some young boys[^2-13] came out of the town and jeered at him. They said to him, "Go on up, baldhead! Go on up, baldhead!"[^2-14]

**24** He turned around, looked at them and cursed them in the name of Yahweh. Then two female bears came out of the woods and mauled forty-two of the youths.

**25** From there he went on to Mount Carmel, and from there he returned to Samaria.

[^2-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-2]: Probably not the Gilgal near Jericho, since from it they "went down" to Bethel (verse 2). It may be a town in the hills north of Bethel.
[^2-3]: Literally "and as your soul lives". Hebrew *nephesh*, "life, self". Also in verses 4 and 6.
[^2-4]: Literally "the sons of the prophets": a community of prophets, perhaps disciples gathered around a leading prophet. Also in verses 5, 7 and 15.
[^2-5]: Literally "from over your head", that is, from his place as your master. Also in verse 5.
[^2-6]: Literally "a mouth of two", the share of the firstborn son, who inherited twice as much as each of his brothers (Deuteronomy 21:17).
[^2-7]: Or "The chariot of Israel and its horsemen!" Elisha speaks of Elijah as Israel's true defense, worth more than its army. The same words are said of Elisha in 13:14.
[^2-8]: Or "Where is Yahweh, the God of Elijah, he himself?" When he struck the water … The Hebrew words translated "he too" can be read with either sentence.
[^2-9]: Or "the wind of Yahweh". Hebrew *ruach* means both.
[^2-10]: Literally "until shame".
[^2-11]: Hebrew *meshakkelet*, "causing bereavement": causing miscarriage or the loss of young, whether of people, animals or crops. Also in verse 21.
[^2-12]: Literally "There will no longer be from there death and unfruitfulness."
[^2-13]: Hebrew *ne'arim*, used of anyone from a small child to a young man. In verse 24 they are called *yeladim*, "boys, youths".
[^2-14]: "Go up" may mock the report that Elijah had gone up to heaven.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Jehoram son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned twelve years.[^3-1]

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh,[^3-2] but not like his father and his mother. He removed the standing stone[^3-3] of Baal that his father had made.

**3** But he held on to the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, which he had caused Israel to commit.[^3-4] He did not turn away from them.[^3-5]

**4** Now Mesha king of Moab was a sheep breeder,[^3-6] and he used to pay the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and the wool of a hundred thousand rams.

**5** But when Ahab died, the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel.

**6** So at that time[^3-7] King Jehoram set out from Samaria and mustered all Israel.

**7** He went and sent this message to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: "The king of Moab has rebelled against me. Will you go with me to war against Moab?" Jehoshaphat said, "I will go up. I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses."

**8** He asked, "Which way shall we go up?" He answered, "By the road through the wilderness of Edom."

**9** So the king of Israel set out, with the king of Judah and the king of Edom.[^3-8] They marched around for seven days, and there was no water for the army or for the animals that followed them.[^3-9]

**10** The king of Israel said, "Oh no! Yahweh has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab!"

**11** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no prophet of Yahweh here, so that we may consult Yahweh through him?" One of the officers[^3-10] of the king of Israel answered, "Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water on Elijah's hands."[^3-11]

**12** Jehoshaphat said, "The word of Yahweh is with him." So the king of Israel, Jehoshaphat and the king of Edom went down to him.

**13** Elisha said to the king of Israel, "What do you want with me?[^3-12] Go to your father's prophets and your mother's prophets." But the king of Israel said to him, "No, for Yahweh has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab."

**14** Elisha said, "As Yahweh of Armies[^3-13] lives, whom I serve,[^3-14] if I did not have respect for Jehoshaphat king of Judah,[^3-15] I would not look at you or even notice you.

**15** But now bring me a musician." And while the musician was playing, the hand of Yahweh[^3-16] came on Elisha.

**16** He said, "This is what Yahweh says: Dig this valley[^3-17] full of ditches.[^3-18]

**17** For this is what Yahweh says: You will see no wind and you will see no rain, yet this valley will be filled with water, and you will drink, you and your livestock and your other animals.

**18** This is a small thing in the eyes of Yahweh. He will also hand Moab over to you.

**19** You will strike down every fortified city and every choice city. You will cut down every good tree, stop up all the springs of water and ruin every good field with stones."

**20** In the morning, at the time when the grain offering is presented,[^3-19] water suddenly came flowing from the direction of Edom, and the land was filled with water.

**21** Now all Moab had heard that the kings had come up to fight against them. So every man old enough to put on a sword belt was called out,[^3-20] and they took their stand at the border.

**22** When they got up early in the morning, the sun was shining on the water, and the Moabites saw the water across from them, red as blood.[^3-21]

**23** They said, "This is blood! The kings must have fought each other and killed one another. Now, Moab, to the plunder!"

**24** But when they came to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rose up and struck down the Moabites, and they fled before them. Then the Israelites struck at the land,[^3-22] slaughtering Moab.

**25** They tore down the cities, and on every good field each man threw his stone, until they had filled it. They stopped up every spring of water and cut down every good tree. Only in Kir-hareseth[^3-23] were its stones left standing,[^3-24] but the slingers surrounded it and attacked it.

**26** When the king of Moab saw that the battle was going against him,[^3-25] he took seven hundred swordsmen with him to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not.

**27** Then he took his firstborn son, who was to become king in his place, and offered him as a burnt offering on the wall. And there was great anger against Israel,[^3-26] so they withdrew from him and returned to their own land.

[^3-1]: On this king's name, see 1:17.
[^3-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3-3]: Hebrew *matsevah*, a stone pillar set up as an object of worship.
[^3-4]: Jeroboam, the first king of the northern kingdom, set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan (1 Kings 12:26–33).
[^3-5]: Literally "from it".
[^3-6]: Hebrew *noqed*, the word used of the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1). Mesha's own account of his revolt against Israel survives in a Moabite inscription of the 9th century BC, the Mesha Stele.
[^3-7]: Literally "on that day".
[^3-8]: Edom was then ruled by a deputy, not a king of its own line (1 Kings 22:48, 22:47 in most English Bibles).
[^3-9]: Literally "the animals that were at their feet".
[^3-10]: Or "servants". Hebrew *avadim* means servants of any rank, including the king's officials.
[^3-11]: That is, he had been Elijah's personal attendant.
[^3-12]: Literally "What is there between me and you?"
[^3-13]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^3-14]: Literally "before whom I stand".
[^3-15]: Literally "if I were not lifting up the face of Jehoshaphat king of Judah".
[^3-16]: A way of speaking of God's power coming on a prophet.
[^3-17]: Hebrew *nachal*, a wadi: a valley with a stream that is dry except in the rainy season. Also in verse 17.
[^3-18]: Literally "Making this wadi ditches, ditches." Or "I will make this valley full of pools."
[^3-19]: Literally "at the going up of the offering": the regular morning sacrifice (Exodus 29:38–41).
[^3-20]: Literally "they were called out, from everyone who girds on a belt and upward".
[^3-21]: Hebrew *adom*, "red", and *dam*, "blood", sound like *Edom*, the direction the water came from (verse 20).
[^3-22]: The Hebrew text is written "they went into it" but read "they struck it". "It" is the land of Moab.
[^3-23]: The capital of Moab, also called Kir-heres (Isaiah 16:7, 11).
[^3-24]: Literally "until he left its stones in Kir-hareseth". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^3-25]: Literally "that the battle was too strong for him".
[^3-26]: The Hebrew does not say whose anger this was.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** The wife of one of the company of prophets[^4-1] cried out to Elisha, "Your servant my husband is dead, and you know that your servant feared Yahweh.[^4-2] But now the creditor has come to take my two children as his slaves."

**2** Elisha said to her, "What shall I do for you? Tell me, what do you have in the house?" She said, "Your servant has nothing at all in the house except a small jar of oil."[^4-3]

**3** He said, "Go and borrow containers from outside, from all your neighbors, empty containers. Don't get just a few.

**4** Then go in and shut the door behind you and your sons. Pour oil into all these containers, and as each one is filled, set it aside."

**5** So she left him and shut the door behind her and her sons. They kept bringing containers to her, and she kept pouring.

**6** When the containers were full, she said to her son, "Bring me another container." But he said to her, "There are no more containers." Then the oil stopped flowing.

**7** She went and told the man of God, and he said, "Go, sell the oil and pay your debt. You and your sons can live on what is left."

**8** One day Elisha went over to Shunem. A wealthy woman[^4-4] lived there, and she urged him to stay for a meal. So whenever he passed by, he would stop there for a meal.

**9** She said to her husband, "Look, I am sure that this man who keeps passing our way is a holy man of God.

**10** Let us make a small walled room on the roof and put a bed, a table, a chair and a lamp there for him. Then whenever he comes to us, he can stay there."

**11** One day he came there, and he went into the upper room and lay down there.

**12** He said to Gehazi his servant,[^4-5] "Call this Shunammite woman." He called her, and she stood before him.

**13** Elisha said to Gehazi, "Tell her, 'Look, you have gone to all this trouble for us.[^4-6] What can be done for you? Can we speak on your behalf to the king or to the commander of the army?'" She said, "I live among my own people."[^4-7]

**14** Elisha said, "Then what can be done for her?" Gehazi said, "Well, she has no son, and her husband is old."

**15** Elisha said, "Call her." He called her, and she stood in the doorway.

**16** Elisha said, "At this season next year,[^4-8] you will be holding a son in your arms." She said, "No, my lord, man of God, do not lie to your servant!"

**17** But the woman became pregnant, and she gave birth to a son at that season the following year, as Elisha had told her.

**18** The child grew. One day he went out to his father, who was with the reapers.

**19** He said to his father, "My head! My head!" His father said to a servant, "Carry him to his mother."

**20** The servant picked him up and brought him to his mother. The boy sat on her lap until noon, and then he died.

**21** She went up and laid him on the bed of the man of God. Then she shut the door on him and went out.

**22** She called to her husband and said, "Please send me one of the servants and one of the donkeys, so that I can hurry to the man of God and come back."

**23** He said, "Why are you going to him today? It is not the New Moon or the Sabbath." She said, "All is well."[^4-9]

**24** She saddled the donkey and said to her servant, "Drive on fast. Do not slow down for me unless I tell you."

**25** So she set out and came to the man of God at Mount Carmel. When the man of God saw her in the distance, he said to Gehazi his servant, "Look, there is the Shunammite.

**26** Now run to meet her and ask her, 'Is all well with you? Is all well with your husband? Is all well with the child?'" She said, "All is well."

**27** But when she came to the man of God on the mountain, she took hold of his feet. Gehazi came near to push her away, but the man of God said, "Leave her alone, for she is in bitter distress,[^4-10] and Yahweh has hidden it from me and has not told me."

**28** She said, "Did I ask my lord for a son? Didn't I say, 'Do not raise my hopes'?"

**29** Elisha said to Gehazi, "Tuck up your robe,[^4-11] take my staff in your hand and go. If you meet anyone, do not greet him, and if anyone greets you, do not answer him. Lay my staff on the boy's face."

**30** But the boy's mother said, "As Yahweh lives, and as you yourself live,[^4-12] I will not leave you." So he got up and followed her.

**31** Gehazi went on ahead of them and laid the staff on the boy's face, but there was no sound and no sign of life.[^4-13] So he went back to meet Elisha and told him, "The boy has not woken up."

**32** When Elisha came into the house, there was the boy, dead, laid on his bed.

**33** He went in and shut the door on the two of them, and he prayed to Yahweh.

**34** Then he got up on the bed and lay on the child. He put his mouth on his mouth, his eyes on his eyes and his hands on his hands,[^4-14] and as he stretched himself out over him,[^4-15] the child's body grew warm.

**35** Elisha got up again and walked once back and forth in the house. Then he went up and stretched himself out over him again. The boy sneezed seven times and opened his eyes.

**36** Elisha called Gehazi and said, "Call this Shunammite." He called her, and when she came to him, he said, "Pick up your son."

**37** She came in, fell at his feet and bowed down to the ground. Then she picked up her son and went out.

**38** Elisha returned to Gilgal, and there was a famine in the land. While the company of prophets was sitting before him,[^4-16] he said to his servant, "Put the large pot on the fire and cook stew for the company of prophets."

**39** One of them went out into the field to gather herbs and found a wild vine. From it he gathered wild gourds,[^4-17] as many as his garment could hold. He came back and cut them up into the pot of stew, though they did not know what they were.

**40** They poured it out for the men to eat. But as they were eating the stew, they cried out, "Man of God, there is death in the pot!" And they could not eat it.

**41** Elisha said, "Bring some flour." He threw it into the pot and said, "Pour it out for the people to eat." And there was nothing harmful in the pot.

**42** A man came from Baal-shalishah, bringing the man of God bread from the first of the harvest: twenty loaves of barley bread, and fresh ears of grain in his sack.[^4-18] Elisha said, "Give it to the people to eat."

**43** His attendant said, "How can I set this before a hundred men?" He said, "Give it to the people to eat, for this is what Yahweh says: They will eat and have some left over."

**44** So he set it before them, and they ate and had some left over, as Yahweh had said.

[^4-1]: Literally "One woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets". On "the company of prophets", see 2:3.
[^4-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-3]: Hebrew *asukh*, a small jar or flask, perhaps for anointing oil.
[^4-4]: Literally "a great woman": a woman of standing and wealth.
[^4-5]: Hebrew *na'ar*, "young man", often used of a personal servant. Also in verses 19, 22, 24, 25 and 38.
[^4-6]: Literally "you have trembled for us with all this trembling".
[^4-7]: That is, she is secure among her own family and needs nothing from the king.
[^4-8]: Literally "At this appointed time, at the time of life". The same phrase is used in Genesis 18:10, 14. Also in verse 17.
[^4-9]: Hebrew *shalom*, "peace, well-being". Also in verse 26.
[^4-10]: Literally "for her soul is bitter to her".
[^4-11]: Literally "Gird up your loins", that is, tuck the long robe into the belt, ready to travel fast.
[^4-12]: Literally "and as your soul lives".
[^4-13]: Literally "no voice and no attention".
[^4-14]: The Hebrew text is written "his hand" but read "his hands".
[^4-15]: Hebrew *gahar*, a rare verb meaning to bend or crouch over (1 Kings 18:42). Also in verse 35.
[^4-16]: That is, as his disciples.
[^4-17]: Probably colocynth, a wild gourd that looks like a small melon and is a strong, dangerous purgative.
[^4-18]: The meaning of the Hebrew word translated "sack", *tsiqlon*, is uncertain.
