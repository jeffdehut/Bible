## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of the LORD[^1-1] that came to Joel[^1-2] son of Pethuel.

**2** Hear this, you elders! Listen, all who live in the land! Has anything like this happened in your days, or in the days of your ancestors?

**3** Tell your children about it, and let your children tell their children, and their children the next generation.

**4** What the cutting locust left, the swarming locust has eaten; what the swarming locust left, the hopping locust has eaten; and what the hopping locust left, the destroying locust has eaten.[^1-3]

**5** Wake up, you drunkards, and weep! Wail, all you wine drinkers, because of the sweet wine, for it has been cut off from your mouth.

**6** For a nation has come up against my land, mighty and without number. Its teeth are a lion's teeth, and it has the fangs of a lioness.

**7** It has laid waste my vine and splintered my fig tree. It has stripped off their bark and thrown it away; their branches are left white.

**8** Mourn like a young woman[^1-4] dressed in sackcloth for the husband of her youth.[^1-5]

**9** Grain offering and drink offering are cut off from the house of the LORD. The priests mourn, those who serve the LORD.

**10** The fields are ruined; the ground mourns,[^1-6] for the grain is ruined, the new wine dries up, and the olive oil fails.

**11** Be dismayed, you farmers;[^1-7] wail, you vinedressers, over the wheat and the barley, because the harvest of the field is lost.

**12** The vine has dried up, and the fig tree withers. Pomegranate, palm and apple tree, all the trees of the field have dried up. Surely joy has dried up and left the people.

**13** Put on sackcloth and lament, you priests! Wail, you who serve at the altar! Come, spend the night in sackcloth, you who serve my God! For grain offering and drink offering are withheld from the house of your God.

**14** Set apart a fast;[^1-8] call a solemn assembly. Gather the elders and all who live in the land to the house of the LORD your God, and cry out to the LORD.

**15** Alas for the day! For the day of the LORD is near; it comes like destruction from the Almighty.[^1-9]

**16** Has not food been cut off before our eyes, joy and gladness from the house of our God?

**17** The seeds shrivel under their clods.[^1-10] The storehouses are desolate; the granaries are torn down, for the grain has dried up.

**18** How the animals groan! The herds of cattle wander in confusion, because there is no pasture for them. Even the flocks of sheep suffer punishment.[^1-11]

**19** To you, LORD, I call, for fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and flame has burned up all the trees of the field.

**20** Even the wild animals long for you,[^1-12] because the streams of water have dried up, and fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

[^1-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-2]: Joel means "The LORD is God".
[^1-3]: Hebrew *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq* and *chasil*, four words for locusts. They may be different kinds of locust or stages in the insect's growth; the exact meanings are uncertain. *Arbeh* is the usual word for locust. Also in 2:25.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *betulah*, a young woman of marriageable age.
[^1-5]: Literally "for the *ba'al* of her youth", probably the man she was to marry.
[^1-6]: Or "dries up". The Hebrew verb can have either meaning.
[^1-7]: Or "Be ashamed". Hebrew *hovishu* sounds like *hovish*, "dries up", in verses 10 and 12.
[^1-8]: Literally "Sanctify a fast". Also in 2:15.
[^1-9]: A play on words: *shod*, "destruction", and *Shaddai*, "the Almighty", a title of God whose meaning is uncertain. The same line is found in Isaiah 13:6.
[^1-10]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Three of its words are found only here.
[^1-11]: Literally "are held guilty".
[^1-12]: Literally "pant for you".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Blow the horn in Zion; sound the alarm on my holy mountain! Let all who live in the land tremble, for the day of the LORD[^2-1] is coming; it is near:

**2** a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness. Like dawn spreading over the mountains[^2-2] a great and mighty people comes. There has never been anything like it, and there will never be again after it, through the years of all generations.

**3** Before them fire devours, and behind them a flame blazes. Before them the land is like the garden of Eden, and behind them a desolate wilderness. Nothing escapes them.

**4** Their appearance is like the appearance of horses, and they run like war horses.

**5** With a noise like chariots they leap on the tops of the mountains, like the crackling of a flame of fire devouring stubble, like a mighty people drawn up for battle.

**6** Before them peoples are in anguish; every face turns pale.[^2-3]

**7** They charge like warriors; they climb the wall like soldiers. Each goes on his way, and they do not swerve from their paths.

**8** They do not jostle one another; each goes in his own track. They burst through the weapons and are not stopped.[^2-4]

**9** They rush on the city; they run along the wall; they climb into the houses; they enter through the windows like a thief.

**10** Before them the earth quakes, the heavens tremble. Sun and moon grow dark, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**11** The LORD raises his voice before his army. His camp is very great; mighty is the one who carries out his word. For the day of the LORD is great and very terrible. Who can endure it?

**12** Yet even now, declares the LORD, return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping and with mourning.

**13** Tear your hearts and not your garments. Return to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in faithful love,[^2-5] and he relents from sending disaster.[^2-6]

**14** Who knows? He may turn and relent, and leave behind him a blessing: a grain offering and a drink offering for the LORD your God.

**15** Blow the horn in Zion! Set apart a fast; call a solemn assembly.

**16** Gather the people; consecrate the congregation. Assemble the elders; gather the children, even nursing infants. Let the bridegroom leave his room and the bride her bridal chamber.[^2-7]

**17** Between the porch and the altar let the priests, who serve the LORD, weep and say, "Spare your people, LORD. Do not make your inheritance a disgrace, a byword among the nations.[^2-8] Why should they say among the peoples, 'Where is their God?'"

**18** Then the LORD became jealous for his land and took pity on his people.[^2-9]

**19** The LORD answered his people, "I am sending you grain, new wine and olive oil, and you will be satisfied with them. I will never again make you a disgrace among the nations.

**20** I will drive the northerner[^2-10] far from you, and banish him into a parched and desolate land, his front into the eastern sea and his rear into the western sea.[^2-11] His stench will rise; his foul smell will rise. For he has done great things!"[^2-12]

**21** Do not be afraid, land; rejoice and be glad, for the LORD has done great things!

**22** Do not be afraid, you wild animals, for the pastures of the wilderness are turning green. The tree bears its fruit; the fig tree and the vine give their full yield.

**23** Children of Zion, rejoice and be glad in the LORD your God, for he has given you the autumn rain in righteousness.[^2-13] He has poured down rain for you, the autumn rain and the spring rain, as before.[^2-14]

**24** The threshing floors will be full of grain; the vats will overflow with new wine and olive oil.

**25** I will repay you for the years that the swarming locust has eaten, the hopping locust, the destroying locust and the cutting locust,[^2-15] my great army that I sent against you.

**26** You will eat your fill and be satisfied, and you will praise the name of the LORD your God, who has dealt wonderfully with you. My people will never again be put to shame.

**27** Then you will know that I am in the middle of Israel, and that I am the LORD your God, and there is no other. My people will never again be put to shame.

[^2-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-2]: Or "Like blackness spread over the mountains", reading the same consonants as *shechor*, "blackness", instead of *shachar*, "dawn".
[^2-3]: Literally "all faces gather a glow". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain (compare Nahum 2:11; 2:10 in most English Bibles).
[^2-4]: Or "and do not break ranks". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^2-6]: Compare Exodus 34:6–7; Jonah 4:2.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *chuppah*, a covering or canopy (see Isaiah 4:5).
[^2-8]: Or "for the nations to rule over them".
[^2-9]: Or "Then the LORD will be jealous for his land and take pity on his people." The Hebrew verbs are in the form usually used to narrate past events.
[^2-10]: Hebrew *ha-tsefoni*, "the northern one": the locust army, pictured as an invader from the north (compare Jeremiah 1:14; Ezekiel 38:6, 15).
[^2-11]: The Dead Sea and the Mediterranean.
[^2-12]: Here perhaps "for he has acted proudly", of the northern army. In verse 21 the same words are used of the LORD.
[^2-13]: Or "he has given you the teacher of righteousness". Hebrew *ha-moreh* can mean "the autumn rain" or "the teacher" (compare Hosea 10:12). *In righteousness* may mean "in due measure" or "as a sign of his faithfulness".
[^2-14]: Or "in the first month". Hebrew *malqosh*, the spring rain of March and April, and *moreh*, the autumn rain that begins the growing season.
[^2-15]: See 1:4.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** After this I will pour out my Spirit[^3-1] on all people.[^3-2] Your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your old men will dream dreams, and your young men will see visions.

**2** Even on the male and female servants[^3-3] in those days I will pour out my Spirit.

**3** I will show wonders in the heavens and on the earth: blood and fire and columns of smoke.

**4** The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and terrible day of the LORD[^3-4] comes.

**5** And everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will be saved,[^3-5] for on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be those who escape, as the LORD has said, and among the survivors will be those whom the LORD calls.

[^3-1]: Verses 1–5 are 2:28–32 in most English Bibles. Peter quotes them at Pentecost in Acts 2:17–21, following the Septuagint, with "in the last days" for "after this" and some other changes. *My Spirit*: or "my spirit", Hebrew *ruach*.
[^3-2]: Literally "all flesh".
[^3-3]: Or "slaves". Acts 2:18 has "my servants".
[^3-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-5]: Or "will escape". The first half of the verse is quoted in Acts 2:21 and Romans 10:13.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** For look, in those days and at that time, when I restore the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem,[^4-1]

**2** I will gather all the nations and bring them down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat.[^4-2] There I will enter into judgment with them on behalf of my people, my inheritance Israel, whom they scattered among the nations, and because they divided up my land.

**3** They cast lots for my people. They traded a boy for a prostitute, and sold a girl for wine and drank it.

**4** What are you to me, Tyre and Sidon and all the regions of Philistia? Are you paying me back for something? If you are paying me back, I will swiftly and speedily return your deeds on your own heads.

**5** For you took my silver and my gold, and carried my rich treasures into your temples.[^4-3]

**6** You sold the people of Judah and Jerusalem to the Greeks,[^4-4] to remove them far from their own territory.

**7** Look, I am going to rouse them from the place where you sold them, and I will return your deeds on your own heads.

**8** I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the people of Judah, and they will sell them to the Sabeans,[^4-5] to a nation far away, for the LORD[^4-6] has spoken.

**9** Proclaim this among the nations: Prepare for war![^4-7] Rouse the warriors! Let all the soldiers draw near and come up!

**10** Beat your plowshares into swords and your pruning knives into spears.[^4-8] Let the weak say, "I am a warrior!"

**11** Hurry[^4-9] and come, all you nations around, and gather there. Bring down your warriors, LORD!

**12** Let the nations rouse themselves and come up to the Valley of Jehoshaphat, for there I will sit to judge all the nations around.

**13** Swing the sickle, for the harvest is ripe. Come, tread the grapes, for the winepress is full. The vats overflow, for their wickedness is great.[^4-10]

**14** Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision![^4-11] For the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision.

**15** Sun and moon grow dark, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**16** The LORD roars from Zion and raises his voice from Jerusalem,[^4-12] and the heavens and the earth shake. But the LORD is a refuge for his people, a stronghold for the Israelites.

**17** Then you will know that I am the LORD your God, who lives in Zion, my holy mountain. Jerusalem will be holy, and foreigners will never again pass through it.

**18** On that day the mountains will drip sweet wine, and the hills will flow with milk. All the streambeds of Judah will flow with water, and a spring will come out from the house of the LORD and water the Valley of Shittim.[^4-13]

**19** Egypt will become a desolation, and Edom a desolate wilderness, because of the violence done to the people of Judah, in whose land they shed innocent blood.

**20** But Judah will be inhabited forever, and Jerusalem from generation to generation.

**21** I will avenge their blood, which I have not yet avenged.[^4-14] The LORD lives in Zion.

[^4-1]: Verses 1–21 are 3:1–21 in most English Bibles.
[^4-2]: Jehoshaphat means "The LORD judges". The valley is not otherwise known; the name fits the judgment that takes place there (verse 12). Since the 4th century AD it has been identified with the Kidron Valley east of Jerusalem.
[^4-3]: Or "your palaces". Hebrew *heikhalot*.
[^4-4]: Hebrew *Yevanim*, the Ionians: the Greeks.
[^4-5]: The people of Sheba in southern Arabia, traders who traveled great distances (1 Kings 10:1–2; Job 1:15).
[^4-6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-7]: Literally "Sanctify war", that is, prepare for battle with the rites that went before it.
[^4-8]: The words reverse Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3.
[^4-9]: The meaning of the Hebrew *ushu* is uncertain. It may mean "hurry" or "come to help".
[^4-10]: Compare Revelation 14:14–20.
[^4-11]: Hebrew *charuts*, "decision" or "verdict". The word can also mean a threshing sledge (Isaiah 41:15).
[^4-12]: The same words begin the book of Amos (Amos 1:2).
[^4-13]: Or "the Valley of Acacias". Compare Ezekiel 47:1–12; Zechariah 14:8.
[^4-14]: Literally "I will declare innocent their blood that I have not declared innocent". The meaning is uncertain. It may be a question: "Shall I leave their blood unpunished? I will not." The Septuagint reads "I will avenge their blood, and I will not leave it unpunished".
