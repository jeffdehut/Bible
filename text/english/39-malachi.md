## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** An oracle.[^1-1] The word of the LORD[^1-2] to Israel through Malachi.[^1-3]

**2** I have loved you, says the LORD. But you say, "How have you loved us?" Was not Esau Jacob's brother? declares the LORD.[^1-4] Yet I loved Jacob,

**3** but Esau I hated.[^1-5] I have made his mountains a desolation and left his inheritance to the jackals of the desert.

**4** If Edom says, "We have been crushed, but we will rebuild the ruins," this is what the LORD of Armies[^1-6] says: They may build, but I will tear down. They will be called the Wicked Country, the people with whom the LORD is angry forever.

**5** Your own eyes will see it, and you will say, "Great is the LORD beyond the borders of Israel!"

**6** A son honors his father, and a servant his master. If I am a father, where is the honor due to me? If I am a master, where is the respect due to me? says the LORD of Armies to you, priests, who despise my name. But you say, "How have we despised your name?"

**7** By offering defiled food[^1-7] on my altar. But you say, "How have we defiled you?" By saying that the table of the LORD[^1-8] may be despised.

**8** When you offer blind animals as sacrifices, is that not wrong? When you offer the lame and the sick, is that not wrong?[^1-9] Try offering them to your governor![^1-10] Would he be pleased with you or show you favor?[^1-11] says the LORD of Armies.

**9** So now, plead with God[^1-12] to be gracious to us! With such offerings from your hands, will he show favor to any of you? says the LORD of Armies.

**10** If only one of you would shut the temple doors, so that you would not light useless fires on my altar! I take no pleasure in you, says the LORD of Armies, and I will not accept an offering[^1-13] from your hands.

**11** For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations. In every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering. For my name is great among the nations, says the LORD of Armies.[^1-14]

**12** But you profane it when you say that the Lord's[^1-15] table is defiled, and its fruit, its food, may be despised.[^1-16]

**13** And you say, "What a weariness this is!" and you sniff at it,[^1-17] says the LORD of Armies. You bring what has been stolen,[^1-18] and the lame and the sick, and you bring these as an offering! Should I accept it from your hands? says the LORD.

**14** Cursed be the cheat who has a male in his flock and vows to give it, but then sacrifices to the Lord one that is blemished! For I am a great King, says the LORD of Armies, and my name is feared among the nations.

[^1-1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden". Compare Zechariah 9:1; 12:1.
[^1-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-3]: *Malachi* means "my messenger" (3:1), and may be a title rather than a name. The Septuagint reads "by the hand of his messenger". The book seems to come from the fifth century BC, after the temple had been rebuilt in 515 BC (Ezra 6:15), when a Persian governor ruled Judah (verse 8). It is set out as a series of disputes, in which the LORD quotes the people's objections and answers them.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^1-5]: The language of choosing: The LORD chose Jacob and passed over Esau (compare Genesis 25:23; 29:30–31; Deuteronomy 21:15). Romans 9:13 quotes verses 2–3.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. The title is used more than twenty times in Malachi.
[^1-7]: Literally "defiled bread", the sacrifices offered on the altar as the LORD's food (Leviticus 21:6).
[^1-8]: The altar. Also in verse 12.
[^1-9]: Literally "there is no evil", perhaps quoting the priests: "There is nothing wrong with that!" Also later in the verse. The law forbade the offering of animals with defects (Leviticus 22:18–25; Deuteronomy 15:21).
[^1-10]: Hebrew *pechah*, the governor of the Persian province of Judah (Haggai 1:1).
[^1-11]: Literally "lift up your face".
[^1-12]: Literally "soften the face of God", spoken with irony.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering. Also in verses 11 and 13.
[^1-14]: The Hebrew has no verb in the main clauses, so the verse may also be translated in the future: "my name will be great among the nations".
[^1-15]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH. Also in verse 14.
[^1-16]: Literally "and its fruit is despised, its food". The meaning of the word for fruit, *niv*, is uncertain.
[^1-17]: This is one of the *tiqqune sopherim*, the rabbinic "corrections of the scribes": the tradition says that the text once read "you sniff at me" and was changed out of reverence for God.
[^1-18]: Or "what was torn by wild animals". Hebrew *gazul*, "seized by force".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** And now, priests, this command is for you.

**2** If you will not listen, and if you will not take it to heart to give honor to my name, says the LORD of Armies,[^2-1] I will send the curse on you and curse your blessings.[^2-2] In fact, I have already cursed them, because you are not taking it to heart.

**3** Look, I am going to rebuke your offspring,[^2-3] and I will spread dung on your faces, the dung of your festival sacrifices,[^2-4] and you will be carried off with it.

**4** Then you will know that I have sent you this command, so that my covenant with Levi may continue,[^2-5] says the LORD of Armies.

**5** My covenant with him was one of life and peace, and I gave them to him. It called for reverence, and he revered me and stood in awe of my name.

**6** True instruction[^2-6] was in his mouth, and no wrong was found on his lips. He walked with me in peace and uprightness, and he turned many away from wrongdoing.

**7** For the lips of a priest should guard knowledge, and people should seek instruction from his mouth, because he is the messenger[^2-7] of the LORD of Armies.

**8** But you have turned aside from the way. You have made many stumble by your instruction. You have corrupted the covenant of Levi, says the LORD of Armies.

**9** So I in turn have made you despised and humiliated before all the people, because you have not kept my ways but have shown partiality in your instruction.[^2-8]

**10** Have we not all one father?[^2-9] Has not one God created us? Why then are we faithless to one another,[^2-10] profaning the covenant of our ancestors?

**11** Judah has been faithless, and a detestable thing has been done in Israel and in Jerusalem. For Judah has profaned the holy place of the LORD, which he loves,[^2-11] and has married the daughter of a foreign god.[^2-12]

**12** May the LORD cut off from the tents of Jacob any man who does this, whoever he may be,[^2-13] even if he brings an offering to the LORD of Armies.

**13** And this is another thing you do: you cover the altar of the LORD with tears, with weeping and groaning, because he no longer looks on the offering or accepts it with pleasure from your hands.

**14** But you say, "Why?" Because the LORD has been a witness between you and the wife of your youth, to whom you have been faithless, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant.[^2-14]

**15** Has not the One made them, and are not flesh and spirit his?[^2-15] And what does the One seek? Godly offspring.[^2-16] So guard yourselves in your spirit, and let no one be faithless to the wife of his youth.

**16** For the man who hates and divorces,[^2-17] says the LORD, the God of Israel, covers his garment with violence,[^2-18] says the LORD of Armies. So guard yourselves in your spirit, and do not be faithless.

**17** You have wearied the LORD with your words. But you say, "How have we wearied him?" By saying, "Everyone who does evil is good in the eyes of the LORD, and he delights in them," or "Where is the God of justice?"

[^2-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^2-2]: Perhaps the blessings that the priests pronounce on the people (Numbers 6:22–27), or the gifts they receive.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *zera*, "seed, offspring". The same consonants can be read *zeroa*, "arm" or "shoulder", the priests' portion of the sacrifices (Deuteronomy 18:3), and the Septuagint reads "the shoulder".
[^2-4]: Hebrew *peresh*, the dung in the entrails of the sacrificed animals, which was carried out of the camp and burned (Exodus 29:14). *You will be carried off with it* is literally "he will carry you to it".
[^2-5]: Literally "to be my covenant with Levi", the covenant with the priestly tribe (Numbers 25:12–13; Deuteronomy 33:8–11; Jeremiah 33:21).
[^2-6]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law". Also in verses 7–9.
[^2-7]: Hebrew *mal'akh*, "messenger", the word in Malachi's name (1:1) and in 3:1.
[^2-8]: Literally "lifting faces in the instruction", favoring some people over others when giving rulings.
[^2-9]: Probably God (1:6), or perhaps Abraham or Jacob.
[^2-10]: Literally "each to his brother".
[^2-11]: Or "has profaned what is holy to the LORD, whom he loves", that is, the people themselves.
[^2-12]: A woman who worships another god. Compare Ezra 9–10; Nehemiah 13:23–27.
[^2-13]: Literally "one who wakes and one who answers", an idiom of uncertain meaning, perhaps "anyone at all".
[^2-14]: Marriage is called a covenant also in Proverbs 2:17 and Ezekiel 16:8.
[^2-15]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult, and every translation of it is uncertain. Literally "And not one he made, and a remnant of spirit to him". The text is pointed *she'ar*, "remnant"; the same consonants can be read *she'er*, "flesh", as here. Or "Did he not make them one, with flesh and spirit?" (compare Genesis 2:24), or "No one who has a remnant of the spirit would do this".
[^2-16]: Literally "a seed of God".
[^2-17]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "For he hates, send away". Traditionally "For I hate divorce", with a small change to the Hebrew. The Septuagint reads "But if you hate her and send her away".
[^2-18]: Perhaps a picture of marriage, in which a man spread his garment over his wife (Ruth 3:9; Ezekiel 16:8). Here he covers it with violence instead.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Look, I am sending my messenger,[^3-1] and he will prepare the way before me. Then suddenly the Lord[^3-2] whom you seek will come to his temple, the messenger of the covenant, in whom you delight. Look, he is coming, says the LORD of Armies.[^3-3]

**2** But who can endure the day of his coming? Who can stand when he appears? For he will be like a refiner's fire and like launderers' soap.[^3-4]

**3** He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, and he will purify the sons of Levi[^3-5] and refine them like gold and silver. Then they will bring offerings[^3-6] to the LORD in righteousness.

**4** Then the offering of Judah and Jerusalem will be pleasing to the LORD, as in the days of old and as in former years.

**5** I will come near to you for judgment. I will be quick to testify against the sorcerers, the adulterers and those who swear falsely, against those who cheat hired workers of their wages[^3-7] and oppress the widow and the fatherless, and against those who deprive the foreigner of justice and do not fear me, says the LORD of Armies.

**6** For I, the LORD, do not change; and you, children of Jacob, have not come to an end.[^3-8]

**7** From the days of your ancestors you have turned aside from my decrees and have not kept them. Return to me, and I will return to you,[^3-9] says the LORD of Armies. But you say, "How shall we return?"

**8** Will a human being rob God?[^3-10] Yet you are robbing me. But you say, "How have we robbed you?" In tithes and contributions.[^3-11]

**9** You are cursed with a curse, yet you are robbing me, the whole nation of you.

**10** Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, so that there may be food in my house. Test me in this, says the LORD of Armies, and see if I will not open for you the windows of heaven[^3-12] and pour out for you a blessing until there is no more need.[^3-13]

**11** I will rebuke the devourer[^3-14] for you, so that it will not destroy the fruit of your ground, and your vine in the field will not lose its fruit, says the LORD of Armies.

**12** Then all the nations will call you blessed, for you will be a land of delight, says the LORD of Armies.

**13** Your words have been harsh against me,[^3-15] says the LORD. But you say, "What have we said against you?"

**14** You have said, "It is useless to serve God. What did we gain by keeping his charge[^3-16] and walking about in mourning before the LORD of Armies?

**15** So now we call the arrogant blessed. Those who do evil not only prosper; they test God and escape."

**16** Then those who feared the LORD spoke with one another. The LORD paid attention and heard, and a book of remembrance[^3-17] was written before him for those who feared the LORD and honored his name.[^3-18]

**17** They will be mine, says the LORD of Armies, my treasured possession,[^3-19] on the day that I act. I will spare them as a man spares his son who serves him.

**18** Then you will again see the difference between the righteous and the wicked, between the one who serves God and the one who does not serve him.

**19** For look, the day is coming, burning like an oven.[^3-20] All the arrogant and all who do evil will be stubble, and the day that is coming will set them on fire, says the LORD of Armies, so that it will leave them neither root nor branch.

**20** But for you who fear my name, the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings.[^3-21] You will go out and leap like calves let out of the stall.

**21** You will trample the wicked, for they will be ashes under the soles of your feet on the day that I act, says the LORD of Armies.

**22** Remember the Law[^3-22] of Moses my servant, the decrees and rulings that I commanded him at Horeb[^3-23] for all Israel.

**23** Look, I am going to send you Elijah the prophet[^3-24] before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.

**24** He will turn the hearts of fathers to their children and the hearts of children to their fathers,[^3-25] so that I will not come and strike the land with a curse of destruction.[^3-26]

[^3-1]: Hebrew *mal'akhi*, "my messenger", the same word as the name Malachi (1:1). Mark 1:2 quotes this line together with Exodus 23:20, "Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you. He will prepare your way," and the Gospels apply it to John the Baptist (Matthew 11:10; Luke 7:27). *Prepare* is the verb of Isaiah 40:3, "prepare the way for the LORD".
[^3-2]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name YHWH.
[^3-3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^3-4]: Hebrew *borit*, lye, an alkali used for washing clothes.
[^3-5]: The priests and Levites (2:4–9).
[^3-6]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering. Also in verse 4.
[^3-7]: Compare Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15.
[^3-8]: Or "you have not stopped being children of Jacob", that is, as deceitful as their ancestor (Genesis 27:36).
[^3-9]: The same words are found in Zechariah 1:3.
[^3-10]: Hebrew *qava*, "rob", a rare verb (Proverbs 22:23). The Septuagint reads "Will a person deceive God?" Also later in the verse and in verse 9.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *terumah*, the portion set apart from the offerings for the priests (Leviticus 7:14; Numbers 18:8–19). The tithes supported the Levites (Numbers 18:21–32; Nehemiah 13:10–12).
[^3-12]: The openings through which the rain was pictured as falling (Genesis 7:11; 2 Kings 7:2).
[^3-13]: Literally "until there is no more enough".
[^3-14]: Probably the locust.
[^3-15]: Literally "Your words have been strong against me".
[^3-16]: Hebrew *mishmeret*, "charge, duty" (Zechariah 3:7). *In mourning* is literally "in black", the clothing of mourners.
[^3-17]: A record of those who belong to the LORD (compare Exodus 32:32; Psalm 69:29, 69:28 in most English Bibles; Daniel 12:1). The kings of Persia kept such books (Esther 6:1–2).
[^3-18]: Literally "thought on his name".
[^3-19]: Hebrew *segullah*, a king's private treasure, the word used of Israel at Sinai (Exodus 19:5; Deuteronomy 7:6). Or "on the day when I make up my treasured possession".
[^3-20]: Verses 19–24 are 4:1–6 in most English Bibles.
[^3-21]: In the ancient Near East the sun was often pictured as a disk with wings. *Wings* may also mean the rays of the sun. *Righteousness* is Hebrew *tsedaqah*, here the justice that puts right what was wrong.
[^3-22]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law", here the Law given through Moses as a whole.
[^3-23]: Another name for Mount Sinai (Deuteronomy 4:10; 5:2).
[^3-24]: Elijah had been taken up to heaven without dying (2 Kings 2:11). The Gospels see this promise fulfilled in John the Baptist (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13; Mark 9:11–13; Luke 1:17). *The great and terrible day of the LORD* is also in Joel 3:4 (2:31 in most English Bibles).
[^3-25]: Luke 1:17 quotes the first half of this line.
[^3-26]: Hebrew *cherem*, the ban by which something was devoted to destruction (Zechariah 14:11). These verses close the Prophets, the second division of the Hebrew Bible, and in most Christian Bibles they close the Old Testament. In the synagogue reading, verse 23 is read again after verse 24, so that the book does not end with a curse.
