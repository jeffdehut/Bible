## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, a servant[^1-1] of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ,[^1-2] for the faith[^1-3] of God's chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness,

**2** in the hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began,[^1-4]

**3** and at the proper time[^1-5] he revealed his word in the proclamation with which I have been entrusted by command of God our Savior,

**4** to Titus, my true child in our common faith: Grace and peace[^1-6] from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

**5** This is why I left you in Crete, so that you might put in order what remained to be done, and appoint elders[^1-7] in every town as I directed you,

**6** if anyone is above reproach, the husband of one wife,[^1-8] with children who are believers[^1-9] and not open to the charge of reckless living or rebellion.

**7** For an overseer,[^1-10] as God's steward, must be above reproach, not arrogant, not quick-tempered, not a drunkard, not violent, not greedy for dishonest gain,

**8** but hospitable, a lover of what is good, self-controlled, upright, holy, disciplined,

**9** holding firmly to the trustworthy word as it was taught, so that he may be able both to encourage people with sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.

**10** For there are many who are rebellious, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision,[^1-11]

**11** who must be silenced, since they are upsetting whole households by teaching what they ought not to teach, for the sake of dishonest gain.

**12** One of them, a prophet of their own, said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons."[^1-12]

**13** This testimony is true. For this reason rebuke them sharply, so that they may be sound in the faith,

**14** not paying attention to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth.

**15** To the pure all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure; both their minds and their consciences are defiled.

**16** They claim to know God, but they deny him by their deeds. They are detestable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work.

[^1-1]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-2]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-3]: Or "in keeping with the faith". Greek *kata*.
[^1-4]: Literally "before eternal times".
[^1-5]: Literally "at his own times".
[^1-6]: Many manuscripts read "Grace, mercy and peace".
[^1-7]: Greek *presbyteroi*, "elders", leaders of the church.
[^1-8]: Literally "a man of one woman". The phrase may mean married only once, faithful to his wife, or not having more than one wife. Compare 1 Timothy 3:2.
[^1-9]: Or "who are faithful". Greek *pistos*, "believing" or "faithful".
[^1-10]: Greek *episkopos*, "overseer", traditionally "bishop". The passage moves from "elders" (verse 5) to "the overseer" here.
[^1-11]: That is, Jewish believers, or those who insisted on circumcision; compare verse 14.
[^1-12]: A line attributed to the Cretan poet Epimenides (about 600 BC). *Lazy gluttons*: literally "idle bellies".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** But as for you, speak what is consistent with sound teaching.

**2** Older men[^2-1] are to be sober-minded, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, in endurance.

**3** Older women likewise are to be reverent in behavior, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine. They are to teach what is good,

**4** so that they may train the young women to love their husbands and to love their children,

**5** to be self-controlled, pure, working at home,[^2-2] kind, submissive[^2-3] to their own husbands, so that the word of God may not be discredited.[^2-4]

**6** Likewise, urge the younger men to be self-controlled.

**7** In everything show yourself to be an example of good works; in your teaching show integrity, dignity,[^2-5]

**8** and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.

**9** Slaves[^2-6] are to submit to their own masters in everything; they are to please them, not to talk back,

**10** not to steal from them, but to show complete and genuine faithfulness, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching about God our Savior.

**11** For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people,[^2-7]

**12** training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly desires and to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives in the present age,

**13** as we wait for the blessed hope and the appearing[^2-8] of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ,[^2-9]

**14** who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people who are his own possession,[^2-10] eager to do good works.

**15** Speak these things; encourage and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

[^2-1]: Verses 2–10 give instructions to different groups in the church: older men, older women, younger women, younger men (with Titus himself as their example), and slaves. Compare Ephesians 5:21–6:9; Colossians 3:18–4:1. *Older men*: Greek *presbytai*, a different word from *presbyteroi*, "elders", in 1:5.
[^2-2]: Many manuscripts read "staying at home".
[^2-3]: Greek *hypotasso*, "to place oneself under, to be subject to"; also in verse 9 (of slaves) and in 3:1 (of all believers toward rulers and authorities). Compare Ephesians 5:21–24.
[^2-4]: Literally "blasphemed". Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^2-5]: Many manuscripts add "incorruptibility".
[^2-6]: Greek *douloi*, "slaves"; *masters* is *despotai*, "owners". Slavery was part of the social order of the Roman world, and some members of the churches were slaves (compare 1 Timothy 6:1–2).
[^2-7]: Or "has appeared to all people, bringing salvation".
[^2-8]: Greek *epiphaneia*, "appearance, manifestation", related to the verb "has appeared" in verse 11. *The appearing of the glory*: or "the glorious appearing".
[^2-9]: Or "of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ". In the Greek one article governs both "God" and "Savior", which many take to mean that both refer to Jesus Christ; others take them as two persons.
[^2-10]: Greek *laos periousios*, the phrase the Septuagint uses for Israel as Yahweh's "treasured possession" (Exodus 19:5; Deuteronomy 14:2). *Redeem us from all lawlessness*: compare Psalm 130:8; Ezekiel 37:23.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Remind them to be subject[^3-1] to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work,

**2** to slander no one, to avoid quarreling, to be considerate, and to show complete gentleness toward all people.

**3** For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to all kinds of desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hated and hating one another.

**4** But when the kindness and love for humankind[^3-2] of God our Savior appeared,

**5** he saved us, not because of works we had done in righteousness, but according to his mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit,[^3-3]

**6** whom he poured out on us richly[^3-4] through Jesus Christ our Savior,

**7** so that, having been declared righteous[^3-5] by his grace, we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

**8** The saying is trustworthy,[^3-6] and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works.[^3-7] These things are excellent and profitable for people.

**9** But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, quarrels and disputes about the Law, for they are unprofitable and worthless.

**10** As for a divisive person,[^3-8] after warning him once and then a second time, have nothing more to do with him,

**11** knowing that such a person is perverted and sinful; he is self-condemned.

**12** When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, do your best to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

**13** Do your best to send Zenas the lawyer[^3-9] and Apollos on their way, and see that they lack nothing.

**14** And let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works, so as to meet urgent needs, so that they will not be unfruitful.

**15** All who are with me greet you. Greet those who love us in the faith. Grace be with you all.[^3-10]

[^3-1]: Greek *hypotasso*, "to place oneself under, to be subject to", as in 2:5 and 2:9.
[^3-2]: Greek *philanthropia*, "love for human beings", the source of the word "philanthropy".
[^3-3]: Or "through the washing of rebirth and of renewal by the Holy Spirit", with one washing that brings both. Greek *palingenesia*, "new birth, regeneration".
[^3-4]: Compare Joel 3:1 (2:28 in most English Bibles).
[^3-5]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^3-6]: See 1 Timothy 1:15. The saying is probably the words of verses 4–7.
[^3-7]: Or "to engage in honorable occupations"; also in verse 14. Greek *proistemi*, "lead, care for, devote oneself to".
[^3-8]: Greek *hairetikos*, "factious, causing divisions", the source of the word "heretic".
[^3-9]: Greek *nomikos*, "legal expert", either in Roman law or in the Law of Moses.
[^3-10]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
