## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** There was a man in the land of Uz[^1-1] whose name was Job. That man was blameless[^1-2] and upright; he feared God and turned away from evil.

**2** Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**3** His livestock numbered seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred female donkeys, and he had a great many servants. That man was the greatest of all the people of the East.[^1-3]

**4** His sons used to go and hold a feast in the house of each one in turn,[^1-4] and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

**5** When the days of feasting had run their course, Job would send for them and purify them.[^1-5] He would get up early in the morning and offer burnt offerings for each one of them, for Job thought, "Perhaps my children[^1-6] have sinned and cursed[^1-7] God in their hearts." This is what Job always did.

**6** One day the sons of God[^1-8] came to present themselves before Yahweh,[^1-9] and the Accuser[^1-10] also came among them.

**7** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Where have you come from?" The Accuser answered Yahweh, "From roaming the earth and walking about on it."

**8** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Have you considered[^1-11] my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth, a blameless and upright man who fears God and turns away from evil."

**9** The Accuser answered Yahweh, "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?

**10** Have you not put a hedge around him and his household and everything he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock have spread out over the land.

**11** But stretch out your hand now and touch everything he has, and he will surely curse you to your face."[^1-12]

**12** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Very well, everything he has is in your power.[^1-13] Only do not stretch out your hand against him." Then the Accuser went out from the presence of Yahweh.

**13** One day, when Job's sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their oldest brother,

**14** a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys were grazing beside them,

**15** and the Sabeans[^1-14] swooped down and took them. They struck down the servants[^1-15] with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**16** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The fire of God[^1-16] fell from the sky and burned up the sheep and the servants and consumed them, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**17** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three raiding parties[^1-17] and made a raid on the camels and took them. They struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**18** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their oldest brother,

**19** and suddenly a great wind came from across the desert and struck the four corners of the house. It fell on the young people,[^1-18] and they died, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**20** Then Job got up, tore his robe and shaved his head. He fell to the ground and worshiped.[^1-19]

**21** He said, "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked I will return there.[^1-20] Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away. May the name of Yahweh be blessed."

**22** In all this Job did not sin, and he did not accuse God of wrongdoing.[^1-21]

[^1-1]: A region east or southeast of Israel, perhaps in Edom (Lamentations 4:21) or northern Arabia.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *tam*: whole, complete, a person of integrity. Also in verse 8.
[^1-3]: Literally "the sons of the east", the peoples east of Israel.
[^1-4]: Literally "in the house of each one, his day": perhaps each son's birthday (compare 3:1), or each one in turn.
[^1-5]: Literally "make them holy". He had them prepare to come before God.
[^1-6]: Literally "my sons".
[^1-7]: Literally "blessed". Here and in verse 11, 2:5 and 2:9, the Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse", to avoid writing the words "curse God".
[^1-8]: Literally "the sons of God" (Hebrew *bene ha-elohim*): heavenly beings who serve in God's court, as in 2:1 and 38:7 (compare Genesis 6:2; Psalm 29:1). The Greek Septuagint reads "the angels of God".
[^1-9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan".
[^1-11]: Literally "set your heart on".
[^1-12]: Literally "if he will not bless you to your face". On "bless" for "curse", see verse 5. The form "if not" is that of an oath: "I swear he will".
[^1-13]: Literally "in your hand".
[^1-14]: Hebrew *Sheva*, Sheba, a people of Arabia.
[^1-15]: Or "the young men". Also in verses 16 and 17.
[^1-16]: Probably lightning. Or "a tremendous fire".
[^1-17]: Literally "set three heads".
[^1-18]: Hebrew *ne'arim*, the word translated "servants" in verses 15–17.
[^1-19]: Or "bowed down", as in worship.
[^1-20]: Perhaps to the earth, pictured as the mother of all (compare Psalm 139:15), or to the grave.
[^1-21]: Literally "he did not give unseemliness to God". Hebrew *tiflah*: what is unfitting or offensive.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** One day the sons of God[^2-1] came to present themselves before Yahweh,[^2-2] and the Accuser[^2-3] also came among them to present himself before Yahweh.

**2** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Where have you come from?" The Accuser answered Yahweh, "From roaming the earth and walking about on it."

**3** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Have you considered my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth, a blameless and upright man who fears God and turns away from evil. He still holds firmly to his integrity, though you incited me against him to ruin him for no reason."[^2-4]

**4** The Accuser answered Yahweh, "Skin for skin![^2-5] A man will give everything he has for his life.

**5** But stretch out your hand now and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will surely curse you to your face."[^2-6]

**6** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Very well, he is in your power. Only spare his life."

**7** So the Accuser went out from the presence of Yahweh, and he struck Job with painful sores from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head.

**8** Job took a piece of broken pottery to scrape himself with as he sat among the ashes.

**9** His wife said to him, "Are you still holding firmly to your integrity? Curse God[^2-7] and die!"

**10** But he said to her, "You are talking like a foolish woman.[^2-8] Shall we accept the good from God, and not accept the bad?" In all this Job did not sin with his lips.

**11** When Job's three friends heard about all this trouble that had come on him, they came, each from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite.[^2-9] They arranged to meet and come to show him sympathy[^2-10] and comfort him.

**12** When they looked up from a distance, they did not recognize him. They wept aloud, and each one tore his robe, and they threw dust into the air over their heads.[^2-11]

**13** Then they sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights. No one said a word to him, for they saw that his pain was very great.

[^2-1]: Literally "the sons of God": heavenly beings who serve in God's court (see 1:6). The Greek Septuagint reads "the angels of God".
[^2-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan".
[^2-4]: Literally "to swallow him up". *For no reason* is Hebrew *chinnam*, the word the Accuser used in 1:9, "for nothing".
[^2-5]: A proverb whose exact sense is uncertain, perhaps "one skin for another": a person will give up anything to save his own skin.
[^2-6]: Literally "if he will not bless you to your face". The Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse" (see 1:5 and 1:11).
[^2-7]: Literally "Bless God". The Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse" (see 1:5).
[^2-8]: Hebrew *nevalot*, "foolish women", in the sense of senseless and godless.
[^2-9]: *Teman* was a region of Edom (Genesis 36:11, 34). *Shuah* was a son of Abraham and Keturah (Genesis 25:2). Where *Naamah* was is not known.
[^2-10]: Literally "to shake the head for him", a gesture of grief and sympathy.
[^2-11]: Literally "they threw dust on their heads toward the sky", a sign of mourning.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** After this Job opened his mouth and cursed the day he was born.[^3-1]

**2** Job said:[^3-2]

**3** "Let the day perish on which I was born, and the night that said, 'A boy has been conceived!'

**4** That day, let it be darkness! Let God[^3-3] above not seek it out, and let no light shine on it.

**5** Let darkness and deep darkness[^3-4] claim it;[^3-5] let a cloud settle over it; let the blackness of the day[^3-6] terrify it.

**6** That night, let thick darkness seize it! Let it not rejoice[^3-7] among the days of the year; let it not come into the count of the months.

**7** Yes, let that night be barren; let no shout of joy come into it.

**8** Let those who curse days curse it, those who are ready to rouse Leviathan.[^3-8]

**9** Let the stars of its dawn[^3-9] be dark; let it wait for light that does not come, and let it not see the eyelids of the morning,[^3-10]

**10** because it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb[^3-11] and hide trouble from my eyes.

**11** Why did I not die at birth,[^3-12] come out of the womb and breathe my last?

**12** Why were there knees to receive me, or breasts for me to nurse?

**13** For now I would be lying down in quiet; I would be asleep, and then I would be at rest

**14** with kings and counselors of the earth, who rebuilt ruins for themselves,[^3-13]

**15** or with princes who had gold, who filled their houses with silver.

**16** Or why was I not hidden away like a stillborn child,[^3-14] like infants who never see the light?

**17** There the wicked stop their raging, and there the weary find rest.[^3-15]

**18** Prisoners are at ease together; they no longer hear the voice of the slave driver.

**19** The small and the great are there alike, and the slave is free from his master.

**20** Why does he[^3-16] give light to the one who suffers, and life to the bitter in soul,

**21** who long for death, but it does not come, who dig for it more than for hidden treasure,

**22** who would rejoice and shout for joy,[^3-17] who would be glad to find the grave?

**23** Why give light to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in?[^3-18]

**24** For sighing comes to me in place of my food,[^3-19] and my groans pour out like water.

**25** For what I feared has come upon me, and what I dreaded has happened to me.

**26** I have no ease, I have no quiet, I have no rest, and turmoil has come."

[^3-1]: Literally "cursed his day". Here the Hebrew uses the ordinary word for "curse" (*qillel*).
[^3-2]: Literally "Job answered and said", a common way of beginning a speech.
[^3-3]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 23.
[^3-4]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death".
[^3-5]: Literally "redeem it", that is, take it back as their own. Hebrew *ga'al*. Some take the verb as a different *ga'al*, "defile it".
[^3-6]: Hebrew *kimrire yom*, a phrase of uncertain meaning: perhaps "the darkenings of the day", such as an eclipse, or "the bitterness of the day".
[^3-7]: The Hebrew vowel points read "let it not rejoice". The same consonants can be read "let it not be joined", which fits the parallel line.
[^3-8]: A sea monster, a figure of chaos in the poetry of the ancient Near East (40:25 (English 41:1); Psalm 74:14; Isaiah 27:1). Those who "curse days" may be sorcerers thought able to bring disaster. Some interpreters read *yam*, "the Sea", for *yom*, "day", in the first line, as a parallel to Leviathan.
[^3-9]: Hebrew *neshef*, "twilight", here the twilight before dawn.
[^3-10]: The first rays of the sun, pictured as the opening eyelids of the dawn.
[^3-11]: Literally "the doors of my belly".
[^3-12]: Literally "from the womb".
[^3-13]: Or "who built for themselves what are now ruins". Hebrew *choravot*, "ruins, desolate places". Some think of monuments or tombs built in desolate places.
[^3-14]: Literally "Or like a hidden miscarriage I would not be".
[^3-15]: Literally "those weary of strength".
[^3-16]: God is meant, though the Hebrew does not name him.
[^3-17]: Literally "who rejoice to rejoicing". Some read *gal*, "a heap (of stones)", for *gil*, "rejoicing": "who would rejoice over a burial mound".
[^3-18]: The verb echoes 1:10, where the Accuser says God has "put a hedge around" Job to protect him. Here the hedge hems him in.
[^3-19]: Literally "before my bread": either "instead of my food" or "at my meals".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered:

**2** "If someone ventures a word to you, will you lose patience? But who can hold back from speaking?

**3** Look, you have instructed many, and you have strengthened weak hands.

**4** Your words have kept the stumbling on their feet, and you have made firm the knees that gave way.

**5** But now it has come to you, and you lose patience; it touches you, and you are dismayed.

**6** Is not your fear of God[^4-1] your confidence, and the integrity of your ways your hope?

**7** Think now: who that was innocent ever perished? Where were the upright ever wiped out?

**8** As I have seen, those who plow wickedness and sow trouble reap it.

**9** At the breath of God[^4-2] they perish; at the blast of his anger they come to an end.

**10** The lion may roar, the fierce lion may growl, but the teeth of the young lions are broken.[^4-3]

**11** The lion perishes for lack of prey, and the cubs of the lioness are scattered.

**12** Now a word came to me in secret;[^4-4] my ear caught a whisper of it,

**13** amid troubled thoughts from visions of the night, when deep sleep falls on people.

**14** Dread came upon me, and trembling, and it made all my bones shake.

**15** A spirit[^4-5] passed before my face; the hair of my body stood on end.

**16** It stood still, but I could not make out its appearance. A form was before my eyes; there was silence, and I heard a voice:[^4-6]

**17** 'Can a mortal be righteous before God? Can a man be pure before his Maker?[^4-7]

**18** If he puts no trust in his servants, and charges his angels with error,[^4-8]

**19** how much more those who live in houses of clay, whose foundation is in the dust, who are crushed as easily as a moth![^4-9]

**20** Between morning and evening they are shattered; they perish forever, and no one notices.

**21** Is not their tent cord pulled up within them?[^4-10] They die, and not in wisdom.'

[^4-1]: Literally "your fear".
[^4-2]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 17.
[^4-3]: Literally "The roar of the lion, and the voice of the fierce lion, and the teeth of the young lions are broken". Verses 10–11 use five different Hebrew words for lions.
[^4-4]: Literally "was stolen to me".
[^4-5]: Or "a wind", "a breath". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^4-6]: Or "I heard a silent voice", "a whisper of a voice" (compare 1 Kings 19:12).
[^4-7]: Or "Can a mortal be more righteous than God? Can a man be purer than his Maker?"
[^4-8]: Hebrew *toholah*, a word found only here, perhaps "error" or "folly".
[^4-9]: Literally "before a moth": perhaps "like a moth", or "sooner than a moth".
[^4-10]: The life of a person is pictured as a tent that collapses when its cord is pulled up. Hebrew *yitram* may instead mean "their excellence": "Is not their excellence taken from them?" The words of the voice may end at verse 17 rather than here.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** "Call out now! Is there anyone who will answer you? To which of the holy ones[^5-1] will you turn?

**2** For resentment kills the fool, and jealousy slays the simple.

**3** I myself have seen a fool taking root, but suddenly I cursed his home.[^5-2]

**4** His children are far from safety; they are crushed in the gate,[^5-3] with no one to rescue them.

**5** The hungry eat his harvest and take it even from among the thorns, and the thirsty pant after their wealth.[^5-4]

**6** For trouble does not come out of the dust, and misery does not sprout from the ground.

**7** Rather, human beings are born to misery, as surely as sparks fly upward.[^5-5]

**8** But as for me, I would appeal to God, and to God I would commit my cause.[^5-6]

**9** He does great things beyond searching out, wonders beyond counting.

**10** He gives rain on the earth and sends water on the fields.

**11** He sets the lowly on high, and those who mourn are lifted to safety.

**12** He frustrates the plans of the crafty, so that their hands achieve no success.[^5-7]

**13** He catches the wise in their own craftiness,[^5-8] and the schemes of the devious are swept away.

**14** By day they run into darkness, and at noon they grope as if it were night.

**15** But he saves the needy from the sword of their mouth,[^5-9] and from the hand of the mighty.

**16** So the poor have hope, and injustice shuts its mouth.

**17** How happy is the one whom God[^5-10] corrects! So do not reject the discipline of the Almighty.[^5-11]

**18** For he wounds, but he binds up; he strikes, but his hands[^5-12] heal.

**19** He will rescue you from six troubles, and in seven no harm will touch you.[^5-13]

**20** In famine he will ransom you from death, and in war from the power of the sword.

**21** You will be hidden from the lash of the tongue, and you will not fear destruction when it comes.

**22** You will laugh at destruction and famine, and you will not fear the wild animals of the earth.

**23** For you will have a covenant with the stones of the field,[^5-14] and the wild animals of the field will be at peace with you.

**24** You will know that your tent is secure; you will inspect your home[^5-15] and find nothing missing.[^5-16]

**25** You will know that your descendants will be many, and your offspring like the grass of the earth.

**26** You will come to the grave in ripe old age,[^5-17] as sheaves of grain are brought up in their season.

**27** Look, we have searched this out; it is true. Hear it, and know it for yourself."

[^5-1]: Heavenly beings (compare 15:15; Psalm 89:6–8 (English 89:5–7)).
[^5-2]: Or "I declared his home cursed". The Hebrew word for "home" can also mean "pasture".
[^5-3]: The city gate, where legal cases were heard.
[^5-4]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. The middle line is literally "and to (or from) thorns he takes it". *The thirsty* reads Hebrew *tsammim* as a short spelling of *tsme'im*. Elsewhere *tsammim* means "snare" (18:9): "and a snare gapes for their wealth".
[^5-5]: Literally "and the sons of Resheph fly high". *Resheph* means "flame", and was also the name of a Canaanite god of plague. Or "as birds fly high". With a slight change of vowels, the first line can be read "human beings give birth to misery".
[^5-6]: The first line has *El*, the second *Elohim*, both "God".
[^5-7]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, "success" or "sound wisdom".
[^5-8]: 1 Corinthians 3:19 quotes this line.
[^5-9]: Literally "from the sword, from their mouth". Their words are pictured as a sword.
[^5-10]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^5-11]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^5-12]: The Hebrew text is written "his hand" but read "his hands".
[^5-13]: A numerical saying: "six, and even seven" means every one of them.
[^5-14]: That is, stones will not spoil your fields.
[^5-15]: Or "your pasture", "your fold".
[^5-16]: Literally "and not miss", a verb that usually means "sin".
[^5-17]: Hebrew *kelach*, a rare word, perhaps "full vigor" or "ripe old age".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Job answered:

**2** "If only my anguish could be weighed, and my calamity laid on the scales with it!

**3** For then it would be heavier than the sand of the seas. That is why my words have been wild.[^6-1]

**4** For the arrows of the Almighty[^6-2] are in me; my spirit drinks their poison. The terrors of God[^6-3] are lined up against me.

**5** Does a wild donkey bray when it has grass, or an ox bellow over its fodder?

**6** Can tasteless food be eaten without salt? Is there any flavor in the white of an egg?[^6-4]

**7** I refuse to touch them; they are like loathsome food to me.[^6-5]

**8** If only my request would be granted, and God would give me what I hope for:

**9** that God would be willing to crush me, that he would let loose his hand and cut me off!

**10** Then I would still have this comfort, and I would leap for joy[^6-6] in pain that does not spare me: that I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

**11** What strength do I have, that I should wait? What end is ahead of me, that I should be patient?[^6-7]

**12** Is my strength the strength of stones? Is my flesh bronze?

**13** Is it not that I have no help in myself, and any resource[^6-8] has been driven from me?

**14** One who is wasting away should have faithful love[^6-9] from his friend, even if he forsakes the fear of the Almighty.[^6-10]

**15** My brothers have been as treacherous as a wadi,[^6-11] like the channels of wadis that run away,

**16** which are dark with ice, where the snow piles up and hides itself,

**17** but when they are scorched[^6-12] they cease; when it is hot, they vanish from their place.

**18** Caravans turn aside from their route;[^6-13] they go up into the wasteland and perish.

**19** The caravans of Tema look for water; the travelers of Sheba hope for it.[^6-14]

**20** They are put to shame because they trusted; they arrive there and are dismayed.

**21** For now you have become like that;[^6-15] you see something terrible and are afraid.

**22** Have I said, 'Give me something,' or 'Pay a bribe for me from your wealth,'

**23** or 'Rescue me from the hand of the enemy,' or 'Ransom me from the hand of the ruthless'?

**24** Teach me, and I will be silent; make me understand where I have gone wrong.

**25** How painful are honest words![^6-16] But what does your arguing prove?[^6-17]

**26** Do you mean to correct my words, and treat the words of a desperate man as wind?

**27** You would even cast lots over an orphan,[^6-18] and bargain over your friend.

**28** But now, please, look at me. Would I lie to your face?

**29** Turn back, please; let there be no injustice. Turn back again; my righteousness is still at stake.[^6-19]

**30** Is there injustice on my tongue? Can my palate not taste what is ruinous?[^6-20]

[^6-1]: Hebrew *la'u*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "wild", "rash" or "stammering".
[^6-2]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. Also in verse 14.
[^6-3]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verses 8 and 9.
[^6-4]: Hebrew *rir challamut*, a phrase of uncertain meaning: perhaps "the white of an egg" or "the slime of the mallow plant".
[^6-5]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "They are like the sickness of my bread".
[^6-6]: Hebrew *salad*, a verb found only here. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "leap for joy", or "recoil", "shudder".
[^6-7]: Literally "that I should lengthen my soul".
[^6-8]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, "success, sound wisdom", as in 5:12.
[^6-9]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^6-10]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "To the one melting from his friend faithful love, and the fear of the Almighty he forsakes". Or "One who withholds faithful love from his friend forsakes the fear of the Almighty".
[^6-11]: A stream bed that runs with water in the rainy season and is dry in summer.
[^6-12]: Hebrew *zarav*, a verb found only here, perhaps "dry up", "flow away" or "be scorched".
[^6-13]: Or "The paths of their course twist and turn", of the streams themselves.
[^6-14]: *Tema* and *Sheba* were trading peoples of Arabia.
[^6-15]: The Hebrew text is written "you have become nothing" (*lo*, "not") but read "you have become (like) it" (*lo*, "to it"), that is, like the dry wadi. The meaning of the read form is uncertain.
[^6-16]: Or "How forceful are honest words!"
[^6-17]: Literally "What does reproving from you reprove?"
[^6-18]: Literally "cause to fall over an orphan", probably lots for a child sold for debt.
[^6-19]: Literally "my righteousness is still in it".
[^6-20]: Hebrew *havvot*, "calamities, ruin", the word translated "calamity" in verse 2.

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** "Does not a human being have hard service[^7-1] on earth? Are not his days like the days of a hired worker?

**2** Like a slave who longs for the shade, like a hired worker who waits for his wages,

**3** so I have been given months of futility[^7-2] as my portion, and nights of misery have been assigned to me.

**4** When I lie down I say, 'When will I get up?' But the night drags on,[^7-3] and I toss and turn until dawn.

**5** My flesh is clothed with worms and crusts of dirt; my skin hardens, then breaks open again.[^7-4]

**6** My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and they come to an end without hope.[^7-5]

**7** Remember that my life is a breath; my eye will never again see good.

**8** The eye that sees me will see me no more; your eyes will be on me, but I will be gone.

**9** As a cloud fades away and is gone, so one who goes down to Sheol[^7-6] does not come up.

**10** He will not return to his house again, and his place will not know him anymore.

**11** So I will not restrain my mouth. I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.

**12** Am I the sea, or the sea monster,[^7-7] that you set a guard over me?

**13** When I say, 'My bed will comfort me, my couch will ease my complaint,'

**14** then you terrify me with dreams and frighten me with visions,

**15** so that I would choose strangling, death rather than these bones of mine.[^7-8]

**16** I have had enough;[^7-9] I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a breath.

**17** What is a human being, that you make so much of him, that you pay attention to him,[^7-10]

**18** that you examine him every morning and test him every moment?

**19** How long will you not look away from me, or leave me alone long enough to swallow my spit?

[^34-13]: Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthless, good-for-nothing".
