## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The vision[^1-1] of Isaiah[^1-2] son of Amoz, which he saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah.[^1-3]

**2** Hear, you heavens, and listen, you earth, for Yahweh[^1-4] has spoken:[^1-5] I raised children[^1-6] and brought them up, but they have rebelled against me.

**3** The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master's feeding trough, but Israel does not know; my people do not understand.

**4** Woe to[^1-7] the sinful nation, a people weighed down with guilt, offspring of evildoers, children who act corruptly! They have abandoned Yahweh; they have despised the Holy One of Israel;[^1-8] they have turned their backs on him.[^1-9]

**5** Why will you be beaten again?[^1-10] Why do you keep on rebelling? The whole head is sick, and the whole heart is faint.

**6** From the sole of the foot to the head there is nothing sound in it: only wounds and welts and open sores. They have not been cleaned or bandaged, or soothed with oil.

**7** Your land is a desolation; your cities are burned with fire. As for your fields, foreigners devour them before your eyes. It is desolate, as when foreigners overthrow a land.[^1-11]

**8** Daughter Zion[^1-12] is left like a shelter in a vineyard, like a hut in a cucumber field, like a city under siege.[^1-13]

**9** If Yahweh of Armies[^1-14] had not left us a few survivors,[^1-15] we would have been like Sodom; we would have resembled Gomorrah.

**10** Hear the word of Yahweh, you rulers of Sodom! Listen to the instruction[^1-16] of our God, you people of Gomorrah!

**11** What are all your sacrifices to me? says Yahweh. I have had more than enough of burnt offerings of rams and the fat of fattened animals. I take no pleasure in the blood of bulls, of lambs and of goats.

**12** When you come to appear before me,[^1-17] who asked you to do this, to trample my courts?

**13** Stop bringing worthless offerings![^1-18] Your incense is detestable to me. New moons and Sabbaths, the calling of assemblies: I cannot bear wickedness together with a solemn assembly.[^1-19]

**14** Your new moons and your appointed festivals I hate with all my being.[^1-20] They have become a burden to me; I am tired of carrying them.

**15** When you spread out your hands in prayer, I will hide my eyes from you. Even when you pray and pray, I am not listening. Your hands are full of blood.

**16** Wash yourselves; make yourselves clean. Take your evil deeds out of my sight. Stop doing evil.

**17** Learn to do good. Seek justice; correct the oppressor.[^1-21] Defend the rights of the fatherless;[^1-22] plead the widow's case.

**18** Come now, let us settle this,[^1-23] says Yahweh. Though your sins are like scarlet, they will be as white as snow. Though they are red as crimson, they will become like wool.[^1-24]

**19** If you are willing and obey, you will eat the good things of the land.

**20** But if you refuse and rebel, you will be eaten by the sword.[^1-25] For the mouth of Yahweh has spoken.

**21** How[^1-26] the faithful city has become a prostitute! She was full of justice; righteousness lived in her, but now murderers.

**22** Your silver has become dross; your best wine[^1-27] is watered down.

**23** Your rulers are rebels[^1-28] and companions of thieves. Every one of them loves a bribe and chases after gifts. They do not defend the rights of the fatherless, and the widow's case never comes before them.

**24** Therefore the Lord[^1-29] Yahweh of Armies, the Mighty One of Israel,[^1-30] declares: Ah,[^1-31] I will get relief from my adversaries and take revenge on my enemies!

**25** I will turn my hand against you. I will smelt away your dross as with lye and remove all your impurities.[^1-32]

**26** I will restore your judges as at the first, and your counselors as at the beginning. After that you will be called the City of Righteousness, the Faithful City.

**27** Zion will be redeemed by justice, and those in her who turn back,[^1-33] by righteousness.

**28** But rebels and sinners will be crushed together, and those who abandon Yahweh will come to an end.

**29** For you[^1-34] will be ashamed of the sacred oaks[^1-35] that you delighted in, and you will blush for the gardens that you chose.

**30** For you will be like an oak whose leaves are withering, like a garden that has no water.

**31** The strong one will become tinder,[^1-36] and his work a spark.[^1-37] Both of them will burn together, with no one to put out the fire.

[^1-1]: Hebrew *chazon*, a revelation that a prophet "saw". The word gives its name to the whole book.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *Yesha'yahu*, "Yahweh saves".
[^1-3]: These kings reigned in the eighth century BC. Isaiah's call came in the year Uzziah died (6:1), about 740 BC, and his last dated words are from Hezekiah's reign (chapters 36–39), about 701 BC.
[^1-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-5]: In Isaiah's poetry the words of the prophet and the words of Yahweh alternate, often with no marked change of speaker. As in Deuteronomy 32, Yahweh's words in the poems are not put in quotation marks.
[^1-6]: Literally "sons". Also in verse 4.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^1-8]: A title of God found often in Isaiah and only rarely elsewhere (2 Kings 19:22; Psalm 71:22): the holy God who has bound himself to Israel.
[^1-9]: Literally "they are estranged backward".
[^1-10]: Or "Where can you be beaten again?" The body is already wounded everywhere (verse 6).
[^1-11]: Literally "like an overthrow by foreigners". *Overthrow* is the word used for the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:25; Deuteronomy 29:22, 29:23 in most English Bibles), which are named in verses 9 and 10.
[^1-12]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman.
[^1-13]: Or "like a guarded city", a watchman's post left standing alone.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. The title is used often in Isaiah. Also in verse 24.
[^1-15]: Literally "a survivor, a little". Or "If Yahweh of Armies had not left us survivors, we would soon have been like Sodom". Romans 9:29 quotes this verse from the Greek Septuagint, which reads "offspring" (literally "seed").
[^1-16]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^1-17]: Literally "to be seen at my face". The same consonants could be read "to see my face".
[^1-18]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering. Literally "offerings of emptiness".
[^1-19]: Hebrew *atsarah*, a festive gathering for worship.
[^1-20]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "soul, self". Literally "my soul hates them".
[^1-21]: Or "set the oppressed free" or "lead the oppressed". The word translated *oppressor*, Hebrew *chamots*, is found only here. The Septuagint reads "rescue the one who is wronged".
[^1-22]: Literally "judge the orphan". Also in verse 23.
[^1-23]: Or "let us argue it out". The verb is a term from the law court. Traditionally "let us reason together".
[^1-24]: Or "If your sins are like scarlet, can they be as white as snow? If they are red as crimson, can they become like wool?"
[^1-25]: The same verb as *eat* in verse 19: they will either eat the land's good things or be eaten by the sword.
[^1-26]: Hebrew *ekhah*, the opening cry of a funeral song, as in Lamentations 1:1.
[^1-27]: Hebrew *sove*, "drink", probably fine wine or beer.
[^1-28]: A play on words: *sarayikh sorerim*, "your rulers are rebels".
[^1-29]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name Yahweh.
[^1-30]: Hebrew *avir yisra'el*, found only here. Compare "the Mighty One of Jacob" (Genesis 49:24; Isaiah 49:26).
[^1-31]: Hebrew *hoy*, the word translated "Woe to" in verse 4. Here it is a cry of grim resolve. *Get relief* is literally "comfort myself".
[^1-32]: Hebrew *bedil*, tin or lead, metals separated from silver when it is refined.
[^1-33]: Or "her captives who return". Hebrew *shavim* can mean those who repent or those who come back.
[^1-34]: Literally "they". The Hebrew moves from "they" to "you" within the verse.
[^1-35]: Or "terebinths". Large trees, and the gardens around them, were places of worship for other gods (57:5; 65:3).
[^1-36]: Hebrew *ne'oret*, the coarse fibers left from beating flax, which catch fire at once.
[^1-37]: Or "its maker". If the "work" is an idol, its maker and the idol burn together.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** This is the word that Isaiah son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem.

**2** In the days to come,[^2-1] the mountain of Yahweh's[^2-2] house will be established as the highest of the mountains;[^2-3] it will be raised above the hills, and all the nations will stream to it.[^2-4]

**3** Many peoples will come and say, "Come, let us go up to the mountain of Yahweh, to the house of the God of Jacob, so that he may teach us his ways and we may walk in his paths." For instruction[^2-5] will go out from Zion, and the word of Yahweh from Jerusalem.

**4** He will judge between the nations and settle disputes for many peoples. They will beat their swords into plowshares[^2-6] and their spears into pruning knives. Nation will not lift up sword against nation, and they will not learn war anymore.

**5** House of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of Yahweh!

**6** For you have abandoned your people, the house of Jacob, because they are full of things from the east[^2-7] and of fortune-tellers like the Philistines, and they strike hands with the children of foreigners.[^2-8]

**7** Their land is filled with silver and gold, and there is no end to their treasures. Their land is filled with horses, and there is no end to their chariots.

**8** Their land is filled with idols.[^2-9] They bow down to the work of their hands, to what their own fingers have made.

**9** So humankind is brought low, and everyone is humbled. Do not forgive them![^2-10]

**10** Go into the rock and hide in the dust from the terror of Yahweh and from the splendor of his majesty.[^2-11]

**11** The proud eyes of humankind will be brought low, and human arrogance will be humbled. Yahweh alone will be exalted on that day.

**12** For Yahweh of Armies[^2-12] has a day against all that is proud and lofty, against all that is lifted up, and it will be brought low,

**13** against all the cedars of Lebanon, high and lifted up, and against all the oaks of Bashan,

**14** against all the high mountains and against all the lofty hills,

**15** against every high tower and against every fortified wall,

**16** against all the ships of Tarshish[^2-13] and against all the beautiful vessels.[^2-14]

**17** Human pride will be humbled, and human arrogance brought low. Yahweh alone will be exalted on that day,

**18** and the idols will vanish completely.

**19** People will go into caves in the rocks and holes in the ground, from the terror of Yahweh and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to make the earth tremble.[^2-15]

**20** On that day people will throw away to the moles[^2-16] and the bats their idols of silver and their idols of gold, which they made for themselves to worship,

**21** and go into the clefts of the rocks and the crevices of the cliffs, from the terror of Yahweh and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to make the earth tremble.

**22** Stop trusting in human beings, who have only a breath in their nostrils. For what are they worth?[^2-17]

[^2-1]: Literally "at the end of the days". Verses 2–4 are found also in Micah 4:1–3, with small differences.
[^2-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Literally "at the head of the mountains".
[^2-4]: Or "will flow to it", like a river.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^2-6]: Hebrew *ittim*, probably iron plow points or hoes. Joel 4:10 (3:10 in most English Bibles) turns the image around: "Beat your plowshares into swords."
[^2-7]: Literally "they are full from the east", perhaps of practices of divination from the east. The Hebrew is difficult.
[^2-8]: Perhaps "they make deals with foreigners". The meaning of the verb is uncertain.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *elilim*, "worthless things, nothings", a word that sounds like *elohim*, "gods". Isaiah uses it often for idols.
[^2-10]: Literally "Do not lift them up". Or "You cannot raise them up".
[^2-11]: The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a), the oldest complete manuscript of Isaiah, does not have the last words of verse 9 ("Do not forgive them!") or verse 10.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^2-13]: Large seagoing ships, of the kind that sailed to Tarshish, a distant port in the west, perhaps in Spain.
[^2-14]: Hebrew *sekhiyyot*, a word found only here. Perhaps "beautiful craft" or "fine images".
[^2-15]: A play on words: *la'arots ha'arets*, "to make the earth tremble". Also in verse 21.
[^2-16]: Hebrew *lachpor perot*, found only here, perhaps "to the burrowers": moles, rats or shrews.
[^2-17]: The Septuagint does not have this verse.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** For look, the Lord[^3-1] Yahweh of Armies[^3-2] is about to take away from Jerusalem and from Judah every support and supply,[^3-3] all supply of bread and all supply of water:

**2** the warrior and the soldier, the judge and the prophet, the diviner and the elder,

**3** the commander of fifty and the man of rank, the counselor, the skilled craftsman[^3-4] and the expert in charms.

**4** I will make boys their rulers, and the reckless[^3-5] will govern them.

**5** The people will oppress one another, each one his neighbor. The young will be insolent to the old, and the nobody to the honored.

**6** A man will take hold of his brother in his father's house and say, "You have a cloak; you be our ruler! Take charge of this heap of ruins!"[^3-6]

**7** On that day he will cry out, "I will not be a healer![^3-7] There is no bread and no cloak in my house. Do not make me ruler of the people."

**8** For Jerusalem has stumbled, and Judah has fallen, because their words and their deeds are against Yahweh, defying his glorious presence.[^3-8]

**9** The look on their faces testifies against them.[^3-9] They parade their sin like Sodom; they do not hide it. Woe to them![^3-10] They have brought disaster on themselves.

**10** Tell the righteous that it will go well with them, for they will eat the fruit of their deeds.

**11** Woe to the wicked! It will go badly with them, for they will be paid back for what their hands have done.

**12** My people! Children are their oppressors,[^3-11] and women rule over them.[^3-12] My people, your guides lead you astray; they have erased the path you should walk.[^3-13]

**13** Yahweh takes his place to bring charges; he stands to judge the peoples.[^3-14]

**14** Yahweh enters into judgment with the elders and leaders of his people: It is you who have stripped the vineyard bare.[^3-15] What you have stolen from the poor is in your houses.

**15** What do you mean by crushing my people and grinding the faces of the poor? declares the Lord Yahweh of Armies.

**16** Yahweh said: Because the daughters of Zion are proud and walk with outstretched necks, flirting with their eyes, tripping along with mincing steps, jingling the bangles on their feet,

**17** the Lord will cover the heads of the daughters of Zion with scabs, and Yahweh will lay bare their foreheads.[^3-16]

**18** On that day the Lord will take away their finery:[^3-17] the anklets, the headbands, the crescent ornaments,

**19** the pendants, the bracelets, the veils,

**20** the headdresses, the ankle chains, the sashes, the perfume boxes,[^3-18] the amulets,

**21** the signet rings, the nose rings,

**22** the festal robes, the capes, the shawls, the purses,

**23** the mirrors,[^3-19] the linen garments, the turbans and the veils.

**24** Instead of perfume there will be rot; instead of a sash, a rope; instead of well-set hair, baldness; instead of a rich robe, a wrapping of sackcloth; instead of beauty, branding.[^3-20]

**25** Your men will fall by the sword, and your warriors in battle.[^3-21]

**26** Her gates will lament and mourn; stripped bare, she will sit on the ground.

[^3-1]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name Yahweh. "The Lord" in verses 17 and 18 is *Adonai*.
[^3-2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 15.
[^3-3]: Hebrew *mash'en u-mash'enah*, the masculine and feminine forms of the same word, "staff": every kind of support.
[^3-4]: Or "the expert in magic arts".
[^3-5]: Hebrew *ta'alulim*, perhaps "caprice, whims", or "infants".
[^3-6]: Literally "and this ruin will be under your hand".
[^3-7]: Literally "a binder of wounds".
[^3-8]: Literally "rebelling against the eyes of his glory".
[^3-9]: Or "Their partiality testifies against them". The Hebrew phrase can mean "regard for faces", that is, favoritism in judgment.
[^3-10]: Hebrew *oy*, a cry of distress close in meaning to *hoy* (1:4). Literally "Woe to their souls!"
[^3-11]: Or "a child is their oppressor". The Hebrew word may also mean "one who gleans": "their oppressors strip them bare".
[^3-12]: The same consonants can be read "creditors" (*noshim*) instead of "women" (*nashim*), as the Septuagint understood them.
[^3-13]: Literally "they have swallowed up the way of your paths".
[^3-14]: The Septuagint reads "his people".
[^3-15]: Literally "burned" or "grazed bare".
[^3-16]: Or "their private parts". The meaning of the Hebrew word is uncertain.
[^3-17]: Many of the words in verses 18–23 are rare, and some of the items cannot be identified with certainty.
[^3-18]: Literally "houses of the *nephesh*", probably small bottles of perfume worn at the throat.
[^3-19]: Or "the papyrus garments", perhaps "the see-through garments".
[^3-20]: The Hebrew is short and abrupt: "for instead of beauty, branding". The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads "for instead of beauty, shame".
[^3-21]: *Your* is feminine singular: Zion is addressed as a woman.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** On that day seven women will take hold of one man and say, "We will eat our own bread and wear our own clothes; only let us be called by your name. Take away our disgrace!"[^4-1]

**2** On that day the branch[^4-2] of Yahweh[^4-3] will be beautiful and glorious, and the fruit of the land will be the pride and splendor of the survivors of Israel.

**3** Whoever is left in Zion and remains in Jerusalem will be called holy, everyone in Jerusalem who is recorded for life.[^4-4]

**4** When the Lord has washed away the filth of the daughters of Zion and rinsed the bloodstains of Jerusalem from within her by a spirit[^4-5] of judgment and a spirit of burning,

**5** then Yahweh will create over the whole site of Mount Zion and over her assemblies a cloud by day, and smoke and the glow of a flaming fire by night.[^4-6] For over all the glory there will be a canopy.[^4-7]

**6** It will be a shelter, a shade from the heat by day, and a refuge and hiding place from storm and rain.

[^4-1]: The shame of being unmarried and childless. So many men will have died in war (3:25) that women will plead for the protection of a husband's name.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *tsemach*, "sprout, growth". Here it may be the new growth of the land, matching "the fruit of the land". In Jeremiah 23:5; 33:15 and Zechariah 3:8; 6:12, "the Branch" is a title of a coming king of David's line, and many later readers took it so here: the Aramaic Targum reads "the anointed one (Messiah) of Yahweh".
[^4-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4-4]: Literally "everyone written for life", that is, entered in the register of the living.
[^4-5]: Or "wind" or "breath". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^4-6]: The cloud and the fire recall the pillar that led Israel through the wilderness (Exodus 13:21–22).
[^4-7]: Hebrew *chuppah*, a covering or canopy, the word later used for the canopy over a bride and groom (Psalm 19:6, 19:5 in most English Bibles; Joel 2:16).

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Let me sing for my beloved a song of my beloved about his vineyard:[^5-1] My beloved had a vineyard on a very fertile hill.[^5-2]

**2** He dug it up and cleared it of stones, and planted it with choice vines.[^5-3] He built a watchtower in the middle of it and also cut out a winepress in it. He waited for it to produce grapes, but it produced rotten grapes.[^5-4]

**3** So now, you who live in Jerusalem and you people of Judah, judge between me and my vineyard.

**4** What more could I have done for my vineyard that I have not done in it? When I waited for it to produce grapes, why did it produce rotten grapes?

**5** Now let me tell you what I am going to do to my vineyard: I will take away its hedge, and it will be grazed bare; I will break down its wall, and it will be trampled.

**6** I will make it a wasteland. It will not be pruned or hoed, and briers and thorns will grow up there. I will also command the clouds not to rain on it.

**7** For the vineyard of Yahweh[^5-5] of Armies is the house of Israel, and the people of Judah are the planting he delighted in. He waited for justice, but saw bloodshed; for righteousness, but heard an outcry.[^5-6]

**8** Woe to[^5-7] those who join house to house and add field to field, until there is no room left, and you are left to live alone in the middle of the land!

**9** Yahweh of Armies has sworn in my hearing:[^5-8] Many houses will surely become desolate, large and fine houses, with no one living in them.

**10** For ten acres[^5-9] of vineyard will produce only one bath,[^5-10] and a homer of seed will produce only an ephah.[^5-11]

**11** Woe to those who rise early in the morning to chase after beer, who stay up late at night till wine sets them on fire!

**12** They have lyre and harp, tambourine and flute, and wine at their feasts, but they pay no attention to the deeds of Yahweh, and they do not see the work of his hands.

**13** Therefore my people go into exile for lack of knowledge.[^5-12] Their nobles are starving,[^5-13] and their masses are parched with thirst.

**14** Therefore Sheol[^5-14] has opened its throat wide[^5-15] and opened its mouth beyond measure. Down go her splendor and her masses, her noisy crowd and all who revel in her.[^5-16]

**15** So humankind is brought low, and everyone is humbled, and the eyes of the proud are humbled.

**16** But Yahweh of Armies is exalted by justice, and the holy God shows himself holy by righteousness.

**17** Lambs will graze there as in their own pasture, and strangers will eat among the ruins of the rich.[^5-17]

**18** Woe to those who drag guilt along with cords of falsehood, and sin as if with cart ropes,

**19** who say, "Let him hurry; let him be quick with his work, so that we may see it! Let the plan of the Holy One of Israel come near and come true, so that we may know it!"

**20** Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who make darkness light and light darkness, who make bitter sweet and sweet bitter!

**21** Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes and clever in their own sight!

**22** Woe to those who are heroes at drinking wine, and champions at mixing strong drinks,

**23** who acquit the guilty for a bribe and deny justice to the innocent!

**24** Therefore, as a tongue of fire devours straw and dry grass sinks down in the flame, their root will be like rot, and their blossom will blow away like dust. For they have rejected the instruction[^5-18] of Yahweh of Armies and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel.

**25** That is why the anger of Yahweh burns against his people. He has stretched out his hand against them and struck them down. The mountains shook, and their corpses were like garbage in the middle of the streets. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.[^5-19]

**26** He will raise a signal flag for the nations far away and whistle for them[^5-20] from the ends of the earth. And look, swiftly, speedily they come!

**27** None of them is weary, and none stumbles; none slumbers or sleeps. Not a belt is loose at the waist, not a sandal strap is broken.

**28** Their arrows are sharp, and all their bows are strung. Their horses' hooves seem like flint, and their chariot wheels like a whirlwind.

**29** Their roar is like a lion's; they roar like young lions. They growl and seize their prey and carry it off, and no one can rescue it.

**30** On that day they will growl over it like the growling of the sea. If anyone looks at the land, there is darkness and distress, and the light is darkened by its clouds.

[^5-1]: Or "my love song about his vineyard". Hebrew *dod*, "beloved", is the word used in the Song of Songs. The prophet sings as the friend of the vineyard's owner, who is revealed in verse 7.
[^5-2]: Literally "on a horn, a son of oil".
[^5-3]: Hebrew *soreq*, a choice variety of red grape (Jeremiah 2:21).
[^5-4]: Hebrew *be'ushim*, "stinking things": sour, rotten or wild grapes. Also in verse 4.
[^5-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^5-6]: A play on words that English cannot reproduce: he waited for *mishpat*, "justice", but saw *mispach*; for *tsedaqah*, "righteousness", but heard *tse'aqah*, the cry of the oppressed (Exodus 3:7, 9). *Mispach* is found only here; it probably means "bloodshed" or "lawlessness".
[^5-7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning, used by the prophets to announce disaster (see 1:4). It begins six woes in this chapter (verses 8, 11, 18, 20, 21 and 22).
[^5-8]: Literally "In my ears, Yahweh of Armies". The verb "has sworn" is not in the Hebrew but is implied by the oath that follows.
[^5-9]: Literally "ten yokes": the land that ten teams of oxen could plow in a day.
[^5-10]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters).
[^5-11]: A homer was ten ephahs (Ezekiel 45:11), probably about 220 liters (6 bushels). The harvest will be only a tenth of the seed sown.
[^5-12]: Or "without knowing it".
[^5-13]: Literally "Their glory are men of hunger". The same consonants can be read "dead from hunger".
[^5-14]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead, pictured here as a monster with an open mouth.
[^5-15]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here "throat, appetite".
[^5-16]: *Her*: Jerusalem's.

**3** The earth will be utterly emptied and utterly plundered, for Yahweh has spoken this word.

**4** The earth dries up[^24-3] and withers; the world languishes and withers; the highest of the people of the earth languish.

**5** The earth is defiled under its inhabitants, for they have broken the laws, violated the decree and broken the everlasting covenant.[^24-4]

**6** Therefore a curse devours the earth, and its people bear their guilt. Therefore the inhabitants of the earth are burned up,[^24-5] and few people are left.

**7** The new wine dries up; the vine languishes. All the merrymakers groan.

**8** The joyful sound of tambourines has stopped; the noise of the revelers has ended; the joyful sound of the lyre has stopped.

**9** They no longer drink wine with a song; beer is bitter to those who drink it.

**10** The city of chaos[^24-6] is broken down; every house is shut up so that no one can enter.

**11** In the streets there is an outcry over wine; all joy has turned to gloom; the gladness of the earth has gone into exile.

**12** Desolation is left in the city, and its gate is battered to ruins.

**13** For so it will be in the middle of the earth, among the peoples: as when an olive tree is beaten, as at the gleaning when the grape harvest is over.

**14** They raise their voices and shout for joy; they cry out from the west[^24-7] over the majesty of Yahweh.

**15** Therefore in the east[^24-8] give glory to Yahweh; in the coastlands of the sea, give glory to the name of Yahweh, the God of Israel.

**16** From the ends of the earth we hear songs: "Glory[^24-9] to the Righteous One!" But I said, "I waste away, I waste away![^24-10] Woe is me! The traitors betray; with treachery the traitors betray!"[^24-11]

**17** Terror and pit and snare are upon you, you who live on the earth![^24-12]

**18** Whoever flees at the sound of terror will fall into the pit, and whoever climbs out of the pit will be caught in the snare. For the windows of heaven are opened,[^24-13] and the foundations of the earth shake.

**19** The earth is utterly broken; the earth is split apart; the earth is violently shaken.

**20** The earth staggers like a drunkard; it sways like a hut.[^24-14] Its rebellion lies heavy on it; it falls and will not rise again.

**21** On that day Yahweh will punish the host of heaven in heaven,[^24-15] and the kings of the earth on the earth.

**22** They will be gathered together like prisoners in a pit; they will be shut up in a prison, and after many days they will be punished.[^24-16]

**23** The moon will be ashamed and the sun abashed,[^24-17] for Yahweh of Armies[^24-18] will reign on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, and his glory will be before his elders.[^24-19]

[^24-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Chapters 24–27 are often called the "Isaiah Apocalypse": poems about the judgment of the whole earth and the final triumph of God.
[^24-2]: A play on sounds: *boqeq … u-voleqah*, "empty … and lay waste". Similar sound-plays run through verses 3, 4, 16, 17 and 19.
[^24-3]: Or "mourns". Hebrew *avlah*, also in verse 7.
[^24-4]: Perhaps the covenant with Noah and all living things (Genesis 9:16). *Laws* is Hebrew *torot*, "instructions"; *decree* is *choq*.
[^24-5]: Or "dwindle". The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain.
[^24-6]: Hebrew *tohu*, the word for the "formless" earth in Genesis 1:2. The city is not named.
[^24-7]: Literally "from the sea", the Mediterranean, to the west of Israel.
[^24-8]: Hebrew *ba'urim*, perhaps "in the regions of light", where the sun rises. The meaning is uncertain.
[^24-9]: Or "Beauty". Hebrew *tsevi*.
[^24-10]: Hebrew *razi li*, a phrase of uncertain meaning, perhaps "leanness to me". The Vulgate reads "My secret is mine".
[^24-11]: A play on words: *bogdim bagadu u-veged bogdim bagadu*.
[^24-12]: A play on sounds: *pachad va-pachat va-fach*. Jeremiah 48:43–44 repeats verses 17–18.
[^24-13]: As in the flood (Genesis 7:11).
[^24-14]: A watchman's hut in a field (1:8).
[^24-15]: Literally "the army of the height in the height": the heavenly powers, perhaps the stars, or the beings thought to rule the nations.
[^24-16]: Or "visited". Hebrew *paqad* (verse 21).
[^24-17]: Hebrew *levanah*, "the white one", and *chammah*, "the hot one", poetic names for the moon and the sun.
[^24-18]: *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^24-19]: Literally "and before his elders, glory". Compare the elders who saw the God of Israel on Mount Sinai (Exodus 24:9–11).

**3** Therefore a strong people will honor you; the city of ruthless nations will fear you.

**4** For you have been a refuge for the poor, a refuge for the needy in their distress, a shelter from the storm and a shade from the heat. For the breath of the ruthless is like a storm against a wall.

**5** Like heat in a dry land, you subdue the uproar of foreigners. As heat is cooled by the shade of a cloud, so the song of the ruthless is silenced.

**6** On this mountain Yahweh of Armies[^25-3] will prepare for all peoples a feast of rich food, a feast of aged wine,[^25-4] of rich food full of marrow, of aged wine well refined.

**7** On this mountain he will swallow up the shroud that covers all peoples, the sheet that is spread over all nations.

**8** He will swallow up death forever.[^25-5] The Lord Yahweh will wipe away the tears from every face and remove the disgrace of his people from all the earth. For Yahweh has spoken.

**9** On that day they will say, "Look, this is our God; we waited for him, and he has saved us. This is Yahweh; we waited for him. Let us rejoice and be glad in his salvation."

**10** For the hand of Yahweh will rest on this mountain. But Moab will be trampled down where he stands, as straw is trampled down in a dung pit.[^25-6]

**11** He will spread out his hands in it, as a swimmer spreads out his hands to swim, but he will bring down his pride, for all the skill of his hands.[^25-7]

**12** The high fortress of your walls he will bring down, lay low and cast to the ground, to the dust.

[^25-2]: Hebrew *emunah omen*, two words from the root *aman*, "be firm" (7:9).
[^25-3]: *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^25-4]: Literally "of lees", wine left to mature on its sediment. Also later in the verse.
[^25-5]: Or "in victory". Hebrew *lanetsach*. Paul quotes this line in 1 Corinthians 15:54, "Death has been swallowed up in victory". Revelation 7:17 and 21:4 echo the next line.
[^25-6]: The Hebrew text is written "in the water of a dung pit" but read "in a dung pit". *Dung pit* (*madmenah*) sounds like Madmen, a town of Moab (Jeremiah 48:2).
[^25-7]: *He* in the first line is probably Moab, and in the second Yahweh. *Skill* (Hebrew *arbot*) is uncertain; perhaps "the tricks" or "the strokes" of his hands.

**1** On that day this song will be sung in the land of Judah: "We have a strong city; he sets up salvation as its walls and ramparts.[^26-1]

**2** Open the gates, so that the righteous nation may enter, the nation that keeps faith.

**3** You keep in perfect peace[^26-2] the one whose mind is steadfast,[^26-3] because he trusts in you.

**4** Trust in Yahweh[^26-4] forever, for in Yah, Yahweh,[^26-5] you have an everlasting Rock.

**5** For he has brought down those who live on high, the lofty city. He lays it low, lays it low to the ground, and casts it down to the dust.

**6** The foot tramples it, the feet of the poor, the steps of the needy."

**7** The path of the righteous is level; you, the Upright One, make smooth the way of the righteous.[^26-6]

**8** Yes, Yahweh, in the path of your judgments we wait for you. Your name and your renown are the desire of our soul.

**9** My soul longs for you in the night; yes, my spirit within me seeks you at dawn. For when your judgments come upon the earth, the inhabitants of the world learn righteousness.

**10** If the wicked are shown favor, they do not learn righteousness. In a land of honesty they act unjustly and do not see the majesty of Yahweh.

**11** Yahweh, your hand is lifted up, but they do not see it. Let them see your zeal for your people and be ashamed; let the fire meant for your enemies consume them.

**12** Yahweh, you will establish peace for us; indeed, all that we have done, you have done for us.

**13** Yahweh our God, other lords besides you have ruled over us, but your name alone do we honor.[^26-7]

**14** The dead do not live; the shades[^26-8] do not rise. That is why you punished them and destroyed them, and wiped out all memory of them.

**15** You have enlarged the nation, Yahweh; you have enlarged the nation; you are glorified. You have extended all the borders of the land.

**16** Yahweh, in distress they came looking for you; they poured out a whispered prayer when your discipline was on them.[^26-9]

**17** As a pregnant woman about to give birth writhes and cries out in her pains, so were we because of you, Yahweh.

**18** We were pregnant, we writhed, but we gave birth only to wind. We have not brought salvation to the earth, and no inhabitants of the world have been born.[^26-10]

**19** Your dead will live; my dead bodies will rise.[^26-11] Wake up and shout for joy, you who dwell in the dust! For your dew is a dew of light,[^26-12] and the earth will give birth to the shades.[^26-13]

**20** Go, my people, enter your rooms and shut your door behind you.[^26-14] Hide for a little while, until the wrath has passed by.

**21** For look, Yahweh is coming out from his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their guilt. The earth will uncover the blood shed on it and will no longer cover its slain.

[^26-2]: Literally "peace, peace". Hebrew *shalom shalom*, doubled for emphasis.
[^26-3]: Hebrew *yetser*, "what is formed": the mind, its purpose and inclination.
[^26-4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^26-5]: Hebrew *Yah YHWH*. *Yah* is a short form of the name Yahweh (12:2).
[^26-6]: Or "the path of the righteous is level; you make level the way of the righteous". Hebrew *yashar* can be a title, "Upright One", or mean "level".
[^26-7]: Literally "only by you do we call your name to mind".
[^26-8]: Hebrew *repha'im*, the spirits of the dead (14:9). Also in verse 19.
[^26-9]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. *Came looking for you* is Hebrew *peqadukha*, perhaps "they missed you".
[^26-10]: Literally "have not fallen", that is, have not been born.
[^26-11]: Literally "my corpse", a collective: the dead of the speaker's own people.
[^26-12]: Or "a dew of herbs". Hebrew *orot* can mean "lights" or "plants" (2 Kings 4:39).
[^26-13]: This verse is often read, with Daniel 12:2, as a promise that the dead will rise. Others take it as an image of the nation's revival, as in Ezekiel 37.
[^26-14]: The Hebrew text is written "your doors" but read "your door".

**1** On that day Yahweh[^27-1] will punish with his sword, hard and great and strong, Leviathan the fleeing serpent, Leviathan the twisting serpent, and he will kill the dragon[^27-2] that is in the sea.[^27-3]

**2** On that day sing of a delightful vineyard:

**3** I, Yahweh, am its keeper; I water it every moment. So that no one may harm it, I guard it night and day.

**4** I have no anger. If only there were briers and thorns to fight against! I would march against them; I would burn them all together.

**6** In days to come[^27-4] Jacob will take root; Israel will blossom and sprout, and fill the face of the world with fruit.

**5** On that day Yahweh of Armies[^28-3] will be a crown of glory and a diadem of beauty for the remnant of his people,

**6** a spirit of justice for the one who sits in judgment, and strength for those who turn back the battle at the gate.

**7** But these too reel from wine and stagger from beer: priest and prophet reel from beer; they are swallowed up by wine; they stagger from beer; they reel when they see visions; they stumble when they give judgment.

**8** For all the tables are covered with vomit; no place is without filth.

**9** Whom is he trying to teach? To whom is he explaining his message? To children just weaned from milk, just taken from the breast?[^28-4]

**10** For it is *tsav la-tsav, tsav la-tsav, qav la-qav, qav la-qav*, a little here, a little there.[^28-5]

**11** Very well, then: with stammering lips and in a foreign tongue he will speak to this people,[^28-6]

**12** to whom he said, "This is the place of rest; give rest to the weary; this is the place of repose." But they would not listen.

**13** So the word of Yahweh will be to them *tsav la-tsav, tsav la-tsav, qav la-qav, qav la-qav*, a little here, a little there, so that they will go and fall backward, be broken, be snared and be captured.

**14** Therefore hear the word of Yahweh, you scoffers who rule this people in Jerusalem![^28-7]

**18** Your covenant with death will be annulled,[^28-13] and your agreement with Sheol will not stand. When the overwhelming scourge passes through, you will be trampled down by it.

**19** As often as it passes through, it will take you; morning after morning it will pass through, by day and by night. To understand the message will be sheer terror.

**20** For the bed is too short to stretch out on, and the blanket too narrow to wrap around you.

**21** For Yahweh will rise up as at Mount Perazim; he will rage as in the valley of Gibeon,[^28-14] to do his work, his strange work, and to perform his task, his alien task.

**22** So now, stop your scoffing, or your chains will be made heavier. For I have heard from the Lord Yahweh of Armies a decree of destruction against the whole land.[^28-15]

**27** Dill is not threshed with a threshing sledge, and a cartwheel is not rolled over cumin. Dill is beaten out with a stick, and cumin with a rod.

**28** Grain for bread is crushed, but not endlessly; he does not keep threshing it forever. He drives the wheel of his cart and his horses over it, but he does not crush it.

**29** This also comes from Yahweh of Armies. His counsel is wonderful, and his wisdom is great.[^28-18]
