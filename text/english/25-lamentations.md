## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** How[^1-1] lonely sits the city that was full of people! She has become like a widow, she who was great among the nations. The princess among the provinces has been put to forced labor.

**2** She weeps bitterly in the night, with tears on her cheeks. Among all her lovers she has no one to comfort her. All her friends have betrayed her; they have become her enemies.

**3** Judah has gone into exile after affliction and harsh servitude.[^1-2] She lives among the nations, but finds no resting place. All her pursuers have overtaken her in the narrow places.[^1-3]

**4** The roads to Zion mourn, for no one comes to the festivals. All her gates are desolate; her priests groan; her young women grieve, and she herself is in bitterness.

**5** Her foes have become her masters;[^1-4] her enemies are at ease. For the LORD[^1-5] has made her suffer for her many rebellions. Her little children have gone into captivity before the foe.

**6** All her splendor has departed from Daughter Zion.[^1-6] Her princes have become like deer that find no pasture; they have gone on without strength before the pursuer.

**7** In the days of her affliction and her homelessness, Jerusalem remembers all the precious things that were hers in days of old. When her people fell into the hand of the foe, and there was no one to help her, the foes looked at her; they laughed at her ruin.[^1-7]

**8** Jerusalem has sinned greatly; therefore she has become a thing to scorn.[^1-8] All who honored her despise her, for they have seen her nakedness. She herself groans and turns away.

**9** Her uncleanness is in her skirts; she did not consider her end. Her fall was astonishing; there is no one to comfort her. Look, LORD, at my affliction, for the enemy has triumphed![^1-9]

**10** The foe has stretched out his hand over all her precious things; for she has seen the nations enter her sanctuary, those you commanded not to enter your assembly.[^1-10]

**11** All her people groan as they search for bread; they give their treasures for food to keep themselves alive.[^1-11] Look, LORD, and see, for I am despised.

**12** Is it nothing to you,[^1-12] all you who pass by on the road? Look and see if there is any pain like my pain, which was dealt out to me, which the LORD inflicted on the day of his burning anger.

**13** From on high he sent fire into my bones, and it overcame them.[^1-13] He spread a net for my feet; he turned me back. He has made me desolate, sick all day long.

**14** The yoke of my rebellions has been bound on;[^1-14] by his hand they were woven together. They have come up on my neck; he has made my strength fail. The Lord has given me into the hands of those I cannot withstand.

**15** The Lord has rejected[^1-15] all my mighty men in my midst; he has called an assembly[^1-16] against me to crush my young men. The Lord has trodden Virgin Daughter Judah as in a winepress.[^1-17]

**16** For these things I weep; my eye, my eye runs down with water, for a comforter is far from me, one to revive my soul. My children are desolate, for the enemy has prevailed.

**17** Zion stretches out her hands, but there is no one to comfort her.[^1-18] The LORD has commanded against Jacob that his neighbors should be his foes. Jerusalem has become an unclean thing[^1-19] among them.

**18** The LORD is in the right, for I rebelled against his word.[^1-20] Hear now, all peoples, and see my pain! My young women and my young men have gone into captivity.

**19** I called to my lovers, but they deceived me. My priests and my elders perished in the city as they searched for food to keep themselves alive.[^1-21]

**20** Look, LORD, for I am in distress! My insides churn; my heart is overturned within me, for I have been very rebellious. Outside, the sword bereaves; inside, there is death.[^1-22]

**21** They have heard that I groan; there is no one to comfort me. All my enemies have heard of my trouble; they rejoice that you have done it. Bring on the day you have announced,[^1-23] and let them become like me.

**22** Let all their evil come before you, and deal with them as you have dealt with me because of all my rebellions. For my groans are many, and my heart is faint.

[^1-1]: Hebrew *eikhah*, "How!", a cry of lament (compare 2 Samuel 1:19), which gives the book its Hebrew name. Chapters 1–4 are acrostic poems. In this chapter the twenty-two verses begin with the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, from *alef* to *tav*. Each verse has three lines, except verse 7, which has four.
[^1-2]: Or "because of affliction and harsh servitude".
[^1-3]: Or "in the midst of her distress".
[^1-4]: Literally "have become the head". Compare Deuteronomy 28:44.
[^1-5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *bat-tsiyyon*, a poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *mishbatteha*, a word found only here, perhaps "her ruin" or "her ceasing".
[^1-8]: Hebrew *nidah*, a word found only here. It may mean "an object of scorn" (from *nud*, "to shake the head"), or it may be another form of *niddah*, "unclean thing" (verse 17).
[^1-9]: Jerusalem herself speaks in this line, in the last line of verse 11, and in verses 12–16 and 18–22. The poem does not mark where her words begin and end, so they are not put in quotation marks.
[^1-10]: Compare Deuteronomy 23:4 (23:3 in most English Bibles).
[^1-11]: Literally "to bring back life (*nephesh*)". Also in verse 19.
[^1-12]: Literally "Not to you". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "May it not happen to you".
[^1-13]: Literally "and it ruled over it".
[^1-14]: Hebrew *nisqad*, a word found only here, of uncertain meaning.
[^1-15]: Or "trampled".
[^1-16]: Hebrew *mo'ed*, an appointed time or festival, as in verse 4.
[^1-17]: Literally "A winepress the Lord has trodden for the virgin, Daughter Judah".
[^1-18]: The poet speaks in this verse.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *niddah*, the uncleanness of a woman during her monthly period (Leviticus 15:19–24).
[^1-20]: Literally "his mouth".
[^1-21]: The Septuagint adds "but they found none".
[^1-22]: Literally "in the house, it is like death".
[^1-23]: Literally "You have brought the day you announced".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** How[^2-1] the Lord in his anger has covered Daughter Zion[^2-2] with a cloud![^2-3] He has thrown down from heaven to earth the splendor of Israel; he has not remembered his footstool[^2-4] in the day of his anger.

**2** The Lord has swallowed up without pity all the dwellings of Jacob. In his fury he has torn down the strongholds of Daughter Judah; he has brought them down to the ground. He has profaned the kingdom and its princes.

**3** In his burning anger he has cut off every horn[^2-5] of Israel. He has drawn back his right hand in the face of the enemy. He has burned against Jacob like a flaming fire that consumes all around.

**4** He has bent his bow like an enemy; his right hand is set like a foe's. He has killed all who were pleasing to the eye. On the tent of Daughter Zion he has poured out his wrath like fire.

**5** The Lord has become like an enemy; he has swallowed up Israel. He has swallowed up all her palaces; he has destroyed his strongholds.[^2-6] He has multiplied mourning and moaning[^2-7] in Daughter Judah.

**6** He has wrecked his booth[^2-8] like a garden; he has destroyed his place of meeting.[^2-9] The LORD[^2-10] has made Zion forget festival and Sabbath, and in his fierce anger he has spurned king and priest.

**7** The Lord has rejected his altar and disowned his sanctuary. He has handed over to the enemy the walls of her palaces. They raised a shout in the house of the LORD as on a festival day.

**8** The LORD determined to destroy the wall of Daughter Zion. He stretched out the measuring line; he did not hold back his hand from destroying.[^2-11] He made rampart and wall lament; together they languish.

**9** Her gates have sunk into the ground; he has ruined and broken her bars. Her king and her princes are among the nations; there is no instruction,[^2-12] and her prophets find no vision from the LORD.

**10** The elders of Daughter Zion sit on the ground in silence. They have thrown dust on their heads and put on sackcloth. The young women of Jerusalem have bowed their heads to the ground.

**11** My eyes are worn out with tears; my insides churn. My heart is poured out on the ground[^2-13] because of the destruction of my dear people,[^2-14] because children and infants faint in the streets of the city.

**12** They say to their mothers, "Where is the grain and the wine?" as they faint like the wounded in the streets of the city, as their life is poured out on their mothers' laps.

**13** What can I say for you?[^2-15] To what can I compare you, Daughter Jerusalem? What can I liken to you, to comfort you, Virgin Daughter Zion? For your ruin is as vast as the sea; who can heal you?

**14** Your prophets have seen for you visions that were empty and false;[^2-16] they did not expose your guilt to restore your fortunes. They have seen for you oracles that were empty and misleading.

**15** All who pass along the road clap their hands at you. They hiss and shake their heads at Daughter Jerusalem: "Is this the city that was called the perfection of beauty, the joy of all the earth?"[^2-17]

**16** All your enemies open their mouths against you; they hiss and grind their teeth. They say, "We have swallowed her up! Ah, this is the day we hoped for; we have found it, we have seen it!"

**17** The LORD has done what he planned; he has carried out his word, which he commanded in days of old. He has torn down without pity; he has let the enemy rejoice over you; he has raised up the horn of your foes.

**18** Their heart cried out to the Lord. O wall of Daughter Zion,[^2-18] let your tears run down like a torrent day and night! Give yourself no relief; do not let your eyes rest.[^2-19]

**19** Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the night watches! Pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord. Lift up your hands to him for the lives of your children, who faint with hunger at the head of every street.

**20** Look, LORD, and consider: to whom have you done this?[^2-20] Should women eat their offspring, the children they have cared for?[^2-21] Should priest and prophet be killed in the sanctuary of the Lord?

**21** Young and old lie on the ground in the streets; my young women and my young men have fallen by the sword. You have killed them on the day of your anger; you have slaughtered without pity.

**22** You summoned, as for a festival day, my terrors from all around.[^2-22] On the day of the LORD's anger, no one escaped or survived. Those I cared for and raised, my enemy has destroyed.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *eikhah*, "How!"; see 1:1. Like chapter 1, this chapter is an acrostic poem: its twenty-two verses begin with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order. Here the letter *pe* (verse 16) comes before *ayin* (verse 17), as in chapters 3 and 4. Each verse has three lines, except verse 19, which has four.
[^2-2]: Hebrew *bat-tsiyyon*; see 1:6.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *ya'iv*, a word found only here, probably "has covered with a cloud". Or "has treated with contempt".
[^2-4]: That is, the temple, or the ark of the covenant in it (Psalm 99:5; 132:7; 1 Chronicles 28:2).
[^2-5]: A picture of strength. Also in verse 17.
[^2-6]: The Hebrew changes from "her" to "his", both referring to Israel.
[^2-7]: A play on sounds: *ta'aniyyah va-aniyyah*.
[^2-8]: That is, the temple. Hebrew *sukko*, "his booth" or "his shelter".
[^2-9]: Or "his appointed festival". Hebrew *mo'ed*, the word translated "festival" later in the verse.
[^2-10]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-11]: Literally "from swallowing".
[^2-12]: Or "the law is no more". Hebrew *torah*, the teaching given by the priests.
[^2-13]: Literally "My liver". The poet speaks in verses 11–19.
[^2-14]: Literally "the daughter of my people", a tender way of speaking of the people as a young woman. Also in 3:48; 4:3, 6, 10.
[^2-15]: Or "What witness can I bring for you?" The meaning of the verb is uncertain.
[^2-16]: Literally "emptiness and whitewash".
[^2-17]: Compare Psalm 48:3 (48:2 in most English Bibles) and 50:2.
[^2-18]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Their heart cried out to the Lord, wall of Daughter Zion".
[^2-19]: Literally "do not let the daughter of your eye be still". The "daughter of the eye" is the pupil.
[^2-20]: Zion speaks in verses 20–22.
[^2-21]: Hebrew *tippuchim*, a word found only here, "tender care". Compare "cared for" in verse 22.
[^2-22]: Compare "terror on every side" in Jeremiah 6:25; 20:10.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** I am the man who has seen affliction under the rod of his wrath.[^3-1]

**2** He has driven me away and made me walk in darkness, not light.

**3** Surely against me he turns his hand again and again, all day long.

**4** He has worn away my flesh and my skin; he has broken my bones.

**5** He has besieged me and surrounded me with bitterness[^3-2] and hardship.

**6** He has made me live in dark places, like those long dead.[^3-3]

**7** He has walled me in so that I cannot get out; he has made my chains heavy.[^3-4]

**8** Though I call out and cry for help, he shuts out my prayer.

**9** He has walled up my ways with hewn stone; he has made my paths crooked.

**10** He is to me a bear lying in wait, a lion in hiding.

**11** He has turned aside my ways and torn me to pieces; he has made me desolate.

**12** He bent his bow and set me up as a target for his arrow.

**13** He has shot into my heart[^3-5] the arrows of his quiver.[^3-6]

**14** I have become a laughingstock to all my people,[^3-7] their mocking song all day long.

**15** He has filled me with bitter herbs; he has made me drink my fill of wormwood.

**16** He has broken my teeth with gravel; he has pressed me down in the ashes.

**17** My soul has been rejected from peace;[^3-8] I have forgotten what good is.

**18** So I said, "My strength has perished, and my hope from the LORD."[^3-9]

**19** Remember my affliction and my homelessness, the wormwood and the bitterness.[^3-10]

**20** My soul remembers it continually and is bowed down within me.[^3-11]

**21** But this I call to mind, and therefore I have hope:

**22** Because of the LORD's faithful love[^3-12] we are not finished,[^3-13] for his compassion does not fail.

**23** It is new every morning; great is your faithfulness.

**24** "The LORD is my portion," says my soul, "therefore I will hope in him."

**25** The LORD is good to those who wait for him, to the soul who seeks him.

**26** It is good to wait in silence for the salvation of the LORD.

**27** It is good for a man to bear the yoke in his youth.

**28** Let him sit alone in silence, for he has laid it on him.[^3-14]

**29** Let him put his mouth in the dust; perhaps there may yet be hope.

**30** Let him offer his cheek to the one who strikes him; let him be filled with disgrace.

**31** For the Lord will not reject forever.

**32** Though he brings grief, he will have compassion according to the greatness of his faithful love.

**33** For he does not afflict willingly,[^3-15] or bring grief to human beings.

**34** To crush underfoot all the prisoners of the land,

**35** to deny a man his rights before the Most High,[^3-16]

**36** to wrong a person in his lawsuit: the Lord does not approve.[^3-17]

**37** Who has spoken and it came to pass, unless the Lord has commanded it?

**38** Is it not from the mouth of the Most High that both evil and good come?

**39** Why should any living person complain, any man, about his sins?[^3-18]

**40** Let us search and examine our ways, and return to the LORD.

**41** Let us lift up our hearts with our hands to God in heaven:

**42** We have rebelled and disobeyed, and you have not forgiven.

**43** You have wrapped yourself in anger and pursued us; you have killed without pity.

**44** You have wrapped yourself in a cloud, so that no prayer can pass through.

**45** You have made us scum and refuse among the peoples.

**46** All our enemies have opened their mouths against us.

**47** Panic and pit have come upon us,[^3-19] devastation and destruction.

**48** My eye runs down with streams of water because of the destruction of my dear people.[^3-20]

**49** My eye flows and does not stop, without any rest,

**50** until the LORD looks down from heaven and sees.

**51** My eye brings grief to my soul because of all the daughters of my city.

**52** Those who are my enemies without cause have hunted me down like a bird.

**53** They tried to end my life in a pit,[^3-21] and they threw stones at me.[^3-22]

**54** Water flowed over my head; I said, "I am cut off!"

**55** I called on your name, LORD, from the depths of the pit.

**56** You heard my voice; do not close your ear to my cry for relief, to my cry for help!

**57** You came near on the day I called to you; you said, "Do not fear!"

**58** You have taken up my case, Lord; you have redeemed my life.

**59** You have seen the wrong done to me, LORD; judge my case!

**60** You have seen all their vengeance, all their plots against me.

**61** You have heard their taunts, LORD, all their plots against me,

**62** the lips of those who rise against me, and their muttering against me all day long.

**63** Look at them, sitting or standing:[^3-23] I am their mocking song.

**64** Pay them back, LORD,[^3-24] according to the work of their hands.

**65** Give them a hardened heart;[^3-25] may your curse be on them!

**66** Pursue them in anger and destroy them from under the heavens of the LORD.[^3-26]

[^3-1]: This chapter is a triple acrostic. It has sixty-six verses, and each letter of the Hebrew alphabet begins three verses in a row: verses 1–3 begin with *alef*, verses 4–6 with *bet*, and so on to *tav* in verses 64–66. As in chapters 2 and 4, *pe* (verses 46–48) comes before *ayin* (verses 49–51). The speaker is one man, who speaks of God as "he" until verse 18.
[^3-2]: Hebrew *rosh*, a bitter, poisonous plant. Also in verse 19.
[^3-3]: The same words are in Psalm 143:3.
[^3-4]: Literally "my bronze", that is, my bronze chains.
[^3-5]: Literally "my kidneys", the inmost being.
[^3-6]: Literally "the sons of his quiver".
[^3-7]: Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac read "all peoples".
[^3-8]: Or "You have rejected my soul from peace".
[^3-9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-10]: Or "The memory of my affliction and my homelessness is wormwood and bitterness".
[^3-11]: The Hebrew text is written "and my soul muses (or complains)" but read "and my soul is bowed down". Or "You will surely remember, and my soul is bowed down within me".
[^3-12]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Here and in verse 32 the word is plural, "acts of faithful love".
[^3-13]: Literally "The faithful love of the LORD, for we are not finished". The Syriac and the Targum read "it is not finished", matching the next line; this reading is followed by many translations.
[^3-14]: Or "when it has been laid on him".
[^3-15]: Literally "from his heart".
[^3-16]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God. Also in verse 38.
[^3-17]: Literally "the Lord does not see". Or "does the Lord not see?"
[^3-18]: The Hebrew text is written "his sin" but read "his sins". Or "Why should a living person complain? Let each one complain about his own sins".
[^3-19]: A play on sounds: *pachad va-fachat*. Compare Isaiah 24:17; Jeremiah 48:43.
[^3-20]: Literally "the daughter of my people"; see 2:11.
[^3-21]: Hebrew *bor*, a pit or cistern, also a picture of the grave. Also in verse 55.
[^3-22]: Or "they set a stone over me".
[^3-23]: Literally "their sitting and their rising".
[^3-24]: Or "You will pay them back, LORD"; also in verses 65–66.
[^3-25]: Hebrew *meginnat lev*, a phrase found only here, perhaps "a covering of the heart", that is, a hardened or anguished heart.
[^3-26]: The Septuagint reads "under heaven, O Lord".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** How[^4-1] the gold has grown dim, how the pure gold has changed! The holy stones[^4-2] lie scattered at the head of every street.

**2** The precious children of Zion, worth their weight in fine gold, how they are regarded as clay jars, the work of a potter's hands!

**3** Even jackals[^4-3] offer the breast and nurse their young, but my dear people[^4-4] has become cruel, like ostriches in the wilderness.[^4-5]

**4** The tongue of the nursing infant sticks to the roof of its mouth for thirst. Little children ask for bread, but no one breaks it for them.

**5** Those who used to eat delicacies are desolate in the streets. Those brought up in scarlet now cling to ash heaps.

**6** The punishment[^4-6] of my dear people is greater than the punishment of Sodom, which was overthrown in a moment, with no hand laid on it.[^4-7]

**7** Her princes[^4-8] were purer than snow, whiter than milk; their bodies were ruddier than coral,[^4-9] their form like sapphire.[^4-10]

**8** Now their appearance is blacker than soot; they are not recognized in the streets. Their skin has shriveled on their bones; it has become as dry as wood.

**9** Better off were those killed by the sword than those killed by hunger, who waste away, pierced through by the lack of the produce of the field.[^4-11]

**10** The hands of compassionate women have boiled their own children; they became their food in the destruction of my dear people.

**11** The LORD[^4-12] has spent his wrath; he has poured out his burning anger. He has kindled a fire in Zion, and it has consumed her foundations.

**12** The kings of the earth did not believe, nor did any of the inhabitants of the world, that foe and enemy could enter the gates of Jerusalem.

**13** It happened because of the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests,[^4-13] who shed in her midst the blood of the righteous.

**14** They wandered blind through the streets, so defiled with blood that no one could touch their garments.

**15** "Go away! Unclean!" people cried to them. "Go away, go away! Do not touch!" So they fled and wandered, and people among the nations said, "They shall not stay here any longer."[^4-14]

**16** The LORD himself[^4-15] has scattered them; he will no longer look on them. No one showed respect to the priests, or favor to the elders.

**17** Our eyes still wear themselves out, looking in vain[^4-16] for help. From our watchtower we watched for a nation that could not save us.

**18** They dogged our steps so that we could not walk in our streets. Our end drew near; our days were numbered,[^4-17] for our end had come.

**19** Our pursuers were swifter than the eagles[^4-18] in the sky. They chased us on the mountains; they lay in wait for us in the wilderness.

**20** The breath of our nostrils, the LORD's anointed,[^4-19] was caught in their pits, the one of whom we said, "Under his shadow we will live among the nations."

**21** Rejoice and be glad, Daughter Edom,[^4-20] you who live in the land of Uz![^4-21] To you also the cup will pass;[^4-22] you will become drunk and strip yourself naked.

**22** Your punishment is complete, Daughter Zion; he will not keep you in exile any longer. But he will punish your iniquity, Daughter Edom; he will expose your sins.

[^4-1]: Hebrew *eikhah*, "How!"; see 1:1. This chapter is an acrostic poem: its twenty-two verses begin with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, with *pe* (verse 16) before *ayin* (verse 17), as in chapters 2 and 3. Each verse here has two lines.
[^4-2]: Perhaps the stones of the temple, or precious stones, a picture of the "precious children" of verse 2.
[^4-3]: The Hebrew text is written *tannin*, which can mean "sea monster" or be another plural form of "jackals", but read *tannim*, "jackals".
[^4-4]: Literally "the daughter of my people"; see 2:11. Also in verses 6 and 10.
[^4-5]: The ostrich was thought to neglect its young (Job 39:13–16).
[^4-6]: Or "iniquity". Hebrew *avon* means both the wrong and its punishment, and *chatta't*, "sin", later in the verse, can also mean its punishment. Also in verse 22.
[^4-7]: Literally "and no hands whirled against it". Or "and no hands were wrung over it".
[^4-8]: Or "Her Nazirites". Hebrew *nezireha*, "her consecrated ones".
[^4-9]: Hebrew *peninim*, perhaps red coral, or pearls.
[^4-10]: Hebrew *sappir*, probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.
[^4-11]: Literally "who flow away, pierced, from the produce of the field".
[^4-12]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-13]: Literally "From the sins of her prophets, the iniquities of her priests".
[^4-14]: Or "They shall not live among us as foreigners any longer".
[^4-15]: Literally "The face of the LORD".
[^4-16]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", the key word of Ecclesiastes; here "in vain".
[^4-17]: Literally "our days were full".
[^4-18]: Or "vultures". Hebrew *nesher*.
[^4-19]: That is, the king of Judah, probably Zedekiah, who was captured as he fled (2 Kings 25:4–7).
[^4-20]: A poetic name for Edom and its people, like "Daughter Zion" for Jerusalem (1:6). Also in verse 22.
[^4-21]: A land to the east or southeast of Israel; compare Job 1:1.
[^4-22]: The cup of God's wrath, which the nations must drink (Jeremiah 25:15–29).

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Remember, LORD,[^5-1] what has happened to us; look and see our disgrace.[^5-2]

**2** Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, our houses to foreigners.

**3** We have become orphans, without a father; our mothers are like widows.

**4** We must pay silver for the water we drink; our wood comes to us at a price.

**5** Our pursuers are at our necks;[^5-3] we are weary and are given no rest.

**6** We have given our hand to Egypt, and to Assyria, to get enough bread.[^5-4]

**7** Our ancestors sinned and are no more, and we bear their iniquities.

**8** Slaves[^5-5] rule over us; there is no one to rescue us from their hand.

**9** We get our bread at the risk of our lives, because of the sword in the wilderness.

**10** Our skin is hot as an oven from the burning heat of hunger.

**11** Women have been raped in Zion, young women in the towns of Judah.

**12** Princes have been hung up by their hands;[^5-6] elders are shown no respect.

**13** Young men carry the millstone,[^5-7] and boys stagger under loads of wood.

**14** The elders have left the city gate; the young men have stopped their music.

**15** The joy of our hearts has ceased; our dancing has turned to mourning.

**16** The crown has fallen from our head. Woe to us, for we have sinned!

**17** Because of this our heart is faint; because of these things our eyes have grown dim:

**18** because Mount Zion lies desolate, with foxes prowling over it.

**19** But you, LORD, reign forever;[^5-8] your throne endures from generation to generation.

**20** Why do you forget us forever? Why do you forsake us for so long?

**21** Bring us back to you, LORD, so that we may return; renew our days as of old,

**22** unless you have utterly rejected us, and are angry with us beyond measure.[^5-9]

[^5-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-2]: This chapter has twenty-two verses, as many as the letters of the Hebrew alphabet, but unlike chapters 1–4 it is not an acrostic. Its lines are shorter, and the whole chapter is a prayer of the people ("we").
