## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the beginning was the Word. The Word was with God, and the Word was God.[^1-1]

**2** He was with God in the beginning.

**3** Through him all things came into being. Without him not one thing came into being.

**4** What came into being in him was life,[^1-2] and that life was the light of all people.

**5** The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.[^1-3]

**6** There was a man sent from God, whose name was John.

**7** He came as a witness, to testify about the light, so that everyone would believe through him.

**8** He himself was not the light; he came to testify about the light.

**9** The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world.[^1-4]

**10** He was in the world, and the world came into being through him, yet the world did not recognize him.

**11** He came to what was his own, and his own people did not receive him.

**12** But to all who did receive him, to those who believe in his name, he gave the right to become children of God,

**13** children born not from human descent,[^1-5] nor from physical desire,[^1-6] nor from a husband's decision,[^1-7] but from God.

**14** The Word became flesh[^1-8] and lived among us.[^1-9] We have seen his glory, the glory of the only Son from the Father,[^1-10] full of grace and truth.

**15** (John testified about him. He cried out, "This is the one I was talking about when I said, 'The one who comes after me ranks ahead of me, because he existed before me.'")

**16** For from his fullness we have all received grace upon grace.[^1-11]

**17** For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

**18** No one has ever seen God. The only God,[^1-12] who is at the Father's side,[^1-13] has made him known.

**19** This is John's testimony, when the Jews of Jerusalem[^1-14] sent priests and Levites to ask him, "Who are you?"

**20** He admitted it openly and did not deny it. He declared, "I am not the Messiah."[^1-15]

**21** They asked him, "What then? Are you Elijah?" He said, "I am not." "Are you the Prophet?"[^1-16] He answered, "No."

**22** So they said to him, "Who are you? We need an answer for those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?"

**23** He said, "I am the voice of someone shouting in the wilderness, 'Make the way for the Lord straight,'[^1-17] as the prophet Isaiah said."

**24** They had been sent by the Pharisees.[^1-18]

**25** They asked him, "Then why are you baptizing, if you are not the Messiah, or Elijah, or the Prophet?"

**26** John answered them, "I baptize with water. Standing among you is someone you do not know.

**27** He is the one who comes after me. I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal."[^1-19]

**28** This happened in Bethany on the other side of the Jordan, where John was baptizing.

**29** The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!

**30** This is the one I was talking about when I said, 'A man is coming after me who ranks ahead of me, because he existed before me.'

**31** I did not know him myself, but I came baptizing with water so that he would be revealed to Israel."

**32** John testified, "I saw the Spirit coming down from heaven like a dove, and it remained on him.

**33** I did not know him myself, but the one who sent me to baptize with water told me, 'When you see the Spirit come down and remain on someone, he is the one who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.'

**34** I have seen it, and I testify that this is God's Chosen One."[^1-20]

**35** The next day John was standing there again with two of his disciples.

**36** He saw Jesus walking by and said, "Look, the Lamb of God!"

**37** The two disciples heard him say this, and they followed Jesus.

**38** Jesus turned around, saw them following, and asked them, "What are you looking for?" They said to him, "Rabbi" (which means Teacher), "where are you staying?"

**39** He said to them, "Come, and you will see." So they went and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day. It was about four in the afternoon.[^1-21]

**40** Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who heard what John said and followed Jesus.

**41** The first thing Andrew did was find his brother Simon and tell him, "We have found the Messiah" (which means the Anointed One).[^1-22]

**42** He brought Simon to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, "You are Simon son of John. You will be called Cephas" (which means Peter).[^1-23]

**43** The next day Jesus decided to go to Galilee. He found Philip and said to him, "Follow me."

**44** Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter.

**45** Philip found Nathanael and told him, "We have found the one Moses wrote about in the Law, and the prophets wrote about too: Jesus son of Joseph, from Nazareth."

**46** Nathanael said to him, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" Philip said to him, "Come and see."

**47** Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward him and said about him, "Here is a true Israelite, in whom there is nothing false."

**48** Nathanael asked him, "How do you know me?" Jesus answered him, "Before Philip called you, I saw you under the fig tree."

**49** Nathanael answered him, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!"

**50** Jesus answered him, "Do you believe because I told you I saw you under the fig tree? You will see greater things than that."

**51** Then he said to him, "Truly, truly, I tell you all:[^1-24] you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God going up and coming down on the Son of Man."

[^1-1]: *Word*: Greek *logos*, "word, message, reason". John uses it as a title for the one who becomes the man Jesus (verse 14). *With God*: or "in the presence of God".
[^1-2]: The SBLGNT, like most modern editions, begins this sentence with the last words of verse 3 ("what has come into being"). Many translations instead end verse 3 with them: "Without him nothing came into being that has come into being. In him was life …"
[^1-3]: Or "has not understood it", "has not grasped it". The Greek verb can mean either.
[^1-4]: Or "The true light, which gives light to everyone who comes into the world, was there."
[^1-5]: Literally "not of bloods".
[^1-6]: Literally "the will of the flesh".
[^1-7]: Or "a man's will". Greek *aner* means man or husband.
[^1-8]: That is, a human being.
[^1-9]: Literally "pitched his tent among us".
[^1-10]: Literally "glory as of an only one from a father". Greek *monogenes*: only, unique, one of a kind.
[^1-11]: Literally "grace in place of grace": one gift of grace after another.
[^1-12]: The SBLGNT and the earliest manuscripts read "the only God" (Greek *monogenes theos*). Most later manuscripts, and the Byzantine text, read "the only Son".
[^1-13]: Literally "who is in the bosom of the Father", a picture of the closest relationship.
[^1-14]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here, as often in John, the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem who send official representatives.
[^1-15]: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^1-16]: The prophet like Moses promised in Deuteronomy 18:15–18.
[^1-17]: Isaiah 40:3. In Isaiah's Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^1-18]: Or "Some Pharisees had been sent."
[^1-19]: Many manuscripts read "He is the one who comes after me, who ranks ahead of me".
[^1-20]: The SBLGNT reads "the Chosen One of God", following a reading given in the margin of Westcott and Hort. The other editions, and many manuscripts, read "the Son of God".
[^1-21]: Literally "about the tenth hour", counting hours from sunrise.
[^1-22]: Literally "which, translated, is *Christos*". *Messiah* (Hebrew and Aramaic) and *Christos* (Greek) both mean "anointed one".
[^1-23]: *Cephas* (Aramaic) and *Peter* (Greek) both mean "rock".
[^1-24]: "You" is plural in Greek. *Truly, truly*: Greek *amen amen*, a solemn affirmation. Many manuscripts read "from now on you will see".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** On the third day there was a wedding at Cana in Galilee, and Jesus' mother was there.

**2** Jesus and his disciples had also been invited to the wedding.

**3** When the wine ran out, Jesus' mother said to him, "They have no wine."

**4** Jesus said to her, "Woman, what does that have to do with me?[^2-1] My hour has not yet come."

**5** His mother said to the servants, "Do whatever he tells you."

**6** Six stone water jars stood there, for the purification rites of the Jews.[^2-2] Each held two or three measures.[^2-3]

**7** Jesus said to them, "Fill the jars with water." So they filled them to the brim.

**8** Then he said to them, "Now draw some out and take it to the master of the feast."[^2-4] So they took it.

**9** The master of the feast tasted the water, which had become wine. He did not know where it had come from, though the servants who had drawn the water knew. He called the bridegroom

**10** and said to him, "Everyone serves the good wine first, and the cheaper wine after the guests have drunk freely.[^2-5] But you have kept the good wine until now."

**11** Jesus did this, the first of his signs,[^2-6] at Cana in Galilee. He revealed his glory, and his disciples believed in him.

**12** After this he went down to Capernaum with his mother, his brothers, and his disciples, and they stayed there a few days.

**13** The Passover of the Jews was near, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**14** In the temple[^2-7] he found people selling cattle, sheep, and doves, and the money changers sitting there.

**15** He made a whip out of cords and drove them all out of the temple, both the sheep and the cattle. He poured out the money changers' coins and overturned their tables.

**16** To those selling doves he said, "Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father's house a marketplace!"

**17** His disciples remembered that it is written, "Zeal for your house will consume me."[^2-8]

**18** So the Jews[^2-9] responded to him, "What sign can you show us to prove you have the right to do these things?"[^2-10]

**19** Jesus answered them, "Destroy this temple,[^2-11] and in three days I will raise it up."

**20** The Jews said, "This temple was forty-six years in the building,[^2-12] and you will raise it up in three days?"

**21** But he was speaking about the temple of his body.

**22** So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.

**23** While he was in Jerusalem at the Passover festival, many believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing.

**24** But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them,[^2-13] because he knew all people.

**25** He did not need anyone to testify about human beings, for he himself knew what was in a person.

[^2-1]: Literally "What to me and to you, woman?", an idiom that distances the speaker from a request; compare Mark 1:24. "Woman" was a polite form of address (also 19:26), not a disrespectful one.
[^2-2]: That is, for the ritual washing required by Jewish custom.
[^2-3]: Greek *metretes*, a liquid measure of about 10 gallons (40 liters). Each jar held about 20 to 30 gallons (75 to 115 liters).
[^2-4]: Greek *architriklinos*, the person in charge of the banquet: a head steward, or a guest presiding over the meal.
[^2-5]: Literally "when they have become drunk".
[^2-6]: Literally "this beginning of the signs". John calls Jesus' miracles "signs": acts that point beyond themselves to who he is.
[^2-7]: Greek *hieron*, the whole temple complex with its courts. In verses 19–21 the word is *naos*, the sanctuary itself.
[^2-8]: Psalm 69:10 (69:9 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew reads "has consumed me".
[^2-9]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here, as often in John, the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem, who were responsible for the temple.
[^2-10]: Literally "What sign do you show us, that you are doing these things?"
[^2-11]: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary. See verse 14.
[^2-12]: Or "This temple has been under construction for forty-six years." Herod the Great began rebuilding the temple about 20 BC.
[^2-13]: *Entrust* is the same Greek verb (*pisteuo*) as *believed* in verse 23.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** There was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a leader of the Jews.[^3-1]

**2** He came to Jesus at night and said to him, "Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher who has come from God. For no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him."

**3** Jesus answered him, "Truly, truly, I tell you, no one can see the kingdom of God without being born from above."[^3-2]

**4** Nicodemus said to him, "How can a man be born when he is old? He can't go back into his mother's womb a second time and be born, can he?"

**5** Jesus answered, "Truly, truly, I tell you, no one can enter the kingdom of God without being born of water and the Spirit.[^3-3]

**6** What is born of the flesh is flesh, and what is born of the Spirit is spirit.

**7** Don't be amazed that I told you, 'You[^3-4] must be born from above.'

**8** The wind[^3-5] blows where it wants. You hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit."

**9** Nicodemus answered him, "How can these things be?"

**10** Jesus answered him, "You are the teacher of Israel, and you do not understand these things?

**11** Truly, truly, I tell you, we speak about what we know and testify to what we have seen, and yet you[^3-6] do not accept our testimony.

**12** If I have told you about earthly things and you do not believe, how will you believe if I tell you about heavenly things?

**13** No one has gone up into heaven except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of Man.[^3-7]

**14** Just as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness,[^3-8] so the Son of Man must be lifted up,[^3-9]

**15** so that everyone who believes may have eternal life in him.[^3-10]

**16** For God loved the world so much that he gave his only Son,[^3-11] so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but will have eternal life.[^3-12]

**17** For God did not send the Son into the world to condemn[^3-13] the world, but so that the world might be saved through him.

**18** Whoever believes in him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already, because they have not believed in the name of the only Son of God.

**19** This is the judgment:[^3-14] the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light, because their deeds were evil.

**20** Everyone who does evil hates the light and does not come to the light, so that their deeds will not be exposed.

**21** But whoever does what is true[^3-15] comes to the light, so that it may be seen plainly that their deeds have been done in God."

**22** After this Jesus and his disciples went into the Judean countryside. He spent time with them there and baptized.

**23** John was also baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because there was plenty of water there, and people kept coming and being baptized.

**24** For John had not yet been put in prison.

**25** A dispute arose between John's disciples and a Jew about purification.

**26** They came to John and said to him, "Rabbi, the man who was with you on the other side of the Jordan, the one you testified about: look, he is baptizing, and everyone is going to him."

**27** John answered, "No one can receive anything unless it has been given to them from heaven.

**28** You yourselves can testify that I said, 'I am not the Messiah, but I have been sent ahead of him.'

**29** The bride belongs to the bridegroom.[^3-16] The bridegroom's friend, who stands by and listens for him, is overjoyed when he hears the bridegroom's voice. So this joy of mine is now complete.

**30** He must become greater; I must become less.

**31** The one who comes from above[^3-17] is above all. The one who is from the earth belongs to the earth and speaks as one from the earth.[^3-18] The one who comes from heaven is above all.

**32** He testifies to what he has seen and heard, yet no one accepts his testimony.

**33** Whoever has accepted his testimony has confirmed[^3-19] that God is truthful.

**34** For the one whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.[^3-20]

**35** The Father loves the Son and has put everything into his hands.

**36** Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life. Whoever rejects[^3-21] the Son will not see life; instead, God's anger remains on him."

[^3-1]: That is, a member of the Jewish ruling council in Jerusalem. Literally "a ruler of the Jews".
[^3-2]: Or "born again", also in verse 7. Greek *anothen* means both "from above" and "again"; Nicodemus takes it in the second sense (verse 4).
[^3-3]: Or "of water and spirit". Greek *pneuma* means "spirit", "wind" or "breath"; see verse 8.
[^3-4]: *You* is plural in Greek.
[^3-5]: Greek *pneuma*, the same word as "Spirit" in this verse. *Sound*: Greek *phone*, also "voice".
[^3-6]: *You* is plural in Greek here and in verse 12.
[^3-7]: Many manuscripts add "who is in heaven".
[^3-8]: Numbers 21:8–9.
[^3-9]: John uses this verb, "lift up", both of Jesus being raised on the cross and of his exaltation (8:28; 12:32–34).
[^3-10]: Or "so that everyone who believes in him may have eternal life". Many manuscripts read "so that everyone who believes in him may not perish but may have eternal life", as in verse 16. *Eternal life*: Greek *zoe aionios*, literally "the life of the age", that is, of the age to come. In John, those who believe have it already (verse 36; 5:24).
[^3-11]: Or "For this is how God loved the world: he gave his only Son". *Only*: Greek *monogenes*, "only, unique, one of a kind" (see 1:14); also in verse 18.
[^3-12]: The Greek has no quotation marks, and it is unclear where Jesus' words end. They may end with verse 15, so that verses 16–21 are the Gospel writer's own reflection. This translation keeps verses 16–21 as part of what Jesus says.
[^3-13]: Or "to judge". Greek *krino* means "judge", and often "condemn"; also in verse 18.
[^3-14]: Or "the verdict", "the reason for condemnation".
[^3-15]: Literally "does the truth".
[^3-16]: Literally "The one who has the bride is the bridegroom."
[^3-17]: Greek *anothen*, as in verses 3 and 7. As at verse 16, it is unclear whether John's words end with verse 30 and verses 31–36 are the Gospel writer's reflection; this translation keeps them as part of what John says.
[^3-18]: Literally "is from the earth and speaks from the earth".
[^3-19]: Literally "has set his seal", as on a document, to certify it.
[^3-20]: Or "for God gives the Spirit without measure". The Greek does not say who gives; many manuscripts add "God".
[^3-21]: Or "disobeys". Greek *apeitheo*, "refuse to believe, refuse to obey".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Jesus learned[^4-1] that the Pharisees had heard he was making and baptizing more disciples than John

**2** (although Jesus himself was not baptizing; his disciples were).

**3** So he left Judea and went back again to Galilee.

**4** He had to pass through Samaria.

**5** So he came to a town in Samaria called Sychar, near the plot of land that Jacob had given to his son Joseph.[^4-2]

**6** Jacob's well was there. Jesus, tired from the journey, sat down just as he was by the well. It was about noon.[^4-3]

**7** A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink."

**8** For his disciples had gone off into the town to buy food.

**9** The Samaritan woman said to him, "How is it that you, a Jew, are asking me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?" For Jews do not associate with Samaritans.[^4-4]

**10** Jesus answered her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that is saying to you, 'Give me a drink,' you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water."[^4-5]

**11** The woman said to him, "Sir,[^4-6] you have nothing to draw water with, and the well is deep. So where do you get this living water?

**12** You aren't greater than our ancestor[^4-7] Jacob, are you? He gave us the well, and he drank from it himself, as did his sons and his livestock."

**13** Jesus answered her, "Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again.

**14** But whoever drinks the water I will give them will never be thirsty again. The water I will give them will become in them a spring of water welling up to eternal life."

**15** The woman said to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I won't be thirsty or have to keep coming here to draw water."

**16** He said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come back here."

**17** The woman answered him, "I have no husband." Jesus said to her, "You are right to say, 'I have no husband.'

**18** For you have had five husbands, and the man you have now is not your husband.[^4-8] What you have said is true."

**19** The woman said to him, "Sir, I can see that you are a prophet.

**20** Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain,[^4-9] but you people say that the place where people must worship is in Jerusalem."

**21** Jesus said to her, "Believe me, woman, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.

**22** You[^4-10] worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, because salvation is from the Jews.

**23** But the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth,[^4-11] for the Father is looking for such people to worship him.

**24** God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth."

**25** The woman said to him, "I know that the Messiah is coming" (the one called Christ).[^4-12] "When he comes, he will tell us everything."

**26** Jesus said to her, "I am he, the one who is speaking to you."[^4-13]

**27** Just then his disciples came back. They were amazed that he was talking with a woman, but no one said, "What do you want?" or "Why are you talking with her?"

**28** Then the woman left her water jar, went off into the town, and said to the people,

**29** "Come and see a man who told me everything I ever did! Could this be the Messiah?"

**30** They came out of the town and began making their way to him.

**31** Meanwhile the disciples were urging him, "Rabbi, eat something."

**32** But he said to them, "I have food to eat that you do not know about."

**33** So the disciples said to one another, "Has someone brought him something to eat?"

**34** Jesus said to them, "My food is to do the will of the one who sent me and to complete his work.

**35** Don't you say, 'Four months more, and then the harvest comes'? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes and see the fields: they are ripe[^4-14] for harvest.

**36** Already[^4-15] the one who reaps is receiving wages and gathering a crop for eternal life, so that the one who sows and the one who reaps may rejoice together.

**37** For here the saying is true: 'One sows and another reaps.'

**38** I sent you to reap what you have not worked for. Others have done the hard work, and you have come in to share the fruit of their labor."[^4-16]

**39** Many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of what the woman said when she testified, "He told me everything I ever did."

**40** So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days.

**41** Many more believed because of what he said.

**42** They said to the woman, "We no longer believe because of what you said, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this really is the Savior of the world."[^4-17]

**43** After the two days he left there for Galilee.

**44** For Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own country.[^4-18]

**45** So when he came into Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him. They had seen everything he had done in Jerusalem at the festival, for they too had gone to the festival.

**46** So he came again to Cana in Galilee, where he had made the water wine. There was a royal official[^4-19] whose son was sick in Capernaum.

**47** When this man heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to him and begged him to come down and heal his son, for he was about to die.

**48** Jesus said to him, "Unless you people[^4-20] see signs and wonders, you will never believe."

**49** The royal official said to him, "Sir, come down before my little boy dies."

**50** Jesus said to him, "Go. Your son lives." The man believed the word Jesus spoke to him and set off.

**51** While he was still on his way down, his servants[^4-21] met him and told him that his boy was alive.

**52** So he asked them at what time he had begun to get better. They said to him, "Yesterday at one in the afternoon[^4-22] the fever left him."

**53** The father realized that this was the time when Jesus had said to him, "Your son lives." And he himself believed, along with his whole household.

**54** This was the second sign that Jesus did after coming from Judea to Galilee.

[^4-1]: The SBLGNT here follows Nestle–Aland (NA28); other editions read "the Lord learned". The Greek sentence in verses 1–3 begins "So when Jesus learned …" and is interrupted by verse 2.
[^4-2]: See Genesis 33:18–19; 48:22; Joshua 24:32.
[^4-3]: Literally "about the sixth hour", counting hours from sunrise.
[^4-4]: Or "do not use the same dishes as Samaritans".
[^4-5]: Or "running water". The phrase meant fresh water from a spring or stream, as opposed to still water in a cistern; Jesus uses it of a gift that gives life.
[^4-6]: Greek *kyrie*, a respectful form of address, "sir" or "lord"; also in verses 15, 19 and 49.
[^4-7]: Literally "our father".
[^4-8]: Greek *aner* means both "husband" and "man", as in verses 16–17.
[^4-9]: Mount Gerizim, where the Samaritans had built a temple.
[^4-10]: *You* is plural in Greek, as in verses 20–21: the Samaritans.
[^4-11]: Or "in the Spirit and in truth"; also in verse 24.
[^4-12]: Greek *Messias* (from Hebrew and Aramaic) and *Christos* (Greek) both mean "anointed one"; see 1:41. The words in parentheses may be the woman's or the Gospel writer's.
[^4-13]: Literally "I am, the one who is speaking to you." Greek *ego eimi*, "I am", the words Jesus uses of himself throughout John (see 6:35; 8:24, 58).
[^4-14]: Literally "white".
[^4-15]: The SBLGNT, like most modern editions, begins this sentence with the last word of verse 35, "already". Others end verse 35 with it: "they are already ripe for harvest."
[^4-16]: Literally "you have entered into their labor".
[^4-17]: Many manuscripts add "the Messiah".
[^4-18]: Or "his own hometown". Compare Mark 6:4.
[^4-19]: Greek *basilikos*, someone in the service of the king, here probably Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee.
[^4-20]: *You* is plural in Greek.
[^4-21]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^4-22]: Literally "at the seventh hour".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** After this there was a festival of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**2** In Jerusalem, by the Sheep Gate,[^5-1] there is a pool called in Hebrew[^5-2] Bethesda,[^5-3] which has five colonnades.

**3** In these lay a large number of sick people: blind, lame, and paralyzed.[^5-4]

**5** One man there had been ill for thirty-eight years.

**6** When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had already been ill for a long time, he said to him, "Do you want to get well?"

**7** The sick man answered him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up. While I am making my way there, someone else goes down ahead of me."

**8** Jesus said to him, "Get up, pick up your mat, and walk."
