## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of the LORD[^1-1] came to Jonah son of Amittai:[^1-2]

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh,[^1-3] the great city, and cry out against it, for their evil has come up before me."

**3** But Jonah got up to run away to Tarshish,[^1-4] away from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa[^1-5] and found a ship going to Tarshish. He paid the fare[^1-6] and went down into the ship to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD.

**4** But the LORD hurled[^1-7] a great wind on the sea, and there was a great storm on the sea, and the ship was about to break up.[^1-8]

**5** The sailors were afraid, and each one cried out to his own god. They hurled the cargo[^1-9] that was in the ship into the sea to lighten it for themselves. But Jonah had gone down into the hold of the ship, and he lay there, fast asleep.[^1-10]

**6** The captain[^1-11] came to him and said to him, "How can you be sleeping?[^1-12] Get up, call on your god! Perhaps the god will give a thought to us, and we will not perish."

**7** Then they said to one another, "Come, let us cast lots, so that we may know on whose account this disaster has come on us." So they cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

**8** They said to him, "Tell us, you on whose account this disaster has come on us:[^1-13] what is your work? Where do you come from? What is your country? From what people are you?"

**9** He said to them, "I am a Hebrew. I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land."

**10** The men were terrified[^1-14] and said to him, "What is this you have done?" For the men knew that he was running away from the presence of the LORD, because he had told them.

**11** They said to him, "What should we do to you, so that the sea will calm down for us?" For the sea was growing more and more stormy.

**12** He said to them, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea, and the sea will calm down for you. For I know that it is on my account that this great storm has come on you."

**13** But the men rowed hard[^1-15] to get back to dry land, and they could not, because the sea was growing more and more stormy against them.

**14** Then they called out to the LORD: "Please, LORD, do not let us perish for this man's life, and do not hold us guilty of innocent blood.[^1-16] For you, LORD, have done as you pleased."

**15** Then they picked Jonah up and hurled him into the sea, and the sea stopped raging.

**16** The men greatly feared the LORD. They offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows.[^1-17]

[^1-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-2]: A prophet from Gath-hepher in Galilee, who foretold the victories of Jeroboam II king of Israel in the eighth century BC (2 Kings 14:25). *Jonah* means "dove".
[^1-3]: The capital of the Assyrian empire, on the Tigris River opposite modern Mosul in Iraq. Assyria destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel in 722 BC.
[^1-4]: A distant port in the far west, perhaps in Spain. Nineveh lay to the east.
[^1-5]: A port on the Mediterranean coast, modern Jaffa, now part of Tel Aviv.
[^1-6]: Literally "he gave its hire", perhaps the hire of the whole ship.
[^1-7]: The verb *hetil*, "hurl", is repeated in verses 5, 12 and 15. So also *went down* in verses 3 and 5 traces Jonah's descent, which continues in 2:7 (2:6 in most English Bibles).
[^1-8]: Literally "the ship thought it would be broken".
[^1-9]: Or "the gear". Hebrew *kelim*, "vessels, objects".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *nirdam*, "in a deep sleep", the word used of Adam's sleep in Genesis 2:21. Also in verse 6.
[^1-11]: Literally "the chief of the sailors".
[^1-12]: Literally "What is with you, sleeping?"
[^1-13]: Literally "Tell us, on account of whom is this disaster on us?" The lot has already fallen on Jonah, so the question may ask him to explain himself.
[^1-14]: Literally "feared a great fear". The same words are used in verse 16, where they fear the LORD.
[^1-15]: Literally "dug".
[^1-16]: The guilt of killing an innocent man (Deuteronomy 21:8).
[^1-17]: In most English Bibles chapter 1 has a verse 17, which is 2:1 in the Hebrew.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** The LORD[^2-1] appointed[^2-2] a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.[^2-3]

**2** Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish.

**3** He said:[^2-4] "I called to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me. From the belly of Sheol[^2-5] I cried for help, and you heard my voice.

**4** You threw me into the deep, into the heart of the seas, and the current[^2-6] surrounded me. All your breakers and your waves swept over me.[^2-7]

**5** I said, 'I have been driven away from your sight. Yet I will look again toward your holy temple.'[^2-8]

**6** The waters closed around me up to my neck;[^2-9] the deep surrounded me; seaweed was wrapped around my head.

**7** I went down to the roots of the mountains;[^2-10] the earth with its bars closed behind me forever. But you brought my life up from the pit,[^2-11] LORD my God.

**8** When my life was ebbing away,[^2-12] I remembered the LORD, and my prayer came to you, to your holy temple.

**9** Those who cling to worthless idols[^2-13] abandon their faithful love.[^2-14]

**10** But I, with a song of thanksgiving, will sacrifice to you. What I have vowed I will pay. Salvation belongs to the LORD!"[^2-15]

**11** Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto the dry land.

[^2-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-2]: Or "provided". The same verb is used of the plant, the worm and the wind in 4:6–8.
[^2-3]: Verse 1 is 1:17 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–11 are 2:1–10. Jesus speaks of the sign of Jonah and his three days and nights in the fish in Matthew 12:39–41.
[^2-4]: Verses 3–10 are a psalm of thanksgiving, sung by someone already rescued. Many of its lines echo the book of Psalms.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. *I called … and he answered me* echoes Psalm 120:1.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *nahar*, "river, current", here the currents of the sea.
[^2-7]: The same line is found in Psalm 42:8 (42:7 in most English Bibles).
[^2-8]: Some scholars read "How will I ever look again toward your holy temple?", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^2-9]: Literally "up to the *nephesh*", the throat or the life (Psalm 69:2, 69:1 in most English Bibles).
[^2-10]: Literally "to the cuttings of the mountains", probably their foundations at the bottom of the sea. *I went down* continues the descent of 1:3 and 1:5.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *shachat*, the pit of the grave.
[^2-12]: Literally "When my *nephesh* fainted within me".
[^2-13]: Literally "those who keep vanities of emptiness", as in Psalm 31:7 (31:6 in most English Bibles).
[^2-14]: Hebrew *chesed*. Perhaps "abandon the God who is faithful to them", or "abandon their loyalty".
[^2-15]: Compare Psalm 3:9 (3:8 in most English Bibles).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** The word of the LORD[^3-1] came to Jonah a second time:

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim to it the message that I tell you."

**3** So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the LORD had said. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city,[^3-2] a three days' walk across.[^3-3]

**4** Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's walk, and he proclaimed, "Forty days more, and Nineveh will be overthrown!"[^3-4]

**5** The people of Nineveh believed God. They proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least.

**6** When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he got up from his throne, took off his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in the ashes.

**7** He had this proclaimed in Nineveh: "By the decree of the king and his nobles: No human being or animal, no herd or flock, is to taste anything. They are not to graze or drink water.

**8** Let human beings and animals be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out to God with all their strength. Let each one turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands.

**9** Who knows? God may turn and relent,[^3-5] and turn from his burning anger, so that we do not perish."

**10** When God saw what they did, that they turned from their evil way, God relented concerning the disaster[^3-6] that he had said he would bring on them, and he did not do it.

[^3-1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-2]: Literally "a great city to God", an idiom for something very great. Or "a great city in God's sight".
[^3-3]: Literally "a walk of three days". The walls of ancient Nineveh were about 7½ miles (12 km) around, so the phrase may include the whole district around the city, or mean the time needed to go through all of it.
[^3-4]: Hebrew *nehpakhet*, the word used of the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:25; Amos 4:11). It can also mean "turned around, changed", which is what happens to Nineveh. The Septuagint reads "Three days more".
[^3-5]: Or "change his mind". Also in verse 10. Compare Joel 2:14.
[^3-6]: Hebrew *ra'ah*, the same word as *evil* earlier in the verse and in verse 8. When the people turn from their evil, God turns from the evil he planned for them. The word is used again in 4:1.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** But to Jonah this seemed very wrong,[^4-1] and he was angry.

**2** He prayed to the LORD[^4-2] and said, "Please, LORD, isn't this what I said while I was still in my own country? That is why I hurried to run away to Tarshish. For I knew that you are a gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger and abounding in faithful love,[^4-3] and that you relent from sending disaster.[^4-4]

**3** So now, LORD, please take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live."[^4-5]

**4** The LORD said, "Is it right for you to be angry?"[^4-6]

**5** Jonah went out of the city and sat down to the east of the city. He made a shelter for himself there and sat under it in the shade, until he could see what would happen to the city.

**6** Then the LORD God[^4-7] appointed a plant,[^4-8] and it grew up over Jonah to be a shade over his head, to save him from his misery.[^4-9] Jonah was very glad about the plant.

**7** But at dawn the next day God appointed a worm, and it attacked the plant, so that it withered.

**8** When the sun rose, God appointed a scorching[^4-10] east wind, and the sun beat down on Jonah's head, so that he grew faint. He asked to die,[^4-11] saying, "It is better for me to die than to live."

**9** But God said to Jonah, "Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?" He said, "It is right for me to be angry, angry enough to die!"

**10** The LORD said, "You were concerned about[^4-12] the plant, for which you did not labor and which you did not make grow. It came up in a night and perished in a night.[^4-13]

**11** And should I not be concerned about Nineveh, the great city, in which there are more than 120,000 people who do not know their right hand from their left,[^4-14] and many animals as well?"

[^4-1]: Literally "It was evil to Jonah, a great evil". The Hebrew uses the word *ra'ah*, "evil, disaster", of 3:10 again.
[^4-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-3]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love. The words echo the LORD's description of himself in Exodus 34:6.
[^4-4]: Literally "and relenting concerning the disaster". The same words are found in Joel 2:13.
[^4-5]: Elijah, in despair, asked to die in similar words (1 Kings 19:4).
[^4-6]: Or "Are you very angry?" Also in verse 9.
[^4-7]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohim*, the form of the divine name used in Genesis 2–3.
[^4-8]: Hebrew *qiqayon*, probably the castor-oil plant, which grows fast and has large leaves. Traditionally "gourd".
[^4-9]: Hebrew *ra'ah*, "evil, misery", again the word of verse 1 and 3:10.
[^4-10]: Hebrew *charishit*, found only here. The meaning is uncertain.
[^4-11]: Literally "He asked for his *nephesh* to die".
[^4-12]: Or "You had pity on". Hebrew *chus*, "to pity, to spare". Also in verse 11.
[^4-13]: Literally "which was the son of a night and perished as the son of a night".
[^4-14]: Perhaps young children, or people who do not know right from wrong. The book ends with the LORD's question.
