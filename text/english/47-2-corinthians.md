## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus[^1-1] by the will of God, and our brother Timothy,[^1-2] to the church of God in Corinth, together with all God's holy people[^1-3] throughout Achaia:[^1-4]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort,[^1-5]

**4** who comforts us in all our affliction,[^1-6] so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.

**5** For just as the sufferings of Christ overflow to us, so also through Christ our comfort overflows.

**6** If we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which is at work when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we also suffer.

**7** And our hope for you is firm,[^1-7] because we know that just as you share in the sufferings, so also you share in the comfort.

**8** For we do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters,[^1-8] of the affliction we experienced in Asia.[^1-9] We were so utterly weighed down, beyond our strength, that we despaired even of life.

**9** Indeed, we felt within ourselves that we had received the sentence of death, so that we would rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead.

**10** He rescued us from so deadly a peril,[^1-10] and he will rescue us. On him we have set our hope that he will rescue us again,

**11** as you also join in helping us by your prayers, so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the gift granted to us through the prayers of many.[^1-11]

**12** For this is our boast, the testimony of our conscience: that we have conducted ourselves in the world, and especially toward you, with the holiness[^1-12] and sincerity that come from God, not by worldly wisdom[^1-13] but by the grace of God.

**13** For we write you nothing other than what you can read and understand,[^1-14] and I hope that you will understand fully,[^1-15]

**14** just as you have understood us in part, that we are your reason for boasting, as you also are ours, on the day of our Lord Jesus.

**15** Because I was confident of this, I wanted to come to you first, so that you might have a second benefit.[^1-16]

**16** I wanted to visit you on my way to Macedonia, and to come back to you from Macedonia, and to be sent on my way by you to Judea.

**17** So when I wanted to do this, did I act lightly? Or do I make my plans according to the flesh,[^1-17] so that with me it is "Yes, yes" and "No, no" at the same time?

**18** As surely as God is faithful, our word to you is not "Yes" and "No".

**19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, whom we proclaimed among you, that is, I and Silvanus[^1-18] and Timothy, was not "Yes" and "No"; in him it has always been "Yes".

**20** For all the promises of God find their "Yes" in him. That is why it is also through him that the "Amen"[^1-19] is spoken through us to the glory of God.

**21** Now it is God who establishes us with you in Christ and has anointed us,[^1-20]

**22** who has also put his seal on us and given us the Spirit in our hearts as a down payment.[^1-21]

**23** I call on God as my witness, on my life,[^1-22] that it was to spare you that I did not come again to Corinth.

**24** Not that we lord it over your faith, but we are workers with you for your joy, for you stand firm by faith.

[^1-1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name. Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-2]: Literally "Timothy the brother".
[^1-3]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^1-4]: The Roman province of southern Greece, with Corinth as its capital.
[^1-5]: Greek *paraklesis*, "comfort, encouragement, appeal", and the related verb *parakaleo*; so throughout verses 3–7.
[^1-6]: Greek *thlipsis*, "pressure, affliction, trouble"; also in verses 6 and 8.
[^1-7]: Many manuscripts arrange verses 6–7 differently: "(6) If we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation, which is at work when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we also suffer. (7) And our hope for you is firm; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort and salvation, because we know …"
[^1-8]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-9]: The Roman province of Asia, with Ephesus as its chief city.
[^1-10]: Literally "from so great a death".
[^1-11]: The Greek is compressed; literally "so that from many faces the gift to us through many may be given thanks for on our behalf".
[^1-12]: Many manuscripts read "with frankness" (or "simplicity", "single-mindedness"), Greek *haplotes*.
[^1-13]: Literally "fleshly wisdom".
[^1-14]: The Greek plays on *anaginosko*, "read", and *epiginosko*, "understand, recognize"; also in verse 14.
[^1-15]: Or "to the end".
[^1-16]: Literally "a second grace", perhaps the benefit of a second visit. Greek *charis*. Some manuscripts read "a second joy".
[^1-17]: Greek *sarx*, here in Paul's moral sense: human nature in its weakness and opposition to God. That is, by merely human, self-interested standards.
[^1-18]: Probably the Silas of Acts 15:40–18:5. *Silvanus* is a Latin form of the name.
[^1-19]: A Hebrew word meaning "it is firm, it is true", said to affirm a prayer or a promise.
[^1-20]: *Anointed* (Greek *chrio*) is related to *Christos*, "Christ", "Anointed One", earlier in the verse.
[^1-21]: Greek *arrabon*, a first payment that guarantees the rest (also 5:5).
[^1-22]: Literally "upon my soul", a form of oath.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** For I made up my mind[^2-1] not to come to you again in grief.[^2-2]

**2** For if I cause you grief, who is there to make me glad but the one whom I have grieved?

**3** And I wrote as I did[^2-3] so that when I came I would not be grieved by those who ought to make me rejoice, for I am confident about all of you that my joy is the joy of all of you.

**4** For I wrote to you out of much affliction and anguish of heart and with many tears, not to grieve you, but to let you know the love that I have especially for you.

**5** If anyone has caused grief, he has not grieved me, but to some extent, not to put it too heavily, he has grieved all of you.[^2-4]

**6** For such a person, this punishment by the majority is enough,

**7** so that now, instead, you should rather forgive and comfort him, so that he will not be overwhelmed[^2-5] by excessive grief.

**8** So I urge you to reaffirm your love for him.

**9** For this is why I wrote: to test you and know whether you are obedient in everything.

**10** Anyone you forgive, I also forgive. Indeed, what I have forgiven, if I have forgiven anything, has been for your sake in the presence of Christ,[^2-6]

**11** so that we may not be outwitted by Satan, for we are not unaware of his schemes.

**12** When I came to Troas for the good news of Christ, even though a door was opened for me in the Lord,

**13** I had no rest in my spirit because I did not find my brother Titus[^2-7] there. So I said goodbye to them and went on to Macedonia.

**14** But thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumphal procession[^2-8] in Christ, and through us spreads in every place the fragrance of the knowledge of him.

**15** For we are the pleasing aroma[^2-9] of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing,

**16** to the one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life. And who is adequate for these things?

**17** For we are not, like so many,[^2-10] peddlers[^2-11] of the word of God; but as people of sincerity, as people sent from God, we speak in Christ before God.

[^2-1]: Many manuscripts read "But I made up my mind".
[^2-2]: Or "not to make you another painful visit". Greek *lype*, "grief, sorrow, pain", and the related verb *lypeo*, "grieve, cause grief"; so throughout verses 1–7.
[^2-3]: Literally "I wrote this very thing". Paul refers to a letter written "with many tears" (verse 4; 7:8). Whether it is 1 Corinthians, a letter now lost, or a letter partly preserved in chapters 10–13 is debated.
[^2-4]: Or "but in part, so that I may not burden all of you".
[^2-5]: Literally "swallowed up".
[^2-6]: Or "as Christ's representative". Literally "in the face of Christ".
[^2-7]: One of Paul's fellow workers (7:6; 8:23; Galatians 2:1).
[^2-8]: Greek *thriambeuo*, used of a Roman general's victory procession, in which conquered captives were led through the city. Paul may picture himself as one of the captives, or as sharing in the triumph. Incense was burned along the route.
[^2-9]: Greek *euodia*, the word the Septuagint uses for the "pleasing aroma" of sacrifices offered to God (for example Genesis 8:21; Leviticus 1:9).
[^2-10]: Many manuscripts read "like the rest".
[^2-11]: Greek *kapeleuo*, to sell goods as a retailer, often with the suggestion of cheating.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, as some do, letters of recommendation to you or from you?

**2** You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts, known and read by everyone.

**3** You show that you are a letter from Christ, delivered by us,[^3-1] written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets that are hearts of flesh.[^3-2]

**4** Such is the confidence we have through Christ toward God.

**5** Not that we are adequate[^3-3] in ourselves to claim anything as coming from us; our adequacy comes from God,

**6** who has made us adequate to be servants[^3-4] of a new covenant,[^3-5] not of the letter but of the Spirit. For the letter[^3-6] kills, but the Spirit gives life.

**7** Now if the ministry[^3-7] of death, carved in letters on stone, came with such glory that the Israelites could not gaze at Moses' face because of the glory of his face,[^3-8] a glory that was being brought to an end,[^3-9]

**8** how much more will the ministry of the Spirit come with glory?

**9** For if there was glory in the ministry of condemnation, much more does the ministry of righteousness overflow with glory.

**10** Indeed, what had glory has in this respect come to have no glory, because of the glory that surpasses it.

**11** For if what was being brought to an end came with glory, much more does what remains have glory.

**12** Since, then, we have such a hope, we act with great boldness,[^3-10]

**13** and not like Moses, who would put a covering[^3-11] over his face so that the Israelites could not gaze at the end[^3-12] of what was being brought to an end.

**14** But their minds were hardened. For to this very day, at the reading of the old covenant,[^3-13] the same covering remains, not lifted, because it is brought to an end in Christ.[^3-14]

**15** Indeed, to this day whenever Moses is read, a covering lies over their hearts.

**16** But whenever someone turns to the Lord, the covering is removed.[^3-15]

**17** Now the Lord is the Spirit,[^3-16] and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom.

**18** And we all, with uncovered faces, beholding as in a mirror[^3-17] the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another,[^3-18] as from the Lord, the Spirit.[^3-19]

[^3-1]: Literally "served by us". Greek *diakoneo*, related to *diakonia*, "ministry", in verses 7–9.
[^3-2]: Compare Exodus 31:18; Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 36:26 ("a heart of flesh").
[^3-3]: Greek *hikanos*, "adequate, sufficient, competent", the word used in 2:16; so also "adequacy" and "made us adequate" in verses 5–6.
[^3-4]: Or "ministers". Greek *diakonoi*.
[^3-5]: Compare Jeremiah 31:31 and 1 Corinthians 11:25.
[^3-6]: Greek *gramma*, "letter, what is written", here probably the law written on stone (verse 7).
[^3-7]: Greek *diakonia*, "service, ministry"; also in verses 8 and 9.
[^3-8]: Exodus 34:29–35. The Hebrew says that the skin of Moses' face "was shining"; the Septuagint says that it "had been glorified".
[^3-9]: Greek *katargeo*, "make ineffective, abolish, bring to an end" (see 1 Corinthians 1:28); also in verses 11, 13 and 14. In verses 7, 11 and 13 it is often translated "fading".
[^3-10]: Greek *parresia*, "boldness, frankness, openness".
[^3-11]: Greek *kalymma*, the word the Septuagint uses in Exodus 34:33–35 for the covering Moses put over his face (Hebrew *masveh*), traditionally "veil"; also in verses 14–16.
[^3-12]: Greek *telos*, "end, goal, outcome".
[^3-13]: Here the scriptures of Moses, read in the synagogue (verse 15).
[^3-14]: Or "the same covering remains, since it is not revealed that in Christ it is being brought to an end". What is "brought to an end" may be the covering or the old covenant.
[^3-15]: Compare Exodus 34:34, "whenever Moses went in before Yahweh to speak with him, he would take the covering off". The subject of "turns" is not stated: it may be anyone, "their hearts" (verse 15), or Moses. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" in Exodus 34:34 is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^3-16]: "The Lord" here takes up the word from verse 16.
[^3-17]: Or "reflecting". Greek *katoptrizomai*, from *katoptron*, "mirror".
[^3-18]: Literally "from glory to glory".
[^3-19]: Or "from the Lord who is the Spirit", or "from the Spirit of the Lord".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Therefore, since we have this ministry[^4-1] through God's mercy,[^4-2] we do not lose heart.

**2** We have renounced the shameful things that are done in secret. We do not live by cunning or distort the word of God, but by openly stating the truth we commend ourselves to everyone's conscience before God.

**3** And even if our good news is covered,[^4-3] it is covered to those who are perishing.

**4** In their case the god of this age[^4-4] has blinded the minds of unbelievers, so that they do not see the light[^4-5] of the good news of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God.

**5** For we do not proclaim ourselves, but Christ Jesus as Lord, and ourselves as your servants[^4-6] for Jesus' sake.

**6** For it is God who said, "Light will shine out of darkness,"[^4-7] who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Christ.

**7** But we have this treasure in clay jars, to show that the extraordinary power belongs to God and does not come from us.

**8** We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair;

**9** persecuted, but not abandoned; struck down, but not destroyed.

**10** We always carry around in the body the dying of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be revealed in our body.

**11** For we who are alive are always being handed over to death for Jesus' sake, so that the life of Jesus may also be revealed in our mortal flesh.

**12** So death is at work in us, but life in you.

**13** Since we have the same spirit of faith[^4-8] as is shown in what is written, "I believed, therefore I spoke,"[^4-9] we also believe, and therefore we also speak,

**14** knowing that the one who raised Jesus[^4-10] will also raise us with Jesus[^4-11] and bring us together with you into his presence.

**15** For all this is for your sake, so that grace, as it reaches more and more people, may make thanksgiving overflow, to the glory of God.

**16** So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer self[^4-12] is wasting away, our inner self is being renewed day by day.

**17** For our light and momentary affliction is producing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison,

**18** because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen. For what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.

[^4-1]: Greek *diakonia*, "service, ministry" (see 3:7).
[^4-2]: Literally "as we have received mercy".
[^4-3]: Greek *kekalymmenon*, related to *kalymma*, "covering" (3:13–16); also later in the verse.
[^4-4]: Most interpreters take this to mean Satan (compare 11:14).
[^4-5]: Or "so that the light … does not dawn on them". Greek *photismos*, "illumination, light"; also in verse 6.
[^4-6]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^4-7]: Compare Genesis 1:3 and Isaiah 9:1 (9:2 in most English Bibles). Many manuscripts read "Let light shine out of darkness".
[^4-8]: Or "the same Spirit of faith".
[^4-9]: Psalm 116:10, following the Septuagint (where it is Psalm 115:1). The Hebrew can be read "I believed, even when I spoke" (see the note there).
[^4-10]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord Jesus". The SBLGNT leaves out "the Lord", following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort.
[^4-11]: Many manuscripts read "through Jesus".
[^4-12]: Literally "our outer human being"; and "our inner one".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** For we know that if the earthly tent we live in[^5-1] is torn down, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.

**2** For in this one we groan, longing to put on over it[^5-2] our dwelling from heaven,

**3** if indeed, when we have put it on,[^5-3] we will not be found naked.

**4** For while we are in this tent, we groan under our burden, because we do not want to be unclothed but to be clothed over, so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life.

**5** The one who has prepared us for this very thing is God, who has given us the Spirit as a down payment.[^5-4]

**6** So we are always confident, and we know that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord,

**7** for we live[^5-5] by faith, not by sight—

**8** yes, we are confident, and we would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord.

**9** So whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to please him.

**10** For all of us must appear[^5-6] before the judgment seat[^5-7] of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or bad.

**11** Therefore, since we know the fear of the Lord, we try to persuade people. But what we are is plain to God, and I hope it is also plain to your consciences.

**12** We are not commending ourselves to you again, but giving you an opportunity to boast about us, so that you may have an answer for those who boast about outward appearance[^5-8] and not about what is in the heart.

**13** For if we are out of our minds, it is for God; if we are in our right mind, it is for you.

**14** For the love of Christ[^5-9] controls us,[^5-10] because we have concluded this: that one has died for all;[^5-11] therefore all have died.

**15** And he died for all, so that those who live would no longer live for themselves but for him who died for them and was raised.

**16** So from now on we know no one according to the flesh.[^5-12] Even if we have known Christ according to the flesh, we no longer know him so.

**17** So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation.[^5-13] The old has passed away; look, the new has come.[^5-14]

**18** All this is from God, who reconciled us to himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation,

**19** that is, in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself,[^5-15] not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation.

**20** So we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making his appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: be reconciled to God.

**21** He made him who knew no sin to be sin[^5-16] for our sake, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.[^5-17]

[^5-1]: Literally "our earthly house of the tent", a picture of the present body.
[^5-2]: Greek *ependyomai*, "put on over", as one garment over another; also in verse 4.
[^5-3]: Some manuscripts read "when we have taken it off".
[^5-4]: Greek *arrabon* (see 1:22).
[^5-5]: Literally "walk". *By sight*: or "by what we see"; literally "through appearance".
[^5-6]: Or "be revealed", "be made known". Greek *phaneroo*, "make visible", translated "plain" twice in verse 11.
[^5-7]: Greek *bema*, the raised platform from which a Roman official heard cases (compare Acts 18:12).
[^5-8]: Literally "in face".
[^5-9]: Most interpreters take this as Christ's love for us; it can also mean our love for Christ.
[^5-10]: Or "compels us", "holds us fast". Greek *synecho*.
[^5-11]: Many manuscripts read "that if one has died for all".
[^5-12]: Greek *kata sarka*, "according to the flesh": by human, worldly standards (see 1:17); also later in the verse. Whether "according to the flesh" describes the way of knowing (a worldly way of knowing Christ) or Christ himself (Christ as a human being) is debated; most take it in the first way.
[^5-13]: Or "he is a new creation". Compare Isaiah 43:18–19 and 65:17.
[^5-14]: Many manuscripts read "look, all things have become new".
[^5-15]: Or "that God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself".
[^5-16]: "He" is God (verse 20). The phrase "made him … to be sin" is much discussed. Some interpreters understand "to be a sin offering": the Hebrew *chatta't* means both "sin" and "sin offering", and the Septuagint sometimes uses *hamartia*, "sin", in the second sense (compare Romans 8:3, "as a sin offering"). Others take it to mean that God treated Christ as a sinner, or that Christ bore sin and its consequences on behalf of others. The phrase "knew no sin" means that he had no sin of his own.
[^5-17]: Greek *dikaiosyne theou*. As in Romans 1:17, it may mean God's own righteousness, his justice and faithfulness, or the right standing that comes from God. Either way, Paul says that "we might become" it "in him".

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** As we work together with him,[^6-1] we also urge you not to receive the grace of God in vain.

**2** For he says, "At an acceptable time I listened to you, and on a day of salvation I helped you."[^6-2] Look, now is the truly acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation.

**3** We put no obstacle in anyone's way, so that our ministry will not be discredited,

**4** but as servants of God we commend ourselves in every way: through great endurance, in afflictions, in hardships, in distresses,

**5** in beatings, in imprisonments, in riots, in labors, in sleepless nights, in hunger,[^6-3]

**6** by purity, by knowledge, by patience, by kindness, by the Holy Spirit,[^6-4] by sincere love,

**7** by truthful speech,[^6-5] by the power of God; with the weapons of righteousness for the right hand and for the left,[^6-6]

**8** through glory and dishonor, through slander and praise. We are treated as deceivers, and yet are true;

**9** as unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and look, we live; as disciplined, and yet not put to death;[^6-7]

**10** as grieving, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.

**11** We have spoken openly to you,[^6-8] Corinthians; our heart is wide open.

**12** You are not restricted by us, but you are restricted in your own affections.[^6-9]

**13** In return (I speak as to children), open wide your hearts too.

**14** Do not be unequally yoked[^6-10] with unbelievers. For what partnership does righteousness have with lawlessness? Or what fellowship does light have with darkness?[^6-11]

**15** What agreement does Christ have with Beliar?[^6-12] Or what share does a believer have with an unbeliever?

**16** What agreement does the temple of God have with idols? For we are the temple of the living God;[^6-13] as God said, "I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they will be my people."[^6-14]

**17** Therefore, "Come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord, and touch nothing unclean; then I will welcome you,[^6-15]

**18** and I will be a father to you, and you will be sons and daughters to me, says the Lord Almighty."[^6-16]

[^6-1]: Literally "working together". "With him", that is, with God (5:20), is implied; some take it as "with you".
[^6-2]: Isaiah 49:8, following the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "In a time of favor I have answered you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you."
[^6-3]: Or "in fastings".
[^6-4]: Or "by holiness of spirit".
[^6-5]: Or "by the message of truth". Literally "by the word of truth".
[^6-6]: Perhaps a weapon in the right hand for attack and a shield in the left for defense.
[^6-7]: Compare Psalm 118:18.
[^6-8]: Literally "Our mouth has been opened to you".
[^6-9]: Literally "in your inward parts", thought of as the seat of the emotions.
[^6-10]: Literally "Do not become mismatched in a yoke", a picture drawn from the law against yoking different kinds of animals together (Leviticus 19:19; Deuteronomy 22:10).
[^6-11]: Verses 6:14–7:1 raise a well-known question about the unity of the letter. The passage interrupts Paul's appeal to the Corinthians to open their hearts, which 7:2 ("Make room for us") seems to continue directly from 6:13, and it uses several words found nowhere else in Paul's letters, such as "Beliar". Some scholars think it is part of another letter (perhaps the one mentioned in 1 Corinthians 5:9) or a piece by another author that was later added here; others think Paul himself wrote it here as part of his appeal. The question arises from the content of the passage, not from differences in the manuscripts recorded in the SBLGNT apparatus.
[^6-12]: A name for Satan, found here only in the New Testament. It comes from the Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthlessness" (compare Deuteronomy 13:14, 13:13 in most English Bibles), and is used in Jewish writings of the time, including the Dead Sea Scrolls.
[^6-13]: Many manuscripts read "you are the temple of the living God".
[^6-14]: Combining Leviticus 26:11–12 and Ezekiel 37:27.
[^6-15]: Isaiah 52:11 ("Touch nothing unclean. Go out from the middle of her; purify yourselves"), close to the Septuagint. "I will welcome you" recalls Ezekiel 20:34 in the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "I will gather you".
[^6-16]: Compare 2 Samuel 7:14 ("I will be his father") and Isaiah 43:6 ("my sons … my daughters"). *The Lord Almighty*: Greek *kyrios pantokrator*, the Septuagint's usual rendering of "Yahweh of Armies" (as in 2 Samuel 7:8). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** Since we have these promises, dear friends,[^7-1] let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit,[^7-2] bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.

**2** Make room for us.[^7-3] We have wronged no one, we have ruined no one, we have taken advantage of no one.

**3** I do not say this to condemn you, for I have said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.

**4** I speak to you with great frankness;[^7-4] I have great reason to boast about you. I am filled with comfort; in all our affliction I am overflowing with joy.

**5** For even when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest.[^7-5] We were afflicted in every way: fights outside, fears inside.

**6** But God, who comforts the downcast,[^7-6] comforted us by the arrival of Titus,

**7** and not only by his arrival but also by the comfort with which he had been comforted by you, as he told us of your longing, your mourning, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced still more.

**8** For even if I grieved you with my letter,[^7-7] I do not regret it. Though I did regret it (I see that that letter grieved you, though only for a while),

**9** now I rejoice, not because you were grieved, but because your grief led to repentance. For you were grieved as God intended,[^7-8] so that you suffered no loss through us.
