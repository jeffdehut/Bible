## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus[^1-1] by command of God our Savior and of Christ Jesus our hope,

**2** to Timothy, my true child in the faith: Grace, mercy and peace from God the Father[^1-2] and Christ Jesus our Lord.

**3** I urged you, as I was going to Macedonia, to stay on in Ephesus,[^1-3] so that you might instruct certain people not to teach any different doctrine

**4** or to devote themselves to myths and endless genealogies, which promote speculations rather than God's plan,[^1-4] which works by faith.

**5** But the aim of our instruction is love that comes from a pure heart and a good conscience and sincere faith.

**6** Some have strayed from these things and turned aside to empty talk,

**7** wanting to be teachers of the Law, though they understand neither what they are saying nor the things about which they make confident assertions.

**8** Now we know that the Law is good, if one uses it lawfully,[^1-5]

**9** understanding this, that the Law is not laid down for a righteous person but for the lawless and rebellious, for the ungodly and sinners, for the unholy and profane, for those who kill their fathers or their mothers,[^1-6] for murderers,

**10** the sexually immoral, men who lie with males,[^1-7] slave traders,[^1-8] liars, perjurers, and whatever else is contrary to sound teaching,

**11** in keeping with the good news of the glory of the blessed God, with which I have been entrusted.

**12** I thank Christ Jesus our Lord, who has strengthened me, because he considered me faithful, appointing me to his service,

**13** though formerly I was a blasphemer and a persecutor and a violent man. But I was shown mercy because I acted in ignorance and unbelief,

**14** and the grace of our Lord overflowed for me, with the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus.

**15** The saying is trustworthy[^1-9] and deserves full acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost.[^1-10]

**16** But I received mercy for this reason, that in me, the foremost, Christ Jesus might display his perfect patience, as an example to those who were going to believe in him for eternal life.

**17** To the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God,[^1-11] be honor and glory forever and ever.[^1-12] Amen.

**18** This instruction I entrust to you, Timothy, my child, in keeping with the prophecies once made about you, so that by them you may fight the good fight,[^1-13]

**19** holding on to faith and a good conscience. By rejecting conscience, some have suffered shipwreck in regard to the faith.

**20** Among them are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I have handed over to Satan, so that they may be taught not to blaspheme.

[^1-1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-2]: Many manuscripts read "God our Father".
[^1-3]: Literally "Just as I urged you to remain in Ephesus …". The Greek sentence is left unfinished; the sense is "so I urge you now".
[^1-4]: Greek *oikonomia*, "management, stewardship, plan", here the ordering of God's household; or "God's training". *Which works by faith*: literally "which is in faith".
[^1-5]: There is a play on words: *nomos*, "law", and *nomimos*, "lawfully".
[^1-6]: Or "those who strike their fathers or their mothers". The Greek words can mean either.
[^1-7]: Greek *arsenokoitai*, a rare compound of *arsen*, "male", and *koite*, "bed", which stand side by side in the Septuagint of Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13. Its exact reference is debated; see the note on 1 Corinthians 6:9.
[^1-8]: Or "kidnappers". Greek *andrapodistai*, people who captured others to sell them as slaves.
[^1-9]: The formula "The saying is trustworthy" occurs five times in the letters to Timothy and Titus (also 3:1; 4:9; 2 Timothy 2:11; Titus 3:8).
[^1-10]: Or "the worst"; also in verse 16. Greek *protos*, "first".
[^1-11]: Many manuscripts read "the only wise God".
[^1-12]: Literally "to the ages of the ages".
[^1-13]: Literally "wage the good warfare".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** First of all, then, I urge that petitions, prayers, intercessions and thanksgivings be made for all people,

**2** for kings and for all who are in authority, so that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life in all godliness and dignity.

**3** This is good, and it pleases God our Savior,

**4** who wants all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

**5** For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and humankind, the human being[^2-1] Christ Jesus,

**6** who gave himself as a ransom[^2-2] for all. This was the testimony given at the proper time.[^2-3]

**7** For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle (I am telling the truth,[^2-4] I am not lying), a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

**8** So I want the men[^2-5] in every place to pray, lifting up holy hands without anger or argument.

**9** Likewise I want the women to adorn themselves in respectable clothing,[^2-6] with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold or pearls or expensive clothes,

**10** but with what is fitting for women who profess godliness: with good works.

**11** A woman should learn in quietness[^2-7] with full submission.[^2-8]

**12** I do not permit a woman to teach or to have authority[^2-9] over a man; she is to be in quietness.

**13** For Adam was formed first, then Eve;[^2-10]

**14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and fell into transgression.[^2-11]

**15** But she will be saved through childbearing, if they continue in faith and love and holiness, with self-control.[^2-12]

[^2-1]: Greek *anthropos*, "human being", the same word as "humankind" (*anthropoi*) earlier in the verse and "people" in verses 1 and 4.
[^2-2]: Greek *antilytron*, "ransom, price paid for release". Compare Mark 10:45.
[^2-3]: Literally "the testimony at its own times".
[^2-4]: Many manuscripts add "in Christ".
[^2-5]: Greek *andres*. In verses 8–15, *aner* means "man" or "husband" and *gyne* "woman" or "wife". Interpreters differ over whether these verses concern men and women in general, or husbands and wives; the passage moves between plural (verses 9–10) and singular (verses 11–12), and between Adam and Eve (verses 13–14) and "she" and "they" (verse 15).
[^2-6]: The verb "pray" in verse 8 may also carry over: "Likewise I want the women to pray …, adorning themselves in respectable clothing".
[^2-7]: Greek *hesychia*, "quietness, calm", also "silence"; also in verse 12. The related adjective is used in verse 2 ("a quiet life").
[^2-8]: Greek *hypotage*, "subjection, submission", from the same root as *hypotasso* (see Ephesians 5:21). The text does not say to whom.
[^2-9]: Greek *authenteo*, a rare verb found only here in the New Testament. Its sense is debated; proposed meanings include "have authority over", "exercise authority", "dominate" and "assume authority". Whether "over a man" belongs with "teach" as well as with this verb is also debated. *I do not permit*: the present tense can also mean "I am not permitting".
[^2-10]: Compare Genesis 2:7, 21–22.
[^2-11]: Literally "was thoroughly deceived and has come into transgression". Compare Genesis 3:13.
[^2-12]: The Greek has "she" (singular) with the first verb and "they" (plural) with the second. "She" may be the woman of verse 14 (Eve) or any woman; "they" may be women, or the woman and her husband, or her children. "Through childbearing" (in Greek, "the childbearing") has been understood as "by bearing children", "kept safe through childbirth", or "through the birth of the Child" (compare Genesis 3:15). *Saved* may mean salvation or being kept safe.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** The saying is trustworthy:[^3-1] whoever aspires to the office of overseer[^3-2] desires a noble task.

**2** An overseer, then, must be above reproach, the husband of one wife,[^3-3] sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach,

**3** not a drunkard, not violent[^3-4] but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money.

**4** He must manage his own household well, keeping his children under control with all dignity

**5** (for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he take care of God's church?).

**6** He must not be a recent convert, so that he may not become conceited and fall into the condemnation of the devil.[^3-5]

**7** Moreover, he must have a good reputation with outsiders, so that he may not fall into disgrace and the snare of the devil.

**8** Deacons[^3-6] likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not given to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain.

**9** They must hold to the mystery[^3-7] of the faith with a clear conscience.

**10** And let them also be tested first; then let them serve if they prove blameless.

**11** Women[^3-8] likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober-minded, faithful in all things.

**12** Deacons must each be the husband of one wife, managing their children and their own households well.

**13** For those who have served well gain a good standing for themselves and great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**14** I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon,

**15** but if I am delayed, I write so that you may know how people ought to conduct themselves[^3-9] in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, a pillar and foundation of the truth.

**16** And undeniably great is the mystery of godliness: He[^3-10] was revealed in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit,[^3-11] seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations, believed in the world, taken up in glory.[^3-12]

[^3-1]: See 1:15. Some take "The saying is trustworthy" with the end of 2:15 instead.
[^3-2]: Greek *episkope*, "oversight". *Overseer* in verse 2 is *episkopos*, traditionally "bishop".
[^3-3]: Literally "a man of one woman"; also in verse 12. The phrase may mean married only once, faithful to his wife, or not having more than one wife.
[^3-4]: Many manuscripts add "not greedy for dishonest gain".
[^3-5]: Or "the judgment that the devil fell under"; or "the judgment brought by the devil". Greek *diabolos* means "slanderer"; compare "slanderers" (*diaboloi*) in verse 11.
[^3-6]: Greek *diakonoi*, "servants", here people holding a recognized role in the church; traditionally "deacons"; also in verse 12. The verb "serve" in verses 10 and 13 is *diakoneo*.
[^3-7]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it; also in verse 16.
[^3-8]: Greek *gynaikes*, "women" or "wives". This may mean the wives of deacons, or women who serve as deacons (compare Phoebe, called a *diakonos* of the church in Romans 16:1). The Greek has no "their".
[^3-9]: Or "how you ought to conduct yourself". The Greek does not name the subject.
[^3-10]: Literally "Who". Many manuscripts read "God was revealed in the flesh". In Greek capital letters the two readings look alike: ΟΣ, "who", and ΘΣ, the usual abbreviation for "God".
[^3-11]: Or "shown to be in the right in the spirit". Greek *dikaioo*, which in Paul's argument about salvation is translated "declare righteous" (traditionally "justify"); here it means "vindicate, prove right". *By the Spirit* may also mean "in the spirit", in contrast with "in the flesh".
[^3-12]: The six short lines of this verse appear to quote an early Christian hymn or creed.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Now the Spirit says explicitly that in later times some will abandon the faith, devoting themselves to deceiving spirits and teachings of demons,

**2** through the hypocrisy of liars whose consciences are seared,[^4-1]

**3** who forbid marriage and demand abstinence from foods[^4-2] that God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth.

**4** For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving,

**5** for it is made holy by the word of God and prayer.

**6** If you put these things before the brothers and sisters,[^4-3] you will be a good servant[^4-4] of Christ Jesus, nourished on the words of the faith and of the good teaching that you have followed.

**7** Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths.[^4-5] Rather, train yourself for godliness;

**8** for physical training is of some value,[^4-6] but godliness is of value in every way, holding promise for the present life and for the life to come.

**9** The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance.[^4-7]

**10** For to this end we toil and suffer reproach,[^4-8] because we have set our hope on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe.

**11** Command and teach these things.

**12** Let no one look down on you because you are young, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love,[^4-9] in faith, in purity.

**13** Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of scripture,[^4-10] to encouragement, to teaching.

**14** Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you through prophecy when the council of elders[^4-11] laid their hands on you.

**15** Practice these things, give yourself wholly to them, so that your progress may be evident to all.

**16** Pay close attention to yourself and to your teaching; persevere in these things, for by doing this you will save both yourself and those who hear you.

[^4-1]: Greek *kausteriazo*, "brand with a hot iron" or "cauterize", that is, made insensitive, or marked as belonging to another.
[^4-2]: Literally "forbidding to marry, to abstain from foods".
[^4-3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^4-4]: Greek *diakonos*, "servant", traditionally "minister".
[^4-5]: Literally "profane and old-womanish myths".
[^4-6]: Literally "is useful for a little", which may mean "for a little" in degree or in time.
[^4-7]: See 1:15. The saying may be the words of verse 8, or of verse 10.
[^4-8]: The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "we toil and strive".
[^4-9]: Many manuscripts add "in spirit".
[^4-10]: Literally "to the reading", the reading aloud of scripture in the gathering of believers.
[^4-11]: Greek *presbyterion*, the elders (*presbyteroi*) as a body.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Do not rebuke an older man,[^5-1] but appeal to him as a father, to younger men as brothers,

**2** to older women as mothers, to younger women as sisters, in all purity.

**3** Honor[^5-2] widows who are truly widows.

**4** But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let them[^5-3] first learn to show godliness toward their own household and to repay their parents,[^5-4] for this is pleasing before God.

**5** The one who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God and continues in petitions and prayers night and day,

**6** but she who lives for pleasure is dead even while she lives.

**7** Command these things as well, so that they may be above reproach.

**8** But if anyone does not provide for their own relatives, and especially for members of their household, they have denied the faith and are worse than an unbeliever.

**9** Let a widow be put on the list if she is not less than sixty years old, the wife of one husband,[^5-5]

**10** and has a reputation for good works: if she has brought up children, if she has shown hospitality, if she has washed the feet of God's holy people,[^5-6] if she has helped those in distress, if she has devoted herself to every good work.

**11** But refuse to enroll younger widows, for when their desires draw them away from Christ,[^5-7] they want to marry,

**12** and so they incur condemnation for having set aside their first pledge.[^5-8]

**13** Besides that, they learn to be idle, going around from house to house, and not only idle but also gossips and busybodies, saying what they should not.

**14** So I want younger widows[^5-9] to marry, bear children, manage their households, and give the adversary[^5-10] no occasion for reproach.

**15** For some have already turned aside to follow Satan.

**16** If any believing woman[^5-11] has widows in her family, let her help them, and let the church not be burdened, so that it may help those who are truly widows.

**17** Let the elders[^5-12] who lead well be considered worthy of double honor,[^5-13] especially those who labor in preaching and teaching.

**18** For the scripture says, "Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain,"[^5-14] and, "The worker deserves his wages."[^5-15]

**19** Do not accept an accusation against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses.[^5-16]

**20** Those who sin, rebuke in the presence of all, so that the rest also may be afraid.

**21** I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen angels to keep these instructions without prejudice, doing nothing out of favoritism.

**22** Do not be hasty in laying hands on anyone, and do not share in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.

**23** No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent illnesses.

**24** The sins of some people are obvious, going before them to judgment, but the sins of others follow after them.

**25** So also good works are obvious, and those that are not cannot remain hidden.

[^5-1]: Greek *presbyteros*, "older man", the word translated "elder" in verses 17 and 19.
[^5-2]: Greek *timao*, "honor", which here may include material support (compare verses 4, 16 and 17).
[^5-3]: That is, the children and grandchildren. Some take "they" to be widows.
[^5-4]: Literally "those who went before them": parents and grandparents.
[^5-5]: Literally "a woman of one man". Compare 3:2.
[^5-6]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^5-7]: Or "when they grow restless against Christ". Greek *katastreniao*, "feel strong desire, grow wanton against".
[^5-8]: Or "their first faith". Greek *pistis*, "faith, faithfulness, pledge".
[^5-9]: Literally "younger women".
[^5-10]: Or "the opponent". This may be a human opponent or Satan (compare verse 15).
[^5-11]: Many manuscripts read "any believing man or woman".
[^5-12]: Greek *presbyteroi*, "elders", leaders of the church; also in verse 19.
[^5-13]: Greek *time*, "honor", also "price, payment"; compare verse 18.
[^5-14]: Deuteronomy 25:4.
[^5-15]: These words are found in Luke 10:7; compare Matthew 10:10.
[^5-16]: Compare Deuteronomy 19:15.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** All who are under the yoke as slaves[^6-1] should regard their own masters as worthy of all honor, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be slandered.[^6-2]

**2** Those who have believing masters must not look down on them because they are brothers and sisters;[^6-3] rather they must serve them all the better, because those who benefit from their good service[^6-4] are believers and beloved. Teach and urge these things.

**3** If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound[^6-5] words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness,

**4** he is conceited and understands nothing, but has a sickly craving for controversies and disputes about words, from which come envy, strife, slanders, evil suspicions,

**5** and constant friction among people who are corrupted in mind and deprived of the truth, who think that godliness is a means of gain.[^6-6]

**6** But godliness with contentment[^6-7] is great gain,

**7** for we brought nothing into the world, so that we cannot take anything out of it either.[^6-8]

**8** But if we have food and clothing,[^6-9] with these we will be content.

**9** But those who want to be rich fall into temptation and a trap and many senseless and harmful desires, which plunge people into ruin and destruction.

**10** For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.[^6-10] Some, by craving it, have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pains.

**11** But you, man of God,[^6-11] flee these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness.

**12** Fight the good fight[^6-12] of the faith; take hold of the eternal life to which you were called; and you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.

**13** I charge you in the presence of God, who gives life to all things, and of Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate[^6-13] made the good confession,

**14** to keep the commandment without stain or reproach until the appearing[^6-14] of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**15** which he will display at the proper time,[^6-15] he who is the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords,[^6-16]

**16** who alone has immortality, who dwells in unapproachable light, whom no human being has seen or can see. To him be honor and eternal dominion. Amen.

**17** As for those who are rich in this present age, command them not to be arrogant, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on God,[^6-17] who richly provides us with everything to enjoy;

**18** to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share,

**19** storing up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of the life that is truly life.[^6-18]

**20** Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you.[^6-19] Avoid the irreverent, empty chatter and the contradictions of what is falsely called knowledge,[^6-20]

**21** which some have professed and so have swerved from the faith. Grace be with you all.[^6-21]

[^6-1]: Greek *douloi*, "slaves". *Masters* in verses 1–2 is *despotai*, "owners". Slavery was part of the social order of the Roman world, and some members of the churches were slaves or owned slaves (compare Ephesians 6:5–9).
[^6-2]: Compare Isaiah 52:5, quoted in Romans 2:24. *Slandered*: Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^6-3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", here fellow believers.
[^6-4]: Or "because those who devote themselves to doing good", taking the words to describe the masters' kindness. Greek *euergesia*, "a good deed, benefit".
[^6-5]: Greek *hygiaino*, "be healthy"; compare "sickly" (*noseo*, "be sick") in verse 4.
[^6-6]: Many manuscripts add "Withdraw from such people."
[^6-7]: Greek *autarkeia*, "self-sufficiency, contentment". Compare Philippians 4:11.
[^6-8]: Literally "because we cannot take anything out either"; the connection of the two clauses is difficult. Many manuscripts read "and it is clear that we cannot take anything out". Compare Job 1:21; Ecclesiastes 5:14 (5:15 in most English Bibles).
[^6-9]: Or "covering", which may include shelter as well as clothing.
[^6-10]: Or "the root of all evils".
[^6-11]: A title used in the Old Testament for Moses and the prophets (Deuteronomy 33:1; 1 Kings 17:18).
[^6-12]: Or "Compete well in the contest". The words come from athletic contests.
[^6-13]: Or "in the time of Pontius Pilate".
[^6-14]: Greek *epiphaneia*, "appearance, manifestation".
[^6-15]: Literally "at his own times".
[^6-16]: Literally "the King of those who reign and Lord of those who rule as lords".
[^6-17]: Many manuscripts read "on the living God".
[^6-18]: Many manuscripts read "take hold of eternal life".
[^6-19]: Greek *paratheke*, "a deposit", something entrusted to another for safekeeping.
[^6-20]: Greek *gnosis*, "knowledge".
[^6-21]: "You" is plural in Greek. Many manuscripts read "Grace be with you" (singular), followed by "Amen".
