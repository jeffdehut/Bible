## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** After the death of Joshua, the Israelites asked Yahweh,[^1-1] "Which of us is to go up first against the Canaanites, to fight against them?"

**2** Yahweh said, "Judah is to go up. I have handed the land over to him."[^1-2]

**3** Judah said to his brother Simeon, "Come up with me into my allotted territory,[^1-3] and let us fight against the Canaanites. Then I too will go with you into your allotted territory." So Simeon went with him.

**4** Judah went up, and Yahweh handed the Canaanites and the Perizzites over to them. They defeated ten thousand men at Bezek.

**5** At Bezek they found Adoni-bezek[^1-4] and fought against him, and they defeated the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

**6** Adoni-bezek fled, but they chased him, caught him, and cut off his thumbs and his big toes.[^1-5]

**7** Adoni-bezek said, "Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to pick up scraps under my table. As I have done, so God has paid me back." They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

**8** The people of Judah[^1-6] fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They put it to the sword[^1-7] and set the city on fire.

**9** Afterward the people of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites who lived in the hill country, in the Negev[^1-8] and in the western foothills.[^1-9]

**10** Judah went against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron (Hebron was formerly called Kiriath-arba),[^1-10] and they defeated Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.[^1-11]

**11** From there Judah went against the people living in Debir. (Debir was formerly called Kiriath-sepher.)[^1-12]

**12** Caleb said, "Whoever attacks Kiriath-sepher and captures it, I will give him my daughter Achsah as his wife."

**13** Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother,[^1-13] captured it, and Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah as his wife.

**14** When she came to him, she urged him[^1-14] to ask her father for a field. She got down from her donkey,[^1-15] and Caleb said to her, "What do you want?"

**15** She said to him, "Give me a blessing.[^1-16] Since you have given me land in the Negev,[^1-17] give me springs of water too." So Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.[^1-18]

**16** The descendants of the Kenite,[^1-19] Moses' father-in-law, went up with the people of Judah from the City of Palms[^1-20] into the wilderness of Judah, which is in the Negev near Arad. They went and settled among the people.[^1-21]

**17** Judah went with his brother Simeon, and they defeated the Canaanites who lived in Zephath. They devoted the city to destruction,[^1-22] and so it was named Hormah.[^1-23]

**18** Judah also captured Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, and Ekron and its territory.[^1-24]

**19** Yahweh was with Judah, and Judah took possession of the hill country. But they could not drive out the people living in the plain,[^1-25] because they had iron chariots.[^1-26]

**20** Hebron was given to Caleb, as Moses had promised,[^1-27] and he drove out from there the three sons of Anak.[^1-28]

**21** But the Benjaminites did not drive out the Jebusites who lived in Jerusalem. So the Jebusites have lived with the Benjaminites in Jerusalem to this day.

**22** The house of Joseph[^1-29] also went up against Bethel, and Yahweh was with them.

**23** The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel. (The city was formerly called Luz.)

**24** The men keeping watch saw a man coming out of the city, and they said to him, "Please show us the way into the city, and we will treat you kindly."[^1-30]

**25** He showed them the way into the city, and they put the city to the sword, but they let the man and his whole family go.

**26** The man went to the land of the Hittites and built a city. He named it Luz, and that is its name to this day.

**27** But Manasseh did not drive out Beth-shean and its villages,[^1-31] or Taanach and its villages, or the people living in Dor[^1-32] and its villages, or the people living in Ibleam and its villages, or the people living in Megiddo and its villages. The Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.

**28** When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor,[^1-33] but they did not drive them out completely.

**29** Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer, so the Canaanites lived among them in Gezer.

**30** Zebulun did not drive out the people living in Kitron or the people living in Nahalol. So the Canaanites lived among them, and they became forced laborers.

**31** Asher did not drive out the people living in Acco or the people living in Sidon, or Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik or Rehob.

**32** So the Asherites lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land, because they did not drive them out.

**33** Naphtali did not drive out the people living in Beth-shemesh or the people living in Beth-anath. So they lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land, but the people of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became forced laborers for them.

**34** The Amorites pressed the Danites back into the hill country. They did not let them come down into the plain.

**35** The Amorites were determined to stay in Har-heres,[^1-34] in Aijalon and in Shaalbim. But the house of Joseph gained the upper hand,[^1-35] and they became forced laborers.

**36** The border of the Amorites ran from the Ascent of Akrabbim,[^1-36] from Sela,[^1-37] and upward.

[^1-1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. To "ask Yahweh" was to seek a ruling from him, probably by the sacred lot (Numbers 27:21; 1 Samuel 14:37–42).
[^1-2]: Literally "Look, I have given the land into his hand." Judah is spoken of as one person, the tribe's ancestor; also in verse 3.
[^1-3]: Literally "into my lot": the territory assigned to each tribe by lot (Joshua 15; 19:1–9).
[^1-4]: *Adoni-bezek* means "lord of Bezek".
[^1-5]: Literally "the thumbs of his hands and of his feet"; also in verse 7.
[^1-6]: Literally "the sons of Judah"; also in verse 9.
[^1-7]: Literally "struck it by the mouth of the sword"; also in verse 25.
[^1-8]: The dry country in the south of the land. Also in verses 15 and 16.
[^1-9]: Hebrew *shephelah*: the low hills between the central hill country and the coastal plain.
[^1-10]: *Kiriath-arba* means "town of Arba" or "town of four".
[^1-11]: The descendants of Anak, a people of great size (Numbers 13:22; Joshua 15:14).
[^1-12]: *Kiriath-sepher* means "town of the book" or "town of the scribe".
[^1-13]: The Hebrew can also mean that Kenaz, not Othniel, was Caleb's younger brother.
[^1-14]: That is, Othniel. The Septuagint reads "he urged her". Compare Joshua 15:18.
[^1-15]: The Hebrew verb is rare and its meaning uncertain. Traditionally "she alighted from her donkey".
[^1-16]: That is, a gift.
[^1-17]: Or "Since you have sent me off to the land of the Negev". Literally "For you have given me the land of the Negev".
[^1-18]: Hebrew *gullot*, perhaps "pools" or "basins". It may also be a place name: "Upper Gulloth and Lower Gulloth".
[^1-19]: The Kenites were a clan who lived in the south of the land. Moses' father-in-law is called a Kenite here and in 4:11.
[^1-20]: Probably Jericho (Deuteronomy 34:3).
[^1-21]: The people are not identified: the people of Judah, or the people of the region.
[^1-22]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^1-23]: *Hormah* sounds like *charam*, "devote to destruction". Compare Numbers 21:3.
[^1-24]: The Septuagint reads "Judah did not take Gaza …", in line with verse 19 and 3:3.
[^1-25]: Literally "for not to drive out", a shortened expression. Hebrew *emeq*, "valley", here the lower, flatter land. Also in verse 34.
[^1-26]: Probably chariots fitted with iron, not made entirely of iron.
[^1-27]: Numbers 14:24; Joshua 14:9–14.
[^1-28]: Or "the three Anakite clans". See verse 10.
[^1-29]: The tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, descended from Joseph. Also in verses 23 and 35.
[^1-30]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal kindness, here a promise to keep faith with him. Compare Joshua 2:12–14.
[^1-31]: Literally "daughters", the smaller places that depended on a town. Also later in the verse.
[^1-32]: The Hebrew text is written "the one living in Dor" but read "the people living in Dor".
[^1-33]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs of conscripted laborers. Also in verses 30, 33 and 35.
[^1-34]: *Har-heres* means "mountain of the sun".
[^1-35]: Literally "the hand of the house of Joseph was heavy".
[^1-36]: *Akrabbim* means "scorpions". The Ascent of Akrabbim was a pass south of the Dead Sea (Numbers 34:4; Joshua 15:3).
[^1-37]: Or "from the rock". *Sela* means "rock".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** The angel of Yahweh[^2-1] went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, "I brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that I promised on oath to your ancestors. I said, 'I will never break my covenant with you,

**2** and you must not make a covenant with the people who live in this land. You must tear down their altars.' But you have not obeyed me.[^2-2] What is this you have done?

**3** So I also say this:[^2-3] I will not drive them out before you. They will become thorns in your sides,[^2-4] and their gods will be a trap for you."

**4** When the angel of Yahweh had spoken these words to all the Israelites, the people wept aloud.[^2-5]

**5** They named that place Bochim,[^2-6] and they offered sacrifices there to Yahweh.

**6** When Joshua had sent the people away, the Israelites went, each to his own inheritance, to take possession of the land.[^2-7]

**7** The people served Yahweh all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work that Yahweh had done for Israel.

**8** Joshua son of Nun, the servant of Yahweh, died at the age of 110.

**9** They buried him in the territory he had inherited, at Timnath-heres[^2-8] in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

**10** That whole generation also was gathered to its ancestors, and after them another generation grew up who did not know Yahweh or the work he had done for Israel.

**11** Then the Israelites did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh and served the Baals.[^2-9]

**12** They abandoned Yahweh, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They followed other gods, gods of the peoples around them, and bowed down to them, and they provoked Yahweh to anger.

**13** They abandoned Yahweh and served Baal and the Ashtoreths.[^2-10]

**14** Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he handed them over to raiders, who plundered them. He sold them into the power[^2-11] of their enemies all around, and they could no longer stand up to their enemies.

**15** Whenever they went out to fight, the hand of Yahweh was against them to bring them disaster, just as Yahweh had said and as Yahweh had sworn to them. They were in great distress.

**16** Then Yahweh raised up judges,[^2-12] and they saved them from the power of those who plundered them.

**17** But they would not listen even to their judges. They prostituted themselves to other gods[^2-13] and bowed down to them. They were quick to turn aside from the way their ancestors had walked in obedience to Yahweh's commands. They did not do as their ancestors did.

**18** Whenever Yahweh raised up judges for them, Yahweh was with the judge and saved them from the power of their enemies as long as the judge lived. For Yahweh was moved to pity[^2-14] by their groaning because of those who oppressed them and crushed them.

**19** But when the judge died, they would turn back and act more corruptly than their ancestors, following other gods to serve them and bow down to them. They did not give up any of their practices or their stubborn ways.[^2-15]

**20** So Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he said, "This nation has broken[^2-16] my covenant, which I commanded their ancestors, and has not obeyed me.

**21** So I in turn will no longer drive out before them any of the nations that Joshua left when he died.

**22** I will use them to test Israel, to see whether they will keep the way of Yahweh and walk in it, as their ancestors kept it, or not."

**23** So Yahweh left those nations in the land. He did not drive them out quickly, and he did not hand them over to Joshua.

[^2-1]: Or "the messenger of Yahweh". Hebrew *mal'akh* means messenger. Here the angel speaks in Yahweh's name, as in Genesis 16:7–13. *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-2]: Literally "listened to my voice"; also in verse 20.
[^2-3]: Or "And I also said". Compare Numbers 33:55; Joshua 23:13.
[^2-4]: Literally "They will be sides to you." The phrase is probably short for "thorns in your sides", as in Numbers 33:55.
[^2-5]: Literally "lifted up their voice and wept".
[^2-6]: *Bochim* means "people weeping".
[^2-7]: Verses 6–9 repeat, with small differences, Joshua 24:28–31.
[^2-8]: In Joshua 19:50 and 24:30 the name is Timnath-serah.
[^2-9]: Hebrew *ha-be'alim*. *Baal*, "lord, master, owner", was the name of the Canaanite storm god. The plural refers to his local forms, or to other gods in general.
[^2-10]: Hebrew *ashtarot*, plural of *Ashtoreth* (Astarte), a Canaanite goddess. The plural refers to her local forms, or to goddesses in general.
[^2-11]: Literally "into the hand"; also in verses 16 and 18.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *shofetim*: leaders who rescued Israel and governed it. The word means more than a judge in a court. Also in verses 17–19.
[^2-13]: Literally "they went whoring after other gods", an image of unfaithfulness to Yahweh, as of a wife to her husband.
[^2-14]: Or "relented". Hebrew *nicham*, "be sorry, change one's mind, feel compassion".
[^2-15]: Literally "They did not let anything fall from their deeds and from their hard way."
[^2-16]: Literally "passed over", that is, transgressed.
