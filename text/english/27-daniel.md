## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah,[^1-1] Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.

**2** The Lord[^1-2] gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, along with some of the vessels of the house of God. He brought them to the land of Shinar,[^1-3] to the house of his god, and he put the vessels in the treasury of his god.[^1-4]

**3** Then the king told Ashpenaz, his chief court official,[^1-5] to bring some of the Israelites, from the royal family and from the nobles:[^1-6]

**4** young men without any defect, good-looking, skilled in all wisdom, well informed and quick to learn,[^1-7] who had the ability to serve in the king's palace. He was to teach them the literature and language of the Babylonians.[^1-8]

**5** The king assigned them a daily ration[^1-9] from the king's food[^1-10] and from the wine he drank. They were to be educated for three years, and at the end of that time they were to enter the king's service.[^1-11]

**6** Among them, from the people of Judah,[^1-12] were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah.

**7** The chief official gave them names. He named Daniel Belteshazzar, Hananiah Shadrach, Mishael Meshach, and Azariah Abednego.[^1-13]

**8** But Daniel made up his mind[^1-14] not to defile himself with the king's food or with the wine he drank, and he asked the chief official to let him not defile himself.

**9** God caused the chief official to show Daniel kindness and compassion.[^1-15]

**10** The chief official said to Daniel, "I am afraid of my lord the king, who has assigned your food and your drink. Why should he see you looking worse[^1-16] than the other young men of your age? You would put my head in danger with the king."[^1-17]

**11** Then Daniel said to the guard[^1-18] whom the chief official had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**12** "Please test your servants for ten days. Let us be given vegetables[^1-19] to eat and water to drink.

**13** Then compare our appearance with the appearance of the young men who eat the king's food,[^1-20] and deal with your servants according to what you see."

**14** He agreed to this[^1-21] and tested them for ten days.

**15** At the end of the ten days they looked better and healthier[^1-22] than all the young men who were eating the king's food.

**16** So the guard took away their food and the wine they were to drink, and gave them vegetables.

**17** To these four young men God gave knowledge and skill in all literature and wisdom. And Daniel had understanding of visions and dreams of every kind.

**18** At the end of the time the king had set for bringing them in, the chief official brought them before Nebuchadnezzar.

**19** The king talked with them, and among them all no one was found to equal Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. So they entered the king's service.

**20** In every matter of wisdom and understanding that the king asked them about, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters[^1-23] in his whole kingdom.

**21** And Daniel remained there until the first year of King Cyrus.[^1-24]

[^1-1]: About 605 BC. Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2 place Nebuchadnezzar's first year in the fourth year of Jehoiakim; the difference may come from different ways of counting a king's years. Compare 2 Kings 24:1 and 2 Chronicles 36:5–7.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH.
[^1-3]: An old name for Babylonia (Genesis 11:2).
[^1-4]: Or "of his gods", here and earlier in the verse. The Hebrew *elohim* can mean "God" or "gods".
[^1-5]: Hebrew *rav sarisav*, "the chief of his *sarisim*": court officials. The word can also mean eunuchs. Also in verses 7–11 and 18.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *partemim*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^1-7]: Literally "knowing knowledge and understanding learning".
[^1-8]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Their "literature" was the learned writings of Babylon, in cuneiform script. In chapters 2–5 the same word is used for a class of Babylonian scholars and diviners.
[^1-9]: Literally "the matter of a day in its day".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *pat-bag*, a word borrowed from Persian: the king's own fare or the rations from his table. Also in verses 8, 13, 15 and 16.
[^1-11]: Literally "to stand before the king". Also in verse 19.
[^1-12]: Literally "the sons of Judah".
[^1-13]: The Hebrew names honor the God of Israel: Daniel means "God is my judge", Hananiah "The LORD has been gracious", Mishael perhaps "Who is what God is?", and Azariah "The LORD has helped". The new names are Babylonian. Belteshazzar probably stands for a Babylonian name meaning "Protect his life"; 4:5 (4:8 in most English Bibles) links it with the god Bel. The meanings of Shadrach and Meshach are uncertain. Abednego probably means "servant of Nego", perhaps a form of Nabu, a god of Babylon.
[^1-14]: Literally "set it on his heart".
[^1-15]: Literally "God gave Daniel *chesed* and compassion before the chief official". *Chesed* is usually "faithful love"; here it is the goodwill the official showed him.
[^1-16]: Or "thinner", "more downcast".
[^1-17]: Literally "You would make my head guilty to the king".
[^1-18]: Hebrew *meltsar*, probably a title borrowed from Akkadian, "guard" or "steward". Older translations took it as a name, Melzar. Also in verse 16.
[^1-19]: Hebrew *zero'im*, "things grown from seed": vegetables, perhaps also grain. Verse 16 has a related word.
[^1-20]: Literally "Let our appearance and the appearance of the young men who eat the king's food be seen before you".
[^1-21]: Literally "He listened to them in this matter".
[^1-22]: Literally "their appearance was seen as good and fatter of flesh".
[^1-23]: Hebrew *chartummim* and *ashshafim*. *Chartummim*, a word borrowed from Egyptian, is used of the Egyptian magicians in Genesis 41 and Exodus 7–9. *Ashshafim* is related to an Akkadian word for priests who performed rituals of exorcism and healing.
[^1-24]: Cyrus king of Persia took Babylon in 539 BC. Daniel 10:1 dates a vision to the third year of Cyrus.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign,[^2-1] Nebuchadnezzar had dreams. His spirit was troubled, and he could not sleep.[^2-2]

**2** So the king gave orders to summon the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans,[^2-3] to tell the king his dreams. They came in and stood before the king.

**3** The king said to them, "I have had a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream."

**4** Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic:[^2-4] "O king, live forever! Tell your servants the dream, and we will give its interpretation."

**5** The king answered the Chaldeans, "This is my firm decision:[^2-5] if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be torn limb from limb,[^2-6] and your houses will be made a dunghill.[^2-7]

**6** But if you tell me the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts, a reward and great honor. So tell me the dream and its interpretation."

**7** They answered a second time, "Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will give its interpretation."

**8** The king answered, "I know for certain that you are trying to gain time,[^2-8] because you see that my decision is firm.

**9** If you do not make the dream known to me, there is only one sentence for you.[^2-9] You have agreed together[^2-10] to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the situation changes.[^2-11] So tell me the dream, and I will know that you can give me its interpretation."

**10** The Chaldeans answered the king, "There is no one on earth who can tell the king what he asks. That is why no king, however great and powerful, has ever asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter or Chaldean.

**11** What the king asks is too difficult. There is no one who can tell it to the king except the gods, and they do not live among mortals."[^2-12]

**12** At this the king became furious and very angry, and he ordered all the wise men of Babylon to be destroyed.

**13** So the decree went out that the wise men were to be killed,[^2-13] and they looked for Daniel and his companions to kill them.

**14** Then Daniel spoke with prudence and discretion[^2-14] to Arioch, the captain of the king's guard,[^2-15] who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

**15** He said to Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the decree from the king so harsh?"[^2-16] Then Arioch explained the matter to Daniel.

**16** So Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, and he would tell the king the interpretation.

**17** Then Daniel went to his house and told the matter to his companions Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**18** so that they would seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**19** Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**20** Daniel said,[^2-17] "Blessed be the name of God forever and ever, for wisdom and power belong to him.

**21** He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding.

**22** He reveals deep and hidden things; he knows what is in the darkness, and light lives with him.

**23** To you, God of my ancestors, I give thanks and praise, for you have given me wisdom and power. Now you have made known to me what we asked of you; you have made known to us what the king asked."[^2-18]

**24** So Daniel went to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said to him, "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon. Bring me in before the king, and I will tell the king the interpretation."

**25** Then Arioch quickly brought Daniel in before the king and said to him, "I have found a man among the exiles from Judah[^2-19] who can make known the interpretation to the king."

**26** The king said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to make known to me the dream I saw and its interpretation?"

**27** Daniel answered the king, "No wise men, enchanters, magicians or diviners[^2-20] can tell the king the mystery he has asked about.

**28** But there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will happen in the last days.[^2-21] This is your dream, the visions that passed through your head as you lay on your bed:

**29** As for you, O king, as you lay on your bed your thoughts turned to what would happen after this, and the revealer of mysteries has made known to you what will happen.

**30** As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me not because I have more wisdom than anyone else alive, but so that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and so that you may understand the thoughts of your heart.[^2-22]

**31** You were looking, O king, and there before you was a great statue.[^2-23] That statue was huge, and its brightness was extraordinary. It stood in front of you, and its appearance was terrifying.

**32** The head of the statue was of fine gold, its chest and arms of silver, its belly and thighs of bronze,

**33** its legs of iron, and its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.[^2-24]

**34** As you watched, a stone was cut out, not by human hands.[^2-25] It struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and smashed them.

**35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold were all smashed together and became like chaff on the threshing floors in summer. The wind carried them away, and not a trace of them could be found.[^2-26] But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

**36** This was the dream, and now we will tell the king its interpretation.

**37** You, O king, are the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the might and the glory.

**38** He has given into your hand human beings, the wild animals and the birds of the sky, wherever they live, and he has made you ruler over them all. You are that head of gold.

**39** After you another kingdom will arise, inferior to you, and then a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over the whole earth.[^2-27]

**40** Then there will be a fourth kingdom, strong as iron. Just as iron smashes and shatters everything, and as iron crushes things,[^2-28] it will smash and crush all these.

**41** As you saw, the feet and toes were partly of potter's clay and partly of iron. So it will be a divided kingdom, but some of the strength of iron will be in it, just as you saw the iron mixed with the wet clay.[^2-29]

**42** As the toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay, so the kingdom will be partly strong and partly brittle.

**43** And as you saw the iron mixed with the wet clay, so they will mix with one another by marriage,[^2-30] but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay.

**44** In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and that kingdom will not be left to another people. It will smash all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, but it will stand forever.

**45** That is the meaning of the stone you saw cut out of the mountain, not by human hands, that smashed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver and the gold.[^2-31] A great God has made known to the king what will happen after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation is trustworthy."

**46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and bowed down to Daniel,[^2-32] and he gave orders to present an offering and incense to him.[^2-33]

**47** The king said to Daniel, "Truly your God is the God of gods, the Lord of kings and a revealer of mysteries, for you have been able to reveal this mystery."

**48** Then the king gave Daniel a high position and many great gifts. He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon.

**49** At Daniel's request, the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego over the administration of the province of Babylon, while Daniel remained at the king's court.[^2-34]

[^2-1]: About 603 BC.
[^2-2]: Literally "and his sleep was done upon him". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *kasdim*, the name of the people of southern Mesopotamia (1:4). Here and in chapters 3–5 it is used for a class of Babylonian scholars, experts in astrology and divination. The *mekhashshefim*, "sorcerers", are those who practice *keshef*, forbidden magic (Exodus 7:11; 22:17; Deuteronomy 18:10). On the magicians and enchanters, see 1:20.
[^2-4]: From "O king" to the end of chapter 7 the book is in Aramaic, the common language of the Babylonian and Persian empires, not in Hebrew. The words "in Aramaic" may also be a note to the reader marking the change of language (compare Ezra 4:7).
[^2-5]: Or "The word has gone out from me". Aramaic *azda*, a word borrowed from Persian, perhaps "certain" or "publicly announced". Also in verse 8.
[^2-6]: Literally "made into limbs".
[^2-7]: Or "a ruin".
[^2-8]: Literally "that you are buying time".
[^2-9]: Aramaic *dat*, "law, decree", here the sentence that will be passed on them.
[^2-10]: The Aramaic text is written "You have prepared" but read "You have agreed together".
[^2-11]: Literally "until the time changes".
[^2-12]: Literally "except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh".
[^2-13]: Or "and the wise men were being killed".
[^2-14]: Literally "returned counsel and judgment".
[^2-15]: Literally "the chief of the executioners" or "the chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal guard (Genesis 37:36; 2 Kings 25:8).
[^2-16]: Or "so urgent".
[^2-17]: Literally "Daniel answered and said". Aramaic often introduces speech in this way, even where nothing has been asked.
[^2-18]: Literally "the matter of the king".
[^2-19]: Literally "the sons of the exile of Judah".
[^2-20]: Aramaic *gazerin*, "those who decide", probably astrologers who determined fate from the stars.
[^2-21]: Literally "at the end of the days", that is, in the future.
[^2-22]: Or "of your mind".
[^2-23]: Aramaic *tselem*, "image": a statue, perhaps the image of a god (compare 3:1).
[^2-24]: Aramaic *chasaf*, baked clay or earthenware, as in pottery. Also in verses 34–35 and 41–45.
[^2-25]: Literally "not by hands". Also in verse 45.
[^2-26]: Literally "no place was found for them".
[^2-27]: The kingdoms that follow Nebuchadnezzar's are not named. Many interpreters understand them as Media, Persia and Greece (compare 5:28; 8:20–21); others as Medo-Persia, Greece and Rome.
[^2-28]: Or "as iron that crushes all these". The Aramaic can be read either way.
[^2-29]: Literally "the clay of mud". Also in verse 43.
[^2-30]: Literally "they will be mixed by the seed of humankind", probably meaning marriage alliances between royal houses (compare 11:6, 17).
[^2-31]: Or "Just as you saw that a stone was cut out of the mountain … and smashed the iron …, so a great God has made known".
[^2-32]: Aramaic *segid*, "bowed low, worshiped", the word used for worshiping the statue in chapter 3.
[^2-33]: Literally "an offering and pleasing aromas". The Aramaic *nichochin* recalls the "pleasing aroma" of the offerings in the Law (Leviticus 1:9).
[^2-34]: Literally "in the gate of the king".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold, sixty cubits high and six cubits wide,[^3-1] and he set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.

**2** Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent word to gather the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces,[^3-2] to come to the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3** So the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces gathered for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up, and they stood in front of the statue that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**4** Then the herald called out loudly, "This is the command to you, peoples, nations and languages:[^3-3]

**5** As soon as you hear the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe[^3-4] and every kind of music, you must fall down and worship the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**6** Whoever does not fall down and worship will immediately[^3-5] be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire."

**7** So as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither and every kind of music, all the peoples, nations and languages fell down and worshiped the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**8** At that time some Chaldeans[^3-6] came forward and denounced the Jews.[^3-7]

**9** They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "O king, live forever!

**10** You, O king, have issued a decree that everyone who hears the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe and every kind of music must fall down and worship the statue of gold,

**11** and that whoever does not fall down and worship will be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire.

**12** There are some Jews whom you appointed over the administration of the province of Babylon: Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men pay no attention to you, O king.[^3-8] They do not serve your gods, and they do not worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

**13** Then Nebuchadnezzar, in a furious rage, ordered Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego to be brought in. So these men were brought before the king.

**14** Nebuchadnezzar said to them, "Is it true,[^3-9] Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods, and that you do not worship the statue of gold that I have set up?

**15** Now if you are ready, as soon as you hear the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe and every kind of music, to fall down and worship the statue I have made, well and good.[^3-10] But if you do not worship, you will immediately be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire. And who is the god who will rescue you from my hands?"

**16** Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar,[^3-11] we have no need to give you an answer in this matter.

**17** If our God whom we serve is able to rescue us, he will rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire and from your hand, O king.[^3-12]

**18** But even if he does not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods, and we will not worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

**19** Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the look on his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. He gave orders to heat the furnace seven times hotter than it was usually heated.

**20** He commanded some of the strongest men in his army to tie up Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and to throw them into the furnace of blazing fire.

**21** So these men were tied up in their cloaks, their tunics, their hats and their other clothes,[^3-13] and they were thrown into the furnace of blazing fire.

**22** Because the king's order was so harsh and the furnace so extremely hot, the flame of the fire killed the men who carried Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego up.

**23** And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, fell, tied up, into the furnace of blazing fire.[^3-14]

**24** Then King Nebuchadnezzar was astonished and jumped up in alarm. He said to his advisers, "Didn't we throw three men, tied up, into the fire?" They answered the king, "Certainly, O king."

**25** He said, "Look! I see four men, untied, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are unharmed. And the fourth looks like a son of the gods."[^3-15]

**26** Then Nebuchadnezzar came near the door of the furnace of blazing fire and said, "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, servants of the Most High God,[^3-16] come out! Come here!" Then Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego came out of the fire.

**27** The satraps, the prefects, the governors and the king's advisers gathered around and saw these men. The fire had had no power over their bodies. The hair of their heads was not singed, their cloaks were not harmed,[^3-17] and there was no smell of fire on them.

**28** Nebuchadnezzar said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who has sent his angel and rescued his servants who trusted in him. They defied the king's command[^3-18] and gave up their bodies rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.

**29** So I issue a decree: anyone of any people, nation or language who says anything against[^3-19] the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego will be torn limb from limb, and his house will be made a dunghill, for there is no other god who is able to rescue like this."

**30** Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the province of Babylon.

**31** King Nebuchadnezzar, to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth:[^3-20] May your peace be great!

**32** It is my pleasure to make known the signs and wonders that the Most High God has done for me.

**33** How great are his signs! How mighty are his wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his rule lasts from generation to generation.

[^3-4]: The names of several of these instruments are borrowed from Greek. *Qatros* (Greek *kitharis*) is a lyre. *Sabbekha* (Greek *sambyke*) is a small harp. *Pesanterin* (Greek *psalterion*) is a stringed instrument, perhaps a zither. *Sumponyah* (Greek *symphonia*) may be a bagpipe or a drum, or it may mean "all together". Also in verses 7, 10 and 15.
[^3-5]: Literally "in that hour". Also in verse 15.
[^3-6]: Or "some Babylonians". The word can mean the Babylonian people or the class of Babylonian scholars (2:2).
[^3-7]: Literally "ate the pieces of the Jews", an Aramaic idiom for slandering or accusing someone.
[^3-8]: Literally "have not set a decree upon you".
[^3-9]: Or "Is it on purpose". The meaning of the Aramaic *hatseda* is uncertain.
[^3-10]: The Aramaic leaves this sentence unfinished, and "well and good" is supplied.
[^3-11]: Or "answered King Nebuchadnezzar". The Masoretic accents make "Nebuchadnezzar" the first word of their reply.
[^3-12]: Or "If it is so, our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire, and he will rescue us from your hand, O king." The Aramaic can be read either way. The translation follows the Masoretic accents.
[^3-13]: The meanings of these Aramaic words, some of them borrowed from Persian, are uncertain. *Sarbal* may be a cloak or trousers, *pattish* a tunic or leggings, and *karbela* a hat or turban.
[^3-14]: After this verse the Greek versions of Daniel (the Septuagint and Theodotion) have a long passage that is not in the Hebrew and Aramaic text: the Prayer of Azariah, a description of the furnace, and the Song of the Three Young Men. The Greek Daniel also has two further stories, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon. These additions are part of the Old Testament in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles and are among the Apocrypha in Protestant Bibles.
[^3-15]: Aramaic *bar-elahin*, "a son of gods", that is, a divine being. In verse 28 Nebuchadnezzar calls him God's angel. Older translations have "the Son of God".
[^3-16]: Aramaic *elaha illa'a*, "the Most High God". Also in verse 32.
[^3-17]: Literally "were not changed".
[^3-18]: Literally "changed the word of the king".
[^3-19]: Or "anything careless against". Aramaic *shalu*, "negligence, a careless word".
[^3-20]: Verses 31–33 are 4:1–3 in most English Bibles. They begin Nebuchadnezzar's letter, which continues through chapter 4.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at ease in my house and flourishing in my palace.[^4-1]

**2** I had a dream, and it made me afraid. As I lay on my bed, the images and the visions that passed through my head terrified me.

**3** So I issued a decree that all the wise men of Babylon should be brought in before me to make known to me the interpretation of the dream.

**4** Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners came in, and I told them the dream, but they did not make known to me its interpretation.

**5** At last Daniel came in before me. His name is Belteshazzar, after the name of my god, and the spirit of the holy gods[^4-2] is in him. I told him the dream:

**6** "Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you, and that no mystery is too difficult for you. Tell me the visions of my dream that I saw, and its interpretation.[^4-3]

**7** These were the visions that passed through my head as I lay on my bed: I was looking, and there was a tree in the middle of the earth, and its height was great.

**8** The tree grew large and strong. Its top reached to the sky, and it could be seen to the ends of the whole earth.

**9** Its leaves were beautiful and its fruit plentiful, and on it was food for all. Under it the wild animals found shade, and in its branches the birds of the sky lived, and from it every living creature was fed.[^4-4]

**10** In the visions that passed through my head as I lay on my bed, I looked, and there was a watcher, a holy one,[^4-5] coming down from heaven.

**11** He called out loudly and said, 'Cut down the tree and cut off its branches! Strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit! Let the animals flee from under it and the birds from its branches.

**12** But leave the stump with its roots in the ground, bound with iron and bronze, in the grass of the field. Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven,[^4-6] and let his share be with the animals in the grass of the earth.

## Chapter 12

*Status: draft*

**1** "At that time Michael, the great prince who stands guard over your people,[^12-1] will arise. There will be a time of distress such as has never been since there was a nation until that time. But at that time your people will be delivered, everyone who is found written in the book.[^12-2]

**2** Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth[^12-3] will awake, some to everlasting life, and some to disgrace and everlasting contempt.[^12-4]

**3** Those who are wise[^12-5] will shine like the brightness of the sky,[^12-6] and those who lead many to righteousness will shine like the stars forever and ever.

**4** But you, Daniel, keep the words secret and seal the book until the time of the end. Many will run to and fro, and knowledge will increase."[^12-7]

**5** Then I, Daniel, looked, and there stood two others, one on this bank of the river and one on that bank of the river.[^12-8]

**6** One of them said to the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the river, "How long will it be until the end of these wonders?"

**7** I heard the man dressed in linen, who was above the waters of the river. He raised his right hand and his left hand toward heaven and swore by the one who lives forever that it would be for a time, times and half a time,[^12-9] and that when the shattering of the power of the holy people comes to an end, all these things will be completed.[^12-10]

**8** I heard, but I did not understand. So I said, "My lord, what will be the outcome of these things?"

**9** He said, "Go your way, Daniel, for the words are kept secret and sealed until the time of the end.

**10** Many will be purified, made white and refined, but the wicked will act wickedly. None of the wicked will understand, but those who are wise will understand.

**11** From the time that the regular offering[^12-11] is taken away and the abomination that causes desolation[^12-12] is set up, there will be 1,290 days.

**12** Blessed is the one who waits and reaches 1,335 days.[^12-13]

**13** But you, go your way to the end. You will rest, and you will rise to receive your allotted share at the end of the days."[^12-14]

[^12-1]: Literally "who stands over the sons of your people". See 10:13, 21.
[^12-2]: Compare Exodus 32:32–33; Psalm 69:29 (69:28 in most English Bibles); Malachi 3:16; Revelation 20:12.
[^12-3]: Literally "the ground of dust". Compare Genesis 3:19.
[^12-4]: This is the clearest statement in the Old Testament of a resurrection of the dead (compare Isaiah 26:19). "Many" may mean a great number, or it may mean that not all will rise. Hebrew *dir'on*, "contempt, abhorrence", is found elsewhere only in Isaiah 66:24. Compare John 5:28–29.
[^12-5]: Hebrew *maskilim*. See 11:33. Also in verse 10.
[^12-6]: Literally "the brightness of the dome", Hebrew *raqia*, the sky pictured as a solid vault (Genesis 1:6–8).
[^12-7]: The Septuagint reads "and the land will be filled with injustice".
[^12-8]: Hebrew *ye'or*, a word usually used of the Nile; here the Tigris (10:4). Also in verses 6–7.
[^12-9]: That is, probably three and a half years. See 7:25.
[^12-10]: Literally "the hand of the holy people". Compare Revelation 10:5–7.
[^12-11]: See 8:11.
[^12-12]: Hebrew *shiqquts shomem*. See 11:31; compare 9:27 and Mark 13:14.
[^12-13]: The numbers in verses 11 and 12 differ from each other and from 8:14. Each is a little more than three and a half years (compare verse 7).
[^12-14]: Literally "and you will stand for your lot at the end of the days". *Rest* may refer to death and *rise* to resurrection (compare verse 2).
