## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the second year of King Darius, on the first day of the sixth month,[^1-1] the word of the LORD[^1-2] came through[^1-3] Haggai the prophet[^1-4] to Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest:[^1-5]

**2** This is what the LORD of Armies[^1-6] says: These people say, "The time has not come, the time for the house of the LORD to be rebuilt."

**3** Then the word of the LORD came through Haggai the prophet:

**4** Is it a time for you yourselves to live in your paneled houses,[^1-7] while this house lies in ruins?

**5** Now this is what the LORD of Armies says: Consider your ways.[^1-8]

**6** You have sown much, but harvested little. You eat, but never have enough; you drink, but never have your fill; you put on clothes, but no one is warm. And whoever earns wages earns them to put into a bag with holes.

**7** This is what the LORD of Armies says: Consider your ways.

**8** Go up into the hills, bring wood and build the house, so that I may take pleasure in it and be honored,[^1-9] says the LORD.

**9** You looked for much, but see, it came to little. When you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? declares the LORD of Armies.[^1-10] Because of my house, which lies in ruins, while each of you runs off to his own house.

**10** Therefore, because of you, the heavens have held back the dew, and the earth has held back its produce.

**11** I have called for a drought[^1-11] on the land and on the hills, on the grain, the new wine and the olive oil, on what the ground produces, on human beings and animals, and on all the work of your hands.

**12** Then Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, and Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and all the rest of the people[^1-12] obeyed the voice of the LORD their God and the words of Haggai the prophet, because the LORD their God had sent him. And the people feared the LORD.

**13** Then Haggai, the LORD's messenger,[^1-13] gave the LORD's message to the people: "I am with you, declares the LORD."

**14** So the LORD stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and the spirit of Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and the spirit of all the rest of the people. They came and began work on the house of the LORD of Armies, their God,

**15** on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month, in the second year of King Darius.[^1-14]

[^1-1]: Darius I, king of Persia (522–486 BC). The date is 29 August 520 BC. The months are counted from the spring, so the sixth month fell in August and September.
[^1-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-3]: Literally "by the hand of". Also in verses 3 and 13 and in 2:1.
[^1-4]: Haggai and Zechariah urged the people of Judah to rebuild the temple (Ezra 5:1–2; 6:14). Exiles had returned from Babylon after 538 BC and laid the foundation of the temple, but the work had stopped (Ezra 3:8–13; 4:24). The name *Haggai* probably means "born on a festival".
[^1-5]: Zerubbabel was a grandson of King Jehoiachin (1 Chronicles 3:17–19). He was governor of the Persian province of Judah. Joshua, called Jeshua in Ezra 3:2, was the grandson of the last high priest before the exile (1 Chronicles 5:40–41, 6:14–15 in most English Bibles).
[^1-6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. The title is used often in Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *sefunim*, "covered", that is, roofed or paneled with wood, as were the royal buildings (1 Kings 7:3, 7).
[^1-8]: Literally "Set your heart on your ways". Also in verse 7.
[^1-9]: Or "glorified".
[^1-10]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH tseva'ot*, "the declaration of the LORD of Armies", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. *Declares the LORD* is also in verse 13.
[^1-11]: Hebrew *chorev*, "drought", which sounds like *charev*, "in ruins" (verses 4 and 9).
[^1-12]: Literally "all the remnant of the people", those who had come back from the exile. Also in verse 14.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *mal'akh*, "messenger", the word also used for an angel. Compare Malachi 3:1.
[^1-14]: 21 September 520 BC.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day of the month,[^2-1] the word of the LORD[^2-2] came through Haggai the prophet:

**2** Speak to Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and to the rest of the people, and say:

**3** Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory?[^2-3] How does it look to you now? Does it not seem to you like nothing at all?

**4** But now be strong, Zerubbabel, declares the LORD.[^2-4] Be strong, Joshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest. Be strong, all you people of the land,[^2-5] declares the LORD, and work, for I am with you, declares the LORD of Armies,[^2-6]

**5** as I promised you[^2-7] when you came out of Egypt. My Spirit[^2-8] remains among you. Do not be afraid.

**6** For this is what the LORD of Armies says: Once more, in a little while,[^2-9] I will shake the heavens and the earth, the sea and the dry land.

**7** I will shake all the nations, and the treasures of all the nations will come,[^2-10] and I will fill this house with glory, says the LORD of Armies.

**8** The silver is mine, and the gold is mine, declares the LORD of Armies.

**9** The glory of this latter house will be greater than that of the former,[^2-11] says the LORD of Armies. And in this place I will give peace, declares the LORD of Armies.

**10** On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius,[^2-12] the word of the LORD came to Haggai the prophet:

**11** This is what the LORD of Armies says: Ask the priests for a ruling:[^2-13]

**12** "If someone carries consecrated meat[^2-14] in the fold of his garment, and with the fold he touches bread or stew or wine or oil or any other food, does it become holy?" The priests answered, "No."

**13** Then Haggai said, "If someone who is unclean from contact with a dead body[^2-15] touches any of these, does it become unclean?" The priests answered, "It becomes unclean."

**14** Then Haggai answered, "So it is with this people, and so it is with this nation before me, declares the LORD, and so with all the work of their hands. Whatever they offer there is unclean.[^2-16]

**15** Now consider[^2-17] from this day onward.[^2-18] Before stone was laid on stone in the temple of the LORD,

**16** how did you fare?[^2-19] When someone came to a heap of twenty measures, there were only ten. When someone came to the wine vat to draw fifty measures from the press,[^2-20] there were only twenty.

**17** I struck you, all the work of your hands, with blight and mildew and hail, yet you did not turn to me,[^2-21] declares the LORD.

**18** Consider from this day onward, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, from the day when the foundation of the temple of the LORD was laid. Consider:

**19** Is there still seed in the barn? Even the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate and the olive tree have not yet borne fruit.[^2-22] From this day on I will bless you."

**20** The word of the LORD came to Haggai a second time on the twenty-fourth day of the month:

**21** Speak to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah: I am about to shake the heavens and the earth.

**22** I will overturn[^2-23] the thrones of kingdoms and destroy the power of the kingdoms of the nations. I will overturn chariots and their riders; horses and their riders will fall, each by the sword of his comrade.[^2-24]

**23** On that day, declares the LORD of Armies, I will take you, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, my servant, declares the LORD, and I will make you like a signet ring,[^2-25] for I have chosen you, declares the LORD of Armies.

[^2-1]: 17 October 520 BC, the last day of the Festival of Shelters (Leviticus 23:34–36).
[^2-2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-3]: Solomon's temple, destroyed by the Babylonians in 587 or 586 BC, about sixty-six years before. Some of the older people had wept when the new foundation was laid (Ezra 3:12).
[^2-4]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *am ha-arets*, here the whole community in Judah.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^2-7]: Literally "the word that I cut with you", the language of making a covenant. The Septuagint does not have the first half of this verse.
[^2-8]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^2-9]: Literally "Yet once, it is a little". Hebrews 12:26 quotes this verse from the Septuagint: "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heaven."
[^2-10]: Hebrew *chemdat*, "what is desired, precious things". The verb is plural, which points to treasures (compare verse 8). Traditionally "the desire of all nations shall come", following the Latin Vulgate, which many Christian readers took as a title of the Messiah.
[^2-11]: Or "The latter glory of this house will be greater than the former".
[^2-12]: 18 December 520 BC. Also in verses 18 and 20.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *torah*, here a priest's decision on a point of the law (Leviticus 10:10–11; Deuteronomy 17:11).
[^2-14]: Literally "holy flesh", meat from a sacrifice. Whatever touched it became holy (Leviticus 6:20, 6:27 in most English Bibles), but holiness did not pass on any further.
[^2-15]: Literally "one unclean of *nephesh*", that is, by contact with a dead person (Numbers 19:11–13, 22). Uncleanness spreads more easily than holiness.
[^2-16]: *There* is probably the altar, rebuilt soon after the return (Ezra 3:2–3).
[^2-17]: Literally "set your heart". Also in verse 18.
[^2-18]: Or "think back from this day". The Hebrew phrase usually looks forward, but verses 15–17 recall the past.
[^2-19]: Literally "since they were". The Septuagint reads "who were you?"
[^2-20]: Hebrew *purah*, a vat or wine trough.
[^2-21]: Literally "and there was nothing of you to me". The line echoes Amos 4:9, where the Hebrew is "you did not return to me".
[^2-22]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Or "Is the seed still in the barn? Have the vine … not yet borne fruit?"
[^2-23]: Hebrew *hafakh*, the word used of the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:25; Amos 4:11).
[^2-24]: Literally "his brother". Compare Judges 7:22.
[^2-25]: A ring with a seal, worn by a king or his officials as the sign of their authority. The LORD had said that he would pull off King Jehoiachin, Zerubbabel's grandfather, like a signet ring (Jeremiah 22:24). Zerubbabel is named in the genealogies of Jesus (Matthew 1:12–13; Luke 3:27).
