## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Simeon[^1-1] Peter, a servant[^1-2] and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who have received a faith as precious as ours[^1-3] through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ:[^1-4]

**2** May grace and peace be yours in abundance through the knowledge[^1-5] of God and of Jesus our Lord.

**3** His divine power has given us everything we need for life and godliness, through the knowledge of the one who called us by his own glory and goodness.[^1-6]

**4** Through these he has given us his precious and very great promises, so that through them you may come to share in the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world because of desire.

**5** For this very reason, make every effort to add goodness to your faith, and knowledge to goodness,[^1-7]

**6** and self-control to knowledge, and endurance to self-control, and godliness to endurance,

**7** and affection for one another to godliness, and love to affection.[^1-8]

**8** For if these qualities are yours and are increasing, they keep you from being useless or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**9** But whoever lacks them is blind, so shortsighted that they have forgotten that they were cleansed from their past sins.

**10** So, brothers and sisters,[^1-9] be all the more eager to make your calling and election secure, for if you do these things, you will never stumble.

**11** For in this way you will be richly provided with an entrance into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

**12** So I intend always to remind you of these things,[^1-10] even though you know them and are firmly established in the truth you now have.

**13** I think it right, as long as I am in this tent,[^1-11] to stir you up by way of reminder,

**14** since I know that I will soon put off my tent, as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me.[^1-12]

**15** And I will make every effort to see that after my departure[^1-13] you will always be able to recall these things.

**16** For we did not follow cleverly devised myths when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but we were eyewitnesses of his majesty.

**17** For he received honor and glory from God the Father when the voice came to him from the Majestic Glory,[^1-14] "This is my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with him."[^1-15]

**18** We ourselves heard this voice come from heaven, when we were with him on the holy mountain.

**19** And we have the prophetic word made more certain,[^1-16] and you will do well to pay attention to it, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star[^1-17] rises in your hearts.

**20** Above all, you must understand this: no prophecy of scripture is a matter of one's own interpretation,[^1-18]

**21** for no prophecy was ever produced[^1-19] by human will, but people spoke from God[^1-20] as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

[^1-1]: Some manuscripts read "Simon". *Simeon* is the Hebrew form of the name (Acts 15:14).
[^1-2]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-3]: Literally "to those who have obtained a faith of equal value with ours".
[^1-4]: Or "of our God and of the Savior Jesus Christ". The Greek has one article for both titles, as in verse 11, "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ"; in verse 2 God and Jesus are named separately.
[^1-5]: Greek *epignosis*, "knowledge, recognition", a key word in this letter (verses 3 and 8; 2:20).
[^1-6]: Or "to his own glory and goodness". Greek *arete*, "excellence, virtue", also in verse 5. Many manuscripts read "through glory and goodness".
[^1-7]: Literally "supply in your faith goodness, and in goodness knowledge". Greek *epichoregeo*, "supply generously", the verb translated "provided" in verse 11.
[^1-8]: Greek *philadelphia*, "love for brothers (and sisters)", and *agape*, "love".
[^1-9]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-10]: Many manuscripts read "So I will not neglect to remind you of these things."
[^1-11]: That is, this body (compare 2 Corinthians 5:1–4).
[^1-12]: Compare John 21:18–19.
[^1-13]: Greek *exodos*, "departure, going out", a way of speaking of death (compare Luke 9:31).
[^1-14]: A reverent way of referring to God. The voice spoke at Jesus' transfiguration (Mark 9:2–8; Matthew 17:1–8; Luke 9:28–36).
[^1-15]: Or "This is my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in him." Compare Matthew 17:5. The Greek sentence is left unfinished.
[^1-16]: Or "And we have the prophetic word, which is more certain". *The prophetic word*: probably the prophecies of scripture (verse 20).
[^1-17]: Greek *phosphoros*, "light-bringer", the planet Venus, which rises before dawn. Compare Numbers 24:17.
[^1-18]: Literally "every prophecy of scripture does not come about from (or 'is not a matter of') one's own explanation". It may mean that the prophets did not produce their messages by their own interpretation of what they saw, as verse 21 goes on to say; or that readers are not to interpret prophecy on their own, apart from the Spirit who gave it.
[^1-19]: Literally "brought", the verb used of the voice "that came" in verses 17–18; also "carried along" later in this verse.
[^1-20]: Many manuscripts read "holy people of God spoke".
