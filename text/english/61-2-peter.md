## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Simeon[^1-1] Peter, a servant[^1-2] and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who have received a faith as precious as ours[^1-3] through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ:[^1-4]

**2** May grace and peace be yours in abundance through the knowledge[^1-5] of God and of Jesus our Lord.

**3** His divine power has given us everything we need for life and godliness, through the knowledge of the one who called us by his own glory and goodness.[^1-6]

**4** Through these he has given us his precious and very great promises, so that through them you may come to share in the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world because of desire.

**5** For this very reason, make every effort to add goodness to your faith, and knowledge to goodness,[^1-7]

**6** and self-control to knowledge, and endurance to self-control, and godliness to endurance,

**7** and affection for one another to godliness, and love to affection.[^1-8]

**8** For if these qualities are yours and are increasing, they keep you from being useless or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**9** But whoever lacks them is blind, so shortsighted that they have forgotten that they were cleansed from their past sins.

**10** So, brothers and sisters,[^1-9] be all the more eager to make your calling and election secure, for if you do these things, you will never stumble.

**11** For in this way you will be richly provided with an entrance into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

**12** So I intend always to remind you of these things,[^1-10] even though you know them and are firmly established in the truth you now have.

**13** I think it right, as long as I am in this tent,[^1-11] to stir you up by way of reminder,

**14** since I know that I will soon put off my tent, as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me.[^1-12]

**15** And I will make every effort to see that after my departure[^1-13] you will always be able to recall these things.

**16** For we did not follow cleverly devised myths when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but we were eyewitnesses of his majesty.

**17** For he received honor and glory from God the Father when the voice came to him from the Majestic Glory,[^1-14] "This is my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with him."[^1-15]

**18** We ourselves heard this voice come from heaven, when we were with him on the holy mountain.

**19** And we have the prophetic word made more certain,[^1-16] and you will do well to pay attention to it, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star[^1-17] rises in your hearts.

**20** Above all, you must understand this: no prophecy of scripture is a matter of one's own interpretation,[^1-18]

**21** for no prophecy was ever produced[^1-19] by human will, but people spoke from God[^1-20] as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

[^1-1]: Some manuscripts read "Simon". *Simeon* is the Hebrew form of the name (Acts 15:14).
[^1-2]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-3]: Literally "to those who have obtained a faith of equal value with ours".
[^1-4]: Or "of our God and of the Savior Jesus Christ". The Greek has one article for both titles, as in verse 11, "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ"; in verse 2 God and Jesus are named separately.
[^1-5]: Greek *epignosis*, "knowledge, recognition", a key word in this letter (verses 3 and 8; 2:20).
[^1-6]: Or "to his own glory and goodness". Greek *arete*, "excellence, virtue", also in verse 5. Many manuscripts read "through glory and goodness".
[^1-7]: Literally "supply in your faith goodness, and in goodness knowledge". Greek *epichoregeo*, "supply generously", the verb translated "provided" in verse 11.
[^1-8]: Greek *philadelphia*, "love for brothers (and sisters)", and *agape*, "love".
[^1-9]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-10]: Many manuscripts read "So I will not neglect to remind you of these things."
[^1-11]: That is, this body (compare 2 Corinthians 5:1–4).
[^1-12]: Compare John 21:18–19.
[^1-13]: Greek *exodos*, "departure, going out", a way of speaking of death (compare Luke 9:31).
[^1-14]: A reverent way of referring to God. The voice spoke at Jesus' transfiguration (Mark 9:2–8; Matthew 17:1–8; Luke 9:28–36).
[^1-15]: Or "This is my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in him." Compare Matthew 17:5. The Greek sentence is left unfinished.
[^1-16]: Or "And we have the prophetic word, which is more certain". *The prophetic word*: probably the prophecies of scripture (verse 20).
[^1-17]: Greek *phosphoros*, "light-bringer", the planet Venus, which rises before dawn. Compare Numbers 24:17.
[^1-18]: Literally "every prophecy of scripture does not come about from (or 'is not a matter of') one's own explanation". It may mean that the prophets did not produce their messages by their own interpretation of what they saw, as verse 21 goes on to say; or that readers are not to interpret prophecy on their own, apart from the Spirit who gave it.
[^1-19]: Literally "brought", the verb used of the voice "that came" in verses 17–18; also "carried along" later in this verse.
[^1-20]: Many manuscripts read "holy people of God spoke".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** But there were also false prophets among the people,[^2-1] just as there will be false teachers among you. They will secretly bring in destructive heresies,[^2-2] even denying the Master[^2-3] who bought them, and so bring swift destruction on themselves.

**2** Many will follow their debauchery,[^2-4] and because of them the way of truth will be slandered.

**3** In their greed they will exploit you with made-up stories. Their condemnation, pronounced long ago, is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep.

**4** For if God did not spare the angels when they sinned, but cast them into Tartarus[^2-5] and committed them to chains of deep darkness[^2-6] to be kept until the judgment;

**5** if he did not spare the ancient world, but protected Noah, a herald of righteousness, with seven others,[^2-7] when he brought the flood on a world of ungodly people;

**6** if he condemned the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to destruction,[^2-8] reducing them to ashes, and made them an example of what is coming for the ungodly;[^2-9]

**7** and if he rescued righteous Lot, who was worn down by the debauched conduct of lawless people

**8** (for that righteous man, living among them day after day, was tormenting his righteous soul over the lawless deeds he saw and heard),

**9** then the Lord knows[^2-10] how to rescue the godly from trial, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment until the day of judgment,[^2-11]

**10** especially those who follow the flesh in its polluting desire[^2-12] and despise authority.[^2-13] Bold and arrogant, they are not afraid to slander the glorious ones,[^2-14]

**11** whereas angels, though greater in strength and power, do not bring a slanderous judgment against them.[^2-15]

**12** But these people, like unreasoning animals, creatures of instinct born to be caught and destroyed, slander what they do not understand,[^2-16] and in their destruction they too will be destroyed.[^2-17]

**13** They will suffer wrong as the wage for their wrongdoing.[^2-18] They count it a pleasure to revel in broad daylight. They are blots and blemishes,[^2-19] reveling in their deceptions[^2-20] as they feast with you.

**14** They have eyes full of adultery[^2-21] that never stop sinning. They entice unstable souls. They have hearts trained in greed. They are accursed children![^2-22]

**15** They have left the straight road and gone astray, following the way of Balaam son of Bosor,[^2-23] who loved the wages of wrongdoing,

**16** but was rebuked for his own wrongdoing: a donkey that cannot speak spoke with a human voice and restrained the prophet's madness.[^2-24]

**17** These people are springs without water and mists[^2-25] driven by a storm. The gloom of darkness has been kept for them.[^2-26]

**18** For they speak arrogant and empty words, and by the desires of the flesh, by debauchery, they entice people who are just escaping[^2-27] from those who live in error.

**19** They promise them freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption. For people are slaves of whatever has mastered them.

**20** For if, after they have escaped the defilements of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they[^2-28] are again entangled in them and overcome, their final condition has become worse than the first.[^2-29]

**21** It would have been better for them never to have known the way of righteousness than, after knowing it, to turn back from the holy commandment that was handed on to them.

**22** What the true proverb says has happened to them: "A dog returns to its own vomit,"[^2-30] and, "A sow that has been washed goes back to wallowing in the mud."[^2-31]

[^2-1]: That is, Israel (compare Deuteronomy 13:2–6, 13:1–5 in most English Bibles). Verses 1–18 closely parallel Jude 4–16, and 3:1–3 parallels Jude 17–18; many of the same Greek words and images are used.
[^2-2]: Greek *haireseis*, "opinions, sects, factions"; literally "heresies of destruction".
[^2-3]: Greek *despotes*, "master, owner" (compare Jude 4).
[^2-4]: Greek *aselgeia*, "debauchery, unrestrained behavior" (compare Jude 4); also in verses 7 and 18.
[^2-5]: Greek *tartaroo*, "cast into Tartarus". In Greek myth Tartarus was the deepest part of the underworld, where the Titans were imprisoned; Jewish writers used the name for the place where rebel angels were held. Compare Jude 6 and the note there; Genesis 6:1–4.
[^2-6]: Some manuscripts read "pits of deep darkness".
[^2-7]: Literally "Noah, the eighth", that is, Noah and seven others (compare 1 Peter 3:20; Genesis 7:13). Genesis does not record Noah's preaching; later Jewish tradition told of it.
[^2-8]: Genesis 19:24–29. Some manuscripts leave out "to destruction".
[^2-9]: Many manuscripts read "an example for those who would live ungodly lives". Compare Jude 7.
[^2-10]: Verses 4–10 are one long sentence in Greek. Its conditions ("if God did not spare …", verses 4–7) are completed here; verse 8 is an aside.
[^2-11]: Or "to keep the unrighteous for punishment on the day of judgment".
[^2-12]: Literally "who go after flesh in desire of defilement" (compare Jude 7).
[^2-13]: Or "lordship". Greek *kyriotes* (compare Jude 8).
[^2-14]: Literally "glories", probably angelic beings (compare Jude 8).
[^2-15]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions add "before the Lord"; the SBLGNT leaves these words out, following a minority reading. Compare Jude 9.
[^2-16]: Literally "slandering in what they do not know". Compare Jude 10.
[^2-17]: Or "they will be destroyed like them", the animals.
[^2-18]: Literally "being wronged (as) the wage of wrong", a play on words. Many manuscripts read "receiving the wage of wrongdoing".
[^2-19]: Greek *spiloi kai momoi*, "spots and blemishes". Compare Jude 12, "hidden reefs" (*spilades*).
[^2-20]: Some manuscripts read "in their love feasts" (Greek *agapais* for *apatais*), as in Jude 12.
[^2-21]: Literally "full of an adulteress".
[^2-22]: Literally "children of a curse", a Hebrew way of speaking.
[^2-23]: Numbers 22–24, where Balaam's father is *Beor* (22:5). Some manuscripts read "Beor" here. Compare Jude 11.
[^2-24]: Numbers 22:21–35.
[^2-25]: Many manuscripts read "clouds", as in Jude 12.
[^2-26]: Many manuscripts add "forever", as in Jude 13.
[^2-27]: Many manuscripts read "who have truly escaped". Literally "who are barely escaping".
[^2-28]: *They* may be the false teachers or the people they entice.
[^2-29]: The same words as in Matthew 12:45.
[^2-30]: Proverbs 26:11, "Like a dog that returns to its vomit is a fool who repeats his folly."
[^2-31]: This saying is not from the Old Testament. A similar saying about a pig is found in the ancient story of Ahiqar.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Dear friends,[^3-1] this is now the second letter I am writing to you.[^3-2] In both of them I am trying to stir up your sincere minds by way of reminder,

**2** so that you will remember the words spoken beforehand by the holy prophets, and the command of the Lord and Savior given through your apostles.[^3-3]

**3** Above all, you must understand this: in the last days scoffers will come with their scoffing,[^3-4] following their own desires,

**4** and saying, "Where is the promise of his coming? Ever since the ancestors fell asleep,[^3-5] everything has continued as it was from the beginning of creation."

**5** For they deliberately overlook this: long ago, by the word of God, the heavens existed and the earth was formed out of water and through water.[^3-6]

**6** Through these[^3-7] the world of that time was flooded with water and perished.

**7** But by the same word[^3-8] the present heavens and earth have been stored up for fire, being kept for the day of judgment and destruction of the ungodly.

**8** But do not overlook this one thing, dear friends: with the Lord one day is like a thousand years, and a thousand years like one day.[^3-9]

**9** The Lord is not slow about his promise, as some count slowness, but he is patient with you,[^3-10] not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance.

**10** But the day of the Lord will come like a thief.[^3-11] On that day the heavens will pass away with a roar, the elements[^3-12] will be dissolved in the heat, and the earth and the works done on it will be found out.[^3-13]

**11** Since all these things are to be dissolved in this way, what kind of people ought you to be, in holy conduct and godliness,

**12** as you wait for the coming of the day of God and hasten it![^3-14] Because of it the heavens will be set on fire and dissolved, and the elements will melt in the heat.[^3-15]

**13** But according to his promise we are waiting for new heavens and a new earth,[^3-16] where righteousness lives.

**14** So, dear friends, since you are waiting for these things, make every effort to be found by him without spot or blemish,[^3-17] at peace.

**15** And regard the patience of our Lord as salvation, just as our dear brother Paul also wrote to you, according to the wisdom given to him,[^3-18]

**16** as he does in all his letters when he speaks in them of these matters. There are some things in them[^3-19] that are hard to understand, which the ignorant and unstable twist, as they do the other scriptures, to their own destruction.[^3-20]

**17** So you, dear friends, since you know this in advance, be on your guard, so that you are not carried away by the error of lawless people and lose your own firm footing.

**18** But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity.[^3-21] Amen.[^3-22]

[^3-1]: Literally "Beloved"; also in verses 8, 14, 15 ("dear brother") and 17.
[^3-2]: The first may be 1 Peter, or a letter now lost.
[^3-3]: Literally "and of the command of your apostles, of the Lord and Savior". Verses 2–3 parallel Jude 17–18.
[^3-4]: Many manuscripts leave out "with their scoffing".
[^3-5]: That is, died. *The ancestors*: literally "the fathers", the ancestors of Israel, or perhaps the first generation of believers.
[^3-6]: Compare Genesis 1:2, 6–10. *Through water*: or "by means of water".
[^3-7]: That is, the water and the word of God, or the waters.
[^3-8]: Many manuscripts read "by his word".
[^3-9]: Compare Psalm 90:4, "For a thousand years in your sight are like yesterday when it has passed".
[^3-10]: Many manuscripts read "with us".
[^3-11]: Many manuscripts add "in the night". Compare Matthew 24:43; 1 Thessalonians 5:2.
[^3-12]: Greek *stoicheia*, "elements": the basic substances of which the world is made, or the heavenly bodies. Also in verse 12.
[^3-13]: Or "will be laid bare", exposed to God's judgment. Many manuscripts read "will be burned up". Some editions read, by a conjectural change, "will not be found".
[^3-14]: Or "and eagerly desire it".
[^3-15]: Compare Isaiah 34:4, "All the army of the heavens will rot away".
[^3-16]: Isaiah 65:17; 66:22.
[^3-17]: The opposite of the false teachers, "blots and blemishes" (2:13).
[^3-18]: It is not known which of Paul's letters is meant.
[^3-19]: Greek *en hais*, "in which", referring to the letters. Many manuscripts read *en hois*, "in which things", referring to the matters Paul writes about.
[^3-20]: This is the only place in the New Testament that refers to a collection of Paul's letters. *The other scriptures* sets them alongside the scriptures, probably the writings of the Old Testament; interpreters differ on what this implies about how Paul's letters were regarded when this was written.
[^3-21]: Literally "to the day of the age", that is, forever.
[^3-22]: Some manuscripts leave out "Amen".
