## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Jude,[^1-1] a servant[^1-2] of Jesus Christ and brother of James, to those who are called, who are loved by God the Father[^1-3] and kept for Jesus Christ:[^1-4]

**2** May mercy, peace and love be yours in abundance.[^1-5]

**3** Dear friends,[^1-6] although I was very eager to write to you about the salvation we share, I found it necessary to write and urge you to contend for the faith that was once for all handed on to God's holy people.[^1-7]

**4** For certain people have slipped in unnoticed, people who were marked out long ago for this condemnation.[^1-8] They are ungodly people, who turn the grace of our God into a license for immorality[^1-9] and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.[^1-10]

**5** Now I want to remind you, though you once knew all this, that Jesus,[^1-11] having saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe.[^1-12]

**6** And the angels who did not keep their own position of authority,[^1-13] but abandoned their proper dwelling, he has kept[^1-14] in eternal chains in deep darkness for the judgment of the great day.[^1-15]

**7** In the same way Sodom and Gomorrah and the towns around them, which like these[^1-16] indulged in sexual immorality and went after other flesh,[^1-17] are set before us as an example, as they undergo the punishment of eternal fire.[^1-18]

**8** Yet in the same way these people too, relying on their dreams,[^1-19] defile the flesh, reject authority[^1-20] and slander the glorious ones.[^1-21]

**9** But when the archangel Michael was disputing with the devil and arguing about the body of Moses, he did not dare to bring a slanderous judgment against him,[^1-22] but said, "The Lord rebuke you!"[^1-23]

**10** But these people slander whatever they do not understand, and the things they do understand by instinct, like unreasoning animals, are the very things that destroy them.[^1-24]

**11** Woe to them![^1-25] For they have gone the way of Cain,[^1-26] they have rushed headlong for profit into Balaam's error,[^1-27] and they have perished in Korah's rebellion.[^1-28]

**12** These people are hidden reefs[^1-29] at your love feasts,[^1-30] feasting with you without fear, shepherds who feed only themselves.[^1-31] They are waterless clouds, carried along by the winds; trees in late autumn without fruit, twice dead, uprooted;

**13** wild waves of the sea, foaming up their own shameful deeds;[^1-32] wandering stars,[^1-33] for whom the gloom of darkness has been kept forever.

**14** Enoch, the seventh from Adam, also prophesied about these people, saying, "Look, the Lord has come[^1-34] with tens of thousands of his holy ones,[^1-35]

**15** to execute judgment on all, and to convict all the ungodly[^1-36] of all their ungodly deeds that they have done in their ungodliness, and of all the harsh things that ungodly sinners have spoken against him."[^1-37]

**16** These people are grumblers and complainers,[^1-38] who follow their own desires. Their mouths speak arrogant words, and they flatter people[^1-39] to gain an advantage.

**17** But you, dear friends, remember the words that were spoken beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**18** For they told you, "In the last time there will be scoffers, who will follow their own ungodly desires."[^1-40]

**19** These are the people who cause divisions, worldly people[^1-41] who do not have the Spirit.[^1-42]

**20** But you, dear friends, building yourselves up on your most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit,

**21** keep yourselves in the love of God, as you wait for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life.

**22** Have mercy on some who are doubting;[^1-43]

**23** save others, snatching them out of the fire;[^1-44] and on others have mercy with fear, hating even the clothing stained by the flesh.[^1-45]

**24** To the one who is able to keep you[^1-46] from stumbling and to make you stand in the presence of his glory without blemish and with great joy,

**25** to the only God our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, power and authority, before all time and now and forever.[^1-47] Amen.

[^1-1]: Greek *Ioudas*, the same name as Judah and Judas. A Judas is named among the brothers of Jesus, with a James, in Mark 6:3.
[^1-2]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^1-3]: Literally "loved in God the Father". Many manuscripts read "made holy by God the Father".
[^1-4]: Or "kept by Jesus Christ".
[^1-5]: Literally "be multiplied to you".
[^1-6]: Literally "Beloved"; also in verses 17 and 20.
[^1-7]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "the saints".
[^1-8]: Or "people about whom this condemnation was written long ago".
[^1-9]: Greek *aselgeia*, "debauchery, unrestrained behavior".
[^1-10]: Many manuscripts read "deny the only Master, God, and our Lord Jesus Christ".
[^1-11]: Greek *Iesous*, which is also the Greek form of the name Joshua. The SBLGNT here follows NA28; other editions read "the Lord".
[^1-12]: Literally "the second time destroyed those who did not believe". See Numbers 14:11, 26–35.
[^1-13]: Or "their own domain". Greek *arche*, "beginning, rule, domain".
[^1-14]: The same Greek verb (*tereo*) as "keep" earlier in the verse, and as "kept" in verses 1 and 13.
[^1-15]: Compare Genesis 6:1–4. The fuller story of these angels, their imprisonment in darkness and their judgment, is told in 1 Enoch 6–16, a Jewish writing from the last centuries BC.
[^1-16]: That is, probably the angels of verse 6.
[^1-17]: Literally "different flesh". This may refer to the men of Sodom's attempt on the angels who visited Lot (Genesis 19:1–11), or more generally to unnatural sexual relations.
[^1-18]: See Genesis 19:24–25.
[^1-19]: Literally "dreaming", perhaps claiming visions as their authority.
[^1-20]: Or "lordship". Greek *kyriotes*, perhaps the authority of the Lord.
[^1-21]: Literally "glories", probably angelic beings.
[^1-22]: Or "to condemn him for slander", that is, to condemn him in insulting words himself.
[^1-23]: *Michael* is one of the chief angels (Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Revelation 12:7). The Old Testament tells of Moses' death and burial (Deuteronomy 34:5–6), but not of this dispute. Early Christian writers, among them Origen, say that the story came from a Jewish writing called the Assumption of Moses, whose ending, where it would have stood, is now lost. *The Lord rebuke you*: compare Zechariah 3:2, where the angel of the LORD says to the Accuser, "The LORD rebuke you". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" there is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^1-24]: Literally "by these they are destroyed".
[^1-25]: Greek *ouai*, a cry of grief and warning, like the prophets' *hoy* ("woe").
[^1-26]: Genesis 4:1–16.
[^1-27]: Numbers 22–24; 31:16. Literally "they have poured themselves out into Balaam's error for wages".
[^1-28]: Numbers 16:1–35. *Rebellion*: literally "speaking against".
[^1-29]: Or "blemishes". Greek *spilades* usually means rocks hidden in the sea; a similar word (*spiloi*) means "stains".
[^1-30]: Greek *agapai*, "loves": the shared meals of the early Christians.
[^1-31]: Literally "feasting together without fear, shepherding themselves". "Without fear" may go with either phrase. Compare Ezekiel 34:2.
[^1-32]: Compare Isaiah 57:20: "its waters toss up mud and mire".
[^1-33]: Greek *asteres planetai*, from which comes the word "planet". In 1 Enoch 18:13–16 and 21:1–6, stars that did not keep to their appointed times are punished.
[^1-34]: Literally "came", a past tense used of a future event, as often in prophecy. *Seventh from Adam*: counting Adam as the first (Genesis 5:3–18).
[^1-35]: Or "with his holy myriads". *Holy ones* here means angels.
[^1-36]: Many manuscripts read "all the ungodly among them". Some manuscripts read "to convict every person".
[^1-37]: Verses 14–15 quote 1 Enoch 1:9. The book of 1 Enoch survives in full only in Ethiopic, with parts in Greek and in Aramaic among the Dead Sea Scrolls. Jude's wording is close to the surviving Greek, though not the same.
[^1-38]: Literally "blaming their lot".
[^1-39]: Literally "admiring faces", showing favoritism.
[^1-40]: Literally "following their own desires for ungodly things".
[^1-41]: Greek *psychikoi*, "natural, unspiritual": people governed by mere human life (*psyche*), not by the Spirit.
[^1-42]: Or "spirit".
[^1-43]: Or "who are disputing".
[^1-44]: The Greek text of verses 22–23 differs widely between manuscripts and editions. Many manuscripts read "Have mercy on some, making a distinction, and save others with fear, snatching them out of the fire, hating even …". Some manuscripts read "Correct some who are doubting", and some have only two groups of people instead of three. *Snatching them out of the fire*: compare Zechariah 3:2; Amos 4:11.
[^1-45]: Literally "the tunic stained by the flesh". Compare Zechariah 3:3–4, where the high priest's "filthy clothes" are taken off him.
[^1-46]: Many manuscripts read "them".
[^1-47]: Many manuscripts read "to the only wise God our Savior, be glory and majesty, power and authority, now and forever", without "through Jesus Christ our Lord" and "before all time". *Forever*: literally "into all the ages".
