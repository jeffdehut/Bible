## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** After the death of Saul, when David had come back from defeating the Amalekites, David stayed in Ziklag for two days.

**2** On the third day a man came from Saul's camp, with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**3** David said to him, "Where have you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**4** David said to him, "What happened? Tell me." He said, "The troops fled from the battle. Many of the troops fell and died, and Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too."

**5** David said to the young man who had brought him the news, "How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?"

**6** The young man who had brought him the news said, "I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul, leaning on his spear. The chariots and the horsemen[^1-1] were closing in on him.

**7** He turned around, saw me, and called to me. I said, 'Here I am.'

**8** He said to me, 'Who are you?' I said to him,[^1-2] 'I am an Amalekite.'

**9** He said to me, 'Please stand over me and kill me, for agony[^1-3] has seized me, and yet my life is still in me.'

**10** So I stood over him and killed him, because I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. Then I took the crown that was on his head and the band that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**11** Then David took hold of his clothes[^1-4] and tore them, and so did all the men who were with him.

**12** They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and for his son Jonathan, and for the people of the LORD[^1-5] and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**13** David said to the young man who had brought him the news, "Where are you from?" He said, "I am the son of a foreigner,[^1-6] an Amalekite."

**14** David said to him, "How is it that you were not afraid to lift your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"[^1-7]

**15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Go, strike him down!" So he struck him, and he died.

**16** David said to him, "Your blood[^1-8] is on your own head, because your own mouth testified against you when you said, 'I killed the LORD's anointed.'"

**17** David sang this lament over Saul and over his son Jonathan,

**18** and he ordered that the people of Judah be taught "The Bow."[^1-9] It is written in the Book of Jashar:[^1-10]

**19** "Your glory,[^1-11] Israel, lies slain on your heights. How the mighty have fallen!

**20** Do not tell it in Gath; do not announce it in the streets of Ashkelon, or the daughters of the Philistines will rejoice; the daughters of the uncircumcised will celebrate.

**21** Mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew and no rain on you, nor fields that yield offerings.[^1-12] For there the shield of the mighty was defiled,[^1-13] the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil.[^1-14]

**22** From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back, and the sword of Saul did not come back empty.

**23** Saul and Jonathan, loved and delightful in their lives, were not parted in their death. They were swifter than eagles; they were stronger than lions.

**24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet and luxury, who put ornaments of gold on your clothing.

**25** How the mighty have fallen in the middle of the battle! Jonathan lies slain on your heights.[^1-15]

**26** I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan. You were very dear to me. Your love for me was more wonderful than the love of women.

**27** How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"

[^1-1]: Literally "the masters of the horsemen", that is, the horsemen or their commanders.
[^1-2]: The Hebrew text is written "He said to him" but read "I said to him".
[^1-3]: Hebrew *shavats*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps cramp, dizziness or the agony of death.
[^1-4]: The Hebrew text is written "his garment" but read "his garments".
[^1-5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^1-7]: Hebrew *meshiach YHWH*: the king anointed with oil at the LORD's command (1 Samuel 10:1). *Meshiach*, "anointed one", is the word that gives English "Messiah". Also in verse 16.
[^1-8]: The Hebrew text is written "your bloods", a plural that can mean "your bloodguilt", but read "your blood".
[^1-9]: Literally "And he said to teach the sons of Judah 'Bow'." *The Bow* is probably the title of the lament, perhaps taken from verse 22. Some take it to mean that David ordered the people of Judah to be taught archery.
[^1-10]: Or "the Book of the Upright". A collection of poems that has not survived, also quoted in Joshua 10:13.
[^1-11]: Hebrew *ha-tsevi*, "the beauty, the glory", probably meaning Saul and Jonathan. The same word means "gazelle", so some translate "The gazelle, Israel, lies slain".
[^1-12]: Literally "fields of contributions", perhaps meaning fields whose crops supply offerings. The meaning is uncertain.
[^1-13]: Or "cast aside as worthless".
[^1-14]: Or "as though not anointed with oil". Leather shields were rubbed with oil before battle. The words may refer to the shield or to Saul, the anointed king.
[^1-15]: Or "Jonathan, slain on your heights!" As in verse 19, "your" is probably Israel.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** After this, David asked the LORD,[^2-1] "Shall I go up to one of the towns of Judah?" The LORD said to him, "Go up." David said, "Where shall I go?" He said, "To Hebron."

**2** So David went up there, with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail the widow[^2-2] of Nabal of Carmel.

**3** David also brought up the men who were with him, each with his household, and they settled in the towns around Hebron.

**4** Then the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. When David was told, "It was the men of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul,"

**5** David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "May the LORD bless you, because you showed this faithful love[^2-3] to your lord Saul and buried him.

**6** Now may the LORD show you faithful love and faithfulness. I too will do good to you, because you have done this.[^2-4]

**7** Now be strong and courageous,[^2-5] for your lord Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them."

**8** Now Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul's army, had taken Ish-bosheth[^2-6] son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim.

**9** He made him king over Gilead, the Ashurites[^2-7] and Jezreel, and over Ephraim, Benjamin and all Israel.

**10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he reigned for two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

**11** The length of time that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

**12** Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

**13** Joab son of Zeruiah and David's servants also went out, and they met them at the pool of Gibeon. They sat down, one group on one side of the pool and the other on the other side.

**14** Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men get up and hold a contest[^2-8] in front of us." Joab said, "Let them get up."

**15** So they got up and went over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David's servants.

**16** Each man grabbed his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent's side, and they fell down together. So that place, which is in Gibeon, was called Helkath-hazzurim.[^2-9]

**17** The battle was very fierce that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were defeated by David's servants.

**18** The three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel was as fast on his feet as a wild gazelle.[^2-10]

**19** Asahel chased Abner, and he did not turn to the right or to the left from following Abner.

**20** Abner looked behind him and said, "Is that you, Asahel?" He said, "It is."

**21** Abner said to him, "Turn aside to your right or to your left. Grab one of the young men and take his gear[^2-11] for yourself." But Asahel would not turn aside from following him.

**22** Abner said again to Asahel, "Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground? How could I then look your brother Joab in the face?"

**23** But he refused to turn aside. So Abner struck him in the stomach[^2-12] with the butt of his spear, and the spear came out of his back. He fell there and died on the spot. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stood still.

**24** But Joab and Abishai chased Abner. As the sun was going down, they came to the hill of Ammah, which is opposite Giah on the way to the wilderness of Gibeon.

**25** The Benjaminites rallied behind Abner and formed a single band, and they took their stand on the top of a hill.

**26** Abner called to Joab, "Must the sword go on devouring forever? Don't you know that it will be bitter in the end? How long will it be before you tell the troops to turn back from chasing their brothers?"

**27** Joab said, "As God lives, if you had not spoken, the troops would not have given up chasing their brothers until morning."[^2-13]

**28** Then Joab blew the ram's horn, and all the troops stopped. They did not chase Israel any longer, and they fought no more.

**29** Abner and his men marched through the Arabah[^2-14] all that night. They crossed the Jordan, went through the whole Bithron,[^2-15] and came to Mahanaim.

**30** Joab came back from chasing Abner and gathered all the troops. Nineteen of David's servants were missing, besides Asahel.

**31** But David's servants had struck down the men of Benjamin, Abner's men, and 360 of them had died.

**32** They took Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb in Bethlehem. Then Joab and his men marched all night, and day broke for them at Hebron.

[^2-1]: Literally "asked of the LORD", that is, sought an answer from the LORD, probably by the sacred lots of the priest (1 Samuel 23:9–12). *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-2]: Literally "the wife".
[^2-3]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 6.
[^2-4]: Literally "And I also will do this good with you, because you have done this thing."
[^2-5]: Literally "let your hands be strong, and be sons of strength".
[^2-6]: Hebrew *Ish-boshet*, "man of shame". In 1 Chronicles 8:33 and 9:39 he is called Eshbaal, "man of Baal" or "Baal exists". *Boshet*, "shame", seems to have been put in place of the name of the Canaanite god Baal (compare Jeremiah 11:13), though *baal* is also an ordinary word meaning "lord".
[^2-7]: Hebrew "the Ashurite". This may mean the tribe of Asher, as the Aramaic Targum understands it. The Syriac and the Vulgate read "Geshur".
[^2-8]: Literally "play", meaning here a contest of arms.
[^2-9]: *Helkath-hazzurim* probably means "field of flints" or "field of blades". The Septuagint reads "field of the plotters".
[^2-10]: Literally "like one of the gazelles in the open country".
[^2-11]: Hebrew *chalitsah*: what is stripped from a fallen soldier, his weapons and armor.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *chomesh*, probably "the belly". Traditionally "the fifth rib".
[^2-13]: Literally "then from the morning the troops would have gone up, each from after his brother." Or "if you had not spoken (as in verse 14), the troops would have stopped chasing their brothers this morning".
[^2-14]: Hebrew *aravah*: the deep valley of the Jordan.
[^2-15]: Hebrew *ha-bitron*, perhaps "the ravine", the name of a valley or region east of the Jordan. Some understand it as "the whole morning".

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** The war between the house of Saul and the house of David went on for a long time. David grew stronger and stronger, while the house of Saul grew weaker and weaker.

**2** Sons were born to David in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam of Jezreel.

**3** His second was Chileab,[^3-1] by Abigail the widow[^3-2] of Nabal of Carmel. The third was Absalom son of Maacah, the daughter of Talmai king of Geshur.

**4** The fourth was Adonijah son of Haggith, and the fifth was Shephatiah son of Abital.

**5** The sixth was Ithream, by David's wife Eglah. These were born to David in Hebron.

**6** While there was war between the house of Saul and the house of David, Abner was making himself strong in the house of Saul.

**7** Now Saul had had a concubine[^3-3] named Rizpah daughter of Aiah. And Ish-bosheth[^3-4] said to Abner, "Why have you slept with[^3-5] my father's concubine?"

**8** Abner was very angry at Ish-bosheth's words, and he said, "Am I a dog's head who belongs to Judah?[^3-6] Today I keep showing faithful love[^3-7] to the house of your father Saul, to his brothers and to his friends, and I have not handed you over to David. Yet today you charge me with a wrong over this woman!

**9** May God deal with Abner, and more severely,[^3-8] if I do not do for David what the LORD[^3-9] swore to him:

**10** to transfer the kingdom from the house of Saul, and to set up the throne of David over Israel and over Judah, from Dan to Beersheba."

**11** Ish-bosheth could not answer Abner another word, because he was afraid of him.

**12** Abner sent messengers to David on his own behalf,[^3-10] saying, "Whose is the land?"[^3-11] and saying, "Make your covenant with me, and my hand will be with you to bring all Israel over to you."

**13** David said, "Good. I will make a covenant with you. But I ask one thing of you: you will not see my face unless you first bring Michal daughter of Saul when you come to see my face."

**14** Then David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, saying, "Give me my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself for a hundred Philistine foreskins."

**15** So Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband, from Paltiel son of Laish.[^3-12]

**16** But her husband went with her, weeping as he followed her all the way to Bahurim. Then Abner said to him, "Go back!" And he went back.

**17** Abner conferred with the elders of Israel, saying, "For some time now[^3-13] you have been wanting David as king over you.

**18** Now do it! For the LORD has said of David, 'By the hand of my servant David I will save[^3-14] my people Israel from the hand of the Philistines and from the hand of all their enemies.'"

**19** Abner also spoke directly to the Benjaminites.[^3-15] Then Abner also went to speak directly to David in Hebron about everything that Israel and the whole house of Benjamin had agreed to.[^3-16]

**20** When Abner came to David at Hebron, with twenty men, David held a feast for Abner and for the men who were with him.

**21** Abner said to David, "Let me go at once and gather all Israel to my lord the king, so that they may make a covenant with you, and you will reign over all that you desire." So David sent Abner away, and he went in peace.

**22** Just then David's servants and Joab came in from a raid, bringing a great amount of plunder with them. But Abner was no longer with David in Hebron, because David had sent him away, and he had gone in peace.

**23** When Joab and all the army that was with him arrived, Joab was told, "Abner son of Ner came to the king, and he sent him away, and he has gone in peace."

**24** Joab went to the king and said, "What have you done? Abner came to you! Why did you send him away, so that he is now gone?

**25** You know Abner son of Ner. He came to deceive you, to find out your going out and your coming in,[^3-17] and to find out everything you are doing."

**26** Joab left David and sent messengers after Abner, and they brought him back from the cistern of Sirah. But David did not know.

**27** When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside into the gateway to speak with him privately, and there he struck him in the stomach.[^3-18] So he died for the blood of Joab's brother Asahel.[^3-19]

**28** Afterward, when David heard of it, he said, "I and my kingdom are forever innocent before the LORD of the blood of Abner son of Ner.

**29** May it fall[^3-20] on the head of Joab and on all his father's house. May Joab's house never be without someone who has a discharge or a skin disease,[^3-21] or who holds a spindle,[^3-22] or who falls by the sword, or who lacks bread."

**30** So Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner, because he had killed their brother Asahel at Gibeon in the battle.

**31** Then David said to Joab and to all the people who were with him, "Tear your clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn before Abner." And King David walked behind the bier.

**32** They buried Abner in Hebron. The king wept aloud at Abner's grave, and all the people wept.

**33** The king sang a lament over Abner and said, "Should Abner have died as a fool dies?[^3-23]

**34** Your hands were not bound; your feet were not put in bronze chains. As one falls before the wicked,[^3-24] you fell." And all the people wept over him again.

**35** Then all the people came to urge David to eat some food[^3-25] while it was still day, but David swore, "May God deal with me, and more severely, if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down."

**36** All the people took note, and it pleased them, just as everything the king did pleased all the people.

**37** So all the people and all Israel knew that day that the king had no part in the killing of Abner son of Ner.

**38** The king said to his servants, "Don't you know that a commander and a great man has fallen in Israel today?

**39** Today I am weak, even though I am anointed king,[^3-26] and these men, the sons of Zeruiah, are too harsh for me. May the LORD repay the one who does evil according to his evil!"

[^3-1]: In 1 Chronicles 3:1 he is called Daniel.
[^3-2]: Literally "the wife".
[^3-3]: A wife of lower rank, often a slave. To take a dead king's concubine could be a claim to his throne (16:21–22).
[^3-4]: Hebrew "he". The name is supplied from verse 8. On the name *Ish-bosheth*, see 2:8.
[^3-5]: Literally "gone in to", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^3-6]: A term of contempt. Abner asks whether Ish-bosheth takes him for a worthless traitor who serves Judah.
[^3-7]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^3-8]: Literally "Thus may God do to Abner, and thus may he add to him", a solemn oath calling down punishment on oneself if one does not keep one's word. Also in verse 35.
[^3-9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-10]: Hebrew *tachtav*, literally "in his place". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "on his own behalf" or "at once".
[^3-11]: That is, perhaps, "Who controls the land, if not I?" or "To whom should the land belong, if not to you?"
[^3-12]: The Hebrew text is written "Lush" but read "Laish".
[^3-13]: Literally "both yesterday and the day before".
[^3-14]: The Hebrew reads "he saved" or "to save" (*hoshia*). Many Hebrew manuscripts and the Septuagint read "I will save" (*oshia*).
[^3-15]: Literally "spoke in the ears of Benjamin". Also later in the verse, "in the ears of David".
[^3-16]: Literally "all that was good in the eyes of Israel and in the eyes of the whole house of Benjamin".
[^3-17]: That is, all your movements and plans.
[^3-18]: Hebrew *chomesh*; see 2:23.
[^3-19]: Literally "for the blood of Asahel his brother".
[^3-20]: Literally "May it whirl" or "May it burst", that is, the guilt for Abner's blood.
[^3-21]: Hebrew *metsora*, someone with *tsara'at*, traditionally "leprosy": a range of skin conditions that made a person ritually unclean (Leviticus 13–14).
[^3-22]: Hebrew *pelekh*, a spindle, probably meaning a man fit only for what was then women's work. Some take it to mean a crutch.
[^3-23]: Hebrew *naval*, a senseless or godless person. The word is the name Nabal (1 Samuel 25:25).
[^3-24]: Literally "sons of injustice".
[^3-25]: Literally "bread".
[^3-26]: Literally "I am tender and anointed king".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** When Saul's son[^4-1] heard that Abner had died in Hebron, he lost courage,[^4-2] and all Israel was alarmed.

**2** Saul's son had two men who were commanders of raiding bands. The name of one was Baanah, and the name of the other was Rechab. They were sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, of the Benjaminites, for Beeroth too is counted as part of Benjamin.

**3** The Beerothites had fled to Gittaim, and they have lived there as foreigners to this day.

**4** Jonathan son of Saul had a son who was lame in both feet. He was five years old when the news about Saul and Jonathan came from Jezreel. His nurse picked him up and fled, but as she hurried to flee, he fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth.[^4-3]

**5** The sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, set out, and they came in the heat of the day to the house of Ish-bosheth, while he was taking his midday rest.

**6** They came into the middle of the house as if to fetch wheat,[^4-4] and they struck him in the stomach. Then Rechab and his brother Baanah slipped away.

**7** They had come into the house while he was lying on his bed in his bedroom, and they struck him and killed him. They cut off his head, took his head, and traveled all night by the road through the Arabah.

**8** They brought the head of Ish-bosheth to David at Hebron and said to the king, "Here is the head of Ish-bosheth son of Saul, your enemy, who sought your life. Today the LORD[^4-5] has given my lord the king vengeance on Saul and on his offspring."

**9** David answered Rechab and his brother Baanah, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, and said to them, "As the LORD lives, who has rescued my life from every trouble,

**10** when someone told me, 'Look, Saul is dead,' and thought he was bringing good news, I seized him and killed him at Ziklag. That was the reward I gave him for his news.[^4-6]

**11** How much more, when wicked men have killed an innocent man in his own house on his bed! Now shall I not demand his blood from your hand and wipe you off the earth?"

**12** So David gave orders to the young men, and they killed them. They cut off their hands and their feet and hung them up beside the pool at Hebron. But they took the head of Ish-bosheth and buried it in Abner's tomb in Hebron.

[^4-1]: Hebrew "the son of Saul", that is, Ish-bosheth (2:8). Also in verse 2.
[^4-2]: Literally "his hands went limp".
[^4-3]: Hebrew *Mephiboshet*. In 1 Chronicles 8:34 and 9:40 he is called Merib-baal. On the change of *baal* to *boshet*, "shame", see 2:8.
[^4-4]: Literally "taking wheat". The Hebrew is difficult. The Septuagint reads "And look, the doorkeeper of the house was cleaning wheat, and she grew drowsy and fell asleep, and Rechab and his brother Baanah slipped in."
[^4-5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-6]: Literally "which was for my giving him good news", that is, his reward for bringing good news (1:15).

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, "Look, we are your own flesh and blood.[^5-1]

**2** For some time now,[^5-2] while Saul was king over us, it was you who led Israel out to battle and brought it back.[^5-3] And the LORD[^5-4] said to you, 'You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be ruler[^5-5] over Israel.'"

**3** All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel.

**4** David was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned for forty years.

**5** In Hebron he reigned over Judah for seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned over all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.

**6** The king and his men went to Jerusalem, to the Jebusites who lived in the land. They said to David, "You will not get in here. Even the blind and the lame will turn you away."[^5-6] They meant,[^5-7] "David cannot get in here."

**7** But David captured the stronghold of Zion, that is, the City of David.

**8** On that day David said, "Whoever would strike down the Jebusites must get up through the water shaft[^5-8] to reach the lame and the blind, who are hated by David."[^5-9] That is why they say, "The blind and the lame will not come into the house."[^5-10]

**9** David lived in the stronghold and called it the City of David. David built all around it, from the Millo[^5-11] inward.

**10** David became greater and greater, for the LORD, the God of Armies,[^5-12] was with him.

**11** Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar logs, carpenters and stonemasons,[^5-13] and they built a house for David.

**12** Then David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel, and that he had exalted his kingdom for the sake of his people Israel.

**13** After he came from Hebron, David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to David.

**14** These are the names of those born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,

**15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia,

**16** Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.

**17** When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, all the Philistines went up to search for David. David heard about it and went down to the stronghold.

**18** The Philistines had come and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**19** David asked the LORD, "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you hand them over to me?" The LORD said to David, "Go up, for I will certainly hand the Philistines over to you."

**20** So David came to Baal-perazim, and David defeated them there. He said, "The LORD has broken through my enemies ahead of me, as water breaks through." That is why that place was called Baal-perazim.[^5-14]

**21** The Philistines abandoned their idols there, and David and his men carried them off.[^5-15]

**22** Once again the Philistines came up and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**23** When David asked the LORD, he said, "Do not go up. Circle around behind them and come at them opposite the balsam trees.[^5-16]

**24** When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, then act quickly, for then the LORD will have gone out ahead of you to strike down the army of the Philistines."

**25** David did as the LORD had commanded him, and he struck down the Philistines from Geba[^5-17] all the way to Gezer.

[^5-1]: Literally "your bone and your flesh".
[^5-2]: Literally "both yesterday and the day before".
[^5-3]: Literally "you were the one who led out and brought in Israel".
[^5-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5-5]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, ruler", someone designated by God to lead (1 Samuel 9:16). Traditionally "prince" or "captain".
[^5-6]: Or "You will not get in here unless you remove the blind and the lame." The Hebrew can be read either way.
[^5-7]: Literally "saying".

**4** is like the light of morning when the sun rises, a morning without clouds, when the grass springs from the earth through sunshine after rain.'[^23-5]

**5** Is not my house like this with God?[^23-6] For he has made an everlasting covenant with me, ordered in every part and secured. Will he not make all my salvation and all my desire grow?

**6** But the worthless[^23-7] are all like thorns that are tossed away, for they cannot be picked up by hand.

**7** Whoever touches them must arm himself with iron or the shaft of a spear, and they are utterly burned up with fire where they lie."[^23-8]

**8** These are the names of David's mighty warriors: Josheb-basshebeth[^23-9] the Tahchemonite, the chief of the officers.[^23-10] He was Adino the Eznite, who killed eight hundred men at one time.[^23-11]

**9** Next to him was Eleazar son of Dodo,[^23-12] son of Ahohi,[^23-13] one of the three mighty warriors with David when they taunted the Philistines who were gathered there for battle. The men of Israel withdrew,

**10** but he stood his ground and struck down the Philistines until his hand grew tired and stuck to the sword. The LORD brought about a great victory that day, and the troops came back after him only to strip the dead.

**11** Next to him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. The Philistines had gathered together at Lehi,[^23-14] where there was a plot of ground full of lentils, and the troops fled from the Philistines.

**12** But he took his stand in the middle of the plot, defended it and struck down the Philistines. And the LORD brought about a great victory.

**13** Three[^23-15] of the Thirty,[^23-16] the chiefs, went down at harvest time[^23-17] and came to David at the cave of Adullam, while a band of Philistines was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.

**14** At that time David was in the stronghold, and a Philistine garrison was then at Bethlehem.

**15** David felt a craving and said, "If only someone would give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate!"

**16** So the three mighty warriors broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate, and carried it back to David. But he would not drink it. He poured it out to the LORD

**17** and said, "Far be it from me, LORD, that I should do this! Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?"[^23-18] So he would not drink it. These were the deeds of the three mighty warriors.

**18** Abishai, the brother of Joab son of Zeruiah, was chief of the Three.[^23-19] He wielded his spear against three hundred men and killed them, and he won a name among the Three.

**19** Was he not the most honored of the Three?[^23-20] He became their commander, but he did not attain to the Three.

**20** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was the son of a valiant man[^23-21] from Kabzeel, a man of great deeds. He struck down two champions[^23-22] of Moab. He also went down and killed a lion inside a pit on a snowy day.

**21** He also struck down an Egyptian, a man of impressive appearance. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand, but Benaiah went down to him with a club, snatched the spear out of the Egyptian's hand and killed him with his own spear.

**22** These were the deeds of Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he won a name among the three mighty warriors.

**23** He was more honored than the Thirty, but he did not attain to the Three. And David put him in charge of his bodyguard.[^23-23]

**24** Among the Thirty were Asahel the brother of Joab; Elhanan son of Dodo, of Bethlehem;

**25** Shammah the Harodite; Elika the Harodite;

**26** Helez the Paltite; Ira son of Ikkesh, the Tekoite;

**27** Abiezer the Anathothite; Mebunnai[^23-24] the Hushathite;

**28** Zalmon the Ahohite; Maharai the Netophathite;

**29** Heleb[^23-25] son of Baanah, the Netophathite; Ittai son of Ribai, from Gibeah of the Benjaminites;

**30** Benaiah the Pirathonite; Hiddai of the ravines of Gaash;

**31** Abi-albon the Arbathite; Azmaveth the Barhumite;

**32** Eliahba the Shaalbonite; the sons of Jashen; Jonathan;[^23-26]

**33** Shammah the Hararite; Ahiam son of Sharar the Hararite;[^23-27]

**34** Eliphelet son of Ahasbai, the son of the Maacathite; Eliam son of Ahithophel, the Gilonite;

**35** Hezrai[^23-28] the Carmelite; Paarai the Arbite;

**36** Igal son of Nathan, from Zobah; Bani the Gadite;

**37** Zelek the Ammonite; Naharai the Beerothite, the armor-bearer[^23-29] of Joab son of Zeruiah;

**38** Ira the Ithrite; Gareb the Ithrite;

**39** and Uriah the Hittite. Thirty-seven in all.

## Chapter 24

*Status: draft*

**1** Again the anger of the LORD[^24-1] burned against Israel, and he incited David against them, saying, "Go, count Israel and Judah."[^24-2]

**2** So the king said to Joab, the commander of the army, who was with him, "Go through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, and register the people, so that I may know the number of the people."

**3** Joab said to the king, "May the LORD your God multiply the people a hundred times over, while the eyes of my lord the king can still see it! But why does my lord the king want to do this?"

**4** But the king's word prevailed against Joab and the commanders of the army. So Joab and the commanders of the army went out from the king's presence to register the people of Israel.

**5** They crossed the Jordan and camped at Aroer, south of the town that is in the middle of the valley,[^24-3] toward Gad and on to Jazer.

**6** Then they came to Gilead and to the land of Tahtim-hodshi,[^24-4] and they came to Dan-jaan[^24-5] and around toward Sidon.

**7** They came to the fortress of Tyre and to all the towns of the Hivites and the Canaanites, and they went out to the Negev of Judah at Beersheba.

**8** So they went through the whole land, and at the end of nine months and twenty days they came back to Jerusalem.

**9** Joab gave the king the total from the registration of the people: in Israel there were eight hundred thousand valiant men who could draw the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand.[^24-6]

**10** But David's conscience troubled him[^24-7] after he had counted the people. David said to the LORD, "I have sinned greatly in what I have done. Now, LORD, please take away the guilt of your servant, for I have acted very foolishly."

**11** When David got up in the morning, the word of the LORD had come to the prophet Gad, David's seer:

**12** "Go and tell David, 'This is what the LORD says: I am setting[^24-8] three things before you. Choose one of them, and I will do it to you.'"

**13** So Gad came to David and told him. He said to him, "Shall seven years[^24-9] of famine come upon you in your land? Or shall you flee for three months before your enemies while they pursue you? Or shall there be three days of plague in your land? Now consider and decide what answer I should take back to the one who sent me."

**14** David said to Gad, "I am in great distress. Let us fall into the hand of the LORD, for his mercy is great, but do not let me fall into human hands."

**15** So the LORD sent a plague on Israel from the morning until the appointed time,[^24-10] and seventy thousand of the people died, from Dan to Beersheba.

**16** When the angel[^24-11] stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, the LORD relented concerning the disaster[^24-12] and said to the angel who was destroying the people, "Enough! Now hold back your hand." The angel of the LORD was then by the threshing floor of Araunah[^24-13] the Jebusite.

**17** When David saw the angel who was striking down the people, he said to the LORD, "I am the one who has sinned; I am the one who has done wrong.[^24-14] But these sheep, what have they done? Please let your hand be against me and against my father's house."

**18** That day Gad came to David and said to him, "Go up and set up an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah[^24-15] the Jebusite."

**19** So David went up at Gad's word, as the LORD had commanded.

**20** When Araunah looked down and saw the king and his servants coming over toward him, Araunah went out and bowed down to the king with his face to the ground.

**21** Araunah said, "Why has my lord the king come to his servant?" David said, "To buy the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to the LORD, so that the plague may be held back from the people."

**22** Araunah said to David, "Let my lord the king take it and offer up whatever seems good to him. Look, here are the oxen for the burnt offering, and the threshing sledges and the oxen's yokes[^24-16] for the wood.

**23** All this, O king, Araunah gives to the king."[^24-17] And Araunah said to the king, "May the LORD your God accept you."

**24** But the king said to Araunah, "No, I will buy it from you for a price. I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God that cost me nothing." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels[^24-18] of silver.

**25** David built an altar to the LORD there and offered burnt offerings and fellowship offerings.[^24-19] Then the LORD answered prayers for the land, and the plague was held back from Israel.[^24-20]
