## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The oracle[^1-1] that Habakkuk the prophet saw.[^1-2]

**2** How long, Yahweh,[^1-3] must I cry for help, and you do not listen? I cry out to you, "Violence!" but you do not save.

**3** Why do you make me see wickedness, and why do you look on trouble?[^1-4] Destruction and violence are before me; there is strife, and conflict rises up.

**4** So the law[^1-5] grows numb, and justice never goes out. For the wicked surround the righteous, so justice goes out twisted.

**5** Look among the nations and see![^1-6] Be astonished, be amazed! For I am doing a deed[^1-7] in your days that you would not believe if you were told.

**6** For look, I am raising up the Chaldeans,[^1-8] that bitter and hasty nation, which marches through the breadth of the earth to seize dwellings that are not its own.

**7** They are dreaded and feared; their justice and their dignity come from themselves.[^1-9]

**8** Their horses are swifter than leopards and fiercer than wolves at evening.[^1-10] Their horsemen gallop on;[^1-11] their horsemen come from far away. They fly like an eagle swooping to devour.

**9** They all come for violence; their faces press forward like the east wind,[^1-12] and they gather captives like sand.

**10** They scoff at kings, and rulers are a joke to them. They laugh at every fortress; they heap up earth[^1-13] and capture it.

**11** Then they sweep past like the wind and go on, guilty men, whose own strength is their god.[^1-14]

**12** Are you not from of old, Yahweh, my God, my Holy One? We will not die.[^1-15] Yahweh, you have appointed them to execute judgment; Rock,[^1-16] you have established them to punish.

**13** Your eyes are too pure to look on evil, and you cannot look on wrongdoing. Why then do you look on the treacherous and keep silent when the wicked swallow up those more righteous than they?

**14** You have made human beings like the fish of the sea, like creeping things that have no ruler.

**15** He[^1-17] brings all of them up with a hook; he drags them out with his net and gathers them in his dragnet. So he rejoices and is glad.

**16** That is why he sacrifices to his net and burns incense to his dragnet, for by them his portion is rich and his food is plentiful.[^1-18]

**17** Will he therefore keep emptying his net[^1-19] and go on killing nations without mercy?

[^1-1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden".
[^1-2]: Nothing certain is known of Habakkuk. He probably prophesied in the last years of the seventh century BC, when the Babylonians were rising to power (verse 6). The book is a dialogue: Habakkuk complains (1:2–4), Yahweh answers (1:5–11), Habakkuk complains again (1:12–17), and Yahweh answers again (2:1–20). As in the other prophetic books, Yahweh's words in the poems are not put in quotation marks.
[^1-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-4]: Or "Why do you make me look on trouble?"
[^1-5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^1-6]: The Septuagint reads "Look, you scoffers", and Paul quotes this verse in that form in Acts 13:41.
[^1-7]: Literally "a deed is doing". *I* is supplied from verse 6.
[^1-8]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, the people of southern Babylonia, whose kings ruled a new Babylonian empire from 626 BC. Under Nebuchadnezzar they defeated Egypt at Carchemish in 605 BC.
[^1-9]: That is, they recognize no law but their own.
[^1-10]: Wolves that hunt at nightfall, hungry after the day (Zephaniah 3:3).
[^1-11]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Perhaps "paw the ground" or "spring forward".
[^1-12]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "the eagerness of their faces is eastward" or "forward".
[^1-13]: A siege ramp, built up against the city wall.
[^1-14]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Then a wind passes on and goes through, and is guilty, this one whose strength is his god".
[^1-15]: Jewish scribal tradition counts this among the "corrections of the scribes", saying that the text once read "You will not die" and was changed out of reverence for God.
[^1-16]: A title of God, the one on whom his people can rely (Deuteronomy 32:4).
[^1-17]: The Babylonian conqueror, pictured as a fisherman.
[^1-18]: Literally "his portion is fat and his food is fat".
[^1-19]: The Habakkuk commentary found at Qumran (1QpHab) reads "his sword".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** I will stand at my watch post and station myself on the rampart. I will watch to see what he will say to me,[^2-1] and what I will answer concerning my complaint.[^2-2]

**2** Then Yahweh[^2-3] answered me: Write down the vision; make it plain on tablets, so that the one who reads it may run.[^2-4]

**3** For the vision is still for the appointed time. It hurries[^2-5] toward the end, and it will not lie. If it seems slow, wait for it; it will surely come, it will not delay.

**4** Look, his soul is puffed up;[^2-6] it is not upright within him. But the righteous will live by his faithfulness.[^2-7]

**5** Moreover, wine[^2-8] is treacherous; the arrogant man will not rest.[^2-9] He opens his throat as wide as Sheol;[^2-10] like death, he is never satisfied. He gathers all the nations for himself and collects all the peoples for himself.

**6** Will not all of them take up a taunt[^2-11] against him, with mocking riddles about him, and say: "Woe to[^2-12] him who heaps up what is not his own (how long?) and loads himself with goods taken in pledge![^2-13]

**7** Will not your creditors[^2-14] suddenly rise up, and those who make you tremble wake up? Then you will become plunder for them.

**8** Because you have plundered many nations, all the rest of the peoples will plunder you, because of the blood of human beings and violence done to the earth, to the city and all who live in it.[^2-15]

**9** Woe to him who gets evil gain for his house, to set his nest on high and escape from the grasp of disaster!

**10** You have planned shame for your house by cutting off many peoples; you have sinned against your own life.[^2-16]

**11** For the stone will cry out from the wall, and the beam[^2-17] will answer it from the woodwork.

**12** Woe to him who builds a city with bloodshed and founds a town on injustice![^2-18]

**13** Is it not from Yahweh of Armies[^2-19] that peoples wear themselves out only for the fire, and nations exhaust themselves for nothing?[^2-20]

**14** For the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of Yahweh, as the waters cover the sea.[^2-21]

**15** Woe to him who makes his neighbors drink, pouring out your wrath[^2-22] and making them drunk, in order to gaze on their nakedness![^2-23]

**16** You will be filled with shame instead of glory. Drink, you too, and show your foreskin![^2-24] The cup in Yahweh's right hand will come around to you, and utter disgrace[^2-25] will cover your glory.

**17** For the violence done to Lebanon[^2-26] will overwhelm you, and the destruction of the animals will terrify you,[^2-27] because of the blood of human beings and violence done to the earth, to the city and all who live in it.

**18** What use is a carved image, once its maker has carved it, or a cast image, a teacher of lies? For its maker trusts in what he has made himself, when he makes idols that cannot speak.[^2-28]

**19** Woe to him who says to a piece of wood, 'Wake up!' and to a silent stone, 'Get up!' Can it teach? Look, it is overlaid with gold and silver, but there is no breath[^2-29] in it at all."

**20** But Yahweh is in his holy temple. Let all the earth be silent before him.[^2-30]

[^2-1]: Literally "what he will speak in me".
[^2-2]: Or "when I am rebuked".
[^2-3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2-4]: Perhaps "so that a herald may run with it", carrying the message, or "so that it can be read easily".
[^2-5]: Literally "it pants". Or "it testifies": the Hebrew word can also mean "witness".
[^2-6]: The meaning of the Hebrew word, *uppelah*, is uncertain. The Septuagint reads "If he shrinks back, my soul takes no pleasure in him".
[^2-7]: Hebrew *emunah*, "faithfulness, steadiness, firmness", from the same root as *amen*. It describes a person who holds firm and can be relied on, and here may mean steady trust in God while waiting for the vision. Or "by his faith". *His* may also refer to God or to the vision. The Septuagint reads "the righteous will live by my faithfulness", that is, God's. Paul quotes the line as "The righteous will live by faith" (Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11), and Hebrews 10:37–38 quotes verses 3–4 from the Septuagint, with the lines of verse 4 in the reverse order: "my righteous one will live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul takes no pleasure in him". Greek *pistis* means both "faith" and "faithfulness".
[^2-8]: The Habakkuk commentary found at Qumran (1QpHab) reads "wealth".
[^2-9]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Perhaps "will not reach his goal" or "will not stay at home".
[^2-10]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. *Throat* is Hebrew *nephesh*, here the appetite.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *mashal*, a saying or proverb, here a mocking song. It runs through verse 19, as five cries of "Woe to" (verses 6, 9, 12, 15 and 19).
[^2-12]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to".
[^2-13]: Hebrew *avtit*, found only here, from *avot*, "pledge": goods taken as security for loans. It sounds like *av tit*, "thick mud".
[^2-14]: Literally "those who bite you". The Hebrew word for interest on a loan, *neshekh*, also means "bite".
[^2-15]: Literally "to the earth, a city and all who live in it". Also in verse 17.
[^2-16]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "soul, self, life".
[^2-17]: Hebrew *kafis*, found only here, probably a rafter or beam. Compare Luke 19:40.
[^2-18]: Compare Micah 3:10.
[^2-19]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^2-20]: Nearly the same words are found in Jeremiah 51:58.
[^2-21]: Compare Isaiah 11:9.
[^2-22]: Hebrew *chemah*, "wrath" or "poison". Some scholars read "from your wineskin", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^2-23]: The Habakkuk commentary found at Qumran (1QpHab) reads "their festivals".
[^2-24]: Hebrew *he'arel*, "show yourself uncircumcised". The Habakkuk commentary found at Qumran (1QpHab), the Septuagint and the Syriac read "and stagger", a word with two of the same letters in the reverse order.
[^2-25]: Hebrew *qiqalon*, found only here, a stronger form of *qalon*, "shame", in the first line.
[^2-26]: The cedar forests of Lebanon were cut down by conquerors for their building projects (Isaiah 14:8; 37:24).
[^2-27]: Literally "will terrify them". The Hebrew form is unusual.
[^2-28]: A play on words: *elilim illemim*, "mute idols". *A teacher of lies* may refer to oracles sought from idols.
[^2-29]: Hebrew *ruach*, "breath, spirit, wind".
[^2-30]: Literally "Hush before him, all the earth!" Compare Zephaniah 1:7; Zechariah 2:17 (2:13 in most English Bibles).
