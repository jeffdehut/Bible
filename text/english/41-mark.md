## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The beginning of the good news about Jesus the Messiah.[^1-1]

**2** It is written in the prophet Isaiah:[^1-2] "Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you. He will prepare your way.

**3** A voice is shouting in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight.'"[^1-3]

**4** So John the Baptizer appeared in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance[^1-4] for the forgiveness of sins.

**5** The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem went out to him. They confessed their sins, and he baptized them in the Jordan River.

**6** John wore clothing made of camel's hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.

**7** He proclaimed, "After me comes someone more powerful than I am. I am not worthy to bend down and untie the strap of his sandals.

**8** I have baptized you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

**9** At that time Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.

**10** As soon as[^1-5] he came up out of the water, he saw the heavens torn open and the Spirit coming down on him like a dove.

**11** And a voice came from the heavens: "You are my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with you."[^1-6]

**12** At once the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness.

**13** He was in the wilderness forty days, being tested[^1-7] by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels took care of him.

**14** After John was arrested,[^1-8] Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the good news of God.

**15** He said, "The time has come, and the kingdom of God has come near. Repent, and believe the good news."

**16** As Jesus was going along beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Simon's brother Andrew casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**17** Jesus said to them, "Come, follow me, and I will make you fish for people."

**18** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**19** Going on a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in their boat, mending the nets.

**20** At once he called them. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and went after him.

**21** They went to Capernaum. As soon as the Sabbath came, he began teaching in the synagogue.[^1-9]

**22** The people were amazed at his teaching, because he taught them like someone with authority, not like the scribes.[^1-10]

**23** Just then, in their synagogue, there was a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

**24** "What do you want with us,[^1-11] Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God!"

**25** Jesus rebuked him: "Be quiet, and come out of him!"

**26** The unclean spirit threw the man into convulsions, shrieked with a loud voice, and came out of him.

**27** Everyone was so astonished that they asked one another, "What is this? A new teaching, and with authority! He even gives orders to the unclean spirits, and they obey him."[^1-12]

**28** At once the news about him spread everywhere, throughout the whole region around Galilee.

**29** As soon as they left the synagogue, they went with James and John to the house of Simon and Andrew.

**30** Simon's mother-in-law was lying in bed with a fever, and they told Jesus about her at once.

**31** He went to her, took her by the hand, and helped her up. The fever left her, and she began to serve them.

**32** That evening, after the sun had set, people brought to him all who were sick and those who were possessed by demons.

**33** The whole town gathered at the door.

**34** He healed many who were sick with various diseases and drove out many demons. But he would not let the demons speak, because they knew who he was.

**35** Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up and went out to a deserted place, and there he prayed.

**36** Simon and those with him went looking for him.

**37** When they found him, they said to him, "Everyone is looking for you."

**38** He answered them, "Let us go somewhere else, to the nearby towns, so that I can proclaim the message there too. That is why I came out."

**39** So he went throughout Galilee, proclaiming the message in their synagogues and driving out demons.

**40** A man with a skin disease[^1-13] came to him. Kneeling down, he begged him, "If you are willing, you can make me clean."

**41** Moved with anger,[^1-14] Jesus reached out his hand and touched him. "I am willing," he said. "Be made clean!"

**42** At once the skin disease left him, and he was made clean.

**43** Jesus sent him away at once with a stern warning:

**44** "See that you say nothing to anyone. But go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as evidence for them."[^1-15]

**45** But the man went out and began to talk about it freely, spreading the news, so that Jesus could no longer go into a town openly. He stayed out in deserted places, and people kept coming to him from everywhere.

[^1-1]: Greek *christos*, "anointed one". The SBLGNT and some early manuscripts end the verse here. Many manuscripts add "the Son of God".
[^1-2]: Many later manuscripts read "in the prophets". The quotation combines Malachi 3:1 (and Exodus 23:20) with Isaiah 40:3.
[^1-3]: Isaiah 40:3. In Isaiah's Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^1-4]: A baptism expressing a change of heart and life. Greek *metanoia*, a turning around of mind and life.
[^1-5]: Greek *euthys*, "immediately", one of Mark's favorite words. It is rendered "at once", "as soon as" or "just then", as the sentence requires.
[^1-6]: Or "You are my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in you."
[^1-7]: Or "tempted". The Greek word means both.
[^1-8]: Literally "handed over".
[^1-9]: Most manuscripts read "On the Sabbath he went into the synagogue and began to teach."
[^1-10]: Experts in the Law of Moses.
[^1-11]: Literally "What to us and to you?", an idiom: "What do we have to do with you?"
[^1-12]: Or "What is this? A new teaching with authority! He even gives orders …"
[^1-13]: Greek *lepros*, traditionally "leper". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy.
[^1-14]: The SBLGNT reads "becoming angry", with one early manuscript and some Latin manuscripts. Most manuscripts read "moved with compassion". Scholars debate which is original: the harder reading, "angry", may have been softened by copyists.
[^1-15]: Or "as a testimony against them".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** A few days later Jesus came back to Capernaum, and people heard that he was at home.

**2** So many gathered that there was no room left, not even outside the door, and he was speaking the message to them.

**3** Some people came, bringing to him a paralyzed man carried by four of them.

**4** Since they could not get him to Jesus because of the crowd, they removed the roof above where he was. After digging through it, they lowered the mat the paralyzed man was lying on.

**5** When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralyzed man, "Son,[^2-1] your sins are forgiven."

**6** Some of the scribes were sitting there, thinking to themselves,

**7** "Why does this man speak like that? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?"

**8** At once Jesus knew in his spirit that they were thinking this, and he said to them, "Why are you thinking these things in your hearts?

**9** Which is easier: to say to the paralyzed man, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up, pick up your mat, and walk'?

**10** But so that you may know that the Son of Man[^2-2] has authority on earth to forgive sins"—he said to the paralyzed man—

**11** "I tell you, get up, pick up your mat, and go home."

**12** The man got up, at once picked up the mat, and walked out in front of them all. Everyone was amazed and praised God, saying, "We have never seen anything like this!"

**13** Jesus went out again beside the sea. The whole crowd kept coming to him, and he taught them.

**14** As he walked along, he saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax booth. "Follow me," Jesus told him. And Levi got up and followed him.

**15** Later Jesus was having a meal in Levi's house,[^2-3] and many tax collectors and sinners were eating with Jesus and his disciples, for there were many of them, and they followed him.

**16** When the scribes who belonged to the Pharisees[^2-4] saw that he was eating with sinners and tax collectors, they asked his disciples, "Why does he eat with tax collectors and sinners?"[^2-5]

**17** Jesus heard this and said to them, "Healthy people don't need a doctor; sick people do. I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

**18** Now John's disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. Some people came and asked Jesus, "Why do John's disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, but your disciples don't?"

**19** Jesus said to them, "Can the wedding guests[^2-6] fast while the bridegroom is with them? As long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

**20** But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then, on that day, they will fast.

**21** "No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment. Otherwise the new patch pulls away from the old cloth, and the tear gets worse.

**22** And no one pours new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the wine will burst the skins, and both the wine and the skins are ruined. No, new wine goes into fresh wineskins."

**23** One Sabbath Jesus was going through the grainfields, and as they made their way, his disciples began picking heads of grain.

**24** The Pharisees said to him, "Look, why are they doing what is not allowed on the Sabbath?"

**25** He answered them, "Have you never read what David did when he and his companions were in need and hungry?

**26** In the time of Abiathar the high priest,[^2-7] he went into the house of God and ate the sacred bread,[^2-8] which only the priests are allowed to eat, and he also gave some to his companions."

**27** Then he said to them, "The Sabbath was made for people, not people for the Sabbath.

**28** So the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath."

[^2-1]: Literally "Child", an affectionate form of address.
[^2-2]: A title Jesus uses for himself in all four Gospels. It recalls "one like a son of man" in Daniel 7:13, and in Hebrew idiom it can also mean simply "a human being".
[^2-3]: Literally "in his house". The Greek may mean Levi's house or Jesus' house.
[^2-4]: Literally "the scribes of the Pharisees". Many manuscripts read "the scribes and the Pharisees".
[^2-5]: Many manuscripts add "and drink".
[^2-6]: Literally "the sons of the bridechamber".
[^2-7]: 1 Samuel 21:2–7 (21:1–6 in most English Bibles) names the priest as Ahimelech, the father of Abiathar (1 Samuel 22:20). Abiathar became the better-known high priest under David.
[^2-8]: Literally "the bread of the presentation", the twelve loaves set out before God in the sanctuary (Leviticus 24:5–9).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Another time Jesus went into a synagogue, and a man was there whose hand was withered.

**2** Some people were watching Jesus closely to see whether he would heal him on the Sabbath, so that they could accuse him.

**3** He said to the man with the withered hand, "Get up and stand in the middle."

**4** Then he asked them, "Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?" But they said nothing.

**5** He looked around at them with anger, deeply grieved at their stubborn hearts,[^3-1] and said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.

**6** The Pharisees went out and at once began plotting with the Herodians[^3-2] against Jesus, how they might destroy him.

**7** Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a large crowd from Galilee followed. People also came from Judea,

**8** from Jerusalem, from Idumea, from beyond the Jordan, and from around Tyre and Sidon. A great crowd came to him when they heard about all he was doing.

**9** He told his disciples to keep a small boat ready for him because of the crowd, so that they would not crush him.

**10** For he had healed many, so that all who had diseases[^3-3] were pushing forward to touch him.

**11** Whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell down before him and shouted, "You are the Son of God!"

**12** But he strictly ordered them not to make him known.

**13** Jesus went up the mountain and called to him those he wanted, and they came to him.

**14** He appointed twelve[^3-4] to be with him, so that he could send them out to proclaim the message

**15** and to have authority to drive out demons.

**16** So he appointed the Twelve:[^3-5] Simon, to whom he gave the name Peter;

**17** James son of Zebedee and John the brother of James, to whom he gave the name Boanerges, which means "Sons of Thunder";

**18** Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,[^3-6]

**19** and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

**20** Then Jesus went home,[^3-7] and again such a crowd gathered that they could not even eat.

**21** When his family[^3-8] heard about it, they went out to take charge of him, for they were saying, "He is out of his mind."

**22** And the scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, "He has Beelzebul,"[^3-9] and, "He drives out demons by the ruler of the demons."

**23** So Jesus called them over and spoke to them in parables: "How can Satan drive out Satan?

**24** If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.

**25** If a household is divided against itself, that household will not be able to stand.

**26** And if Satan has risen up against himself and is divided, he cannot stand; his end has come.

**27** But no one can go into a strong man's house and carry off his possessions without first tying up the strong man. Then he can plunder his house.

**28** "Truly I tell you, people[^3-10] will be forgiven everything: their sins and whatever blasphemies they speak.

**29** But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin."[^3-11]

**30** He said this because they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit."

**31** Then his mother and his brothers came. Standing outside, they sent word to him, calling for him.

**32** A crowd was sitting around him, and they told him, "Look, your mother and your brothers[^3-12] are outside looking for you."

**33** He answered them, "Who are my mother and my brothers?"

**34** Looking at those sitting in a circle around him, he said, "Here are my mother and my brothers!

**35** Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother."

[^3-1]: Literally "the hardness of their heart".
[^3-2]: Supporters of the family of Herod.
[^3-3]: Literally "scourges", a word for painful afflictions.
[^3-4]: Many manuscripts add "whom he also named apostles".
[^3-5]: Some manuscripts leave out "So he appointed the Twelve".
[^3-6]: From an Aramaic word meaning "zealous"; Luke calls him "Simon the Zealot" (Luke 6:15).
[^3-7]: Literally "into a house".
[^3-8]: Literally "those from him", that is, his relatives or associates.
[^3-9]: A name for the prince of demons. In the Hebrew Bible, Baal-zebub ("lord of flies") was a god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2).
[^3-10]: Literally "the sons of men".
[^3-11]: Most later manuscripts read "eternal judgment".
[^3-12]: Many manuscripts add "and your sisters".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Again Jesus began to teach beside the sea. Such a large crowd gathered around him that he got into a boat on the sea and sat down, while the whole crowd stayed on the shore at the water's edge.

**2** He taught them many things in parables, and in his teaching he said:

**3** "Listen! A farmer went out to sow.

**4** As he was sowing, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up.

**5** Other seed fell on rocky ground where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly because the soil was shallow.

**6** But when the sun rose, the plants were scorched, and because they had no roots, they withered.

**7** Other seed fell among thorns. The thorns grew up and choked it, and it produced no grain.

**8** Still other seed fell on good soil. It came up, grew, and produced grain, yielding thirty, sixty, and a hundred times as much."

**9** Then he said, "Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear."

**10** When he was alone, the people around him, together with the Twelve, asked him about the parables.

**11** He told them, "The secret[^4-1] of the kingdom of God has been given to you. But to those outside, everything comes in parables,

**12** so that 'they may look and look but not see, and listen and listen but not understand; otherwise they might turn and be forgiven.'"[^4-2]

**13** Then he said to them, "Don't you understand this parable? Then how will you understand any of the parables?

**14** The farmer sows the word.[^4-3]

**15** Some people are like the seed along the path, where the word is sown. As soon as they hear it, Satan comes and takes away the word that was sown in them.

**16** Others are like the seed sown on rocky ground. When they hear the word, they receive it at once with joy.

**17** But they have no root in themselves and last only a short time. When trouble or persecution comes because of the word, they quickly fall away.[^4-4]

**18** Others are like the seed sown among thorns. They hear the word,

**19** but the worries of this life,[^4-5] the deceitfulness of wealth, and the desire for other things come in and choke the word, and it produces nothing.

**20** And others are like the seed sown on good soil. They hear the word, accept it, and produce a crop: thirty, sixty, and a hundred times as much."

**21** He said to them, "Is a lamp brought in to be put under a basket[^4-6] or under a bed? Isn't it put on a lampstand?

**22** For nothing is hidden except to be revealed, and nothing is kept secret except to come out into the open.

**23** If anyone has ears to hear, let them hear."

**24** He also said to them, "Pay attention to what you hear. The measure you use will be used to measure you, and you will be given even more.

**25** For whoever has will be given more, but whoever does not have, even what they have will be taken away from them."

**26** He also said, "The kingdom of God is like this: a man scatters seed on the ground.

**27** He sleeps and gets up, night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows, though he does not know how.

**28** The ground produces grain by itself: first the stalk, then the head, then the full grain in the head.

**29** As soon as the grain is ripe, he puts in the sickle, because the harvest has come."

**30** He also said, "What shall we compare the kingdom of God to, or what parable shall we use for it?

**31** It is like a mustard seed. When it is sown in the ground, it is the smallest of all the seeds on earth.

**32** Yet once it is sown, it grows up and becomes the largest of all garden plants. It puts out such big branches that the birds of the sky can nest in its shade."

**33** With many parables like these he spoke the word to them, as much as they could understand.

**34** He did not speak to them without a parable, but when he was alone with his own disciples, he explained everything.

**35** That day, when evening came, he said to them, "Let us cross over to the other side."

**36** Leaving the crowd behind, they took him along in the boat, just as he was. Other boats were with him.

**37** A fierce windstorm came up, and the waves were breaking over the boat, so that it was already filling up.

**38** But Jesus was in the stern, asleep on a cushion. They woke him and said to him, "Teacher, don't you care that we are about to die?"

**39** He got up, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, "Quiet! Be still!" The wind died down, and there was a great calm.

**40** He said to them, "Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?"[^4-7]

**41** They were filled with great fear and said to one another, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

[^4-1]: Greek *mysterion*, something once hidden and now revealed.
[^4-2]: Isaiah 6:9–10.
[^4-3]: That is, God's message.
[^4-4]: Or "stumble"; Greek *skandalizo*.
[^4-5]: Literally "of the age".
[^4-6]: Greek *modios*, a container for measuring grain, holding about 8 liters (2 gallons).
[^4-7]: Most later manuscripts read "Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?"

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** They came to the other side of the sea, to the region of the Gerasenes.[^5-1]

**2** As soon as Jesus got out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit came from the tombs to meet him.

**3** He lived among the tombs, and no one could restrain him anymore, not even with a chain.

**4** He had often been bound with shackles and chains, but he tore the chains apart and broke the shackles in pieces. No one was strong enough to subdue him.

**5** Night and day, among the tombs and in the hills, he kept shrieking and cutting himself with stones.

**6** When he saw Jesus from a distance, he ran and knelt down before him.

**7** He shouted at the top of his voice, "What do you want with me,[^5-2] Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg you by God, do not torment me!"

**8** For Jesus had been saying to him, "Unclean spirit, come out of the man!"

**9** Jesus asked him, "What is your name?" He replied, "My name is Legion,[^5-3] for there are many of us."

**10** And he begged Jesus again and again not to send them out of the region.

**11** A large herd of pigs was feeding there on the hillside.

**12** The spirits begged him, "Send us into the pigs, so that we may go into them."

**13** He gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and went into the pigs, and the herd, about two thousand of them, rushed down the steep bank into the sea and drowned in the sea.

**14** The men who tended the pigs ran off and reported it in the town and in the countryside, and people came to see what had happened.

**15** They came to Jesus and saw the man who had been possessed by demons, the one who had had the legion, sitting there, dressed and in his right mind. And they were afraid.

**16** Those who had seen it told them what had happened to the demon-possessed man, and about the pigs.

**17** Then they began to beg Jesus to leave their region.

**18** As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man who had been possessed by demons begged to go with him.

**19** But Jesus did not let him. Instead he told him, "Go home to your own people, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you, and how he has had mercy on you."

**20** So the man went away and began to proclaim in the Decapolis[^5-4] how much Jesus had done for him. And everyone was amazed.

**21** When Jesus had crossed again in the boat to the other side, a large crowd gathered around him, and he stayed beside the sea.

**22** One of the leaders of the synagogue, named Jairus, came, and when he saw Jesus, he fell at his feet.

**23** He pleaded earnestly with him, "My little daughter is dying. Please come and put your hands on her, so that she will be healed and live."

**24** So Jesus went with him. A large crowd followed him, pressing in on him from all sides.

**25** A woman was there who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years.

**26** She had endured a great deal under many doctors and had spent all she had. Yet instead of getting better, she had grown worse.

**27** She had heard about Jesus, so she came up behind him in the crowd and touched his cloak.

**28** For she said, "If I can just touch his clothes, I will be healed."

**29** At once her bleeding stopped,[^5-5] and she felt in her body that she was healed of her affliction.

**30** At once Jesus realized that power had gone out from him. He turned around in the crowd and asked, "Who touched my clothes?"

**31** His disciples said to him, "You see the crowd pressing in on you, and you ask, 'Who touched me?'"

**32** But he kept looking around to see who had done it.

**33** Then the woman, frightened and trembling because she knew what had happened to her, came and fell down before him and told him the whole truth.

**34** He said to her, "Daughter, your faith has healed you.[^5-6] Go in peace, and be free of your affliction."

**35** While he was still speaking, people came from the house of the synagogue leader and said, "Your daughter has died. Why trouble the teacher any further?"

**36** But Jesus overheard[^5-7] what they said and told the synagogue leader, "Don't be afraid; just believe."

**37** He did not let anyone follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James.

**38** They came to the synagogue leader's house, and Jesus saw a commotion, with people weeping and wailing loudly.

**39** He went in and said to them, "Why all this commotion and weeping? The child is not dead but asleep."

**40** They laughed at him. But he put them all outside, took the child's father and mother and those who were with him, and went in where the child was.

**41** He took the child by the hand and said to her, "Talitha koum," which means, "Little girl, I tell you, get up!"

**42** At once the girl got up and began to walk around. She was twelve years old. At this they were completely overwhelmed with amazement.

**43** He gave them strict orders not to let anyone know about this, and he told them to give her something to eat.

[^5-1]: Many later manuscripts read "Gadarenes".
[^5-2]: Literally "What to me and to you?"; see 1:24.
[^5-3]: A Roman legion was a unit of several thousand soldiers.
[^5-4]: "The Ten Towns", a group of Greek cities east of the Jordan.
[^5-5]: Literally "the source of her blood dried up".
[^5-6]: Or "has saved you". The Greek verb *sozo* means both.
[^5-7]: Or "ignored". The Greek verb can mean either.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Jesus left there and went to his hometown, and his disciples followed him.

**2** When the Sabbath came, he began to teach in the synagogue. Many who heard him were astonished. "Where did this man get these things?" they asked. "What is this wisdom that has been given to him? And these mighty works done by his hands!

**3** Isn't this the carpenter,[^6-1] the son of Mary and the brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? Aren't his sisters here with us?" And they took offense at him.

**4** Jesus said to them, "A prophet is honored everywhere except in his hometown, among his relatives, and in his own household."

**5** He could not do any mighty work there, except that he laid his hands on a few sick people and healed them.

**6** He was amazed at their unbelief. Then he went around the surrounding villages, teaching.

**7** He called the Twelve to him and began to send them out two by two, and he gave them authority over the unclean spirits.

**8** He instructed them to take nothing for the journey except a walking stick: no bread, no bag, no money in their belts.

**9** They were to wear sandals, but not to put on two tunics.

**10** He told them, "Whenever you enter a house, stay there until you leave that place.

**11** If any place will not welcome you or listen to you, shake the dust off your feet as you leave, as a testimony against them."[^6-2]

**12** So they went out and proclaimed that people should repent.

**13** They drove out many demons, anointed many sick people with oil, and healed them.

**14** King Herod[^6-3] heard about this, for Jesus' name had become well known. People were saying,[^6-4] "John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead! That is why these powers are at work in him."

**15** Others said, "He is Elijah." Still others said, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets of long ago."

**16** But when Herod heard of it, he said, "John, whom I beheaded, has been raised."

**17** For Herod himself had sent men to arrest John and had him bound in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, whom Herod had married.

**18** John had been telling Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife."

**19** So Herodias held a grudge against John and wanted to kill him, but she could not,

**20** because Herod feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man, and he protected him. When he heard John, he was greatly perplexed,[^6-5] yet he liked to listen to him.

**21** An opportunity came on Herod's birthday, when he gave a banquet for his high officials, his military commanders, and the leading men of Galilee.

**22** His daughter Herodias[^6-6] came in and danced, and she pleased Herod and his guests. The king said to the girl, "Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you."

**23** He swore an oath to her: "Whatever you ask me, I will give you, up to half my kingdom."

**24** She went out and said to her mother, "What should I ask for?" Her mother answered, "The head of John the Baptizer."

**25** At once the girl hurried back to the king and made her request: "I want you to give me the head of John the Baptist on a platter, right now."

**26** The king was deeply distressed, but because of his oaths and his guests, he did not want to refuse her.

**27** So the king at once sent an executioner with orders to bring John's head. The man went and beheaded John in the prison,

**28** brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother.

**29** When John's disciples heard about it, they came, took his body, and laid it in a tomb.

**30** The apostles gathered around Jesus and told him all they had done and taught.

**31** He said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while." For so many people were coming and going that they had no chance even to eat.

**32** So they went away in the boat by themselves to a deserted place.

**33** But many people saw them leaving and recognized them. They ran there on foot from all the towns and arrived ahead of them.

**34** When Jesus got out of the boat, he saw a large crowd, and he had compassion on them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. So he began to teach them many things.

**35** When it was already late, his disciples came to him and said, "This is a deserted place, and it is already very late.

**36** Send the people away so that they can go to the surrounding farms and villages and buy themselves something to eat."

**37** But he answered them, "You give them something to eat." They said to him, "Should we go and spend two hundred denarii[^6-7] on bread and give it to them to eat?"

**38** He asked them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see." When they found out, they said, "Five, and two fish."

**39** Then he directed them to have all the people sit down in groups on the green grass.

**40** So they sat down in groups, by hundreds and by fifties.

**41** He took the five loaves and the two fish, looked up to heaven, and blessed them.[^6-8] Then he broke the loaves and kept giving them to his disciples to set before the people, and he divided the two fish among them all.

**42** They all ate and were satisfied.

**43** And they picked up twelve baskets full of broken pieces of bread and fish.

**44** Those who had eaten the loaves numbered five thousand men.

**45** At once Jesus made his disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side, to Bethsaida, while he sent the crowd away.

**46** After saying goodbye to them, he went up on the mountain to pray.

**47** When evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on land.

**48** He saw that they were straining at the oars, because the wind was against them. Shortly before dawn[^6-9] he came toward them, walking on the sea. He intended to pass by them.

**49** When they saw him walking on the sea, they thought he was a ghost, and they cried out,

**50** because they all saw him and were terrified. But at once he spoke to them: "Take courage! It is I.[^6-10] Don't be afraid."

**51** Then he climbed into the boat with them, and the wind died down. They were completely astounded,

**52** because they had not understood about the loaves. Their hearts were hardened.

**53** When they had crossed over, they came to land at Gennesaret and anchored there.

**54** As soon as they got out of the boat, people recognized Jesus.

**55** They ran throughout that whole region and began to carry the sick on mats to wherever they heard he was.

**56** Wherever he went, into villages, towns, or the countryside, they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him to let them touch even the edge of his cloak. And all who touched it were healed.[^6-11]

[^6-1]: Greek *tekton*, a builder or craftsman who worked in wood or stone.
[^6-2]: Many later manuscripts add "Truly I tell you, it will be more bearable for Sodom and Gomorrah on the day of judgment than for that town."
[^6-3]: Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee. He was not formally a king.
[^6-4]: Many manuscripts read "He was saying".
[^6-5]: Many manuscripts read "he did many things".
[^6-6]: Many manuscripts read "the daughter of Herodias herself". The historian Josephus names Herodias's daughter Salome.
[^6-7]: A denarius was a day's wage for a laborer, so this was about eight months' wages.
[^6-8]: Or "gave thanks".
[^6-9]: Literally "about the fourth watch of the night", between 3 and 6 a.m.
[^6-10]: Literally "I am". The same words are used of God in the Greek Old Testament (Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10).
[^6-11]: Or "were saved".
