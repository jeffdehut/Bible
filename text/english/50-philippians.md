## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul and Timothy, servants[^1-1] of Christ Jesus,[^1-2] to all God's holy people[^1-3] in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, together with the overseers[^1-4] and deacons:[^1-5]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** I thank my God every time I remember you,[^1-6]

**4** always in every prayer of mine for all of you, praying with joy,

**5** because of your partnership[^1-7] in the good news from the first day until now.

**6** I am confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.

**7** It is right for me to think this way about all of you, because I have you in my heart,[^1-8] since both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the good news you all share with me in grace.

**8** For God is my witness how I long for all of you with the affection[^1-9] of Christ Jesus.

**9** And this is my prayer: that your love may overflow more and more in knowledge and all insight,

**10** so that you may approve what is excellent,[^1-10] and may be pure and blameless for the day of Christ,

**11** filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

**12** I want you to know, brothers and sisters,[^1-11] that what has happened to me has actually served to advance the good news,

**13** so that it has become clear throughout the whole praetorian guard[^1-12] and to everyone else that I am in chains for Christ.[^1-13]

**14** And most of the brothers and sisters, made confident in the Lord by my chains,[^1-14] are much more bold to speak the word[^1-15] without fear.

**15** Some, it is true, proclaim Christ even from envy and rivalry, but others from goodwill.

**16** These do it out of love, knowing that I am put here for the defense of the good news.[^1-16]

**17** The others proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition,[^1-17] not sincerely, thinking they will stir up trouble for me in my chains.

**18** What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in this I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice,

**19** for I know that through your prayers and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance,[^1-18]

**20** according to my eager expectation and hope that I will not be put to shame in anything, but that with all boldness, now as always, Christ will be exalted in my body, whether by life or by death.

**21** For to me, to live is Christ and to die is gain.

**22** But if I am to live in the flesh,[^1-19] that means fruitful work for me. Yet which I will choose I cannot say.[^1-20]

**23** I am torn between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better,

**24** but to remain in the flesh is more necessary for your sake.

**25** Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with all of you for your progress and joy in the faith,

**26** so that your boasting in Christ Jesus may overflow because of me, through my coming[^1-21] to you again.

**27** Only, live as citizens[^1-22] worthy of the good news of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear about you that you are standing firm in one spirit, with one mind[^1-23] striving side by side for the faith of the good news,

**28** and that you are not frightened in any way by your opponents. (This is a sign to them of destruction, but of your salvation, and that from God.

**29** For it has been granted to you, for the sake of Christ, not only to believe in him but also to suffer for him.)

**30** You are engaged in the same struggle that you saw I had and now hear that I have.

[^1-1]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^1-2]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-3]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^1-4]: Greek *episkopoi*, "overseers", traditionally "bishops".
[^1-5]: Greek *diakonoi*, "servants", here used of people holding a recognized role in the church; traditionally "deacons".
[^1-6]: Or "for all your remembrance of me". Literally "at all the remembrance of you".
[^1-7]: Greek *koinonia*, "sharing, partnership, fellowship".
[^1-8]: Or "because you have me in your heart".
[^1-9]: Literally "with the inward parts", the seat of the deepest feelings.
[^1-10]: Or "discern what matters". Literally "test the things that differ".
[^1-11]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 14.
[^1-12]: Greek *praitorion*, the emperor's guard, or the headquarters of a governor.
[^1-13]: Literally "that my chains have become evident in Christ".
[^1-14]: Or "most of the brothers and sisters in the Lord, made confident by my chains".
[^1-15]: Some manuscripts read "the word of God".
[^1-16]: Many manuscripts have verses 16 and 17 in the reverse order.
[^1-17]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^1-18]: Or "salvation". The words "this will turn out for my deliverance" are the same as Job 13:16 in the Septuagint.
[^1-19]: That is, in the body; also in verse 24.
[^1-20]: The Greek is abrupt; literally "But if to live in the flesh, this for me is fruit of work; and what I will choose I do not make known."
[^1-21]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival".
[^1-22]: Greek *politeuomai*, "to live as a citizen". Philippi was a Roman colony, and many of its people were Roman citizens.
[^1-23]: Literally "with one soul". *In one spirit*: or "in one Spirit".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** So if there is any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love, any sharing in the Spirit,[^2-1] any affection and compassion,

**2** make my joy complete by being of the same mind, having the same love, being one in spirit[^2-2] and of one mind.

**3** Do nothing out of selfish ambition[^2-3] or conceit,[^2-4] but in humility regard others as more important than yourselves,

**4** each of you looking not only to your own interests but also to the interests of others.[^2-5]

**5** Have this attitude among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus:[^2-6]

**6** who, being in the form[^2-7] of God,[^2-8] did not regard equality with God as something to be grasped,[^2-9]

**7** but emptied himself,[^2-10] taking the form of a servant,[^2-11] being made in human likeness. And being found in appearance as a human being,

**8** he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross.

**9** Therefore God also highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name,

**10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven and on earth and under the earth,

**11** and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord,[^2-12] to the glory of God the Father.

**12** So then, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed, not only as in my presence[^2-13] but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,

**13** for it is God who is at work in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.[^2-14]

**14** Do everything without grumbling or arguing,

**15** so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation,[^2-15] among whom you shine like lights[^2-16] in the world,

**16** holding fast[^2-17] the word of life, so that I may have reason to boast on the day of Christ that I did not run in vain or labor in vain.

**17** But even if I am being poured out like a drink offering on the sacrifice and service[^2-18] of your faith, I rejoice, and I rejoice with all of you.

**18** In the same way you too should rejoice, and rejoice with me.

**19** I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be cheered when I learn how you are.

**20** For I have no one else like him,[^2-19] who will genuinely care about your concerns.

**21** For they all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.

**22** But you know his proven worth, how as a child with a father he has served[^2-20] with me in the good news.

**23** So I hope to send him as soon as I see how things go with me,

**24** and I am confident in the Lord that I myself will also come soon.

**25** But I have thought it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier, and your messenger[^2-21] and minister[^2-22] to my need,

**26** for he has been longing for all of you,[^2-23] and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill.

**27** Indeed he was ill, close to death. But God had mercy on him, and not only on him but on me also, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.

**28** So I am sending[^2-24] him all the more eagerly, so that when you see him again you may rejoice, and I may be less anxious.

**29** So welcome him in the Lord with all joy, and hold people like him in honor,

**30** because he came close to death for the work of Christ,[^2-25] risking his life to make up for what was lacking in your service to me.[^2-26]

[^2-1]: Greek *koinonia*, "sharing, fellowship". Or "fellowship with the Spirit", or "any common spirit".
[^2-2]: Greek *sympsychoi*, "one in soul".
[^2-3]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^2-4]: Literally "empty glory".
[^2-5]: Literally "not looking each to your own things, but also each to the things of others".
[^2-6]: The second half of the verse has no verb. Or "which you also have in Christ Jesus", that is, the attitude that belongs to those who are in Christ. Many manuscripts read "For let this attitude be in you which was also in Christ Jesus".
[^2-7]: Greek *morphe*, "form", the outward shape in which something appears; also in verse 7 ("the form of a servant"). How far the word also implies the nature of what appears is debated.
[^2-8]: Or "though he was in the form of God", or "because he was in the form of God". The Greek participle allows either.
[^2-9]: Greek *harpagmos*, a rare word, from *harpazo*, "to seize". It may mean something to be seized, which he did not yet have, or something already his, to be held on to or used for his own advantage.
[^2-10]: Greek *ekenosen*, from *kenoo*, "to empty", also "to make of no account". The verse does not say of what he emptied himself; the phrases that follow describe what it involved.
[^2-11]: Or "a slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^2-12]: Verses 10–11 echo Isaiah 45:23, where Yahweh, God's personal name, says, "To me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear allegiance." The Septuagint has "every tongue will confess to God". *Lord*: Greek *kyrios*, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the name Yahweh.
[^2-13]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", as in 1:26.
[^2-14]: Literally "for the good pleasure".
[^2-15]: Compare Deuteronomy 32:5.
[^2-16]: Or "stars". Compare Daniel 12:3.
[^2-17]: Or "holding out", that is, offering.
[^2-18]: Greek *leitourgia*, a public or priestly service; compare *leitourgos*, "minister", in verse 25, and verse 30.
[^2-19]: Greek *isopsychos*, "of equal soul, like-minded".
[^2-20]: Greek *douleuo*, "serve as a slave".
[^2-21]: Greek *apostolos*, "one who is sent", the word translated "apostle" elsewhere.
[^2-22]: Greek *leitourgos*, one who performs a public or priestly service.
[^2-23]: Some manuscripts read "longing to see all of you".
[^2-24]: Literally "I sent", written from the point of view of the readers, who will receive the letter when Epaphroditus arrives.
[^2-25]: Some manuscripts read "the work of the Lord".
[^2-26]: Literally "to fill up your lack of service toward me". Greek *leitourgia*, as in verse 17.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Finally,[^3-1] my brothers and sisters,[^3-2] rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you is no trouble for me, and it is a safeguard for you.

**2** Watch out for the dogs, watch out for the evil workers, watch out for the mutilation.[^3-3]

**3** For we are the circumcision, who worship by the Spirit of God and boast in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh,[^3-4]

**4** though I myself have reason for confidence in the flesh too. If anyone else thinks they have reason for confidence in the flesh, I have more:

**5** circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew born of Hebrews;[^3-5] as to the Law, a Pharisee;

**6** as to zeal, a persecutor of the church; as to the righteousness that is in the Law, blameless.

**7** But whatever gains I had, these I have come to regard as loss because of Christ.

**8** More than that, I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things, and I regard them as rubbish,[^3-6] so that I may gain Christ

**9** and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the Law, but the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ,[^3-7] the righteousness from God that depends on faith,

**10** that I may know him and the power of his resurrection and the sharing of his sufferings, being conformed to his death,

**11** if somehow I may attain to the resurrection from the dead.

**12** Not that I have already obtained it or have already been made perfect,[^3-8] but I press on to take hold of it, because Christ[^3-9] has taken hold of me.[^3-10]

**13** Brothers and sisters, I do not[^3-11] consider that I have taken hold of it myself. But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead,

**14** I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call[^3-12] of God in Christ Jesus.

**15** So let those of us who are mature[^3-13] think this way. And if you think differently about anything, God will reveal that to you too.

**16** Only let us keep in step with what we have already reached.[^3-14]

**17** Brothers and sisters, join in imitating me, and watch those who live[^3-15] as we do, since you have us as an example.

**18** For many live, as I have often told you and now tell you even with tears, as enemies of the cross of Christ.

**19** Their end is destruction, their god is their belly,[^3-16] and their glory is in their shame, with their minds set on earthly things.

**20** For our citizenship[^3-17] is in heaven, and from there we eagerly await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ,

**21** who will transform our lowly body[^3-18] to be like his glorious body, by the working of the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself.

[^3-1]: Or "Furthermore". Greek *to loipon*, "as for the rest".
[^3-2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 13 and 17.
[^3-3]: Greek *katatome*, "cutting up, mutilation", a play on *peritome*, "circumcision", in verse 3.
[^3-4]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh": here what is merely human and outward, such as the physical rite and the descent listed in verses 5–6; also in verse 4. Compare "the flesh" in Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God.
[^3-5]: Literally "a Hebrew from Hebrews".
[^3-6]: Greek *skybala*, "refuse, dung".
[^3-7]: Or "through the faithfulness of Christ". Literally "through faith of Christ".
[^3-8]: Or "have already reached the goal". Greek *teleioo*, "complete, make perfect".
[^3-9]: The SBLGNT here follows Tregelles; other editions read "Christ Jesus".
[^3-10]: Or "to take hold of that for which Christ took hold of me".
[^3-11]: Some manuscripts read "not yet".
[^3-12]: Or "heavenly call". Literally "the call above".
[^3-13]: Greek *teleioi*, "mature, complete", from the same root as "made perfect" in verse 12.
[^3-14]: Literally "Only, to what we have attained, keep in line by the same." Many manuscripts add "rule; let us think the same thing".
[^3-15]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life; also in verse 18.
[^3-16]: Or "stomach", that is, their own appetites.
[^3-17]: Greek *politeuma*, "commonwealth, state, citizenship", related to the verb in 1:27.
[^3-18]: Literally "the body of our humiliation". *His glorious body*: literally "the body of his glory".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Therefore, my brothers and sisters,[^4-1] whom I love and long for, my joy and crown, stand firm in the Lord in this way, my beloved.

**2** I urge Euodia and I urge Syntyche to agree with each other[^4-2] in the Lord.

**3** Yes, and I ask you too, true companion,[^4-3] help these women, who have struggled together with me in the good news, along with Clement and the rest of my fellow workers, whose names are in the book of life.

**4** Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice.

**5** Let your gentleness[^4-4] be known to everyone. The Lord is near.[^4-5]

**6** Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.

**7** And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

**8** Finally, brothers and sisters, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence and if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things.

**9** What you have learned and received and heard and seen in me, practice these things. And the God of peace will be with you.

**10** I rejoiced greatly in the Lord that now at last you have revived your concern for me.[^4-6] You were indeed concerned, but you had no opportunity.

**11** Not that I am speaking out of need, for I have learned to be content[^4-7] in whatever circumstances I am.

**12** I know how to be brought low, and I know how to have plenty. In any and every circumstance I have learned the secret[^4-8] of being well fed and of going hungry, of having plenty and of being in need.

**13** I can do all things through him who strengthens me.[^4-9]

**14** Yet you did well to share with me in my trouble.

**15** And you Philippians know yourselves that in the beginning of the good news, when I left Macedonia, no church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving[^4-10] except you only,

**16** for even in Thessalonica you sent help for my need more than once.[^4-11]

**17** Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that increases to your account.

**18** I have received full payment, and more. I am well supplied, now that I have received from Epaphroditus what you sent, a pleasing aroma,[^4-12] an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.

**19** And my God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.

**20** To our God and Father be the glory forever and ever.[^4-13] Amen.

**21** Greet every one of God's holy people[^4-14] in Christ Jesus. The brothers who are with me greet you.[^4-15]

**22** All God's holy people greet you, especially those of Caesar's household.[^4-16]

**23** The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.[^4-17]

[^4-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 8.
[^4-2]: Literally "to think the same thing".
[^4-3]: Greek *syzygos*, "yokefellow, partner". It may also be a personal name: "true Syzygus".
[^4-4]: Or "forbearance", "reasonableness".
[^4-5]: Or "The Lord is close at hand".
[^4-6]: Literally "you have blossomed again in thinking of me".
[^4-7]: Greek *autarkes*, "self-sufficient", a word the Stoic philosophers used for a person not ruled by circumstances.
[^4-8]: Greek *myeo*, "initiate", a word used of initiation into the mystery religions.
[^4-9]: Many manuscripts read "through Christ who strengthens me".
[^4-10]: Literally "in an account of giving and receiving", the language of business; also "account" in verse 17 and "received full payment" in verse 18.
[^4-11]: Literally "both once and twice".
[^4-12]: Literally "a smell of fragrance", the language of the sacrifices in the Old Testament (Exodus 29:18; Leviticus 1:9).
[^4-13]: Literally "to the ages of the ages".
[^4-14]: Literally "every holy one", traditionally "every saint"; also "God's holy people" in verse 22.
[^4-15]: Greek *adelphoi*, here Paul's companions; the word can include women.
[^4-16]: That is, people in the emperor's service, whether slaves or freed slaves.
[^4-17]: Many manuscripts read "be with you all. Amen."
