## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** In the days when the judges ruled,[^1-1] there was a famine in the land. A man from Bethlehem in Judah went to live for a while[^1-2] in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons.

**2** The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites[^1-3] from Bethlehem in Judah. They came to the country of Moab and stayed there.

**3** Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.

**4** They married Moabite women. One was named Orpah and the other Ruth. They lived there about ten years.

**5** Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died, and the woman was left without her two boys and without her husband.

**6** So she got up with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that the LORD[^1-4] had come to the help of his people and given them food.[^1-5]

**7** She set out from the place where she had been living, and her two daughters-in-law went with her. They went along the road to return to the land of Judah.

**8** Then Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go back, each of you, to your mother's house. May the LORD show you faithful love,[^1-6] as you have shown it to the dead and to me.

**9** May the LORD grant that each of you find rest[^1-7] in the home of a husband." Then she kissed them, and they wept aloud.[^1-8]

**10** They said to her, "No, we will go back with you to your people."

**11** But Naomi said, "Go back, my daughters. Why would you go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb who could become your husbands?[^1-9]

**12** Go back, my daughters, go, for I am too old to have a husband. Even if I said, 'There is hope for me,' even if I had a husband tonight and then gave birth to sons,

**13** would you wait for them until they grew up? Would you keep yourselves from marrying for them? No, my daughters. It is far more bitter for me than for you,[^1-10] for the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."

**14** Then they wept aloud again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye, but Ruth clung[^1-11] to her.

**15** Naomi said, "Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods.[^1-12] Go back with your sister-in-law."

**16** But Ruth said, "Don't urge me to leave you or to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go, and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God.

**17** Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do this to me, and more besides, if anything but death separates me from you."[^1-13]

**18** When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she said no more to her.

**19** So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they came to Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them, and the women said, "Can this be Naomi?"

**20** She said to them, "Don't call me Naomi.[^1-14] Call me Mara, for the Almighty[^1-15] has made my life very bitter.

**21** I went away full, but the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when the LORD has testified against me[^1-16] and the Almighty has brought disaster on me?"

**22** So Naomi came back, and with her Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, who came back[^1-17] from the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.[^1-18]

[^1-1]: Literally "In the days of the judging of the judges". On the judges, see Judges 2:16.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *gur*: to live as a foreigner in a land not one's own.
[^1-3]: Ephrath or Ephrathah was another name for Bethlehem or its district (4:11; Genesis 35:19).
[^1-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-5]: Literally "bread". *Bethlehem* means "house of bread".
[^1-6]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness, especially within a relationship of commitment. It is a key word in Ruth (2:20; 3:10). The Hebrew text is written "The LORD will show you faithful love" but read "May the LORD show you faithful love".
[^1-7]: That is, security and a settled home. Compare 3:1.
[^1-8]: Literally "they lifted up their voice and wept"; also in verse 14.
[^1-9]: Naomi alludes to the custom by which a brother of a man who died childless married his widow (Deuteronomy 25:5–10; compare Genesis 38:8).
[^1-10]: Or "It is very bitter for me because of you".
[^1-11]: Hebrew *davaq*, the verb used in Genesis 2:24, "holds fast to his wife".
[^1-12]: Or "her god".
[^1-13]: A form of oath. Literally "Thus may the LORD do to me, and thus may he add: death will separate me and you." Or "… if even death separates me from you."
[^1-14]: *Naomi* means "pleasant", and *Mara* means "bitter".
[^1-15]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty". The meaning of the name is uncertain. Also in verse 21.
[^1-16]: The Hebrew vowel points read "has testified against me". The same consonants can be read "has afflicted me", as the Septuagint understood them.
[^1-17]: Literally "who returned".
[^1-18]: In the spring, around April.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Naomi had a relative[^2-1] on her husband's side, a man of standing[^2-2] from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz.

**2** Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, "Let me go to the fields and glean[^2-3] among the ears of grain behind anyone who will be kind to me."[^2-4] Naomi said to her, "Go, my daughter."

**3** So she went and came and gleaned in the field behind the reapers. As it happened, she came to the part of the field that belonged to Boaz, who was from the clan of Elimelech.

**4** Just then Boaz came from Bethlehem. He said to the reapers, "The LORD[^2-5] be with you!" They said to him, "The LORD bless you!"

**5** Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?"

**6** The servant in charge of the reapers answered, "She is the young Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab.

**7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves behind the reapers.' So she came, and she has been on her feet from early morning until now, except for a short rest in the shelter."[^2-6]

**8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen to me, my daughter.[^2-7] Don't go to glean in another field, and don't leave this one. Stay close[^2-8] to my young women here.

**9** Keep your eyes on the field they are reaping, and follow after them. I have ordered the young men not to touch you. And when you are thirsty, go to the jars and drink from what the young men have drawn."

**10** Then she fell on her face, bowing to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor with you, that you take notice of me, when I am a foreigner?"[^2-9]

**11** Boaz answered her, "I have been told everything you have done for your mother-in-law since your husband died: how you left your father and your mother and the land of your birth, and came to a people you did not know before.[^2-10]

**12** May the LORD repay you for what you have done. May you receive a full reward from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

**13** She said, "May I go on finding favor with you, my lord,[^2-11] for you have comforted me and spoken kindly[^2-12] to your servant, though I am not even like one of your servants."

**14** At mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come over here and eat some of the bread, and dip your piece in the vinegar."[^2-13] So she sat down beside the reapers, and he handed her roasted grain. She ate until she was satisfied, and she had some left over.

**15** When she got up to glean, Boaz ordered his young men, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and don't humiliate her.

**16** And pull out some stalks for her from the bundles and leave them for her to glean. Don't rebuke her."

**17** So she gleaned in the field until evening. Then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it came to about an ephah[^2-14] of barley.

**18** She picked it up and went into the town, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. Then Ruth brought out and gave her what she had left over after she had eaten her fill.

**19** Her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you glean today? Where did you work? May the man who took notice of you be blessed!" So she told her mother-in-law about the man she had worked with. She said, "The name of the man I worked with today is Boaz."

**20** Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May he be blessed by the LORD, who has not stopped showing his faithful love[^2-15] to the living and the dead!" Naomi also said to her, "The man is a close relative of ours. He is one of our guardian-redeemers."[^2-16]

**21** Ruth the Moabite said, "He even said to me, 'Stay close to my young men until they have finished all my harvest.'"

**22** Naomi said to Ruth her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, so that no one will harm you in another field."

**23** So she stayed close to the young women of Boaz to glean until the end of the barley harvest and the wheat harvest.[^2-17] And she lived with her mother-in-law.

[^2-1]: The Hebrew text is written "an acquaintance" but read "a relative".
[^2-2]: Hebrew *gibbor chayil*, "a mighty man of strength", here a man of wealth and good standing. Compare 3:11, where Ruth is called "a woman of *chayil*".
[^2-3]: To gather the grain the harvesters left behind. The law of Moses gave the poor, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow the right to glean (Leviticus 19:9–10; Deuteronomy 24:19).
[^2-4]: Literally "after one in whose eyes I find favor".
[^2-5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-6]: The Hebrew of the end of this verse is uncertain. Literally "this her sitting the house a little".
[^2-7]: Literally "Have you not heard, my daughter?"
[^2-8]: Hebrew *davaq*, "cling, hold fast", the verb used in 1:14. Also in verses 21 and 23.
[^2-9]: In Hebrew, "take notice of" (*hakkir*) and "foreigner" (*nokhriyyah*) sound alike.
[^2-10]: Literally "yesterday and the day before".
[^2-11]: Or "You are very kind to me, my lord". Literally "Let me find favor in your eyes, my lord."
[^2-12]: Literally "spoken to the heart of".
[^2-13]: Or "sour wine", a drink in which bread was dipped.
[^2-14]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel).
[^2-15]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness. See 1:8. The Hebrew can also mean that Boaz is the one "who has not stopped showing his faithful love".
[^2-16]: Hebrew *go'el*, traditionally "kinsman-redeemer". A *go'el* was a close relative with the duty to protect the interests of a family member in need: to buy back land a relative had been forced to sell (Leviticus 25:25), to buy back a relative who had sold himself into slavery (Leviticus 25:47–49), and to avenge a relative who had been killed (Numbers 35:19). In Ruth the right to buy back Elimelech's land goes together with marriage to Ruth, to keep the dead man's name alive (4:5, 10; compare Deuteronomy 25:5–10).
[^2-17]: From about April to June.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, shouldn't I look for a home for you,[^3-1] where you will be well provided for?

**2** Now, isn't Boaz, whose young women you have been with, a relative of ours? Tonight he will be winnowing barley at the threshing floor.[^3-2]

**3** So wash, put on perfume, and get dressed,[^3-3] and go down[^3-4] to the threshing floor. But don't let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.

**4** When he lies down, note the place where he lies. Then go in, uncover his feet,[^3-5] and lie down.[^3-6] He will tell you what to do."

**5** She said to her, "I will do everything you tell me."[^3-7]

**6** So she went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed her.

**7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk and was in good spirits,[^3-8] he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then she came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.

**8** In the middle of the night the man was startled and turned over,[^3-9] and there was a woman lying at his feet!

**9** He said, "Who are you?" She said, "I am Ruth, your servant. Spread the corner of your garment[^3-10] over your servant, for you are a guardian-redeemer."[^3-11]

**10** He said, "May you be blessed by the LORD,[^3-12] my daughter. This last act of faithful love[^3-13] is greater than the first, for you have not gone after the young men, whether poor or rich.

**11** Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I will do for you everything you ask, for everyone in the town[^3-14] knows that you are a woman of worth.[^3-15]

**12** It is true that I am a guardian-redeemer,[^3-16] but there is another guardian-redeemer more closely related than I am.

**13** Stay here tonight. In the morning, if he will act as your guardian-redeemer, good; let him do it. But if he is not willing to act as your guardian-redeemer, then, as surely as the LORD lives, I will do it myself. Lie down until morning."

**14** So she lay at his feet until morning, but she got up before anyone could recognize anyone else. He said, "It must not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**15** He said, "Bring the shawl you are wearing and hold it out." She held it, and he measured out six measures of barley[^3-17] and put it on her. Then he went into the town.

**16** When she came to her mother-in-law, Naomi said, "How did it go,[^3-18] my daughter?" Then she told her everything the man had done for her.

**17** She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said to me,[^3-19] 'Don't go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.'"

**18** Naomi said, "Wait here, my daughter, until you find out how the matter turns out. For the man will not rest until he has settled the matter today."

[^3-1]: Literally "a resting place": security and a settled home. Compare 1:9.
[^3-2]: Winnowing was done in the evening breeze, by tossing the threshed grain into the air so that the wind blew the chaff away.
[^3-3]: Literally "put your garments on you". The Hebrew text is written "your garment" but read "your garments".
[^3-4]: The Hebrew text is written "and I will go down" but read "and you will go down". The written form may be an old spelling of "you".
[^3-5]: Hebrew *margelot*, "the place of the feet". Also in verses 7, 8 and 14.
[^3-6]: The Hebrew text is written "and I will lie down" but read "and you will lie down". As in verse 3, the written form may be an old spelling of "you".
[^3-7]: The words "to me" are read in the Hebrew tradition but not written in the text.
[^3-8]: Literally "his heart was good".
[^3-9]: Or "leaned forward", or "felt about".
[^3-10]: Literally "your wing". Hebrew *kanaf* means both "wing" and the corner or edge of a garment; compare 2:12. To spread one's garment over a woman was a sign of taking her as wife (Ezekiel 16:8).
[^3-11]: Hebrew *go'el*. See 2:20.
[^3-12]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3-13]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness. See 1:8. The first act was probably her loyalty to Naomi (2:11).
[^3-14]: Literally "all the gate of my people". The town gate was where its elders met and its business was done.
[^3-15]: Hebrew *eshet chayil*, "a woman of strength", "a capable woman", as in Proverbs 31:10. Compare 2:1, where Boaz is a man of *chayil*.
[^3-16]: The Hebrew text has an extra word here, *im*, which is written but not read.
[^3-17]: Literally "six of barley". The measure is not stated. Also in verse 17.
[^3-18]: Literally "Who are you, my daughter?"
[^3-19]: The words "to me" are read in the Hebrew tradition but not written in the text.

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** Meanwhile Boaz went up to the town gate[^4-1] and sat down there. Just then the guardian-redeemer[^4-2] Boaz had spoken about came by. Boaz said, "Come over here, my friend,[^4-3] and sit down." So he came over and sat down.

**2** Boaz took ten men from the elders of the town and said, "Sit down here." And they sat down.

**3** Then he said to the guardian-redeemer, "Naomi, who has come back from the country of Moab, is selling[^4-4] the piece of land that belonged to our relative[^4-5] Elimelech.

**4** I thought I should let you know[^4-6] and say, 'Buy it in the presence of those sitting here[^4-7] and in the presence of the elders of my people.' If you will redeem it, redeem it. But if you will not redeem it,[^4-8] tell me, so that I may know. For there is no one but you to redeem it, and I come after you." He said, "I will redeem it."

**5** Then Boaz said, "On the day you buy the field from Naomi, you are also acquiring Ruth the Moabite, the dead man's widow,[^4-9] in order to keep the dead man's name alive on his inheritance."[^4-10]

**6** The guardian-redeemer said, "Then I cannot redeem it for myself, or I would ruin my own inheritance. Take over my right of redemption yourself, for I cannot redeem it."

**7** In earlier times in Israel, this was the custom for redeeming and exchanging property, to make any transaction final: one man took off his sandal and gave it to the other. This was the way of confirming a transaction in Israel.[^4-11]

**8** So the guardian-redeemer said to Boaz, "Buy it yourself," and he took off his sandal.

**9** Then Boaz said to the elders and all the people, "You are witnesses today that I have bought from Naomi everything that belonged to Elimelech and everything that belonged to Chilion and Mahlon.

**10** I have also acquired Ruth the Moabite, Mahlon's widow, to be my wife, in order to keep the dead man's name alive on his inheritance, so that the dead man's name will not be cut off from among his relatives or from the gate of his town.[^4-12] You are witnesses today."

**11** All the people at the gate and the elders said, "We are witnesses. May the LORD[^4-13] make the woman who is coming into your house like Rachel and Leah, the two who built up the house of Israel. May you prosper[^4-14] in Ephrathah and be famous in Bethlehem.

**12** And through the children[^4-15] that the LORD will give you by this young woman, may your family be like the family of Perez, whom Tamar bore to Judah."[^4-16]

**13** So Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. He slept with her,[^4-17] and the LORD enabled her to conceive, and she gave birth to a son.

**14** Then the women said to Naomi, "Blessed be the LORD, who has not left you without a guardian-redeemer today! May his name be famous in Israel!

**15** He will restore your life and take care of you in your old age. For your daughter-in-law, who loves you and is better to you than seven sons, has given birth to him."

**16** Then Naomi took the child, held him in her lap,[^4-18] and cared for him.

**17** The neighbor women gave him a name, saying, "A son has been born to Naomi!" They named him Obed.[^4-19] He was the father of Jesse, the father of David.

**18** These are the generations[^4-20] of Perez: Perez fathered Hezron,

**19** Hezron fathered Ram, Ram fathered Amminadab,

**20** Amminadab fathered Nahshon, Nahshon fathered Salmon,[^4-21]

**21** Salmon fathered Boaz, Boaz fathered Obed,

**22** Obed fathered Jesse, and Jesse fathered David.

[^4-1]: The gate of a town was where its elders met and where legal business was done.
[^4-2]: Hebrew *go'el*. See 2:20.
[^4-3]: Hebrew *peloni almoni*, "so-and-so", used when the narrator does not give a name.
[^4-4]: Or "has sold".
[^4-5]: Literally "our brother".
[^4-6]: Literally "uncover your ear".
[^4-7]: Or "in the presence of the people who live here".
[^4-8]: Literally "if he will not redeem it". The Septuagint reads "if you will not redeem it".
[^4-9]: Literally "and from Ruth the Moabite, the wife of the dead man, you are acquiring". The Hebrew text is written "I am acquiring" but read "you are acquiring". With the written form, Boaz says: "On the day you buy the field from Naomi, I am acquiring Ruth the Moabite …"
[^4-10]: Literally "to raise up the name of the dead man on his inheritance", that is, to provide him with an heir who would inherit his land. Also in verse 10.
[^4-11]: Compare Deuteronomy 25:9–10.
[^4-12]: Literally "from the gate of his place".
[^4-13]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4-14]: Or "show your worth". Hebrew *chayil*, "strength, worth, wealth", as in 2:1 and 3:11.
[^4-15]: Literally "seed".
[^4-16]: Genesis 38:27–30. Perez was an ancestor of Boaz (verses 18–21).
[^4-17]: Literally "went in to her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^4-18]: Literally "laid him in her bosom".
[^4-19]: *Obed* means "one who serves".
[^4-20]: Hebrew *toledot*: the descendants of someone and their story. Compare Genesis 2:4.
[^4-21]: Hebrew *Salmah* here, but *Salmon* in verse 21: two forms of the same name.
