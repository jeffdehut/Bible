## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** Paul, Silvanus and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:[^1-1]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God the Father[^1-2] and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters,[^1-3] as is fitting, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing,

**4** so that we ourselves boast about you in the churches of God, for your endurance and faith in all the persecutions and afflictions you are enduring.

**5** This is evidence[^1-4] of the righteous judgment of God, so that you may be considered worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are also suffering,

**6** since indeed it is just for God to repay with affliction those who afflict you,

**7** and to give relief to you who are afflicted, and to us as well, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels[^1-5]

**8** in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance on those who do not know God and on those who do not obey the good news of our Lord Jesus.[^1-6]

**9** They will pay the penalty of eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might,[^1-7]

**10** when he comes on that day[^1-8] to be glorified among his holy people[^1-9] and to be marveled at among all who have believed, because our testimony to you was believed.

**11** To this end we always pray for you, that our God may make you worthy of his calling, and by his power may fulfill every desire for goodness and every work of faith,

**12** so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.[^1-10]

[^1-1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^1-2]: Many manuscripts read "God our Father".
[^1-3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^1-4]: Greek *endeigma*, "proof, sign". "This" refers to their endurance and faith in suffering (verse 4).
[^1-5]: Literally "with the angels of his power".
[^1-6]: Compare Isaiah 66:15 and Jeremiah 10:25.
[^1-7]: Or "eternal destruction that comes from the presence of the Lord …". The words echo Isaiah 2:10, 19, 21, as worded in the Septuagint: "from the presence of the fear of the Lord and from the glory of his might". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^1-8]: In the Greek, "on that day" comes at the end of the verse.
[^1-9]: Literally "his holy ones", traditionally "his saints".
[^1-10]: Or "of our God and Lord, Jesus Christ". In the Greek one article governs both "God" and "Lord".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Now concerning the coming[^2-1] of our Lord Jesus Christ and our being gathered together to him, we ask you, brothers and sisters,[^2-2]

**2** not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed, either by a spirit[^2-3] or by a spoken word or by a letter, as if from us,[^2-4] to the effect that the day of the Lord[^2-5] has come.[^2-6]

**3** Let no one deceive you in any way. For that day will not come[^2-7] unless the rebellion[^2-8] comes first and the man of lawlessness[^2-9] is revealed, the son of destruction,[^2-10]

**4** who opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god or object of worship, so that he takes his seat in the temple[^2-11] of God,[^2-12] proclaiming himself to be God.[^2-13]

**5** Do you not remember that I told you these things while I was still with you?

**6** And you know what is restraining[^2-14] him now, so that he will be revealed in his own time.

**7** For the mystery[^2-15] of lawlessness is already at work; only the one who now restrains will do so until he is taken out of the way.[^2-16]

**8** And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill[^2-17] with the breath of his mouth[^2-18] and destroy by the appearance of his coming.

**9** The coming[^2-19] of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan, with every kind of power and false signs and wonders,[^2-20]

**10** and with every kind of wicked deception for those who are perishing, because they did not accept the love of the truth so as to be saved.

**11** For this reason God sends[^2-21] them a powerful delusion,[^2-22] so that they will believe the lie,

**12** so that all who did not believe the truth but delighted in wrongdoing will be judged.

**13** But we ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters loved by the Lord, because God chose you as firstfruits[^2-23] for salvation, through sanctification by the Spirit[^2-24] and belief in the truth.

**14** To this he called you through our good news, so that you may obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**15** So then, brothers and sisters, stand firm and hold to the traditions that you were taught, whether by word of mouth or by our letter.

**16** Now may our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God our Father, who loved us and by grace gave us eternal comfort and good hope,

**17** comfort[^2-25] your hearts and strengthen them in every good work and word.

[^2-1]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord Jesus. In verse 9 the same word is used of the lawless one.
[^2-2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 13 and 15.
[^2-3]: That is, an utterance claimed to come from the Spirit, such as a prophecy.
[^2-4]: "As if from us" may refer to the letter only, or to all three.
[^2-5]: Many manuscripts read "the day of Christ".
[^2-6]: Or "is already here". Greek *enesteken*, "has come, is present".
[^2-7]: The Greek leaves the sentence unfinished; "that day will not come" is supplied from verse 2.
[^2-8]: Greek *apostasia*, "rebellion, revolt", used both of political revolt and of rebellion against God; traditionally "falling away".
[^2-9]: Greek *anomia*, "lawlessness"; also in verse 7. Many manuscripts read "the man of sin".
[^2-10]: A Hebrew way of speaking: one who belongs to destruction or is destined for it. The same phrase is used of Judas in John 17:12.
[^2-11]: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary of a temple, the building where the deity was thought to dwell.
[^2-12]: Many manuscripts read "he takes his seat as God in the temple of God".
[^2-13]: Compare Daniel 11:36 and Ezekiel 28:2.
[^2-14]: Greek *to katechon*, "that which restrains", neuter; in verse 7, *ho katechon*, "the one who restrains", masculine. Paul does not say who or what this is, since his readers already know (verse 5), and many explanations have been proposed. The verb *katecho* can also mean "hold fast" or "occupy", and some take it in one of those senses. *Now* may also go with "you know": "And now you know what is restraining him".
[^2-15]: Greek *mysterion*, here something hidden that is already at work but not yet openly revealed.
[^2-16]: Literally "only the one who now restrains, until he comes out of the midst". The Greek has no main verb.
[^2-17]: Many manuscripts leave out "Jesus", and many read "will consume" for "will kill".
[^2-18]: Compare Isaiah 11:4.
[^2-19]: Greek *parousia*, the word used in verses 1 and 8 of the coming of the Lord.
[^2-20]: Literally "signs and wonders of falsehood". *False* may describe all three.
[^2-21]: Many manuscripts read "will send".
[^2-22]: Literally "a working of error".
[^2-23]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28. Other editions, and many manuscripts, read "God chose you from the beginning".
[^2-24]: Or "through the sanctifying of your spirit". Greek *hagiasmos*, "being made holy".
[^2-25]: The Greek verbs "comfort" and "strengthen" are singular, though they have two subjects (compare 1 Thessalonians 3:11).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Finally, brothers and sisters,[^3-1] pray for us, that the word of the Lord may spread rapidly[^3-2] and be honored, just as it was with you,

**2** and that we may be rescued from perverse and evil people; for not everyone has faith.

**3** But the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one.[^3-3]

**4** And we have confidence in the Lord about you, that you are doing and will do the things we command.

**5** May the Lord direct your hearts into the love of God[^3-4] and into the endurance of Christ.[^3-5]

**6** Now we command you, brothers and sisters, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, to keep away from any brother or sister[^3-6] who is living in idleness[^3-7] and not according to the tradition they received[^3-8] from us.

**7** For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us, because we were not idle when we were with you,

**8** nor did we eat anyone's bread without paying for it, but with labor and toil we worked night and day so as not to be a burden to any of you.

**9** It was not that we do not have the right, but to give you ourselves as an example to imitate.

**10** For even when we were with you, we gave you this command: if anyone is not willing to work, they should not eat.

**11** For we hear that some among you are living in idleness, not busy at work but busybodies.[^3-9]

**12** Now such people we command and urge in the Lord Jesus Christ to work quietly and earn their own living.[^3-10]

**13** As for you, brothers and sisters, do not grow weary in doing good.

**14** If anyone does not obey what we say in this letter,[^3-11] take note of that person and do not associate with them, so that they may be ashamed.

**15** Yet do not regard them as an enemy, but warn them as a brother or sister.

**16** Now may the Lord of peace himself give you peace at all times and in every way. The Lord be with all of you.

**17** I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand.[^3-12] This is the mark in every letter; this is how I write.

**18** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you.[^3-13]

[^3-1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 6 and 13.
[^3-2]: Literally "may run".
[^3-3]: Or "from evil".
[^3-4]: Or "into love for God".
[^3-5]: Or "the steadfastness that Christ gives", or "patient waiting for Christ".
[^3-6]: Greek *adelphos*, "brother", here any fellow believer; also in verse 15.
[^3-7]: Greek *ataktos*, "out of order, disorderly", used of soldiers out of line and of people who neglect their work; also in verses 7 and 11.
[^3-8]: Some manuscripts read "you received".
[^3-9]: There is a play on words: *ergazomai*, "work", and *periergazomai*, "be a busybody", literally "work around".
[^3-10]: Literally "eat their own bread".
[^3-11]: Or "If anyone does not obey what we say, take note of that person by letter".
[^3-12]: Literally "The greeting with my own hand, of Paul". Paul usually dictated his letters to a scribe and wrote the last words himself.
[^3-13]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
