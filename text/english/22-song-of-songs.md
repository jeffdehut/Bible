## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's.[^1-1]

**2** Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth![^1-2] For your love[^1-3] is better than wine.

**3** Your oils have a pleasing fragrance; your name is oil poured out.[^1-4] That is why the young women love you.

**4** Draw me after you; let us run! The king has brought me into his rooms. We will rejoice and be glad in you;[^1-5] we will praise your love more than wine. How rightly they love you![^1-6]

**5** I am dark, and lovely,[^1-7] daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar,[^1-8] like the curtains of Solomon.

**6** Do not stare at me because I am dark, because the sun has gazed on me. My mother's sons were angry with me; they made me keep the vineyards, but my own vineyard I have not kept.[^1-9]

**7** Tell me, you whom my soul loves,[^1-10] where do you pasture your flock? Where do you make it lie down at noon? For why should I be like a woman who veils herself[^1-11] beside the flocks of your companions?

**8** If you do not know,[^1-12] most beautiful of women, follow the tracks of the flock, and pasture your young goats beside the shepherds' tents.

**9** To a mare[^1-13] among Pharaoh's chariots I compare you, my darling.[^1-14]

**10** Your cheeks are lovely with ornaments, your neck with strings of beads.

**11** We will make you ornaments of gold, studded with silver.

**12** While the king was at his couch,[^1-15] my nard[^1-16] gave out its fragrance.

**13** My beloved[^1-17] is to me a pouch of myrrh that lies between my breasts.[^1-18]

**14** My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms in the vineyards of En-gedi.[^1-19]

**15** How beautiful you are, my darling! How beautiful you are! Your eyes are doves.[^1-20]

**16** How handsome you are, my beloved, how delightful![^1-21] Our bed is green with leaves.

**17** The beams of our house are cedars, our rafters are junipers.[^1-22]

[^1-1]: "Song of Songs" is a Hebrew superlative: the finest of songs. *Which is Solomon's* may also mean "for Solomon" or "about Solomon".
[^1-2]: The Song is a series of love poems in which a woman and a man speak to each other and about each other, with other voices (the "daughters of Jerusalem" and others). The Hebrew does not say who is speaking, but its forms of "you", "I" and "my" often show whether a man or a woman speaks or is addressed. The notes give the probable speaker where it matters. Here the woman speaks, to the man ("you" is masculine), through verse 7.
[^1-3]: Hebrew *dodim*, love shown in caresses and lovemaking. Also in verse 4.
[^1-4]: A play on words: *shemen*, "oil", and *shem*, "name".
[^1-5]: "We" may be the young women of verse 3, or the woman and the man together. "You" is masculine.
[^1-6]: Literally "With uprightness they love you".
[^1-7]: Or "but lovely".
[^1-8]: A nomadic people of the Arabian desert, whose tents were made of black goats' hair.
[^1-9]: Probably a picture of herself.
[^1-10]: Literally "whom my soul (*nephesh*) loves", that is, whom I love with all my being.
[^1-11]: Perhaps a woman taken for a prostitute (compare Genesis 38:14–15). The Vulgate reads "one who wanders".
[^1-12]: "You" is feminine. The man answers her, or the young women do.
[^1-13]: Literally "my mare". Chariot horses were stallions, so a mare among them would draw every eye.
[^1-14]: Hebrew *ra'yati*, "my companion, my friend", the man's name for the woman throughout the Song. The man speaks in verses 9–11.
[^1-15]: Or "at his table", where guests reclined on couches. The woman speaks in verses 12–14.
[^1-16]: A costly perfume made from a plant of the Himalayas.
[^1-17]: Hebrew *dodi*, "my beloved", the woman's name for the man throughout the Song. It comes from the same root as *dodim*, "love", in verse 2.
[^1-18]: Or "that spends the night between my breasts".
[^1-19]: An oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea.
[^1-20]: The man speaks ("you" is feminine).
[^1-21]: The woman speaks ("you" is masculine) in verses 16–17.
[^1-22]: Hebrew *berotim*, perhaps junipers or cypresses.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** I am a crocus[^2-1] of Sharon, a lily of the valleys.

**2** Like a lily among thorns, so is my darling among the young women.[^2-2]

**3** Like an apple tree[^2-3] among the trees of the forest, so is my beloved among the young men. In his shade I delighted to sit, and his fruit was sweet to my taste.[^2-4]

**4** He brought me to the house of wine, and his banner over me was love.[^2-5]

**5** Sustain me with raisin cakes, refresh me with apples,[^2-6] for I am sick with love.

**6** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.

**7** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field:[^2-7] do not stir up or awaken love until it is ready.[^2-8]

**8** Listen! My beloved![^2-9] Look, here he comes, leaping over the mountains, bounding over the hills.

**9** My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag. Look, there he stands behind our wall, gazing in through the windows, peering through the lattice.

**10** My beloved spoke and said to me, "Arise, my darling, my beautiful one, and come away.

**11** For look, the winter is past; the rain is over and gone.

**12** The blossoms have appeared in the land; the time of pruning[^2-10] has come, and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.

**13** The fig tree ripens its early figs, and the vines in blossom give out their fragrance. Arise, my darling, my beautiful one, and come away."

**14** My dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the hiding places of the cliff,[^2-11] let me see your form, let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet, and your form is lovely.

**15** Catch the foxes for us, the little foxes that ruin the vineyards, for our vineyards are in blossom.[^2-12]

**16** My beloved is mine and I am his; he grazes among the lilies.[^2-13]

**17** Until the day breathes[^2-14] and the shadows flee, turn, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag on the cleft mountains.[^2-15]

[^2-1]: Hebrew *chavatselet*, a meadow flower, perhaps a crocus or an asphodel; traditionally "rose". Sharon is the coastal plain south of Mount Carmel. The woman probably speaks.
[^2-2]: The man speaks.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *tappuach*, perhaps an apricot. Also in verse 5.
[^2-4]: The woman speaks in verses 3–13.
[^2-5]: Or "and his look upon me was love".
[^2-6]: The commands are plural: she speaks to the young women.
[^2-7]: An oath by the animals of love. In Hebrew *bi-tsva'ot o be-ayelot ha-sadeh* sounds like the oaths "by (Yahweh of) Armies", *tseva'ot*, and "by God Almighty", *El Shaddai*. The same refrain comes in 3:5, and in a shorter form in 8:4.
[^2-8]: Literally "until it pleases".
[^2-9]: Literally "The voice of my beloved!"
[^2-10]: Or "the time of singing". Hebrew *zamir* can mean either.
[^2-11]: The man's words go on, either still quoted by the woman or spoken by him ("your" is feminine). Literally "in the hiding place of the steep slope".
[^2-12]: Who speaks is not clear; the command is plural.
[^2-13]: Or "he pastures his flock among the lilies".
[^2-14]: That is, until the cool breeze of evening, or until the dawn.
[^2-15]: Or "on the mountains of Bether". Hebrew *bater* means "division, cleft". Compare 8:14.

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** On my bed, night after night,[^3-1] I sought the one my soul loves;[^3-2] I sought him, but did not find him.

**2** I will get up now and go around the city, through the streets and through the squares; I will seek the one my soul loves. I sought him, but did not find him.

**3** The watchmen found me as they went around the city. "Have you seen the one my soul loves?"

**4** Scarcely had I passed them when I found the one my soul loves. I held him and would not let him go until I had brought him into my mother's house, into the room of the one who conceived me.

**5** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field:[^3-3] do not stir up or awaken love until it is ready.

**6** Who is this coming up from the wilderness like columns of smoke,[^3-4] perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all the powders of the merchant?

**7** Look, it is Solomon's couch![^3-5] Sixty warriors surround it, from the warriors of Israel.

**8** All of them hold swords and are trained in battle, each with his sword at his side,[^3-6] against the terrors of the night.

**9** King Solomon made himself a carriage[^3-7] from the wood of Lebanon.

**10** He made its posts of silver, its back of gold, its seat of purple; its inside was inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem.[^3-8]

**11** Go out and look, daughters of Zion, at King Solomon, at the crown with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, on the day of his heart's joy.

[^3-1]: Literally "in the nights". The woman speaks in verses 1–5.
[^3-2]: Literally "whom my soul (*nephesh*) loves"; see 1:7. Also in verses 2–4.
[^3-3]: See 2:7.
[^3-4]: "Who" is feminine in the Hebrew. It may refer to the woman, or to Solomon's couch (verse 7), a feminine word.
[^3-5]: Hebrew *mittah*, a bed or couch, here one carried in procession.
[^3-6]: Literally "on his thigh".
[^3-7]: Hebrew *appiryon*, a word found only here, probably borrowed from another language: a sedan chair or palanquin.
[^3-8]: Literally "its inside inlaid, love, from the daughters of Jerusalem". Or "inlaid with love for the daughters of Jerusalem".

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** How beautiful you are, my darling![^4-1] How beautiful you are! Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats streaming down from Mount Gilead.

**2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes that have come up from the washing; all of them bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**3** Your lips are like a scarlet thread, and your mouth is lovely. Your temples[^4-2] are like a slice of pomegranate behind your veil.

**4** Your neck is like the tower of David, built in courses,[^4-3] on which hang a thousand shields, all the shields of warriors.

**5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that graze among the lilies.

**6** Until the day breathes[^4-4] and the shadows flee, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.

**7** You are altogether beautiful, my darling; there is no flaw in you.

**8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride, come with me from Lebanon. Look down[^4-5] from the peak of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon,[^4-6] from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.

**9** You have stolen my heart,[^4-7] my sister,[^4-8] my bride; you have stolen my heart with one glance of your eyes, with one jewel of your necklace.

**10** How beautiful is your love,[^4-9] my sister, my bride! How much better is your love than wine, and the fragrance of your oils than any spice!

**11** Your lips drip honey from the comb, my bride; honey and milk are under your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**12** A garden locked is my sister, my bride, a pool[^4-10] locked, a fountain sealed.

**13** Your shoots[^4-11] are a park[^4-12] of pomegranates with choice fruits, henna with nard,

**14** nard and saffron, cane and cinnamon, with every kind of incense tree, myrrh and aloes, with all the finest spices.

**15** You are a fountain in the gardens, a well of living water,[^4-13] streams flowing down from Lebanon.

**16** Awake, north wind, and come, south wind! Blow on my garden, let its spices flow.[^4-14] Let my beloved come into his garden and eat its choice fruits.

[^4-1]: The man speaks ("you" is feminine) in verses 1–15.
[^4-2]: Or "cheeks".
[^4-3]: Hebrew *le-talpiyyot*, a word found only here, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "in courses (of stone)", "for an armory", or "with turrets".
[^4-4]: See 2:17.
[^4-5]: Or "Come down", or "Journey".
[^4-6]: Amana is a peak of the Anti-Lebanon range. Senir is another name for Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9), or a peak near it.
[^4-7]: Hebrew *libbavtini*, from *lev*, "heart". Or "You have made my heart beat faster".
[^4-8]: An affectionate name for a beloved woman, as in ancient Egyptian love songs. Also in verses 10 and 12.
[^4-9]: Hebrew *dodim*; see 1:2. Also later in the verse.
[^4-10]: Hebrew *gal*, perhaps "pool" or "spring". The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "a garden", as in the first line.
[^4-11]: Or "Your channels". Hebrew *shelachayikh*, perhaps the shoots of a plant or the channels that water a garden.
[^4-12]: Hebrew *pardes*, a Persian word for an enclosed park, the source of the English word "paradise".
[^4-13]: That is, fresh, flowing water.
[^4-14]: Who speaks the first half of the verse is not clear: the man, or the woman calling her body "my garden". In the second half the woman speaks ("my beloved").

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride;[^5-man] I have gathered my myrrh with my spice, I have eaten my honeycomb[^5-comb] with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, friends, drink, and be drunk with love![^5-lovers]

**2** I was asleep, but my heart was awake.[^5-woman] Listen! My beloved is knocking.[^5-knock] "Open to me, my sister, my darling, my dove, my perfect one, for my head is wet with dew, my locks with the drops of the night."

**3** I have taken off my robe; how could I put it on again? I have washed my feet; how could I soil them?

**4** My beloved put his hand in through the opening,[^5-hole] and my heart[^5-inner] trembled for him.

**5** I rose to open to my beloved, and my hands dripped with myrrh, my fingers with flowing myrrh, on the handles of the bolt.

**6** I opened to my beloved, but my beloved had turned and gone. My soul went out when he spoke.[^5-soul] I sought him, but did not find him; I called him, but he did not answer me.

**7** The watchmen found me as they went around the city. They struck me, they wounded me; they took my shawl from me, those watchmen of the walls.

**8** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem: if you find my beloved, what will you tell him? That I am sick with love.

**9** What is your beloved more than any other beloved, most beautiful of women? What is your beloved more than any other beloved, that you make us swear like this?[^5-daughters]

**10** My beloved is radiant and ruddy,[^5-answer] outstanding among ten thousand.

**11** His head is the finest gold; his locks are wavy,[^5-wavy] black as a raven.

**12** His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, bathed in milk, sitting by brimming pools.[^5-pools]

**13** His cheeks are like beds of spices, towers of perfume.[^5-towers] His lips are lilies, dripping with flowing myrrh.

**14** His arms[^5-arms] are rods of gold set with jewels of Tarshish.[^5-tarshish] His body is polished ivory, covered with sapphires.[^5-sapphires]

**15** His legs are pillars of alabaster,[^5-alabaster] set on bases of fine gold. His appearance is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

**16** His mouth[^5-palate] is sweetness itself, and he is altogether desirable. This is my beloved and this is my friend,[^5-friend] daughters of Jerusalem.

[^5-alabaster]: Or "marble". Hebrew *shesh*.
[^5-answer]: The woman answers in verses 10–16.
[^5-arms]: Literally "His hands".
[^5-comb]: Hebrew *ya'ar*, usually "forest", here "honeycomb", as in 1 Samuel 14:27.
[^5-daughters]: The daughters of Jerusalem speak ("your" is feminine, and "us").
[^5-friend]: Hebrew *re'i*, the masculine form of *ra'yati*, "my darling" (1:9).
[^5-hole]: Literally "the hole", perhaps the opening for the key or latch.
[^5-inner]: Literally "my inner parts".
[^5-knock]: Literally "The voice of my beloved knocking!"
[^5-lovers]: Or "drink deeply, you lovers!" Hebrew *dodim* can mean "love" (1:2) or "lovers". Who speaks this line is not clear: the man, or another voice.
[^5-man]: The man speaks ("my sister, my bride"); see 4:9.
[^5-palate]: Literally "His palate".
[^5-pools]: Literally "sitting on fullness": perhaps by full pools, or set like gems in their setting.
[^5-sapphires]: Hebrew *sappirim*, probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.
[^5-soul]: Literally "My soul (*nephesh*) went out at his speaking". Or "My heart sank at his leaving".
[^5-tarshish]: A precious stone, perhaps named after the faraway land of Tarshish: perhaps beryl or topaz.
[^5-towers]: With different vowels, the Hebrew can be read "that produce perfume", as the Septuagint reads.
[^5-wavy]: Hebrew *taltallim*, a word found only here, perhaps "wavy", "curly", or "like clusters of dates".
[^5-woman]: The woman speaks in verses 2–8.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Where has your beloved gone, most beautiful of women? Where has your beloved turned, that we may seek him with you?[^6-daughters]

**2** My beloved has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to graze in the gardens and to gather lilies.[^6-woman]

**3** I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine; he grazes among the lilies.

**4** You are beautiful, my darling, as Tirzah,[^6-tirzah] lovely as Jerusalem, awe-inspiring as armies with banners.[^6-banners]

**5** Turn your eyes away from me, for they overwhelm me. Your hair is like a flock of goats streaming down from Gilead.

**6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes that have come up from the washing; all of them bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**7** Your temples are like a slice of pomegranate behind your veil.

**8** There are sixty queens and eighty concubines, and young women without number.

**9** But my dove, my perfect one, is the only one, the only one of her mother, the favorite of the one who bore her. The young women saw her and called her blessed; the queens and concubines saw her and praised her:[^6-praise]

**10** Who is this who looks down like the dawn, beautiful as the moon, bright as the sun,[^6-moon] awe-inspiring as armies with banners?

**11** I went down to the walnut grove to look at the green shoots of the valley,[^6-valley] to see whether the vine had budded, whether the pomegranates were in bloom.[^6-who]

**12** Before I knew it, my desire set me among the chariots of my noble people.[^6-obscure]

[^6-banners]: Hebrew *ka-nidgalot*, "like those with banners": perhaps armies, or the stars. Also in verse 10.
[^6-daughters]: The daughters of Jerusalem speak ("your" is feminine).
[^6-moon]: Hebrew *levanah*, "the white one", and *chammah*, "the hot one", poetic words for the moon and the sun.
[^6-obscure]: The Hebrew is very obscure. Literally "I did not know, my soul set me chariots of my people, noble". Or "the chariots of Amminadib", taking the last words as a name.
[^6-praise]: Literally "and they praised her". Verse 10 may give the words of their praise.
[^6-tirzah]: An early capital of the northern kingdom of Israel (1 Kings 14:17; 16:23). The name means "pleasing". The man speaks in verses 4–10 ("you" is feminine).
[^6-valley]: Hebrew *nachal*, a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season.
[^6-who]: Who speaks in verses 11–12 is not clear: perhaps the woman, perhaps the man.
[^6-woman]: The woman speaks in verses 2–3.

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** Turn back, turn back, O Shulammite![^7-shulammite] Turn back, turn back, that we may look at you! Why would you look at the Shulammite, as at the dance of the two camps?[^7-camps]

**2** How beautiful are your feet in sandals, O noble daughter![^7-noble] The curves of your thighs are like jewels, the work of a craftsman's hands.

**3** Your navel is a rounded bowl; may it never lack mixed wine.[^7-wine] Your belly is a heap of wheat, encircled with lilies.

**4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

**5** Your neck is like a tower of ivory. Your eyes are pools in Heshbon,[^7-heshbon] by the gate of Bath-rabbim.[^7-bathrabbim] Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, looking out toward Damascus.

**6** Your head crowns you like Carmel,[^7-carmel] and the flowing hair of your head is like purple;[^7-purple] a king is held captive in its tresses.[^7-tresses]

**7** How beautiful you are, and how pleasant, O love, with its delights!

**8** Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts are like its clusters.

**9** I said, "I will climb the palm tree; I will take hold of its fruit." Oh, may your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your breath[^7-nose] like apples,

**10** and your mouth[^7-palate] like the best wine, going down smoothly for my beloved,[^7-mybeloved] gliding over the lips of sleepers.[^7-sleepers]

**11** I am my beloved's, and his desire[^7-desire] is for me.

**12** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the fields; let us spend the night in the villages.[^7-villages]

**13** Let us go early to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has budded, whether its blossoms have opened, whether the pomegranates are in bloom. There I will give you my love.[^7-dodim]

**14** The mandrakes[^7-mandrakes] give out their fragrance, and at our doors are all kinds of choice fruits, new as well as old, which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

[^7-bathrabbim]: The name means "daughter of many".
[^7-camps]: Or "the dance of Mahanaim", a place whose name means "two camps" (Genesis 32:3, 32:2 in English Bibles).
[^7-carmel]: The wooded mountain range on the coast. The same consonants can be read *karmil*, "crimson" (2 Chronicles 2:6).
[^7-desire]: Hebrew *teshuqah*, found elsewhere only in Genesis 3:16 and 4:7.
[^7-dodim]: Hebrew *dodim*; see 1:2.
[^7-heshbon]: A city east of the Jordan, known for its pools.
[^7-mandrakes]: Hebrew *duda'im*, a plant believed to stir love and help conception (Genesis 30:14–16). The word sounds like *dodim*, "love", and *dodi*, "my beloved".
[^7-mybeloved]: "My beloved" is the woman's name for the man (1:13). Here she seems to take up the man's words and finish them.
[^7-noble]: Literally "daughter of a noble". The man probably speaks in verses 2–10 ("you" is feminine).
[^7-nose]: Literally "your nose".
[^7-palate]: Literally "your palate".
[^7-purple]: The costly dark red-purple dye worn by kings.
[^7-shulammite]: Verse 1 is 6:13 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–14 are 7:1–13. *Shulammite* may mean "woman of Shulem", perhaps Shunem (1 Kings 1:3), or it may be a feminine form of the name Solomon (*Shelomoh*). Others ("we") speak in the first half of the verse; who answers in the second half is not clear.
[^7-sleepers]: The Septuagint reads "over my lips and teeth".
[^7-tresses]: Hebrew *rehatim*, elsewhere "watering troughs" (Genesis 30:38), here the flowing locks of her hair.
[^7-villages]: Or "among the henna bushes". Hebrew *kefarim* can mean either (compare 1:14).
[^7-wine]: Wine mixed with spices or water.
