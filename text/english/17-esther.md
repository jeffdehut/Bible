## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** This happened in the days of Ahasuerus,[^1-1] the Ahasuerus who reigned over 127 provinces,[^1-2] from India to Cush.[^1-3]

**2** In those days, when King Ahasuerus sat on his royal throne in the citadel of Susa,[^1-4]

**3** in the third year of his reign[^1-5] he gave a banquet for all his officials and his servants. The army[^1-6] of Persia and Media, the nobles[^1-7] and the officials of the provinces were in his presence,

**4** while he displayed the riches of his royal glory and the splendor and pomp of his greatness for many days, 180 days.

**5** When these days were over, the king gave a banquet lasting seven days for all the people who were present in the citadel of Susa, from the greatest to the least, in the court of the garden of the king's palace.[^1-8]

**6** There were hangings of white cotton and violet,[^1-9] fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and marble pillars. There were couches of gold and silver on a pavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl and precious stones.[^1-10]

**7** Drinks were served in gold vessels, each vessel different from the others, and there was royal wine in abundance, as befitted the king's generosity.[^1-11]

**8** The drinking was according to the law:[^1-12] no one was compelled. For the king had instructed all the officials of his household to do as each man wished.

**9** Queen Vashti also gave a banquet for the women in the royal palace that belonged to King Ahasuerus.

**10** On the seventh day, when the king's heart was merry with wine, he told Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha and Abagtha, Zethar and Carkas, the seven eunuchs[^1-13] who served King Ahasuerus,

**11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king wearing the royal crown, to show the peoples and the officials her beauty, for she was beautiful to look at.

**12** But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's command, which the eunuchs brought. The king became very angry, and his rage burned within him.

**13** Then the king spoke to the wise men who understood the times,[^1-14] for this was the king's practice with all who knew law and justice.[^1-15]

**14** Those closest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media who had access to the king[^1-16] and held the first place in the kingdom.

**15** He asked,[^1-17] "According to the law, what is to be done to Queen Vashti, because she has not obeyed the command of King Ahasuerus, which the eunuchs brought?"

**16** Memucan said in the presence of the king and the officials, "Queen Vashti has done wrong not only to the king, but also to all the officials and all the peoples in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**17** For what the queen has done will become known to all the women, and they will look on their husbands with contempt. They will say, 'King Ahasuerus commanded Queen Vashti to be brought before him, but she did not come.'

**18** This very day the noble ladies of Persia and Media who have heard what the queen has done will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be no end of contempt and anger.

**19** If it pleases the king, let a royal order go out from him, and let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media, so that it cannot be revoked,[^1-18] that Vashti is never again to come before King Ahasuerus. And let the king give her royal position to another woman who is better than she.

**20** When the king's edict[^1-19] that he makes is heard throughout his whole kingdom, vast as it is, all the women will give honor to their husbands, from the greatest to the least."

**21** The advice pleased the king and the officials, and the king did as Memucan proposed.

**22** He sent letters to all the king's provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, that every man should be master in his own house and should speak the language of his own people.[^1-20]

[^1-1]: Hebrew *Achashverosh*: Xerxes I, king of Persia from 486 to 465 BC.
[^1-2]: Hebrew *medinot*, provinces or districts: smaller divisions of the empire than the satrapies (3:12).
[^1-3]: Hebrew *Hoddu* and *Kush*: the Indus valley in the east and the land south of Egypt (ancient Nubia) in the west.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *Shushan ha-birah*. Susa, in what is now southwest Iran, was one of the royal cities of the Persian kings. The citadel was the fortified royal quarter of the city.
[^1-5]: 483 BC.
[^1-6]: Or "the powerful men".
[^1-7]: Hebrew *partemim*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^1-8]: Hebrew *bitan*, a word borrowed from Persian for a palace or pavilion.
[^1-9]: Hebrew *tekhelet*, a violet-blue.
[^1-10]: The precise meaning of several of these Hebrew words for fabrics and stones is uncertain.
[^1-11]: Literally "according to the hand of the king".
[^1-12]: Hebrew *dat*, "law, decree", a word borrowed from Persian. It is the usual word for law in Esther.
[^1-13]: Hebrew *sarisim*, court officials. In Esther they serve the king and keep the women of the palace (2:3, 14–15), so the word probably has its narrower sense, "eunuchs".
[^1-14]: Perhaps those skilled in reading the signs of the times, such as astrologers.
[^1-15]: Literally "for so was the king's word before all who knew law and judgment".
[^1-16]: Literally "who saw the king's face". Compare Ezra 7:14.
[^1-17]: "He asked" is not in the Hebrew; the king's question follows directly from verse 13.
[^1-18]: Literally "so that it will not pass away". Compare 8:8; Daniel 6:9, 13, 16 (6:8, 12, 15 in most English Bibles).
[^1-19]: Hebrew *pitgam*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^1-20]: The meaning of the last words is uncertain. Literally "and speaking according to the language of his people".

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** After these things, when the anger of King Ahasuerus had died down, he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her.

**2** Then the king's young men who attended him said, "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king.

**3** Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom to gather every beautiful young virgin to the citadel of Susa, to the house of the women, into the care of Hegai, the king's eunuch,[^2-1] who is in charge of the women, and let their beauty treatments be given to them.

**4** And let the young woman who pleases the king become queen instead of Vashti." This pleased the king, and he did so.

**5** Now there was a Jewish man in the citadel of Susa whose name was Mordecai[^2-2] son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite.[^2-3]

**6** He[^2-4] had been taken into exile from Jerusalem with the exiles who were carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken into exile.[^2-5]

**7** He was bringing up Hadassah,[^2-6] that is, Esther,[^2-7] the daughter of his uncle, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was lovely in form and beautiful to look at, and when her father and her mother died, Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

**8** When the king's command and his law had been proclaimed, and many young women had been gathered to the citadel of Susa into the care of Hegai, Esther also was taken to the king's palace, into the care of Hegai, who was in charge of the women.

**9** The young woman pleased him and won his favor.[^2-8] He quickly provided her with her beauty treatments and her portions of food, and with seven young women chosen from the king's palace, and he moved her and her young women to the best place in the house of the women.

**10** Esther had not made known her people or her family background, because Mordecai had commanded her not to make it known.

**11** Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the court of the house of the women, to learn how Esther was[^2-9] and what was being done with her.

**12** Each young woman's turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus after she had been treated for twelve months according to the regulations for the women. For this was how the days of their beauty treatment were completed: six months with oil of myrrh, and six months with spices and the cosmetics for women.

**13** Then the young woman would go in to the king like this: whatever she asked for was given to her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's palace.

**14** In the evening she would go in, and in the morning she would return to the second house of the women,[^2-10] into the care of Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch, who was in charge of the concubines. She would not go in to the king again unless the king desired her and she was summoned by name.

**15** When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her as his own daughter, to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, who was in charge of the women, advised. And Esther won the favor of all who saw her.

**16** Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, to his royal palace, in the tenth month, which is the month of Tebeth,[^2-11] in the seventh year of his reign.[^2-12]

**17** The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she won his grace and favor more than all the virgins. So he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti.

**18** Then the king gave a great banquet for all his officials and his servants, Esther's banquet. He granted a holiday[^2-13] to the provinces and gave gifts with royal generosity.[^2-14]

**19** When the virgins were gathered a second time,[^2-15] Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate.[^2-16]

**20** Esther had still not made known her family background or her people, as Mordecai had commanded her, for Esther obeyed Mordecai's word, just as she had when she was being brought up by him.

**21** In those days, while Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold, became angry and plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.

**22** The plot became known to Mordecai, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther told the king in Mordecai's name.

**23** When the matter was investigated and found to be true, both men were hanged on a pole.[^2-17] And it was recorded in the book of the chronicles[^2-18] in the king's presence.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *saris*; see 1:10. Also in verses 14–15 and 21.
[^2-2]: The name is related to that of Marduk, the chief god of Babylon.
[^2-3]: Literally "a man of Yemini". Saul's father was also Kish, a Benjaminite (1 Samuel 9:1), and Shimei a Benjaminite of Saul's family (2 Samuel 16:5).
[^2-4]: "He" may refer to Mordecai or to Kish, his great-grandfather.
[^2-5]: In 597 BC (2 Kings 24:10–16). Jeconiah is also called Jehoiachin.
[^2-6]: *Hadassah* means "myrtle".
[^2-7]: *Esther* may come from the Persian word for "star", or from the name of the Babylonian goddess Ishtar.
[^2-8]: Hebrew *chesed*, here "kindness, favor". Also in verse 17.
[^2-9]: Literally "to know the peace of Esther".
[^2-10]: Or "she would return to the house of the women a second time".
[^2-11]: Around December–January.
[^2-12]: 479 BC.
[^2-13]: Or "a remission of taxes".
[^2-14]: Literally "according to the hand of the king".
[^2-15]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps a second gathering of young women after Esther became queen.
[^2-16]: The gate was where the king's officials did their business. Sitting there may mean that Mordecai held an office (compare 3:2–3).
[^2-17]: Hebrew *ets*, "tree, wood": a pole or stake. Traditionally "gallows". The Persians executed people by impaling them (compare Ezra 6:11).
[^2-18]: Literally "the book of the events of the days", the royal records (compare 6:1; 10:2).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** After these things King Ahasuerus promoted Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite.[^3-1] He raised him up and set his seat above all the officials who were with him.

**2** All the king's servants who were in the king's gate would kneel and bow down to Haman, for the king had commanded this concerning him. But Mordecai would not kneel or bow down.

**3** Then the king's servants who were in the king's gate said to Mordecai, "Why do you disobey the king's command?"

**4** When they had spoken to him day after day and he would not listen to them, they told Haman, to see whether Mordecai's words would stand, for he had told them that he was a Jew.

**5** When Haman saw that Mordecai would not kneel or bow down to him, Haman was filled with rage.

**6** But he scorned laying hands on Mordecai alone, for they had told him who Mordecai's people were. So Haman sought to destroy all the Jews, the people of Mordecai, throughout the whole kingdom of Ahasuerus.

**7** In the first month, which is the month of Nisan,[^3-2] in the twelfth year of King Ahasuerus,[^3-3] they cast pur,[^3-4] that is, the lot, before Haman, from day to day and from month to month,[^3-5] until the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.[^3-6]

**8** Then Haman said to King Ahasuerus, "There is a certain people scattered and dispersed among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws[^3-7] are different from those of every other people, and they do not keep the king's laws. It is not in the king's interest to let them be.

**9** If it pleases the king, let an order be written to destroy them, and I will weigh out ten thousand talents[^3-8] of silver into the hands of those who carry out the work, to bring into the king's treasuries."

**10** So the king took his signet ring off his hand and gave it to Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of the Jews.

**11** The king said to Haman, "The silver is given to you,[^3-9] and the people as well, to do with them as you see fit."

**12** Then the king's scribes were summoned on the thirteenth day of the first month, and an order was written, just as Haman commanded, to the king's satraps,[^3-10] to the governors over each province and to the officials of each people, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language. It was written in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed with the king's signet ring.

**13** Letters were sent by couriers to all the king's provinces, to destroy, to kill and to annihilate all the Jews, young and old, children and women, in one day, the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, and to plunder their goods.

**14** A copy[^3-11] of the document was to be issued as law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that they would be ready for that day.

**15** The couriers went out in haste at the king's command, and the law was issued in the citadel of Susa. The king and Haman sat down to drink, but the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.

[^3-1]: Agag was the king of the Amalekites, Israel's old enemies, whom Saul son of Kish spared (1 Samuel 15:8–9; compare 2:5).
[^3-2]: Around March–April.
[^3-3]: 474 BC.
[^3-4]: Hebrew *pur*, a word borrowed from Akkadian *puru*, "lot". The festival of Purim takes its name from it (9:24–26).
[^3-5]: That is, to choose the day and the month.
[^3-6]: Around February–March.
[^3-7]: Hebrew *datehem*, plural of *dat*, "law". Also "the king's laws" later in the verse.
[^3-8]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so ten thousand talents was about 375 tons (340 metric tons).
[^3-9]: Or "Keep the silver".
[^3-10]: Hebrew *achashdarpenim*, from a Persian title: the satraps, who governed the large regions of the Persian Empire, above the governors of its provinces.
[^3-11]: Hebrew *patshegen*, a word borrowed from Persian (compare Ezra 4:11).

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** When Mordecai learned all that had been done, he tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes. He went out into the middle of the city and cried out with a loud and bitter cry.

**2** He went only as far as the front of the king's gate, for no one was allowed to enter the king's gate wearing sackcloth.

**3** In every province, wherever the king's command and his law reached, there was great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping and lamenting, and many lay in sackcloth and ashes.[^4-1]

**4** When Esther's young women and her eunuchs[^4-2] came and told her, the queen was deeply distressed. She sent clothes for Mordecai to put on, so that he would take off his sackcloth, but he would not accept them.

**5** Then Esther called Hathach, one of the king's eunuchs, whom he had appointed to attend her, and ordered him to go to Mordecai to learn what this was and why it was happening.

**6** So Hathach went out to Mordecai in the city square in front of the king's gate.

**7** Mordecai told him all that had happened to him, and the exact amount of money that Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews.

**8** He also gave him a copy of the written law that had been issued in Susa for their destruction, to show Esther and explain to her, and to order her to go to the king to plead with him and make a request before him for her people.

**9** Hathach went and told Esther what Mordecai had said.

**10** Then Esther spoke to Hathach and ordered him to say to Mordecai,

**11** "All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that for any man or woman who goes to the king in the inner court without being summoned, there is one law: to be put to death, unless the king holds out the gold scepter to him, so that he may live. And I have not been summoned to come to the king for these thirty days."

**12** When Esther's words were reported to Mordecai,

**13** Mordecai told them to reply to Esther, "Do not imagine that because you are in the king's palace you alone of all the Jews will escape.

**14** For if you keep silent at this time, relief and deliverance will come to the Jews from another place,[^4-3] but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether it was not for a time like this that you have come to the kingdom?"[^4-4]

**15** Then Esther told them to reply to Mordecai,

**16** "Go, gather all the Jews who are found in Susa, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. My young women and I will also fast in the same way. Then I will go in to the king, though it is against the law. And if I perish, I perish."

**17** So Mordecai went away and did everything that Esther had commanded him.

[^4-1]: Literally "sackcloth and ashes were spread out for many".
[^4-2]: Hebrew *sarisim*; see 1:10. Also in verse 5.
[^4-3]: The Hebrew book of Esther never mentions God, and this translation does not add him. "Another place" is not explained: some take it as a veiled reference to God, others as help from some other quarter. The Greek version of Esther in the Septuagint adds long passages, including prayers, that speak of God.
[^4-4]: Or "to royal rank".

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** On the third day Esther put on her royal robes[^5-1] and stood in the inner court of the king's palace, opposite the king's hall. The king was sitting on his royal throne in the royal hall, facing the entrance of the hall.

**2** When the king saw Queen Esther standing in the court, she won his favor, and the king held out to Esther the gold scepter that was in his hand. So Esther came near and touched the tip of the scepter.

**3** The king said to her, "What is it, Queen Esther? What is your request? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be given to you."

**4** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the king and Haman come today to the banquet that I have prepared for him."

**5** The king said, "Bring Haman quickly, so that we may do what Esther has asked." So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

**6** As they were drinking wine,[^5-2] the king said to Esther, "What is your petition? It will be granted to you. And what is your request? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done."

**7** Esther answered, "My petition and my request is this:

**8** If I have found favor in the eyes of the king, and if it pleases the king to grant my petition and to do what I request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I will prepare for them, and tomorrow I will do as the king has said."

**9** Haman went out that day joyful and in good spirits. But when Haman saw Mordecai in the king's gate, and Mordecai neither stood up nor trembled before him, Haman was filled with rage against Mordecai.

**10** Nevertheless Haman restrained himself and went home. He sent for his friends and his wife Zeresh and brought them in.

**11** Haman told them of the glory of his wealth, the number of his sons, and all the ways the king had honored him and promoted him above the officials and the king's servants.

**12** Haman said, "What is more, Queen Esther let no one but me come with the king to the banquet she prepared. And tomorrow too I am invited by her, together with the king.

**13** Yet all this is worth nothing to me, as long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting in the king's gate."

**14** Then his wife Zeresh and all his friends said to him, "Have a pole[^5-3] made, fifty cubits high,[^5-4] and in the morning tell the king to have Mordecai hanged on it. Then go with the king to the banquet in good spirits." The idea pleased Haman, and he had the pole made.

[^5-1]: Literally "Esther put on royalty".
[^5-2]: Literally "At the banquet of wine". Also in 7:2.
[^5-3]: Hebrew *ets*, "tree, wood": a pole or stake; see 2:23.
[^5-4]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so fifty cubits was about 75 feet (23 meters).

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** That night the king could not sleep.[^6-1] So he ordered the book of records, the chronicles,[^6-2] to be brought, and they were read before the king.

**2** It was found written there how Mordecai had told about Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold, who had plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.[^6-3]

**3** The king said, "What honor or distinction has been given to Mordecai for this?" The king's young men who attended him said, "Nothing has been done for him."

**4** The king said, "Who is in the court?" Now Haman had just come into the outer court of the king's palace to speak to the king about hanging Mordecai on the pole that he had prepared for him.

**5** The king's young men said to him, "Haman is standing in the court." And the king said, "Let him come in."

**6** So Haman came in, and the king said to him, "What should be done for the man whom the king delights to honor?" Haman said to himself, "Whom would the king delight to honor more than me?"

**7** So Haman said to the king, "For the man whom the king delights to honor,

**8** let them bring a royal robe that the king has worn, and a horse that the king has ridden, one with a royal crown on its head.[^6-4]

**9** Let the robe and the horse be handed over to one of the king's most noble officials. Let them dress the man whom the king delights to honor, and lead him on horseback through the city square, proclaiming before him, 'This is what is done for the man whom the king delights to honor!'"

**10** Then the king said to Haman, "Hurry, take the robe and the horse, as you have said, and do this for Mordecai the Jew, who sits in the king's gate. Do not leave out anything of all that you have said."

**11** So Haman took the robe and the horse. He dressed Mordecai and led him on horseback through the city square, proclaiming before him, "This is what is done for the man whom the king delights to honor!"

**12** Then Mordecai returned to the king's gate. But Haman hurried home, mourning and with his head covered.

**13** Haman told his wife Zeresh and all his friends everything that had happened to him. Then his wise men and his wife Zeresh said to him, "If Mordecai, before whom you have begun to fall, is of Jewish descent,[^6-5] you will not prevail against him. You will surely fall before him."

**14** While they were still speaking with him, the king's eunuchs arrived and hurried Haman off to the banquet that Esther had prepared.

[^6-1]: Literally "the king's sleep fled".
[^6-2]: Literally "the book of the memorials, the events of the days". See 2:23.
[^6-3]: See 2:21–23, where the first name is spelled Bigthan.
[^6-4]: Or "and on whose head a royal crown has been placed". The Hebrew may refer to the horse or to the king.
[^6-5]: Literally "is of the seed of the Jews".

## Chapter 7

*Status: draft*

**1** So the king and Haman went in to drink with Queen Esther.

**2** On the second day too, as they were drinking wine, the king said to Esther, "What is your petition, Queen Esther? It will be granted to you. And what is your request? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done."

**3** Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favor in your eyes, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given to me as my petition, and my people as my request.

**4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, killed and annihilated. If we had only been sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent, for that trouble would not be worth troubling the king."[^7-1]

**5** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to do this?"[^7-2]

**6** Esther said, "An adversary and an enemy: this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

**7** The king got up in his rage from the drinking of wine and went into the palace garden, but Haman stayed behind to beg Queen Esther for his life, for he saw that the king had determined to do him harm.

**8** When the king returned from the palace garden to the place where they had been drinking wine, Haman was falling on the couch where Esther was. The king said, "Will he even assault[^7-3] the queen while I am in the house?" As soon as the words left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**9** Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs[^7-4] in attendance on the king, said, "Look, the pole that Haman made for Mordecai, whose word saved the king,[^7-5] is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high."[^7-6] The king said, "Hang him on it!"

**10** So they hanged Haman on the pole that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's rage died down.

[^7-1]: The meaning of the last words is uncertain. Perhaps "for the distress would not have justified troubling the king", or "but the enemy could not make up for the king's loss".
[^7-2]: Literally "whose heart has filled him to do so".
[^7-3]: Hebrew *kavash*, "subdue, force".
[^7-4]: Hebrew *sarisim*; see 1:10.
[^7-5]: Literally "who spoke good concerning the king" (2:21–23).
[^7-6]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so fifty cubits was about 75 feet (23 meters).

## Chapter 8

*Status: draft*

**1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. And Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told him what Mordecai was to her.

**2** The king took off his signet ring, which he had taken back from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. And Esther put Mordecai in charge of the house of Haman.

**3** Then Esther spoke again to the king. She fell at his feet and wept, and pleaded with him to avert the evil of Haman the Agagite and the plot that he had devised against the Jews.

**4** The king held out the gold scepter to Esther, and Esther got up and stood before the king.

**5** She said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favor with him, and if the thing seems right to the king, and I am pleasing in his eyes, let an order be written to revoke the letters devised by Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite,[^8-1] which he wrote to destroy the Jews who are in all the king's provinces.

**6** For how can I bear to see the disaster that is coming on my people? And how can I bear to see the destruction of my kindred?"

**7** King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew, "Look, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and they have hanged him on the pole[^8-2] because he reached out his hand against the Jews.

**8** Now you write as you see fit concerning the Jews, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's signet ring. For a document that is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked."

**9** So the king's scribes were summoned at that time, on the twenty-third day of the third month, which is the month of Sivan.[^8-3] An order was written, just as Mordecai commanded, to the Jews, and to the satraps,[^8-4] the governors and the officials of the provinces from India to Cush, 127 provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and their own language.

**10** Mordecai wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed it with the king's signet ring, and he sent letters by couriers on horseback, riding swift horses from the royal service, bred from the royal stud.[^8-5]

**11** By these letters the king allowed the Jews in every city to gather and defend their lives, to destroy, kill and annihilate any armed force of any people or province that might attack them, with their children and women,[^8-6] and to plunder their goods,

**12** on one day throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.

**13** A copy of the document was to be issued as law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready on that day to take revenge on their enemies.

**14** The couriers, riding the swift horses from the royal service, went out in haste, urged on by the king's command. And the law was issued in the citadel of Susa.

**15** Mordecai went out from the king's presence in royal robes of violet and white, with a great gold crown and a cloak of fine linen and purple. And the city of Susa shouted for joy and rejoiced.

**16** For the Jews there was light and gladness, joy and honor.

**17** In every province and in every city, wherever the king's command and his law reached, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, a banquet and a holiday.[^8-7] And many of the peoples of the land became Jews,[^8-8] for fear of the Jews had fallen on them.

[^8-1]: Literally "the letters, the plot of Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite".
[^8-2]: Hebrew *ets*; see 2:23.
[^8-3]: Around May–June, two months after Haman's decree (3:12).
[^8-4]: Hebrew *achashdarpenim*: the satraps, who governed the large regions of the Persian Empire; see 3:12.
[^8-5]: The meaning of several of these Hebrew words is uncertain. *Achashteranim*, here "from the royal service", is a word borrowed from Persian. Also in verse 14.
[^8-6]: The Hebrew is ambiguous. The children and women may be those of the attackers, as in Haman's decree (3:13), or those of the Jews who are attacked.
[^8-7]: Literally "a good day".
[^8-8]: Or "professed to be Jews". Hebrew *mityahadim* is found only here.

## Chapter 9

*Status: draft*

**1** In the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, on the thirteenth day of the month, when the king's command and his law were about to be carried out, on the very day when the enemies of the Jews had hoped to gain power over them, it was turned the other way:[^9-1] the Jews gained power over those who hated them.

**2** The Jews gathered in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought to harm them. No one could stand against them, for fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.

**3** All the officials of the provinces, the satraps,[^9-2] the governors and those who carried out the king's business supported the Jews, for fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.

**4** For Mordecai was great in the king's palace, and his fame was spreading through all the provinces, as the man Mordecai grew more and more powerful.

**5** The Jews struck down all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them, and they did as they pleased to those who hated them.

**6** In the citadel of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.

**7** They also killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,

**8** Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,

**9** Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha,

**10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.[^9-3] But they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**11** That same day the number of those killed in the citadel of Susa was reported to the king.

**12** The king said to Queen Esther, "In the citadel of Susa the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your petition? It will be granted to you. And what further request do you have? It will be done."

**13** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews who are in Susa be allowed tomorrow also to act according to today's law, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the pole."[^9-4]

**14** So the king commanded that this be done. A law was issued in Susa, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged.

**15** The Jews who were in Susa gathered again on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, and they killed three hundred men in Susa. But they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**16** The rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces also gathered to defend their lives, and they gained rest from their enemies. They killed seventy-five thousand[^9-5] of those who hated them, but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**17** This was on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on the fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and joy.

**18** But the Jews who were in Susa gathered on the thirteenth day and on the fourteenth day, and on the fifteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and joy.

**19** That is why the Jews of the countryside, who live in the unwalled towns, keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of joy and feasting, a holiday,[^9-6] and a day for sending portions of food to one another.

**20** Mordecai recorded these things, and he sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, near and far,

**21** to require them to keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day every year,

**22** as the days on which the Jews gained rest from their enemies, and as the month that had been turned for them from sorrow into joy and from mourning into a holiday. They were to make them days of feasting and joy, of sending portions of food to one another and gifts to the poor.

**23** So the Jews accepted as a custom what they had begun to do, and what Mordecai had written to them.

**24** For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them. He had cast pur, that is, the lot, to throw them into confusion[^9-7] and destroy them.

**25** But when it came[^9-8] before the king, he commanded by letter that the evil plot that Haman had devised against the Jews should come back on his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the pole.

**26** That is why these days are called Purim,[^9-9] after the word pur. So, because of everything written in this letter, and because of what they had seen concerning this and what had happened to them,

**27** the Jews established it and took it on themselves,[^9-10] and on their descendants and on all who would join them, that without fail they would keep these two days every year, as it was written and at the appointed time,

**28** and that these days would be remembered and kept in every generation, in every family, every province and every city, and that these days of Purim would never cease among the Jews, nor would the memory of them come to an end among their descendants.

**29** Then Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter about Purim.[^9-11]

**30** Letters were sent to all the Jews, to the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, with words of peace and truth,

**31** to establish these days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established for them, and as they had established for themselves and for their descendants, concerning their fasts and their lamenting.

**32** Esther's command confirmed these regulations about Purim, and it was recorded in writing.[^9-12]

[^9-2]: Hebrew *achashdarpenim*: the satraps, who governed the large regions of the Persian Empire; see 3:12.
[^9-3]: Literally "the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, they killed". In Hebrew the verb comes at the end of the list.
[^9-4]: They had already been killed (verses 7–10), so their bodies were to be displayed. Hebrew *ets*, "tree, wood"; see 2:23.

## Chapter 10

**1** King Ahasuerus imposed forced labor[^10-1] on the land and on the coastlands of the sea.

**2** All his powerful and mighty acts, and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai, whom the king made great, are they not written in the book of the chronicles[^10-2] of the kings of Media and Persia?

**3** For Mordecai the Jew was second only to King Ahasuerus. He was great among the Jews and well liked by his many kindred, for he sought the good of his people and spoke for the welfare[^10-3] of all his descendants.[^10-4]
