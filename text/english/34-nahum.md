## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** An oracle[^1-1] concerning Nineveh.[^1-2] The book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.[^1-3]

**2** The LORD[^1-4] is a jealous[^1-5] and avenging God; the LORD is avenging and full of wrath.[^1-6] The LORD takes vengeance on his adversaries and keeps his anger for his enemies.

**3** The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and he will by no means leave the guilty unpunished.[^1-7] The LORD's way is in the whirlwind and the storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet.

**4** He rebukes the sea and dries it up; he makes all the rivers run dry. Bashan and Carmel wither, and the blossom of Lebanon fades.[^1-8]

**5** The mountains quake before him, and the hills melt away. The earth heaves[^1-9] at his presence, the world and all who live in it.

**6** Who can stand before his fury? Who can endure the heat of his anger? His wrath is poured out like fire, and the rocks are shattered before him.

**7** The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of distress. He knows[^1-10] those who take refuge in him.

**8** But with an overwhelming flood he will make a complete end of her place,[^1-11] and he will pursue his enemies into darkness.

**9** What are you plotting against the LORD?[^1-12] He will make a complete end; distress will not rise up a second time.

**10** For like tangled thorns, and drunk as with their drink,[^1-13] they will be consumed like dry stubble, completely.

**11** From you has come one who plots evil against the LORD, a counselor of wickedness.[^1-14]

**12** This is what the LORD says: Though they are at full strength and many, even so they will be cut down and pass away.[^1-15] Though I have afflicted you, I will afflict you no more.[^1-16]

**13** And now I will break his yoke from off you and tear off your bonds.

**14** The LORD has given a command concerning you:[^1-17] No more descendants will bear your name.[^1-18] From the house of your gods I will cut off the carved image and the cast idol. I will prepare your grave, for you are worthless.[^1-19]

[^1-1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden of Nineveh".
[^1-2]: The capital of Assyria (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh fell to the Babylonians and the Medes in 612 BC. Nahum also speaks of the fall of Thebes in Egypt, in 663 BC, as past (3:8), so he prophesied between those dates.
[^1-3]: *Nahum* means "comfort". The town of Elkosh is unknown.
[^1-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^1-5]: Hebrew *qanno*, "jealous, zealous", the God who demands the loyalty of his people and defends them (Exodus 20:5; 34:14). Verses 2–8 form a partial acrostic poem: their lines begin with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, from *aleph* to *kaph*, with some gaps.
[^1-6]: Literally "a lord of wrath".
[^1-7]: The words echo the LORD's description of himself in Exodus 34:6–7 (compare Numbers 14:18), but where Exodus has "abounding in faithful love", Nahum has "great in power".
[^1-8]: Bashan, Carmel and Lebanon were famous for their pastures, their forests and their fruitfulness.
[^1-9]: Literally "lifts itself up".
[^1-10]: Or "cares for".
[^1-11]: That is, Nineveh's place. The Hebrew is feminine.
[^1-12]: Or "What do you think of the LORD?" The speaker may be addressing Nineveh or Judah.
[^1-13]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "For until thorns entangled, and as their drink drunk, they are consumed like dry stubble, full".
[^1-14]: Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthlessness", used of what is wicked and destructive. *You* is Nineveh, and the plotter may be a king of Assyria.
[^1-15]: The Hebrew is difficult. *They* are the Assyrians.
[^1-16]: *You* (feminine) is Judah, also in verse 13.
[^1-17]: *You* (masculine) is the king of Assyria.
[^1-18]: Literally "No more of your name will be sown".
[^1-19]: Or "for you are contemptible", literally "for you are light", of no weight. In most English Bibles chapter 1 has a verse 15, which is 2:1 in the Hebrew.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** Look, on the mountains, the feet of the one who brings good news,[^2-1] who proclaims peace![^2-2] Celebrate your festivals, Judah; fulfill your vows. For never again will the wicked one[^2-3] pass through you; he is completely cut off.

**2** One who scatters[^2-4] has come up against you. Guard the fortress! Watch the road! Brace yourself![^2-5] Gather all your strength!

**3** For the LORD[^2-6] is restoring the pride of Jacob, like the pride of Israel, for plunderers have plundered them and ruined their vine branches.

**4** The shields of his warriors are red;[^2-7] his soldiers are clothed in scarlet. The chariots flash like fire[^2-8] on the day he prepares for battle, and the spears[^2-9] are brandished.

**5** The chariots race madly through the streets; they rush back and forth in the squares. They look like torches; they dart about like lightning.

**6** He summons his nobles;[^2-10] they stumble as they go. They hurry to her wall, and the siege shield[^2-11] is set up.

**7** The gates of the rivers are opened,[^2-12] and the palace melts away.

**8** It is decided:[^2-13] she is stripped and carried away. Her slave women moan like the sound of doves and beat their breasts.

**9** Nineveh is like a pool of water whose waters are draining away.[^2-14] "Stop! Stop!" they cry, but no one turns back.

**10** Plunder the silver! Plunder the gold! There is no end to the treasure, the wealth of every kind of precious thing.

**11** Emptiness, desolation and ruin![^2-15] Hearts melt, knees give way, every waist trembles,[^2-16] and every face turns pale.[^2-17]

**12** Where is the lions' den now, the feeding place of the young lions, where the lion and the lioness went, and the lion's cub, with no one to make them afraid?[^2-18]

**13** The lion tore enough for his cubs and strangled prey for his lionesses. He filled his caves with prey and his dens with torn flesh.

**14** Look, I am against you, declares the LORD of Armies.[^2-19] I will burn your chariots[^2-20] in smoke, and the sword will devour your young lions. I will cut off your prey from the earth, and the voice of your messengers[^2-21] will be heard no more.

[^2-1]: Hebrew *mevasser*, from *basar*, "bring good news", as in Isaiah 40:9 and 52:7.
[^2-2]: Verse 1 is 1:15 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–14 are 2:1–13. The first line is close to Isaiah 52:7, which Paul quotes in Romans 10:15.
[^2-3]: Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthlessness" (1:11), here the Assyrian enemy.
[^2-4]: Or "a shatterer". Some scholars read *mappets*, "a war club" (Jeremiah 51:20), with a small change to the Hebrew. The commands that follow are spoken to Nineveh, in mockery.
[^2-5]: Literally "Strengthen your loins".
[^2-6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2-7]: The attacking army, the Babylonians and the Medes. Their shields may have been painted red or covered with red leather.
[^2-8]: Literally "with the fire of *peladot* are the chariots". The Hebrew word *peladot* is found only here, and its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "steel" or "torches".
[^2-9]: Literally "the cypresses", spears with shafts of cypress wood. The Septuagint reads "the horsemen".
[^2-10]: Literally "He remembers his mighty ones". He may be the king of Nineveh or the commander of the attackers.
[^2-11]: Hebrew *sokhekh*, a shelter that protected soldiers attacking a city wall.
[^2-12]: Perhaps the sluice gates of the canals and rivers around Nineveh, or the city gates by the river.
[^2-13]: The meaning of the first words is uncertain. The older English translations took the Hebrew *hutsav*, "it is fixed", as the name of a queen, Huzzab.
[^2-14]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Nineveh is like a pool of water from the days that she is, and they are fleeing". The Septuagint reads "Nineveh is like a pool of water; her waters …".
[^2-15]: Hebrew *buqah u-mevuqah u-mevullaqah*, three words of similar sound and meaning.
[^2-16]: Literally "there is anguish in all loins".
[^2-17]: Literally "all faces gather a glow". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain (compare Joel 2:6).
[^2-18]: The kings of Assyria compared themselves to lions and were proud of hunting them. Nineveh is pictured as their den, filled with prey from the nations.
[^2-19]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^2-20]: Literally "her chariots". The Hebrew moves between "you" and "her".
[^2-21]: The envoys of Assyria, who carried its demands to subject peoples (2 Kings 18:17–35; 19:9–13).

## Chapter 3

*Status: draft*

**1** Woe to[^3-1] the city of bloodshed, full of lies, full of plunder, never without prey!

**2** The crack of the whip, the rumble of the wheel, the galloping horse and the jolting chariot!

**3** Horsemen charging, flashing swords and glittering spears! Many slain, heaps of corpses, bodies without end! They stumble over the bodies.

**4** All because of the endless prostitution of the prostitute, so charming, the mistress of sorceries, who sells nations by her prostitution and peoples[^3-2] by her sorceries.[^3-3]

**5** Look, I am against you, declares the LORD of Armies.[^3-4] I will lift your skirts over your face. I will show the nations your nakedness, and the kingdoms your shame.[^3-5]

**6** I will throw filth[^3-6] on you, treat you with contempt, and make you a spectacle.

**7** Everyone who sees you will run from you and say, "Nineveh is devastated! Who will mourn for her?" Where can I find anyone to comfort you?[^3-7]

**8** Are you better than Thebes,[^3-8] which sat by the Nile, with water all around her, whose rampart was the sea, whose wall was the water?[^3-9]

**9** Cush and Egypt were her strength, without limit; Put and the Libyans were among her helpers.[^3-10]

**10** Yet she too went into exile; she went into captivity. Her infants too were dashed to pieces at the head of every street. They cast lots for her nobles, and all her great men were bound in chains.

**11** You too will become drunk;[^3-11] you will go into hiding.[^3-12] You too will seek refuge from the enemy.

**12** All your fortresses are like fig trees with the first-ripe figs: if they are shaken, the figs fall into the mouth of the eater.

**13** Look, your troops are women in your midst.[^3-13] The gates of your land are wide open to your enemies; fire has devoured your gate bars.

**14** Draw water for the siege! Strengthen your fortresses! Go into the clay and tread the mortar! Take hold of the brick mold![^3-14]

**15** There the fire will devour you; the sword will cut you off. It will devour you as the hopping locust does.[^3-15] Multiply like the hopping locust! Multiply like the swarming locust!

**16** You have increased your merchants more than the stars of the sky. The hopping locust strips the land[^3-16] and flies away.

**17** Your guards are like the swarming locust, your officials[^3-17] like swarms of locusts that settle on the walls on a cold day. When the sun rises, they fly away, and no one knows where they are.

**18** Your shepherds are asleep, king of Assyria; your nobles lie down to rest.[^3-18] Your people are scattered on the mountains, and there is no one to gather them.

**19** There is no relief for your injury; your wound is fatal. All who hear the news about you clap their hands over you, for who has not felt your endless cruelty?[^3-19]

[^3-1]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to".
[^3-2]: Literally "clans".
[^3-3]: Nineveh is pictured as a prostitute and a sorceress, who lured nations into her power and sold them into slavery.
[^3-4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^3-5]: The public shaming of an unfaithful woman (Jeremiah 13:22, 26; Ezekiel 16:37).
[^3-6]: Hebrew *shiqqutsim*, "detestable things", a word often used of idols.
[^3-7]: *Comfort* (*nacham*) plays on the name Nahum, "comfort" (1:1).
[^3-8]: Hebrew *No-amon*, "city of Amon", the great city of Upper Egypt, sacked by the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal in 663 BC.
[^3-9]: Literally "whose rampart was the sea, from the sea her wall". *The sea* and *the Nile* (literally "the Niles", its channels) are the great river.
[^3-10]: Literally "were your help". Cush, the land on the upper Nile, ruled Egypt at the time. Put was perhaps a region of Libya.
[^3-11]: Drunk with the cup of the LORD's anger (Obadiah 16; Jeremiah 25:15–29).
[^3-12]: Or "you will faint". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^3-13]: That is, weak and unable to fight (Isaiah 19:16; Jeremiah 50:37). The Hebrew is feminine throughout verses 11–17, addressing Nineveh.
[^3-14]: Or "the brick kiln". The walls of Nineveh were built of sun-dried brick. The commands are mockery: no preparations will save the city.
[^3-15]: Hebrew *yeleq*, the "hopping locust" of Joel 1:4. Also in verse 16. The *swarming locust* is *arbeh*, the usual word for locust (also in verse 17). In verse 17, *swarms of locusts* is Hebrew *gov govai*.
[^3-16]: Or "sheds its skin". *The land* is added.
[^3-17]: Hebrew *minnezarayikh* and *tafsarayikh*, both rare words for officials, perhaps borrowed from the Assyrian language (Jeremiah 51:27).
[^3-18]: The rulers of Assyria are asleep in death. *Lie down to rest* is literally "dwell". Some scholars read "sleep", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^3-19]: Literally "for on whom has your evil not passed continually?" Like the book of Jonah, which also concerns Nineveh, Nahum ends with a question.
