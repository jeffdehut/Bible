## Chapter 1

*Status: draft*

**1** The words of Nehemiah son of Hacaliah.[^1-1] In the month of Kislev,[^1-2] in the twentieth year,[^1-3] I was in the citadel of Susa,[^1-4]

**2** when Hanani, one of my brothers, came with some men from Judah. I asked them about the Jews who had escaped, those who had survived the captivity, and about Jerusalem.

**3** They said to me, "The survivors who are left from the captivity there in the province are in great trouble and disgrace. The wall of Jerusalem is broken down, and its gates have been burned with fire."

**4** When I heard these words, I sat down and wept, and I mourned for days. I kept fasting and praying before the God of heaven.

**5** I said, "Please, Yahweh,[^1-5] God of heaven, the great and awesome God, who keeps covenant and faithful love[^1-6] with those who love him and keep his commands,[^1-7]

**6** let your ear be attentive and your eyes open, to hear the prayer of your servant that I am praying before you today, day and night, for the Israelites your servants. I confess the sins of the Israelites, which we have committed against you. Both I and my father's house have sinned.

**7** We have acted very corruptly against you, and we have not kept the commands, the decrees and the rulings that you commanded your servant Moses.

**8** Remember the word that you commanded your servant Moses: 'If you are unfaithful, I will scatter you among the peoples.[^1-8]

**9** But if you return to me and keep my commands and do them, even if your exiles are at the far edge of the heavens, I will gather them from there and bring them to the place that I have chosen to make my name dwell there.'[^1-9]

**10** They are your servants and your people, whom you redeemed by your great power and your strong hand.

**11** Please, Lord, let your ear be attentive to the prayer of your servant, and to the prayer of your servants who delight to fear your name. Give your servant success today, and grant that he may find compassion before this man." Now I was cupbearer[^1-10] to the king.

[^1-1]: In the Hebrew Bible, Ezra and Nehemiah are counted as a single book. Most of Nehemiah is told by Nehemiah himself, in the first person.
[^1-2]: The ninth month, around November–December.
[^1-3]: The twentieth year of King Artaxerxes I (2:1), 445 BC.
[^1-4]: Hebrew *Shushan ha-birah*. Susa, in what is now southwest Iran, was one of the royal cities of the Persian kings.
[^1-5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^1-6]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^1-7]: Compare Deuteronomy 7:9, 21; Daniel 9:4.
[^1-8]: Compare Leviticus 26:33; Deuteronomy 4:27; 28:64.
[^1-9]: Compare Deuteronomy 30:1–5; 12:5, 11.
[^1-10]: Hebrew *mashqeh*, the official who served the king his wine: a position of great trust and closeness to the king.

## Chapter 2

*Status: draft*

**1** In the month of Nisan,[^2-1] in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, when wine was set before him, I took up the wine and gave it to the king. I had not been sad in his presence before.[^2-2]

**2** So the king said to me, "Why is your face sad, when you are not ill? This can only be sadness of heart." Then I was very much afraid.

**3** I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why should my face not be sad, when the city where my ancestors are buried lies in ruins, and its gates have been destroyed by fire?"[^2-3]

**4** The king said to me, "What is it you are asking for?" So I prayed to the God of heaven,

**5** and I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor with you, send me to Judah, to the city where my ancestors are buried, so that I may rebuild it."

**6** Then the king, with the queen[^2-4] sitting beside him, said to me, "How long will your journey take, and when will you return?" So it pleased the king to send me, and I gave him a set time.

**7** I also said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the governors of the province Beyond the River,[^2-5] so that they will let me pass through until I come to Judah,

**8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the king's forest,[^2-6] so that he will give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress that belongs to the temple,[^2-7] for the wall of the city and for the house that I will live in." And the king granted me these, because the good hand of my God was on me.

**9** Then I came to the governors of the province Beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. The king had sent army officers and horsemen with me.

**10** When Sanballat the Horonite[^2-8] and Tobiah the Ammonite official[^2-9] heard of this, it displeased them greatly[^2-10] that someone had come to seek the good of the Israelites.

**11** So I came to Jerusalem, and I was there for three days.

**12** Then I got up in the night, I and a few men with me. I had not told anyone what my God was putting in my heart to do for Jerusalem. There was no animal with me except the animal I was riding.

**13** I went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon Spring[^2-11] and to the Dung Gate, and I inspected the walls of Jerusalem, which had been broken down, and its gates, which had been destroyed by fire.

**14** Then I went on to the Fountain Gate and to the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.

**15** So I went up through the valley[^2-12] by night and inspected the wall. Then I turned back and came in through the Valley Gate, and so I returned.

**16** The officials[^2-13] did not know where I had gone or what I was doing. I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles,[^2-14] the officials or the rest who would do the work.

**17** Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we will no longer be a disgrace."

**18** I told them how the hand of my God had been good to me, and also what the king had said to me. They said, "Let us get up and build!" So they strengthened their hands for the good work.[^2-15]

**19** But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite official and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and despised us. They said, "What is this thing you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**20** I answered them, "The God of heaven will give us success, and we his servants will get up and build. But you have no share, no claim and no memorial in Jerusalem."[^2-16]

[^2-1]: The first month, around March–April, four months after the events of 1:1.
[^2-2]: Or "I had never been sad in his presence".
[^2-3]: Literally "have been eaten by fire". Also in verse 13.
[^2-4]: Hebrew *shegal*, the king's consort.
[^2-5]: Hebrew *ever ha-nahar*, "beyond the river": the Persian province west of the Euphrates, which included Syria, Phoenicia and Judah. Also in verse 9.
[^2-6]: Hebrew *pardes*, a word borrowed from Persian for a walled park or royal forest. The English word "paradise" comes from it.
[^2-7]: Literally "the house". Hebrew *birah*: a fortress or citadel, here guarding the temple (compare 7:2).
[^2-8]: Probably from Beth-horon, northwest of Jerusalem. Documents from Elephantine in Egypt show that Sanballat was governor of Samaria.
[^2-9]: Literally "the servant", probably a title for an official of the Persian king. Also in verse 19.
[^2-10]: Literally "it was evil to them, a great evil".
[^2-11]: Or "the Serpent's Spring". Hebrew *Ein ha-tannin*.
[^2-12]: Hebrew *nachal*, a valley with a stream that runs in the rainy season: here the Kidron valley, east of the city.
[^2-13]: Hebrew *seganim*, a title borrowed from Akkadian: local officials or magistrates. Also later in the verse.
[^2-14]: Hebrew *chorim*, literally "free men": the leading families.
[^2-15]: Literally "for the good".
[^2-16]: Hebrew *tsedaqah*, "righteousness", here a legal right. Their "memorial" would be a place in the city's memory.

## Chapter 3

**1** Then Eliashib the high priest and his fellow priests set to work and built the Sheep Gate.[^3-1] They consecrated it and set up its doors. They consecrated it as far as the Tower of the Hundred, as far as the Tower of Hananel.

**2** Next to him[^3-2] the men of Jericho built, and next to him Zaccur son of Imri built.

**3** The descendants of Hassenaah[^3-3] built the Fish Gate. They laid its beams and set up its doors, its bolts and its bars.

**4** Next to them Meremoth son of Uriah, son of Hakkoz, made repairs.[^3-4] Next to them Meshullam son of Berechiah, son of Meshezabel, made repairs. Next to them Zadok son of Baana made repairs.

**5** Next to them the men of Tekoa made repairs, but their nobles would not put their necks to the work of their lords.[^3-5]

**6** Joiada son of Paseah and Meshullam son of Besodeiah repaired the Old Gate.[^3-6] They laid its beams and set up its doors, its bolts and its bars.

**7** Next to them Melatiah the Gibeonite and Jadon the Meronothite, the men of Gibeon and Mizpah, made repairs, for the seat of the governor of the province Beyond the River.[^3-7]

**8** Next to him Uzziel son of Harhaiah, one of the goldsmiths,[^3-8] made repairs. Next to him Hananiah, one of the perfumers,[^3-9] made repairs. They restored[^3-10] Jerusalem as far as the Broad Wall.

**9** Next to them Rephaiah son of Hur, ruler of half the district[^3-11] of Jerusalem, made repairs.

**10** Next to them Jedaiah son of Harumaph made repairs opposite his house. Next to him Hattush son of Hashabneiah made repairs.

**11** Malchijah son of Harim and Hasshub son of Pahath-moab repaired another section and the Tower of the Ovens.

**12** Next to him Shallum son of Hallohesh, ruler of half the district of Jerusalem, made repairs, he and his daughters.

**13** Hanun and the inhabitants of Zanoah repaired the Valley Gate. They rebuilt it and set up its doors, its bolts and its bars, and they repaired a thousand cubits[^3-12] of the wall, as far as the Dung Gate.

**14** Malchijah son of Rechab, ruler of the district of Beth-haccherem, repaired the Dung Gate. He rebuilt it and set up its doors, its bolts and its bars.

**15** Shallun son of Col-hozeh, ruler of the district of Mizpah, repaired the Fountain Gate. He rebuilt it, roofed it, and set up[^3-13] its doors, its bolts and its bars. He also repaired the wall of the Pool of Shelah[^3-14] by the king's garden, as far as the steps that go down from the City of David.

**16** After him Nehemiah son of Azbuk, ruler of half the district of Beth-zur, made repairs as far as a point opposite the tombs of David, as far as the artificial pool[^3-15] and the House of the Warriors.[^3-16]

**17** After him the Levites made repairs: Rehum son of Bani. Next to him Hashabiah, ruler of half the district of Keilah, made repairs for his district.

**18** After him their kinsmen made repairs: Bavvai son of Henadad,[^3-17] ruler of half the district of Keilah.

**19** Next to him Ezer son of Jeshua, ruler of Mizpah, repaired another section, opposite the ascent to the armory at the angle.[^3-18]

**20** After him Baruch son of Zaccai[^3-19] eagerly[^3-20] repaired another section, from the angle to the door of the house of Eliashib the high priest.

**21** After him Meremoth son of Uriah, son of Hakkoz, repaired another section, from the door of Eliashib's house to the end of Eliashib's house.

**22** After him the priests, the men of the surrounding region,[^3-21] made repairs.

**23** After him Benjamin and Hasshub made repairs opposite their house. After him Azariah son of Maaseiah, son of Ananiah, made repairs beside his house.

**24** After him Binnui son of Henadad repaired another section, from the house of Azariah to the angle and to the corner.

**25** Palal son of Uzai made repairs opposite the angle and the tower that projects from the upper house of the king, at the court of the guard.[^3-22] After him Pedaiah son of Parosh made repairs.

**26** The temple servants[^3-23] were living on the Ophel,[^3-24] as far as a point opposite the Water Gate on the east and the projecting tower.[^3-25]

**27** After him the men of Tekoa repaired another section, from opposite the great projecting tower as far as the wall of the Ophel.

**28** Above the Horse Gate the priests made repairs, each one opposite his own house.

**29** After them Zadok son of Immer made repairs opposite his house. After him Shemaiah son of Shecaniah, the keeper of the East Gate, made repairs.

**30** After him[^3-26] Hananiah son of Shelemiah and Hanun, the sixth son of Zalaph, repaired another section. After him Meshullam son of Berechiah made repairs opposite his room.

**31** After him Malchijah, one of the goldsmiths, made repairs as far as the house of the temple servants and the merchants, opposite the Muster Gate,[^3-27] and as far as the upper room at the corner.

**32** And between the upper room at the corner and the Sheep Gate the goldsmiths and the merchants made repairs.

**33** When Sanballat heard that we were building the wall, he was angry and greatly enraged, and he mocked the Jews.[^3-28]

**34** He said in the presence of his associates[^3-29] and the army of Samaria, "What are these feeble Jews doing? Will they restore it for themselves?[^3-30] Will they offer sacrifices? Will they finish it in a day? Will they bring the stones back to life out of the heaps of rubble, burned as they are?"

**35** Tobiah the Ammonite was beside him, and he said, "Even what they are building, if a fox went up on it, it would break down their stone wall!"

**36** Hear us, our God, for we are despised. Turn their insults back on their own heads, and give them up to be plundered in a land where they are captives.

**37** Do not cover their guilt, and do not let their sin be wiped out from before you, for they have provoked you to anger in front of the builders.[^3-31]

**38** So we built the wall, and the whole wall was joined together up to half its height,[^3-32] for the people had a heart to work.

[^3-11]: Hebrew *pelekh*, a district or administrative region. Also in verses 12 and 14–18.

[^3-14]: Or "the Pool of Siloam" (compare Isaiah 8:6; John 9:7).

## Chapter 4

*Status: draft*

**1** When Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites and the people of Ashdod heard that the repair of the walls of Jerusalem was going ahead,[^4-1] and that the gaps were beginning to be closed, they were very angry.

**2** They all plotted together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to cause confusion in it.

**3** But we prayed to our God, and we set a guard against them day and night because of them.

**4** Meanwhile Judah said, "The strength of the burden carriers is failing, and there is so much rubble. We cannot build the wall."

**5** And our enemies said, "They will not know or see anything until we come into their midst and kill them and put a stop to the work."

**6** When the Jews who lived near them came, they told us ten times over,[^4-2] "From every place where you turn, they will be upon us."[^4-3]

**7** So I stationed people in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall, in the open places. I stationed the people by families, with their swords, their spears and their bows.

**8** I looked things over, then stood up and said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "Do not be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and fight for your brothers, your sons and your daughters, your wives and your homes."

**9** When our enemies heard that we knew of it, and that God had frustrated their plan, we all returned[^4-4] to the wall, each to his own work.

**10** From that day on, half of my servants did the work, while the other half held the spears, the shields, the bows and the coats of armor. The officers stood behind the whole house of Judah.

**11** Those who were building the wall, and those who carried the loads as they loaded them,[^4-5] each did the work with one hand and held a weapon[^4-6] with the other.

**12** Each of the builders had his sword strapped at his side as he built. The man who blew the horn[^4-7] stayed beside me.

**13** I said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "The work is great and spread out, and we are separated along the wall, far from one another.

**14** Wherever you hear the sound of the horn, gather to us there. Our God will fight for us."

**15** So we did the work, while half of them held spears, from the break of dawn until the stars came out.

**16** At that time I also said to the people, "Let every man and his servant spend the night inside Jerusalem, so that they can guard us by night and work by day."

**17** So neither I nor my brothers nor my servants nor the men of the guard who followed me took off our clothes. Each had his weapon, even at the water.[^4-8]

[^4-1]: Literally "that new flesh had grown on the walls of Jerusalem", the image of a wound healing. Verses 1–17 are 4:7–23 in most English Bibles.
[^4-2]: That is, again and again.
[^4-3]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "from all the places that you will return to us". It may be an urgent plea, "You must come back to us", or a warning that the enemy would attack from every side.
[^4-4]: The Hebrew text is written "and we will return" but read "and we returned".
[^4-5]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^4-6]: Hebrew *shelach*, a weapon, perhaps a spear or javelin.
[^4-7]: Hebrew *shofar*, a ram's horn, used to give signals. Also in verse 14.
[^4-8]: The Hebrew is obscure. Literally "each his weapon, the water". It may mean that each man kept his weapon with him even when he went to get water.

## Chapter 5

*Status: draft*

**1** Now there was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.[^5-1]

**2** Some were saying, "We, our sons and our daughters, are many. Let us get grain, so that we can eat and stay alive."

**3** Others were saying, "We are mortgaging our fields, our vineyards and our houses to get grain because of the famine."

**4** Still others were saying, "We have borrowed money for the king's tax on our fields and our vineyards.[^5-2]

**5** Now our flesh is the same as the flesh of our brothers, and our children are the same as their children. Yet here we are, forcing our sons and our daughters to become slaves. Some of our daughters have already been forced into it, and we are powerless,[^5-3] because our fields and our vineyards belong to others."

**6** I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these words.

**7** I thought it over,[^5-4] and then I brought charges against the nobles and the officials. I said to them, "You are lending at interest,[^5-5] each to his own brother!" And I called a great assembly against them.

**8** I said to them, "As far as we were able, we have bought back our fellow Jews who had been sold to the nations. But now you are selling your own brothers, so that they can be sold to us!" They were silent and could not find a word to say.

**9** I also said,[^5-6] "What you are doing is not right. Should you not walk in the fear of our God, to avoid the taunts of the nations, our enemies?

**10** I too, and my brothers and my servants, have been lending them money and grain. Let us give up this lending at interest.

**11** Give back to them this very day their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves and their houses, and the hundredth part[^5-7] of the money, the grain, the new wine and the olive oil that you have been exacting from them."

**12** They said, "We will give it back, and we will demand nothing from them. We will do just as you say." Then I called the priests and made them swear to do as they had promised.[^5-8]

**13** I also shook out the fold of my robe and said, "So may God shake out of his house and out of the fruit of his labor every man who does not keep this promise. So may he be shaken out and emptied!" And the whole assembly said, "Amen,"[^5-9] and they praised Yahweh.[^5-10] And the people did as they had promised.

**14** Moreover, from the day I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes,[^5-11] twelve years, neither I nor my brothers ate the food allowance of the governor.

**15** The former governors who were before me laid heavy burdens on the people and took from them food and wine, besides forty shekels[^5-12] of silver. Even their servants lorded it over the people. But I did not do so, because of the fear of God.

**16** I also devoted myself to the work on this wall, and we did not acquire any land.[^5-13] All my servants were gathered there for the work.

**17** Moreover, there were at my table the Jews and the officials, 150 men, besides those who came to us from the nations around us.

**18** What was prepared for each day was one ox and six choice sheep, and birds were prepared for me, and once every ten days plenty of wine of every kind. Yet with all this I did not demand the governor's food allowance, because the work weighed heavily on this people.

**19** Remember me for good, my God, for all that I have done for this people.

[^5-1]: Literally "against their brothers the Jews".
[^5-2]: Or "We have borrowed money against our fields and our vineyards to pay the king's tax".
[^5-3]: Literally "there is no power in our hand".
[^5-4]: Literally "My heart took counsel within me".
[^5-5]: Or "taking pledges". The meaning of Hebrew *masha* is uncertain: a loan, the interest on it, or a pledge given for it. Also in verse 10.
[^5-6]: The Hebrew text is written "he said" but read "I said".
[^5-7]: Probably the interest charged, one percent a month.
[^5-8]: Literally "made them swear to do according to this word", probably the nobles and officials. Also "promise" in verse 13.
[^5-9]: *Amen* means "So be it", "It is true".
[^5-10]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5-11]: From 445 to 433 BC.
[^5-12]: A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so forty shekels was about 1 pound (460 grams). The meaning may be "forty shekels of silver a day".
[^5-13]: Literally "a field". That is, he did not take over the land of those in debt.

## Chapter 6

*Status: draft*

**1** Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem the Arab and the rest of our enemies heard that I had rebuilt the wall and that no gap was left in it, though up to that time I had not yet set up the doors in the gates.

**2** Then Sanballat and Geshem sent me this message: "Come, let us meet together in one of the villages[^6-1] in the plain of Ono." But they were planning to do me harm.

**3** So I sent messengers to them, saying, "I am doing a great work, and I cannot come down. Why should the work stop while I leave it and come down to you?"

**4** They sent me the same message four times, and I gave them the same answer.

**5** Then Sanballat sent his servant to me with the same message a fifth time, with an open letter in his hand.

**6** In it was written: "It is reported among the nations, and Gashmu[^6-2] says so too, that you and the Jews are planning to rebel. That is why you are building the wall. And according to these reports, you are about to become their king.

**7** You have even appointed prophets to proclaim about you in Jerusalem, 'There is a king in Judah!' Now this report will reach the king. So come now, and let us take counsel together."

**8** I sent him this reply: "Nothing like what you are saying has happened. You are making it up out of your own head."[^6-3]

**9** For they were all trying to frighten us, thinking, "Their hands will drop from the work, and it will not be done." But now, strengthen my hands![^6-4]

**10** I went to the house of Shemaiah son of Delaiah, son of Mehetabel, who was confined at home.[^6-5] He said, "Let us meet at the house of God, inside the temple,[^6-6] and let us shut the doors of the temple, for they are coming to kill you. They are coming to kill you tonight."

**11** But I said, "Should a man like me run away? And can a man like me go into the temple and live?[^6-7] I will not go in."

**12** Then I realized that God had not sent him. He had spoken this prophecy against me because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him.

**13** He had been hired for this purpose: to frighten me, so that I would do as he said and sin, and they would have a reason to give me a bad name, so that they could taunt me.

**14** Remember, my God, Tobiah and Sanballat for these deeds of theirs, and also Noadiah the prophetess and the rest of the prophets who were trying to frighten me.

**15** So the wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of Elul,[^6-8] in fifty-two days.

**16** When all our enemies heard of it, all the nations around us were afraid[^6-9] and lost their confidence,[^6-10] for they knew that this work had been done by our God.

**17** In those days too, the nobles of Judah were sending many letters to Tobiah, and Tobiah's letters were coming to them.

**18** For many in Judah were bound to him by oath, because he was the son-in-law of Shecaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah.

**19** They also kept telling me about his good deeds and reporting my words to him. And Tobiah sent letters to frighten me.

[^6-1]: Or "at Chephirim". Hebrew *kephirim* may mean "villages" or be the name of a place.
