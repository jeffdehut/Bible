**1** After this I saw another angel coming down from heaven, with great authority, and the earth was lit up by his glory.[^1]

**2** He called out with a mighty voice, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great![^2] She has become a dwelling place of demons, a haunt[^3] for every unclean spirit, a haunt for every unclean bird, and a haunt for every unclean beast,[^4] unclean and hated.[^5]

**3** For all the nations have fallen[^6] because of the wine of the passion of her sexual immorality, and the kings of the earth have committed sexual immorality with her, and the merchants of the earth have grown rich from the power of her luxury."[^7]

**4** Then I heard another voice from heaven saying, "Come out of her, my people,[^8] so that you do not take part in her sins, and so that you do not receive any of her plagues.

**5** For her sins are piled up to heaven,[^9] and God has remembered her crimes.

**6** Pay her back as she herself has paid others,[^10] and repay her double for her works. In the cup she mixed, mix her a double portion.[^11]

**7** As much as she glorified herself and lived in luxury, give her that much torment and grief. For in her heart she says, 'I sit as a queen. I am no widow, and I will never see grief.'[^12]

**8** That is why her plagues will come in a single day: death and grief and famine. And she will be burned up with fire, for mighty is the Lord God who judges her.[^13]

**9** The kings of the earth who committed sexual immorality and lived in luxury with her will weep and mourn over her when they see the smoke of her burning.[^14]

**10** They will stand far off, in fear of her torment, and say, 'Woe, woe,[^15] you great city, Babylon, you mighty city! In one hour your judgment has come.'

**11** And the merchants of the earth weep and mourn over her, because no one buys their cargo anymore:[^16]

**12** cargo of gold, silver, precious stones and pearls; fine linen, purple, silk and scarlet; all kinds of citron wood,[^17] all kinds of articles of ivory, all kinds of articles of costly wood, bronze, iron and marble;

**13** cinnamon, spice,[^18] incense, myrrh[^19] and frankincense; wine, olive oil, fine flour and wheat; cattle and sheep; horses and carriages; and slaves, that is, human lives.[^20]

**14** The fruit that your soul longed for has gone from you, and all your luxury and your splendor have perished from you, and they will never be found again.[^21]

**15** The merchants of these things, who grew rich from her, will stand far off in fear of her torment, weeping and mourning,

**16** and saying, 'Woe, woe, the great city, which was dressed in fine linen and purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and precious stones and pearls!

**17** For in one hour such great wealth has been laid waste!' And every ship's captain and every passenger,[^22] and sailors, and all who make their living from the sea, stood far off

**18** and cried out as they saw the smoke of her burning, 'What city was like the great city?'[^23]

**19** And they threw dust on their heads[^24] and cried out, weeping and mourning, 'Woe, woe, the great city, where all who had ships at sea grew rich from her wealth! For in one hour she has been laid waste.'

**20** Rejoice over her, heaven, and you, God's holy people[^25] and apostles and prophets, for God has given judgment for you against her."[^26]

**21** Then a mighty angel picked up a stone like a great millstone and threw it into the sea, saying, "With such violence Babylon the great city will be thrown down, and will never be found again.[^27]

**22** The sound of harpists and musicians, of flute players and trumpeters, will never be heard in you again. No craftsman of any craft will ever be found in you again. The sound of the millstone will never be heard in you again.

**23** The light of a lamp will never shine in you again. The voice of bridegroom and bride will never be heard in you again.[^28] For your merchants were the great ones of the earth,[^29] and all the nations were deceived by your sorcery.

**24** And in her was found the blood of prophets and of God's holy people, and of all who have been slaughtered on the earth."[^30]

[^1]: Compare Ezekiel 43:2, "and the earth shone with his glory".
[^2]: See 14:8; Isaiah 21:9. Many manuscripts have "Fallen" only once.
[^3]: Or "a prison"; also later in the verse.
[^4]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28; other editions leave out "a haunt for every unclean beast".
[^5]: Compare Isaiah 13:21–22; 34:11–15; Jeremiah 50:39; 51:37, where wild creatures haunt the ruins of Babylon and Edom.
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "have drunk". *Passion*: see the note on 14:8.
[^7]: Or "the power of her self-indulgence", her excessive wealth and display.
[^8]: Jeremiah 51:45, "Go out from her midst, my people!"; compare Isaiah 48:20; 52:11; Jeremiah 50:8; 51:6.
[^9]: Compare Jeremiah 51:9, "her judgment reaches to the heavens".
[^10]: Compare Psalm 137:8; Jeremiah 50:29, "Repay her for her deeds; do to her just as she has done."
[^11]: Literally "double the double according to her works; in the cup in which she mixed, mix for her double". Compare Jeremiah 16:18.
[^12]: Compare Isaiah 47:7–8, "I will be queen forever … I will never sit as a widow".
[^13]: Compare Isaiah 47:9, "Both of these will come on you in a moment, in a single day"; Jeremiah 50:34, "But their Redeemer is strong".
[^14]: Verses 9–19 draw on the lament over Tyre in Ezekiel 26–27.
[^15]: Greek *ouai*, a cry of grief and warning, like the prophets' *hoy* ("woe"); also in verses 16 and 19.
[^16]: Compare Ezekiel 27:12–24, the list of Tyre's trade.
[^17]: A fragrant wood from North Africa, prized for fine furniture.
[^18]: Greek *amomon*, a spice plant from India. Many manuscripts leave out "spice".
[^19]: Or "perfume". Greek *myron*, "perfumed ointment".
[^20]: Literally "and bodies, and souls of human beings". Greek *somata*, "bodies", was a common term for slaves. Compare Ezekiel 27:13, "They gave human beings … for your merchandise."
[^21]: Literally "and they will never find them again". Many manuscripts read "and you will never find them again".
[^22]: Literally "everyone who sails to a place"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^23]: Compare Ezekiel 27:32, "Who was like Tyre, like one silenced in the middle of the sea?"
[^24]: Compare Ezekiel 27:30, "They will throw dust on their heads".
[^25]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "the saints"; also in verse 24.
[^26]: Literally "God has judged your judgment from her". It is not certain where the voice of verse 4 stops speaking; this translation takes verses 4–20 as its words. Compare Jeremiah 51:48, "Then heaven and earth and all that is in them will shout for joy over Babylon".
[^27]: Compare Jeremiah 51:63–64, "tie a stone to it and throw it into the middle of the Euphrates. Then say, 'So will Babylon sink and never rise again'".
[^28]: Compare Jeremiah 25:10, "the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride, the sound of the millstones and the light of the lamp"; Ezekiel 26:13.
[^29]: Compare Isaiah 23:8, of Tyre, "whose merchants are princes".
[^30]: Compare Jeremiah 51:49, "as the slain of all the earth have fallen because of Babylon".
