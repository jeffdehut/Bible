**1** Then I was given a measuring reed like a staff,[^1] and I was told,[^2] "Get up and measure the temple of God and the altar, and those who worship there.

**2** But leave out the court outside the temple; do not measure it, for it has been given to the nations,[^3] and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months.[^4]

**3** And I will give authority to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy for 1,260 days, dressed in sackcloth."[^5]

**4** These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth.[^6]

**5** If anyone wants to harm them, fire comes out of their mouths and consumes their enemies.[^7] If anyone wants to harm them, this is how they must be killed.

**6** They have authority to shut the sky, so that no rain falls during the days of their prophesying,[^8] and they have authority over the waters, to turn them into blood,[^9] and to strike the earth with every kind of plague, as often as they wish.

**7** When they have finished their testimony, the beast[^10] that comes up out of the abyss will make war on them, conquer them and kill them.

**8** Their dead bodies[^11] will lie in the street of the great city, which is spiritually[^12] called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified.

**9** For three and a half days, people from every people and tribe and language and nation look at their dead bodies, and they do not allow them to be placed in a tomb.

**10** Those who live on the earth rejoice over them and celebrate, and they will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who live on the earth.

**11** But after the three and a half days, the breath of life[^13] from God entered them, and they stood on their feet,[^14] and great fear fell on those who saw them.

**12** Then they heard[^15] a loud voice from heaven saying to them, "Come up here!" And they went up to heaven in the cloud, while their enemies watched them.

**13** At that hour[^16] there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell. Seven thousand people[^17] were killed in the earthquake, and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven.

**14** The second woe[^18] has passed. Look, the third woe is coming soon.

**15** Then the seventh angel blew his trumpet, and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Messiah,[^19] and he will reign forever and ever."[^20]

**16** Then the twenty-four elders who sit on their thrones before God fell on their faces and worshiped God,

**17** saying, "We give you thanks, Lord God Almighty, who is and who was,[^21] because you have taken your great power and have begun to reign.

**18** The nations raged,[^22] but your anger has come, and the time for the dead to be judged, and for rewarding your servants the prophets and your holy people[^23] and those who fear your name, both small and great,[^24] and for destroying those who destroy the earth."

**19** Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant[^25] was seen in his temple. And there were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, an earthquake[^26] and great hail.

[^1]: Greek *kalamos*, "reed", here a measuring rod. Compare Ezekiel 40:3, "a measuring reed in his hand"; Zechariah 2:5–6 (2:1–2 in most English Bibles).
[^2]: Literally "saying". The Greek does not say who speaks. It is unclear where the speech ends; it may continue to the end of verse 10.
[^3]: Or "the Gentiles".
[^4]: That is, three and a half years, the same length of time as the 1,260 days of verse 3 and 12:6, and as "a time, times and half a time" in 12:14 and Daniel 7:25; 12:7. Compare Luke 21:24.
[^5]: Rough cloth of goat's hair, worn in mourning and repentance.
[^6]: Zechariah 4:3, 11–14, "There are two olive trees beside it"; "These are the two anointed ones who stand by the Lord of the whole earth."
[^7]: Compare 2 Kings 1:10, where Elijah calls down fire on his enemies; Jeremiah 5:14, "I am going to make my words in your mouth a fire".
[^8]: Compare Elijah in 1 Kings 17:1, "there will be no dew or rain in these years except at my word".
[^9]: Compare Moses in Exodus 7:17–20.
[^10]: Greek *therion*, "wild animal, beast", the word used for the beasts of Daniel 7 in the Greek Old Testament. Compare Daniel 7:3, 21, where the horn "was making war against the holy ones and was prevailing over them". The beast appears again in 13:1 and later chapters.
[^11]: Literally "their corpse"; also in verse 9.
[^12]: Or "symbolically", "prophetically".
[^13]: Or "a spirit of life".
[^14]: Ezekiel 37:10, "the breath came into them. They came to life and stood on their feet".
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "Then I heard".
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "On that day".
[^17]: Literally "names of people, seven thousand".
[^18]: Greek *ouai*. See 8:13.
[^19]: Greek *tou christou autou*, "his Christ", "his Anointed One". Compare Psalm 2:2, "against Yahweh and against his anointed one". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" there is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^20]: Compare Exodus 15:18; Daniel 2:44; 7:14, 27.
[^21]: The title no longer includes "who is to come", as in 1:4, 8 and 4:8. *Almighty*: see 1:8.
[^22]: Compare Psalm 2:1, "Why do the nations rage"; 99:1, "Yahweh reigns! Let the peoples tremble!"
[^23]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "the saints".
[^24]: Compare Psalm 115:13, "He will bless those who fear Yahweh, the small together with the great."
[^25]: Many manuscripts read "the ark of the covenant of the Lord".
[^26]: Many manuscripts leave out "an earthquake".
