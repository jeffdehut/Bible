**1** Then I looked, and there was the Lamb, standing on Mount Zion,[^1] and with him 144,000 who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads.

**2** And I heard a sound from heaven like the sound of many waters and like the sound of loud thunder. The sound I heard was like harpists playing on their harps.

**3** They sang what seemed to be a new song[^2] before the throne and before the four living creatures and the elders. No one could learn the song except the 144,000 who had been bought from the earth.

**4** These are the ones who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are virgins.[^3] These are the ones who follow the Lamb wherever he goes. They have been bought[^4] from among humankind as firstfruits[^5] for God and for the Lamb,

**5** and no lie was found in their mouths;[^6] they are blameless.

**6** Then I saw another angel flying in midheaven, with eternal good news to proclaim to those who live[^7] on the earth, to every nation and tribe and language and people.

**7** He said with a loud voice, "Fear God[^8] and give him glory, for the hour of his judgment has come. Worship him who made heaven and earth, the sea and the springs of water."[^9]

**8** Another angel, a second, followed, saying, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great,[^10] who made all the nations drink the wine of the passion of her sexual immorality!"[^11]

**9** Another angel, a third, followed them, saying with a loud voice, "If anyone worships the beast and its image, and receives a mark on their forehead or on their hand,

**10** they too will drink the wine of God's fury, poured out undiluted[^12] into the cup of his anger.[^13] They will be tormented with fire and sulfur in the presence of the holy angels and in the presence of the Lamb.[^14]

**11** The smoke of their torment goes up forever and ever.[^15] There is no rest day or night for those who worship the beast and its image, and for anyone who receives the mark of its name."

**12** This calls for the endurance of God's holy people,[^16] those who keep the commands of God and their faith in Jesus.[^17]

**13** Then I heard a voice from heaven saying, "Write: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on." "Yes," says the Spirit, "they will rest from their labors, for their works go with them."[^18]

**14** Then I looked, and there was a white cloud, and seated on the cloud was one like a son of man,[^19] with a golden crown on his head and a sharp sickle in his hand.

**15** Another angel came out of the temple, calling with a loud voice to the one seated on the cloud, "Put in your sickle and reap, for the hour to reap has come, because the harvest of the earth is ripe."[^20]

**16** So the one seated on the cloud swung his sickle over the earth, and the earth was harvested.

**17** Another angel came out of the temple in heaven, and he too had a sharp sickle.

**18** Then another angel came from the altar,[^21] the one who has authority over fire, and he called with a loud voice to the one who had the sharp sickle, "Put in your sharp sickle and gather the clusters from the vine of the earth, for its grapes are ripe."

**19** So the angel swung his sickle over the earth and gathered the vintage of the earth, and he threw it into the great winepress of God's fury.[^22]

**20** The winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood flowed from the winepress as high as the horses' bridles, for a distance of 1,600 stadia.[^23]

[^1]: Compare Psalm 2:6, "I myself have installed my king on Zion, my holy mountain"; Joel 3:5 (2:32 in most English Bibles).
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "They sang a new song".
[^3]: Greek *parthenoi*, "virgins", here used of men. The image may draw on the rule that Israel's soldiers kept away from women while on campaign (1 Samuel 21:5–6; 21:4–5 in most English Bibles; 2 Samuel 11:11), or on the prophets' picture of idolatry as sexual unfaithfulness (compare 2:14, 20).
[^4]: Many manuscripts add "by Jesus".
[^5]: The first and best part of the harvest, which belonged to God (Exodus 23:19; compare Jeremiah 2:3).
[^6]: Compare Zephaniah 3:13, "and no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouths"; Isaiah 53:9.
[^7]: Literally "those who sit", that is, are settled.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "Fear the Lord".
[^9]: Compare Exodus 20:11, "For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea and everything in them"; Psalm 146:6.
[^10]: Isaiah 21:9, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon!"; compare Jeremiah 51:7–8 and Daniel 4:27 (4:30 in most English Bibles), "Is this not great Babylon". Many manuscripts have "Fallen" only once.
[^11]: Greek *thymos* means both "passion" and "fury", as in "God's fury" in verse 10. Compare Jeremiah 51:7, "The nations drank her wine; therefore the nations have gone mad."
[^12]: Literally "mixed undiluted". Wine was normally mixed with water.
[^13]: Compare Psalm 75:9 (75:8 in most English Bibles), "For in the hand of the LORD there is a cup, with foaming wine"; Isaiah 51:17; Jeremiah 25:15.
[^14]: Compare Genesis 19:24; Isaiah 34:9–10.
[^15]: Isaiah 34:10, "its smoke will go up forever".
[^16]: Literally "Here is the endurance of the holy ones", traditionally "the saints".
[^17]: Or "and the faith of Jesus", "faithfulness to Jesus".
[^18]: Or "Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord. From now on, yes, says the Spirit, they will rest …". *Go with them*: literally "follow with them".
[^19]: That is, one in human form. See 1:13; Daniel 7:13.
[^20]: Joel 4:13 (3:13 in most English Bibles), "Swing the sickle, for the harvest is ripe". *Is ripe*: literally "has dried", that is, is fully ripe.
[^21]: Literally "And another angel from the altar". The SBLGNT here follows the margin of Westcott and Hort; the other editions read "came out from the altar".
[^22]: Compare Isaiah 63:3, "I have trodden the winepress alone"; Lamentations 1:15; Joel 4:13 (3:13 in most English Bibles), "Come, tread the grapes, for the winepress is full."
[^23]: About 180 miles (300 kilometers). A *stadion* was about 600 feet (185 meters).
