**1** When he opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour.

**2** And I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them.

**3** Another angel came and stood at the altar, holding a golden censer.[^1] He was given a great quantity of incense to offer with the prayers of all God's holy people[^2] on the golden altar before the throne.

**4** The smoke of the incense went up with the prayers of God's holy people from the angel's hand before God.

**5** Then the angel took the censer, filled it with fire from the altar and threw it on the earth,[^3] and there were peals of thunder, rumblings, flashes of lightning and an earthquake.[^4]

**6** Then the seven angels who had the seven trumpets got ready to blow them.

**7** The first angel blew his trumpet, and there came hail and fire mixed with blood, and they were thrown on the earth.[^5] A third of the earth was burned up, a third of the trees were burned up, and all the green grass was burned up.

**8** The second angel blew his trumpet, and something like a great mountain burning with fire was thrown into the sea. A third of the sea became blood,[^6]

**9** a third of the creatures in the sea, those that had life, died, and a third of the ships were destroyed.

**10** The third angel blew his trumpet, and a great star, burning like a torch, fell from heaven. It fell on a third of the rivers and on the springs of water.

**11** The name of the star is Wormwood.[^7] A third of the waters became wormwood, and many people died from the waters, because they had been made bitter.

**12** The fourth angel blew his trumpet, and a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of them were darkened. For a third of the day there was no light, and likewise for a third of the night.[^8]

**13** Then I looked, and I heard an eagle[^9] flying in midheaven, saying with a loud voice, "Woe, woe, woe[^10] to those who live on the earth, because of the remaining trumpet blasts that the three angels are about to blow!"[^11]

[^1]: Or "a golden pan for incense". Greek *libanotos*, here the vessel in which incense was burned.
[^2]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "the saints"; also in verse 4. *To offer with the prayers*: literally "to give to the prayers".
[^3]: Compare Ezekiel 10:2, "Fill your hands with burning coals … and scatter them over the city"; Leviticus 16:12.
[^4]: Literally "thunders and voices and lightnings and an earthquake". See 4:5.
[^5]: Compare the seventh plague of Egypt, Exodus 9:23–24: "There was hail, and fire flashing in the middle of the hail."
[^6]: Compare the first plague of Egypt, Exodus 7:20–21.
[^7]: A plant with a bitter taste. Compare Jeremiah 9:14 (9:15 in most English Bibles); 23:15, "I am going to feed them wormwood and make them drink poisoned water."
[^8]: Compare the ninth plague of Egypt, darkness, Exodus 10:21–23.
[^9]: Literally "one eagle".
[^10]: Greek *ouai*, a cry of grief and warning, like the prophets' *hoy* ("woe"). The first two woes follow the fifth and sixth trumpets (9:12; 11:14).
[^11]: Literally "because of the remaining sounds of the trumpet of the three angels who are about to blow".
