**1** The revelation[^1] of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show his servants[^2] what must soon take place.[^3] He made it known[^4] by sending his angel to his servant John,

**2** who testified to the word of God and to the testimony of Jesus Christ,[^5] everything that he saw.

**3** Blessed are the one who reads aloud[^6] the words of this prophecy and those who hear them and keep what is written in it, for the time is near.[^7]

**4** John, to the seven churches in Asia:[^8] Grace to you and peace from him who is and who was and who is to come,[^9] and from the seven spirits[^10] who are before his throne,

**5** and from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead and the ruler of the kings of the earth.[^11] To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood,[^12]

**6** and has made us a kingdom, priests[^13] to his God and Father, to him be the glory and the power forever and ever. Amen.

**7** Look, he is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see him, even those who pierced him, and all the tribes of the earth will mourn over him.[^14] Yes, amen.

**8** "I am the Alpha and the Omega,"[^15] says the Lord God, "who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty."[^16]

**9** I, John, your brother, who share with you in the suffering[^17] and the kingdom and the endurance that are in Jesus,[^18] was on the island called Patmos[^19] because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus.

**10** I was in the Spirit[^20] on the Lord's day,[^21] and I heard behind me a loud voice like a trumpet,

**11** saying, "Write what you see in a scroll,[^22] and send it to the seven churches: to Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea."

**12** I turned to see whose voice was speaking to me,[^23] and when I turned I saw seven golden lampstands,

**13** and in the middle of the lampstands one like a son of man,[^24] dressed in a robe that reached to his feet, with a golden sash around his chest.[^25]

**14** His head and his hair were white like white wool, like snow, and his eyes were like a flame of fire.[^26]

**15** His feet were like fine bronze[^27] glowing in a furnace, and his voice was like the sound of many waters.[^28]

**16** In his right hand he held seven stars, and out of his mouth came a sharp two-edged sword,[^29] and his face was like the sun shining in its full strength.

**17** When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. He placed his right hand on me and said, "Do not be afraid. I am the first and the last,[^30]

**18** and the living one. I was dead, and look, I am alive forever and ever,[^31] and I hold the keys of death and of Hades.[^32]

**19** So write what you have seen, what is, and what is going to take place after this.[^33]

**20** As for the mystery of the seven stars that you saw in my right hand, and of the seven golden lampstands: the seven stars are the angels[^34] of the seven churches, and the seven lampstands are the seven churches.

[^1]: Greek *apokalypsis*, "unveiling, revelation", from which comes the book's other name, "the Apocalypse".
[^2]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*; also *doulos*, "servant", later in the verse.
[^3]: Compare Daniel 2:28–29, 45, "what will happen".
[^4]: Or "He made it known by signs". Greek *semaino*, "signify, indicate".
[^5]: Or "the testimony about Jesus Christ"; also in verse 9.
[^6]: Literally "the one who reads", that is, aloud to the gathered church.
[^7]: This is the first of seven sayings in Revelation that begin "Blessed" (14:13; 16:15; 19:9; 20:6; 22:7, 14).
[^8]: The Roman province of Asia, in the west of what is now Turkey.
[^9]: Greek *apo ho on*, "from he who is". After *apo*, "from", Greek grammar requires another form of *ho on* (the genitive), but John keeps the title unchanged, as though it were a name that cannot be altered. "Who was" (*ho en*) is also irregular: the verb "be" has no past participle in Greek, so John uses the past tense with the article. The title recalls Exodus 3:14, where the Greek Old Testament has "I am the one who is" (*ho on*). Many manuscripts read "from God, who is …".
[^10]: Or "the sevenfold Spirit". The seven spirits appear again in 3:1; 4:5; 5:6, where they are seven torches burning before the throne and the seven eyes of the Lamb (compare Zechariah 4:2, 10). They have been understood as the Holy Spirit in his fullness, seven being a number of completeness (compare Isaiah 11:2), or as seven angels who stand before God (compare 8:2).
[^11]: Psalm 89:28, 38 (89:27, 37 in most English Bibles).
[^12]: Or "at the cost of his blood"; literally "in his blood". Many manuscripts read "washed us from our sins in his blood".
[^13]: Compare Exodus 19:6, "a kingdom of priests"; also Revelation 5:10.
[^14]: Daniel 7:13; Zechariah 12:10–14. *Mourn*: literally "beat their breasts".
[^15]: The first and last letters of the Greek alphabet. Compare 21:6; 22:13.
[^16]: Greek *pantokrator*, "ruler of all". The Greek Old Testament uses it to translate "of Armies" in the title "Yahweh of Armies", and *Shaddai*, "the Almighty" (as in Job 5:17). "The Lord God, the Almighty" is its rendering of "Yahweh, the God of Armies" in Amos 4:13, where in the Hebrew "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^17]: Or "the tribulation". Greek *thlipsis*, "pressure, affliction, suffering".
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "in Christ Jesus" here, and "the testimony of Jesus Christ" later in the verse.
[^19]: A small island in the Aegean Sea, off the west coast of what is now Turkey.
[^20]: Or "in the spirit", that is, in a state of vision; also in 4:2; 17:3; 21:10.
[^21]: Greek *kyriake hemera*, "the day belonging to the Lord", usually understood as Sunday, the first day of the week.
[^22]: Greek *biblion*, a book, here in the form of a scroll.
[^23]: Literally "to see the voice that was speaking with me".
[^24]: That is, one in human form. See Daniel 7:13, and compare Daniel 10:16. The phrase has no article, as in Daniel; contrast "the Son of Man" in the Gospels.
[^25]: Compare Daniel 10:5, "a man dressed in linen, with a belt of fine gold … around his waist".
[^26]: Daniel 7:9, "the hair of his head like pure wool"; 10:6, "his eyes like flaming torches".
[^27]: Greek *chalkolibanon*, a word found only here and in 2:18, probably a fine alloy of copper or bronze. Compare Daniel 10:6, "his arms and his legs like the gleam of polished bronze", and Ezekiel 1:7.
[^28]: Ezekiel 43:2, of the glory of God, "Its sound was like the sound of many waters"; compare Ezekiel 1:24.
[^29]: Compare Isaiah 49:2, "He made my mouth like a sharp sword", and 11:4.
[^30]: Isaiah 44:6; 48:12, where God says, "I am the first and I am the last." Compare also Daniel 10:9–12.
[^31]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
[^32]: Greek *Hades*, the realm of the dead, like the Hebrew Sheol.
[^33]: Or "So write what you have seen: both what is and what is going to take place after this."
[^34]: Or "messengers". Greek *angeloi*, used throughout Revelation for angels; also in chapters 2 and 3.
