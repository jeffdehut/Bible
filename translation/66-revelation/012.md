**1** A great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars on her head.[^1]

**2** She was pregnant, and she cried out in labor, in agony to give birth.[^2]

**3** Then another sign appeared in heaven: there was a great red dragon,[^3] with seven heads and ten horns,[^4] and seven diadems[^5] on his heads.

**4** His tail swept away a third of the stars of heaven and threw them to the earth.[^6] The dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that he might devour her child as soon as she gave birth.

**5** She gave birth to a son, a male child, who is going to rule all the nations with an iron rod.[^7] But her child was snatched up to God and to his throne.

**6** The woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, so that she might be taken care of there for 1,260 days.[^8]

**7** Then war broke out in heaven. Michael[^9] and his angels fought against the dragon. The dragon and his angels fought back,

**8** but he was not strong enough, and there was no longer any place for them in heaven.

**9** The great dragon was thrown down, that ancient serpent,[^10] who is called the devil and Satan,[^11] who deceives the whole world. He was thrown down to the earth, and his angels were thrown down with him.

**10** Then I heard a loud voice in heaven saying, "Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God, and the authority of his Messiah,[^12] have come, for the accuser[^13] of our brothers and sisters[^14] has been thrown down, who accuses them before our God day and night.

**11** They have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they did not love their lives even in the face of death.[^15]

**12** So rejoice, you heavens, and you who dwell in them![^16] But woe[^17] to the earth and the sea, for the devil has come down to you in great fury, because he knows that his time is short."

**13** When the dragon saw that he had been thrown down to the earth, he pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child.

**14** But the woman was given the two wings of the great eagle,[^18] so that she could fly into the wilderness, to her place, where she is taken care of for a time, times and half a time,[^19] away from the serpent.[^20]

**15** Then the serpent poured water like a river out of his mouth after the woman, to sweep her away with the flood.

**16** But the earth helped the woman. The earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had poured out of his mouth.[^21]

**17** Then the dragon was enraged with the woman, and went off to make war on the rest of her offspring,[^22] those who keep the commands of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus.

**18** And he stood on the sand of the seashore.[^23]

[^1]: Compare Joseph's dream in Genesis 37:9, "The sun, the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me."
[^2]: Compare Isaiah 66:7–8; Micah 4:10.
[^3]: Greek *drakon*, "dragon, serpent", used in the Greek Old Testament for monsters of the sea such as Leviathan (Isaiah 27:1, "he will kill the dragon that is in the sea"; Psalm 74:13–14). See verse 9.
[^4]: Compare the fourth beast of Daniel 7:7, which "had ten horns".
[^5]: Greek *diadema*, a royal crown, as distinct from a victor's wreath (*stephanos*, verse 1).
[^6]: Compare Daniel 8:10, "it threw down to the earth some of the host and some of the stars".
[^7]: Psalm 2:9; see the note on 2:27. Compare also Isaiah 66:7, "she delivered a son".
[^8]: See the note on 11:2.
[^9]: One of the chief angels, the guardian of Israel (Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Jude 9).
[^10]: The serpent of Genesis 3:1–15.
[^11]: *Devil*: Greek *diabolos*, "slanderer". *Satan*: from the Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, accuser" (compare Job 1:6–12; Zechariah 3:1–2).
[^12]: Greek *tou christou autou*, "his Christ". See 11:15.
[^13]: Greek *kategor*, an unusual form of *kategoros*, "accuser", the meaning of the Hebrew *satan*.
[^14]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^15]: Literally "they did not love their life until death".
[^16]: Compare Isaiah 44:23; 49:13, "Shout for joy, you heavens!" *Dwell*: Greek *skenoo*, "live in a tent, dwell" (see 7:15).
[^17]: Greek *ouai*. See 8:13.
[^18]: Compare Exodus 19:4, "how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself"; Deuteronomy 32:11; Isaiah 40:31.
[^19]: That is, probably three and a half years, as in Daniel 7:25; 12:7. See the note on 11:2.
[^20]: Literally "from the face of the serpent".
[^21]: Compare Exodus 15:12, "the earth swallowed them"; Numbers 16:30–32.
[^22]: Literally "seed". Compare Genesis 3:15, "between your offspring and her offspring".
[^23]: Many manuscripts read "And I stood on the sand of the seashore." In some English Bibles, including the King James Version, this sentence begins 13:1.
