**1** The elder,[^1] to the chosen lady[^2] and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who have come to know the truth,

**2** because of the truth that remains in us and will be with us forever:

**3** Grace, mercy and peace will be with us from God the Father and from Jesus Christ,[^3] the Father's Son, in truth and love.

**4** I was very glad to find some of your children walking in the truth, just as we were commanded by the Father.[^4]

**5** And now I ask you, lady, not as though I were writing you a new command, but the one we have had from the beginning: let us love one another.

**6** And this is love: that we walk according to his commands. This is the command, just as you heard from the beginning: that you walk in it.[^5]

**7** For many deceivers have gone out into the world,[^6] people who do not acknowledge Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh.[^7] Such a person is the deceiver and the antichrist.[^8]

**8** Watch yourselves, so that you do not lose what we have worked for, but receive a full reward.[^9]

**9** Everyone who goes too far[^10] and does not remain in the teaching of Christ[^11] does not have God. Whoever remains in the teaching[^12] has both the Father and the Son.

**10** If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive them into your house, and do not greet them.[^13]

**11** For whoever greets them shares in their evil works.

**12** I have much to write to you, but I would rather not do it with paper and ink. Instead I hope to be with you and talk face to face,[^14] so that your joy may be complete.[^15]

**13** The children of your chosen sister greet you.[^16]

[^1]: Greek *presbyteros*, "elder", here a title the writer uses for himself, as in 3 John 1.
[^2]: Or "to the chosen Kyria", or "to Electa, the lady"; either word could be a name. The "lady" may be a particular woman, or a figure for a church and its members (compare verse 13).
[^3]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord Jesus Christ".
[^4]: Literally "just as we received a command from the Father".
[^5]: That is, in love, or in the command.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "have entered the world".
[^7]: Or "as having come in the flesh". The Greek has a present participle, "coming"; compare 1 John 4:2, "has come in the flesh".
[^8]: Literally "This is the deceiver and the antichrist." See 1 John 2:18.
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "so that we do not lose what we have worked for, but we receive a full reward". Some manuscripts read "what you have worked for".
[^10]: Literally "goes ahead". Many manuscripts read "transgresses".
[^11]: Or "the teaching about Christ". Greek *tou Christou*, "of the Christ".
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "the teaching of Christ".
[^13]: Literally "do not say 'Rejoice!' to them", the ordinary greeting; also in verse 11.
[^14]: Literally "mouth to mouth".
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "our joy".
[^16]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
