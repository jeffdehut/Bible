**1** In the twenty-fifth year of our exile, at the beginning of the year, on the tenth day of the month, in the fourteenth year after the city was struck down,[^1] on that very day the hand of Yahweh[^2] was on me, and he brought me there.

**2** In visions of God he brought me to the land of Israel and set me down on a very high mountain, and on it, to the south, was something like the structure of a city.

**3** He brought me there, and there was a man whose appearance was like bronze, with a linen cord and a measuring reed in his hand. He was standing in the gateway.

**4** The man said to me, "Son of man,[^3] look with your eyes, hear with your ears, and pay attention to everything I am going to show you, for you have been brought here so that I may show it to you. Tell the house of Israel everything you see."

**5** There was a wall all around the outside of the house.[^4] In the man's hand was a measuring reed six cubits long, each cubit being a cubit and a handbreadth.[^5] He measured the thickness[^6] of the wall: one reed; and its height: one reed.

**6** Then he went to the gate that faced east. He climbed its steps[^7] and measured the threshold of the gate: one reed wide; and the other threshold: one reed wide.

**7** Each alcove[^8] was one reed long and one reed wide, and between the alcoves were five cubits. The threshold of the gate beside the porch[^9] of the gate, on the inside, was one reed.

**8** He measured the porch of the gate on the inside: one reed.

**9** Then he measured the porch of the gate: eight cubits; and its jambs:[^10] two cubits. The porch of the gate was on the inside.

**10** The alcoves of the east gate were three on one side and three on the other. The three had the same measurements, and the jambs on either side had the same measurements.

**11** He measured the width of the entrance of the gate: ten cubits; and the length of the gate: thirteen cubits.

**12** There was a barrier of one cubit in front of the alcoves, one cubit on either side; and the alcoves were six cubits on one side and six cubits on the other.

**13** He measured the gate from the roof of one alcove to the roof of the other:[^11] the width was twenty-five cubits, from doorway to opposite doorway.

**14** He made the jambs sixty cubits, and the gate reached all around to the jamb of the court.[^12]

**15** From the front of the entrance gate to the front of the porch of the inner gate was fifty cubits.

**16** The alcoves and their jambs had narrow windows[^13] all around, on the inside of the gate, and so did the porches.[^14] There were windows all around on the inside, and on the jambs were palm trees.[^15]

**17** Then he brought me into the outer court, and there were rooms and a pavement made for the court all around. Thirty rooms faced the pavement.

**18** The pavement ran beside the gates, corresponding to the length of the gates. This was the lower pavement.

**19** Then he measured the distance from the front of the lower gate to the front of the inner court, outside: a hundred cubits, on the east and on the north.

**20** As for the gate of the outer court that faced north, he measured its length and its width.

**21** Its alcoves, three on one side and three on the other, its jambs and its porches had the same measurements as the first gate: fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide.

**22** Its windows, its porches and its palm trees had the same measurements as those of the gate that faced east. Seven steps led up to it, and its porches were in front of them.

**23** The inner court had a gate opposite the north gate, as it had for the east gate. He measured from gate to gate: a hundred cubits.

**24** Then he led me toward the south, and there was a gate facing south. He measured its jambs and its porches; they had the same measurements as the others.

**25** It and its porches had windows all around, like those other windows. It was fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide.

**26** Seven steps led up to it, and its porches were in front of them. It had palm trees on its jambs, one on either side.

**27** The inner court had a gate toward the south, and he measured from gate to gate toward the south: a hundred cubits.

**28** Then he brought me into the inner court through the south gate, and he measured the south gate. It had the same measurements as the others.

**29** Its alcoves, its jambs and its porches had the same measurements as the others. It and its porches had windows all around. It was fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide.

**30** There were porches all around, twenty-five cubits long and five cubits wide.[^16]

**31** Its porches faced the outer court, and there were palm trees on its jambs. Its stairway had eight steps.

**32** Then he brought me into the inner court on the east side, and he measured the gate. It had the same measurements as the others.

**33** Its alcoves, its jambs and its porches had the same measurements as the others. It and its porches had windows all around. It was fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide.

**34** Its porches faced the outer court, and there were palm trees on its jambs on either side. Its stairway had eight steps.

**35** Then he brought me to the north gate, and he measured it. It had the same measurements as the others,

**36** with its alcoves, its jambs and its porches, and it had windows all around. It was fifty cubits long and twenty-five cubits wide.

**37** Its jambs faced the outer court, and there were palm trees on its jambs on either side. Its stairway had eight steps.

**38** There was a room with its doorway by the jambs of the gates, where the burnt offering[^17] was to be washed.

**39** In the porch of the gate were two tables on one side and two tables on the other, on which the burnt offering, the sin offering[^18] and the guilt offering[^19] were to be slaughtered.

**40** At the outer side, as one goes up to the entrance of the north gate, were two tables; and on the other side of the porch of the gate were two tables.

**41** So there were four tables on one side and four tables on the other, beside the gate: eight tables on which they were to slaughter.

**42** There were also four tables of dressed stone[^20] for the burnt offering, a cubit and a half long, a cubit and a half wide and a cubit high. On them they were to lay the instruments with which they slaughtered the burnt offering and the sacrifice.

**43** Hooks,[^21] a handbreadth long, were fastened all around on the inside, and the meat of the offering was to be laid on the tables.

**44** Outside the inner gate, in the inner court, were rooms for the singers,[^22] one beside the north gate, facing south, and one beside the east gate, facing north.

**45** He said to me, "This room that faces south is for the priests who have charge of the house,

**46** and the room that faces north is for the priests who have charge of the altar. These are the sons of Zadok,[^23] who alone among the sons of Levi may come near to Yahweh to serve him."

**47** He measured the court: a hundred cubits long and a hundred cubits wide, a square. The altar was in front of the house.

**48** Then he brought me to the porch of the house and measured the jambs of the porch: five cubits on one side and five cubits on the other. The width of the gate was three cubits on one side and three cubits on the other.[^24]

**49** The porch was twenty cubits long and eleven cubits wide, and it was reached by steps.[^25] There were pillars[^26] beside the jambs, one on either side.

[^1]: In 573 BC, probably in April. *At the beginning of the year* (Hebrew *rosh hashanah*) probably means the first month, as in 45:18–21; the tenth day of that month was the day the Passover lamb was chosen (Exodus 12:3). The city had fallen in 586 BC (33:21).
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^4]: That is, the temple. "The house" is the usual word for the temple in chapters 40–48. Chapters 40–42 describe the temple area, its gates and courts, and its buildings; 43:1–12 describes the return of the glory of Yahweh to it.
[^5]: A common cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm). This long cubit, a handbreadth longer, was about 21 inches (53 cm), so the reed was about 10½ feet (3.2 m), and the wall was as thick as it was high.
[^6]: Literally "width". Here and throughout chapters 40–42, "width" and "length" translate the Hebrew words *rochav* and *orekh*.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "its step" but read "its steps". In this chapter the Hebrew is often written with a singular "its" but read with a plural: "its jamb" but "its jambs" (verses 9, 21, 24, 26, 29, 31, 33, 34, 36, 37), "its alcove" but "its alcoves" (verses 21, 29, 33, 36), "its porch" but "its porches" (verses 21, 22, 24–26, 29, 33, 34, 36), "its window" but "its windows" and "its palm tree" but "its palm trees" (verse 22), "its step" but "its steps" (verses 26, 31, 34, 37). The written forms may be an older spelling of the plural, as in verse 31, where "its porches" is spelled without the usual *y*; where each gate had only one porch, "its porches" may mean the porch and its parts.
[^8]: Hebrew *ta*, a small room or recess in the gateway, probably for guards (1 Kings 14:28). Traditionally "chamber". Also in verses 10, 12, 13, 16, 21, 29, 33 and 36.
[^9]: Hebrew *ulam*: an entrance hall or vestibule, as at Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:3).
[^10]: Hebrew *ayil*, probably the thick wall or pilaster on either side of a passage. The meaning is uncertain.
[^11]: Literally "from the roof of the alcove to its roof". The width was probably measured from the back of an alcove on one side to the back of the alcove opposite.
[^12]: The meaning of this verse is uncertain. Literally "And he made jambs, sixty cubits, and to the jamb of the court the gate all around, all around." Some take "he made" to mean "he measured". The Septuagint reads differently.
[^13]: Hebrew *atumot*, perhaps "narrow", "latticed" or "with recessed frames" (1 Kings 6:4). Also in 41:16, 26.
[^14]: Hebrew *elammot*, plural of *elam*, another form of *ulam*, "porch". Also in verses 21–36.
[^15]: Carved palm trees, as on the walls of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:29). Also in verses 22, 26, 31, 34 and 37.
[^16]: The meaning of this verse is uncertain, and the Septuagint does not have it.
[^17]: Hebrew *olah*, from the verb "go up": an animal burned whole on the altar (Leviticus 1). Also in verses 39 and 42.
[^18]: Hebrew *chatta't*. Or "purification offering" (Leviticus 4).
[^19]: Hebrew *asham*. Many scholars translate it "reparation offering" (Leviticus 5:14–6:7, 5:14–26 in the Hebrew).
[^20]: Hebrew *gazit*: stone cut and dressed to shape.
[^21]: Hebrew *shefattayim*, perhaps "double hooks" or "ledges". The meaning is uncertain.
[^22]: The Septuagint reads "two rooms", and "the south gate" instead of "the east gate". The rooms in verses 45–46 are for priests.
[^23]: Zadok was the priest whom Solomon made chief priest in place of Abiathar (1 Kings 2:35). In Ezekiel only his descendants may serve at the altar (43:19; 44:15–16).
[^24]: The Septuagint adds "The width of the doorway was fourteen cubits", and reads "the side walls of the doorway of the porch were three cubits on one side and three cubits on the other".
[^25]: Literally "and by the steps by which they went up to it". The Septuagint reads "by ten steps".
[^26]: Like the two bronze pillars that stood at the porch of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 7:15–22).
