**1** The word of the LORD[^1] came to me:

**2** What do you mean by quoting this proverb about the land of Israel: "The fathers eat sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge"?[^2]

**3** As I live, declares the Lord GOD,[^3] you will no longer use this proverb in Israel.

**4** Look, all lives are mine. The life of the father, like the life of the son, is mine. The person who sins is the one who will die.[^4]

**5** Suppose a man is righteous and does what is just and right.

**6** He does not eat on the mountains[^5] or lift up his eyes to the idols[^6] of the house of Israel. He does not defile his neighbor's wife or come near a woman during her menstrual impurity.[^7]

**7** He does not oppress anyone. He returns what was given him in pledge for a debt.[^8] He does not commit robbery. He gives his bread to the hungry and covers the naked with clothing.

**8** He does not lend at interest or take any profit.[^9] He keeps his hand from wrongdoing and judges fairly between one person and another.

**9** He walks in my decrees and keeps my rulings, acting faithfully. Such a man is righteous; he will surely live, declares the Lord GOD.

**10** But suppose he fathers a son who is violent, who sheds blood, and who does any one of these things[^10]

**11** (though the father did none of them).[^11] He eats on the mountains and defiles his neighbor's wife.

**12** He oppresses the poor and needy, commits robbery, and does not return a pledge. He lifts up his eyes to the idols and does detestable things.[^12]

**13** He lends at interest and takes profit. Will he live? He will not live! He has done all these detestable things; he will surely be put to death. His blood will be on his own head.[^13]

**14** But suppose this son fathers a son who sees all the sins his father has done. He sees them, and does not do the like.

**15** He does not eat on the mountains or lift up his eyes to the idols of the house of Israel. He does not defile his neighbor's wife.

**16** He does not oppress anyone, does not take anything in pledge,[^14] and does not commit robbery. He gives his bread to the hungry and covers the naked with clothing.

**17** He keeps his hand from harming the poor,[^15] and does not take interest or profit. He keeps my rulings and walks in my decrees. He will not die for his father's guilt; he will surely live.

**18** As for his father, because he practiced extortion, robbed his brother, and did what was not good among his people, look, he dies for his guilt.

**19** Yet you say, "Why does the son not bear the guilt of the father?" When the son has done what is just and right, and has kept all my decrees and done them, he will surely live.

**20** The person who sins is the one who will die. The son will not bear the guilt of the father, nor will the father bear the guilt of the son. The righteousness of the righteous will be on him, and the wickedness of the wicked will be on him.

**21** But if the wicked person turns away from all the sins[^16] he has committed, and keeps all my decrees and does what is just and right, he will surely live; he will not die.

**22** None of the transgressions he has committed will be remembered against him. Because of the righteousness he has done, he will live.

**23** Do I take any pleasure in the death of the wicked, declares the Lord GOD? Do I not rather want him to turn from his ways and live?

**24** But if a righteous person turns away from his righteousness and does wrong, doing all the same detestable things that the wicked person does, will he live? None of the righteous deeds[^17] he has done will be remembered. Because of the unfaithfulness he has committed and the sin he has committed, he will die for them.

**25** Yet you say, "The way of the Lord[^18] is not fair."[^19] Hear now, house of Israel: Is my way not fair? Is it not your ways that are not fair?

**26** When a righteous person turns away from his righteousness and does wrong and dies because of it,[^20] he dies for the wrong he has done.

**27** And when a wicked person turns away from the wickedness he has committed and does what is just and right, he will save his life.[^21]

**28** Because he considers and turns away from all the transgressions he has committed, he will surely live; he will not die.

**29** Yet the house of Israel says, "The way of the Lord is not fair." Are my ways not fair, house of Israel? Is it not your ways that are not fair?

**30** Therefore I will judge you, house of Israel, each according to his ways, declares the Lord GOD. Turn back,[^22] and turn away from all your transgressions, so that guilt will not be your downfall.[^23]

**31** Throw off all the transgressions you have committed, and make for yourselves a new heart and a new spirit. Why should you die, house of Israel?

**32** For I take no pleasure in the death of anyone who dies, declares the Lord GOD. So turn back and live!

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "the fathers eat unripe grapes, and the sons' teeth become blunt": the sourness makes the teeth feel rough. The same proverb is quoted in Jeremiah 31:29.
[^3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "life, person, soul". Traditionally "The soul that sinneth, it shall die." Also in verse 20.
[^5]: That is, he does not eat sacrificial meals at the shrines on the hills (6:13; 22:9). Also in verses 11 and 15.
[^6]: Hebrew *gillulim*, a contemptuous word for idols, perhaps related to the word for dung. Also in verses 12 and 15.
[^7]: Hebrew *niddah* (Leviticus 15:19–24; 18:19).
[^8]: Literally "his pledge, for a debt, he returns". The law required a creditor to give back a poor man's cloak, taken in pledge, by sunset (Exodus 22:25–26, 22:26–27 in most English Bibles; Deuteronomy 24:10–13).
[^9]: Hebrew *neshekh* and *tarbit*, two words for interest, perhaps interest taken in advance and interest added at repayment. The law forbade lending at interest to a fellow Israelite (Leviticus 25:35–37). Also in verses 13 and 17.
[^10]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and does a brother of one of these".
[^11]: Or "and does none of these duties".
[^12]: Hebrew *to'evah*, what is abhorrent, especially in worship. Also in verses 13 and 24.
[^13]: Literally "his blood will be in him": he alone is responsible for his death.
[^14]: Literally "does not take a pledge". Compare verse 7.
[^15]: Literally "he turns back his hand from the poor". Compare verse 8.
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "all his sin" but read "all his sins".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "all his righteousness" but read "all his righteous deeds".
[^18]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH. Also in verse 29.
[^19]: Hebrew *yittakhen*, "be measured, be right, be in order". Also in verse 29.
[^20]: Literally "and dies on them".
[^21]: Literally "keep his *nephesh* alive".
[^22]: Or "Repent". Hebrew *shuvu*, "turn back, return". Also in verse 32.
[^23]: Literally "so that guilt will not be a stumbling block for you". See 3:20.
