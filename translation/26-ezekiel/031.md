**1** In the eleventh year, in the third month, on the first day of the month,[^1] the word of Yahweh[^2] came to me:

**2** Son of man,[^3] say to Pharaoh king of Egypt and to his hordes:[^4] Whom are you like in your greatness?

**3** Look, Assyria[^5] was a cedar in Lebanon, with beautiful branches and shady foliage, towering high, with its top among the clouds.[^6]

**4** The waters made it grow; the deep made it tall. The deep sent its rivers flowing all around the place where it was planted, and sent out its channels to all the trees of the field.

**5** So it towered higher than all the trees of the field. Its boughs grew many and its branches[^7] grew long, because of the abundant waters as it spread them out.

**6** All the birds of the sky nested in its boughs; all the animals of the field gave birth under its branches; and all the great nations lived in its shade.

**7** It was beautiful in its greatness, in the length of its branches, for its roots went down to abundant waters.

**8** The cedars in the garden of God could not rival it; the junipers[^8] could not compare with its boughs, and the plane trees were nothing like its branches. No tree in the garden of God was like it in its beauty.

**9** I made it beautiful with its many branches, and all the trees of Eden, which were in the garden of God, envied it.

**10** Therefore this is what the Lord Yahweh[^9] says: Because you[^10] towered so high, and it set its top among the clouds, and its heart grew proud of its height,

**11** I handed it over to the ruler of the nations,[^11] who was sure to deal with it as its wickedness deserved. I drove it out.

**12** Foreigners, the most ruthless of the nations, cut it down and left it. On the mountains and in all the valleys its branches fell, and its boughs lay broken in all the ravines of the land. All the peoples of the earth came out from its shade and left it.

**13** All the birds of the sky settle on its fallen trunk, and all the animals of the field are among its branches.

**14** This is so that no trees by the waters may tower high or set their tops among the clouds, and no trees that drink water may reach up to them in height. For all of them are given over to death, to the world below,[^12] among human beings, with those who go down to the Pit.

**15** This is what the Lord Yahweh says: On the day it went down to Sheol,[^13] I brought mourning: I covered the deep over it[^14] and held back its rivers, and the mighty waters were stopped. I clothed Lebanon in gloom for it, and all the trees of the field wilted because of it.

**16** I made the nations quake at the sound of its fall, when I brought it down to Sheol with those who go down to the Pit. And all the trees of Eden, the choicest and best of Lebanon, all the trees that drink water, were comforted[^15] in the world below.

**17** They too went down with it to Sheol, to those killed by the sword, and so did its allies,[^16] who lived in its shade among the nations.

**18** Which of the trees of Eden were you like in glory and greatness? Yet you will be brought down with the trees of Eden to the world below. You will lie among the uncircumcised,[^17] with those killed by the sword. This is Pharaoh and all his hordes, declares the Lord Yahweh.

[^1]: In June 587 BC.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^4]: Or "his multitude". Hebrew *hamon* (29:19). Also in verse 18.
[^5]: Hebrew *Ashshur*. Assyria, once the greatest of empires, had fallen to Babylon a few decades earlier (612–609 BC), and Pharaoh is invited to compare himself with it. Some scholars read *te'ashur*, "a cypress" (27:6), with a small change to the Hebrew, so that the tree is Pharaoh himself.
[^6]: Hebrew *avotim*, "thick boughs" or "clouds". Also in verses 10 and 14.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "its branch" but read "its branches".
[^8]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the juniper or the cypress (27:5).
[^9]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^10]: The Hebrew moves here from "you" to "it".
[^11]: Hebrew *el goyim*, "the mighty one of the nations", probably the king of Babylon.
[^12]: Hebrew *erets tachtit*, "the land below", the underworld. Also in verses 16 and 18.
[^13]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^14]: Literally "I caused mourning; I covered over it the deep". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps the deep was covered as a sign of mourning, or closed over the fallen tree.
[^15]: Or "consoled", as they saw the great tree share their fate.
[^16]: Literally "its arm", those who were its strength.
[^17]: That is, those who died in disgrace (28:10).
