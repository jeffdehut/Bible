**1** The word of Yahweh[^1] came to me:

**2** You, son of man,[^2] raise a lament[^3] over Tyre.

**3** Say to Tyre, who lives at the gateway to the sea,[^4] trader to the peoples of many coastlands: This is what the Lord Yahweh[^5] says: Tyre, you have said, "I am perfect in beauty."

**4** Your borders are in the heart of the seas; your builders made your beauty perfect.

**5** They built all your planks[^6] of juniper[^7] from Senir;[^8] they took a cedar from Lebanon to make a mast for you.

**6** They made your oars of oaks from Bashan; they made your deck[^9] of ivory set in cypress[^10] from the coastlands of Kittim.[^11]

**7** Your sail was fine linen with embroidery from Egypt, to serve as your banner. Your awning was blue and purple cloth[^12] from the coastlands of Elishah.[^13]

**8** The people of Sidon and Arvad[^14] were your rowers. Your own skilled men, Tyre, were in you; they were your helmsmen.[^15]

**9** The elders of Gebal[^16] and its skilled men were in you, repairing your seams.[^17] All the ships of the sea and their sailors were in you, to trade for your merchandise.

**10** Persia, Lud and Put[^18] were in your army, your men of war. They hung shield and helmet in you; they gave you your splendor.

**11** The men of Arvad and your army[^19] were on your walls all around, and the Gammadim[^20] were in your towers. They hung their shields[^21] on your walls all around; they made your beauty perfect.

**12** Tarshish[^22] traded with you because of your great wealth of every kind.[^23] They gave silver, iron, tin and lead for your wares.[^24]

**13** Javan, Tubal and Meshech[^25] were your merchants. They gave human beings[^26] and articles of bronze for your merchandise.

**14** From Beth-togarmah[^27] they gave horses, war horses[^28] and mules for your wares.

**15** The men of Dedan[^29] were your merchants. Many coastlands were your markets;[^30] they paid you with ivory tusks[^31] and ebony.

**16** Aram[^32] traded with you because of your many products. They gave turquoise,[^33] purple cloth, embroidered work, fine linen,[^34] coral and rubies[^35] for your wares.

**17** Judah and the land of Israel were your merchants. They gave wheat from Minnith,[^36] pannag,[^37] honey, oil and balm for your merchandise.

**18** Damascus traded with you because of your many products and your great wealth of every kind, in wine from Helbon[^38] and wool from Zahar.[^39]

**19** Vedan and Javan from Uzal[^40] paid for your wares: wrought iron, cassia and cane[^41] were among your merchandise.

**20** Dedan was your merchant in saddlecloths[^42] for riding.

**21** Arabia and all the princes of Kedar[^43] were your dealers.[^44] In lambs, rams and goats, in these they traded with you.

**22** The merchants of Sheba and Raamah[^45] were your merchants. They gave the finest of all spices, every kind of precious stone, and gold for your wares.

**23** Haran, Canneh and Eden,[^46] the merchants of Sheba, Asshur and Kilmad were your merchants.

**24** They were your merchants in fine garments,[^47] in cloaks of blue cloth and embroidered work, and in carpets of many colors, bound with cords and made secure, in your marketplace.[^48]

**25** The ships of Tarshish carried[^49] your merchandise. You were filled and heavily laden[^50] in the heart of the seas.

**26** Your rowers have brought you out into the mighty waters. The east wind has wrecked you in the heart of the seas.

**27** Your wealth, your wares and your merchandise, your sailors and your helmsmen, those who repair your seams and those who trade your merchandise, and all your men of war who are in you, with all the company that is within you, will fall into the heart of the seas on the day of your downfall.

**28** At the sound of the cry of your helmsmen the shorelands[^51] will shake.

**29** All who handle the oar will come down from their ships. The sailors and all the helmsmen of the sea will stand on the shore.

**30** They will raise their voices over you and cry out bitterly. They will throw dust on their heads and roll in the ashes.

**31** They will shave their heads bald for you and put on sackcloth.[^52] They will weep over you in bitterness of soul, with bitter mourning.

**32** In their wailing they will raise a lament for you and lament over you: "Who was like Tyre, like one silenced[^53] in the middle of the sea?

**33** When your wares went out from the seas, you satisfied many peoples. With your great wealth and your merchandise you made the kings of the earth rich.

**34** Now that you are wrecked by the seas, in the depths of the waters,[^54] your merchandise and all the company within you have gone down.

**35** All the inhabitants of the coastlands are appalled at you. Their kings shudder with horror; their faces are contorted.

**36** The traders among the peoples hiss at you.[^55] You have come to a dreadful end,[^56] and you will be no more, forever."

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^3]: Hebrew *qinah*, a funeral song. The lament pictures Tyre as a splendid merchant ship that sinks at sea.
[^4]: Literally "at the entrances of the sea": Tyre was an island city with harbors on the coast of Phoenicia.
[^5]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^6]: Hebrew *luchotayim*, a dual form: perhaps the planks of the ship's two sides.
[^7]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the juniper or the cypress.
[^8]: The Amorite name of Mount Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9).
[^9]: Literally "your boards", the word used for the frames of the tabernacle (Exodus 26:15).
[^10]: Literally "ivory, daughter of Ashurim". The same consonants can be read *bit'ashurim*, "in cypress" or "in boxwood" (Hebrew *te'ashur*, Isaiah 41:19).
[^11]: Cyprus, named after its city Kition. Later the name was used more widely for the lands and peoples of the west.
[^12]: Hebrew *tekhelet* and *argaman*: wool dyed violet-blue and red-purple (Exodus 25:4).
[^13]: Probably Cyprus, or part of it (Genesis 10:4).
[^14]: Sidon was a port city north of Tyre; Arvad was an island city farther north, off the coast of Syria.
[^15]: Hebrew *chovelim*, from *chevel*, "rope": the sailors who handled the rigging and steered the ship. Traditionally "pilots". Also in verses 27, 28 and 29.
[^16]: Byblos, a port city on the coast north of Sidon.
[^17]: Literally "strengthening your breaches": caulking the seams of the ship. Also in verse 27.
[^18]: *Lud* was probably Lydia, in western Asia Minor; *Put* was probably Libya.
[^19]: The same consonants can be read "and Helech", probably Cilicia in southeastern Asia Minor.
[^20]: A people or place not otherwise known.
[^21]: Hebrew *shelatim*, perhaps "shields" or "quivers".
[^22]: A distant port in the west, perhaps Tartessus in southern Spain.
[^23]: Literally "Tarshish was your trader from the abundance of all wealth". Also in verse 18.
[^24]: Hebrew *izbonim*, a word found only in this chapter, perhaps "wares" or "exports". Also in verses 14, 16, 19, 22, 27 and 33.
[^25]: *Javan* is Greece, or the Greeks of Ionia in western Asia Minor. *Tubal* and *Meshech* were peoples of central and eastern Asia Minor (Genesis 10:2).
[^26]: Literally "the *nephesh* of human beings": people sold as slaves.
[^27]: Literally "From the house of Togarmah", probably in eastern Asia Minor (Genesis 10:3).
[^28]: Or "horsemen".
[^29]: The Septuagint reads "the Rhodians", the people of the island of Rhodes. In Hebrew the letters *d* and *r* are easily confused. Dedan, in verse 20, was in northwestern Arabia.
[^30]: Literally "the market of your hand".
[^31]: Literally "horns of ivory".
[^32]: Aram is Syria. Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac read "Edom", which differs by one similar letter.
[^33]: Hebrew *nofekh*, one of the stones of the high priest's breastpiece (Exodus 28:18). The identification is uncertain.
[^34]: Hebrew *buts*, a fine linen; a different word from *shesh* in verse 7.
[^35]: The identification of *ra'mot*, "coral", and *kadkod*, "rubies", is uncertain.
[^36]: A town in Ammon (Judges 11:33).
[^37]: Hebrew *pannag*, a word found only here, of unknown meaning: perhaps a kind of cake, or millet.
[^38]: A place in the hills north of Damascus, known for its wine.
[^39]: Or "white wool".
[^40]: The meaning is uncertain. *Vedan* can also be read "and Dan". *Javan* here may be a place in Arabia rather than Greece (verse 13), and *Uzal* was probably in Arabia (Genesis 10:27). The word read "from Uzal" (*me'uzzal*) can also mean "spun", as of yarn.
[^41]: Cassia and cane were aromatic spices (Exodus 30:23–24).
[^42]: Hebrew *bigde chofesh*, "garments of spreading", probably saddle blankets.
[^43]: A tribe of the Syrian–Arabian desert, known for its flocks (Isaiah 60:7; Jeremiah 49:28–29).
[^44]: Literally "the traders of your hand".
[^45]: Peoples of southern Arabia (Genesis 10:7).
[^46]: *Haran* was a city in northern Mesopotamia (Genesis 11:31), and *Eden* was probably Beth-eden on the Euphrates (Amos 1:5). *Canneh* and *Kilmad* are not otherwise known. *Asshur* is Assyria, or a place of that name.
[^47]: Literally "in perfect things".
[^48]: The meaning of several words in this verse is uncertain. *Ginze beromim*, "carpets of many colors", may be "chests of colored cloth"; *aruzim*, "made secure", may mean "made of cedar".
[^49]: Hebrew *sharotayikh*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "your caravans" or "your carriers".
[^50]: Or "very glorious". The Hebrew verb *kaved* means both "be heavy" and "be honored".
[^51]: Hebrew *migreshot*, usually the open land around a town; here perhaps the coastlands. The meaning is uncertain.
[^52]: Shaving the head and wearing sackcloth, a coarse cloth of goats' hair, were signs of mourning.
[^53]: Hebrew *kedumah*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "like one silenced" or "like one destroyed".
[^54]: Literally "At the time of being broken from the seas in the depths of the waters".
[^55]: Hissing or whistling was a gesture of shock and scorn at a ruin (1 Kings 9:8; Jeremiah 19:8).
[^56]: Literally "You have become terrors". The same words end 28:19, and similar words end 26:21.
