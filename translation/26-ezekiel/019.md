**1** As for you, take up a lament[^1] for the princes[^2] of Israel,

**2** and say: What a lioness your mother was![^3] She lay down among lions; among young lions she reared her cubs.

**3** She brought up one of her cubs, and he became a young lion. He learned to tear prey; he devoured people.

**4** The nations heard about him; he was caught in their pit. They brought him with hooks to the land of Egypt.[^4]

**5** When she saw that she had waited in vain,[^5] that her hope was lost, she took another of her cubs and made him a young lion.

**6** He prowled among the lions; he became a young lion. He learned to tear prey; he devoured people.

**7** He knew their widows[^6] and laid waste their cities. The land and everything in it was appalled at the sound of his roaring.

**8** Then the nations from the provinces all around set themselves against him. They spread their net over him; he was caught in their pit.

**9** With hooks they put him in a cage and brought him to the king of Babylon. They brought him into strongholds,[^7] so that his voice would no longer be heard on the mountains of Israel.[^8]

**10** Your mother was like a vine in your blood,[^9] planted by the water. It was fruitful and full of branches because of the abundant water.

**11** It had strong branches, fit for the scepters of rulers.[^10] It grew tall among the thick boughs;[^11] it stood out in its height, with its many branches.

**12** But it was uprooted in fury and thrown to the ground. The east wind dried up its fruit. Its strong branch was torn off and withered; fire consumed it.

**13** Now it is planted in the wilderness, in a dry and thirsty land.

**14** Fire has gone out from its branch[^12] and consumed its fruit. No strong branch is left on it, no scepter for ruling. This is a lament, and it has become a lament.

[^1]: Hebrew *qinah*, a funeral song. Its lines usually have a limping rhythm, three beats followed by two. Also in verse 14.
[^2]: Hebrew *nesi'im*, plural of *nasi*. See 7:27.
[^3]: *Your* is masculine singular, addressed to the prince. Also in verse 10.
[^4]: Jehoahaz, king of Judah for three months in 609 BC, was taken to Egypt by Pharaoh Neco (2 Kings 23:31–34).
[^5]: The meaning of the Hebrew word (*nochalah*) is uncertain.
[^6]: The meaning is uncertain. The same word is used for "strongholds" in Isaiah 13:22, and many translate "He broke down their strongholds", with a small change to the Hebrew verb.
[^7]: Or "into nets". Hebrew *metsodot* can mean either.
[^8]: Both Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:15) and Zedekiah (2 Kings 25:7) were taken to the king of Babylon.
[^9]: The meaning of "in your blood" is uncertain. Some take it to mean "in your likeness" (from *damah*, "be like"); others change the Hebrew slightly to read "in a vineyard".
[^10]: Hebrew *matteh* means both "branch" and "staff, scepter"; *shevet*, "scepter", also means "tribe". Also in verses 12 and 14.
[^11]: Or "among the clouds".
[^12]: Literally "from the branch of its shoots".
