**1** The word of the LORD[^1] came to me:

**2** Son of man,[^2] pose a riddle and tell a parable[^3] to the house of Israel.

**3** Say: This is what the Lord GOD[^4] says: A great eagle,[^5] with great wings and long pinions, full of feathers of many colors,[^6] came to Lebanon and took the top of the cedar.

**4** He broke off the topmost of its young shoots and carried it to a land of traders,[^7] and set it in a city of merchants.

**5** Then he took some of the seed of the land and put it in a fertile field. He set it like a willow beside abundant waters.[^8]

**6** It sprouted and became a spreading vine, low in height. Its branches turned toward him, and its roots remained beneath him.[^9] So it became a vine, and it produced shoots and sent out leafy branches.

**7** But there was another great eagle,[^10] with great wings and thick plumage. And look, this vine bent its roots toward him and stretched out its branches to him, away from the beds where it was planted, so that he might water it.

**8** It had been planted in good soil beside abundant waters, so that it could produce branches, bear fruit and become a splendid vine.

**9** Say: This is what the Lord GOD says: Will it prosper? Will he not pull up its roots and strip off its fruit, so that it withers, so that all its fresh sprouting leaves wither? It will not take a strong arm or many people to pull it up by its roots.

**10** Look, it is planted, but will it prosper? Will it not wither completely when the east wind strikes it? It will wither on the beds where it sprouted.

**11** The word of the LORD came to me:

**12** Say to the rebellious house: Do you not know what these things mean? Say: Look, the king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and took its king and its officials and brought them to himself in Babylon.[^11]

**13** He took one of the royal family[^12] and made a covenant with him, putting him under an oath. He also took away the leading men[^13] of the land,

**14** so that the kingdom would be humble and not lift itself up, and would keep his covenant in order to survive.

**15** But he rebelled against him by sending his envoys to Egypt, so that they would give him horses and a large army. Will he prosper? Will a man who does such things escape? Can he break a covenant and escape?

**16** As I live, declares the Lord GOD, he will die in Babylon, in the land of the king who made him king, whose oath he despised and whose covenant he broke, there with him in Babylon.

**17** Pharaoh with his mighty army and great horde will not help him[^14] in the war, when ramps are thrown up and siege walls built to cut off many lives.

**18** He despised the oath by breaking the covenant. Though he had given his hand in pledge, he did all these things. He will not escape.

**19** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: As I live, I will surely bring down on his head my oath that he despised and my covenant that he broke.

**20** I will spread my net over him, and he will be caught in my snare. I will bring him to Babylon and enter into judgment with him there for the unfaithfulness he committed against me.

**21** All the fugitives among all his troops will fall by the sword, and those who are left will be scattered to every wind. Then you will know that I, the LORD, have spoken.

**22** This is what the Lord GOD says: I myself will take a sprig from the lofty top of the cedar and set it out. I will break off a tender shoot from the topmost of its young shoots, and I myself will plant it on a high and lofty mountain.

**23** On the high mountain of Israel I will plant it, and it will put out branches and bear fruit and become a majestic cedar. Birds of every kind[^15] will nest under it; they will nest in the shade of its branches.

**24** All the trees of the field will know that I am the LORD. I bring down the high tree and make the low tree high; I dry up the green tree and make the dry tree flourish. I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will do it.[^16]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "son of man": a human being, a mortal.
[^3]: Hebrew *chidah*, "riddle", and *mashal*, "parable, proverb".
[^4]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Or "vulture". Hebrew *nesher*. Also in verse 7.
[^6]: Literally "which had the embroidery".
[^7]: Literally "a land of Canaan". *Canaan* also means "traders" (16:29).
[^8]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "he took on many waters, a willow he set it". The word for *willow*, *tsaftsafah*, is found only here.
[^9]: *Him* is probably the eagle; the Hebrew could also mean "toward it … beneath it", the vine itself.
[^10]: Literally "one great eagle".
[^11]: In 597 BC, when King Nebuchadnezzar took King Jehoiachin and his officials to Babylon (2 Kings 24:10–16).
[^12]: Literally "of the seed of the kingdom": Zedekiah, Jehoiachin's uncle (2 Kings 24:17; 2 Chronicles 36:13).
[^13]: Literally "the rams of the land".
[^14]: Literally "will not act with him".
[^15]: Literally "every bird of every wing".
[^16]: Literally "and I have done it".
