**1** Then he called out in my hearing with a loud voice, "Come near, you who are to punish the city,[^1] each with his weapon of destruction in his hand!"

**2** And six men came from the direction of the upper gate, which faces north, each with his weapon for smashing in his hand. Among them was a man clothed in linen, with a writing case[^2] at his waist. They came in and stood beside the bronze altar.

**3** Now the glory of the God of Israel had gone up from the cherub[^3] on which it had been, to the threshold of the house. He called to the man clothed in linen, who had the writing case at his waist.

**4** The LORD[^4] said to him, "Go through the city, through Jerusalem, and put a mark[^5] on the foreheads of the people who sigh and groan over all the detestable practices[^6] that are committed in it."

**5** To the others he said in my hearing, "Go through the city after him and strike. Do not let your eye pity,[^7] and do not spare.

**6** Kill old men, young men and young women, little children and women, until they are destroyed. But do not come near anyone who has the mark on him. Begin at my sanctuary." So they began with the elders[^8] who were in front of the house.

**7** He said to them, "Defile the house, and fill the courts with the slain. Go!" So they went out and struck down people in the city.

**8** While they were striking them down, and I was left alone, I fell on my face and cried out, "Ah, Lord GOD![^9] Will you destroy all the remnant of Israel when you pour out your fury on Jerusalem?"

**9** He said to me, "The guilt of the house of Israel and Judah is very, very great. The land is full of bloodshed, and the city is full of injustice.[^10] For they say, 'The LORD has abandoned the land, and the LORD does not see.'

**10** As for me, my eye will not pity, and I will not spare. I will bring their ways down on their heads."

**11** Then the man clothed in linen, with the writing case at his waist, brought back word: "I have done everything you commanded me."[^11]

[^1]: Or "you executioners of the city". Hebrew *pequddot*, "punishments", or "those appointed".
[^2]: Literally "the scribe's case", holding pens and ink. Also in verses 3 and 11.
[^3]: Hebrew *keruv* (plural *keruvim*, "cherubim"): a winged heavenly being who guards holy places (Genesis 3:24). Two gold cherubim spread their wings over the ark in the most holy place (1 Kings 6:23–28; 8:6–7).
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Hebrew *tav*, the name of the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet, which in the old script was written like an X or a cross. Also in verse 6.
[^6]: Hebrew *to'evot*, things that are abhorrent, especially in worship.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "your eyes" but read "your eye".
[^8]: Literally "the men, the elders".
[^9]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^10]: Hebrew *mutteh*, "what is bent, perverted", found only here (compare 7:10).
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "as you commanded me" but read "according to all that you commanded me".
