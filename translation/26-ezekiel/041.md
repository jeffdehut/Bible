**1** Then he brought me into the main hall[^1] and measured the jambs:[^2] six cubits[^3] wide on one side and six cubits wide on the other, the width of the tent.[^4]

**2** The entrance was ten cubits wide, and the side walls of the entrance were five cubits on one side and five cubits on the other. He measured its length: forty cubits; and its width: twenty cubits.

**3** Then he went inside and measured the jambs of the entrance: two cubits; the entrance: six cubits; and the width of the entrance: seven cubits.[^5]

**4** He measured its length: twenty cubits; and its width: twenty cubits, at the far end of the main hall. He said to me, "This is the most holy place."[^6]

**5** Then he measured the wall of the house:[^7] six cubits thick; and the width of the side rooms[^8] all around the house: four cubits.

**6** The side rooms were one above another, thirty-three of them.[^9] There were ledges in the wall of the house all around for the side rooms, so that they would be supported, but they were not fastened into the wall of the house.[^10]

**7** The side rooms grew wider as they went around, story by story upward, for the structure around the house went up story by story all around the house. So the house was wider at the top, and one went up from the lowest story to the highest by way of the middle one.[^11]

**8** I saw that the house had a raised platform all around. The foundations of the side rooms were a full reed, six cubits to the edge.[^12]

**9** The thickness of the outer wall of the side rooms was five cubits. There was an open area[^13] beside the side structure of the house.

**10** Between the rooms there was a width of twenty cubits all around the house, on every side.[^14]

**11** The doorways of the side rooms opened onto the open area, one doorway toward the north and one doorway toward the south. The width of the open area was five cubits all around.

**12** The building that faced the separate yard[^15] on the west side[^16] was seventy cubits wide. The wall of the building was five cubits thick all around, and its length was ninety cubits.

**13** Then he measured the house: a hundred cubits long; and the separate yard and the building with its walls: a hundred cubits long.

**14** The width of the front of the house and of the separate yard on the east was a hundred cubits.

**15** He measured the length of the building facing the separate yard at the back of the house, with its galleries[^17] on either side: a hundred cubits. The main hall, the inner room[^18] and the porches[^19] of the court,

**16** the thresholds, the narrow windows[^20] and the galleries all around the three of them, opposite the threshold, were paneled with wood all around, from the floor up to the windows; and the windows were covered.

**17** Above the entrance, as far as the inner room and on the outside, and on all the walls all around, inside and outside, were measured panels.[^21]

**18** They were carved with cherubim[^22] and palm trees, with a palm tree between cherub and cherub. Each cherub had two faces:

**19** a human face toward the palm tree on one side, and a lion's face toward the palm tree on the other side. They were carved on the whole temple all around.

**20** From the floor to above the entrance, cherubim and palm trees were carved on the wall of the main hall.[^23]

**21** The doorposts of the main hall were square. In front of the holy place was something that looked like this:[^24]

**22** an altar of wood, three cubits high and two cubits long, with its corners. Its length[^25] and its walls were of wood. He said to me, "This is the table[^26] that is before the LORD."[^27]

**23** The main hall and the holy place each had a double door.

**24** Each door had two leaves, two leaves that swung: two leaves for one door and two leaves for the other.

**25** On the doors of the main hall were carved cherubim and palm trees like those carved on the walls. There was a canopy[^28] of wood on the front of the porch outside.

**26** There were narrow windows and palm trees on either side, on the side walls of the porch, and on the side rooms of the house and on the canopies.

[^1]: Hebrew *heikhal*, "palace, temple": here the large main room of the temple, in front of the most holy place, as in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:3, 17). Traditionally "the temple" or "the nave".
[^2]: Hebrew *elim*, probably the thick walls on either side of a passage (40:9). Also in verse 3.
[^3]: The long cubit of 40:5, about 21 inches (53 cm). A reed (verse 8) was six such cubits, about 10½ feet (3.2 m).
[^4]: Literally "the width of the tent". The meaning is uncertain.
[^5]: The Septuagint reads "and the side walls of the entrance were seven cubits on either side".
[^6]: Literally "the holy of holies": the innermost room. Its measurements are those of the inner sanctuary of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:20).
[^7]: That is, the temple, as throughout chapters 40–48.
[^8]: Hebrew *tsela*, "rib, side": rooms built against the outside of the temple walls, as in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:5–10).
[^9]: Literally "side room to side room, three and thirty times". Many take this to mean three stories of thirty rooms each.
[^10]: Compare 1 Kings 6:6. Literally "and they came into the wall that belonged to the house, for the side rooms all around, to be held, but they were not held in the wall of the house".
[^11]: The meaning of this verse is uncertain. *The structure around* (Hebrew *musav*) may also mean a winding stair.
[^12]: Hebrew *atsilah*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "to the edge" or "to the joint".
[^13]: Hebrew *munnach*, "what is left": a free space or terrace. Also in verse 11. The meaning of verses 9–11 is uncertain.
[^14]: Probably the rooms in the courts (40:17; 42:1–14). The open space between them and the temple was twenty cubits wide.
[^15]: Hebrew *gizrah*, "cut-off area": an enclosed yard behind the temple, separated from the rest. Also in verses 13–15 and 42:1, 10, 13.
[^16]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean.
[^17]: Hebrew *attiqim*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "galleries", "terraces" or "passages". Also in verse 16 and 42:3, 5.
[^18]: Literally "and the inner main hall". The meaning is uncertain.
[^19]: Hebrew *ulam*: an entrance hall or vestibule (40:7).
[^20]: Hebrew *atumot*, perhaps "narrow" or "latticed" (40:16). Also in verse 26.
[^21]: Hebrew *middot*, "measurements". The meaning of this verse is uncertain.
[^22]: Hebrew *keruvim* (singular *keruv*, "cherub"): winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (Genesis 3:24; Exodus 25:18–22). Solomon's temple had carvings of cherubim and palm trees (1 Kings 6:29).
[^23]: In the Hebrew text, dots are written over the word for "main hall", a scribal mark of doubt.
[^24]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "The main hall: a doorpost squared; and the front of the holy place: the appearance like the appearance." *The holy place* is probably the inner room (verse 4).
[^25]: The Septuagint reads "two cubits long and two cubits wide", and "its base" for "its length".
[^26]: Perhaps the table for the bread of the Presence (Exodus 25:23–30), or an altar of incense (Exodus 30:1–10), which stood in front of the inner room.
[^27]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^28]: Hebrew *av*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps a canopy, an overhang, or a threshold (1 Kings 7:6). Also in verse 26.
