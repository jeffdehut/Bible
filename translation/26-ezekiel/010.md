**1** I looked, and there on the dome[^1] above the heads of the cherubim[^2] was something like lapis lazuli,[^3] with the appearance of the likeness of a throne, which appeared above them.

**2** He spoke to the man clothed in linen and said, "Go in between the whirling wheels[^4] underneath the cherub. Fill your hands with burning coals from among the cherubim and scatter them over the city." And he went in as I watched.

**3** Now the cherubim were standing on the south side[^5] of the house when the man went in, and the cloud filled the inner court.

**4** Then the glory of Yahweh[^6] rose from the cherub to the threshold of the house. The house was filled with the cloud, and the court was full of the brightness of the glory of Yahweh.

**5** The sound of the wings of the cherubim could be heard as far as the outer court, like the voice of God Almighty[^7] when he speaks.

**6** When he commanded the man clothed in linen, "Take fire from between the whirling wheels, from among the cherubim," the man went in and stood beside a wheel.[^8]

**7** Then a cherub stretched out his hand from among the cherubim to the fire that was among the cherubim. He took some of it and put it into the hands of the man clothed in linen, who took it and went out.

**8** The cherubim appeared to have the form of human hands under their wings.

**9** I looked, and there were four wheels beside the cherubim, one wheel beside each cherub,[^9] and the appearance of the wheels was like the gleam of beryl.[^10]

**10** As for their appearance, the four of them had the same likeness, like a wheel within a wheel.

**11** When they went, they went in any of their four directions; they did not turn as they went. In whatever direction the head faced,[^11] they went after it; they did not turn as they went.

**12** Their whole body, their backs, their hands and their wings, and the wheels, were full of eyes all around, the wheels of all four of them.

**13** As for the wheels, they were called in my hearing "the whirling wheels".

**14** Each one had four faces: the first face was the face of the cherub, the second face was a human face, the third the face of a lion, and the fourth the face of an eagle.[^12]

**15** Then the cherubim rose up. These were the living creatures[^13] that I had seen by the river Chebar.

**16** When the cherubim went, the wheels went beside them, and when the cherubim lifted their wings to rise from the ground, the wheels did not turn from beside them either.

**17** When the cherubim stood still, the wheels stood still, and when they rose, the wheels rose with them, for the spirit of the living creatures[^14] was in them.

**18** Then the glory of Yahweh went out from over the threshold of the house and stood over the cherubim.

**19** The cherubim lifted their wings and rose from the ground before my eyes as they went out, with the wheels beside them. They stopped at the entrance of the east gate of the house of Yahweh, and the glory of the God of Israel was over them above.

**20** These were the living creatures that I had seen beneath the God of Israel by the river Chebar, and I knew that they were cherubim.

**21** Each had four faces and each had four wings, and under their wings was the likeness of human hands.

**22** As for the likeness of their faces, they were the same faces that I had seen by the river Chebar.[^15] Each of them went straight ahead.

[^1]: Hebrew *raqia*. See 1:22.
[^2]: Hebrew *keruvim*, winged heavenly beings (9:3).
[^3]: Hebrew *sappir*, traditionally "sapphire". See 1:26.
[^4]: Hebrew *galgal*, "wheel, whirl", a different word from *ofan*, "wheel", in verses 6 and 9–19. Also in verses 6 and 13.
[^5]: Literally "on the right".
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^7]: Hebrew *El Shaddai*, traditionally "God Almighty"; the meaning of *Shaddai* is uncertain.
[^8]: Hebrew *ofan*, the word used for the wheels in chapter 1.
[^9]: Literally "one wheel beside one cherub, and one wheel beside one cherub".
[^10]: Hebrew *tarshish*. See 1:16.
[^11]: Or "the front", or "the first one".
[^12]: In 1:10 the four faces are those of a human being, a lion, an ox and an eagle. The Septuagint does not have this verse.
[^13]: Hebrew *chayyah*, "living creature", in the singular, used of the whole group, as in 1:20–22. Also in verses 17 and 20.
[^14]: Or "the spirit of life". See 1:20.
[^15]: Literally "the faces that I had seen by the river Chebar, their appearance and themselves". The meaning of the last words is uncertain.
