**1** "'When you allot the land as an inheritance, you are to set apart a contribution[^1] for Yahweh,[^2] a holy portion of the land, twenty-five thousand long and ten thousand wide.[^3] It will be holy throughout its whole area all around.

**2** Of this, a square of five hundred by five hundred is to be for the sanctuary, with fifty cubits[^4] of open land around it.

**3** From this measured area you are to measure off a section twenty-five thousand long and ten thousand wide, and in it will be the sanctuary, the most holy place.

**4** It is the holy portion of the land. It will be for the priests who serve in the sanctuary, who come near to serve Yahweh. It will be a place for their houses and a holy area for the sanctuary.

**5** An area twenty-five thousand long and ten thousand wide will belong to the Levites who serve the house,[^5] as their property, for twenty rooms.[^6]

**6** Alongside the holy contribution you are to assign as the city's property an area five thousand wide and twenty-five thousand long. It will belong to the whole house of Israel.

**7** The prince[^7] is to have the land on both sides of the holy contribution and the city's property, alongside the holy contribution and the city's property, on the west side westward and on the east side eastward. Its length will correspond to one of the tribal portions, from the western border to the eastern border.[^8]

**8** This land will be his property in Israel. My princes will no longer oppress my people, but will give the rest of the land to the house of Israel according to their tribes.

**9** This is what the Lord Yahweh[^9] says: Enough, you princes of Israel! Put away violence and oppression, and do what is just and right. Stop evicting my people,[^10] declares the Lord Yahweh.

**10** You are to have honest scales, an honest ephah and an honest bath.[^11]

**11** The ephah and the bath are to be the same size, the bath holding a tenth of a homer and the ephah a tenth of a homer. The homer[^12] is to be the standard measure for both.

**12** The shekel is to be twenty gerahs. Twenty shekels, twenty-five shekels and fifteen shekels are to be your mina.[^13]

**13** This is the contribution you are to present: a sixth of an ephah from each homer of wheat, and a sixth of an ephah from each homer of barley.

**14** The prescribed portion of oil, measured by the bath, is a tenth of a bath from each kor.[^14] Ten baths make a homer, for ten baths are a homer.

**15** And one sheep is to be given from every flock of two hundred, from the well-watered pastures of Israel, for grain offerings,[^15] burnt offerings[^16] and fellowship offerings,[^17] to make atonement[^18] for the people, declares the Lord Yahweh.

**16** All the people of the land are to give this contribution to the prince in Israel.

**17** It will be the prince's duty to provide the burnt offerings, the grain offerings and the drink offerings at the festivals, the new moons and the Sabbaths, at all the appointed festivals of the house of Israel. He is to provide the sin offering,[^19] the grain offering, the burnt offering and the fellowship offerings, to make atonement for the house of Israel.

**18** This is what the Lord Yahweh says: In the first month, on the first day of the month, you are to take a young bull[^20] without defect and purify the sanctuary from sin.[^21]

**19** The priest is to take some of the blood of the sin offering and put it on the doorposts of the house, on the four corners of the ledge of the altar, and on the doorposts of the gate of the inner court.

**20** You are to do the same on the seventh day of the month[^22] for anyone who sins unintentionally or through ignorance. So you will make atonement for the house.

**21** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month, you are to celebrate the Passover, a festival lasting a week,[^23] when unleavened bread is to be eaten.

**22** On that day the prince is to provide, for himself and for all the people of the land, a bull as a sin offering.

**23** During the seven days of the festival he is to provide as a burnt offering to Yahweh seven bulls and seven rams without defect, each day for the seven days, and a male goat each day as a sin offering.

**24** As a grain offering he is to provide an ephah for each bull and an ephah for each ram, with a hin[^24] of oil for each ephah.

**25** In the seventh month, on the fifteenth day of the month, at the festival,[^25] he is to provide the same for seven days: the same sin offerings, burnt offerings, grain offerings and oil.

[^1]: Hebrew *terumah*: something set apart and given for a sacred purpose (44:30). Also in verses 6, 7, 13 and 16, and in chapter 48.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: The unit is not given; probably the long cubit of 40:5, so that the area was about 8 miles by 3 miles (13 by 5.3 km). The Septuagint reads "twenty thousand wide", which fits verses 3–5 and 48:8–13. The area is described again in 48:8–22.
[^4]: The long cubit of 40:5, about 21 inches (53 cm).
[^5]: That is, the temple, as throughout chapters 40–48.
[^6]: The Septuagint reads "for cities to live in".
[^7]: Hebrew *nasi*, "leader, chief", the ruler of Israel (44:3). Also in verses 8, 9, 16, 17 and 22.
[^8]: The land is divided into strips running from west to east, one for each tribe (chapter 48).
[^9]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh".
[^10]: Literally "Lift your evictions from my people".
[^11]: The ephah, a dry measure, and the bath, a liquid measure, were the same size (verse 11): probably about 22 liters, two-thirds of a bushel or about 6 gallons.
[^12]: A homer was probably about 220 liters (6 bushels). The word means "donkey-load".
[^13]: Together sixty shekels. A shekel was about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams) and a gerah about 1/50 ounce (0.6 gram), so this mina was about 1½ pounds (690 grams).
[^14]: A kor was the same size as a homer, about 220 liters.
[^15]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute": an offering of flour or grain (Leviticus 2). Also in verses 17, 24 and 25.
[^16]: Hebrew *olah*: an animal burned whole on the altar (40:38; Leviticus 1).
[^17]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings" (Leviticus 3).
[^18]: Hebrew *kipper*, traditionally "make atonement": "wipe clean, purge" (43:20). Also in verses 17 and 20.
[^19]: Hebrew *chatta't*. Or "purification offering" (40:39). Also in verses 19, 22, 23 and 25.
[^20]: Literally "a bull, a son of the herd".
[^21]: Hebrew *chitte'*, from the same root as *chatta't*, "sin offering" (43:20).
[^22]: The Septuagint reads "in the seventh month, on the first day of the month".
[^23]: Literally "a festival of weeks of days".
[^24]: A hin was about a gallon (4 liters).
[^25]: The Festival of Shelters (Leviticus 23:33–36).
