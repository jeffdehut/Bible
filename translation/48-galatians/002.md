**1** Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along also.

**2** I went up because of a revelation, and I laid before them the good news that I proclaim among the Gentiles, but privately, before those who were regarded as leaders,[^1] for fear that I might be running, or had run, in vain.

**3** But not even Titus, who was with me, was compelled to be circumcised, though he was a Greek.

**4** But because of the false brothers[^2] secretly brought in, who slipped in to spy on the freedom that we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might enslave us —

**5** we did not give in to them and submit even for a moment, so that the truth of the good news might remain with you.

**6** And from those who were regarded as important—what they once were makes no difference to me; God shows no partiality[^3]—for those leaders added nothing to my message.[^4]

**7** On the contrary, they saw that I had been entrusted with the good news for the uncircumcised, just as Peter had been with the good news for the circumcised

**8** (for he who was at work in Peter for his apostleship to the circumcised was also at work in me for the Gentiles).

**9** And when James and Cephas[^5] and John, who were regarded as pillars, recognized the grace that had been given to me, they gave me and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship, agreeing that we should go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised.

**10** Only, we were to remember the poor, the very thing I was also eager to do.

**11** But when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned.

**12** For before certain people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles. But when they came,[^6] he drew back and separated himself, because he was afraid of the circumcision group.[^7]

**13** And the rest of the Jews joined him in this hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy.

**14** But when I saw that they were not walking in step with the truth of the good news, I said to Cephas in front of them all, "If you, though you are a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how can you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?"[^8]

**15** We ourselves are Jews by birth and not Gentile sinners;

**16** yet we know that a person is not declared righteous[^9] by works of the Law but[^10] through faith in Jesus Christ.[^11] So we too have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be declared righteous by faith in Christ and not by works of the Law, because by works of the Law no human being[^12] will be declared righteous.

**17** But if, in seeking to be declared righteous in Christ, we ourselves have also been found to be sinners, is Christ then a servant of sin? By no means![^13]

**18** For if I build up again the very things I tore down, I show myself to be a lawbreaker.

**19** For through the Law I died to the Law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ.

**20** It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh[^14] I live by faith in the Son of God,[^15] who loved me and gave himself for me.

**21** I do not set aside the grace of God; for if righteousness comes through the Law, then Christ died for nothing.

[^1]: Literally "those who seemed", that is, those regarded as important; also in verses 6 and 9.
[^2]: Or "false believers". Greek *pseudadelphoi*.
[^3]: Literally "God does not receive the face of a human being". Compare Deuteronomy 10:17.
[^4]: Literally "for to me those who seemed added nothing". Or "imposed nothing more on me".
[^5]: The Aramaic name of Peter (see 1:18); also in verses 11 and 14. Many manuscripts read "Peter" in verses 11 and 14.
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "when he came".
[^7]: Literally "those of circumcision", probably Jewish believers who insisted that Gentile believers be circumcised; or Jews in general.
[^8]: Greek *ioudaizo*, "live as a Jew", adopting Jewish customs. Paul's words to Cephas may end here, or may continue to the end of verse 21.
[^9]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify"; also in verse 17.
[^10]: Greek *ean me*, usually "except", here generally understood as "but only".
[^11]: Or "through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ". Likewise "by faith in Christ" later in the verse may be "by the faithfulness of Christ". Literally "through faith of Jesus Christ" and "from faith of Christ".
[^12]: Literally "no flesh". Compare Psalm 143:2.
[^13]: Greek *me genoito*, "may it not be", a strong denial.
[^14]: That is, in this bodily life. Greek *sarx*, "flesh", here not in Paul's moral sense.
[^15]: Or "by the faithfulness of the Son of God". Some manuscripts read "by faith in God and Christ".
