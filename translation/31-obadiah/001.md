**1** The vision[^1] of Obadiah.[^2] This is what the Lord Yahweh[^3] says concerning Edom:[^4] We have heard a report from Yahweh, and an envoy has been sent among the nations: "Rise up! Let us rise against her for battle!"[^5]

**2** Look, I will make you small among the nations; you will be utterly despised.[^6]

**3** The pride of your heart has deceived you, you who live in the clefts of the rock,[^7] whose dwelling is on the heights, who say in your heart, "Who will bring me down to the ground?"

**4** Though you build as high as the eagle,[^8] though your nest is set among the stars, from there I will bring you down, declares Yahweh.[^9]

**5** If thieves came to you, if robbers by night (how you will be destroyed!),[^10] would they not steal only what they wanted? If grape pickers came to you, would they not leave gleanings?

**6** How Esau[^11] has been searched out, his hidden treasures hunted down!

**7** All your allies[^12] have driven you to the border. The men who were at peace with you have deceived you and overpowered you. Those who eat your bread set a trap under you.[^13] There is no understanding in him.[^14]

**8** On that day, declares Yahweh, will I not destroy the wise from Edom, and understanding from the mountain of Esau?[^15]

**9** Your warriors will be terrified, Teman,[^16] so that everyone will be cut off from the mountain of Esau by slaughter.[^17]

**10** Because of the violence done to your brother Jacob,[^18] shame will cover you, and you will be cut off forever.

**11** On the day you stood aloof,[^19] on the day strangers carried off his wealth and foreigners entered his gates[^20] and cast lots for Jerusalem, you too were like one of them.

**12** Do not gloat[^21] over your brother on the day of his misfortune. Do not rejoice over the people of Judah on the day of their ruin. Do not boast[^22] on the day of distress.

**13** Do not enter the gate of my people on the day of their disaster.[^23] Do not gloat, you too, over his suffering on the day of his disaster. Do not lay hands on his wealth on the day of his disaster.

**14** Do not stand at the crossroads[^24] to cut off his fugitives. Do not hand over his survivors on the day of distress.

**15** For the day of Yahweh is near, against all the nations. As you have done, it will be done to you; what you have dealt out will come back on your own head.

**16** For just as you[^25] drank on my holy mountain, so all the nations will drink without ceasing. They will drink and gulp it down,[^26] and be as though they had never been.

**17** But on Mount Zion there will be those who escape, and it will be holy. The house of Jacob will take possession of their own possessions.[^27]

**18** The house of Jacob will be a fire, the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau stubble. They will set it ablaze and consume it, and there will be no survivor of the house of Esau, for Yahweh has spoken.

**19** The people of the Negev will possess the mountain of Esau, and the people of the Shephelah the land of the Philistines.[^28] They will possess the territory of Ephraim and the territory of Samaria, and Benjamin will possess Gilead.

**20** The exiles of this army of the Israelites will possess what belongs to the Canaanites as far as Zarephath,[^29] and the exiles of Jerusalem who are in Sepharad[^30] will possess the cities of the Negev.

**21** Deliverers[^31] will go up on Mount Zion to rule[^32] the mountain of Esau, and the kingdom will belong to Yahweh.

[^1]: Hebrew *chazon*, a revelation that a prophet "saw" (Isaiah 1:1).
[^2]: The name means "servant of Yahweh". Nothing more is known of the prophet. Verses 11–14 seem to describe the fall of Jerusalem to Babylon in 587 or 586 BC, when the Edomites joined in the plunder (Psalm 137:7; Lamentations 4:21–22; Ezekiel 35:5, 15).
[^3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD". *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Edom, the land of the descendants of Esau, lay south and east of the Dead Sea. Verses 1–9 closely parallel Jeremiah's oracle against Edom (Jeremiah 49:7–22): verses 1–4 match Jeremiah 49:14–16, verse 5 Jeremiah 49:9, verse 6 Jeremiah 49:10, and verse 8 echoes Jeremiah 49:7. One prophet may have drawn on the other, or both on an older oracle.
[^5]: Jeremiah 49:14 has "I have heard" and "Gather together and come against her".
[^6]: Or "Look, I have made you small among the nations; you are utterly despised." The Hebrew verb is in the past tense, describing what Yahweh has determined as already done.
[^7]: Hebrew *sela*, "rock", perhaps also the name of Sela, an Edomite stronghold (2 Kings 14:7). Much of Edom was rugged mountain country, where later the city of Petra was cut into the cliffs.
[^8]: Or "vulture". Hebrew *nesher* is used of both.
[^9]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verse 8.
[^10]: Literally "how you have been destroyed", a cry that breaks into the sentence. Thieves and grape pickers leave something behind, but those who destroy Edom will leave nothing.
[^11]: The brother of Jacob and ancestor of Edom (Genesis 36:1). The name stands for the people of Edom.
[^12]: Literally "the men of your covenant".
[^13]: Literally "your bread they set as a trap under you". The meaning of the word for trap, *mazor*, is uncertain. It may mean "wound".
[^14]: That is, Edom does not see what is happening (verse 8). Or "There is no understanding of it".
[^15]: Edom was known for its wisdom (Jeremiah 49:7; Job 2:11).
[^16]: A region of Edom, here standing for the whole land.
[^17]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate join "by slaughter" to verse 10: "Because of the slaughter and the violence done to your brother Jacob".
[^18]: Jacob, the ancestor of Israel, was Esau's twin brother (Genesis 25:24–26).
[^19]: Literally "stood opposite", that is, stood by and watched.
[^20]: The Hebrew text is written "his gate" but read "his gates".
[^21]: Literally "Do not look on", that is, with pleasure at another's ruin. The Hebrew of verses 12–14 uses the form of a command, as though the day were still happening. Or "You should not have gloated", and so through verse 14.
[^22]: Literally "make your mouth large".
[^23]: Hebrew *eidam*, "their disaster", and *eido*, "his disaster", sound like *Edom*.
[^24]: Hebrew *pereq*, found only here and in Nahum 3:1, where it means "plunder". Here perhaps a fork in the road or a mountain pass by which refugees escaped.
[^25]: The Hebrew changes here from "you" (one person) to "you" (many). It may refer to the people of Judah, who drank the cup of Yahweh's anger, or to the Edomites, who drank in celebration on Mount Zion. On the cup of Yahweh's anger, see Jeremiah 25:15–29 and 49:12.
[^26]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Perhaps "stagger".
[^27]: The same consonants can be read "will dispossess those who dispossessed them", as the Septuagint reads.
[^28]: The Negev was the dry country in the south of Judah, and the Shephelah the low hills in the west, toward the coastal plain. *The people of* is added: literally "the Negev will possess … and the Shephelah …".
[^29]: The Hebrew of the first half of the verse is difficult. Literally "the exiles of this army of the Israelites, which Canaanites as far as Zarephath". *This army* may instead be read "Halah", a place in Assyria to which Israelites were exiled (2 Kings 17:6), with a small change to the Hebrew. Zarephath was a town on the coast between Tyre and Sidon (1 Kings 17:9).
[^30]: Probably Sardis, in western Asia Minor. In later Hebrew *Sepharad* became the name for Spain.
[^31]: Hebrew *moshi'im*, "saviors", as in the book of Judges (Judges 3:9, 15). The Septuagint reads "those who have been rescued".
[^32]: Hebrew *shafat*, "judge", here in the sense of govern, as the judges ruled Israel.
