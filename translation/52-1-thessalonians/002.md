**1** For you yourselves know, brothers and sisters,[^1] that our visit to you was not in vain,

**2** but though we had already suffered and been shamefully treated at Philippi, as you know, we had the courage in our God to speak to you the good news of God in the face of much opposition.

**3** For our appeal does not come from error or impure motives, nor is it made with deceit,

**4** but just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the good news, so we speak, not to please people, but to please God, who tests our hearts.[^2]

**5** For we never came with words of flattery, as you know, nor with a pretext for greed (God is witness),

**6** nor did we seek glory from people, whether from you or from others,

**7** although we could have made our weight felt[^3] as apostles of Christ. Instead we were gentle[^4] among you, like a nursing mother caring for her own children.

**8** So, because we cared for you so much, we were glad to share with you not only the good news of God but also our own lives, because you had become dear to us.

**9** For you remember, brothers and sisters, our labor and toil. We worked night and day so as not to be a burden to any of you while we proclaimed to you the good news of God.

**10** You are witnesses, and God also, how holy and righteous and blameless was our conduct toward you who believe,

**11** just as you know how we treated each one of you, as a father treats his own children,[^5]

**12** encouraging you, comforting you, and urging you to live[^6] in a way worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory.

**13** And for this reason we also thank God constantly, that when you received the word of God that you heard from us,[^7] you accepted it not as a human word but as what it really is, the word of God, which is also at work in you who believe.

**14** For you, brothers and sisters, became imitators of the churches of God in Christ Jesus that are in Judea, because you suffered the same things from your own countrymen as they did from the Jews,[^8]

**15** who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets,[^9] and drove us out. They do not please God and are hostile to all people,

**16** hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles so that they may be saved. So they always fill up the measure of their sins.[^10] But wrath has come upon them at last.[^11]

**17** But we, brothers and sisters, when we were torn away[^12] from you for a short time, in person but not in heart, were all the more eager, with great longing, to see you face to face.

**18** For we wanted to come to you (I, Paul, more than once[^13]), but Satan[^14] hindered us.

**19** For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting (is it not you?) before our Lord Jesus at his coming?[^15]

**20** For you are our glory and our joy.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 9, 14 and 17.
[^2]: Compare Jeremiah 11:20.
[^3]: Or "we could have been a burden to you" (compare verse 9). Literally "to be in weight". In some English Bibles these words are the end of verse 6.
[^4]: Greek *epioi*. Some manuscripts read *nepioi*, "infants".
[^5]: The Greek has no verb here; literally "how each one of you, as a father his own children, encouraging you …".
[^6]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^7]: Literally "having received from us a word of hearing of God".
[^8]: Greek *Ioudaioi*, "Jews" or "Judeans". The comparison is with the opposition that the churches in Judea faced from their own people.
[^9]: Or "who killed the Lord Jesus, and drove out the prophets and us". Many manuscripts read "their own prophets".
[^10]: Literally "so as always to fill up their sins".
[^11]: Or "completely", or "to the end". Greek *eis telos*.
[^12]: Greek *aporphanizo*, "make an orphan of".
[^13]: Literally "both once and twice".
[^14]: Greek *Satanas*, from Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, accuser".
[^15]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord Jesus. The word recurs in 3:13; 4:15; 5:23.
