**1** I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at ease in my house and flourishing in my palace.[^1]

**2** I had a dream, and it made me afraid. As I lay on my bed, the images and the visions that passed through my head terrified me.

**3** So I issued a decree that all the wise men of Babylon should be brought in before me to make known to me the interpretation of the dream.

**4** Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners came in, and I told them the dream, but they did not make known to me its interpretation.

**5** At last Daniel came in before me. His name is Belteshazzar, after the name of my god, and the spirit of the holy gods[^2] is in him. I told him the dream:

**6** "Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you, and that no mystery is too difficult for you. Tell me the visions of my dream that I saw, and its interpretation.[^3]

**7** These were the visions that passed through my head as I lay on my bed: I was looking, and there was a tree in the middle of the earth, and its height was great.

**8** The tree grew large and strong. Its top reached to the sky, and it could be seen to the ends of the whole earth.

**9** Its leaves were beautiful and its fruit plentiful, and on it was food for all. Under it the wild animals found shade, and in its branches the birds of the sky lived, and from it every living creature was fed.[^4]

**10** In the visions that passed through my head as I lay on my bed, I looked, and there was a watcher, a holy one,[^5] coming down from heaven.

**11** He called out loudly and said, 'Cut down the tree and cut off its branches! Strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit! Let the animals flee from under it and the birds from its branches.

**12** But leave the stump with its roots in the ground, bound with iron and bronze, in the grass of the field. Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven,[^6] and let his share be with the animals in the grass of the earth.

**13** Let his mind be changed from a human mind,[^7] and let him be given the mind of an animal. And let seven periods of time[^8] pass over him.

**14** The sentence is by the decree of the watchers, and the decision by the word of the holy ones, so that the living may know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of human beings. He gives it to whomever he wishes, and he sets over it the lowliest of people.'

**15** This is the dream that I, King Nebuchadnezzar, saw. Now you, Belteshazzar, tell me its interpretation, for none of the wise men of my kingdom can make known to me its interpretation. But you can, because the spirit of the holy gods is in you."

**16** Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was dismayed for a while,[^9] and his thoughts terrified him. The king said, "Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its interpretation alarm you." Belteshazzar answered, "My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you, and its interpretation for your enemies!

**17** The tree that you saw, which grew large and strong, whose top reached to the sky and which could be seen through the whole earth,

**18** whose leaves were beautiful and whose fruit was plentiful, on which was food for all, under which the wild animals lived, and in whose branches the birds of the sky nested,

**19** it is you, O king! You have grown great and strong. Your greatness has grown and reaches to the sky, and your rule to the ends of the earth.

**20** And as for the watcher, a holy one, whom the king saw coming down from heaven and saying, 'Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump with its roots in the ground, bound with iron and bronze, in the grass of the field. Let him be drenched with the dew of heaven, and let his share be with the wild animals, until seven periods of time pass over him,'

**21** this is the interpretation, O king, and it is the decree of the Most High that has come upon my lord the king:

**22** You will be driven away from people, and you will live with the wild animals. You will be fed grass like oxen and be drenched with the dew of heaven, and seven periods of time will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of human beings and gives it to whomever he wishes.

**23** And as it was commanded to leave the stump with the roots of the tree, your kingdom will be secure for you from the time you know that Heaven[^10] rules.

**24** Therefore, O king, may my advice be acceptable to you. Break off[^11] your sin[^12] by doing what is right,[^13] and your iniquities by showing mercy to the oppressed. Perhaps your prosperity will be prolonged."

**25** All this came upon King Nebuchadnezzar.

**26** At the end of twelve months he was walking on the roof of the royal palace of Babylon.

**27** The king said, "Is this not great Babylon, which I have built as a royal residence by my mighty power and for the glory of my majesty?"

**28** The words were still in the king's mouth when a voice came down from heaven: "To you it is declared, King Nebuchadnezzar: the kingdom has departed from you.

**29** You will be driven away from people, and you will live with the wild animals. You will be fed grass like oxen, and seven periods of time will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of human beings and gives it to whomever he wishes."

**30** At that very moment the word was fulfilled against Nebuchadnezzar. He was driven away from people and ate grass like oxen, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven, until his hair grew long like eagles' feathers and his nails like birds' claws.[^14]

**31** At the end of that time,[^15] I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my reason returned to me. I blessed the Most High, and I praised and honored the one who lives forever, for his rule is an everlasting rule, and his kingdom lasts from generation to generation.

**32** All who live on the earth are counted as nothing. He does as he wishes with the host of heaven and with those who live on the earth. No one can hold back his hand or say to him, "What have you done?"

**33** At that time my reason returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom my majesty and splendor returned to me. My advisers and my nobles sought me out, and I was restored to my kingdom, and still greater majesty was added to me.

**34** Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise, exalt and honor the King of heaven, for all his works are true and his ways are just, and he is able to humble those who walk in pride.

[^1]: Verses 1–34 are 4:4–37 in most English Bibles. The Septuagint text of chapters 4–6 differs widely from the Aramaic.
[^2]: Or "the spirit of the holy God". Aramaic *ruach elahin qaddishin*. Also in verses 6 and 15.
[^3]: The king then tells the dream himself. The words may mean "Here are the visions of my dream that I saw; tell me its interpretation."
[^4]: Literally "all flesh was fed".
[^5]: Aramaic *ir*, "a watcher", from a word meaning "to be awake": an angel, one of the heavenly beings who keep watch. "A watcher, a holy one" may describe one being or two. Also in verses 14 and 20.
[^6]: Here the image passes from the tree to the person it stands for.
[^7]: Literally "Let his heart be changed from man".
[^8]: Aramaic *iddanin*, "times", probably years. Also in verses 20, 22 and 29.
[^9]: Literally "for about one hour", that is, a short time.
[^10]: A reverent way of referring to God.
[^11]: Or "Atone for". Aramaic *peruq*, "break off, set free, redeem".
[^12]: The Aramaic text is written "your sins" but read "your sin".
[^13]: Aramaic *tsidqah*, "righteousness". In later Jewish usage the word also means gifts to the poor.
[^14]: A Dead Sea Scroll, the Prayer of Nabonidus (4Q242), tells a similar story of Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon, who was struck with an illness for seven years while he lived at Teima in Arabia.
[^15]: Literally "At the end of the days".
