**1** In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon,[^1] Daniel had a dream, and visions passed through his head as he lay on his bed. Then he wrote down the dream and told the main points of the account.[^2]

**2** Daniel said,[^3] "I was looking in my vision by night, and there were the four winds of heaven stirring up the great sea.

**3** Four great beasts came up out of the sea, each different from the others.

**4** The first was like a lion and had eagle's wings. As I watched, its wings were plucked off, and it was lifted up from the ground and made to stand on two feet like a human being, and a human mind was given to it.

**5** Then there was another beast, a second one, like a bear. It was raised up on one side, and it had three ribs in its mouth between its teeth. It was told, 'Get up, devour much flesh!'

**6** After this I looked, and there was another, like a leopard. It had four wings of a bird on its back, and the beast had four heads, and it was given authority to rule.

**7** After this I was looking in the visions of the night, and there was a fourth beast, terrifying and dreadful and extremely strong. It had great iron teeth. It devoured and crushed, and it trampled what was left with its feet. It was different from all the beasts before it, and it had ten horns.

**8** While I was considering the horns, there was another horn, a small one, coming up among them, and three of the first horns were uprooted before it. This horn had eyes like human eyes and a mouth speaking arrogant words.[^4]

**9** As I looked, thrones were set in place, and the Ancient of Days[^5] took his seat. His clothing was white as snow, and the hair of his head like pure wool. His throne was flames of fire, and its wheels were burning fire.

**10** A river of fire was flowing, coming out from before him. Thousands upon thousands served him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him.[^6] The court sat in judgment, and the books were opened.

**11** Then I kept looking because of the sound of the arrogant words that the horn was speaking. As I watched, the beast was killed, and its body destroyed and given over to the burning fire.

**12** As for the rest of the beasts, their authority was taken away, but their lives were prolonged for a season and a time.

**13** I was looking in the visions of the night, and there, with the clouds of heaven, one like a son of man[^7] was coming. He came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him.

**14** He was given authority, glory and kingship, so that all peoples, nations and languages would serve him.[^8] His authority is an everlasting authority that will not pass away, and his kingdom is one that will not be destroyed.

**15** As for me, Daniel, my spirit was distressed within me,[^9] and the visions that passed through my head terrified me.

**16** I approached one of those standing there and asked him the truth about all this. So he told me and made known to me the interpretation of these things:

**17** 'These great beasts, which are four, are four kings who will arise from the earth.

**18** But the holy ones of the Most High[^10] will receive the kingdom, and they will possess the kingdom forever, forever and ever.'

**19** Then I wanted to know the truth about the fourth beast, which was different from all the others, extremely terrifying, with teeth of iron and claws of bronze, which devoured and crushed and trampled what was left with its feet,

**20** and about the ten horns on its head, and the other horn that came up, before which three fell: the horn that had eyes and a mouth speaking arrogant words, and that looked greater than the others.

**21** As I watched, this horn was making war against the holy ones and was prevailing over them,

**22** until the Ancient of Days came and judgment was given in favor of the holy ones of the Most High,[^11] and the time came when the holy ones took possession of the kingdom.

**23** This is what he said: 'The fourth beast will be a fourth kingdom on earth, which will be different from all the kingdoms. It will devour the whole earth, trample it down and crush it.

**24** As for the ten horns, out of this kingdom ten kings will arise, and another will arise after them. He will be different from the earlier ones, and he will bring down three kings.

**25** He will speak words against the Most High and wear down the holy ones of the Most High. He will try to change the sacred seasons and the law,[^12] and they will be given into his hand for a time, times and half a time.[^13]

**26** But the court will sit in judgment, and his authority will be taken away, to be destroyed and abolished forever.

**27** Then the kingship and the authority and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole heaven will be given to the people of the holy ones of the Most High. Their kingdom will be an everlasting kingdom, and all rulers will serve and obey them.'[^14]

**28** Here is the end of the account. As for me, Daniel, my thoughts greatly terrified me, and my face turned pale, but I kept the matter in my heart."

[^1]: Perhaps about 550 BC, when Nabonidus left Belshazzar in charge of Babylon (see 5:1).
[^2]: Literally "he said the head of the words". The meaning of the Aramaic is uncertain.
[^3]: Literally "Daniel answered and said".
[^4]: Literally "great things". Also in verses 11 and 20.
[^5]: Aramaic *attiq yomin*, "one advanced in days", an aged figure: God seated as judge. Here the phrase has no article ("one ancient of days"); in verses 13 and 22 it is "the Ancient of Days".
[^6]: Literally "A thousand thousands served him, and a myriad of myriads stood before him".
[^7]: Aramaic *kevar enash*, "like a son of man", that is, one in human form, in contrast to the beasts. *Bar enash*, like Hebrew *ben adam*, means "a human being" (compare 8:17). In the interpretation (verses 18, 22 and 27) the kingdom is given to "the holy ones of the Most High". Interpreters have understood the figure as a symbol of God's faithful people, as an angel such as Michael (10:21; 12:1), or as a heavenly ruler. Later Jewish writings (1 Enoch 37–71; 4 Ezra 13) and some rabbinic teachers took him to be the Messiah. In the Gospels, Jesus' title "the Son of Man" draws on this verse (Mark 13:26; 14:62), and Revelation 1:7, 13 alludes to it.
[^8]: Aramaic *pelach*, "serve", the verb used for serving God or the gods (3:12, 14, 17–18, 28; 6:17, 21). Also in verse 27.
[^9]: Literally "within its sheath", the body as the sheath of the spirit.
[^10]: Aramaic *qaddishe elyonin*. In the Old Testament "holy ones" usually means angels (4:10, 14; 8:13; Psalm 89:6, 8), but here it may mean God's faithful people (compare verse 27, "the people of the holy ones of the Most High"). *Elyonin*, "Most High", is plural in form, probably as a mark of majesty. Also in verses 22, 25 and 27.
[^11]: Or "the right to judge was given to the holy ones of the Most High".
[^12]: Literally "the times and the law", probably the sacred calendar and festivals and the law of God. Many interpreters identify the small horn with Antiochus IV Epiphanes, king of the Seleucid empire from 175 to 164 BC, who banned the Jewish festivals and the observance of the Law (1 Maccabees 1:41–64; compare Daniel 8:9–12; 11:21–45). Others see a later ruler.
[^13]: That is, probably three and a half years. Compare 12:7 and Revelation 12:14.
[^14]: Literally "His kingdom … serve and obey him". The singular pronoun may refer to the people or to the Most High.
