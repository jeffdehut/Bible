**1** In the first year of Darius son of Ahasuerus, a Mede by descent,[^1] who was made king over the kingdom of the Babylonians,[^2]

**2** in the first year of his reign, I, Daniel, understood from the books the number of years that, according to the word of the LORD[^3] to Jeremiah the prophet, must pass until the desolation of Jerusalem came to an end: seventy years.[^4]

**3** So I turned my face to the Lord God,[^5] to seek him by prayer and pleas for mercy, with fasting, sackcloth and ashes.

**4** I prayed to the LORD my God and made confession. I said, "Ah, Lord, the great and awesome God, who keeps covenant and faithful love with those who love him and keep his commands,[^6]

**5** we have sinned and done wrong. We have acted wickedly and rebelled, turning away from your commands and your rulings.

**6** We have not listened to your servants the prophets, who spoke in your name to our kings, our officials and our ancestors, and to all the people of the land.

**7** To you, Lord, belongs righteousness, but to us open shame,[^7] as it is this day: to the people of Judah, to those who live in Jerusalem, and to all Israel, those near and those far away, in all the lands to which you have driven them because of the unfaithfulness they have committed against you.

**8** LORD, to us belongs open shame: to our kings, our officials and our ancestors, because we have sinned against you.

**9** To the Lord our God belong compassion and forgiveness, though we have rebelled against him.[^8]

**10** We have not obeyed the voice of the LORD our God by living according to his instructions,[^9] which he set before us through his servants the prophets.

**11** All Israel has broken your law and turned away, refusing to obey your voice. So the curse and the oath written in the Law of Moses, the servant of God, have been poured out on us, because we have sinned against him.[^10]

**12** He has carried out his word[^11] that he spoke against us and against our rulers who ruled us,[^12] by bringing on us a great disaster. Under the whole heaven nothing has ever been done like what has been done to Jerusalem.

**13** Just as it is written in the Law of Moses, all this disaster has come on us. Yet we have not sought the favor of the LORD our God[^13] by turning from our iniquities and paying heed to your truth.

**14** So the LORD kept watch over the disaster and brought it on us, for the LORD our God is righteous in all the works he has done, and we have not obeyed his voice.

**15** And now, Lord our God, who brought your people out of the land of Egypt with a mighty hand and made a name for yourself, as it is this day, we have sinned, we have acted wickedly.

**16** Lord, in keeping with all your righteous acts, let your anger and your wrath turn away from your city Jerusalem, your holy mountain. Because of our sins and the iniquities of our ancestors, Jerusalem and your people have become a disgrace to all who are around us.

**17** Now, our God, listen to the prayer of your servant and to his pleas for mercy. For the Lord's sake,[^14] make your face shine on your desolate sanctuary.

**18** Turn your ear, my God, and hear. Open your eyes and see our desolations and the city that is called by your name.[^15] We do not lay our pleas before you because of our righteous acts, but because of your great compassion.

**19** Lord, hear! Lord, forgive! Lord, listen and act! Do not delay, for your own sake, my God, for your city and your people are called by your name."

**20** While I was still speaking and praying, confessing my sin and the sin of my people Israel, and laying my plea before the LORD my God for the holy mountain of my God,

**21** while I was still speaking in prayer, the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the first, came to me in swift flight[^16] at about the time of the evening offering.[^17]

**22** He gave me understanding[^18] and spoke with me. He said, "Daniel, I have now come out to give you insight and understanding.

**23** At the beginning of your pleas for mercy a word went out, and I have come to tell it to you, for you are greatly loved.[^19] So consider the word and understand the vision.

**24** Seventy weeks[^20] are decreed for your people and your holy city, to finish the transgression, to put an end to sin,[^21] to atone for iniquity, to bring in everlasting righteousness, to seal up vision and prophet, and to anoint a most holy place.[^22]

**25** Know and understand this: from the going out of the word to restore and rebuild Jerusalem until an anointed one, a ruler,[^23] there will be seven weeks. Then for sixty-two weeks it will be built again, with square and moat,[^24] but in troubled times.[^25]

**26** After the sixty-two weeks, an anointed one will be cut off and will have nothing.[^26] The people of a ruler who is to come will destroy the city and the sanctuary. Its end will come with a flood,[^27] and to the end there will be war. Desolations are decreed.

**27** He will make a firm covenant with many[^28] for one week, and for half of the week he will put a stop to sacrifice and offering. And on a wing of abominations will come one who makes desolate,[^29] until the end that is decreed is poured out on the one who makes desolate."

[^1]: Literally "from the seed of Media". On Darius the Mede, see 6:1.
[^2]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "the Chaldeans". See 1:4.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. This chapter is the only place in Daniel where the name is used.
[^4]: Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10.
[^5]: Hebrew *Adonai ha-Elohim*. *Adonai*, "the Lord", is also used in verses 4, 7, 9, 15–17 and 19.
[^6]: Compare Deuteronomy 7:9; Nehemiah 1:5. *Faithful love* is Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^7]: Literally "shame of face". Also in verse 8.
[^8]: Or "for we have rebelled against him".
[^9]: Hebrew *torot*, the plural of *torah*, "instruction, law".
[^10]: See Leviticus 26:14–39; Deuteronomy 28:15–68; 29:19–20 (29:20–21 in most English Bibles).
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "his words" but read "his word".
[^12]: Literally "our judges who judged us".
[^13]: Literally "we have not softened the face of the LORD our God".
[^14]: Literally "For the sake of the Lord". Theodotion reads "For your own sake, Lord".
[^15]: Literally "over which your name is called". Also in verse 19.
[^16]: Or "when I was completely weary". The meaning of the Hebrew *mu'af bi'af* is uncertain: it may come from a word meaning "to fly" or from one meaning "to be weary".
[^17]: Hebrew *minchat erev*, offered in the late afternoon (1 Kings 18:36; Ezra 9:4–5).
[^18]: The Septuagint and Theodotion read "He came", a reading that differs from the Hebrew by one letter.
[^19]: Literally "you are precious things". Also in 10:11, 19.
[^20]: Hebrew *shavu'im shiv'im*, "seventy sevens": seventy periods of seven, usually understood as weeks of years, 490 years in all (compare Leviticus 25:8). The seventy years of Jeremiah's prophecy (verse 2) become seventy times seven years. Also in verses 25–27.
[^21]: The Hebrew text is written "to seal up sins" but read "to put an end to sin".
[^22]: Or "a most holy one". Hebrew *qodesh qodashim*, "holy of holies", which elsewhere means the most holy place in the tabernacle and temple (Exodus 26:33–34) or the most holy things of the sanctuary (Exodus 29:37; 30:29). Some ancient and later interpreters understood it of a person.
[^23]: Hebrew *mashiach nagid*, "an anointed one, a ruler" or "an anointed ruler". *Mashiach* means one anointed with oil, as kings and priests were. Interpreters who follow the Masoretic accents (see the next note) have identified this figure with Cyrus, whom Isaiah 45:1 calls the LORD's anointed, or with Zerubbabel or the high priest Joshua after the exile (Ezra 3:2). Christian tradition read here the coming of the Messiah, Jesus: "unto the Messiah the Prince" (King James Version).
[^24]: Hebrew *rechov ve-charuts*, "open square and moat". *Charuts* may also mean "decreed".
[^25]: The translation follows the Masoretic accents, which separate the seven weeks from the sixty-two weeks. The Vulgate and older English translations such as the King James Version join them: "there will be seven weeks and sixty-two weeks; the street will be built again".
[^26]: Literally "and there will not be to him". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "and will have no one" or "and will be no more". Many modern interpreters identify this anointed one with the high priest Onias III, who was murdered in about 171 BC (2 Maccabees 4:33–35), and the coming ruler with Antiochus IV (see 8:9). Christian tradition has understood the verse of the death of Jesus and the destruction of Jerusalem by the Romans in AD 70.
[^27]: Or "His end will come in a flood", referring to the ruler.
[^28]: Hebrew *la-rabbim*, "with the many", a phrase that recurs in 11:33, 39; 12:3. *He* is probably the coming ruler of verse 26.
[^29]: The Hebrew is difficult. "Wing" may mean a corner or a high point of the temple or its altar. *Shiqqutsim meshomem*, "abominations that make desolate", is echoed in 11:31 and 12:11 ("the abomination that causes desolation"), probably a pagan altar or image set up in the temple in 167 BC (1 Maccabees 1:54). The phrase is taken up in Mark 13:14 and Matthew 24:15.
