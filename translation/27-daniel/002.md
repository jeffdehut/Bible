**1** In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar's reign,[^1] Nebuchadnezzar had dreams. His spirit was troubled, and he could not sleep.[^2]

**2** So the king gave orders to summon the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans,[^3] to tell the king his dreams. They came in and stood before the king.

**3** The king said to them, "I have had a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream."

**4** Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic:[^4] "O king, live forever! Tell your servants the dream, and we will give its interpretation."

**5** The king answered the Chaldeans, "This is my firm decision:[^5] if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be torn limb from limb,[^6] and your houses will be made a dunghill.[^7]

**6** But if you tell me the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts, a reward and great honor. So tell me the dream and its interpretation."

**7** They answered a second time, "Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will give its interpretation."

**8** The king answered, "I know for certain that you are trying to gain time,[^8] because you see that my decision is firm.

**9** If you do not make the dream known to me, there is only one sentence for you.[^9] You have agreed together[^10] to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the situation changes.[^11] So tell me the dream, and I will know that you can give me its interpretation."

**10** The Chaldeans answered the king, "There is no one on earth who can tell the king what he asks. That is why no king, however great and powerful, has ever asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter or Chaldean.

**11** What the king asks is too difficult. There is no one who can tell it to the king except the gods, and they do not live among mortals."[^12]

**12** At this the king became furious and very angry, and he ordered all the wise men of Babylon to be destroyed.

**13** So the decree went out that the wise men were to be killed,[^13] and they looked for Daniel and his companions to kill them.

**14** Then Daniel spoke with prudence and discretion[^14] to Arioch, the captain of the king's guard,[^15] who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

**15** He said to Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the decree from the king so harsh?"[^16] Then Arioch explained the matter to Daniel.

**16** So Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, and he would tell the king the interpretation.

**17** Then Daniel went to his house and told the matter to his companions Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**18** so that they would seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**19** Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**20** Daniel said,[^17] "Blessed be the name of God forever and ever, for wisdom and power belong to him.

**21** He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to those who have understanding.

**22** He reveals deep and hidden things; he knows what is in the darkness, and light lives with him.

**23** To you, God of my ancestors, I give thanks and praise, for you have given me wisdom and power. Now you have made known to me what we asked of you; you have made known to us what the king asked."[^18]

**24** So Daniel went to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said to him, "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon. Bring me in before the king, and I will tell the king the interpretation."

**25** Then Arioch quickly brought Daniel in before the king and said to him, "I have found a man among the exiles from Judah[^19] who can make known the interpretation to the king."

**26** The king said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to make known to me the dream I saw and its interpretation?"

**27** Daniel answered the king, "No wise men, enchanters, magicians or diviners[^20] can tell the king the mystery he has asked about.

**28** But there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will happen in the last days.[^21] This is your dream, the visions that passed through your head as you lay on your bed:

**29** As for you, O king, as you lay on your bed your thoughts turned to what would happen after this, and the revealer of mysteries has made known to you what will happen.

**30** As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me not because I have more wisdom than anyone else alive, but so that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and so that you may understand the thoughts of your heart.[^22]

**31** You were looking, O king, and there before you was a great statue.[^23] That statue was huge, and its brightness was extraordinary. It stood in front of you, and its appearance was terrifying.

**32** The head of the statue was of fine gold, its chest and arms of silver, its belly and thighs of bronze,

**33** its legs of iron, and its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.[^24]

**34** As you watched, a stone was cut out, not by human hands.[^25] It struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and smashed them.

**35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold were all smashed together and became like chaff on the threshing floors in summer. The wind carried them away, and not a trace of them could be found.[^26] But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

**36** This was the dream, and now we will tell the king its interpretation.

**37** You, O king, are the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the might and the glory.

**38** He has given into your hand human beings, the wild animals and the birds of the sky, wherever they live, and he has made you ruler over them all. You are that head of gold.

**39** After you another kingdom will arise, inferior to you, and then a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over the whole earth.[^27]

**40** Then there will be a fourth kingdom, strong as iron. Just as iron smashes and shatters everything, and as iron crushes things,[^28] it will smash and crush all these.

**41** As you saw, the feet and toes were partly of potter's clay and partly of iron. So it will be a divided kingdom, but some of the strength of iron will be in it, just as you saw the iron mixed with the wet clay.[^29]

**42** As the toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay, so the kingdom will be partly strong and partly brittle.

**43** And as you saw the iron mixed with the wet clay, so they will mix with one another by marriage,[^30] but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay.

**44** In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and that kingdom will not be left to another people. It will smash all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, but it will stand forever.

**45** That is the meaning of the stone you saw cut out of the mountain, not by human hands, that smashed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver and the gold.[^31] A great God has made known to the king what will happen after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation is trustworthy."

**46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and bowed down to Daniel,[^32] and he gave orders to present an offering and incense to him.[^33]

**47** The king said to Daniel, "Truly your God is the God of gods, the Lord of kings and a revealer of mysteries, for you have been able to reveal this mystery."

**48** Then the king gave Daniel a high position and many great gifts. He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon.

**49** At Daniel's request, the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego over the administration of the province of Babylon, while Daniel remained at the king's court.[^34]

[^1]: About 603 BC.
[^2]: Literally "and his sleep was done upon him". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^3]: Hebrew *kasdim*, the name of the people of southern Mesopotamia (1:4). Here and in chapters 3–5 it is used for a class of Babylonian scholars, experts in astrology and divination. The *mekhashshefim*, "sorcerers", are those who practice *keshef*, forbidden magic (Exodus 7:11; 22:17; Deuteronomy 18:10). On the magicians and enchanters, see 1:20.
[^4]: From "O king" to the end of chapter 7 the book is in Aramaic, the common language of the Babylonian and Persian empires, not in Hebrew. The words "in Aramaic" may also be a note to the reader marking the change of language (compare Ezra 4:7).
[^5]: Or "The word has gone out from me". Aramaic *azda*, a word borrowed from Persian, perhaps "certain" or "publicly announced". Also in verse 8.
[^6]: Literally "made into limbs".
[^7]: Or "a ruin".
[^8]: Literally "that you are buying time".
[^9]: Aramaic *dat*, "law, decree", here the sentence that will be passed on them.
[^10]: The Aramaic text is written "You have prepared" but read "You have agreed together".
[^11]: Literally "until the time changes".
[^12]: Literally "except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh".
[^13]: Or "and the wise men were being killed".
[^14]: Literally "returned counsel and judgment".
[^15]: Literally "the chief of the executioners" or "the chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal guard (Genesis 37:36; 2 Kings 25:8).
[^16]: Or "so urgent".
[^17]: Literally "Daniel answered and said". Aramaic often introduces speech in this way, even where nothing has been asked.
[^18]: Literally "the matter of the king".
[^19]: Literally "the sons of the exile of Judah".
[^20]: Aramaic *gazerin*, "those who decide", probably astrologers who determined fate from the stars.
[^21]: Literally "at the end of the days", that is, in the future.
[^22]: Or "of your mind".
[^23]: Aramaic *tselem*, "image": a statue, perhaps the image of a god (compare 3:1).
[^24]: Aramaic *chasaf*, baked clay or earthenware, as in pottery. Also in verses 34–35 and 41–45.
[^25]: Literally "not by hands". Also in verse 45.
[^26]: Literally "no place was found for them".
[^27]: The kingdoms that follow Nebuchadnezzar's are not named. Many interpreters understand them as Media, Persia and Greece (compare 5:28; 8:20–21); others as Medo-Persia, Greece and Rome.
[^28]: Or "as iron that crushes all these". The Aramaic can be read either way.
[^29]: Literally "the clay of mud". Also in verse 43.
[^30]: Literally "they will be mixed by the seed of humankind", probably meaning marriage alliances between royal houses (compare 11:6, 17).
[^31]: Or "Just as you saw that a stone was cut out of the mountain … and smashed the iron …, so a great God has made known".
[^32]: Aramaic *segid*, "bowed low, worshiped", the word used for worshiping the statue in chapter 3.
[^33]: Literally "an offering and pleasing aromas". The Aramaic *nichochin* recalls the "pleasing aroma" of the offerings in the Law (Leviticus 1:9).
[^34]: Literally "in the gate of the king".
