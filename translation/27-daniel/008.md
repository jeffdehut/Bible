**1** In the third year of the reign of King Belshazzar, a vision appeared to me, Daniel, after the one that had appeared to me before.[^1]

**2** I looked in the vision, and as I looked, I was in the fortress of Susa,[^2] in the province of Elam. I looked in the vision, and I was beside the Ulai Canal.[^3]

**3** I lifted my eyes and looked, and there was a ram standing beside the canal. It had two horns. Both horns were long, but one was longer than the other, and the longer one came up last.

**4** I saw the ram charging westward, northward and southward. No beast could stand before it, and there was no one who could rescue from its power. It did as it pleased and made itself great.

**5** As I was considering this, a male goat came from the west across the face of the whole earth, without touching the ground. The goat had a conspicuous horn between its eyes.

**6** It came to the ram with the two horns, which I had seen standing beside the canal, and it charged at it in furious strength.

**7** I saw it reach the ram. It was enraged against it, and it struck the ram and broke its two horns. The ram had no strength to stand before it. It threw the ram to the ground and trampled on it, and there was no one who could rescue the ram from its power.

**8** The male goat made itself very great. But at the height of its power the great horn was broken, and in its place four conspicuous horns came up toward the four winds of heaven.

**9** Out of one of them came another horn, a small one,[^4] which grew exceedingly great toward the south, toward the east and toward the beautiful land.[^5]

**10** It grew as high as the host of heaven, and it threw down to the earth some of the host and some of the stars, and trampled on them.

**11** It made itself great, even up to the commander of the host.[^6] The regular offering was taken away from him,[^7] and the place of his sanctuary was thrown down.

**12** Because of transgression, the host was given over, together with the regular offering.[^8] It threw truth to the ground, and it acted and succeeded.

**13** Then I heard a holy one speaking, and another holy one said to the one who was speaking,[^9] "How long will the vision last, concerning the regular offering, the transgression that makes desolate,[^10] and the giving over of both the sanctuary and the host to be trampled?"

**14** He said to me,[^11] "For 2,300 evenings and mornings.[^12] Then the sanctuary will be restored to its rightful state."[^13]

**15** While I, Daniel, was seeing the vision and trying to understand it, there stood before me one who looked like a man.[^14]

**16** And I heard a human voice from between the banks of the Ulai, and it called out, "Gabriel,[^15] make this man understand the vision."

**17** He came near where I stood, and when he came I was terrified and fell on my face. He said to me, "Understand, son of man,[^16] that the vision is for the time of the end."

**18** While he was speaking with me, I fell into a deep sleep with my face to the ground. Then he touched me and set me on my feet.[^17]

**19** He said, "I am going to make known to you what will happen at the end of the time of wrath,[^18] for it concerns the appointed time of the end.[^19]

**20** The ram that you saw with the two horns: these are the kings of Media and Persia.

**21** The shaggy male goat is the king of Greece,[^20] and the great horn between its eyes is the first king.[^21]

**22** As for the horn that was broken and the four that arose in its place: four kingdoms will arise from his nation, but not with his power.[^22]

**23** At the end of their rule, when the transgressors have reached their full measure, a king will arise, fierce-looking and skilled in intrigue.[^23]

**24** His power will be mighty, but not by his own power. He will cause astonishing destruction,[^24] and he will succeed in what he does. He will destroy the mighty and the people of the holy ones.[^25]

**25** By his cunning he will make deceit succeed under his hand, and in his heart he will make himself great. Without warning[^26] he will destroy many. He will even stand up against the Prince of princes, but he will be broken, and not by human hand.[^27]

**26** The vision of the evenings and the mornings that has been told is true. But you, keep the vision secret,[^28] for it refers to many days from now."

**27** And I, Daniel, was overcome[^29] and lay sick for some days. Then I got up and went about the king's business. But I was appalled by the vision, and no one could explain it.[^30]

[^1]: From here to the end of the book the text is in Hebrew again. The Aramaic section, 2:4–7:28, has ended.
[^2]: Or "in Susa the citadel". Susa was later one of the royal cities of the Persian kings (Nehemiah 1:1; Esther 1:2).
[^3]: Hebrew *uval*, probably "canal" or "watercourse"; the Septuagint and the Vulgate understood "gate". The Ulai flowed past Susa. Also in verses 3 and 6.
[^4]: Literally "a horn from smallness". This horn is probably Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175–164 BC), king of the Seleucid empire, one of the kingdoms that came from the empire of Alexander the Great (verses 21–22). In 167 BC he stopped the sacrifices in the temple in Jerusalem (1 Maccabees 1:20–64). Compare 7:8, 25; 11:21–45.
[^5]: Hebrew *ha-tsevi*, "the beauty, the glory", here the land of Israel (compare 11:16, 41; Jeremiah 3:19; Ezekiel 20:6).
[^6]: Hebrew *sar ha-tsava*, "the prince of the host": God himself, or an angelic commander of his armies (compare Joshua 5:14–15).
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "It took the regular offering away from him" but read "The regular offering was taken away from him". *Ha-tamid*, "the continual", is the regular burnt offering made every morning and evening (Exodus 29:38–42; Numbers 28:3–8). Also in verses 12–13.
[^8]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. It may also mean "A host was set over the regular offering in transgression".
[^9]: Hebrew *palmoni*, "a certain one", used of someone whose name is not given.
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-pesha shomem*. Compare "the abomination that causes desolation" in 9:27; 11:31 and 12:11.
[^11]: The Septuagint and Theodotion read "to him".
[^12]: Literally "evening morning": either 2,300 days, or 2,300 evening and morning offerings, that is, 1,150 days.
[^13]: Literally "will be put right" or "will be vindicated". The temple was cleansed and rededicated in December 164 BC (1 Maccabees 4:36–59), the origin of the festival of Hanukkah.
[^14]: Hebrew *gever*, "a man", which sounds like the first part of the name Gabriel in verse 16.
[^15]: Gabriel means "man of God" or "God is mighty". With Michael (10:13, 21; 12:1), he is one of the only two angels named in the Hebrew Bible. Compare Luke 1:19, 26.
[^16]: Hebrew *ben adam*, "a human being, a mortal", the title by which God addresses Ezekiel. Compare 7:13.
[^17]: Literally "made me stand where I stood".
[^18]: Hebrew *za'am*, God's anger against his people (compare Isaiah 10:5, 25).
[^19]: Or "for the end will come at the appointed time".
[^20]: Hebrew *Yavan*, Ionia: the Greeks.
[^21]: Alexander the Great, who conquered the Persian Empire between 334 and 330 BC and died in 323 BC.
[^22]: After Alexander's death his empire was divided among his generals. Among the kingdoms that came from it were the Ptolemaic kingdom in Egypt and the Seleucid kingdom in Syria and Mesopotamia.
[^23]: Literally "strong of face and understanding riddles".
[^24]: Literally "He will destroy wonderfully".
[^25]: Or "the holy people". Compare 7:27.
[^26]: Or "When they feel secure". Literally "in ease".
[^27]: Literally "and without a hand". Antiochus IV died of illness in 164 BC while on campaign in Persia (1 Maccabees 6:1–16).
[^28]: Literally "shut up the vision".
[^29]: Literally "was finished". The meaning of the Hebrew *nihyeti* is uncertain.
[^30]: Or "and I did not understand it". Literally "and there was no one who understood".
