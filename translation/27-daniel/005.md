**1** King Belshazzar[^1] gave a great feast for a thousand of his nobles, and he drank wine in front of the thousand.

**2** Under the influence of the wine,[^2] Belshazzar gave orders to bring in the vessels of gold and silver that his father[^3] Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the temple in Jerusalem, so that the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines could drink from them.

**3** So they brought in the vessels of gold that had been taken from the temple, the house of God in Jerusalem, and the king and his nobles, his wives and his concubines drank from them.

**4** They drank wine and praised the gods of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone.

**5** At that moment the fingers of a human hand appeared[^4] and wrote on the plaster of the wall of the king's palace, opposite the lampstand. And the king watched the hand[^5] as it wrote.

**6** Then the king's face turned pale,[^6] and his thoughts terrified him. His legs gave way,[^7] and his knees knocked together.

**7** The king called out loudly to bring in the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners. The king said to the wise men of Babylon, "Whoever reads this writing and tells me its interpretation will be clothed in purple and have a chain of gold around his neck, and he will rule as third in the kingdom."[^8]

**8** Then all the king's wise men came in, but they could not read the writing or make known its interpretation to the king.

**9** Then King Belshazzar was greatly terrified, and his face turned pale, and his nobles were bewildered.

**10** Because of the words of the king and his nobles, the queen[^9] came into the banquet hall. The queen said, "O king, live forever! Do not let your thoughts terrify you or your face turn pale.

**11** There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods.[^10] In the days of your father, light, insight and wisdom like the wisdom of the gods were found in him. King Nebuchadnezzar your father, your father the king, made him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners,

**12** because an extraordinary spirit, knowledge and insight, and the ability to interpret dreams, explain riddles and solve problems[^11] were found in this Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar. Now let Daniel be called, and he will tell the interpretation."

**13** Then Daniel was brought in before the king. The king said to Daniel, "Are you that Daniel, one of the exiles from Judah, whom the king my father brought from Judah?

**14** I have heard of you that the spirit of the gods[^12] is in you, and that light, insight and extraordinary wisdom are found in you.

**15** Now the wise men and the enchanters were brought in before me to read this writing and make known to me its interpretation, but they could not tell the interpretation of the words.

**16** But I have heard of you that you can give interpretations and solve problems. Now if you can read the writing and make known to me its interpretation, you will be clothed in purple and have a chain of gold around your neck, and you will rule as third in the kingdom."

**17** Then Daniel answered the king, "Keep your gifts for yourself, and give your rewards to someone else. But I will read the writing to the king and make known to him the interpretation.

**18** O king, the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father the kingdom, greatness, glory and majesty.

**19** Because of the greatness he gave him, all peoples, nations and languages trembled in fear before him. Whomever he wished he killed, and whomever he wished he kept alive; whomever he wished he raised up, and whomever he wished he brought low.

**20** But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit was hardened so that he acted proudly, he was brought down from his royal throne, and his glory was taken from him.

**21** He was driven away from human beings, and his mind was made like that of an animal. He lived with the wild donkeys, he was fed grass like oxen, and his body was drenched with the dew of heaven, until he knew that the Most High God rules over the kingdom of human beings and sets over it whomever he wishes.

**22** But you, his son Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this.

**23** You have lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven. The vessels of his house were brought before you, and you and your nobles, your wives and your concubines have drunk wine from them. You have praised the gods of silver and gold, bronze, iron, wood and stone, which do not see or hear or know. But the God who holds in his hand your breath and all your ways, you have not honored.

**24** So he sent the hand,[^13] and this writing was inscribed.

**25** This is the writing that was inscribed: *Mene, mene, tekel, u-pharsin.*[^14]

**26** This is the interpretation of the words: *Mene*: God has numbered your kingdom and brought it to an end.

**27** *Tekel*: you have been weighed on the scales and found lacking.

**28** *Peres*: your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians."

**29** Then at Belshazzar's command they clothed Daniel in purple, put a chain of gold around his neck, and proclaimed that he would rule as third in the kingdom.

**30** That very night Belshazzar the Chaldean king was killed.[^15]

[^1]: Belshazzar was the son of Nabonidus, the last king of Babylon, and ruled in Babylon while his father was away in Arabia. Babylonian records call him "the king's son", though one text says that Nabonidus entrusted the kingship to him.
[^2]: Or "When he had tasted the wine".
[^3]: Nebuchadnezzar was not Belshazzar's father in the usual sense. The Aramaic word can also mean "ancestor" or "predecessor". Also in verses 11, 13 and 18, and "son" in verse 22.
[^4]: Literally "came out".
[^5]: Aramaic *pas yeda*, perhaps the palm or the back of the hand. Also in verse 24.
[^6]: Literally "his brightness changed". Also in verses 9 and 10.
[^7]: Literally "the joints of his hips were loosened".
[^8]: Or "as one of three rulers in the kingdom". If Nabonidus was first and Belshazzar second, the third place may have been the highest that Belshazzar could give. Also in verses 16 and 29.
[^9]: Probably the queen mother, the widow of an earlier king; the king's wives are already at the feast (verses 2–3).
[^10]: Or "the spirit of the holy God". See 4:5.
[^11]: Literally "loosen knots", perhaps also meaning to undo spells. Also in verse 16.
[^12]: Or "the spirit of God".
[^13]: Literally "From his presence the palm of the hand was sent".
[^14]: The words are names of weights or coins: *mene*, a mina; *tekel*, a shekel; and *parsin* (with *u-*, "and"), half-minas or half-shekels. Daniel reads them as verbs: *mene*, "numbered"; *tekel*, "weighed"; *peres* (the singular of *parsin*), "divided". *Peres* also sounds like *Paras*, "Persia".
[^15]: Babylon fell to the army of Cyrus king of Persia in October 539 BC.
