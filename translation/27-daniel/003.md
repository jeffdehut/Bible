**1** King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold, sixty cubits high and six cubits wide,[^1] and he set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.

**2** Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent word to gather the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces,[^2] to come to the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3** So the satraps, the prefects, the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces gathered for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up, and they stood in front of the statue that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**4** Then the herald called out loudly, "This is the command to you, peoples, nations and languages:[^3]

**5** As soon as you hear the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe[^4] and every kind of music, you must fall down and worship the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**6** Whoever does not fall down and worship will immediately[^5] be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire."

**7** So as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither and every kind of music, all the peoples, nations and languages fell down and worshiped the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**8** At that time some Chaldeans[^6] came forward and denounced the Jews.[^7]

**9** They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "O king, live forever!

**10** You, O king, have issued a decree that everyone who hears the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe and every kind of music must fall down and worship the statue of gold,

**11** and that whoever does not fall down and worship will be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire.

**12** There are some Jews whom you appointed over the administration of the province of Babylon: Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men pay no attention to you, O king.[^8] They do not serve your gods, and they do not worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

**13** Then Nebuchadnezzar, in a furious rage, ordered Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego to be brought in. So these men were brought before the king.

**14** Nebuchadnezzar said to them, "Is it true,[^9] Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods, and that you do not worship the statue of gold that I have set up?

**15** Now if you are ready, as soon as you hear the sound of the horn, the pipe, the lyre, the harp, the zither, the bagpipe and every kind of music, to fall down and worship the statue I have made, well and good.[^10] But if you do not worship, you will immediately be thrown into a furnace of blazing fire. And who is the god who will rescue you from my hands?"

**16** Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar,[^11] we have no need to give you an answer in this matter.

**17** If our God whom we serve is able to rescue us, he will rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire and from your hand, O king.[^12]

**18** But even if he does not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods, and we will not worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

**19** Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the look on his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. He gave orders to heat the furnace seven times hotter than it was usually heated.

**20** He commanded some of the strongest men in his army to tie up Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and to throw them into the furnace of blazing fire.

**21** So these men were tied up in their cloaks, their tunics, their hats and their other clothes,[^13] and they were thrown into the furnace of blazing fire.

**22** Because the king's order was so harsh and the furnace so extremely hot, the flame of the fire killed the men who carried Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego up.

**23** And these three men, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, fell, tied up, into the furnace of blazing fire.[^14]

**24** Then King Nebuchadnezzar was astonished and jumped up in alarm. He said to his advisers, "Didn't we throw three men, tied up, into the fire?" They answered the king, "Certainly, O king."

**25** He said, "Look! I see four men, untied, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are unharmed. And the fourth looks like a son of the gods."[^15]

**26** Then Nebuchadnezzar came near the door of the furnace of blazing fire and said, "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, servants of the Most High God,[^16] come out! Come here!" Then Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego came out of the fire.

**27** The satraps, the prefects, the governors and the king's advisers gathered around and saw these men. The fire had had no power over their bodies. The hair of their heads was not singed, their cloaks were not harmed,[^17] and there was no smell of fire on them.

**28** Nebuchadnezzar said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who has sent his angel and rescued his servants who trusted in him. They defied the king's command[^18] and gave up their bodies rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.

**29** So I issue a decree: anyone of any people, nation or language who says anything against[^19] the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego will be torn limb from limb, and his house will be made a dunghill, for there is no other god who is able to rescue like this."

**30** Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the province of Babylon.

**31** King Nebuchadnezzar, to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth:[^20] May your peace be great!

**32** It is my pleasure to make known the signs and wonders that the Most High God has done for me.

**33** How great are his signs! How mighty are his wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his rule lasts from generation to generation.

[^1]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the statue was about 90 feet (27 m) high and 9 feet (2.7 m) wide.
[^2]: Titles of officials of the Babylonian and Persian empires, several of them borrowed from Persian. The exact duties of some are uncertain. The satraps governed the large regions of the Persian Empire (Esther 3:12). Also in verses 3 and 27.
[^3]: That is, peoples of every language. Also in verses 7, 29 and 31.
[^4]: The names of several of these instruments are borrowed from Greek. *Qatros* (Greek *kitharis*) is a lyre. *Sabbekha* (Greek *sambyke*) is a small harp. *Pesanterin* (Greek *psalterion*) is a stringed instrument, perhaps a zither. *Sumponyah* (Greek *symphonia*) may be a bagpipe or a drum, or it may mean "all together". Also in verses 7, 10 and 15.
[^5]: Literally "in that hour". Also in verse 15.
[^6]: Or "some Babylonians". The word can mean the Babylonian people or the class of Babylonian scholars (2:2).
[^7]: Literally "ate the pieces of the Jews", an Aramaic idiom for slandering or accusing someone.
[^8]: Literally "have not set a decree upon you".
[^9]: Or "Is it on purpose". The meaning of the Aramaic *hatseda* is uncertain.
[^10]: The Aramaic leaves this sentence unfinished, and "well and good" is supplied.
[^11]: Or "answered King Nebuchadnezzar". The Masoretic accents make "Nebuchadnezzar" the first word of their reply.
[^12]: Or "If it is so, our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the furnace of blazing fire, and he will rescue us from your hand, O king." The Aramaic can be read either way. The translation follows the Masoretic accents.
[^13]: The meanings of these Aramaic words, some of them borrowed from Persian, are uncertain. *Sarbal* may be a cloak or trousers, *pattish* a tunic or leggings, and *karbela* a hat or turban.
[^14]: After this verse the Greek versions of Daniel (the Septuagint and Theodotion) have a long passage that is not in the Hebrew and Aramaic text: the Prayer of Azariah, a description of the furnace, and the Song of the Three Young Men. The Greek Daniel also has two further stories, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon. These additions are part of the Old Testament in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles and are among the Apocrypha in Protestant Bibles.
[^15]: Aramaic *bar-elahin*, "a son of gods", that is, a divine being. In verse 28 Nebuchadnezzar calls him God's angel. Older translations have "the Son of God".
[^16]: Aramaic *elaha illa'a*, "the Most High God". Also in verse 32.
[^17]: Literally "were not changed".
[^18]: Literally "changed the word of the king".
[^19]: Or "anything careless against". Aramaic *shalu*, "negligence, a careless word".
[^20]: Verses 31–33 are 4:1–3 in most English Bibles. They begin Nebuchadnezzar's letter, which continues through chapter 4.
