**1** In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah,[^1] Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.

**2** The Lord[^2] gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, along with some of the vessels of the house of God. He brought them to the land of Shinar,[^3] to the house of his god, and he put the vessels in the treasury of his god.[^4]

**3** Then the king told Ashpenaz, his chief court official,[^5] to bring some of the Israelites, from the royal family and from the nobles:[^6]

**4** young men without any defect, good-looking, skilled in all wisdom, well informed and quick to learn,[^7] who had the ability to serve in the king's palace. He was to teach them the literature and language of the Babylonians.[^8]

**5** The king assigned them a daily ration[^9] from the king's food[^10] and from the wine he drank. They were to be educated for three years, and at the end of that time they were to enter the king's service.[^11]

**6** Among them, from the people of Judah,[^12] were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah.

**7** The chief official gave them names. He named Daniel Belteshazzar, Hananiah Shadrach, Mishael Meshach, and Azariah Abednego.[^13]

**8** But Daniel made up his mind[^14] not to defile himself with the king's food or with the wine he drank, and he asked the chief official to let him not defile himself.

**9** God caused the chief official to show Daniel kindness and compassion.[^15]

**10** The chief official said to Daniel, "I am afraid of my lord the king, who has assigned your food and your drink. Why should he see you looking worse[^16] than the other young men of your age? You would put my head in danger with the king."[^17]

**11** Then Daniel said to the guard[^18] whom the chief official had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah,

**12** "Please test your servants for ten days. Let us be given vegetables[^19] to eat and water to drink.

**13** Then compare our appearance with the appearance of the young men who eat the king's food,[^20] and deal with your servants according to what you see."

**14** He agreed to this[^21] and tested them for ten days.

**15** At the end of the ten days they looked better and healthier[^22] than all the young men who were eating the king's food.

**16** So the guard took away their food and the wine they were to drink, and gave them vegetables.

**17** To these four young men God gave knowledge and skill in all literature and wisdom. And Daniel had understanding of visions and dreams of every kind.

**18** At the end of the time the king had set for bringing them in, the chief official brought them before Nebuchadnezzar.

**19** The king talked with them, and among them all no one was found to equal Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. So they entered the king's service.

**20** In every matter of wisdom and understanding that the king asked them about, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters[^23] in his whole kingdom.

**21** And Daniel remained there until the first year of King Cyrus.[^24]

[^1]: About 605 BC. Jeremiah 25:1 and 46:2 place Nebuchadnezzar's first year in the fourth year of Jehoiakim; the difference may come from different ways of counting a king's years. Compare 2 Kings 24:1 and 2 Chronicles 36:5–7.
[^2]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH.
[^3]: An old name for Babylonia (Genesis 11:2).
[^4]: Or "of his gods", here and earlier in the verse. The Hebrew *elohim* can mean "God" or "gods".
[^5]: Hebrew *rav sarisav*, "the chief of his *sarisim*": court officials. The word can also mean eunuchs. Also in verses 7–11 and 18.
[^6]: Hebrew *partemim*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^7]: Literally "knowing knowledge and understanding learning".
[^8]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Their "literature" was the learned writings of Babylon, in cuneiform script. In chapters 2–5 the same word is used for a class of Babylonian scholars and diviners.
[^9]: Literally "the matter of a day in its day".
[^10]: Hebrew *pat-bag*, a word borrowed from Persian: the king's own fare or the rations from his table. Also in verses 8, 13, 15 and 16.
[^11]: Literally "to stand before the king". Also in verse 19.
[^12]: Literally "the sons of Judah".
[^13]: The Hebrew names honor the God of Israel: Daniel means "God is my judge", Hananiah "The LORD has been gracious", Mishael perhaps "Who is what God is?", and Azariah "The LORD has helped". The new names are Babylonian. Belteshazzar probably stands for a Babylonian name meaning "Protect his life"; 4:5 (4:8 in most English Bibles) links it with the god Bel. The meanings of Shadrach and Meshach are uncertain. Abednego probably means "servant of Nego", perhaps a form of Nabu, a god of Babylon.
[^14]: Literally "set it on his heart".
[^15]: Literally "God gave Daniel *chesed* and compassion before the chief official". *Chesed* is usually "faithful love"; here it is the goodwill the official showed him.
[^16]: Or "thinner", "more downcast".
[^17]: Literally "You would make my head guilty to the king".
[^18]: Hebrew *meltsar*, probably a title borrowed from Akkadian, "guard" or "steward". Older translations took it as a name, Melzar. Also in verse 16.
[^19]: Hebrew *zero'im*, "things grown from seed": vegetables, perhaps also grain. Verse 16 has a related word.
[^20]: Literally "Let our appearance and the appearance of the young men who eat the king's food be seen before you".
[^21]: Literally "He listened to them in this matter".
[^22]: Literally "their appearance was seen as good and fatter of flesh".
[^23]: Hebrew *chartummim* and *ashshafim*. *Chartummim*, a word borrowed from Egyptian, is used of the Egyptian magicians in Genesis 41 and Exodus 7–9. *Ashshafim* is related to an Akkadian word for priests who performed rituals of exorcism and healing.
[^24]: Cyrus king of Persia took Babylon in 539 BC. Daniel 10:1 dates a vision to the third year of Cyrus.
