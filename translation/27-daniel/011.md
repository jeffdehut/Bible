**1** "And as for me, in the first year of Darius the Mede, I stood up to support and strengthen him.[^1]

**2** Now I will tell you the truth. Three more kings will arise in Persia, and the fourth will be far richer than all of them. When he has grown strong through his riches, he will stir up everyone against the kingdom of Greece.[^2]

**3** Then a mighty king will arise, who will rule with great authority and do as he pleases.[^3]

**4** But as soon as he has arisen, his kingdom will be broken and divided toward the four winds of heaven. It will not go to his descendants, nor will it have the power with which he ruled, for his kingdom will be uprooted and given to others besides these.

**5** The king of the south will grow strong, but one of his commanders will grow stronger than he and will rule, and his dominion will be a great dominion.[^4]

**6** After some years they will make an alliance. The daughter of the king of the south will come to the king of the north to make an agreement, but she will not keep her power,[^5] and he and his power[^6] will not last. She will be given up, together with those who brought her, her father[^7] and the one who supported her in those times.[^8]

**7** A shoot from her roots will arise in his place.[^9] He will come against the army and enter the fortress of the king of the north. He will deal with them and prevail.

**8** He will also carry off to Egypt their gods, with their cast images[^10] and their precious vessels of silver and gold. Then for some years he will keep away from the king of the north.

**9** Then the king of the north[^11] will come into the realm of the king of the south, but he will return to his own land.

**10** His sons will prepare for war and assemble a multitude of great forces. One of them will keep coming, overflow and pass through, and again he will wage war as far as his fortress.[^12]

**11** Then the king of the south will be enraged and go out and fight against him, the king of the north. He will raise a great multitude, but the multitude will be given into his hand.[^13]

**12** When the multitude has been carried off, his heart will be lifted up. He will bring down tens of thousands, but he will not prevail.

**13** For the king of the north will again raise a multitude, greater than the first, and after some years[^14] he will come on with a great army and much equipment.

**14** In those times many will rise up against the king of the south. Violent men from among your own people will lift themselves up to fulfill the vision, but they will stumble.

**15** Then the king of the north will come and pile up a siege ramp and capture a fortified city. The forces of the south will not stand, not even his chosen troops, and there will be no strength to stand.

**16** The one who comes against him will do as he pleases, and no one will stand before him. He will stand in the beautiful land,[^15] and destruction will be in his hand.[^16]

**17** He will set his face to come with the strength of his whole kingdom, and he will make an agreement with him[^17] and carry it out. He will give him a daughter in marriage[^18] in order to destroy the kingdom,[^19] but she will not stand by him or be on his side.

**18** Then he will turn his face to the coastlands and capture many. But a commander will put an end to his insolence; indeed, he will turn his insolence back on him.[^20]

**19** Then he will turn his face back toward the fortresses of his own land, but he will stumble and fall and be found no more.[^21]

**20** In his place will arise one who sends an oppressor through the glory of the kingdom.[^22] But within a few days he will be broken, though not in anger or in battle.

**21** In his place will arise a contemptible person, who has not been given royal majesty. He will come in without warning[^23] and seize the kingdom through intrigue.[^24]

**22** Overwhelming forces will be swept away before him[^25] and broken, and also a prince of the covenant.[^26]

**23** After an alliance is made with him, he will act deceitfully. He will rise to power with only a small nation.

**24** Without warning he will come into the richest parts of the province and do what neither his ancestors nor their ancestors had done: he will scatter plunder, spoil and goods among them. He will devise plans against fortresses, but only for a time.

**25** He will stir up his strength and his courage against the king of the south with a great army, and the king of the south will wage war with a very great and powerful army, but he will not stand, because of the plots devised against him.[^27]

**26** Those who eat his food[^28] will destroy him. His army will be swept away,[^29] and many will fall slain.

**27** The two kings, their hearts set on evil, will sit at one table and speak lies to each other, but it will not succeed, for the end is still to come at the appointed time.

**28** He will return to his land with great wealth, but his heart will be set against the holy covenant. He will take action and return to his own land.[^30]

**29** At the appointed time he will come again into the south, but this time it will not be as it was before.

**30** Ships of Kittim[^31] will come against him, and he will lose heart. He will turn back and rage against the holy covenant and take action. He will return and pay heed to those who abandon the holy covenant.

**31** Forces sent by him[^32] will rise up and profane the sanctuary, the fortress. They will take away the regular offering[^33] and set up the abomination that causes desolation.[^34]

**32** Those who act wickedly against the covenant he will corrupt with flattery, but the people who know their God will stand firm and take action.

**33** Those among the people who are wise[^35] will give understanding to many, though for some days they will fall by sword and flame, by captivity and plunder.

**34** When they fall, they will receive a little help,[^36] and many will join them insincerely.

**35** Some of the wise will fall, so that they may be refined, purified and made white, until the time of the end, for it is still to come at the appointed time.

**36** The king will do as he pleases. He will exalt himself and make himself greater than every god, and he will say astonishing things against the God of gods. He will succeed until the wrath is completed, for what has been decreed will be done.

**37** He will pay no attention to the gods of his ancestors,[^37] or to the one desired by women,[^38] or to any god, for he will make himself greater than them all.

**38** Instead, he will honor a god of fortresses in their place.[^39] A god whom his ancestors did not know he will honor with gold and silver, with precious stones and costly gifts.

**39** He will deal with the strongest fortresses with the help of a foreign god.[^40] Those who acknowledge him he will greatly honor. He will make them rulers over many and distribute land as a reward.[^41]

**40** At the time of the end the king of the south will engage him in battle,[^42] but the king of the north will storm against him with chariots and horsemen and many ships. He will invade countries, overflow and pass through.

**41** He will enter the beautiful land, and many[^43] will fall. But these will escape from his hand: Edom, Moab and the leaders of the Ammonites.[^44]

**42** He will stretch out his hand against countries, and the land of Egypt will not escape.

**43** He will gain control of the treasures of gold and silver and all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Cushites[^45] will follow in his steps.

**44** But reports from the east and the north will alarm him, and he will go out in great fury to destroy many and devote them to destruction.[^46]

**45** He will pitch his royal tents[^47] between the seas and the beautiful holy mountain.[^48] Yet he will come to his end, and no one will help him.

[^1]: Probably Michael (10:21). The speech of the man dressed in linen (10:5) continues to 12:4. The kings in this chapter are not named; the notes give the rulers most interpreters identify them with.
[^2]: Probably Xerxes I (486–465 BC), who invaded Greece in 480 BC.
[^3]: Alexander the Great (336–323 BC). See 8:21–22.
[^4]: The king of the south is Ptolemy I of Egypt; the commander who became stronger is probably Seleucus I, who founded the Seleucid kingdom of Syria and Mesopotamia, the kingdom of "the king of the north".
[^5]: Literally "she will not retain the strength of the arm".
[^6]: Or "his offspring", reading the same consonants differently. Literally "his arm".
[^7]: Literally "the one who begot her". The same consonants can be read "her child".
[^8]: Probably Berenice, daughter of Ptolemy II, who married the Seleucid king Antiochus II in about 252 BC. After his death she and her infant son were murdered.
[^9]: Literally "on his base". Probably Berenice's brother Ptolemy III, who invaded the Seleucid kingdom in 246 BC.
[^10]: Or "their princes". Hebrew *nesikhehem*.
[^11]: Literally "he". Probably Seleucus II.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "His son … and they will wage war as far as its fortress" but read "His sons … and he will wage war as far as his fortress". The sons are probably Seleucus III and Antiochus III, who campaigned against Egypt.
[^13]: The king of the north will raise a great army, but it will be given into the hand of the king of the south: probably the defeat of Antiochus III by Ptolemy IV at Raphia in 217 BC.
[^14]: Literally "at the end of the times, years". Antiochus III returned and defeated Egypt at Panium in about 200 BC, and took control of Judea.
[^15]: The land of Israel. See 8:9.
[^16]: Or "and all of it will be in his hand".
[^17]: Or "he will bring an agreement with him". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^18]: Literally "the daughter of women". Probably Cleopatra I, daughter of Antiochus III, who married Ptolemy V in 194/193 BC.
[^19]: Literally "to destroy it" or "to destroy her".
[^20]: The second half of the verse is difficult in Hebrew. Antiochus III campaigned in Asia Minor and Greece and was defeated by the Romans under Lucius Scipio at Magnesia in 190 BC.
[^21]: Antiochus III was killed in 187 BC while plundering a temple in Elymais.
[^22]: Or "for the glory of the kingdom", that is, to raise money. Probably Seleucus IV (187–175 BC), whose minister Heliodorus tried to seize the temple treasury in Jerusalem (2 Maccabees 3). "The glory of the kingdom" may be the land of Israel or its temple. Seleucus IV was murdered.
[^23]: Literally "in ease". See 8:25. Also in verse 24. The contemptible person is Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175–164 BC), the small horn of 8:9.
[^24]: Literally "smooth things". Also in verse 34, "insincerely".
[^25]: Literally "The arms of the flood will be flooded before him".
[^26]: Perhaps the high priest Onias III, who was deposed in 175 BC and murdered in about 171 BC (2 Maccabees 4:7–10, 33–35; compare Daniel 9:26).
[^27]: Antiochus IV's first campaign against Egypt and Ptolemy VI, in 170–169 BC.
[^28]: Hebrew *pat-bag*. See 1:5.
[^29]: Or "His army will sweep everything away".
[^30]: On his return from Egypt in 169 BC Antiochus IV plundered the temple in Jerusalem (1 Maccabees 1:20–24).
[^31]: Hebrew *Kittim*, the people of Cyprus, later used of other peoples of the west. Here the Romans: in 168 BC a Roman envoy forced Antiochus IV to leave Egypt.
[^32]: Literally "Arms from him".
[^33]: See 8:11.
[^34]: Hebrew *ha-shiqquts meshomem*. In 167 BC a pagan altar was built on the altar of burnt offering in the temple (1 Maccabees 1:54, 59). Compare 9:27; 12:11; Mark 13:14.
[^35]: Hebrew *maskilim*, "those who have insight" or "those who give insight". Also in verse 35 and 12:3, 10.
[^36]: Perhaps the revolt led by Judas Maccabeus, which began in 167 BC.
[^37]: Or "the God of his ancestors".
[^38]: Probably a god popular among women, such as Tammuz or Adonis (compare Ezekiel 8:14).
[^39]: Literally "on his base".
[^40]: Or "He will man the strongest fortresses with the people of a foreign god", reading the same consonants differently (*am*, "people", for *im*, "with").
[^41]: Or "for a price".
[^42]: Literally "will butt against him" (compare 8:4). Verses 40–45 do not match what is known of the end of Antiochus IV, who died in Persia in 164 BC (1 Maccabees 6:1–16; see 8:25). Interpreters differ on whether they describe his end in general or symbolic terms, or the end of a ruler still to come.
[^43]: Or "many countries". The same consonants can be read "tens of thousands".
[^44]: Literally "the first of the sons of Ammon".
[^45]: The people of Cush, the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia).
[^46]: Hebrew *le-hacharim*, to devote to God by destroying completely (as in Joshua 6:17–21).
[^47]: Hebrew *appeden*, a word borrowed from Persian for a palace or a royal pavilion.
[^48]: Literally "the mountain of holy beauty": Mount Zion, where the temple stood. "The seas" may be the Mediterranean.
