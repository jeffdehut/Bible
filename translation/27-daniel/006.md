**1** And Darius the Mede[^1] received the kingdom, when he was about sixty-two years old.[^2]

**2** It pleased Darius to appoint 120 satraps over the kingdom, to be throughout the whole kingdom,

**3** and over them three chief officials,[^3] of whom Daniel was one. The satraps were to give account to them, so that the king would not suffer loss.

**4** Then this Daniel distinguished himself above the chief officials and the satraps, because an extraordinary spirit was in him, and the king planned to set him over the whole kingdom.

**5** Then the chief officials and the satraps tried to find grounds for a charge against Daniel in his conduct of the kingdom's affairs, but they could find no grounds and no corruption, because he was trustworthy, and no negligence or corruption was found in him.

**6** Then these men said, "We will not find any grounds for a charge against this Daniel unless we find something against him in the law of his God."

**7** Then these chief officials and satraps came as a group[^4] to the king and said to him, "King Darius, live forever!

**8** All the chief officials of the kingdom, the prefects and the satraps, the advisers and the governors have agreed that the king should issue a decree and enforce a ban: anyone who makes a request of any god or human being for thirty days, except of you, O king, will be thrown into the lions' pit.[^5]

**9** Now, O king, issue the ban and sign the document, so that it cannot be changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked."

**10** So King Darius signed the document, the ban.

**11** When Daniel knew that the document had been signed, he went into his house. In its upper room were windows open toward Jerusalem. Three times a day he knelt down, prayed and gave thanks before his God, just as he had done before.

**12** Then these men came as a group and found Daniel making his request and pleading for mercy before his God.

**13** So they came near and spoke before the king about the king's ban: "Did you not sign a ban that anyone who makes a request of any god or human being for thirty days, except of you, O king, will be thrown into the lions' pit?" The king answered, "The matter stands firm, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot be revoked."

**14** Then they said before the king, "Daniel, one of the exiles from Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the ban that you have signed. Three times a day he makes his request."

**15** When the king heard this, he was greatly distressed, and he set his mind on rescuing Daniel. Until the sun went down he did all he could to save him.

**16** Then these men came as a group to the king and said to the king, "Know, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians that no ban or decree that the king issues can be changed."

**17** So the king gave the order, and Daniel was brought and thrown into the lions' pit. The king said to Daniel, "May your God, whom you serve continually, rescue you!"[^6]

**18** A stone was brought and placed over the mouth of the pit, and the king sealed it with his own signet ring and with the signet rings of his nobles, so that nothing might be changed concerning Daniel.

**19** Then the king went to his palace and spent the night fasting. No entertainment[^7] was brought before him, and sleep fled from him.

**20** Then at dawn, as soon as it was light, the king got up and went in haste to the lions' pit.

**21** When he came near the pit, he called to Daniel in an anguished voice. The king said to Daniel, "Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to rescue you from the lions?"

**22** Then Daniel spoke with the king: "O king, live forever!

**23** My God sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths, and they have not harmed me, because I was found innocent before him. And before you too, O king, I have done no harm."

**24** Then the king was overjoyed, and he ordered that Daniel be lifted out of the pit. So Daniel was lifted out of the pit, and no harm was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

**25** At the king's command, the men who had denounced Daniel[^8] were brought and thrown into the lions' pit, they, their children and their wives. Before they reached the bottom of the pit, the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.

**26** Then King Darius wrote to all the peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth: "May your peace be great!

**27** I issue a decree that in all the dominion of my kingdom people are to tremble with fear before the God of Daniel. For he is the living God, and he endures forever. His kingdom will never be destroyed, and his rule will last to the end.

**28** He rescues and saves, and he does signs and wonders in heaven and on earth. He has rescued Daniel from the power of the lions."

**29** So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian.[^9]

[^1]: Darius the Mede is not known from sources outside the Bible, which say that Babylon was taken by Cyrus king of Persia. Some identify him with Gubaru, the governor whom Cyrus set over Babylon, and others with Cyrus himself (see verse 29). Daniel 9:1 calls him the son of Ahasuerus.
[^2]: This verse is 5:31 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–29 are 6:1–28.
[^3]: Aramaic *sarekhin*, a word probably borrowed from Persian. Also in verses 4–5, 7 and 8.
[^4]: Or "came in haste" or "conspired". The meaning of the Aramaic *hargishu* is uncertain. Also in verses 12 and 16.
[^5]: Aramaic *gov*, "pit", traditionally "den".
[^6]: Or "Your God, whom you serve continually, will rescue you!"
[^7]: The meaning of the Aramaic *dachavan* is unknown. Suggestions include food, music and concubines.
[^8]: Literally "who had eaten the pieces of Daniel". See 3:8.
[^9]: Or "in the reign of Darius, that is, the reign of Cyrus the Persian".
