**1** On the third day there was a wedding at Cana in Galilee, and Jesus' mother was there.

**2** Jesus and his disciples had also been invited to the wedding.

**3** When the wine ran out, Jesus' mother said to him, "They have no wine."

**4** Jesus said to her, "Woman, what does that have to do with me?[^1] My hour has not yet come."

**5** His mother said to the servants, "Do whatever he tells you."

**6** Six stone water jars stood there, for the purification rites of the Jews.[^2] Each held two or three measures.[^3]

**7** Jesus said to them, "Fill the jars with water." So they filled them to the brim.

**8** Then he said to them, "Now draw some out and take it to the master of the feast."[^4] So they took it.

**9** The master of the feast tasted the water, which had become wine. He did not know where it had come from, though the servants who had drawn the water knew. He called the bridegroom

**10** and said to him, "Everyone serves the good wine first, and the cheaper wine after the guests have drunk freely.[^5] But you have kept the good wine until now."

**11** Jesus did this, the first of his signs,[^6] at Cana in Galilee. He revealed his glory, and his disciples believed in him.

**12** After this he went down to Capernaum with his mother, his brothers, and his disciples, and they stayed there a few days.

**13** The Passover of the Jews was near, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**14** In the temple[^7] he found people selling cattle, sheep, and doves, and the money changers sitting there.

**15** He made a whip out of cords and drove them all out of the temple, both the sheep and the cattle. He poured out the money changers' coins and overturned their tables.

**16** To those selling doves he said, "Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father's house a marketplace!"

**17** His disciples remembered that it is written, "Zeal for your house will consume me."[^8]

**18** So the Jews[^9] responded to him, "What sign can you show us to prove you have the right to do these things?"[^10]

**19** Jesus answered them, "Destroy this temple,[^11] and in three days I will raise it up."

**20** The Jews said, "This temple was forty-six years in the building,[^12] and you will raise it up in three days?"

**21** But he was speaking about the temple of his body.

**22** So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.

**23** While he was in Jerusalem at the Passover festival, many believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing.

**24** But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them,[^13] because he knew all people.

**25** He did not need anyone to testify about human beings, for he himself knew what was in a person.

[^1]: Literally "What to me and to you, woman?", an idiom that distances the speaker from a request; compare Mark 1:24. "Woman" was a polite form of address (also 19:26), not a disrespectful one.
[^2]: That is, for the ritual washing required by Jewish custom.
[^3]: Greek *metretes*, a liquid measure of about 10 gallons (40 liters). Each jar held about 20 to 30 gallons (75 to 115 liters).
[^4]: Greek *architriklinos*, the person in charge of the banquet: a head steward, or a guest presiding over the meal.
[^5]: Literally "when they have become drunk".
[^6]: Literally "this beginning of the signs". John calls Jesus' miracles "signs": acts that point beyond themselves to who he is.
[^7]: Greek *hieron*, the whole temple complex with its courts. In verses 19–21 the word is *naos*, the sanctuary itself.
[^8]: Psalm 69:10 (69:9 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew reads "has consumed me".
[^9]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here, as often in John, the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem, who were responsible for the temple.
[^10]: Literally "What sign do you show us, that you are doing these things?"
[^11]: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary. See verse 14.
[^12]: Or "This temple has been under construction for forty-six years." Herod the Great began rebuilding the temple about 20 BC.
[^13]: *Entrust* is the same Greek verb (*pisteuo*) as *believed* in verse 23.
