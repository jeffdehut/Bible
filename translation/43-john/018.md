**1** After saying these things, Jesus went out with his disciples across the Kidron Valley.[^1] There was a garden there, and he and his disciples went into it.

**2** Judas, who was betraying him, also knew the place, because Jesus had often met there with his disciples.

**3** So Judas came there, bringing a detachment of soldiers[^2] and some officers from the chief priests and the Pharisees, with lanterns and torches and weapons.

**4** Then Jesus, knowing everything that was going to happen to him, went out and said to them, "Who are you looking for?"

**5** They answered him, "Jesus of Nazareth." He said to them, "I am he."[^3] Judas, who was betraying him, was standing with them.

**6** When he said to them, "I am he," they drew back and fell to the ground.

**7** So he asked them again, "Who are you looking for?" And they said, "Jesus of Nazareth."

**8** Jesus answered, "I told you that I am he. So if you are looking for me, let these men go."

**9** This was to fulfill the word he had spoken: "I have not lost a single one of those you have given me."[^4]

**10** Then Simon Peter, who had a sword, drew it and struck the high priest's servant,[^5] cutting off his right ear. The servant's name was Malchus.

**11** Jesus said to Peter, "Put your sword back into its sheath. Shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?"

**12** So the detachment of soldiers, their commander,[^6] and the officers of the Jews[^7] arrested Jesus and tied him up.

**13** They led him first to Annas, for he was the father-in-law of Caiaphas, who was high priest that year.[^8]

**14** It was Caiaphas who had advised the Jews that it was better for one man to die for the people.[^9]

**15** Simon Peter and another disciple were following Jesus. That disciple was known to the high priest, and he went with Jesus into the high priest's courtyard,

**16** but Peter stood outside at the gate. So the other disciple, the one known to the high priest, went out and spoke to the woman who kept the gate, and brought Peter in.

**17** The servant girl who kept the gate said to Peter, "You aren't one of this man's disciples too, are you?" He said, "I am not."[^10]

**18** The servants and the officers had made a charcoal fire, because it was cold, and they were standing there warming themselves. Peter also was standing with them, warming himself.

**19** Then the high priest questioned Jesus about his disciples and about his teaching.

**20** Jesus answered him, "I have spoken openly to the world. I have always taught in synagogues and in the temple, where all the Jews come together. I have said nothing in secret.

**21** Why do you question me? Question those who heard what I said to them. They know what I said."

**22** When he said this, one of the officers standing nearby struck Jesus in the face, saying, "Is that how you answer the high priest?"

**23** Jesus answered him, "If I have said something wrong, testify to what is wrong. But if I spoke rightly, why do you strike me?"

**24** Then Annas sent him, still tied up, to Caiaphas the high priest.

**25** Meanwhile Simon Peter was standing there warming himself. They said to him, "You aren't one of his disciples too, are you?" He denied it and said, "I am not."

**26** One of the high priest's servants, a relative of the man whose ear Peter had cut off, said, "Didn't I see you in the garden with him?"

**27** Again Peter denied it, and at once a rooster crowed.

**28** Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas to the Praetorium.[^11] It was early morning. They themselves did not enter the Praetorium, so that they would not become ritually unclean but could eat the Passover.

**29** So Pilate went out to them and said, "What charge are you bringing against this man?"

**30** They answered him, "If this man were not a criminal,[^12] we would not have handed him over to you."

**31** Pilate said to them, "Take him yourselves and judge him by your own law." The Jews said to him, "We are not permitted to put anyone to death."

**32** This was to fulfill the word Jesus had spoken when he indicated what kind of death he was going to die.[^13]

**33** Then Pilate went back into the Praetorium, called Jesus, and said to him, "Are you the King of the Jews?"

**34** Jesus answered, "Are you saying this on your own, or did others tell you about me?"

**35** Pilate answered, "I am not a Jew, am I? Your own nation and the chief priests have handed you over to me. What have you done?"

**36** Jesus answered, "My kingdom is not of this world. If my kingdom were of this world, my attendants[^14] would be fighting to keep me from being handed over to the Jews. But as it is, my kingdom is not from here."

**37** Pilate said to him, "So you are a king?" Jesus answered, "You say that I am a king.[^15] For this I was born, and for this I came into the world: to testify to the truth. Everyone who belongs to the truth listens to my voice."

**38** Pilate said to him, "What is truth?" After saying this, he went out again to the Jews and said to them, "I find no grounds for a charge against him.

**39** But you have a custom that I release one man for you at the Passover. So do you want me to release for you the King of the Jews?"

**40** They shouted back, "Not this man, but Barabbas!" Now Barabbas was a robber.[^16]

[^1]: Literally "across the winter stream Kidron", a valley east of Jerusalem whose stream flowed only in the rainy season. Many manuscripts read "the winter stream of the Cedars".
[^2]: Greek *speira*, a cohort, a Roman military unit nominally of 600 soldiers; also in verse 12.
[^3]: Literally "I am" (Greek *ego eimi*); also in verses 6 and 8. In answer to their question it means "I am he", the one you are looking for. John also records that at these words they drew back and fell to the ground (verse 6); compare 8:24, 28, 58.
[^4]: See 6:39; 17:12.
[^5]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*; also in verses 18 and 26.
[^6]: Greek *chiliarchos*, "commander of a thousand", a Roman officer.
[^7]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*. In the account of Jesus' arrest and trial the word refers to the Jewish authorities in Jerusalem, the chief priests and their officers (verses 3 and 35), except in the title "King of the Jews" and in verse 20, where it means the Jewish people as a whole.
[^8]: Annas had been high priest from AD 6 to 15, and five of his sons held the office after him. Caiaphas was high priest from about AD 18 to 36.
[^9]: See 11:49–50.
[^10]: Greek *ouk eimi*, also in verse 25; compare Jesus' *ego eimi*, "I am he", in verses 5–8.
[^11]: Greek *praitorion*, the Praetorium, the residence of the Roman governor when he was in Jerusalem.
[^12]: Literally "doing evil".
[^13]: See 12:32–33.
[^14]: Greek *hyperetai*, the word used for the "officers" in verses 3, 12, 18 and 22.
[^15]: Or "You are right to say that I am a king." The reply neither simply affirms nor denies.
[^16]: Or "a bandit", "a rebel". Greek *lestes*.
