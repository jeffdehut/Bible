**1** In the beginning was the Word. The Word was with God, and the Word was God.[^1]

**2** He was with God in the beginning.

**3** Through him all things came into being. Without him not one thing came into being.

**4** What came into being in him was life,[^2] and that life was the light of all people.

**5** The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.[^3]

**6** There was a man sent from God, whose name was John.

**7** He came as a witness, to testify about the light, so that everyone would believe through him.

**8** He himself was not the light; he came to testify about the light.

**9** The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world.[^4]

**10** He was in the world, and the world came into being through him, yet the world did not recognize him.

**11** He came to what was his own, and his own people did not receive him.

**12** But to all who did receive him, to those who believe in his name, he gave the right to become children of God,

**13** children born not from human descent,[^5] nor from physical desire,[^6] nor from a husband's decision,[^7] but from God.

**14** The Word became flesh[^8] and lived among us.[^9] We have seen his glory, the glory of the only Son from the Father,[^10] full of grace and truth.

**15** (John testified about him. He cried out, "This is the one I was talking about when I said, 'The one who comes after me ranks ahead of me, because he existed before me.'")

**16** For from his fullness we have all received grace upon grace.[^11]

**17** For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.

**18** No one has ever seen God. The only God,[^12] who is at the Father's side,[^13] has made him known.

**19** This is John's testimony, when the Jews of Jerusalem[^14] sent priests and Levites to ask him, "Who are you?"

**20** He admitted it openly and did not deny it. He declared, "I am not the Messiah."[^15]

**21** They asked him, "What then? Are you Elijah?" He said, "I am not." "Are you the Prophet?"[^16] He answered, "No."

**22** So they said to him, "Who are you? We need an answer for those who sent us. What do you say about yourself?"

**23** He said, "I am the voice of someone shouting in the wilderness, 'Make the way for the Lord straight,'[^17] as the prophet Isaiah said."

**24** They had been sent by the Pharisees.[^18]

**25** They asked him, "Then why are you baptizing, if you are not the Messiah, or Elijah, or the Prophet?"

**26** John answered them, "I baptize with water. Standing among you is someone you do not know.

**27** He is the one who comes after me. I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandal."

**28** This happened in Bethany on the other side of the Jordan, where John was baptizing.

**29** The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!

**30** This is the one I was talking about when I said, 'A man is coming after me who ranks ahead of me, because he existed before me.'

**31** I did not know him myself, but I came baptizing with water so that he would be revealed to Israel."

**32** John testified, "I saw the Spirit coming down from heaven like a dove, and it remained on him.

**33** I did not know him myself, but the one who sent me to baptize with water told me, 'When you see the Spirit come down and remain on someone, he is the one who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.'

**34** I have seen it, and I testify that this is God's Chosen One."[^19]

**35** The next day John was standing there again with two of his disciples.

**36** He saw Jesus walking by and said, "Look, the Lamb of God!"

**37** The two disciples heard him say this, and they followed Jesus.

**38** Jesus turned around, saw them following, and asked them, "What are you looking for?" They said to him, "Rabbi" (which means Teacher), "where are you staying?"

**39** He said to them, "Come, and you will see." So they went and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day. It was about four in the afternoon.[^20]

**40** Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who heard what John said and followed Jesus.

**41** The first thing Andrew did was find his brother Simon and tell him, "We have found the Messiah" (which means the Anointed One).[^21]

**42** He brought Simon to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said, "You are Simon son of John. You will be called Cephas" (which means Peter).[^22]

**43** The next day Jesus decided to go to Galilee. He found Philip and said to him, "Follow me."

**44** Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter.

**45** Philip found Nathanael and told him, "We have found the one Moses wrote about in the Law, and the prophets wrote about too: Jesus son of Joseph, from Nazareth."

**46** Nathanael said to him, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" Philip said to him, "Come and see."

**47** Jesus saw Nathanael coming toward him and said about him, "Here is a true Israelite, in whom there is nothing false."

**48** Nathanael asked him, "How do you know me?" Jesus answered him, "Before Philip called you, I saw you under the fig tree."

**49** Nathanael answered him, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!"

**50** Jesus answered him, "Do you believe because I told you I saw you under the fig tree? You will see greater things than that."

**51** Then he said to him, "Truly, truly, I tell you all:[^23] you will see heaven opened, and the angels of God going up and coming down on the Son of Man."

[^1]: *Word*: Greek *logos*, "word, message, reason". John uses it as a title for the one who becomes the man Jesus (verse 14). *With God*: or "in the presence of God".
[^2]: The SBLGNT, like most modern editions, begins this sentence with the last words of verse 3 ("what has come into being"). Many translations instead end verse 3 with them: "Without him nothing came into being that has come into being. In him was life …"
[^3]: Or "has not understood it", "has not grasped it". The Greek verb can mean either.
[^4]: Or "The true light, which gives light to everyone who comes into the world, was there."
[^5]: Literally "not of bloods".
[^6]: Literally "the will of the flesh".
[^7]: Or "a man's will". Greek *aner* means man or husband.
[^8]: That is, a human being.
[^9]: Literally "pitched his tent among us".
[^10]: Literally "glory as of an only one from a father". Greek *monogenes*: only, unique, one of a kind.
[^11]: Literally "grace in place of grace": one gift of grace after another.
[^12]: The SBLGNT and the earliest manuscripts read "the only God" (Greek *monogenes theos*). Most later manuscripts, and the Byzantine text, read "the only Son".
[^13]: Literally "who is in the bosom of the Father", a picture of the closest relationship.
[^14]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here, as often in John, the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem who send official representatives.
[^15]: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^16]: The prophet like Moses promised in Deuteronomy 18:15–18.
[^17]: Isaiah 40:3. In Isaiah's Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^18]: Or "Some Pharisees had been sent."
[^19]: The SBLGNT reads "the Chosen One of God", with some early manuscripts. Most manuscripts and editions read "the Son of God".
[^20]: Literally "about the tenth hour", counting hours from sunrise.
[^21]: Literally "which, translated, is *Christos*". *Messiah* (Hebrew and Aramaic) and *Christos* (Greek) both mean "anointed one".
[^22]: *Cephas* (Aramaic) and *Peter* (Greek) both mean "rock".
[^23]: "You" is plural in Greek. *Truly, truly*: Greek *amen amen*, a solemn affirmation.
