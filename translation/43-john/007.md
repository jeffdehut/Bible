**1** After this Jesus went about in Galilee. He did not want to go about in Judea, because the Jews[^1] were looking for a way to kill him.

**2** Now the festival of the Jews called the Festival of Shelters[^2] was near.

**3** So his brothers said to him, "Leave here and go to Judea, so that your disciples may also see the works you are doing.

**4** For no one who wants to be known publicly does things in secret. If you are doing these things, show yourself to the world."

**5** For not even his brothers believed in him.

**6** Jesus said to them, "My time[^3] has not yet come, but your time is always here.

**7** The world cannot hate you, but it hates me, because I testify about it that its works are evil.

**8** You go up to the festival. I am not going up[^4] to this festival, because my time has not yet fully come."

**9** After saying this, he stayed in Galilee.

**10** But after his brothers had gone up to the festival, then he went up too, not openly but in secret, as it were.

**11** The Jews were looking for him at the festival and saying, "Where is that man?"

**12** There was a lot of muttering about him among the crowds. Some were saying, "He is a good man." Others were saying, "No, he is leading the people astray."

**13** Yet no one spoke openly about him, for fear of the Jews.

**14** When the festival was already half over, Jesus went up into the temple and began to teach.

**15** The Jews were amazed and said, "How does this man have such learning,[^5] when he has never studied?"

**16** Jesus answered them, "My teaching is not mine, but comes from the one who sent me.

**17** If anyone wants to do his will, he will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own.

**18** Whoever speaks on his own seeks his own glory, but whoever seeks the glory of the one who sent him is true, and there is nothing false[^6] in him.

**19** Didn't Moses give you the law? Yet none of you keeps the law. Why are you trying to kill me?"

**20** The crowd answered, "You have a demon! Who is trying to kill you?"

**21** Jesus answered them, "I did one work, and you are all amazed.

**22** For this reason[^7] Moses gave you circumcision (not that it comes from Moses, but from the patriarchs[^8]), and you circumcise a man on the Sabbath.

**23** If a man receives circumcision on the Sabbath so that the law of Moses will not be broken, are you angry with me because I made a man completely well on the Sabbath?

**24** Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment."

**25** Then some of the people of Jerusalem began to say, "Isn't this the man they are trying to kill?

**26** And look, he is speaking openly, and they say nothing to him. Can it be that the authorities really know that this is the Messiah?

**27** But we know where this man comes from. When the Messiah comes, no one will know where he comes from."

**28** Then Jesus cried out as he was teaching in the temple, "You know me, and you know where I come from![^9] Yet I have not come on my own. The one who sent me is true, and you do not know him.

**29** I know him, because I come from him, and he sent me."

**30** So they tried to seize him, but no one laid a hand on him, because his hour had not yet come.

**31** Yet many in the crowd believed in him, and they said, "When the Messiah comes, will he do more signs than this man has done?"

**32** The Pharisees heard the crowd muttering these things about him, and the chief priests and the Pharisees sent officers[^10] to arrest him.

**33** Then Jesus said, "I am with you only a little while longer, and then I am going to the one who sent me.

**34** You will look for me, but you will not find me. Where I am, you cannot come."

**35** The Jews said to one another, "Where does this man intend to go, that we will not find him? He doesn't intend to go to the Dispersion among the Greeks[^11] and teach the Greeks, does he?

**36** What does he mean by saying, 'You will look for me, but you will not find me,' and 'Where I am, you cannot come'?"

**37** On the last day of the festival, the great day, Jesus stood up and cried out, "If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink.

**38** Whoever believes in me, as the scripture has said, 'Rivers of living water will flow from within him.'"[^12]

**39** He said this about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were going to receive. For the Spirit had not yet been given,[^13] because Jesus had not yet been glorified.

**40** When they heard these words, some in the crowd said, "This really is the Prophet."[^14]

**41** Others said, "This is the Messiah." But some said, "Surely the Messiah does not come from Galilee?

**42** Hasn't the scripture said that the Messiah comes from the descendants[^15] of David, and from Bethlehem, the village where David lived?"[^16]

**43** So the crowd was divided because of him.

**44** Some of them wanted to seize him, but no one laid hands on him.

**45** Then the officers went back to the chief priests and Pharisees, who asked them, "Why didn't you bring him?"

**46** The officers answered, "No one ever spoke like this!"[^17]

**47** The Pharisees answered them, "You haven't been led astray too, have you?

**48** Has any of the authorities or of the Pharisees believed in him?

**49** But this crowd, which does not know the law, is accursed."

**50** Nicodemus, who had come to Jesus earlier and was one of them, said to them,

**51** "Does our law judge a man without first hearing from him and finding out what he is doing?"

**52** They answered him, "Are you from Galilee too? Look into it, and you will see that no prophet arises from Galilee."[^18]

**53** ⟦[^19]Then each of them went home.

[^1]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here, and in verses 11, 13, 15 and 35, the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem. In verse 13 the people who fear them are themselves Jews.
[^2]: Greek *skenopegia*, "the setting up of shelters", traditionally "Tabernacles" or "Booths". It was the autumn harvest festival, when people lived for seven days in shelters made of branches (Leviticus 23:33–43).
[^3]: Greek *kairos*, the right or appointed time; also in verse 8.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "I am not yet going up".
[^5]: Literally "How does this man know letters", that is, learning in the scriptures, gained by study with a teacher.
[^6]: Or "nothing unrighteous".
[^7]: Or, ending verse 21 with these words, "and you are all amazed because of it. Moses gave you circumcision …"
[^8]: Literally "the fathers": Abraham and his descendants (Genesis 17:9–14). Circumcision on the eighth day (Leviticus 12:3) was carried out even when that day was a Sabbath.
[^9]: Or "So you know me, and you know where I come from?"
[^10]: Greek *hyperetai*, attendants or officers of the temple guard; also in verses 45–46.
[^11]: Greek *diaspora*, the Jews living scattered among the nations outside the land of Israel. "The Greeks" here may mean non-Jews in general.
[^12]: Literally "out of his belly": from the depths of his being. Verses 37–38 can also be punctuated "If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me; and let the one who believes in me drink. As the scripture has said, 'Rivers of living water will flow from within him.'" Read that way, "him" may be Jesus. No single passage says these words; compare Isaiah 58:11; Ezekiel 47:1–12; Zechariah 14:8.
[^13]: Literally "For the Spirit was not yet". Many manuscripts read "the Holy Spirit was not yet"; some read "the Spirit had not yet been given".
[^14]: The prophet like Moses promised in Deuteronomy 18:15–18; see 1:21.
[^15]: Literally "the seed".
[^16]: See 2 Samuel 7:12; Psalm 89:4–5 (89:3–4 in most English Bibles); Micah 5:1 (5:2 in most English Bibles).
[^17]: Literally "Never has a man spoken like this." Many manuscripts read "No one ever spoke like this man."
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "no prophet has arisen from Galilee".
[^19]: The passage in double brackets ⟦ ⟧, John 7:53–8:11 (the woman caught in adultery), is not in the earliest manuscripts of John, including the papyri P66 and P75 and Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus (4th century). Where later manuscripts include it, it usually stands here, but some place it after John 7:36, 7:44 or 21:25, or after Luke 21:38, and some mark it as doubtful. Its vocabulary and style differ from the rest of John. The SBLGNT prints it in double brackets, as a later addition to the Gospel; this translation does the same. Many scholars think it preserves an early story about Jesus that circulated on its own.
