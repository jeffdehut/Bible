**1** Then Pilate took Jesus and had him flogged.[^1]

**2** The soldiers twisted together a crown of thorns and put it on his head, and they put a purple robe on him.

**3** They kept coming up to him and saying, "Hail, king of the Jews!" and they struck him in the face.

**4** Pilate went out again and said to them, "Look, I am bringing him out to you, so that you may know that I find no grounds for a charge against him."

**5** So Jesus came out, wearing the crown of thorns and the purple robe. Pilate said to them,[^2] "Here is the man!"

**6** When the chief priests and the officers saw him, they shouted, "Crucify! Crucify!" Pilate said to them, "Take him yourselves and crucify him, for I find no grounds for a charge against him."

**7** The Jews[^3] answered him, "We have a law, and according to that law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God."[^4]

**8** When Pilate heard this statement, he was even more afraid.

**9** He went back into the Praetorium and said to Jesus, "Where are you from?" But Jesus gave him no answer.

**10** So Pilate said to him, "Will you not speak to me? Don't you know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?"

**11** Jesus answered him, "You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given to you from above. So the one who handed me over to you has the greater sin."

**12** From then on Pilate tried to release him, but the Jews shouted, "If you release this man, you are no friend of Caesar![^5] Everyone who makes himself a king opposes Caesar."

**13** When Pilate heard these words, he brought Jesus out and sat down on the judgment seat[^6] at a place called the Stone Pavement, in Hebrew[^7] Gabbatha.

**14** It was the day of Preparation[^8] for the Passover, about noon.[^9] He said to the Jews, "Here is your king!"

**15** They shouted, "Take him away! Take him away! Crucify him!" Pilate said to them, "Shall I crucify your king?" The chief priests answered, "We have no king but Caesar."

**16** Then he handed him over to them to be crucified. So they took Jesus,

**17** and carrying the cross by himself, he went out to what is called the Place of the Skull, which in Hebrew is called Golgotha.

**18** There they crucified him, and with him two others, one on each side, with Jesus in the middle.

**19** Pilate also wrote a notice and put it on the cross. It read, "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews."

**20** Many of the Jews read this notice, because the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city, and it was written in Hebrew, Latin, and Greek.

**21** So the chief priests of the Jews said to Pilate, "Do not write, 'The King of the Jews,' but, 'This man said, "I am king of the Jews."'"

**22** Pilate answered, "What I have written, I have written."

**23** When the soldiers had crucified Jesus, they took his clothes and divided them into four parts, one part for each soldier. They also took his tunic. The tunic was seamless, woven in one piece from the top down.

**24** So they said to one another, "Let's not tear it, but cast lots for it to see whose it will be." This was to fulfill the scripture that says, "They divided my clothes among them, and for my garment they cast lots."[^10] So that is what the soldiers did.

**25** Standing near the cross of Jesus were his mother, his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas,[^11] and Mary Magdalene.

**26** When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing nearby, he said to his mother, "Woman,[^12] here is your son."

**27** Then he said to the disciple, "Here is your mother." And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.

**28** After this, Jesus, knowing that everything had now been completed, said, so that the scripture would be fulfilled,[^13] "I am thirsty."

**29** A jar full of sour wine[^14] was standing there. So they put a sponge full of the sour wine on a stalk of hyssop[^15] and held it to his mouth.

**30** When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, "It is finished."[^16] Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.

**31** Since it was the day of Preparation, the Jews did not want the bodies to remain on the cross during the Sabbath, for that Sabbath was a great day.[^17] So they asked Pilate to have the men's legs broken and the bodies taken away.

**32** So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first man, and then of the other one who had been crucified with Jesus.

**33** But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs.

**34** Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.

**35** The one who saw it has testified, and his testimony is true. He knows that he is telling the truth, so that you also may believe.[^18]

**36** For these things happened so that the scripture would be fulfilled: "Not one of his bones will be broken."[^19]

**37** And again another scripture says, "They will look on the one they have pierced."[^20]

**38** After this, Joseph of Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus, though secretly because he feared the Jews, asked Pilate to let him take away the body of Jesus. Pilate gave him permission, so he came and took his body away.

**39** Nicodemus, who had first come to Jesus at night, also came, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred litras.[^21]

**40** They took the body of Jesus and wrapped it in strips of linen cloth with the spices, as is the burial custom of the Jews.

**41** At the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden there was a new tomb in which no one had yet been laid.

**42** And so, because it was the Jewish day of Preparation and the tomb was nearby, they laid Jesus there.

[^1]: Literally "and flogged him", that is, had him flogged by the soldiers.
[^2]: Literally "he said to them".
[^3]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*. Here, as in chapter 18, the word refers to the chief priests and their officers (verse 6); also in verses 12, 14, 31 and 38. In verses 20, 40 and 42 it refers to the Jewish people and their customs.
[^4]: Or "a son of God". Compare Leviticus 24:16.
[^5]: "Friend of Caesar" may have been an honorary title for loyal supporters of the emperor.
[^6]: Or "and seated him on the judgment seat". The Greek verb can mean "sit down" or "make (someone) sit". The judgment seat was the raised platform from which a magistrate gave judgment.
[^7]: That is, in the language of the Jews of the time, which may mean Aramaic; also in verses 17 and 20.
[^8]: Greek *paraskeue*, the day of preparation before a Sabbath or a festival, which also became the word for Friday. "The Preparation of the Passover" may mean the day before the Passover, or the Friday of Passover week. Also in verses 31 and 42.
[^9]: Literally "about the sixth hour", counting hours from sunrise.
[^10]: Psalm 22:19 (22:18 in most English Bibles).
[^11]: Literally "Mary of Clopas", probably his wife, though possibly his daughter. The Greek may also list three women rather than four, with Mary the wife of Clopas being the sister of Jesus' mother.
[^12]: See the note on 2:4.
[^13]: Or "knowing that everything had now been completed so that the scripture would be fulfilled, said". The Greek verb for "fulfilled" here (*teleioo*, "complete") is related to "completed" earlier in the verse and "finished" in verse 30. The scripture is perhaps Psalm 69:22 (69:21 in most English Bibles); compare Psalm 22:16 (22:15 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Cheap wine vinegar, a common drink of soldiers and laborers.
[^15]: A small plant whose stems were used to sprinkle blood or water in rituals (Exodus 12:22).
[^16]: Greek *tetelestai*, "It has been completed", the same word as "had been completed" in verse 28.
[^17]: Probably because the Sabbath fell during the Passover festival.
[^18]: Or "may go on believing". The SBLGNT reads the present tense here; many manuscripts read the aorist, which may suggest "come to believe". See the note on 20:31.
[^19]: Exodus 12:46; Numbers 9:12; Psalm 34:21 (34:20 in most English Bibles).
[^20]: Zechariah 12:10.
[^21]: A *litra* was a Roman pound, about 12 ounces (325 grams), so this was about 70 pounds (33 kilograms).
