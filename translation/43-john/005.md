**1** After this there was a festival of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem.

**2** In Jerusalem, by the Sheep Gate,[^1] there is a pool called in Hebrew[^2] Bethesda,[^3] which has five colonnades.

**3** In these lay a large number of sick people: blind, lame, and paralyzed.[^4]

**5** One man there had been ill for thirty-eight years.

**6** When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had already been ill for a long time, he said to him, "Do you want to get well?"

**7** The sick man answered him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up. While I am making my way there, someone else goes down ahead of me."

**8** Jesus said to him, "Get up, pick up your mat, and walk."

**9** At once the man was well, and he picked up his mat and walked. Now that day was a Sabbath.

**10** So the Jews[^5] said to the man who had been healed, "It is the Sabbath. It is not lawful for you to carry your mat."

**11** But he answered them, "The man who made me well said to me, 'Pick up your mat and walk.'"

**12** They asked him, "Who is the man who told you, 'Pick it up and walk'?"

**13** But the man who had been healed did not know who it was, for Jesus had slipped away, since there was a crowd in the place.

**14** Later Jesus found him in the temple and said to him, "Look, you have been made well. Stop sinning, or something worse may happen to you."

**15** The man went away and told the Jews that it was Jesus who had made him well.

**16** So the Jews began to persecute Jesus,[^6] because he was doing these things on the Sabbath.

**17** But Jesus answered them, "My Father is working until now, and I am working too."

**18** For this reason the Jews tried all the harder to kill him, because he was not only breaking the Sabbath, but he was also calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God.

**19** So Jesus answered them, "Truly, truly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing. Whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise.

**20** For the Father loves[^7] the Son and shows him everything he himself is doing. And he will show him greater works than these, so that you will be amazed.

**21** For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whomever he wishes.

**22** The Father judges no one, but he has given all judgment to the Son,

**23** so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him.

**24** Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever hears my word and believes the one who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment,[^8] but has passed from death to life.

**25** Truly, truly, I tell you, the hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live.

**26** For just as the Father has life in himself, so he has also granted the Son to have life in himself.

**27** And he has given him authority to carry out judgment, because he is the Son of Man.[^9]

**28** Do not be amazed at this, for the hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice

**29** and come out: those who have done good, to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment.[^10]

**30** I can do nothing on my own. I judge as I hear, and my judgment is just, because I seek not my own will but the will of the one who sent me.

**31** If I testify about myself, my testimony is not true.[^11]

**32** There is another who testifies about me, and I know that the testimony he gives about me is true.

**33** You sent messengers to John, and he has testified to the truth.

**34** Not that I accept testimony from a human being; I say these things so that you may be saved.

**35** He was a lamp that burned and gave light, and for a while you were willing to rejoice in his light.

**36** But I have a testimony greater than John's.[^12] The works that the Father has given me to complete, the very works I am doing, testify about me that the Father has sent me.

**37** And the Father who sent me has himself testified about me. You have never heard his voice or seen his form,

**38** and you do not have his word living[^13] in you, because you do not believe the one he sent.

**39** You search the scriptures[^14] because you think that in them you have eternal life. It is they that testify about me,

**40** yet you refuse to come to me to have life.

**41** I do not accept glory[^15] from human beings.

**42** But I know you: you do not have the love of God[^16] in you.

**43** I have come in my Father's name, and you do not accept me. If someone else comes in his own name, you will accept him.

**44** How can you believe, when you accept glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?

**45** Do not think that I will accuse you before the Father. Your accuser is Moses, on whom you have set your hope.

**46** If you believed Moses, you would believe me, for he wrote about me.

**47** But if you do not believe what he wrote, how will you believe what I say?"

[^1]: Literally "by the sheep (gate)". The Greek does not name the noun; it may mean the Sheep Gate (Nehemiah 3:1) or a sheep pool.
[^2]: That is, in the language of the Jews of the time, which may mean Aramaic.
[^3]: Some manuscripts read "Bethzatha".
[^4]: Literally "withered". Many manuscripts add "waiting for the water to move" at the end of verse 3, and then verse 4: "For an angel would come down from time to time into the pool and stir up the water. Whoever stepped in first after the water was stirred up was made well of whatever disease he had." The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave these words out, so this translation has no verse 4.
[^5]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*, "the Jews" or "the Judeans". Here and through this chapter the word refers to the religious authorities in Jerusalem, not to the Jewish people as a whole (the healed man is himself a Jew).
[^6]: Many manuscripts add "and were trying to kill him".
[^7]: Greek *phileo*. In 3:35 the verb for the Father's love of the Son is *agapao*. John uses the two verbs with much the same meaning.
[^8]: Or "condemnation". Greek *krisis*, "judgment", often in the sense of condemnation; also in verse 29.
[^9]: Or "because he is a son of man", that is, a human being. The Greek has no article here, as in "one like a son of man" in Daniel 7:13. Elsewhere John always has "the Son of Man".
[^10]: Compare Daniel 12:2.
[^11]: That is, not valid as evidence on its own; see Deuteronomy 19:15.
[^12]: Some manuscripts read "But I, who am greater than John, have testimony."
[^13]: Or "remaining". Greek *meno*, "remain, stay, live", a key word in John.
[^14]: Or "Search the scriptures". The Greek can be a statement or a command.
[^15]: Or "praise", "honor"; also in verse 44.
[^16]: Or "love for God".
