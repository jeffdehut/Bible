**1** "Truly, truly, I tell you, whoever does not enter the sheepfold by the gate but climbs in some other way is a thief and a robber.[^1]

**2** The one who enters by the gate is the shepherd of the sheep.

**3** The gatekeeper opens the gate for him, and the sheep listen to his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.

**4** When he has brought out all his own, he goes ahead of them, and the sheep follow him, because they know his voice.

**5** They will never follow a stranger, but will run away from him, because they do not know the voice of strangers."

**6** Jesus told them this figure of speech,[^2] but they did not understand what he was telling them.

**7** So Jesus said to them again, "Truly, truly, I tell you, I am the gate for the sheep.[^3]

**8** All who came before me[^4] are thieves and robbers, but the sheep did not listen to them.

**9** I am the gate. Whoever enters through me will be saved. He will come in and go out and find pasture.

**10** The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I came so that they may have life, and have it in abundance.

**11** I am the good shepherd.[^5] The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.

**12** The hired man, who is not the shepherd and does not own the sheep, sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and runs away, and the wolf seizes them and scatters them.

**13** He runs away because he is a hired man and does not care about the sheep.

**14** I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and my own know me,[^6]

**15** just as the Father knows me and I know the Father. And I lay down my life for the sheep.

**16** I have other sheep that are not of this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. So there will be one flock, one shepherd.

**17** This is why the Father loves me: because I lay down my life so that I may take it up again.

**18** No one takes it[^7] from me, but I lay it down of my own accord. I have authority to lay it down, and I have authority to take it up again. This command I received from my Father."

**19** Again there was a division among the Jews[^8] because of these words.

**20** Many of them were saying, "He has a demon and is out of his mind. Why do you listen to him?"

**21** Others were saying, "These are not the words of someone possessed by a demon. Can a demon open the eyes of the blind?"

**22** Then came the Festival of Dedication[^9] in Jerusalem. It was winter,

**23** and Jesus was walking in the temple, in Solomon's Colonnade.[^10]

**24** So the Jews gathered around him and said to him, "How long will you keep us in suspense?[^11] If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly."

**25** Jesus answered them, "I told you, and you do not believe. The works that I do in my Father's name testify about me.

**26** But you do not believe, because you are not among my sheep.[^12]

**27** My sheep listen to my voice. I know them, and they follow me.

**28** I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand.

**29** My Father, in what he has given me, is greater than all,[^13] and no one can snatch them out of the Father's hand.

**30** I and the Father are one."[^14]

**31** Again the Jews picked up stones to stone him.

**32** Jesus answered them, "I have shown you many good works from the Father. For which of these works are you going to stone me?"

**33** The Jews answered him, "We are not stoning you for a good work, but for blasphemy, because you, a man, are making yourself God."[^15]

**34** Jesus answered them, "Is it not written in your law, 'I said, "You are gods"'?[^16]

**35** If he called them gods to whom the word of God came (and scripture cannot be set aside),[^17]

**36** do you say of the one whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world, 'You are blaspheming,' because I said, 'I am the Son of God'?

**37** If I am not doing the works of my Father, do not believe me.

**38** But if I am doing them, even if you do not believe me, believe the works, so that you may know and understand[^18] that the Father is in me and I am in the Father."

**39** Again they tried to seize him, but he escaped from their hands.

**40** He went away again across the Jordan to the place where John had been baptizing at first, and he stayed there.

**41** Many people came to him, and they said, "John did no sign, but everything John said about this man was true."

**42** And many believed in him there.

[^1]: Or "a bandit". Greek *lestes*; also in verse 8.
[^2]: Greek *paroimia*, a figure of speech, a saying with a hidden meaning.
[^3]: Or "the door"; also in verses 1–2 and 9. See the note on 6:35.
[^4]: Many manuscripts leave out "before me".
[^5]: Greek *kalos*, "good, noble, fine"; also in verse 14.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "and I am known by my own".
[^7]: Some manuscripts read "No one has taken it".
[^8]: Greek *hoi Ioudaioi*. Here Jesus' hearers in Jerusalem, among them the Pharisees of 9:40. In verses 24, 31 and 33 they are his opponents in the temple.
[^9]: Greek *enkainia*, "renewal, dedication": the winter festival (Hanukkah) that celebrated the rededication of the temple in 164 BC, after the Syrian king Antiochus IV had defiled it (1 Maccabees 4:36–59).
[^10]: A covered walkway with columns along the east side of the temple courts (see Acts 3:11).
[^11]: Literally "How long will you take away our life?" or "lift up our soul", that is, keep us in doubt.
[^12]: Many manuscripts add "as I told you".
[^13]: The SBLGNT here follows a reading given in the margin of Westcott and Hort; literally "My Father, what he has given me, is greater than all". Some manuscripts read "What my Father has given me is greater than all." Many manuscripts read "My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all."
[^14]: Greek *hen*, neuter: literally "one thing".
[^15]: Or "a god".
[^16]: Psalm 82:6.
[^17]: Literally "cannot be loosed", that is, cannot be annulled.
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "know and believe".
