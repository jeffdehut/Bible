**1** Jesus learned[^1] that the Pharisees had heard he was making and baptizing more disciples than John

**2** (although Jesus himself was not baptizing; his disciples were).

**3** So he left Judea and went back again to Galilee.

**4** He had to pass through Samaria.

**5** So he came to a town in Samaria called Sychar, near the plot of land that Jacob had given to his son Joseph.[^2]

**6** Jacob's well was there. Jesus, tired from the journey, sat down just as he was by the well. It was about noon.[^3]

**7** A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink."

**8** For his disciples had gone off into the town to buy food.

**9** The Samaritan woman said to him, "How is it that you, a Jew, are asking me, a Samaritan woman, for a drink?" For Jews do not associate with Samaritans.[^4]

**10** Jesus answered her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that is saying to you, 'Give me a drink,' you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water."[^5]

**11** The woman said to him, "Sir,[^6] you have nothing to draw water with, and the well is deep. So where do you get this living water?

**12** You aren't greater than our ancestor[^7] Jacob, are you? He gave us the well, and he drank from it himself, as did his sons and his livestock."

**13** Jesus answered her, "Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again.

**14** But whoever drinks the water I will give them will never be thirsty again. The water I will give them will become in them a spring of water welling up to eternal life."

**15** The woman said to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I won't be thirsty or have to keep coming here to draw water."

**16** He said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come back here."

**17** The woman answered him, "I have no husband." Jesus said to her, "You are right to say, 'I have no husband.'

**18** For you have had five husbands, and the man you have now is not your husband.[^8] What you have said is true."

**19** The woman said to him, "Sir, I can see that you are a prophet.

**20** Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain,[^9] but you people say that the place where people must worship is in Jerusalem."

**21** Jesus said to her, "Believe me, woman, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.

**22** You[^10] worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, because salvation is from the Jews.

**23** But the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth,[^11] for the Father is looking for such people to worship him.

**24** God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth."

**25** The woman said to him, "I know that the Messiah is coming" (the one called Christ).[^12] "When he comes, he will tell us everything."

**26** Jesus said to her, "I am he, the one who is speaking to you."[^13]

**27** Just then his disciples came back. They were amazed that he was talking with a woman, but no one said, "What do you want?" or "Why are you talking with her?"

**28** Then the woman left her water jar, went off into the town, and said to the people,

**29** "Come and see a man who told me everything I ever did! Could this be the Messiah?"

**30** They came out of the town and began making their way to him.

**31** Meanwhile the disciples were urging him, "Rabbi, eat something."

**32** But he said to them, "I have food to eat that you do not know about."

**33** So the disciples said to one another, "Has someone brought him something to eat?"

**34** Jesus said to them, "My food is to do the will of the one who sent me and to complete his work.

**35** Don't you say, 'Four months more, and then the harvest comes'? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes and see the fields: they are ripe[^14] for harvest.

**36** Already[^15] the one who reaps is receiving wages and gathering a crop for eternal life, so that the one who sows and the one who reaps may rejoice together.

**37** For here the saying is true: 'One sows and another reaps.'

**38** I sent you to reap what you have not worked for. Others have done the hard work, and you have come in to share the fruit of their labor."[^16]

**39** Many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of what the woman said when she testified, "He told me everything I ever did."

**40** So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days.

**41** Many more believed because of what he said.

**42** They said to the woman, "We no longer believe because of what you said, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this really is the Savior of the world."[^17]

**43** After the two days he left there for Galilee.

**44** For Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own country.[^18]

**45** So when he came into Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him. They had seen everything he had done in Jerusalem at the festival, for they too had gone to the festival.

**46** So he came again to Cana in Galilee, where he had made the water wine. There was a royal official[^19] whose son was sick in Capernaum.

**47** When this man heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to him and begged him to come down and heal his son, for he was about to die.

**48** Jesus said to him, "Unless you people[^20] see signs and wonders, you will never believe."

**49** The royal official said to him, "Sir, come down before my little boy dies."

**50** Jesus said to him, "Go. Your son lives." The man believed the word Jesus spoke to him and set off.

**51** While he was still on his way down, his servants[^21] met him and told him that his boy was alive.

**52** So he asked them at what time he had begun to get better. They said to him, "Yesterday at one in the afternoon[^22] the fever left him."

**53** The father realized that this was the time when Jesus had said to him, "Your son lives." And he himself believed, along with his whole household.

**54** This was the second sign that Jesus did after coming from Judea to Galilee.

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord learned". The Greek sentence in verses 1–3 begins "So when Jesus learned …" and is interrupted by verse 2.
[^2]: See Genesis 33:18–19; 48:22; Joshua 24:32.
[^3]: Literally "about the sixth hour", counting hours from sunrise.
[^4]: Or "do not use the same dishes as Samaritans".
[^5]: Or "running water". The phrase meant fresh water from a spring or stream, as opposed to still water in a cistern; Jesus uses it of a gift that gives life.
[^6]: Greek *kyrie*, a respectful form of address, "sir" or "lord"; also in verses 15, 19 and 49.
[^7]: Literally "our father".
[^8]: Greek *aner* means both "husband" and "man", as in verses 16–17.
[^9]: Mount Gerizim, where the Samaritans had built a temple.
[^10]: *You* is plural in Greek, as in verses 20–21: the Samaritans.
[^11]: Or "in the Spirit and in truth"; also in verse 24.
[^12]: Greek *Messias* (from Hebrew and Aramaic) and *Christos* (Greek) both mean "anointed one"; see 1:41. The words in parentheses may be the woman's or the Gospel writer's.
[^13]: Literally "I am, the one who is speaking to you." Greek *ego eimi*, "I am", the words Jesus uses of himself throughout John (see 6:35; 8:24, 58).
[^14]: Literally "white".
[^15]: The SBLGNT, like most modern editions, begins this sentence with the last word of verse 35, "already". Others end verse 35 with it: "they are already ripe for harvest."
[^16]: Literally "you have entered into their labor".
[^17]: Many manuscripts add "the Messiah".
[^18]: Or "his own hometown". Compare Mark 6:4.
[^19]: Greek *basilikos*, someone in the service of the king, here probably Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee.
[^20]: *You* is plural in Greek.
[^21]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^22]: Literally "at the seventh hour".
