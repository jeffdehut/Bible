**1** When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up on the mountain. After he sat down, his disciples came to him,

**2** and he began to teach them,[^1] saying:

**3** "Blessed[^2] are the poor in spirit, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs.

**4** Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted.[^3]

**5** Blessed are the gentle, for they will inherit the earth.[^4]

**6** Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness,[^5] for they will be filled.

**7** Blessed are the merciful, for they will be shown mercy.

**8** Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.[^6]

**9** Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God.[^7]

**10** Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs.

**11** "Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you, and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me.

**12** Rejoice and be glad, because your reward in heaven is great. For that is how they persecuted the prophets who were before you.

**13** "You are the salt of the earth. But if salt loses its taste,[^8] how can it be made salty again? It is no longer good for anything, except to be thrown out and trampled underfoot by people.

**14** "You are the light of the world. A town set on a hill cannot be hidden.

**15** People do not light a lamp and put it under a basket,[^9] but on a lampstand, and it gives light to everyone in the house.

**16** In the same way, let your light shine before people, so that they may see your good works and praise your Father in heaven.

**17** "Don't think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets.[^10] I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them.

**18** Truly I tell you, until heaven and earth pass away, not the smallest letter or the least stroke of a letter[^11] will pass from the Law until everything has taken place.

**19** So whoever breaks[^12] one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven. But whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.

**20** For I tell you, unless your righteousness goes far beyond that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.

**21** "You have heard that it was said to the people of long ago,[^13] 'You must not murder,' and 'Whoever murders will be liable to judgment.'[^14]

**22** But I tell you that everyone who is angry with their brother[^15] will be liable to judgment. Whoever says to their brother, 'Raca,'[^16] will be liable to the council.[^17] And whoever says, 'You fool!' will be liable to the fire of hell.[^18]

**23** "So if you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you,

**24** leave your gift there in front of the altar. First go and be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift.

**25** Come to terms quickly with your opponent while you are still with him on the way to court.[^19] Otherwise your opponent may hand you over to the judge, and the judge to the officer, and you will be thrown into prison.

**26** Truly I tell you, you will never get out of there until you have paid the last penny.[^20]

**27** "You have heard that it was said, 'You must not commit adultery.'[^21]

**28** But I tell you that anyone who looks at a woman[^22] with desire for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart.

**29** If your right eye causes you to stumble,[^23] tear it out and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to be thrown into hell.

**30** And if your right hand causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to lose one part of your body than for your whole body to go into hell.

**31** "It was also said, 'Whoever divorces his wife must give her a certificate of divorce.'[^24]

**32** But I tell you that anyone who divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality,[^25] causes her to commit adultery,[^26] and whoever marries a divorced woman commits adultery.

**33** "Again, you have heard that it was said to the people of long ago, 'Do not swear falsely, but carry out the oaths you have made to the Lord.'[^27]

**34** But I tell you, do not swear at all: not by heaven, because it is God's throne,

**35** or by the earth, because it is his footstool, or by Jerusalem, because it is the city of the great King.[^28]

**36** And do not swear by your head, because you cannot make one hair white or black.

**37** Let what you say be simply 'Yes, yes' or 'No, no'.[^29] Anything more than this comes from the evil one.[^30]

**38** "You have heard that it was said, 'Eye for eye and tooth for tooth.'[^31]

**39** But I tell you, do not resist an evil person.[^32] If anyone slaps you on the right cheek, turn the other cheek to him as well.

**40** If anyone wants to take you to court and take your tunic, let him have your cloak as well.[^33]

**41** If anyone forces you to go one mile,[^34] go with him two.

**42** Give to the one who asks you, and do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you.

**43** "You have heard that it was said, 'Love your neighbor and hate your enemy.'[^35]

**44** But I tell you, love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you,[^36]

**45** so that you may be children[^37] of your Father in heaven. For he makes his sun rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the righteous and the unrighteous.

**46** If you love those who love you, what reward do you have? Don't even the tax collectors do the same?

**47** And if you greet only your brothers,[^38] what are you doing more than others? Don't even the Gentiles do the same?

**48** So be perfect,[^39] as your heavenly Father is perfect.

[^1]: Literally "opening his mouth, he taught them".
[^2]: Greek *makarioi*, "blessed, happy, fortunate": those whom God favors. Also in verses 4–11.
[^3]: Compare Isaiah 61:1–3, with its good news for the poor and comfort for all who mourn.
[^4]: Or "the meek", "the humble"; *the earth*: or "the land". Compare Psalm 37:11, "But the humble will inherit the land."
[^5]: Or "for justice", "for what is right".
[^6]: Compare Psalm 24:3–4.
[^7]: Literally "sons of God".
[^8]: The Greek verb also means "become foolish".
[^9]: Greek *modios*, a container for measuring grain, holding about 8 liters (2 gallons).
[^10]: *The Law or the Prophets*: the Hebrew scriptures.
[^11]: Literally "not one *iota* or one horn". The *iota* is the smallest letter of the Greek alphabet (compare the Hebrew *yod*), and the "horn" is a tiny stroke that tells one letter from another.
[^12]: Or "sets aside", "annuls".
[^13]: Or "by the people of long ago"; also in verse 33.
[^14]: Exodus 20:13; Deuteronomy 5:17. The second sentence sums up laws such as Exodus 21:12.
[^15]: Or "brother or sister", a fellow member of God's people; also in verses 23–24. Many manuscripts add "without cause".
[^16]: An Aramaic insult meaning "empty-headed".
[^17]: Greek *synedrion*, a council of judges; in Jerusalem, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^18]: Literally "the Gehenna of fire". Greek *Gehenna*, from the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, used as a name for the place of final judgment. Also in verses 29–30.
[^19]: Literally "on the way".
[^20]: Greek *kodrantes*, from the Latin *quadrans*, a small Roman coin worth 1/64 of a denarius.
[^21]: Exodus 20:14; Deuteronomy 5:18.
[^22]: Or "a married woman". Greek *gyne* means both "woman" and "wife".
[^23]: Or "to sin"; also in verse 30.
[^24]: Deuteronomy 24:1.
[^25]: Literally "except for a matter of *porneia*", a general word for sexual immorality.
[^26]: Or "makes her the victim of adultery".
[^27]: Compare Leviticus 19:12; Numbers 30:3 (30:2 in most English Bibles); Deuteronomy 23:22 (23:21 in most English Bibles). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^28]: Isaiah 66:1; Psalm 48:3 (48:2 in most English Bibles).
[^29]: That is, a plain yes or no, without an oath.
[^30]: Or "from evil".
[^31]: Exodus 21:24; Leviticus 24:20; Deuteronomy 19:21.
[^32]: Or "do not resist evil".
[^33]: The tunic was worn next to the skin; the cloak was the outer garment, which a poor person also used as a blanket at night (Exodus 22:25–26; 22:26–27 in most English Bibles).
[^34]: Greek *milion*, a Roman mile, about 1,480 meters (4,860 feet). Soldiers and officials could force people to carry loads for them.
[^35]: Leviticus 19:18. The words "and hate your enemy" are not found in the Old Testament.
[^36]: Many manuscripts read "love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you".
[^37]: Literally "sons".
[^38]: Or "brothers and sisters". Many manuscripts read "your friends" and, later in the verse, "the tax collectors".
[^39]: Or "complete", "whole". Compare Leviticus 19:2; Deuteronomy 18:13.
