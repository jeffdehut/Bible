**1** When Jesus came down from the mountain, large crowds followed him.

**2** Just then a man with a skin disease[^1] came and knelt before him[^2] and said, "Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean."

**3** Jesus reached out his hand and touched him. "I am willing," he said. "Be made clean!" At once he was cleansed of his skin disease.

**4** Then Jesus said to him, "See that you tell no one. But go, show yourself to the priest, and offer the gift that Moses commanded, as evidence for them."[^3]

**5** When Jesus had entered Capernaum, a centurion[^4] came to him, pleading with him

**6** and saying, "Lord, my servant[^5] is lying at home paralyzed, suffering terribly."

**7** Jesus said to him, "I will come and heal him."[^6]

**8** The centurion answered, "Lord, I am not worthy to have you come under my roof. Just say the word, and my servant will be healed.

**9** For I too am a man under authority,[^7] with soldiers under me. I say to one, 'Go,' and he goes, and to another, 'Come,' and he comes, and to my servant,[^8] 'Do this,' and he does it."

**10** When Jesus heard this, he was amazed and said to those who were following him, "Truly I tell you, I have not found anyone in Israel with such great faith.[^9]

**11** I tell you, many will come from east and west and will recline at table with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven,

**12** but the heirs of the kingdom[^10] will be thrown out into the outer darkness, where there will be weeping and grinding of teeth."

**13** Then Jesus said to the centurion, "Go. Let it be done for you as you have believed." And the servant was healed at that very hour.

**14** When Jesus came into Peter's house, he saw Peter's mother-in-law lying in bed with a fever.

**15** He touched her hand, and the fever left her. She got up and began to serve him.

**16** That evening people brought to him many who were possessed by demons. He drove out the spirits with a word and healed all who were sick,

**17** so that what had been spoken through the prophet Isaiah would be fulfilled: "He took our weaknesses and carried our diseases."[^11]

**18** When Jesus saw the large crowds[^12] around him, he gave orders to cross to the other side.

**19** A scribe came and said to him, "Teacher, I will follow you wherever you go."

**20** Jesus said to him, "Foxes have dens, and the birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man[^13] has nowhere to lay his head."

**21** Another of the disciples said to him, "Lord, first let me go and bury my father."

**22** But Jesus said to him, "Follow me, and let the dead bury their own dead."

**23** When he got into the boat, his disciples followed him.

**24** Suddenly a great storm[^14] arose on the sea, so that the boat was being swamped by the waves. But Jesus was asleep.

**25** They went and woke him, saying, "Lord, save us! We are about to die!"

**26** He said to them, "Why are you so afraid, you of little faith?" Then he got up and rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm.

**27** The men were amazed and said, "What kind of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him?"

**28** When he came to the other side, to the region of the Gadarenes,[^15] two men possessed by demons came out of the tombs to meet him. They were so violent that no one could pass along that road.

**29** Suddenly they cried out, "What do you want with us,[^16] Son of God? Have you come here to torment us before the appointed time?"

**30** Some distance from them a large herd of pigs was feeding.

**31** The demons begged him, "If you drive us out, send us into the herd of pigs."

**32** He said to them, "Go!" So they came out and went into the pigs, and the whole herd rushed down the steep bank into the sea and died in the water.

**33** The men who tended the pigs ran off, went into the town, and reported everything, including what had happened to the men possessed by demons.

**34** Then the whole town came out to meet Jesus, and when they saw him, they begged him to leave their region.

[^1]: Greek *lepros*, traditionally "leper". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy.
[^2]: Or "worshiped him". Greek *proskyneo*; see 2:2.
[^3]: Or "as a testimony against them". The offering is described in Leviticus 14:1–32.
[^4]: A Roman officer in command of about a hundred soldiers.
[^5]: Greek *pais*, "boy", which can mean a son or a servant; also in verses 8 and 13.
[^6]: Or "Am I to come and heal him?"
[^7]: Some manuscripts add "appointed".
[^8]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*, a different word from the one in verses 6, 8 and 13.
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "not even in Israel have I found such great faith".
[^10]: Literally "the sons of the kingdom", those who expected to belong to it.
[^11]: Isaiah 53:4, translated from the Hebrew. The Septuagint reads "he bears our sins".
[^12]: Some manuscripts read "a crowd".
[^13]: A title Jesus uses for himself in all four Gospels. It recalls "one like a son of man" in Daniel 7:13, and in Hebrew idiom it can also mean simply "a human being".
[^14]: Greek *seismos*, "shaking", the usual word for an earthquake.
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "Gergesenes". Gadara was a town of the Decapolis, southeast of the Sea of Galilee. Mark 5:1 has "Gerasenes".
[^16]: Literally "What to us and to you?", an idiom: "What do we have to do with you?"
