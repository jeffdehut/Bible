**1** Early in the morning all the chief priests and the elders of the people reached a decision against Jesus, to put him to death.

**2** They bound him, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate the governor.[^1]

**3** When Judas, his betrayer, saw that Jesus had been condemned, he was seized with remorse[^2] and returned the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and the elders.

**4** "I have sinned," he said, "by betraying innocent blood."[^3] "What is that to us?" they said. "See to it yourself."

**5** So he threw the silver into the temple[^4] and left. Then he went away and hanged himself.

**6** The chief priests picked up the silver and said, "It is not lawful to put this into the treasury,[^5] since it is blood money."[^6]

**7** So they consulted together and used the money to buy the Potter's Field as a burial place for foreigners.

**8** That is why that field has been called the Field of Blood to this day.

**9** Then what had been spoken through the prophet Jeremiah was fulfilled: "They took[^7] the thirty pieces of silver, the price of the one whose price had been set, the price set on him by some of the people of Israel,

**10** and they gave them for the potter's field, as the Lord directed me."[^8]

**11** Meanwhile Jesus stood before the governor, and the governor asked him, "Are you the king of the Jews?" Jesus said, "You say so."[^9]

**12** When he was accused by the chief priests and the elders, he made no answer.

**13** Then Pilate said to him, "Don't you hear how many charges they are bringing against you?"

**14** But he did not answer him, not even to a single charge, so that the governor was greatly amazed.

**15** At the festival the governor used to release for the crowd one prisoner, whomever they wanted.

**16** At that time they had a notorious prisoner called Jesus Barabbas.[^10]

**17** So when they had gathered, Pilate said to them, "Whom do you want me to release for you: Jesus Barabbas, or Jesus who is called the Messiah?"

**18** For he knew it was out of envy that they had handed him over.

**19** While he was sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent him a message: "Have nothing to do with that righteous man, for today I have suffered a great deal in a dream because of him."

**20** But the chief priests and the elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and to have Jesus put to death.

**21** The governor answered them, "Which of the two do you want me to release for you?" "Barabbas," they said.

**22** Pilate said to them, "Then what shall I do with Jesus who is called the Messiah?" They all said, "Let him be crucified!"

**23** He said, "Why? What wrong has he done?" But they shouted all the louder, "Let him be crucified!"

**24** When Pilate saw that he was getting nowhere, but that instead a riot was starting, he took water and washed his hands in front of the crowd. "I am innocent of this man's blood,"[^11] he said. "See to it yourselves."

**25** All the people answered, "His blood be on us and on our children!"[^12]

**26** Then he released Barabbas for them. He had Jesus flogged, and handed him over to be crucified.

**27** Then the governor's soldiers took Jesus into the governor's headquarters[^13] and gathered the whole cohort[^14] around him.

**28** They stripped him and put a scarlet cloak on him.

**29** They twisted together a crown of thorns and put it on his head, and put a reed in his right hand. Then they knelt in front of him and mocked him: "Hail, king of the Jews!"

**30** They spat on him, took the reed, and kept striking him on the head.

**31** When they had finished mocking him, they took off the cloak and put his own clothes back on him. Then they led him away to crucify him.

**32** As they were going out, they found a man from Cyrene named Simon, and they forced him to carry Jesus' cross.

**33** They came to a place called Golgotha, which means "Place of a Skull",

**34** and they gave him wine mixed with gall[^15] to drink. But when he tasted it, he refused to drink it.

**35** When they had crucified him, they divided his clothes among them by casting lots.[^16]

**36** Then they sat down and kept watch over him there.

**37** Above his head they put the written charge against him: "This is Jesus, the King of the Jews."

**38** Then two rebels[^17] were crucified with him, one on his right and one on his left.

**39** Those who passed by insulted him, shaking their heads[^18]

**40** and saying, "You who would destroy the temple and build it in three days, save yourself! If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross!"

**41** In the same way the chief priests, with the scribes and the elders,[^19] mocked him:

**42** "He saved others, but he cannot save himself! He is the king of Israel![^20] Let him come down now from the cross, and we will believe in him.

**43** He trusts in God. Let God rescue him now, if he delights in him.[^21] For he said, 'I am God's Son.'"

**44** The rebels who were crucified with him also taunted him in the same way.

**45** From noon until three in the afternoon[^22] darkness came over the whole land.[^23]

**46** About three in the afternoon Jesus cried out with a loud voice, "Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani?" which means, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"[^24]

**47** When some of those standing there heard this, they said, "This man is calling Elijah."

**48** At once one of them ran, took a sponge, filled it with sour wine,[^25] put it on a reed, and gave it to him to drink.

**49** But the others said, "Wait, let us see whether Elijah comes to save him."[^26]

**50** Jesus cried out again with a loud voice and gave up his spirit.[^27]

**51** At that moment the veil[^28] of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom. The earth shook, and the rocks were split.

**52** The tombs were opened, and many bodies of God's holy people who had died[^29] were raised.

**53** They came out of the tombs, and after his resurrection they went into the holy city and appeared to many people.[^30]

**54** When the centurion and those with him who were keeping watch over Jesus saw the earthquake and what had happened, they were terrified and said, "Truly this man was God's Son!"[^31]

**55** Many women were there, watching from a distance. They had followed Jesus from Galilee to look after his needs.[^32]

**56** Among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James and Joseph,[^33] and the mother of the sons of Zebedee.

**57** When evening came, a rich man from Arimathea named Joseph arrived, who had himself become a disciple of Jesus.

**58** He went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. Then Pilate ordered that it be given to him.

**59** Joseph took the body and wrapped it in a clean linen cloth,

**60** and laid it in his own new tomb, which he had cut out of the rock. Then he rolled a large stone against the entrance of the tomb and went away.

**61** Mary Magdalene and the other Mary were there, sitting opposite the tomb.

**62** The next day, the day after the Preparation,[^34] the chief priests and the Pharisees gathered before Pilate

**63** and said, "Sir, we remember that while that deceiver was still alive he said, 'After three days I will rise.'

**64** So order the tomb to be made secure until the third day. Otherwise his disciples may come[^35] and steal him away and tell the people, 'He has been raised from the dead,' and this last deception will be worse than the first."

**65** Pilate said to them, "You have a guard.[^36] Go, make it as secure as you know how."

**66** So they went and made the tomb secure, sealing the stone and posting the guard.

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "Pontius Pilate the governor".
[^2]: Or "he changed his mind", the word used in 21:29, 32.
[^3]: Some manuscripts read "righteous blood".
[^4]: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary building itself; also in verses 40 and 51.
[^5]: Greek *korbanas*, the temple treasury; compare *korban*, "an offering" (Mark 7:11).
[^6]: Literally "the price of blood".
[^7]: Or "I took". The Greek form can mean either.
[^8]: The words are closest to Zechariah 11:12–13, where the prophet is paid "thirty pieces of silver" and throws them "to the potter" in the house of the LORD. Matthew names Jeremiah, who goes to a potter's house (Jeremiah 18:2–3), buys a potter's clay jar and speaks of burial in the Valley of Ben-hinnom (19:1–11), and buys a field (32:6–9). The quotation follows neither the Hebrew nor the Septuagint of Zechariah word for word. In the Hebrew of Zechariah, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^9]: Literally "You say." The reply neither simply affirms nor denies; compare 26:25, 64.
[^10]: Many manuscripts leave out "Jesus", here and in verse 17.
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "of the blood of this righteous man".
[^12]: A formula for accepting responsibility for a death; compare 2 Samuel 1:16; Jeremiah 26:15.
[^13]: Greek *praitorion*, the Praetorium, the Roman governor's residence.
[^14]: A Roman military unit, nominally 600 soldiers.
[^15]: Something bitter. Compare Psalm 69:22 (69:21 in most English Bibles), where the Septuagint reads "gall". Many manuscripts read "sour wine".
[^16]: See Psalm 22:19 (22:18 in most English Bibles).
[^17]: Or "robbers", "bandits". Greek *lestai*; also in verse 44.
[^18]: See Psalm 22:8 (22:7 in most English Bibles).
[^19]: Many manuscripts add "and the Pharisees".
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "If he is the king of Israel".
[^21]: See Psalm 22:9 (22:8 in most English Bibles): "Let him rescue him … since he delights in him!" Matthew's wording is close to the Septuagint, "He hoped in the Lord".
[^22]: Literally "the sixth hour"; "three in the afternoon" is literally "the ninth hour". Also in verse 46.
[^23]: Or "the whole earth".
[^24]: Psalm 22:2 (22:1 in most English Bibles). *Eli* is the Hebrew form of "my God"; *lema sabachthani* is Aramaic. Some manuscripts read "Eloi, Eloi", the Aramaic form, as in Mark 15:34.
[^25]: Cheap wine vinegar, a common drink of soldiers and laborers. See Psalm 69:22 (69:21 in most English Bibles).
[^26]: Some manuscripts add "And another took a spear and pierced his side, and water and blood came out" (compare John 19:34).
[^27]: Literally "let go the spirit".
[^28]: Greek *katapetasma*, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the veil that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33).
[^29]: Literally "of the holy ones who had fallen asleep". *The holy ones* is traditionally "the saints".
[^30]: Or "After his resurrection they came out of the tombs and went into the holy city …" The Greek can be read either way.
[^31]: Or "a son of God". The Greek has no article.
[^32]: Or "to serve him".
[^33]: Many manuscripts read "Joses", as in Mark 15:40.
[^34]: The day of Preparation was the day before the Sabbath (Mark 15:42), so this was the Sabbath.
[^35]: Many manuscripts add "by night".
[^36]: Or "Take a guard." Greek *koustodia*, from Latin *custodia*, a guard of soldiers; also in verse 66.
