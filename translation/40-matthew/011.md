**1** When Jesus had finished giving instructions to his twelve disciples,[^1] he went on from there to teach and proclaim the message in their towns.

**2** When John, who was in prison, heard about the works of the Messiah, he sent word through his disciples[^2]

**3** and asked him, "Are you the one who is to come, or should we expect someone else?"

**4** Jesus answered them, "Go and tell John what you hear and see:

**5** the blind see again, the lame walk, those with skin diseases are made clean, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news proclaimed to them.[^3]

**6** And blessed is anyone who does not take offense at me."[^4]

**7** As they were leaving, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: "What did you go out into the wilderness to look at? A reed shaken by the wind?

**8** If not, what did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft clothing? Look, those who wear soft clothing are in kings' houses.

**9** Then why did you go out? To see a prophet?[^5] Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

**10** This is the one about whom it is written: 'Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you. He will prepare your way before you.'[^6]

**11** Truly I tell you, among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist. Yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he is.

**12** From the days of John the Baptist until now, the kingdom of heaven has been suffering violence, and violent people are seizing it.[^7]

**13** For all the Prophets and the Law prophesied until John.

**14** And if you are willing to accept it, he is Elijah who is to come.[^8]

**15** Whoever has ears,[^9] let them hear.

**16** "To what shall I compare this generation? It is like children sitting in the marketplaces and calling out to the others:

**17** 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a funeral song, and you did not mourn.'[^10]

**18** For John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, 'He has a demon.'

**19** The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'Look at him! A glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!' Yet wisdom is proved right[^11] by her deeds."[^12]

**20** Then Jesus began to denounce the towns where most of his mighty works had been done, because they had not repented.

**21** "Woe to you,[^13] Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes.

**22** But I tell you, it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for you.

**23** And you, Capernaum, will you be lifted up to heaven? No, you will go down to Hades.[^14] For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day.

**24** But I tell you that it will be more bearable for the land of Sodom on the day of judgment than for you."

**25** At that time Jesus said, "I praise you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and understanding and have revealed them to little children.

**26** Yes, Father, for this was what pleased you.

**27** "All things have been handed over to me by my Father. No one knows the Son except the Father, and no one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal him.

**28** "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.

**29** Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.[^15]

**30** For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

[^1]: See 7:28.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "he sent two of his disciples".
[^3]: Compare Isaiah 35:5–6; 26:19; 61:1.
[^4]: Or "who does not stumble because of me". Greek *skandalizo*.
[^5]: Or "Then what did you go out to see? A prophet?"
[^6]: Malachi 3:1, with words from Exodus 23:20 ("Look, I am sending an angel ahead of you"). In Malachi the Hebrew reads "he will prepare the way before me".
[^7]: The meaning is uncertain. Or "the kingdom of heaven has been forcefully advancing, and forceful people are taking hold of it".
[^8]: Compare Malachi 3:23 (4:5 in most English Bibles).
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "ears to hear".
[^10]: Literally "you did not beat your breasts", a sign of mourning.
[^11]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justified": shown to be right.
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "by her children".
[^13]: Greek *ouai*, a cry of grief and warning, like the prophets' *hoy* ("woe").
[^14]: Greek *Hades*, the realm of the dead, like the Hebrew Sheol. Compare Isaiah 14:13–15. Many manuscripts read "And you, Capernaum, who are lifted up to heaven, will be brought down to Hades."
[^15]: Jeremiah 6:16.
