**1** At that time Herod the tetrarch[^1] heard the reports about Jesus,

**2** and he said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist. He has been raised from the dead, and that is why these powers are at work in him."

**3** For Herod had arrested John, bound him, and put him in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip,

**4** because John had been telling him, "It is not lawful for you to have her."

**5** Herod wanted to kill him, but he was afraid of the crowd, because they regarded John as a prophet.

**6** But on Herod's birthday the daughter of Herodias danced in front of them all and pleased Herod,

**7** so he promised with an oath to give her whatever she asked.

**8** Prompted by her mother, she said, "Give me here on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**9** The king was distressed, but because of his oaths and his guests he ordered it to be given.

**10** He sent and had John beheaded in the prison.

**11** His head was brought on a platter and given to the girl, and she brought it to her mother.

**12** John's disciples came, took the body, and buried him. Then they went and told Jesus.

**13** When Jesus heard this, he withdrew from there in a boat to a deserted place by himself. When the crowds heard of it, they followed him on foot from the towns.

**14** When he got out of the boat, he saw a large crowd, and he had compassion on them and healed their sick.

**15** When evening came, the disciples came to him and said, "This is a deserted place, and it is already late. Send the crowds away so that they can go into the villages and buy themselves food."

**16** But Jesus said to them, "They don't need to go away. You give them something to eat."

**17** They said to him, "We have nothing here but five loaves and two fish."

**18** He said, "Bring them here to me."

**19** He ordered the crowds to sit down on the grass. Taking the five loaves and the two fish, he looked up to heaven and blessed them.[^2] Then he broke the loaves and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.

**20** They all ate and were satisfied, and they picked up twelve baskets full of the broken pieces that were left over.

**21** Those who had eaten numbered about five thousand men, besides women and children.

**22** At once Jesus made the disciples get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side, while he sent the crowds away.

**23** After he had sent the crowds away, he went up on the mountain by himself to pray. When evening came, he was there alone.

**24** But the boat was already many stadia[^3] from land, battered by the waves, because the wind was against them.

**25** Shortly before dawn[^4] he came toward them, walking on the sea.

**26** When the disciples saw him walking on the sea, they were terrified. "It's a ghost!" they said, and they cried out in fear.

**27** But at once Jesus spoke to them: "Take courage! It is I.[^5] Don't be afraid."

**28** Peter answered him, "Lord, if it is you, tell me to come to you on the water."

**29** He said, "Come." So Peter got out of the boat, walked on the water, and came toward Jesus.[^6]

**30** But when he saw the strong[^7] wind, he was afraid, and as he began to sink, he cried out, "Lord, save me!"

**31** At once Jesus reached out his hand and caught him. "You of little faith," he said to him, "why did you doubt?"

**32** When they had climbed into the boat, the wind died down.

**33** Those in the boat worshiped him, saying, "Truly you are the Son of God."

**34** When they had crossed over, they came to land at Gennesaret.

**35** When the people of that place recognized him, they sent word throughout that whole region and brought to him all who were sick.

**36** They begged him to let them touch just the edge[^8] of his cloak, and all who touched it were healed.[^9]

[^1]: Herod Antipas, ruler of Galilee and Perea. Greek *tetraarches*, "tetrarch", the ruler of part of a kingdom. In verse 9 he is called "the king".
[^2]: Or "gave thanks".
[^3]: A stadion was about 185 meters (607 feet). Many manuscripts read "the boat was already in the middle of the sea".
[^4]: Literally "in the fourth watch of the night", between 3 and 6 a.m.
[^5]: Literally "I am". The same words are used of God in the Greek Old Testament (Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10).
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "walked on the water to go to Jesus".
[^7]: Some manuscripts leave out "strong".
[^8]: Or "the tassel"; see 9:20.
[^9]: Or "were saved".
