**1** Six days later Jesus took with him Peter, James, and James's brother John, and led them up a high mountain by themselves.

**2** There he was transformed in front of them. His face shone like the sun, and his clothes became as white as the light.

**3** Suddenly Moses and Elijah appeared to them, talking with him.

**4** Peter said to Jesus, "Lord, it is good for us to be here. If you want, I will make three shelters here:[^1] one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah."

**5** While he was still speaking, a bright cloud overshadowed them, and a voice from the cloud said, "This is my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with him.[^2] Listen to him!"

**6** When the disciples heard this, they fell face down to the ground and were terrified.

**7** But Jesus came and touched them. "Get up," he said, "and don't be afraid."

**8** When they looked up, they saw no one except Jesus himself, alone.

**9** As they were coming down the mountain, Jesus ordered them, "Don't tell anyone about the vision until the Son of Man has been raised from the dead."

**10** The disciples asked him, "Then why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?"

**11** He answered, "Elijah is indeed coming,[^3] and he will restore all things.

**12** But I tell you, Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but did to him whatever they wanted. In the same way the Son of Man too is going to suffer at their hands."

**13** Then the disciples understood that he was speaking to them about John the Baptist.

**14** When they came to the crowd, a man came up to Jesus and knelt before him.

**15** "Lord," he said, "have mercy on my son. He has seizures[^4] and suffers terribly.[^5] He often falls into the fire, and often into the water.

**16** I brought him to your disciples, but they could not heal him."

**17** Jesus answered, "You unbelieving and twisted generation![^6] How long must I be with you? How long must I put up with you? Bring him here to me."

**18** Jesus rebuked it,[^7] and the demon came out of him, and the boy was healed from that moment.

**19** Then the disciples came to Jesus privately and asked, "Why couldn't we drive it out?"

**20** He told them, "Because of your little faith.[^8] For truly I tell you, if you have faith like a mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, 'Move from here to there,' and it will move. Nothing will be impossible for you."[^9]

**22** When they gathered[^10] in Galilee, Jesus said to them, "The Son of Man is going to be handed over to people,

**23** and they will kill him, and on the third day he will be raised." And they were filled with grief.

**24** When they came to Capernaum, the collectors of the two-drachma tax[^11] came to Peter and said, "Doesn't your teacher pay the two-drachma tax?"

**25** "Yes, he does," he said. When Peter came into the house, Jesus spoke to him first: "What do you think, Simon? From whom do the kings of the earth collect duties or taxes?[^12] From their own sons, or from others?"

**26** When Peter said, "From others," Jesus said to him, "Then the sons are free.

**27** But so that we don't offend them,[^13] go to the sea and throw out a hook. Take the first fish that comes up, and when you open its mouth, you will find a stater.[^14] Take it and give it to them for me and for you."

[^1]: Or "tents". Many manuscripts read "let us make three shelters here".
[^2]: Or "This is my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in him." The voice at Jesus' baptism said the same words (3:17).
[^3]: Many manuscripts add "first".
[^4]: Literally "he is moonstruck", probably epilepsy (compare 4:24).
[^5]: Some manuscripts read "is very ill".
[^6]: Compare Deuteronomy 32:5, "a crooked and twisted generation".
[^7]: Or "him". The Greek pronoun can refer to the demon or to the boy.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "your unbelief".
[^9]: Many manuscripts add verse 21: "But this kind does not come out except by prayer and fasting." The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave it out, so this translation has no verse 21.
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "While they were staying".
[^11]: Greek *ta didrachma*, "the two drachmas", about two days' wages: the yearly tax that Jewish men paid for the temple (compare Exodus 30:11–16).
[^12]: Greek *tele*, customs and tolls, and *kensos*, the poll tax.
[^13]: Literally "cause them to stumble".
[^14]: A silver coin worth four drachmas, enough to pay the tax for two people.
