**1** Jesus got into a boat, crossed over, and came to his own town.

**2** Just then some people brought to him a paralyzed man lying on a stretcher. When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralyzed man, "Take heart, son;[^1] your sins are forgiven."

**3** Then some of the scribes said to themselves, "This man is blaspheming!"

**4** Knowing[^2] their thoughts, Jesus said, "Why are you thinking evil things in your hearts?

**5** Which is easier: to say, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up and walk'?

**6** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"—then he said to the paralyzed man—"get up, pick up your stretcher, and go home."

**7** And the man got up and went home.

**8** When the crowds saw this, they were filled with awe,[^3] and they praised God, who had given such authority to human beings.

**9** As Jesus walked on from there, he saw a man named Matthew[^4] sitting at the tax booth. "Follow me," he told him. And Matthew got up and followed him.

**10** Later, as Jesus was having a meal in the house,[^5] many tax collectors and sinners came and were eating with Jesus and his disciples.

**11** When the Pharisees saw this, they asked his disciples, "Why does your teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?"

**12** When he heard this, he said, "Healthy people don't need a doctor; sick people do.

**13** Go and learn what this means: 'I desire mercy, not sacrifice.'[^6] For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."[^7]

**14** Then John's disciples came to him and asked, "Why do we and the Pharisees fast often,[^8] but your disciples do not fast?"

**15** Jesus said to them, "Can the wedding guests[^9] mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them? But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast.

**16** "No one puts a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment, for the patch pulls away from the garment, and the tear gets worse.

**17** Neither do people pour new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the skins burst, the wine spills out, and the skins are ruined. No, they pour new wine into fresh wineskins, and both are preserved."

**18** While he was saying these things to them, a leader[^10] came and knelt before him and said, "My daughter has just died. But come and lay your hand on her, and she will live."

**19** Jesus got up and followed him, and so did his disciples.

**20** Just then a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years came up behind him and touched the edge[^11] of his cloak,

**21** for she said to herself, "If I can just touch his cloak, I will be healed."

**22** Jesus turned, and when he saw her, he said, "Take heart, daughter. Your faith has healed you."[^12] And the woman was healed from that hour.

**23** When Jesus came into the leader's house and saw the flute players[^13] and the crowd making a commotion,

**24** he said, "Go away. The girl is not dead but asleep." And they laughed at him.

**25** But when the crowd had been put outside, he went in and took her by the hand, and the girl got up.

**26** The news of this spread through all that region.

**27** As Jesus went on from there, two blind men followed him, shouting, "Have mercy on us, Son of David!"[^14]

**28** When he had gone into the house, the blind men came to him. Jesus asked them, "Do you believe that I am able to do this?" "Yes, Lord," they said to him.

**29** Then he touched their eyes and said, "Let it be done for you according to your faith."

**30** And their eyes were opened. Jesus warned them sternly, "See that no one knows about this."

**31** But they went out and spread the news about him all through that region.

**32** As they were going out, some people brought to him a man who could not speak[^15] and was possessed by a demon.

**33** When the demon had been driven out, the man who could not speak began to speak. The crowds were amazed and said, "Nothing like this has ever been seen in Israel!"

**34** But the Pharisees said, "He drives out demons by the ruler of the demons."

**35** Jesus went through all the towns and villages, teaching in their synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness.[^16]

**36** When he saw the crowds, he had compassion on them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.[^17]

**37** Then he said to his disciples, "The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few.

**38** So ask the Lord of the harvest to send out workers into his harvest."

[^1]: Literally "child", an affectionate form of address.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "Seeing".
[^3]: Literally "they were afraid". Many manuscripts read "they were amazed".
[^4]: Mark 2:14 and Luke 5:27 call the tax collector Levi.
[^5]: Literally "in the house", perhaps Matthew's house or the one where Jesus was staying.
[^6]: Hosea 6:6. Matthew follows the Septuagint, which has "mercy" where the Hebrew has *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^7]: Many manuscripts add "to repentance".
[^8]: Some manuscripts leave out "often".
[^9]: Literally "the sons of the bridechamber".
[^10]: Greek *archon*, "ruler, leader"; also in verse 23.
[^11]: Or "the tassel". Jewish men wore tassels on the corners of their cloaks (Numbers 15:38–39).
[^12]: Or "has saved you". The Greek verb *sozo* means both; also in verse 21, "healed", and later in this verse.
[^13]: Musicians hired to play at funerals.
[^14]: A title of the Messiah, the descendant of David (1:1; compare 2 Samuel 7:12–16).
[^15]: Greek *kophos*, which can mean unable to speak or unable to hear; also in verse 33.
[^16]: Many manuscripts add "among the people".
[^17]: Compare Numbers 27:17; Ezekiel 34:5.
