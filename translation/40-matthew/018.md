**1** At that time the disciples came to Jesus and asked, "Who, then, is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?"

**2** He called a little child to him and stood the child among them,

**3** and said, "Truly I tell you, unless you change[^1] and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.

**4** So whoever humbles themselves like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven.

**5** And whoever welcomes one such child in my name welcomes me.

**6** "If anyone causes one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble,[^2] it would be better for them to have a large millstone[^3] hung around their neck and be drowned in the depths of the sea.

**7** Woe to the world[^4] because of the things that make people stumble! Such things are bound to come, but woe to the person through whom they come!

**8** If your hand or your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life maimed or lame than to have two hands or two feet and be thrown into the eternal fire.

**9** And if your eye causes you to stumble, tear it out and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into the fire of hell.[^5]

**10** "See that you do not look down on one of these little ones. For I tell you, their angels in heaven always see the face of my Father in heaven.[^6]

**12** What do you think? If a man has a hundred sheep and one of them wanders away, won't he leave the ninety-nine on the hills and go to look for the one that wandered away?

**13** And if he finds it, truly I tell you, he rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine that did not wander away.

**14** In the same way, it is not the will of your Father[^7] in heaven that one of these little ones should be lost.[^8]

**15** "If your brother[^9] sins against you,[^10] go and show him his fault, just between the two of you. If he listens to you, you have won your brother back.

**16** But if he does not listen, take one or two others with you, so that 'every matter may be established on the testimony of two witnesses or three.'[^11]

**17** If he refuses to listen to them, tell it to the church.[^12] And if he refuses to listen even to the church, treat him as you would a Gentile or a tax collector.

**18** Truly I tell you,[^13] whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.

**19** Again, truly I tell you, if two of you on earth agree about anything they ask for, it will be done for them by my Father in heaven.

**20** For where two or three are gathered in my name, I am there among them."

**21** Then Peter came to him and said, "Lord, how many times shall my brother sin against me and I forgive him? As many as seven times?"

**22** Jesus said to him, "I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times.[^14]

**23** "For this reason the kingdom of heaven is like a king who wanted to settle accounts with his servants.[^15]

**24** As he began the settlement, a man who owed him ten thousand talents[^16] was brought to him.

**25** Since he could not pay, his master ordered that he be sold, together with his wife and children and everything he had, and the debt repaid.

**26** So the servant fell to his knees before him and said, 'Be patient with me,[^17] and I will pay you everything.'

**27** The master of that servant had compassion on him. He released him and forgave him the loan.

**28** "But when that servant went out, he found one of his fellow servants who owed him a hundred denarii.[^18] He seized him and began to choke him, saying, 'Pay back what you owe!'

**29** His fellow servant fell down[^19] and begged him, 'Be patient with me, and I will pay you back.'

**30** But he refused. Instead he went and had him thrown into prison until he could pay the debt.

**31** When his fellow servants saw what had happened, they were deeply distressed. They went and told their master everything that had happened.

**32** Then his master called him in and said to him, 'You wicked servant! I forgave you all that debt because you begged me.

**33** Shouldn't you have had mercy on your fellow servant, just as I had mercy on you?'

**34** In anger his master handed him over to the torturers until he paid back all he owed.

**35** This is how my heavenly Father will treat each of you, unless you forgive your brother from your heart."[^20]

[^1]: Literally "turn".
[^2]: Or "to sin"; also in verses 7–9.
[^3]: Literally "a donkey's millstone", one large enough to be turned by a donkey.
[^4]: Greek *ouai*, a cry of grief and warning, like the prophets' *hoy* ("woe"); also later in the verse.
[^5]: Literally "the Gehenna of fire". Greek *Gehenna*, from the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, used as a name for the place of final judgment.
[^6]: Many manuscripts add verse 11: "For the Son of Man came to save what was lost" (compare Luke 19:10). The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave it out, so this translation has no verse 11.
[^7]: Some manuscripts read "my Father".
[^8]: Or "should perish".
[^9]: Or "brother or sister", a fellow member of God's people; also in verses 21 and 35.
[^10]: Some manuscripts leave out "against you".
[^11]: Deuteronomy 19:15. Literally "on the mouth of two witnesses or three".
[^12]: Greek *ekklesia*, "assembly", here the community of Jesus' followers.
[^13]: "You" is plural in this verse. *Will be bound … will be loosed*: or "will have been bound … will have been loosed". Jewish teachers used "bind" and "loose" for forbidding and permitting.
[^14]: Or "seventy times seven". The Greek Old Testament uses the same words in Genesis 4:24, "seventy-seven times".
[^15]: Or "slaves"; also in verses 26–33. Greek *doulos*.
[^16]: A talent was worth about 6,000 denarii, some twenty years' wages for a laborer. Ten thousand talents was a vast sum.
[^17]: Many manuscripts add "Master,".
[^18]: A denarius was a day's wage for a laborer, so this was about four months' wages.
[^19]: Many manuscripts add "at his feet".
[^20]: Many manuscripts add "their wrongdoings".
