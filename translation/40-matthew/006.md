**1** "Be careful not to practice your righteousness[^1] in front of people in order to be seen by them. If you do, you have no reward from your Father in heaven.

**2** "So when you give to the poor, do not sound a trumpet before you, as the hypocrites[^2] do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that they may be praised by people. Truly I tell you, they have received their reward in full.[^3]

**3** But when you give to the poor, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing,

**4** so that your giving may be in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.[^4]

**5** "And when you pray, do not be like the hypocrites, because they love to stand and pray in the synagogues and on the street corners, so that people may see them. Truly I tell you, they have received their reward in full.

**6** But when you pray, go into your inner room, shut your door, and pray to your Father, who is in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.

**7** "When you pray, do not babble on like the Gentiles, for they think they will be heard because of their many words.

**8** Do not be like them, for your Father[^5] knows what you need before you ask him.

**9** "This, then, is how you should pray: 'Our Father in heaven, may your name be honored as holy.

**10** May your kingdom come, may your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.

**11** Give us today our daily bread.[^6]

**12** And forgive us our debts,[^7] as we also have forgiven our debtors.[^8]

**13** And do not bring us into testing,[^9] but rescue us from the evil one.'[^10]

**14** For if you forgive others their wrongdoings, your heavenly Father will also forgive you.

**15** But if you do not forgive others,[^11] your Father will not forgive your wrongdoings either.

**16** "When you fast, do not look gloomy like the hypocrites. They disfigure their faces so that people may see that they are fasting.[^12] Truly I tell you, they have received their reward in full.

**17** But when you fast, put oil on your head and wash your face,

**18** so that your fasting may be seen not by people but by your Father, who is in secret. And your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you.

**19** "Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust[^13] destroy, and where thieves break in[^14] and steal.

**20** But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys, and where thieves do not break in or steal.

**21** For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

**22** "The eye is the lamp of the body. So if your eye is healthy,[^15] your whole body will be full of light.

**23** But if your eye is bad,[^16] your whole body will be full of darkness. So if the light in you is darkness, how great that darkness is!

**24** "No one can serve[^17] two masters. Either they will hate the one and love the other, or they will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money.[^18]

**25** "That is why I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat,[^19] or about your body, what you will wear. Isn't life more than food, and the body more than clothing?

**26** Look at the birds of the sky. They do not sow or reap or gather into barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren't you worth much more than they are?

**27** Which of you by worrying can add one cubit[^20] to your span of life?

**28** "And why do you worry about clothing? Consider how the lilies[^21] of the field grow. They do not labor or spin.

**29** Yet I tell you that not even Solomon in all his glory was dressed like one of these.

**30** If that is how God clothes the grass of the field, which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, won't he much more clothe you, you of little faith?

**31** So do not worry, saying, 'What will we eat?' or 'What will we drink?' or 'What will we wear?'

**32** For the Gentiles seek after all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them all.

**33** But seek first the kingdom[^22] and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.

**34** So do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own.

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "your acts of charity".
[^2]: Greek *hypokrites*, an actor, someone playing a part; also in verses 5 and 16.
[^3]: Greek *apecho*, a word used on receipts: "paid in full". Also in verses 5 and 16.
[^4]: Many manuscripts add "openly"; also in verse 6.
[^5]: Some manuscripts read "God your Father".
[^6]: Greek *epiousios*, a rare word whose meaning is uncertain. It may mean "for the coming day" (tomorrow's bread, or the bread of the age to come), "needed for existence", or "for today". "Daily" is the traditional rendering. Jerome (about AD 400) reported that a Hebrew gospel known to him had *mahar*, "tomorrow".
[^7]: That is, the wrongs we have done, our sins (verses 14–15; compare Luke 11:4).
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "as we also forgive our debtors".
[^9]: Or "temptation".
[^10]: Or "from evil". Many manuscripts add "For yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory forever. Amen." Compare 1 Chronicles 29:11.
[^11]: Some manuscripts add "their wrongdoings".
[^12]: There is a play on words in the Greek: they make their faces "disappear" so that they may "appear" to people as fasting.
[^13]: Literally "eating", whether by insects or by rust; also in verse 20.
[^14]: Literally "dig through", that is, through the mud-brick wall of a house; also in verse 20.
[^15]: Literally "single": sound, clear, and also generous.
[^16]: Literally "evil", which can also mean "unhealthy". An "evil eye" was an expression for envy or stinginess (20:15).
[^17]: Or "be a slave to"; also later in the verse.
[^18]: Greek *mamonas*, from the Aramaic *mamon*, "wealth, property".
[^19]: Most editions add "or what you will drink" (compare verse 31). The SBLGNT leaves it out, following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort.
[^20]: About 18 inches (45 cm). *Span of life*: or "height".
[^21]: Probably wild flowers in general.
[^22]: Some manuscripts read "the kingdom of God".
