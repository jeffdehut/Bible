**1** Then Pharisees and scribes came to Jesus from Jerusalem and said,

**2** "Why do your disciples break the tradition of the elders? They do not wash their hands when they eat."

**3** He answered them, "And why do you break the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition?

**4** For God said, 'Honor your father and your mother,'[^1] and, 'Whoever curses father or mother must be put to death.'[^2]

**5** But you say that if anyone tells their father or mother, 'Whatever support you might have had from me is a gift to God,'[^3]

**6** then they need not honor their father.[^4] So you have canceled the word of God[^5] for the sake of your tradition.

**7** You hypocrites! Isaiah prophesied rightly about you when he said:

**8** 'These people[^6] honor me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me.

**9** They worship me in vain, teaching human rules as if they were doctrines.'"[^7]

**10** Then he called the crowd to him and said to them, "Listen and understand.

**11** It is not what goes into the mouth that makes a person unclean,[^8] but what comes out of the mouth. That is what makes a person unclean."

**12** Then the disciples came and said to him, "Do you know that the Pharisees took offense when they heard this saying?"

**13** He answered, "Every plant that my heavenly Father has not planted will be pulled up by the roots.

**14** Leave them alone. They are blind guides of the blind.[^9] And if one blind person guides another, both will fall into a pit."

**15** Peter said to him, "Explain this parable to us."

**16** Jesus said, "Are you still so dull too?

**17** Don't you see that whatever goes into the mouth passes into the stomach and then is thrown out of the body?[^10]

**18** But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and that is what makes a person unclean.

**19** For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false testimony, and slander.

**20** These are what make a person unclean. But eating with unwashed hands does not make a person unclean."

**21** Jesus left there and withdrew to the region of Tyre and Sidon.

**22** Just then a Canaanite woman[^11] from that region came out and kept crying out, "Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is severely possessed by a demon."

**23** But he did not answer her a word. His disciples came and urged him, "Send her away, because she keeps crying out after us."

**24** He answered, "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel."

**25** But she came and knelt before him, saying, "Lord, help me!"

**26** He answered, "It isn't right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs."[^12]

**27** She said, "Yes, Lord, for even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table."

**28** Then Jesus answered her, "Woman, your faith is great! Let it be done for you as you wish." And her daughter was healed from that hour.

**29** Jesus left there and went along the Sea of Galilee. Then he went up on the mountain and sat down there.

**30** Large crowds came to him, bringing with them the mute,[^13] the blind, the lame, the crippled, and many others. They laid them at his feet, and he healed them.

**31** So the crowd was amazed when they saw the mute speaking, the crippled made well,[^14] the lame walking, and the blind seeing. And they praised the God of Israel.

**32** Jesus called his disciples to him and said, "I have compassion on the crowd, because they have already been with me three days and have nothing to eat. I do not want to send them away hungry, or they may collapse on the way."

**33** The disciples said to him, "Where could we get enough bread in this remote place to feed such a large crowd?"

**34** Jesus asked them, "How many loaves do you have?" "Seven," they said, "and a few small fish."

**35** He told the crowd to sit down on the ground.

**36** Then he took the seven loaves and the fish, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.

**37** They all ate and were satisfied, and they picked up seven large baskets full of the broken pieces that were left over.

**38** Those who had eaten numbered four thousand men, besides women and children.

**39** After sending the crowds away, he got into the boat and went to the region of Magadan.[^15]

[^1]: Exodus 20:12; Deuteronomy 5:16.
[^2]: Exodus 21:17; Leviticus 20:9.
[^3]: Literally "is a gift", that is, an offering dedicated to God. Mark 7:11 gives the Hebrew word *Corban*.
[^4]: Some manuscripts add "or mother".
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "the commandment of God".
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "These people draw near to me with their mouth and".
[^7]: Isaiah 29:13, quoted from the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "and their fear of me is a human command learned by rote".
[^8]: Literally "makes a person common", meaning ritually unclean; also in verses 18 and 20.
[^9]: Some manuscripts read "They are blind guides".
[^10]: Literally "is thrown out into the latrine".
[^11]: The ancient name for the people of this coast (Genesis 10:15–19). Mark 7:26 calls her "a Greek, born in Syrian Phoenicia".
[^12]: Greek *kynaria*, "little dogs", house dogs; also in verse 27.
[^13]: Greek *kophos*; see 9:32. Also in verse 31.
[^14]: Some manuscripts leave out "the crippled made well".
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "Magdala". Magadan is otherwise unknown; Mark 8:10 has "Dalmanutha".
