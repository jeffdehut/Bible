**1** "For the kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire workers for his vineyard.

**2** He agreed with the workers on a denarius[^1] for the day and sent them into his vineyard.

**3** About nine in the morning[^2] he went out and saw others standing idle in the marketplace.

**4** He said to them, 'You also go into the vineyard, and I will pay you whatever is right.'

**5** So they went. He went out again about noon and about three in the afternoon and did the same.

**6** About five in the afternoon he went out and found others standing around. He said to them, 'Why have you been standing here all day doing nothing?'

**7** 'Because no one has hired us,' they said. He said to them, 'You also go into the vineyard.'[^3]

**8** "When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his manager, 'Call the workers and pay them their wages, beginning with the last and going on to the first.'

**9** Those hired about five in the afternoon came, and each of them received a denarius.

**10** When the first came, they thought they would receive more. But each of them also received a denarius.

**11** When they received it, they grumbled against the landowner,

**12** saying, 'These last ones worked only one hour, and you have made them equal to us, who have borne the burden of the day and the scorching heat.'

**13** He answered one of them, 'Friend, I am not being unfair to you. Didn't you agree with me on a denarius?

**14** Take what is yours and go. I want to give this last one the same as I gave you.

**15** Don't I have the right to do what I want with what is mine? Or are you envious because I am generous?'[^4]

**16** So the last will be first, and the first last."[^5]

**17** As Jesus was going up to Jerusalem, he took the twelve disciples aside by themselves, and on the way he said to them,

**18** "Look, we are going up to Jerusalem, and the Son of Man will be handed over to the chief priests and the scribes. They will condemn him to death

**19** and hand him over to the Gentiles to mock, flog, and crucify him. And on the third day he will be raised."

**20** Then the mother of the sons of Zebedee came to Jesus with her sons. She knelt before him and asked him for something.

**21** He said to her, "What do you want?" She said to him, "Say that these two sons of mine will sit, one at your right and one at your left, in your kingdom."

**22** Jesus answered, "You don't know what you are asking. Can you drink the cup I am about to drink?"[^6] They said to him, "We can."

**23** He said to them, "You will drink my cup,[^7] but to sit at my right and at my left is not mine to grant. Those places belong to those for whom they have been prepared by my Father."

**24** When the ten heard about this, they became indignant with the two brothers.

**25** Jesus called them together and said, "You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and the great ones exercise authority over them.

**26** It will not be so among you.[^8] Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant,

**27** and whoever wants to be first among you must be your slave,

**28** just as the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."

**29** As they were leaving Jericho, a large crowd followed him.

**30** Two blind men were sitting by the roadside. When they heard that Jesus was passing by, they shouted, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"

**31** The crowd rebuked them and told them to be quiet, but they shouted all the more, "Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!"

**32** Jesus stopped, called them, and said, "What do you want me to do for you?"

**33** They said to him, "Lord, let our eyes be opened."

**34** Jesus had compassion on them and touched their eyes. At once they regained their sight and followed him.

[^1]: A denarius was the usual day's wage for a laborer.
[^2]: Literally "about the third hour", counting hours from sunrise. In verse 5, "noon" and "three in the afternoon" are literally "the sixth and the ninth hour"; in verses 6 and 9, "five in the afternoon" is "the eleventh hour".
[^3]: Many manuscripts add "and you will receive whatever is right".
[^4]: Literally "Or is your eye evil because I am good?" An "evil eye" was an idiom for envy or stinginess.
[^5]: Many manuscripts add "For many are called, but few are chosen" (compare 22:14).
[^6]: Many manuscripts add "or be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with?"
[^7]: Many manuscripts add "and be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with".
[^8]: Some manuscripts read "It is not so among you", as in Mark 10:43.
