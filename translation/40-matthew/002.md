**1** After Jesus was born in Bethlehem in Judea, in the days of King Herod,[^1] magi[^2] from the east arrived in Jerusalem

**2** and asked, "Where is the one who has been born king of the Jews? We saw his star at its rising[^3] and have come to worship him."[^4]

**3** When King Herod heard this, he was disturbed, and all Jerusalem with him.

**4** He called together all the chief priests and scribes of the people and asked them where the Messiah was to be born.

**5** They told him, "In Bethlehem in Judea, because this is what was written through the prophet:

**6** 'And you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah, for out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd my people Israel.'"[^5]

**7** Then Herod secretly called the magi and found out from them exactly when the star had appeared.

**8** He sent them to Bethlehem and said, "Go and search carefully for the child. When you find him, report to me, so that I too may come and worship him."

**9** After listening to the king, they set out. And there was the star they had seen at its rising, going ahead of them, until it came and stopped over the place where the child was.

**10** When they saw the star, they were overjoyed.[^6]

**11** They went into the house and saw the child with his mother Mary, and they fell down and worshiped him. Then they opened their treasure chests and offered him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh.[^7]

**12** And since they had been warned in a dream not to go back to Herod, they returned to their own country by another route.

**13** After they had gone, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said, "Get up, take the child and his mother, and flee to Egypt. Stay there until I tell you, because Herod is going to search for the child to kill him."

**14** So Joseph got up, took the child and his mother during the night, and left for Egypt.

**15** He stayed there until the death of Herod. This was so that what the Lord had said through the prophet would be fulfilled: "Out of Egypt I called my son."[^8]

**16** When Herod saw that he had been tricked by the magi, he was furious. He sent men to kill all the boys in Bethlehem and all its surrounding region who were two years old and under, according to the time he had found out exactly from the magi.

**17** Then what had been spoken through the prophet Jeremiah was fulfilled:

**18** "A voice was heard in Ramah,[^9] weeping and great mourning: Rachel weeping for her children,[^10] and she refused to be comforted, because they are no more."[^11]

**19** After Herod died, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt

**20** and said, "Get up, take the child and his mother, and go to the land of Israel, because those who were seeking the child's life are dead."

**21** So Joseph got up, took the child and his mother, and went into the land of Israel.

**22** But when he heard that Archelaus[^12] was reigning over Judea in place of his father Herod, he was afraid to go there. And having been warned in a dream, he went away to the region of Galilee.

**23** He went and settled in a town called Nazareth, so that what had been spoken through the prophets would be fulfilled: "He will be called a Nazarene."[^13]

[^1]: Herod the Great, who ruled Judea as king under the Romans from 37 to 4 BC.
[^2]: Greek *magoi*, experts in astrology and the interpretation of dreams, probably from Persia or Babylonia. Traditionally "wise men".
[^3]: Or "in the east"; also in verse 9.
[^4]: Or "pay him homage"; also in verses 8 and 11. Greek *proskyneo*, to bow down before someone, a king or God.
[^5]: Micah 5:1 (5:2 in most English Bibles), quoted freely, with words from 2 Samuel 5:2 ("You will shepherd my people Israel"). Micah's Hebrew reads "But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, too small to be among the clans of Judah, from you one will come out for me to be ruler in Israel."
[^6]: Literally "they rejoiced with very great joy".
[^7]: Frankincense and myrrh are fragrant resins from trees, used for incense, perfume and medicine.
[^8]: Hosea 11:1. Matthew follows the Hebrew; the Septuagint reads "out of Egypt I called his children".
[^9]: Many manuscripts add "lamentation,".
[^10]: Rachel, the mother of Joseph and Benjamin, was buried on the road to Bethlehem (Genesis 35:19).
[^11]: Jeremiah 31:15. Matthew's Greek is shorter than the Hebrew, which reads "lamentation and bitter weeping" and "she refuses to be comforted for her children".
[^12]: A son of Herod. He ruled Judea, Samaria and Idumea from 4 BC to AD 6, with the title of ethnarch ("ruler of a people"), not king.
[^13]: No passage in the Old Testament has these words. *Nazarene* may recall the Hebrew *netser*, "branch" (Isaiah 11:1), or *nazir*, "Nazirite", one set apart to God (Judges 13:5–7). Or "that he would be called a Nazarene".
