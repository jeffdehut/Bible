**1** Then Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tested[^1] by the devil.

**2** After fasting forty days and forty nights, he was hungry.

**3** The tempter came and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread."

**4** Jesus answered, "It is written: 'People do not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.'"[^2]

**5** Then the devil took him to the holy city and set him on the highest point[^3] of the temple,

**6** and he said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down. For it is written: 'He will command his angels concerning you, and they will lift you up in their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.'"[^4]

**7** Jesus said to him, "It is also written: 'Do not put the Lord your God to the test.'"[^5]

**8** Again the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory.

**9** And he said to him, "I will give you all these things if you will fall down and worship me."

**10** Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan![^6] For it is written: 'Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.'"[^7]

**11** Then the devil left him, and angels came and took care of him.

**12** When Jesus heard that John had been arrested,[^8] he withdrew to Galilee.

**13** He left Nazareth and went to live in Capernaum, which is by the sea in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali,

**14** so that what had been spoken through the prophet Isaiah would be fulfilled:

**15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations:[^9]

**16** the people sitting in darkness have seen a great light, and on those sitting in the land and shadow of death a light has dawned."[^10]

**17** From that time Jesus began to proclaim, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near."

**18** As Jesus was walking beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon, who is called Peter, and his brother Andrew, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**19** He said to them, "Come, follow me, and I will make you fish for people."

**20** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**21** Going on from there, he saw two other brothers, James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in the boat with their father Zebedee, mending their nets, and he called them.

**22** At once they left the boat and their father and followed him.

**23** Jesus went throughout Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

**24** The news about him spread through all Syria, and people brought to him all who were ill, suffering from various diseases and pains: those possessed by demons, those who had seizures,[^11] and the paralyzed. And he healed them.

**25** Large crowds followed him from Galilee, the Decapolis,[^12] Jerusalem, Judea, and the region beyond the Jordan.

[^1]: Or "tempted". The Greek word means both. Also in verse 3, "the tempter".
[^2]: Deuteronomy 8:3. Matthew follows the Septuagint, which has "every word" and "God"; the Hebrew reads "everything that comes from the mouth of Yahweh".
[^3]: Literally "the little wing", perhaps a high corner of the temple's outer wall.
[^4]: Psalm 91:11–12, leaving out "to guard you in all your ways".
[^5]: Deuteronomy 6:16. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh; also in verse 10.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "Get behind me, Satan!"
[^7]: Deuteronomy 6:13. The Hebrew reads "Fear Yahweh your God. Serve him"; Matthew has "worship" and "only", in a form close to the Septuagint.
[^8]: Literally "handed over".
[^9]: Or "of the Gentiles".
[^10]: Isaiah 8:23–9:1 (9:1–2 in most English Bibles), in a shortened form. Isaiah's Hebrew has "walked in darkness" and "the land of deep darkness" (traditionally "the shadow of death").
[^11]: Literally "those who were moonstruck", probably people with epilepsy.
[^12]: "The Ten Towns", a group of Greek cities east of the Jordan.
