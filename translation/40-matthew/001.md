**1** The record of the genealogy[^1] of Jesus the Messiah,[^2] the son of David, the son of Abraham.[^3]

**2** Abraham fathered Isaac, Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob fathered Judah and his brothers.[^4]

**3** Judah fathered Perez and Zerah by Tamar, Perez fathered Hezron, and Hezron fathered Ram.[^5]

**4** Ram fathered Amminadab, Amminadab fathered Nahshon, and Nahshon fathered Salmon.

**5** Salmon fathered Boaz by Rahab,[^6] Boaz fathered Obed by Ruth, and Obed fathered Jesse.[^7]

**6** Jesse fathered King David. David[^8] fathered Solomon by the wife of Uriah.[^9]

**7** Solomon fathered Rehoboam, Rehoboam fathered Abijah, and Abijah fathered Asaph.[^10]

**8** Asaph fathered Jehoshaphat, Jehoshaphat fathered Joram, and Joram fathered Uzziah.[^11]

**9** Uzziah fathered Jotham, Jotham fathered Ahaz, and Ahaz fathered Hezekiah.

**10** Hezekiah fathered Manasseh, Manasseh fathered Amos,[^12] and Amos fathered Josiah.

**11** Josiah fathered Jeconiah[^13] and his brothers at the time of the exile to Babylon.[^14]

**12** After the exile to Babylon, Jeconiah fathered Shealtiel, and Shealtiel fathered Zerubbabel.[^15]

**13** Zerubbabel fathered Abiud,[^16] Abiud fathered Eliakim, and Eliakim fathered Azor.

**14** Azor fathered Zadok, Zadok fathered Achim, and Achim fathered Eliud.

**15** Eliud fathered Eleazar, Eleazar fathered Matthan, and Matthan fathered Jacob.

**16** Jacob fathered Joseph, the husband of Mary. From her[^17] was born Jesus, who is called the Messiah.

**17** So there were fourteen generations in all from Abraham to David, fourteen generations from David to the exile to Babylon, and fourteen generations from the exile to Babylon to the Messiah.[^18]

**18** This is how the birth of Jesus the Messiah[^19] came about. His mother Mary was engaged[^20] to Joseph, but before they came together, she was found to be pregnant by the Holy Spirit.

**19** Her husband Joseph was a righteous man and did not want to disgrace her publicly, so he decided to divorce her[^21] quietly.

**20** But when he had thought this over, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, "Joseph son of David, don't be afraid to take Mary home as your wife, because what has been conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.

**21** She will give birth to a son, and you are to name him Jesus,[^22] because he will save his people from their sins."

**22** All this took place so that what the Lord had said through the prophet would be fulfilled:

**23** "Look, the virgin will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel,"[^23] which means "God is with us."

**24** When Joseph woke up from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord had commanded him and took his wife home.

**25** He did not have sexual relations with her[^24] until she had given birth to a son,[^25] and he named him Jesus.

[^1]: Literally "The book of the genesis". Greek *genesis* means origin or birth (as in verse 18). In the Greek Old Testament the same phrase introduces the record of Adam's descendants (Genesis 5:1; compare 2:4).
[^2]: Or "Jesus Christ". Greek *christos*, "anointed one".
[^3]: *Son* here means descendant.
[^4]: Verses 2–6 follow the line in Ruth 4:18–22 and 1 Chronicles 1:34; 2:1–15. The names in verses 2–16 are spelled as in this translation's Old Testament; Matthew gives them in their Greek forms, such as *Phares* (Perez), *Zara* (Zerah) and *Hesrom* (Hezron). The notes mention the forms that differ more widely.
[^5]: Greek *Aram* here and in verse 4. The Hebrew name is Ram (Ruth 4:19; 1 Chronicles 2:9–10).
[^6]: The Old Testament does not name Salmon's wife. Rahab is the name of the woman of Jericho who hid the Israelite spies (Joshua 2; 6:25). Salmon is called Salma in 1 Chronicles 2:11 and Salmah in Ruth 4:20.
[^7]: Greek *Boes* (Boaz) and *Iobed* (Obed).
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "King David".
[^9]: Literally "by her of Uriah", that is, Bathsheba (2 Samuel 11:3; 12:24).
[^10]: Many manuscripts read *Asa*, the king's name in 1 Kings 15:8–24 and 1 Chronicles 3:10; also in verse 8. Asaph is also the name of a psalm writer (Psalms 50; 73–83).
[^11]: Greek *Ozias*. Uzziah, also called Azariah (2 Kings 15:1–7; 1 Chronicles 3:12), was Joram's great-great-grandson: the list passes over Ahaziah, Joash and Amaziah (1 Chronicles 3:11–12).
[^12]: Many manuscripts read *Amon*, the king's name in 2 Kings 21:18–26 and 1 Chronicles 3:14; also later in the verse. Amos is also the name of a prophet.
[^13]: Greek *Iechonias*. Jeconiah, also called Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:6), was Josiah's grandson, the son of Jehoiakim (1 Chronicles 3:15–16).
[^14]: Greek *metoikesia*, "removal, deportation"; also in verses 12 and 17. The people of Judah were taken to Babylon in the days of Jehoiachin and Zedekiah (2 Kings 24:10–16; 25:8–21).
[^15]: Greek *Salathiel* and *Zorobabel*. In 1 Chronicles 3:17–19 Zerubbabel is the son of Pedaiah, Shealtiel's brother; elsewhere he is called the son of Shealtiel (Ezra 3:2; Haggai 1:1).
[^16]: The names from Abiud to Jacob (verses 13–16) are not found in the Old Testament, which lists other descendants of Zerubbabel (1 Chronicles 3:19–24).
[^17]: *Her* is feminine in the Greek. Here the pattern "X fathered Y" changes, and Jesus is said to be born of Mary (verses 18–25).
[^18]: Matthew arranges the genealogy in three sets of fourteen. As the names are listed, the first set has fourteen names (Abraham to David) and the second has fourteen (Solomon to Jeconiah, leaving out the kings mentioned in the note on verse 8). The third has thirteen (Shealtiel to Jesus) unless Jeconiah is counted again at its start. The letters of David's name in Hebrew (DWD) have the number values 4, 6 and 4, which add up to fourteen.
[^19]: Some manuscripts read "the birth of the Messiah".
[^20]: Or "betrothed", "pledged to be married". A betrothal was legally binding: Joseph is already called her husband (verse 19), and ending it required a divorce.
[^21]: Literally "release her", the word used for divorce (5:31–32).
[^22]: *Jesus* is the Greek form of the Hebrew name Joshua (*Yeshua*), which means "Yahweh saves". Yahweh is God's personal name.
[^23]: Isaiah 7:14. Matthew follows the Greek Septuagint, which has *parthenos*, "virgin", where the Hebrew has *almah*, "young woman", a girl old enough to marry. The Hebrew reads "she will call"; Matthew reads "they will call".
[^24]: Literally "he was not knowing her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^25]: Many manuscripts read "her firstborn son".
