**1** Dishonest scales are detestable[^1] to Yahweh,[^2] but an accurate weight[^3] is his delight.

**2** When pride comes, disgrace comes too, but with the humble is wisdom.

**3** The integrity of the upright guides them, but the crookedness of the treacherous destroys them.

**4** Wealth is of no use on the day of wrath, but righteousness rescues from death.

**5** The righteousness of the blameless makes their way straight, but the wicked[^4] fall by their own wickedness.

**6** The righteousness of the upright rescues them, but the treacherous are caught by their own craving.[^5]

**7** When a wicked person dies, his hope perishes, and the expectation placed in wealth perishes too.[^6]

**8** The righteous[^7] person is rescued from trouble, and the wicked comes in his place.

**9** With his mouth the godless destroys his neighbor, but by knowledge the righteous are rescued.

**10** When the righteous prosper, the city rejoices, and when the wicked perish, there are shouts of joy.

**11** By the blessing of the upright a city is raised up, but by the mouth of the wicked it is torn down.

**12** Whoever belittles his neighbor lacks sense,[^8] but a person of insight[^9] keeps silent.

**13** A gossip goes around revealing secrets, but one who is trustworthy in spirit keeps a matter hidden.

**14** Without guidance[^10] a people falls, but with many advisers there is victory.

**15** Whoever puts up security for a stranger will surely suffer for it, but whoever hates shaking hands on a pledge is safe.[^11]

**16** A gracious woman takes hold of honor, and ruthless men take hold of wealth.[^12]

**17** A kind man[^13] benefits himself, but a cruel man brings trouble on his own body.

**18** The wicked earns deceptive wages, but whoever sows righteousness reaps a true reward.

**19** Truly righteousness leads to life,[^14] but whoever pursues evil goes to his death.

**20** People with crooked hearts are detestable to Yahweh, but those whose way is blameless are his delight.

**21** You can be sure[^15] the evil person will not go unpunished, but the descendants of the righteous will escape.

**22** Like a gold ring in a pig's snout is a beautiful woman who lacks good judgment.[^16]

**23** The desire of the righteous ends only in good; the hope of the wicked, in wrath.

**24** One person gives freely and yet grows richer; another holds back more than is right and only comes to need.

**25** A generous person[^17] will grow fat, and whoever gives water will himself be watered.

**26** People curse whoever holds back grain, but blessing is on the head of the one who sells it.

**27** Whoever eagerly seeks good is seeking favor, but evil comes to the one who looks for it.

**28** Whoever trusts in his riches will fall, but the righteous will flourish like a green leaf.

**29** Whoever brings trouble on his household will inherit the wind, and the fool[^18] will be servant to the wise of heart.

**30** The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life, and whoever wins people over is wise.[^19]

**31** If the righteous are repaid on earth, how much more the wicked and the sinner![^20]

[^1]: Hebrew *to'evah*, something loathsome, traditionally "an abomination" (see 3:32). Also in verse 20.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "a full stone", a weight used with scales.
[^4]: Hebrew *rasha*: a wicked person, one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^5]: Or "by their own destructive schemes". Hebrew *havvah*, as in 10:3.
[^6]: Hebrew *onim*, probably "strength, wealth". Or "and the hope of the wicked perishes", taking *onim* from *aven*, "iniquity".
[^7]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: a righteous person, one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^8]: Literally "lacks heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^9]: Literally "a man of insights". Hebrew *tevunot*, plural of *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^10]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, as in 1:5.
[^11]: See 6:1–5. *Shaking hands* is literally "those who strike", that is, strike hands to seal a pledge.
[^12]: The Septuagint has four lines: "A gracious wife brings glory to her husband, but a woman who hates righteousness is a throne of dishonor. The lazy come to lack wealth, but the courageous lean on wealth."
[^13]: Hebrew *ish chesed*, "a man of faithful love".
[^14]: Or "One who is steadfast in righteousness will live".
[^15]: Literally "Hand to hand", perhaps the gesture of a pledge.
[^16]: Hebrew *ta'am*, "taste", here discretion, good judgment.
[^17]: Literally "A soul of blessing". Growing fat is a picture of prosperity.
[^18]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^19]: Literally "and whoever takes lives (*nephashot*) is wise". Elsewhere the phrase means "take lives, kill" (1:19), but here it probably means winning people over. The Septuagint reads "but the lives of the lawless are taken away before their time".
[^20]: 1 Peter 4:18 quotes this verse from the Septuagint: "If the righteous is scarcely saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?"
