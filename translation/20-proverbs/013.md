**1** A wise son heeds his father's discipline,[^1] but a mocker[^2] does not listen to rebuke.

**2** From the fruit of his mouth a person eats what is good, but the treacherous crave violence.[^3]

**3** Whoever guards his mouth preserves his life; whoever opens his lips wide comes to ruin.

**4** The lazy person[^4] craves and gets nothing, but the diligent are fully satisfied.[^5]

**5** The righteous[^6] hate what is false, but the wicked[^7] bring shame and disgrace.[^8]

**6** Righteousness guards the one whose way is blameless, but wickedness overthrows the sinner.[^9]

**7** One pretends to be rich, yet has nothing; another pretends to be poor, yet has great wealth.

**8** Wealth is the ransom for a person's life, but the poor hear no threat.

**9** The light of the righteous shines brightly,[^10] but the lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out.

**10** Pride brings only strife, but wisdom is with those who take advice.

**11** Wealth gained from nothing[^11] dwindles, but whoever gathers little by little makes it grow.

**12** Hope deferred makes the heart sick, but a desire fulfilled is a tree of life.

**13** Whoever despises the word will pay for it,[^12] but whoever respects the command will be rewarded.

**14** The instruction of the wise is a fountain of life, for turning away from the snares of death.

**15** Good sense[^13] wins favor, but the way of the treacherous is hard.[^14]

**16** Every prudent person[^15] acts with knowledge, but a fool[^16] spreads out his folly.[^17]

**17** A wicked messenger falls into trouble, but a faithful envoy brings healing.

**18** Poverty and disgrace come to whoever ignores discipline, but whoever heeds correction will be honored.

**19** Desire fulfilled is sweet to the soul, but turning away from evil is detestable to fools.

**20** Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise,[^18] but the companion of fools will suffer harm.[^19]

**21** Trouble pursues sinners, but good rewards the righteous.

**22** A good person leaves an inheritance to his grandchildren, but the sinner's wealth is stored up for the righteous.

**23** The unplowed field of the poor yields much food, but it is swept away by injustice.[^20]

**24** Whoever spares his rod hates his son, but whoever loves him is careful to discipline him.

**25** The righteous eats until he is satisfied, but the stomach of the wicked goes hungry.

[^1]: Literally "A wise son, a father's discipline". Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verses 18 and 24.
[^2]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22).
[^3]: Literally "but the appetite (*nephesh*) of the treacherous, violence".
[^4]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^5]: Literally "the appetite (*nephesh*) of the diligent will be made fat".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^7]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^8]: Or "make themselves a stench and a disgrace".
[^9]: Literally "overthrows sin".
[^10]: Literally "rejoices".
[^11]: Literally "from a breath (*hevel*)", that is, gained without effort or by dishonest means. The Septuagint reads "Wealth gained in haste".
[^12]: Or "will be held in pledge by it".
[^13]: Hebrew *sekhel tov*, "good sense".
[^14]: Hebrew *eitan*, usually "lasting, enduring"; here perhaps "hard, rough". The Septuagint reads "leads to ruin".
[^15]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, prudent" (see 1:4).
[^16]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 19 and 20.
[^17]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "Walk with the wise and become wise" but read "Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise".
[^19]: In Hebrew there is a play on words: *ro'eh*, "companion", and *yero'a*, "will suffer harm".
[^20]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "Much food, the fallow ground of the poor, but there is what is swept away without justice".
