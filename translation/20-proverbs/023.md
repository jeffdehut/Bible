**1** When you sit down to eat with a ruler, consider carefully what is before you,[^1]

**2** and put a knife to your throat if you have a big appetite.[^2]

**3** Do not crave his delicacies, for they are deceptive food.

**4** Do not wear yourself out to get rich; stop relying on your own understanding.[^3]

**5** When your eyes fly to it, it is gone;[^4] for it will surely make itself wings and fly off to the sky like an eagle.

**6** Do not eat the bread of a stingy person,[^5] and do not crave his delicacies,

**7** for he is like one who keeps count in his mind.[^6] "Eat and drink!" he says to you, but his heart is not with you.

**8** You will vomit up the morsel you have eaten and waste your pleasant words.

**9** Do not speak in the hearing of a fool,[^7] for he will despise the good sense of your words.

**10** Do not move an ancient boundary stone[^8] or encroach on the fields of the fatherless,

**11** for their redeemer[^9] is strong; he will plead their case against you.

**12** Apply your heart to discipline[^10] and your ears to words of knowledge.

**13** Do not withhold discipline from a child; if you strike him with a rod, he will not die.

**14** Strike him with the rod, and you will rescue his life from Sheol.[^11]

**15** My son, if your heart is wise, my heart too will be glad,

**16** and my inmost being[^12] will rejoice when your lips speak what is right.

**17** Do not let your heart envy sinners, but continue[^13] in the fear of the LORD[^14] all day long.

**18** For surely there is a future, and your hope will not be cut off.

**19** Listen, my son, and be wise, and direct your heart in the way.

**20** Do not be among those who drink too much wine or those who gorge themselves on meat,

**21** for the drunkard and the glutton will become poor, and drowsiness clothes them in rags.

**22** Listen to your father, who gave you life, and do not despise your mother when she is old.

**23** Buy truth and do not sell it, wisdom, discipline and understanding.

**24** The father of a righteous person[^15] will greatly rejoice; whoever fathers a wise son will be glad in him.

**25** Let your father and mother be glad; let her who bore you rejoice.

**26** My son, give me your heart, and let your eyes keep to my ways.[^16]

**27** For a prostitute is a deep pit, and a foreign woman[^17] is a narrow well.

**28** She lies in wait like a robber and increases the number of the treacherous among men.

**29** Who has woe? Who has sorrow? Who has quarrels? Who has complaints? Who has wounds for no reason? Who has bloodshot eyes?

**30** Those who linger over wine, who go to taste mixed wine.

**31** Do not look at wine when it is red, when it sparkles in the cup,[^18] when it goes down smoothly.

**32** In the end it bites like a snake and stings like a viper.

**33** Your eyes will see strange things,[^19] and your heart will speak perverse things.

**34** You will be like one who lies down in the middle of the sea, like one who lies on top of a mast.[^20]

**35** "They hit me," you will say,[^21] "but I was not hurt; they beat me, but I did not feel it. When will I wake up? I will look for another drink."[^22]

[^1]: Or "who is before you".
[^2]: Literally "if you are a master of appetite (*nephesh*)".
[^3]: Literally "from your understanding (*binah*), stop". Or "have the sense to stop".
[^4]: Literally "Will you let your eyes fly to it, and it is not?"
[^5]: Literally "one who is evil of eye".
[^6]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "for as he has calculated in his soul, so is he". With different vowels, the same consonants can be read "like a hair in his throat".
[^7]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22).
[^8]: Compare 22:28.
[^9]: Hebrew *go'el*, a relative whose duty was to defend a family member and buy back what he had lost (Leviticus 25:25; Ruth 4:4–6). Here probably God (compare 22:23).
[^10]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verses 13 and 23.
[^11]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^12]: Literally "my kidneys", thought of as the seat of the emotions.
[^13]: Literally "but in the fear of the LORD all day".
[^14]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^15]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "delight in my ways" but read "keep to my ways".
[^17]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16).
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "in the purse" but read "in the cup". Literally "when it gives its eye in the cup".
[^19]: Or "strange women" (22:14).
[^20]: Hebrew *chibbel*, a word found only here, probably "mast" or "rigging".
[^21]: The words *you will say* are not in the Hebrew. The verse gives the drunkard's own words.
[^22]: Literally "I will seek it again".
