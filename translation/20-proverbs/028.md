**1** The wicked[^1] flee when no one pursues, but the righteous[^2] are as bold as a lion.

**2** When a land rebels, it has many rulers, but through a person who understands and knows, its stability lasts.[^3]

**3** A poor man who oppresses the poor is like a driving rain that leaves no food.

**4** Those who abandon the law[^4] praise the wicked, but those who keep the law fight against them.

**5** Evil people do not understand justice, but those who seek Yahweh[^5] understand everything.

**6** Better a poor person who walks in integrity than one whose ways are crooked,[^6] even if he is rich.

**7** Whoever keeps the law is a discerning son, but a companion of gluttons shames his father.

**8** Whoever increases his wealth by interest and profit[^7] gathers it for one who is kind to the poor.

**9** Whoever turns his ear away from hearing the law, even his prayer is detestable.

**10** Whoever leads the upright astray into an evil way will fall into his own pit, but the blameless will inherit good.

**11** A rich man is wise in his own eyes, but a poor man who is discerning sees through him.

**12** When the righteous triumph, there is great glory, but when the wicked rise, people hide.[^8]

**13** Whoever covers his offenses will not prosper, but whoever confesses and abandons them will find mercy.

**14** Happy[^9] is the one who is always reverent,[^10] but whoever hardens his heart will fall into trouble.

**15** Like a roaring lion or a charging bear is a wicked ruler over a poor people.

**16** A ruler who lacks insight[^11] is a great oppressor, but whoever hates unjust gain will live long.

**17** A man burdened with someone's blood will be a fugitive until the Pit;[^12] let no one hold him back.

**18** Whoever walks blamelessly will be kept safe, but whoever is crooked in his ways will fall all at once.

**19** Whoever works his land will have plenty of bread, but whoever chases worthless things will have plenty of poverty.

**20** A faithful person will have many blessings, but whoever hurries to get rich will not go unpunished.

**21** Showing partiality is not good, yet for a piece of bread a man will do wrong.

**22** A stingy man[^13] hurries after wealth and does not know that poverty will come upon him.

**23** Whoever corrects someone will in the end[^14] find more favor than one who flatters with his tongue.

**24** Whoever robs his father or his mother and says, "It is no offense," is a companion of a destroyer.

**25** The greedy person[^15] stirs up strife, but whoever trusts in Yahweh will prosper.

**26** Whoever trusts in his own heart is a fool,[^16] but whoever walks in wisdom will be kept safe.

**27** Whoever gives to the poor will not lack, but whoever shuts his eyes will receive many curses.

**28** When the wicked rise, people hide, but when they perish, the righteous increase.

[^1]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^2]: Hebrew *tsaddiqim*, plural of *tsaddiq*: those who do what is right (see 2:20).
[^3]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "but by a person who understands, who knows, so it lasts long".
[^4]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction", here probably God's law. Also in verses 7 and 9.
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: Literally "crooked of two ways", perhaps double-dealing. Also in verse 18. Compare 19:1.
[^7]: Lending at interest to a fellow Israelite was forbidden (Leviticus 25:36–37).
[^8]: Literally "a person must be searched for".
[^9]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^10]: Literally "who fears always", that is, fears God, or fears doing wrong.
[^11]: Literally "lacking insights". Hebrew *tevunot*, plural of *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^12]: That is, until death. Hebrew *bor*, "pit", as in 1:12.
[^13]: Literally "A man with an evil eye".
[^14]: Hebrew *acharai*, literally "after me", probably "afterward".
[^15]: Literally "One who is wide of appetite (*nephesh*)".
[^16]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22).
