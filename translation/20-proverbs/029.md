**1** Whoever is often corrected yet stays stiff-necked will suddenly be broken, and there will be no healing.

**2** When the righteous[^1] increase, the people rejoice, but when the wicked[^2] rule, the people groan.

**3** A man who loves wisdom makes his father glad, but a companion of prostitutes squanders his wealth.

**4** By justice a king gives a land stability, but a man who exacts contributions[^3] tears it down.

**5** A man who flatters his neighbor is spreading a net for his feet.[^4]

**6** An evil man is snared by his own offense, but the righteous sings and rejoices.

**7** The righteous knows the rights of the poor; the wicked has no such understanding.[^5]

**8** Mockers[^6] set a city aflame, but the wise turn away anger.

**9** If a wise man goes to court with a fool,[^7] the fool rages and laughs,[^8] and there is no peace.

**10** Bloodthirsty men hate the blameless, and as for the upright, they seek his life.[^9]

**11** A fool[^10] gives full vent to his anger,[^11] but a wise person quietly holds it back.

**12** If a ruler listens to lies, all his officials become wicked.

**13** The poor and the oppressor meet together; the LORD[^12] gives light to the eyes of both.[^13]

**14** If a king judges the poor with faithfulness, his throne will be established forever.

**15** The rod and correction give wisdom, but a child left to himself brings shame to his mother.

**16** When the wicked increase, offense increases, but the righteous will see their downfall.

**17** Discipline your son, and he will give you rest; he will bring delight to your soul.

**18** Where there is no vision,[^14] the people run wild, but whoever keeps the law[^15] is happy.[^16]

**19** A servant cannot be disciplined by words alone; though he understands, he will not respond.

**20** Do you see someone who is hasty in his words? There is more hope for a fool than for him.

**21** If someone pampers his servant from youth, in the end it will bring grief.[^17]

**22** An angry man stirs up strife, and a hot-tempered man commits many offenses.

**23** A person's pride will bring him low, but the lowly in spirit will gain honor.

**24** Whoever is a partner with a thief hates his own life; he hears the curse but does not testify.[^18]

**25** Fear of people sets a snare, but whoever trusts in the LORD is kept safe.

**26** Many seek the favor of a ruler, but justice for a person comes from the LORD.

**27** An unjust man is detestable to the righteous, and one whose way is upright is detestable to the wicked.

[^1]: Hebrew *tsaddiqim*, plural of *tsaddiq*: those who do what is right (see 2:20).
[^2]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^3]: Hebrew *terumot*, "contributions", perhaps heavy taxes or bribes.
[^4]: Either his neighbor's feet or his own.
[^5]: Literally "the wicked does not understand knowledge".
[^6]: Literally "Men of mockery". Hebrew *latson*, from the same root as *lets*, "mocker" (see 1:22).
[^7]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^8]: Or "whether the wise man is angry or laughs".
[^9]: Literally "and the upright seek his life". This may mean that the upright care for the blameless person's life, or that the bloodthirsty seek the life of the upright.
[^10]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verse 20.
[^11]: Literally "brings out all his spirit (*ruach*)".
[^12]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^13]: Compare 22:2.
[^14]: Hebrew *chazon*, a prophet's vision, revelation from God.
[^15]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction", here probably God's law.
[^16]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^17]: Literally "and his end will be *manon*", a word found only here, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "grief", "insolence" or "an heir".
[^18]: The *curse* is the public oath that called on witnesses to come forward (Leviticus 5:1).
