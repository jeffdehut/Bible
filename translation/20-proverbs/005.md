**1** My son, pay attention to my wisdom; turn your ear to my insight,[^1]

**2** so that you may keep discretion,[^2] and your lips may guard knowledge.

**3** For the lips of the strange woman[^3] drip honey, and her speech[^4] is smoother than oil.

**4** But in the end she is as bitter as wormwood,[^5] as sharp as a two-edged sword.

**5** Her feet go down to death; her steps lead straight to Sheol.[^6]

**6** She gives no thought to the path of life; her tracks wander, and she does not know it.[^7]

**7** So now, sons, listen to me, and do not turn away from the words of my mouth.

**8** Keep your way far from her, and do not go near the door of her house,

**9** or you will give your vigor to others and your years to someone cruel,

**10** or strangers will feast on your strength, and what you have toiled for will go to the house of a foreigner.

**11** At the end of your life you will groan, when your flesh and your body are worn out,

**12** and you will say, "How I hated discipline![^8] How my heart spurned correction!

**13** I did not listen to the voice of my teachers or turn my ear to my instructors.

**14** I have almost come to utter ruin[^9] in the middle of the assembly and the community."

**15** Drink water from your own cistern, flowing water from your own well.

**16** Should your springs overflow into the street, streams of water in the public squares?[^10]

**17** Let them be yours alone, not shared with strangers.

**18** Let your fountain be blessed, and rejoice in the wife of your youth,

**19** a loving doe, a graceful deer. Let her breasts satisfy you at all times; be intoxicated[^11] always with her love.

**20** Why should you be intoxicated, my son, with a strange woman, and embrace the breast of a foreign woman?[^12]

**21** For a person's ways are in full view of the LORD's eyes,[^13] and he examines all their paths.

**22** The wicked man's own iniquities will capture him, and he will be held fast by the cords of his sin.

**23** He will die for lack of discipline, and in his great folly[^14] he will go astray.

[^1]: Hebrew *tevunah*, from the same root as *binah*, "understanding" (see 2:2).
[^2]: Hebrew *mezimmot*, plural of *mezimmah*, as in 1:4.
[^3]: Hebrew *zarah*, "strange woman", that is, a woman outside one's marriage (see 2:16). Also in verse 20.
[^4]: Literally "her palate".
[^5]: A bitter plant.
[^6]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^7]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "The path of life, lest she make it level (or weigh it); her tracks wander; she does not know". The verbs could also be "you": "so that you do not consider the path of life … you do not know it".
[^8]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verse 23.
[^9]: Literally "I was almost in all evil". Or "I almost did every kind of evil". The *assembly and the community* may be the gathering that judged wrongdoing.
[^10]: Or "Let your springs overflow …", meaning many children; but the question in verses 16–17 more likely warns against spending oneself on other women.
[^11]: Hebrew *shagah*, "stagger, go astray": here the dizziness of love. The same verb is translated *be intoxicated* in verse 20 and *go astray* in verse 23.
[^12]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16).
[^13]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^14]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
