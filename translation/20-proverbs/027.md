**1** Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring.

**2** Let a stranger praise you, and not your own mouth, an outsider,[^1] and not your own lips.

**3** A stone is heavy and sand is weighty, but a fool's provocation is heavier than both.[^2]

**4** Wrath is cruel and anger is a flood, but who can stand before jealousy?

**5** Better open correction than hidden love.

**6** Wounds from a friend can be trusted, but the kisses of an enemy are lavish.[^3]

**7** One who is full tramples on honey, but to one who is hungry everything bitter is sweet.

**8** Like a bird that strays from its nest is a man who strays from his home.

**9** Oil and incense make the heart glad, and the sweetness of a friend comes from heartfelt advice.[^4]

**10** Do not abandon your friend or your father's friend, and do not go to your brother's house on the day of your disaster. Better a neighbor nearby than a brother far away.

**11** Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad, so that I can answer anyone who taunts me.

**12** The prudent[^5] sees danger and hides himself; the simple[^6] go on and pay for it.[^7]

**13** Take the garment of one who puts up security for a stranger; hold it in pledge for a foreign woman.[^8]

**14** Whoever blesses his neighbor with a loud voice early in the morning, it will be counted to him as a curse.

**15** A constant dripping on a rainy day and a quarrelsome wife are alike;

**16** whoever restrains her restrains the wind or grasps oil with his right hand.[^9]

**17** As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another.[^10]

**18** Whoever tends a fig tree will eat its fruit, and whoever looks after his master will be honored.

**19** As water reflects the face, so the heart reflects the person.[^11]

**20** Sheol and Abaddon[^12] are never satisfied, and human eyes are never satisfied.

**21** The crucible is for silver and the furnace for gold, and a person is tested by the praise he receives.[^13]

**22** Even if you pound a fool[^14] in a mortar, with a pestle among the grain, his folly will not leave him.

**23** Know well the condition of your flocks; give careful attention to your herds,

**24** for riches do not last forever, and does a crown last for all generations?

**25** When the grass is gone and the new growth appears, and the plants of the hills are gathered in,

**26** the lambs will provide your clothing, and the goats the price of a field,

**27** and there will be enough goats' milk for your food, for the food of your household, and to keep your servant girls alive.

[^1]: Hebrew *zar* and *nokhri*, the masculine forms of the words used for the "strange woman" and the "foreign woman" (2:16).
[^2]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^3]: Hebrew *na'tarot*, perhaps "many, lavish", or "deceitful".
[^4]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "and the sweetness of his friend from the advice of the soul". Or "and the sweetness of a friend is better than one's own advice".
[^5]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, prudent" (see 1:4).
[^6]: Hebrew *peta'im*, plural of *peti*: naive, inexperienced people, easily led (see 1:4).
[^7]: The verse repeats 22:3.
[^8]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16). The verse repeats 20:16.
[^9]: The Hebrew of this verse is obscure. Literally "Those who hide her hide wind, and oil his right hand meets".
[^10]: Literally "so a man sharpens the face of his friend".
[^11]: Literally "As in water face to face, so the heart of a person to a person". This may mean that a person's heart shows what he is, or that one sees oneself in another's heart.
[^12]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead, and *Abaddon*, "Destruction", another name for it (see 15:11).
[^13]: Literally "and a man according to his praise". It may mean that a person is tested by how he takes praise, or known by what he praises.
[^14]: Hebrew *ewil*, as in verse 3. *Folly* is *iwwelet*, the related word.
