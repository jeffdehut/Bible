**1** The proverbs of Solomon.[^1] A wise son makes a father glad, but a foolish[^2] son is his mother's grief.

**2** Treasures gained by wickedness are of no use, but righteousness rescues from death.

**3** The LORD[^3] will not let the righteous[^4] go hungry, but he thrusts away the craving[^5] of the wicked.[^6]

**4** A slack hand makes a person poor, but the hand of the diligent brings wealth.

**5** A son who gathers in summer is sensible;[^7] a son who sleeps at harvest brings shame.

**6** Blessings are on the head of the righteous, but the mouth of the wicked conceals violence.[^8]

**7** The memory of the righteous is a blessing, but the name of the wicked will rot.

**8** The wise in heart accept commands, but a chattering fool[^9] will be ruined.

**9** Whoever walks in integrity walks securely, but whoever makes his ways crooked will be found out.

**10** Whoever winks with the eye causes trouble, and a chattering fool will be ruined.[^10]

**11** The mouth of the righteous is a fountain of life, but the mouth of the wicked conceals violence.

**12** Hatred stirs up quarrels, but love covers all offenses.[^11]

**13** Wisdom is found on the lips of the discerning,[^12] but a rod is for the back of one who lacks sense.[^13]

**14** The wise store up knowledge, but the mouth of a fool brings ruin near.

**15** The wealth of the rich is their strong city; the ruin of the poor is their poverty.

**16** The wages of the righteous lead to life; the income of the wicked leads to sin.[^14]

**17** Whoever heeds discipline[^15] is on the path to life, but whoever rejects correction leads others astray.[^16]

**18** Whoever hides hatred has lying lips, and whoever spreads slander is a fool.

**19** When words are many, wrongdoing is not lacking, but whoever restrains his lips is sensible.

**20** The tongue of the righteous is choice silver; the heart of the wicked is worth little.

**21** The lips of the righteous feed many, but fools die for lack of sense.

**22** The blessing of the LORD makes rich, and he adds no sorrow to it.[^17]

**23** To a fool, doing wrong is like play, and so is wisdom to a person of insight.[^18]

**24** What the wicked dreads will come upon him, but what the righteous desire will be granted.

**25** When the storm has passed, the wicked are gone, but the righteous are a lasting foundation.

**26** Like vinegar to the teeth and smoke to the eyes, so is the lazy person[^19] to those who send him.

**27** The fear of the LORD adds days, but the years of the wicked will be cut short.

**28** The hope of the righteous ends in joy, but the expectation of the wicked will perish.

**29** The way of the LORD is a stronghold for the blameless, but ruin for those who do evil.

**30** The righteous will never be shaken, but the wicked will not remain in the land.

**31** The mouth of the righteous yields wisdom, but a perverse tongue will be cut off.

**32** The lips of the righteous know what is pleasing, but the mouth of the wicked knows what is perverse.

[^1]: A new collection begins here (10:1–22:16), made up mostly of two-line sayings, often contrasting the wise and the foolish, the righteous and the wicked.
[^2]: Hebrew *kesil*, the most common word for "fool" in Proverbs: someone dull and self-satisfied who does not want to learn (see 1:22). Also in verses 18 and 23.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: a righteous person, one who does what is right toward God and other people (see 2:20).
[^5]: Hebrew *havvah*, "craving", a word that can also mean "destruction", as in Psalm 52:4 (52:2 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: wicked people, those who are guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^7]: Hebrew *maskil*, from the same root as *sekhel*, "good sense": one who acts with good sense. Also in verse 19.
[^8]: Or "but violence covers the mouth of the wicked". Also in verse 11.
[^9]: Literally "a fool of lips". Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7). Also in verses 10, 14 and 21.
[^10]: The second line repeats the second line of verse 8. The Septuagint reads "but whoever rebukes openly makes peace".
[^11]: Compare 1 Peter 4:8 and James 5:20.
[^12]: Hebrew *navon*, from the same root as *binah*, "understanding".
[^13]: Literally "lacks heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding. Also in verse 21.
[^14]: Or "to punishment". Hebrew *chatta't*, "sin", which can also mean its penalty.
[^15]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2).
[^16]: Or "goes astray".
[^17]: Or "and toil adds nothing to it".
[^18]: Literally "Like laughter to a fool is doing wickedness, and wisdom to a man of insight (*tevunah*)". That is, a person of insight delights in wisdom as a fool delights in wrongdoing.
[^19]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
