**1** The words of King Lemuel, an oracle[^1] that his mother taught him.

**2** What, my son? What, son of my womb? What, son of my vows?[^2]

**3** Do not give your strength to women, your ways to those who destroy kings.

**4** It is not for kings, Lemuel, not for kings to drink wine, nor for rulers to ask, "Where is beer?"[^3]

**5** or they will drink and forget what has been decreed and pervert the rights of all the afflicted.

**6** Give beer to one who is perishing, and wine to those who are bitter in soul.

**7** Let them drink and forget their poverty and remember their trouble no more.

**8** Open your mouth for those who cannot speak, for the rights of all who are destitute.[^4]

**9** Open your mouth, judge rightly, and defend the rights of the poor and needy.

**10** A capable wife,[^5] who can find? She is worth far more than coral.[^6]

**11** Her husband's heart trusts in her, and he will not lack gain.[^7]

**12** She does him good and not harm all the days of her life.

**13** She seeks out wool and flax and works with willing hands.

**14** She is like the merchant ships; she brings her food from far away.

**15** She gets up while it is still night and provides food for her household and portions[^8] for her servant girls.

**16** She considers a field and buys it; from her earnings she plants a vineyard.

**17** She wraps herself in strength and makes her arms strong.[^9]

**18** She sees that her trading is profitable; her lamp does not go out at night.

**19** She puts her hands to the distaff, and her palms grasp the spindle.

**20** She opens her hand to the poor and reaches out her hands to the needy.

**21** She is not afraid of the snow for her household, for all her household are clothed in scarlet.[^10]

**22** She makes coverings for herself; her clothing is fine linen and purple.

**23** Her husband is known at the city gates, when he sits among the elders of the land.

**24** She makes linen garments and sells them, and supplies sashes to the merchants.[^11]

**25** Strength and dignity are her clothing, and she laughs at the time to come.

**26** She opens her mouth with wisdom, and loving instruction[^12] is on her tongue.

**27** She watches over the ways of her household and does not eat the bread of idleness.

**28** Her children rise up and call her happy;[^13] her husband too, and he praises her:

**29** "Many women have done capably,[^14] but you surpass them all."

**30** Charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting,[^15] but a woman who fears the LORD[^16] is to be praised.

**31** Give her the fruit of her hands, and let her works praise her in the gates.

[^1]: Or "The words of Lemuel king of Massa" (see 30:1). Verses 2–9 are the words of his mother.
[^2]: The Hebrew word for "son" here is *bar*, the Aramaic word, used three times in this verse.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "or beer" but read "Where is beer?"
[^4]: Literally "all the sons of passing away", perhaps those who are perishing or have no one to stand up for them.
[^5]: Hebrew *eshet chayil*, "a woman of strength, of worth", as in 12:4 and Ruth 3:11. Verses 10–31 form an acrostic poem: each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet, from *aleph* to *tav*.
[^6]: Hebrew *peninim*, as in 3:15.
[^7]: Literally "plunder".
[^8]: Or "tasks".
[^9]: Literally "She girds her waist with strength".
[^10]: With different vowels, "clothed in double garments", as the Vulgate reads.
[^11]: Literally "to the Canaanite", a word that came to mean "trader".
[^12]: Literally "the instruction (*torah*) of faithful love (*chesed*)". Or "kind teaching".
[^13]: The verb is from the same root as *ashrei*, "happy" (see 3:13).
[^14]: Hebrew *chayil*, the word translated "capable" in verse 10. Literally "Many daughters have done capably".
[^15]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", the word used throughout Ecclesiastes.
[^16]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
