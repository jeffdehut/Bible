**1** The words of Agur son of Jakeh, the oracle.[^1] The man declares to Ithiel, to Ithiel and Ucal:[^2]

**2** Surely I am too stupid[^3] to be a man; I do not have human understanding.[^4]

**3** I have not learned wisdom, nor do I have knowledge of the Holy One.[^5]

**4** Who has gone up to heaven and come down? Who has gathered the wind in his fists? Who has wrapped up the waters in a cloak? Who has established all the ends of the earth? What is his name, and what is his son's name? Surely you know![^6]

**5** Every word of God[^7] is refined; he is a shield to those who take refuge in him.

**6** Do not add to his words, or he will rebuke you, and you will be found a liar.[^8]

**7** Two things I ask of you; do not refuse me before I die:

**8** keep falsehood and lies far from me; give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with the bread I need,[^9]

**9** or I may become full and deny you and say, "Who is Yahweh?"[^10] or I may become poor and steal, and profane the name of my God.

**10** Do not slander a servant to his master, or he will curse you, and you will be held guilty.

**11** There are those[^11] who curse their father and do not bless their mother.

**12** There are those who are pure in their own eyes, yet are not washed of their filth.

**13** There are those whose eyes are so haughty, whose eyelids are lifted high.

**14** There are those whose teeth are swords, whose jaws are knives, to devour the poor from the earth and the needy from among humankind.

**15** The leech[^12] has two daughters: "Give! Give!" There are three things that are never satisfied, four that never say, "Enough!":[^13]

**16** Sheol,[^14] the barren womb, land that is never satisfied with water, and fire that never says, "Enough!"

**17** The eye that mocks a father and scorns obedience[^15] to a mother will be pecked out by the ravens of the valley and eaten by the young vultures.[^16]

**18** There are three things too wonderful for me, four that I do not understand:

**19** the way of an eagle in the sky, the way of a snake on a rock, the way of a ship on the high seas, and the way of a man with a young woman.[^17]

**20** This is the way of an adulterous woman: she eats and wipes her mouth and says, "I have done nothing wrong."

**21** Under three things the earth trembles, under four it cannot bear up:

**22** a servant when he becomes king, a godless fool[^18] when he is filled with food,

**23** an unloved woman when she gets a husband, and a servant girl when she takes the place of her mistress.

**24** Four things on earth are small, but they are exceedingly wise:

**25** the ants are a people without strength, yet they store up their food in summer;

**26** the rock badgers[^19] are a people without power, yet they make their homes in the rocks;

**27** the locusts have no king, yet they all go out in ranks;

**28** the lizard[^20] can be caught in the hand,[^21] yet it lives in kings' palaces.

**29** There are three things that are stately in their stride, four that are stately in their walk:

**30** the lion, mightiest among beasts, which does not turn back before anything;

**31** the strutting rooster,[^22] or the male goat, and a king with his army around him.[^23]

**32** If you have been foolish, exalting yourself, or if you have been scheming, put your hand over your mouth.

**33** For pressing milk produces curds, pressing the nose produces blood, and pressing anger produces strife.[^24]

[^1]: Or "of Massa", an Arabian tribe descended from Ishmael (Genesis 25:14); also in 31:1. Nothing more is known of Agur, and it is not clear where his words end. On the order of chapters 30–31 in the Septuagint, see the note on 24:23.
[^2]: With different vowels and a different division of the words, "I am weary, O God; I am weary, O God, and I am worn out".
[^3]: Hebrew *ba'ar*, "brutish", as in 12:1.
[^4]: Hebrew *binah*, as in 1:2.
[^5]: Literally "and the knowledge of the Holy One I know"; the "not" of the first line probably carries over. Or "yet I know the knowledge of the Holy One". *The Holy One* is Hebrew *qedoshim*, as in 9:10.
[^6]: Or "if you know!"
[^7]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God". The line is nearly the same as Psalm 18:31 (18:30 in most English Bibles) and 2 Samuel 22:31.
[^8]: Compare Deuteronomy 4:2; 13:1 (12:32 in most English Bibles).
[^9]: Literally "the bread of my portion".
[^10]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^11]: Literally "A generation", that is, a kind of people. Also in verses 12–14.
[^12]: Hebrew *aluqah*, a word found only here, probably "leech".
[^13]: The pattern "three … four" builds up to the last item in the list (compare 6:16). Also in verses 18, 21 and 29.
[^14]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^15]: Hebrew *yiqqahat*, a rare word, perhaps "obedience" (Genesis 49:10).
[^16]: Or "young eagles". Hebrew *nesher*.
[^17]: Hebrew *almah*, a young woman old enough to marry (Genesis 24:43; Isaiah 7:14).
[^18]: Hebrew *nabal*: a senseless, godless person (see 17:7).
[^19]: Hebrew *shefannim*, the rock hyrax, a small animal that lives among rocks (Psalm 104:18).
[^20]: Hebrew *semamit*, probably a lizard or gecko; traditionally "spider".
[^21]: Or "grips with its hands".
[^22]: Hebrew *zarzir motnayim*, literally "girded at the waist", an animal that cannot be identified with certainty: perhaps a rooster, a greyhound or a war horse.
[^23]: Hebrew *alqum*, a word of uncertain meaning: perhaps "his army", or "against whom no one rises up".
[^24]: In Hebrew *af*, "nose", and *appayim*, "anger" (literally "nostrils"), are the same word.
