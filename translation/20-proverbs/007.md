**1** My son, keep my words and store up my commands within you.

**2** Keep my commands and live; guard my instruction like the pupil of your eye.[^1]

**3** Tie them on your fingers; write them on the tablet of your heart.

**4** Say to wisdom, "You are my sister,"[^2] and call understanding[^3] your close relative,

**5** so that they may keep you from the strange woman,[^4] from the foreign woman[^5] with her smooth words.

**6** For at the window of my house I looked down through my lattice,[^6]

**7** and I saw among the simple,[^7] I noticed among the young men, a youth who lacked sense.[^8]

**8** He was passing along the street near her corner, stepping out on the road to her house,

**9** in the twilight, in the evening of the day, in the black of night[^9] and the darkness.

**10** And look, a woman came to meet him, dressed like a prostitute, with a guarded heart.[^10]

**11** She is loud and defiant; her feet do not stay at home.

**12** Now in the street, now in the squares, she lies in wait at every corner.

**13** She took hold of him and kissed him, and with a brazen face she said to him,

**14** "I had fellowship offerings to make;[^11] today I have fulfilled my vows.

**15** That is why I came out to meet you, to look for you, and I have found you.

**16** I have spread my couch with coverings, with colored linen[^12] from Egypt.

**17** I have sprinkled my bed with myrrh, aloes and cinnamon.

**18** Come, let us drink our fill of love until morning; let us delight ourselves with lovemaking.

**19** For my husband[^13] is not at home; he has gone on a long journey.

**20** He took a bag of money with him; he will not come home until the full moon."[^14]

**21** With all her persuasion[^15] she led him astray; with her smooth lips she seduced him.

**22** All at once he followed her, like an ox going to the slaughter, like a fool[^16] in fetters going to be punished,[^17]

**23** until an arrow pierces his liver, like a bird rushing into a trap, not knowing that it will cost him his life.

**24** So now, sons, listen to me, and pay attention to the words of my mouth.

**25** Do not let your heart turn aside to her ways; do not stray onto her paths.

**26** For many are the victims she has brought down, and all those she has killed are a mighty throng.

**27** Her house is the way to Sheol,[^18] going down to the chambers of death.

[^1]: Literally "the little man of your eye", the pupil, which is guarded with great care (Deuteronomy 32:10).
[^2]: *Sister* could also be a term of affection for a bride (Song of Songs 4:9–12).
[^3]: Hebrew *binah*, as in 1:2.
[^4]: Hebrew *ishah zarah*, a woman outside one's marriage (see 2:16). In this chapter she is a married woman (verse 19).
[^5]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16).
[^6]: The Septuagint makes the woman the one who looks out of the window of her house.
[^7]: Hebrew *peta'im*, plural of *peti*: naive, inexperienced people, easily led (see 1:4).
[^8]: Literally "lacking heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^9]: Literally "in the pupil of the night".
[^10]: Literally "guarded of heart", that is, hiding her intentions.
[^11]: Literally "Sacrifices of fellowship offerings were on me". The worshiper ate the meat of these offerings (Leviticus 7:15–16), so she has a feast at home.
[^12]: Hebrew *etun*, a word found only here, probably linen yarn.
[^13]: Literally "the man".
[^14]: Hebrew *kese*, probably the day of the full moon (Psalm 81:4; 81:3 in most English Bibles).
[^15]: Hebrew *leqach*, the word translated "learning" in 1:5, here her persuasive teaching.
[^16]: Hebrew *ewil* (see 1:7).
[^17]: The Hebrew of this line is obscure. Literally "and like an anklet to the discipline of a fool". Some scholars read "like a stag that leaps into a noose", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^18]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
