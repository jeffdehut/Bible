**1** A good name is to be chosen rather than great riches, and favor is better than silver and gold.

**2** The rich and the poor meet together; Yahweh[^1] is the maker of them all.

**3** The prudent[^2] sees danger and hides himself, but the simple[^3] go on and pay for it.

**4** The reward for humility and the fear of Yahweh is riches, honor and life.[^4]

**5** Thorns and snares are in the way of the crooked; whoever guards his life will keep far from them.

**6** Train a child in the way he should go;[^5] even when he is old he will not turn away from it.

**7** The rich rules over the poor, and the borrower is servant[^6] to the lender.

**8** Whoever sows injustice will reap trouble, and the rod of his fury will come to an end.[^7]

**9** Whoever has a generous eye will be blessed, for he gives some of his bread to the poor.

**10** Drive out the mocker,[^8] and strife goes out; quarreling and insults will stop.

**11** Whoever loves purity of heart[^9] and whose speech is gracious will have the king as his friend.

**12** The eyes of Yahweh watch over knowledge, but he overturns the words of the treacherous.

**13** The lazy person[^10] says, "There is a lion outside! I will be killed in the streets!"

**14** The mouth of strange women[^11] is a deep pit; whoever is cursed by Yahweh will fall into it.

**15** Folly[^12] is bound up in the heart of a child, but the rod of discipline[^13] will drive it far from him.

**16** Whoever oppresses the poor to increase his own wealth, or gives to the rich, comes only to need.[^14]

**17** Turn your ear and listen to the words of the wise,[^15] and set your heart on my knowledge,

**18** for it is pleasant if you keep them within you,[^16] if all of them are ready on your lips.

**19** So that your trust may be in Yahweh, I have taught you today, even you.

**20** Have I not written for you excellent sayings[^17] of advice and knowledge,

**21** to teach you what is right and true, so that you may bring back a true report to those who sent you?

**22** Do not rob the poor because he is poor, and do not crush the afflicted at the gate,[^18]

**23** for Yahweh will plead their case and will take the life of those who rob them.

**24** Do not make friends with a hot-tempered person, and do not go about with a man given to anger,

**25** or you will learn his ways[^19] and get yourself caught in a snare.

**26** Do not be one of those who shake hands on a pledge, who put up security for debts.

**27** If you have nothing with which to pay, why should your bed be taken from under you?

**28** Do not move the ancient boundary stone that your ancestors set up.

**29** Do you see a man skilled in his work? He will serve before kings; he will not serve before obscure people.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, prudent" (see 1:4). The verse is repeated in 27:12.
[^3]: Hebrew *peta'im*, plural of *peti*: naive, inexperienced people, easily led (see 1:4).
[^4]: Literally "The reward of humility, the fear of Yahweh, riches and honor and life". Or "The reward of humility, which is the fear of Yahweh, is riches …".
[^5]: Literally "according to his way": the way he should go, or the way that suits him.
[^6]: Or "slave".
[^7]: The Septuagint adds a line, "God blesses a man who is cheerful and generous", echoed in 2 Corinthians 9:7.
[^8]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22).
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "the pure of heart" but read "purity of heart".
[^10]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^11]: Hebrew *zarot*, plural of *zarah*, "strange woman", a woman outside one's marriage (see 2:16).
[^12]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^13]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2).
[^14]: The Hebrew is compact. Or "Oppressing the poor may increase a person's wealth, but giving to the rich leads only to need".
[^15]: A new collection begins here (22:17–24:22), "the words of the wise" (compare 24:23). Much of 22:17–23:11 closely resembles an Egyptian work, the Instruction of Amenemope, which is arranged in thirty chapters (compare verse 20).
[^16]: Literally "in your belly".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written *shilshom*, "formerly", but read *shalishim*, a word that usually means "officers" and is traditionally taken here as "excellent things". Many scholars read *sheloshim*, "thirty (sayings)", with a small change to the Hebrew (compare the note on verse 17).
[^18]: The town gate, where the court met.
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "his way" but read "his ways".
