**1** These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied.[^1]

**2** It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, and the glory of kings to search out a matter.

**3** As the heavens are high and the earth is deep, so the heart of kings is unsearchable.

**4** Remove the dross from silver, and a vessel comes out for the silversmith.

**5** Remove the wicked[^2] from the king's presence, and his throne will be established in righteousness.

**6** Do not put yourself forward before the king, and do not stand in the place of the great,

**7** for it is better to be told, "Come up here," than to be put lower in the presence of a noble whom your eyes have seen.[^3]

**8** Do not go out hastily to court, for what will you do in the end, when your neighbor puts you to shame?

**9** Argue your case with your neighbor, but do not reveal another's secret,

**10** or the one who hears it will disgrace you,[^4] and your bad name will never go away.

**11** Like apples of gold in settings of silver is a word spoken at the right time.[^5]

**12** Like a gold earring or an ornament of fine gold is a wise person who corrects a listening ear.

**13** Like the coolness of snow at harvest time is a faithful messenger to those who send him; he refreshes the life of his masters.

**14** Like clouds and wind without rain is a man who boasts of a gift he does not give.

**15** With patience a ruler can be persuaded, and a gentle tongue can break a bone.

**16** If you find honey, eat only what you need, or you will have too much and vomit it up.

**17** Seldom set foot in your neighbor's house, or he will have too much of you and hate you.

**18** Like a club, a sword or a sharp arrow is a man who gives false testimony against his neighbor.

**19** Like a broken tooth or a lame foot is trust in a treacherous person in a time of trouble.

**20** Like one who takes off a garment on a cold day, like vinegar on soda,[^6] is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.

**21** If your enemy[^7] is hungry, give him bread to eat; if he is thirsty, give him water to drink,

**22** for you will heap burning coals on his head, and the LORD[^8] will reward you.[^9]

**23** The north wind brings forth rain, and a secret tongue, angry faces.

**24** Better to live in a corner of the roof than in a house shared with a quarrelsome wife.[^10]

**25** Like cold water to a weary soul is good news from a distant land.

**26** Like a muddied spring or a polluted fountain is a righteous person[^11] who gives way before the wicked.

**27** It is not good to eat much honey, nor is it glorious to seek one's own glory.[^12]

**28** Like a city broken into and left without walls is a person who has no control over his spirit.

[^1]: A new collection begins here (chapters 25–29). Hezekiah reigned more than two centuries after Solomon (2 Kings 18–20).
[^2]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^3]: Or "What your eyes have seen, (8) do not bring hastily to court". Compare Luke 14:8–10.
[^4]: Hebrew *yechassedkha*, from the verb related to *chesed*, "disgrace" (14:34).
[^5]: Literally "on its wheels", probably "at the right time" or "in the right way".
[^6]: Hebrew *neter*, natron or soda, which fizzes when vinegar is poured on it.
[^7]: Literally "the one who hates you".
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: Romans 12:20 quotes verses 21–22.
[^10]: The verse repeats 21:9.
[^11]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^12]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "and searching out their glory is glory". The translation carries over the "not" of the first line.
