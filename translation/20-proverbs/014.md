**1** The wise woman builds her house,[^1] but folly[^2] tears it down with her own hands.

**2** Whoever walks uprightly fears the LORD,[^3] but whoever is devious in his ways despises him.

**3** In the mouth of a fool[^4] is a rod for his pride,[^5] but the lips of the wise protect them.

**4** Where there are no oxen, the manger is clean,[^6] but much produce comes by the strength of an ox.

**5** A faithful witness does not lie, but a false witness breathes out lies.

**6** A mocker[^7] seeks wisdom and finds none, but knowledge comes easily to the discerning.

**7** Leave the presence of a fool,[^8] for you will not find words of knowledge on his lips.

**8** The wisdom of the prudent[^9] is to understand his way, but the folly of fools is deceit.

**9** Fools mock at making amends,[^10] but among the upright there is goodwill.

**10** The heart knows its own bitterness, and no stranger shares in its joy.

**11** The house of the wicked[^11] will be destroyed, but the tent of the upright will flourish.

**12** There is a way that seems right to a person, but in the end it leads to death.[^12]

**13** Even in laughter the heart may ache, and joy may end in grief.

**14** The faithless in heart will have his fill of his own ways, and a good person of what is his.[^13]

**15** The simple[^14] believe everything, but the prudent watch their steps.

**16** The wise is cautious[^15] and turns away from evil, but a fool is reckless and overconfident.

**17** A quick-tempered person does foolish things, and a man of schemes[^16] is hated.

**18** The simple inherit folly, but the prudent are crowned with knowledge.

**19** The evil bow down before the good, and the wicked at the gates of the righteous.[^17]

**20** The poor are disliked even by their neighbors, but the rich have many friends.

**21** Whoever despises his neighbor sins, but happy[^18] is the one who is kind to the humble.[^19]

**22** Do not those who plot evil go astray? But faithful love and truth[^20] belong to those who plan good.

**23** In all hard work there is profit, but mere talk leads only to poverty.

**24** The crown of the wise is their wealth, but the folly of fools is folly.

**25** A truthful witness saves lives, but one who breathes out lies is a deceiver.

**26** In the fear of the LORD there is strong confidence, and his children will have a refuge.

**27** The fear of the LORD is a fountain of life, for turning away from the snares of death.

**28** In a multitude of people is a king's glory, but without people a ruler is ruined.

**29** Whoever is slow to anger has great insight,[^21] but whoever is quick-tempered displays folly.

**30** A calm heart gives life to the body,[^22] but envy rots the bones.

**31** Whoever oppresses the poor insults his Maker, but whoever is kind to the needy honors him.

**32** The wicked is thrown down by his own evil, but the righteous finds refuge even in his death.[^23]

**33** Wisdom rests in the heart of the discerning, and even among fools she makes herself known.[^24]

**34** Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a disgrace[^25] to any people.

**35** A king delights in a servant who acts with good sense,[^26] but his wrath falls on one who acts shamefully.

[^1]: Literally "The wise ones of women has built her house". With different vowels, "Wisdom (*chokhmot*, as in 9:1) has built her house", and the second line would then speak of Folly (compare 9:13).
[^2]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7). Also in verses 8, 17, 18, 24 and 29.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7). Also in verse 9.
[^5]: Literally "a rod of pride". The word for *rod* can also mean "shoot, branch" (Isaiah 11:1): his proud words may bring a beating on him, or his mouth sprouts pride.
[^6]: Or "the manger is empty of grain".
[^7]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22).
[^8]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 8, 16, 24 and 33.
[^9]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, prudent" (see 1:4). Also in verses 15 and 18.
[^10]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Fools, he mocks guilt". Hebrew *asham* means "guilt", "making amends" or "guilt offering". Or "Guilt mocks fools".
[^11]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: those who are guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^12]: Literally "but its end is ways of death". The verse is repeated in 16:25.
[^13]: Literally "and from upon him a good man". Some scholars read "from his deeds", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^14]: Hebrew *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led (see 1:4). Also in verse 18.
[^15]: Literally "fears". Or "fears (the LORD)".
[^16]: Hebrew *mezimmot*, as in 12:2.
[^17]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^18]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "the poor" but read "the humble".
[^20]: Hebrew *chesed ve-emet*: faithful love and reliability (see 3:3). Literally "faithful love and truth, those who plan good".
[^21]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^22]: Literally "A heart of healing (or calm) is the life of the flesh".
[^23]: The Septuagint reads "but whoever trusts in his own holiness is righteous", probably reading *betummo*, "in his integrity", in place of *bemoto*, "in his death", a word with the same letters in a different order.
[^24]: The Septuagint reads "but in the heart of fools it is not known".
[^25]: Hebrew *chesed*, here a rarer word meaning "shame, disgrace" (Leviticus 20:17), spelled like the word for "faithful love".
[^26]: Hebrew *maskil*, as in 10:5.
