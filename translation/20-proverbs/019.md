**1** Better a poor person who walks in integrity than one with crooked lips who is a fool.[^1]

**2** Desire without knowledge is not good, and whoever hurries with his feet sins.[^2]

**3** A person's folly[^3] ruins his way, but his heart rages against the LORD.[^4]

**4** Wealth adds many friends, but the poor is separated from his friend.

**5** A false witness will not go unpunished, and one who breathes out lies will not escape.

**6** Many seek the favor of a generous person, and everyone is a friend to one who gives gifts.

**7** All the brothers of a poor person hate him; how much more do his friends keep far from him! He pursues them with words, but words are all he has.[^5]

**8** Whoever gains sense[^6] loves himself; whoever keeps insight[^7] will find good.

**9** A false witness will not go unpunished, and one who breathes out lies will perish.

**10** Luxury is not fitting for a fool, still less for a servant to rule over princes.

**11** A person's good sense[^8] makes him slow to anger, and it is his glory to overlook an offense.

**12** A king's rage is like the roar of a lion, but his favor is like dew on the grass.

**13** A foolish son is ruin to his father, and a wife's quarrels are a constant dripping.[^9]

**14** House and wealth are an inheritance from fathers, but a sensible wife[^10] is from the LORD.

**15** Laziness casts into a deep sleep, and an idle person will go hungry.

**16** Whoever keeps the command keeps his life; whoever is careless of his ways will die.[^11]

**17** Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the LORD, and he will repay him for his deed.

**18** Discipline your son while there is hope; do not set your heart on his death.[^12]

**19** A person of great anger[^13] must pay the penalty; if you rescue him, you will have to do it again.

**20** Listen to advice and accept discipline,[^14] so that you may be wise in the end.

**21** Many plans are in a person's heart, but it is the purpose of the LORD that will stand.

**22** What is desired in a person is faithful love,[^15] and it is better to be poor than a liar.

**23** The fear of the LORD leads to life; whoever has it rests content, untouched by harm.

**24** The lazy person[^16] buries his hand in the dish; he will not even bring it back to his mouth.

**25** Strike a mocker,[^17] and the simple[^18] will learn prudence;[^19] correct a discerning person, and he will gain knowledge.

**26** Whoever robs his father and drives out his mother is a son who brings shame and disgrace.

**27** Stop listening to discipline, my son, and you will stray from the words of knowledge.

**28** A worthless witness[^20] mocks at justice, and the mouth of the wicked[^21] swallows iniquity.

**29** Punishments are ready for mockers, and beatings for the backs of fools.

[^1]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 10, 13 and 29. Compare 28:6.
[^2]: Or "misses the way". The verb *chata* means both "sin" and "miss" (8:36).
[^3]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. The text is written *lo*, "not", but read *lo*, "to him": literally "pursuing words, they are his". On the written form the line means "he pursues them with words, but they are gone". The verse has three lines instead of two, and the Septuagint has a different and longer text.
[^6]: Literally "gains heart".
[^7]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^8]: Hebrew *sekhel*, "good sense, insight".
[^9]: That is, the dripping of a leaking roof (27:15).
[^10]: Hebrew *ishah maskalet*, a wife who acts with good sense (compare *maskil*, 10:5).
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "will be put to death" but read "will die". *Is careless of his ways* is literally "despises his ways".
[^12]: Literally "and to his death do not lift up your soul". This may mean "do not be a party to his ruin" by neglecting discipline, or "do not let your anger go so far as to kill him".
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written with a word of unclear meaning, perhaps "rough", but read "great".
[^14]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verse 27.
[^15]: Hebrew *chesed*. Or, taking *chesed* as in 14:34, "A person's greed is his disgrace".
[^16]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6). The verse is repeated in 26:15.
[^17]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22). Also in verse 29.
[^18]: Hebrew *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led (see 1:4).
[^19]: Hebrew *ya'rim*, from the same root as *ormah*, "prudence" (see 1:4).
[^20]: Hebrew *ed beliyya'al*, "a witness of worthlessness" (see 6:12).
[^21]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: those who are guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
