**1** Whoever loves discipline[^1] loves knowledge, but whoever hates correction is stupid.[^2]

**2** A good person obtains favor from Yahweh,[^3] but he condemns a man who schemes.[^4]

**3** No one is made secure by wickedness, but the root of the righteous[^5] will never be moved.

**4** A capable wife[^6] is her husband's crown, but a wife who brings shame is like rot in his bones.

**5** The plans of the righteous are just; the guidance[^7] of the wicked[^8] is deceit.

**6** The words of the wicked are an ambush for blood, but the mouth of the upright rescues them.

**7** The wicked are overthrown and are no more, but the house of the righteous will stand.

**8** A man is praised according to his good sense,[^9] but one with a twisted heart will be despised.

**9** Better to be lowly and have a servant[^10] than to act important and have no bread.

**10** The righteous person cares about the life of his animal,[^11] but even the compassion of the wicked is cruel.

**11** Whoever works his land will have plenty of bread, but whoever chases worthless things lacks sense.[^12]

**12** The wicked covet the catch of evildoers, but the root of the righteous yields fruit.[^13]

**13** By the sin of his lips an evil person is trapped,[^14] but the righteous escapes from trouble.

**14** From the fruit of his mouth a person is filled with good, and the deeds of a person's hands are repaid to him.[^15]

**15** The way of a fool[^16] is right in his own eyes, but whoever listens to advice is wise.

**16** A fool shows his anger at once, but the prudent[^17] overlooks an insult.

**17** Whoever speaks the truth[^18] tells what is right, but a false witness tells deceit.

**18** Some people's rash words are like sword thrusts, but the tongue of the wise brings healing.

**19** Truthful lips will endure forever, but a lying tongue lasts only a moment.

**20** Deceit is in the heart of those who plot evil, but those who counsel peace have joy.

**21** No harm will happen to the righteous, but the wicked are filled with trouble.

**22** Lying lips are detestable to Yahweh, but those who act faithfully are his delight.

**23** A prudent person conceals knowledge, but the heart of fools[^19] proclaims folly.[^20]

**24** The hand of the diligent will rule, but the slack hand will be put to forced labor.

**25** Anxiety in a person's heart weighs it down, but a good word makes it glad.

**26** The righteous person guides his neighbor,[^21] but the way of the wicked leads them astray.

**27** The lazy man does not roast his game, but diligence is a person's precious wealth.[^22]

**28** In the path of righteousness is life, and in its way there is no death.[^23]

[^1]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2).
[^2]: Hebrew *ba'ar*, "brutish", like an animal without understanding (also 30:2).
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "a man of schemes". Hebrew *mezimmot*, the word translated "discretion" in 1:4, here in a bad sense.
[^5]: Hebrew *tsaddiqim*, plural of *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^6]: Hebrew *eshet chayil*, "a woman of strength, of worth", as in 31:10 and Ruth 3:11.
[^7]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, as in 1:5.
[^8]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^9]: Hebrew *sekhel*, "good sense, insight".
[^10]: Or, with different vowels, "and work for oneself".
[^11]: Literally "knows the soul (*nephesh*) of his animal", that is, cares about its needs.
[^12]: Literally "lacks heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^13]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "The wicked desires the net (or prey) of evil men, and the root of the righteous gives".
[^14]: Or "In the sin of the lips is an evil trap".
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "return to him" but read "repay him".
[^16]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7). Also in verse 16.
[^17]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, prudent" (see 1:4). Also in verse 23.
[^18]: Literally "Whoever breathes out faithfulness" (compare 6:19).
[^19]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil* (see 1:22).
[^20]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^21]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Or "The righteous person is better off than his neighbor", or "The righteous person searches out a way for his friend".
[^22]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "Slackness does not roast its game, and the wealth of a person is precious, diligence". *Roast* may instead mean "catch". Or "but a diligent person gains precious wealth".
[^23]: Literally "and the way of its path, no death". With different vowels, *al*, "no", becomes *el*, "to": "but another path leads to death". The Septuagint has "to death".
