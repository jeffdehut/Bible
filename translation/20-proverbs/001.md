**1** The proverbs[^1] of Solomon son of David, king of Israel,

**2** for learning wisdom[^2] and discipline,[^3] for grasping words of understanding,[^4]

**3** for receiving discipline in wise conduct, in righteousness, justice and fairness,

**4** for giving prudence[^5] to the simple,[^6] knowledge[^7] and discretion[^8] to the young.

**5** Let the wise listen and add to their learning; let the discerning gain guidance,[^9]

**6** for understanding a proverb and a figure of speech,[^10] the words of the wise and their riddles.

**7** The fear of Yahweh[^11] is the beginning[^12] of knowledge; fools[^13] despise wisdom and discipline.

**8** Listen, my son,[^14] to your father's discipline, and do not abandon your mother's instruction.[^15]

**9** For they are a graceful garland for your head and a necklace for your neck.

**10** My son, if sinners entice you, do not give in.

**11** If they say, "Come with us; let us lie in wait to shed blood;[^16] let us ambush the innocent for no reason;

**12** let us swallow them alive like Sheol,[^17] whole, like those who go down to the Pit;[^18]

**13** we will find all kinds of precious wealth; we will fill our houses with plunder.

**14** Throw in your lot with us; we will all have one purse,"

**15** my son, do not walk on the road with them; keep your foot from their path.

**16** For their feet run to evil, and they hurry to shed blood.[^19]

**17** For it is useless to spread a net in the sight of any bird.[^20]

**18** But these men lie in wait for their own blood; they ambush their own lives.

**19** Such are the ways of everyone who is greedy for gain: it takes away the life of those who possess it.

**20** Wisdom[^21] cries aloud in the street; in the squares she raises her voice.

**21** At the head of the bustling streets she calls out; at the entrances of the city gates she speaks her words:

**22** "How long, you simple ones, will you love being simple? How long will mockers[^22] delight in their mocking, and fools[^23] hate knowledge?

**23** Turn back at my correction![^24] Look, I will pour out my spirit[^25] to you; I will make my words known to you.

**24** Because I called and you refused, because I stretched out my hand and no one paid attention,

**25** because you ignored all my advice and would have none of my correction,

**26** I in turn will laugh at your disaster; I will mock when what you dread comes,

**27** when what you dread comes like a storm, and your disaster sweeps in like a whirlwind, when distress and anguish come upon you.

**28** Then they will call to me, but I will not answer; they will search hard for me, but they will not find me.

**29** Because they hated knowledge and did not choose the fear of Yahweh,

**30** because they would have none of my advice and spurned all my correction,

**31** they will eat the fruit of their own way and be stuffed full of their own schemes.

**32** For the waywardness of the simple will kill them, and the complacency of fools will destroy them.

**33** But whoever listens to me will live in safety and be at ease, with no dread of harm."

[^1]: Hebrew *mishle*, the plural of *mashal*: a proverb or short saying, often one that makes a comparison. *Mishle* is the book's name in Hebrew. Verses 1–7 introduce the whole book.
[^2]: Hebrew *chokhmah*: skill in living, wisdom that is both practical and moral. It is the key word of the book.
[^3]: Hebrew *musar*: training that forms character, through teaching, correction and, where needed, punishment. Traditionally "instruction". This translation uses "discipline" throughout. Also in verses 3, 7 and 8.
[^4]: Hebrew *binah*: the ability to discern, to see what matters and tell things apart. *Grasping* translates a verb from the same root.
[^5]: Hebrew *ormah*: shrewdness, good judgment. The related word *arum* describes the "crafty" snake in Genesis 3:1, but in Proverbs these words name a good quality.
[^6]: Hebrew *peta'im*, plural of *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led either way. Also in verses 22 and 32.
[^7]: Hebrew *da'at*: knowledge gained by learning and experience, especially knowledge of God and of how to live. Also in verses 7, 22 and 29.
[^8]: Hebrew *mezimmah*: the ability to think things through and make plans. The same word can mean a scheme in a bad sense (12:2; 14:17).
[^9]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, perhaps connected with steering a ship by its ropes: the skill of finding the right course.
[^10]: Hebrew *melitsah*, a rare word: perhaps an allusive saying or a figure whose meaning must be worked out (compare Habakkuk 2:6).
[^11]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The fear of Yahweh* is reverence for him that shows itself in obedience. It is a theme of the whole book (9:10; 15:33; 31:30).
[^12]: Or "the first principle", "the chief part". Hebrew *reshit*.
[^13]: Hebrew *ewilim*, plural of *ewil*. Proverbs uses several words for "fool". The *ewil* is morally perverse, stubborn and quick to quarrel. The *kesil* (verse 22), the most common word, is dull and self-satisfied, closed to learning. Both are translated "fool", and a note gives the Hebrew word at its first occurrence in each chapter.
[^14]: The teacher addresses his pupil as "my son", as a father would his son. The address recurs throughout chapters 1–7 and often later.
[^15]: Hebrew *torah*, "teaching, instruction".
[^16]: Literally "for blood".
[^17]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^18]: Hebrew *bor*, "pit, cistern", here the grave or the realm of the dead.
[^19]: Isaiah 59:7 has nearly the same words; Romans 3:15 quotes them.
[^20]: Literally "in the eyes of every owner of a wing". The point of the comparison is uncertain. Perhaps a bird that sees a trap is not caught by it, yet these men do not see the trap they are setting for themselves (verse 18). Or the word translated *useless* (Hebrew *chinnam*) means "for no reason", as in verse 11.
[^21]: Here wisdom is pictured as a woman who calls out in public, as also in 8:1–36 and 9:1–6. The Hebrew form, *chokhmot*, is unusual; it may be a plural that stresses her greatness (also 9:1).
[^22]: Hebrew *letsim*, plural of *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction. Proverbs treats the mocker as beyond teaching (9:7–8; 13:1).
[^23]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil*, the most common word for "fool" in Proverbs: someone dull and self-satisfied who does not want to learn. Also in verse 32. Compare the *ewil* in verse 7.
[^24]: Or "If you turn back at my correction, I will pour out …". Hebrew *tokhachat*, "correction, rebuke". Also in verses 25 and 30.
[^25]: Or "my thoughts". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, breath, mind". Literally "I will make my spirit gush out to you".
