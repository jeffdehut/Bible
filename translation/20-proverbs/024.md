**1** Do not envy evil people, and do not desire to be with them,

**2** for their hearts plot violence, and their lips talk of trouble.

**3** By wisdom a house is built, and by insight[^1] it is established;

**4** by knowledge its rooms are filled with all kinds of precious and pleasant wealth.

**5** A wise man is strong,[^2] and a person of knowledge increases his strength,

**6** for by guidance[^3] you wage your war, and with many advisers there is victory.

**7** Wisdom[^4] is too high for a fool;[^5] in the gate he does not open his mouth.

**8** Whoever plans to do evil will be called a schemer.[^6]

**9** The scheming of folly[^7] is sin, and a mocker[^8] is detestable to people.

**10** If you lose heart in the day of trouble, your strength is small.[^9]

**11** Rescue those who are being taken away to death; hold back those who are staggering to the slaughter.

**12** If you say, "But we did not know this," does not the one who weighs hearts understand? Does not the one who guards your life know it? He will repay each person according to what he has done.[^10]

**13** Eat honey, my son, for it is good, and the honeycomb is sweet to your taste.

**14** Know that wisdom is like that for your soul; if you find it, there will be a future, and your hope will not be cut off.

**15** Do not lie in wait like a wicked man[^11] against the home of the righteous;[^12] do not destroy his resting place.

**16** For the righteous falls seven times and rises again, but the wicked stumble in times of disaster.

**17** Do not rejoice when your enemy[^13] falls, and do not let your heart be glad when he stumbles,

**18** or the LORD[^14] will see it and be displeased, and turn his anger away from him.

**19** Do not fret because of evildoers or envy the wicked,

**20** for the evil person has no future; the lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out.

**21** My son, fear the LORD and the king; do not associate with those who are given to change,[^15]

**22** for disaster from them will rise suddenly, and who knows the ruin the two of them can bring?[^16]

**23** These also are sayings of the wise.[^17] Showing partiality[^18] in judgment is not good.

**24** Whoever says to the wicked, "You are righteous," peoples will curse him, nations will denounce him.

**25** But those who rebuke the wicked will find delight, and a good blessing will come upon them.

**26** Whoever gives an honest answer kisses the lips.[^19]

**27** Put your outdoor work in order and get your fields ready; after that, build your house.[^20]

**28** Do not be a witness against your neighbor without cause; would you deceive with your lips?

**29** Do not say, "I will do to him as he has done to me; I will pay the man back for what he has done."

**30** I passed by the field of a lazy man,[^21] by the vineyard of a man who lacked sense,[^22]

**31** and look, it was all overgrown with thistles; its surface was covered with weeds, and its stone wall was broken down.

**32** I looked and took it to heart; I saw and received discipline:[^23]

**33** a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest,

**34** and poverty will come on you like a prowler, and need like an armed man.[^24]

[^1]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^2]: Literally "is in strength". The Septuagint reads "A wise man is better than a strong one".
[^3]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, as in 1:5.
[^4]: Hebrew *chokhmot*, as in 1:20.
[^5]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^6]: Literally "a master of schemes". Hebrew *mezimmot*, as in 12:2.
[^7]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^8]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22).
[^9]: In Hebrew there is a play on *tsarah*, "trouble", and *tsar*, "small, narrow".
[^10]: Compare Psalm 62:13 (62:12 in most English Bibles); Romans 2:6.
[^11]: Or "Do not lie in wait, you wicked man". Hebrew *rasha* (see 2:22).
[^12]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "your enemies" but read "your enemy".
[^14]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^15]: Hebrew *shonim*, "those who change", perhaps rebels. The Septuagint reads "and do not disobey either of them".
[^16]: *The two of them* are probably the LORD and the king (verse 21).
[^17]: A short collection follows (verses 23–34). From here to the end of the book the Septuagint arranges the material differently. After 24:22 it has 30:1–14, then 24:23–34, then 30:15–33 and 31:1–9, then chapters 25–29, and last 31:10–31.
[^18]: Literally "Recognizing faces".
[^19]: A kiss on the lips may have been a sign of friendship and loyalty.
[^20]: Perhaps "establish your family".
[^21]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^22]: Literally "lacking heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^23]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2).
[^24]: Verses 33–34 repeat 6:10–11.
