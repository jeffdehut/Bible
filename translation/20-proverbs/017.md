**1** Better a dry crust with peace and quiet than a house full of feasting[^1] with strife.

**2** A servant who acts wisely[^2] will rule over a son who acts shamefully, and will share the inheritance among the brothers.

**3** The crucible is for silver and the furnace for gold, but the LORD[^3] tests hearts.

**4** An evildoer listens to wicked lips; a liar pays attention to a destructive tongue.

**5** Whoever mocks the poor insults his Maker; whoever rejoices at disaster will not go unpunished.

**6** Grandchildren are the crown of the old, and fathers are the glory of their children.

**7** Fine speech is not fitting for a godless fool,[^4] still less are lying lips for a noble.

**8** A bribe is a lucky stone[^5] in the eyes of its owner; wherever he turns, he succeeds.

**9** Whoever covers an offense seeks love, but whoever repeats a matter separates close friends.

**10** A rebuke goes deeper into a discerning person than a hundred blows into a fool.[^6]

**11** An evil person seeks only rebellion, and a cruel messenger will be sent against him.

**12** Better to meet a bear robbed of her cubs than a fool in his folly.[^7]

**13** Whoever repays good with evil, evil will not leave his house.

**14** Starting a quarrel is like letting out water; so drop the dispute before it breaks out.

**15** Whoever acquits the wicked[^8] and whoever condemns the righteous,[^9] both are detestable to the LORD.

**16** Why should a fool have money in his hand to buy wisdom, when he has no sense?[^10]

**17** A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for times of trouble.

**18** One who lacks sense[^11] shakes hands on a pledge and puts up security in front of his neighbor.[^12]

**19** Whoever loves offense loves strife; whoever makes his doorway high is looking for destruction.[^13]

**20** Whoever has a crooked heart finds no good, and whoever has a perverse tongue falls into trouble.

**21** Whoever fathers a fool has grief, and the father of a godless fool has no joy.

**22** A glad heart is good medicine,[^14] but a crushed spirit dries up the bones.

**23** The wicked accepts a bribe in secret[^15] to pervert the paths of justice.

**24** Wisdom is right in front of the discerning, but the eyes of a fool wander to the ends of the earth.

**25** A foolish son is a grief to his father and bitterness to the mother who bore him.

**26** It is not good to punish the righteous, or to strike nobles for their uprightness.[^16]

**27** Whoever restrains his words has knowledge, and one who is calm in spirit[^17] is a person of insight.[^18]

**28** Even a fool[^19] who keeps silent is considered wise; whoever closes his lips is thought discerning.

[^1]: Literally "sacrifices". The meat of some sacrifices was eaten at a feast (7:14).
[^2]: Hebrew *maskil*, as in 10:5.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *nabal*: a senseless, godless person, the word used of Nabal in 1 Samuel 25:25. Also in verse 21.
[^5]: Literally "a stone of favor", a charm thought to bring good fortune.
[^6]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 12, 16, 21, 24 and 25.
[^7]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^8]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^9]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^10]: Literally "and there is no heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^11]: Literally "lacks heart".
[^12]: That is, guarantees his neighbor's debt (see 6:1–5). Literally "before his neighbor".
[^13]: *Makes his doorway high* may be a picture of arrogance or of a showy house; some take *doorway* to mean the mouth.
[^14]: Hebrew *gehah*, a word found only here, probably "healing".
[^15]: Literally "from the bosom", from the fold of a garment.
[^16]: Or "against what is right".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "and cool of spirit" but read "precious (that is, restrained) of spirit".
[^18]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^19]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
