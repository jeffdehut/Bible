**1** The king's heart is like streams of water in the hand of Yahweh;[^1] he turns it wherever he wishes.

**2** All a person's ways seem right in his own eyes, but Yahweh weighs hearts.

**3** Doing righteousness and justice is more acceptable to Yahweh than sacrifice.

**4** Haughty eyes and a proud heart, the lamp[^2] of the wicked,[^3] are sin.

**5** The plans of the diligent lead only to profit, but everyone who is hasty comes only to need.

**6** Getting treasures by a lying tongue is a vapor[^4] blown away, for those who seek death.[^5]

**7** The violence of the wicked will sweep them away, because they refuse to do what is just.

**8** The way of a guilty person[^6] is crooked, but the conduct of the pure is upright.

**9** Better to live in a corner of the roof than in a house shared with a quarrelsome wife.

**10** The soul of the wicked craves evil; his neighbor finds no mercy in his eyes.

**11** When a mocker[^7] is punished, the simple[^8] becomes wise; when a wise person is instructed, he gains knowledge.

**12** The Righteous One[^9] observes the house of the wicked; he throws the wicked down to ruin.

**13** Whoever shuts his ears to the cry of the poor will himself call out and not be answered.

**14** A gift given in secret calms anger, and a bribe in the fold of the cloak calms fierce wrath.

**15** When justice is done, it is a joy to the righteous but terror to evildoers.

**16** Whoever wanders from the way of good sense will rest in the assembly of the dead.[^10]

**17** Whoever loves pleasure will be poor; whoever loves wine and oil will not grow rich.

**18** The wicked is a ransom for the righteous, and the treacherous takes the place of the upright.

**19** Better to live in a desert land than with a quarrelsome and angry wife.

**20** Precious treasure and oil are in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish person[^11] swallows it up.

**21** Whoever pursues righteousness and faithful love will find life, righteousness[^12] and honor.

**22** A wise person climbs the city of the mighty and brings down the stronghold they trusted in.

**23** Whoever guards his mouth and his tongue guards his life from troubles.

**24** The proud and arrogant person, "Mocker" is his name; he acts with insolent fury.

**25** The craving of the lazy person[^13] kills him, for his hands refuse to work.

**26** All day long he craves and craves,[^14] but the righteous gives and does not hold back.

**27** The sacrifice of the wicked is detestable; how much more when he brings it with evil intent!

**28** A false witness will perish, but one who listens will speak forever.[^15]

**29** A wicked man puts on a bold face, but the upright gives thought to his way.[^16]

**30** There is no wisdom, no insight,[^17] no counsel that can stand against Yahweh.

**31** The horse is made ready for the day of battle, but victory belongs to Yahweh.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: The Hebrew vowel points read *nir*, "newly plowed ground" (as in 13:23): "the plowing of the wicked". The same consonants can be read *ner*, "lamp" (13:9; 20:27).
[^3]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: those who are guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^4]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", the word used throughout Ecclesiastes.
[^5]: The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "a snare of death".
[^6]: Hebrew *vazar*, a word found only here, probably "guilty". Or "The way of a man is crooked and strange".
[^7]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22). Also in verse 24.
[^8]: Hebrew *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led (see 1:4).
[^9]: Probably God (compare Job 34:17). Or "A righteous person observes the house of the wicked; the wicked are thrown down to ruin". Hebrew *tsaddiq* (see 2:20).
[^10]: Hebrew *rephaim*, the spirits of the dead (see 2:18).
[^11]: Literally "a fool (*kesil*) of a man" (see 1:22).
[^12]: Or "prosperity", as in 8:18.
[^13]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^14]: Literally "he craves a craving", probably the lazy person of verse 25.
[^15]: Or "will speak with lasting effect". Hebrew *lanetsach*, "forever, to the end".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "makes his ways firm" but read "gives thought to his way".
[^17]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
