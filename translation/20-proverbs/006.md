**1** My son, if you have put up security for your neighbor,[^1] if you have shaken hands on a pledge for a stranger,[^2]

**2** if you have been trapped by the words of your mouth, caught by the words of your mouth,

**3** then do this, my son, and free yourself, since you have fallen into your neighbor's power: go, humble yourself, and press your neighbor hard.

**4** Give no sleep to your eyes and no slumber to your eyelids.

**5** Free yourself like a gazelle from the hunter's hand,[^3] like a bird from the hand of the fowler.

**6** Go to the ant, you lazy person;[^4] watch her ways and be wise.

**7** She has no commander, no overseer or ruler,

**8** yet she prepares her food in summer and gathers her provisions at harvest.[^5]

**9** How long will you lie there, you lazy person? When will you get up from your sleep?

**10** A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest,

**11** and poverty will come on you like a prowler,[^6] and need like an armed man.[^7]

**12** A worthless person,[^8] a wicked man, goes about with a crooked mouth,

**13** winking with his eyes, signaling with his feet, pointing with his fingers,

**14** with perversity in his heart, always plotting evil, stirring up quarrels.

**15** So disaster will come on him suddenly; in an instant he will be broken, and there will be no healing.

**16** There are six things that Yahweh[^9] hates, seven that are detestable to him:[^10]

**17** haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood,

**18** a heart that devises wicked plans, feet that hurry to run to evil,

**19** a false witness who breathes out lies,[^11] and one who stirs up quarrels among brothers.

**20** My son, keep your father's command, and do not abandon your mother's instruction.

**21** Bind them on your heart always; tie them around your neck.

**22** When you walk, they[^12] will guide you; when you lie down, they will watch over you; and when you wake up, they will talk with you.

**23** For the command is a lamp, and the instruction is a light, and the corrections of discipline[^13] are the way of life,

**24** to keep you from the evil woman,[^14] from the smooth tongue of the foreign woman.[^15]

**25** Do not desire her beauty in your heart, and do not let her captivate you with her eyelashes.

**26** For a prostitute costs only a loaf of bread,[^16] but another man's wife hunts down a precious life.

**27** Can a man scoop fire into his lap without his clothes being burned?

**28** Can a man walk on hot coals without his feet being scorched?

**29** So it is with the one who goes in to his neighbor's wife: no one who touches her will go unpunished.

**30** People do not despise a thief if he steals to satisfy his hunger when he is starving.[^17]

**31** Yet if he is caught, he must pay back sevenfold; he must give up all the wealth of his house.

**32** The man who commits adultery with a woman lacks sense;[^18] whoever does it destroys himself.

**33** He will get blows and disgrace, and his shame will never be wiped away.

**34** For jealousy arouses a husband's fury, and he will show no mercy on the day of revenge.

**35** He will not accept any compensation; he will refuse it, however great the bribe.[^19]

[^1]: That is, guaranteed someone else's debt. Proverbs often warns against this (11:15; 17:18; 20:16; 22:26–27; 27:13).
[^2]: Literally "struck your palms for a stranger", a gesture that sealed an agreement. Hebrew *zar*, someone outside one's family or circle (compare 2:16).
[^3]: Literally "from the hand".
[^4]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard". Also in verse 9.
[^5]: The Septuagint adds three lines about the bee, a hard worker whose produce is used by kings and ordinary people for their health.
[^6]: Hebrew *mehallekh*, "one who walks about", perhaps a prowler or a vagrant. Verses 10–11 are repeated in 24:33–34.
[^7]: Literally "a man of a shield".
[^8]: Hebrew *adam beliyya'al*, "a person of worthlessness", a phrase for someone who is wicked and destructive.
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^10]: Literally "an abomination (*to'evah*) of his soul" (see 3:32). The pattern "six … seven" builds up to the last item in the list (compare 30:15–31; Job 5:19).
[^11]: Hebrew *yafiach*, perhaps "who breathes out", or a noun meaning "witness": "a witness of lies, a false witness".
[^12]: Literally "she", probably the instruction of verse 20.
[^13]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2).
[^14]: With different vowels, the same consonants read "from your neighbor's wife". The Septuagint reads "from a married woman".
[^15]: Hebrew *nokhriyyah*, "foreign woman" (see 2:16).
[^16]: Or "For on account of a prostitute a man is brought down to a loaf of bread". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^17]: Or "Do people not despise a thief, even if he steals to satisfy his hunger?"
[^18]: Literally "lacks heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^19]: Or "however many gifts you offer".
