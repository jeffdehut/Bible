**1** Like snow in summer and like rain at harvest, so honor is not fitting for a fool.[^1]

**2** Like a sparrow flitting, like a swallow flying, so an undeserved curse comes home to the one who utters it.[^2]

**3** A whip for the horse, a bridle for the donkey, and a rod for the backs of fools.

**4** Do not answer a fool according to his folly,[^3] or you will be like him yourself.

**5** Answer a fool according to his folly, or he will be wise in his own eyes.[^4]

**6** Whoever sends a message by the hand of a fool cuts off his own feet and drinks violence.

**7** Like the legs of a lame person, which hang useless, is a proverb in the mouth of fools.

**8** Like tying a stone in a sling[^5] is giving honor to a fool.

**9** Like a thorn that gets into the hand of a drunkard is a proverb in the mouth of fools.

**10** Like an archer who wounds everyone is one who hires a fool or hires passersby.[^6]

**11** Like a dog that returns to its vomit is a fool who repeats his folly.[^7]

**12** Do you see a person wise in his own eyes? There is more hope for a fool than for him.

**13** The lazy person[^8] says, "There is a lion in the road! A lion in the streets!"[^9]

**14** As a door turns on its hinges, so the lazy person turns on his bed.

**15** The lazy person buries his hand in the dish; he is too tired to bring it back to his mouth.[^10]

**16** The lazy person is wiser in his own eyes than seven people who answer sensibly.

**17** Like one who grabs a dog by the ears is a passerby who meddles[^11] in a quarrel not his own.

**18** Like a madman who shoots flaming arrows, arrows and death,

**19** so is the man who deceives his neighbor and says, "I was only joking!"

**20** Without wood a fire goes out, and without a gossip[^12] a quarrel dies down.

**21** As charcoal to embers and wood to fire, so is a quarrelsome man for kindling strife.

**22** The words of a gossip are like tasty morsels; they go down into the inner parts of the body.[^13]

**23** Like glaze[^14] coating an earthenware pot are burning lips with an evil heart.

**24** Whoever hates disguises it with his lips, but inside he harbors deceit.

**25** When he speaks graciously, do not believe him, for seven detestable things[^15] are in his heart.

**26** His hatred may be covered by deception, but his evil will be exposed in the assembly.

**27** Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, and a stone will come back on the one who rolls it.

**28** A lying tongue hates those it crushes,[^16] and a flattering mouth works ruin.

[^1]: Hebrew *kesil*, the most common word for "fool" in Proverbs: someone dull and self-satisfied who does not want to learn (see 1:22). Throughout verses 1–12.
[^2]: Literally "comes to him", probably the one who utters it. The Hebrew text is written "does not come", which gives the sense "so an undeserved curse does not land".
[^3]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7). Also in verses 5 and 11.
[^4]: Verses 4 and 5 are set side by side, giving opposite advice.
[^5]: Or "Like a bag of precious stones in a heap of stones".
[^6]: The Hebrew of this verse is very uncertain. Literally "Much (or 'An archer', or 'A master') wounding (or 'producing') all, and one who hires a fool and one who hires passersby".
[^7]: 2 Peter 2:22 quotes the first line.
[^8]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6). Also in verses 14–16.
[^9]: Two different Hebrew words for "lion". Compare 22:13.
[^10]: Compare 19:24.
[^11]: Hebrew *mit'abber*, usually "becomes enraged"; here probably "gets involved".
[^12]: Hebrew *nirgan*, "whisperer", as in 16:28. Also in verse 22.
[^13]: The verse repeats 18:8.
[^14]: The Hebrew vowel points read "silver of dross". The same consonants, divided into words differently, can be read "like glaze", a word known from other ancient languages.
[^15]: Hebrew *to'evot*, plural of *to'evah*, "something loathsome" (see 3:32).
[^16]: Literally "hates its crushed ones". The meaning is uncertain.
