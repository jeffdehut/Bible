**1** A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.

**2** The tongue of the wise makes knowledge attractive,[^1] but the mouth of fools[^2] gushes folly.[^3]

**3** The eyes of the LORD[^4] are everywhere, watching the evil and the good.

**4** A soothing tongue is a tree of life, but a twisted one crushes the spirit.

**5** A fool[^5] despises his father's discipline,[^6] but whoever heeds correction is prudent.[^7]

**6** In the house of the righteous[^8] there is great treasure, but the income of the wicked[^9] brings trouble.

**7** The lips of the wise spread knowledge, but the heart of fools is not so.[^10]

**8** The sacrifice of the wicked is detestable to the LORD, but the prayer of the upright is his delight.

**9** The way of the wicked is detestable to the LORD, but he loves the one who pursues righteousness.

**10** Severe discipline awaits the one who leaves the path; whoever hates correction will die.

**11** Sheol and Abaddon[^11] lie open before the LORD; how much more the hearts of humankind!

**12** A mocker[^12] does not love the one who corrects him; he will not go to the wise.

**13** A glad heart makes a cheerful face, but by heartache the spirit is crushed.

**14** The heart of the discerning seeks knowledge, but the mouth of fools[^13] feeds on folly.

**15** All the days of the poor are hard, but a cheerful heart has a continual feast.

**16** Better a little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure with turmoil.

**17** Better a meal of vegetables where there is love than a fattened ox with hatred.

**18** A hot-tempered man stirs up strife, but one who is slow to anger calms a quarrel.

**19** The way of the lazy person[^14] is like a hedge of thorns, but the path of the upright is a highway.

**20** A wise son makes a father glad, but a foolish man despises his mother.

**21** Folly is a joy to one who lacks sense,[^15] but a person of insight[^16] walks straight ahead.

**22** Plans fail without counsel, but with many advisers they succeed.

**23** A person finds joy in giving an apt answer, and a word at the right time, how good it is!

**24** The path of life leads upward for the sensible,[^17] so that he may turn away from Sheol below.

**25** The LORD tears down the house of the proud, but he sets firm the boundary of the widow.

**26** Evil plans are detestable to the LORD, but gracious words are pure.[^18]

**27** Whoever is greedy for gain brings trouble on his household, but whoever hates bribes will live.

**28** The heart of the righteous ponders how to answer, but the mouth of the wicked gushes evil things.

**29** The LORD is far from the wicked, but he hears the prayer of the righteous.

**30** Bright eyes[^19] gladden the heart; good news refreshes the bones.[^20]

**31** The ear that listens to life-giving correction will live among the wise.

**32** Whoever ignores discipline despises himself, but whoever listens to correction gains sense.[^21]

**33** The fear of the LORD is the discipline of wisdom,[^22] and humility comes before honor.

[^1]: Or "uses knowledge well".
[^2]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 7, 14 and 20.
[^3]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7). Also in verses 14 and 21.
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^6]: Hebrew *musar*: discipline, training and correction (see 1:2). Also in verses 10, 32 and 33.
[^7]: Hebrew *ya'rim*, a verb from the same root as *ormah*, "shrewdness, prudence" (see 1:4).
[^8]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^9]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^10]: Or "is not steady".
[^11]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead, and *Abaddon*, "Destruction", another name for it (Job 26:6; 28:22). Also in verse 24.
[^12]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22).
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "the face of fools" but read "the mouth of fools".
[^14]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^15]: Literally "lacks heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding.
[^16]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^17]: Hebrew *maskil*, as in 10:5.
[^18]: Or "but pleasant words are pure in his sight".
[^19]: Literally "The light of the eyes".
[^20]: Literally "makes the bones fat".
[^21]: Literally "gains heart".
[^22]: Or "is training in wisdom".
