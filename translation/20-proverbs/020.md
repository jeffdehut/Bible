**1** Wine is a mocker,[^1] beer a brawler, and whoever is led astray by it is not wise.

**2** The terror of a king is like the roar of a lion; whoever provokes him forfeits his life.

**3** It is an honor for a person to keep away from strife, but every fool[^2] bursts into quarrels.

**4** The lazy person[^3] does not plow in autumn; at harvest he looks for a crop, and there is nothing.

**5** The purpose in a person's heart is deep water, but a person of insight[^4] draws it out.

**6** Many a person proclaims his own loyalty,[^5] but a faithful person, who can find?

**7** The righteous[^6] walks in his integrity; happy[^7] are his children after him.

**8** A king who sits on the throne of judgment winnows out all evil with his eyes.

**9** Who can say, "I have made my heart clean; I am pure from my sin"?

**10** Two different weights and two different measures,[^8] both are detestable to Yahweh.[^9]

**11** Even a child makes himself known by his actions,[^10] whether his conduct is pure and right.

**12** The ear that hears and the eye that sees, Yahweh has made them both.

**13** Do not love sleep, or you will become poor; open your eyes, and you will have plenty of bread.

**14** "Bad, bad," says the buyer; but when he goes on his way, then he boasts.

**15** There is gold and plenty of coral,[^11] but lips of knowledge are a precious jewel.

**16** Take the garment of one who puts up security for a stranger; hold it in pledge for a foreign woman.[^12]

**17** Bread gained by deceit is sweet to a person, but afterward his mouth will be full of gravel.

**18** Plans are established by advice; wage war with guidance.[^13]

**19** Whoever goes about as a gossip reveals secrets, so do not associate with one who talks too much.

**20** Whoever curses his father or his mother, his lamp will go out in the blackest darkness.

**21** An inheritance gained in haste at the beginning[^14] will not be blessed in the end.

**22** Do not say, "I will pay back evil!" Wait for Yahweh, and he will rescue you.

**23** Two different weights are detestable to Yahweh, and dishonest scales are not good.

**24** A man's steps are from Yahweh; how then can anyone understand his own way?

**25** It is a trap for a person to say rashly, "It is holy!"[^15] and only afterward to think about his vows.

**26** A wise king winnows out the wicked[^16] and drives the wheel over them.[^17]

**27** The human spirit[^18] is the lamp of Yahweh, searching all the inner parts of the body.

**28** Faithful love and truth[^19] guard a king; by faithful love his throne is upheld.

**29** The glory of young men is their strength, and the splendor of the old is their gray hair.

**30** Blows that wound scour away evil, and beatings, the inner parts of the body.[^20]

[^1]: Hebrew *lets*, the word used for the person who scorns wisdom (see 1:22).
[^2]: Hebrew *ewil*, the morally perverse, stubborn fool (see 1:7).
[^3]: Hebrew *atsel*, traditionally "sluggard" (see 6:6).
[^4]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^5]: Hebrew *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^7]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^8]: Literally "a stone and a stone, an ephah and an ephah", one for buying and one for selling. Hebrew *ephah*, a measure for grain, used here for any measure of volume. Compare Deuteronomy 25:13–15.
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^10]: Or "Even a child may disguise himself by his actions".
[^11]: Hebrew *peninim*, as in 3:15.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "for foreigners" but read "for a foreign woman". Compare 27:13.
[^13]: Hebrew *tachbulot*, as in 1:5.
[^14]: The Hebrew text is written with a different word, perhaps "gained greedily", but read "gained in haste".
[^15]: That is, to dedicate something to God without thinking.
[^16]: Hebrew *resha'im*, plural of *rasha*: those who are guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^17]: The wheel of a threshing cart, used to separate grain from straw (Isaiah 28:27–28).
[^18]: Literally "The breath (*neshamah*) of a human being" (Genesis 2:7).
[^19]: Hebrew *chesed ve-emet*: faithful love and reliability (see 3:3).
[^20]: Literally "and blows, the chambers of the belly". The verb of the first line is understood again here.
