**1** Wisdom[^1] has built her house; she has carved out her seven pillars.

**2** She has slaughtered her meat, mixed her wine, and set her table.

**3** She has sent out her young women; she calls from the highest points of the city,

**4** "Whoever is simple,[^2] turn in here!" To the one who lacks sense[^3] she says,

**5** "Come, eat my bread and drink the wine I have mixed.

**6** Leave simple ways[^4] and live, and walk in the way of understanding."[^5]

**7** Whoever disciplines a mocker[^6] invites insult; whoever corrects a wicked person[^7] gets hurt.[^8]

**8** Do not correct a mocker, or he will hate you; correct a wise person, and he will love you.

**9** Instruct a wise person,[^9] and he will become wiser still; teach a righteous person,[^10] and he will add to his learning.

**10** The fear of the LORD[^11] is the beginning[^12] of wisdom, and knowledge of the Holy One[^13] is understanding.

**11** For through me[^14] your days will be many, and years will be added to your life.

**12** If you are wise, you are wise for your own good; if you mock, you alone will bear it.

**13** The woman Folly[^15] is loud; she is simple and knows nothing at all.

**14** She sits at the door of her house, on a seat at the highest point of the city,

**15** calling to those who pass by, who are going straight along their way,

**16** "Whoever is simple, turn in here!" And to the one who lacks sense she says,

**17** "Stolen water is sweet, and bread eaten in secret is delicious."

**18** But he does not know that the dead[^16] are there, that her guests are in the depths of Sheol.[^17]

[^1]: Hebrew *chokhmot*, as in 1:20. In verses 1–6 Wisdom is pictured as a woman who gives a banquet and invites the simple. In verses 13–18 Folly makes the same invitation.
[^2]: Hebrew *peti*: a naive, inexperienced person, easily led (see 1:4). Also in verses 6, 13 and 16.
[^3]: Literally "lacking heart". In Hebrew thought the heart is the seat of understanding. Also in verse 16.
[^4]: Or "Leave the simple". Hebrew *peta'im*, "simple ones" or "simpleness".
[^5]: Hebrew *binah*, as in 1:2. Also in verse 10.
[^6]: Hebrew *lets*: one who scorns wisdom and laughs at correction (see 1:22). *Disciplines* is from the same root as *musar*, "discipline". Verses 7–12 interrupt Wisdom's invitation with sayings. It is not clear who speaks them; the speaker of verse 11 may be Wisdom.
[^7]: Hebrew *rasha*: a wicked person, one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^8]: Literally "his blemish".
[^9]: Literally "Give to a wise person".
[^10]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: a righteous person, one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^11]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. On *the fear of the LORD*, see 1:7.
[^12]: Hebrew *techillah*, "beginning, first step", a different word from the one in 1:7.
[^13]: Hebrew *qedoshim*, plural, probably to express majesty, as in 30:3. Or "knowledge of holy things".
[^14]: Wisdom, or the teacher.
[^15]: Literally "A woman of folly". Hebrew *kesilut*, from the same root as *kesil*, "fool" (see 1:22). She is pictured as the opposite of Wisdom in verses 1–6.
[^16]: Hebrew *rephaim*, the spirits of the dead (see 2:18).
[^17]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
