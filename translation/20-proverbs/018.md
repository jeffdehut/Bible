**1** Whoever isolates himself seeks his own desire; he bursts out against all sound wisdom.[^1]

**2** A fool[^2] takes no pleasure in insight,[^3] but only in displaying his own mind.

**3** When the wicked[^4] comes, contempt comes too, and with dishonor comes disgrace.

**4** The words of a person's mouth are deep waters; the fountain of wisdom is a flowing stream.

**5** It is not good to show favor to the wicked,[^5] or to deprive the righteous[^6] of justice.

**6** A fool's lips walk into a quarrel, and his mouth invites a beating.

**7** A fool's mouth is his ruin, and his lips are a snare for his life.

**8** The words of a gossip are like tasty morsels;[^7] they go down into the inner parts of the body.

**9** Whoever is slack in his work is brother to one who destroys.

**10** The name of Yahweh[^8] is a strong tower; the righteous run into it and are safe.

**11** The wealth of the rich is their strong city; in their imagination[^9] it is like a high wall.

**12** Before destruction a person's heart is proud, but humility comes before honor.

**13** Whoever answers before he listens, it is folly[^10] and shame to him.

**14** A person's spirit can endure sickness, but a crushed spirit, who can bear it?

**15** The heart of the discerning acquires knowledge, and the ear of the wise seeks knowledge.

**16** A gift opens the way for the giver[^11] and brings him before the great.

**17** The first to state his case seems right, until the other comes and questions him.

**18** Casting the lot puts an end to disputes and decides between powerful opponents.

**19** An offended brother is harder to win than a strong city,[^12] and quarrels are like the bars of a fortress.

**20** From the fruit of a person's mouth his stomach is filled; he is satisfied with the yield of his lips.

**21** Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruit.

**22** He who finds a wife finds a good thing and obtains favor from Yahweh.[^13]

**23** The poor speak with pleading, but the rich answer harshly.

**24** A man of many companions may come to ruin,[^14] but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother.

[^1]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, as in 2:7. The verb *bursts out* is used of quarrels in 17:14 and 20:3.
[^2]: Hebrew *kesil* (see 1:22). Also in verses 6 and 7.
[^3]: Hebrew *tevunah* (see 2:2).
[^4]: Hebrew *rasha*: one who is guilty of wrong (see 2:22).
[^5]: Literally "to lift up the face of the wicked".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*: one who does what is right (see 2:20).
[^7]: Hebrew *mitlahamim*, a rare word, probably "tasty morsels" swallowed greedily. The verse is repeated in 26:22.
[^8]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^9]: Or "in their own conceit". Compare verse 10.
[^10]: Hebrew *iwwelet*, "folly", the word related to *ewil*, "fool" (see 1:7).
[^11]: Literally "A person's gift makes room for him".
[^12]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "A brother offended, than a strong city". The Septuagint reads "A brother helped by a brother is like a strong and high city".
[^13]: The Septuagint reads "a good wife".
[^14]: Or "A man of many friends has them only for company". The verb may come from a root meaning "be broken" or from one meaning "keep company".
