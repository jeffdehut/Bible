**1** Paul, a servant[^1] of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ,[^2] for the faith[^3] of God's chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness,

**2** in the hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began,[^4]

**3** and at the proper time[^5] he revealed his word in the proclamation with which I have been entrusted by command of God our Savior,

**4** to Titus, my true child in our common faith: Grace and peace[^6] from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

**5** This is why I left you in Crete, so that you might put in order what remained to be done, and appoint elders[^7] in every town as I directed you,

**6** if anyone is above reproach, the husband of one wife,[^8] with children who are believers[^9] and not open to the charge of reckless living or rebellion.

**7** For an overseer,[^10] as God's steward, must be above reproach, not arrogant, not quick-tempered, not a drunkard, not violent, not greedy for dishonest gain,

**8** but hospitable, a lover of what is good, self-controlled, upright, holy, disciplined,

**9** holding firmly to the trustworthy word as it was taught, so that he may be able both to encourage people with sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.

**10** For there are many who are rebellious, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision,[^11]

**11** who must be silenced, since they are upsetting whole households by teaching what they ought not to teach, for the sake of dishonest gain.

**12** One of them, a prophet of their own, said, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons."[^12]

**13** This testimony is true. For this reason rebuke them sharply, so that they may be sound in the faith,

**14** not paying attention to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth.

**15** To the pure all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving nothing is pure; both their minds and their consciences are defiled.

**16** They claim to know God, but they deny him by their deeds. They are detestable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work.

[^1]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^2]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^3]: Or "in keeping with the faith". Greek *kata*.
[^4]: Literally "before eternal times".
[^5]: Literally "at his own times".
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "Grace, mercy and peace".
[^7]: Greek *presbyteroi*, "elders", leaders of the church.
[^8]: Literally "a man of one woman". The phrase may mean married only once, faithful to his wife, or not having more than one wife. Compare 1 Timothy 3:2.
[^9]: Or "who are faithful". Greek *pistos*, "believing" or "faithful".
[^10]: Greek *episkopos*, "overseer", traditionally "bishop". The passage moves from "elders" (verse 5) to "the overseer" here.
[^11]: That is, Jewish believers, or those who insisted on circumcision; compare verse 14.
[^12]: A line attributed to the Cretan poet Epimenides (about 600 BC). *Lazy gluttons*: literally "idle bellies".
