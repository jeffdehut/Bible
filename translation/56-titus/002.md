**1** But as for you, speak what is consistent with sound teaching.

**2** Older men[^1] are to be sober-minded, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, in endurance.

**3** Older women likewise are to be reverent in behavior, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine. They are to teach what is good,

**4** so that they may train the young women to love their husbands and to love their children,

**5** to be self-controlled, pure, working at home,[^2] kind, submissive[^3] to their own husbands, so that the word of God may not be discredited.[^4]

**6** Likewise, urge the younger men to be self-controlled.

**7** In everything show yourself to be an example of good works; in your teaching show integrity, dignity,[^5]

**8** and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.

**9** Slaves[^6] are to submit to their own masters in everything; they are to please them, not to talk back,

**10** not to steal from them, but to show complete and genuine faithfulness, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching about God our Savior.

**11** For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people,[^7]

**12** training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly desires and to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives in the present age,

**13** as we wait for the blessed hope and the appearing[^8] of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ,[^9]

**14** who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people who are his own possession,[^10] eager to do good works.

**15** Speak these things; encourage and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

[^1]: Verses 2–10 give instructions to different groups in the church: older men, older women, younger women, younger men (with Titus himself as their example), and slaves. Compare Ephesians 5:21–6:9; Colossians 3:18–4:1. *Older men*: Greek *presbytai*, a different word from *presbyteroi*, "elders", in 1:5.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "staying at home".
[^3]: Greek *hypotasso*, "to place oneself under, to be subject to"; also in verse 9 (of slaves) and in 3:1 (of all believers toward rulers and authorities). Compare Ephesians 5:21–24.
[^4]: Literally "blasphemed". Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^5]: Many manuscripts add "incorruptibility".
[^6]: Greek *douloi*, "slaves"; *masters* is *despotai*, "owners". Slavery was part of the social order of the Roman world, and some members of the churches were slaves (compare 1 Timothy 6:1–2).
[^7]: Or "has appeared to all people, bringing salvation".
[^8]: Greek *epiphaneia*, "appearance, manifestation", related to the verb "has appeared" in verse 11. *The appearing of the glory*: or "the glorious appearing".
[^9]: Or "of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ". In the Greek one article governs both "God" and "Savior", which many take to mean that both refer to Jesus Christ; others take them as two persons.
[^10]: Greek *laos periousios*, the phrase the Septuagint uses for Israel as Yahweh's "treasured possession" (Exodus 19:5; Deuteronomy 14:2). *Redeem us from all lawlessness*: compare Psalm 130:8; Ezekiel 37:23.
