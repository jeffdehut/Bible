**1** Remind them to be subject[^1] to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work,

**2** to slander no one, to avoid quarreling, to be considerate, and to show complete gentleness toward all people.

**3** For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to all kinds of desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hated and hating one another.

**4** But when the kindness and love for humankind[^2] of God our Savior appeared,

**5** he saved us, not because of works we had done in righteousness, but according to his mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit,[^3]

**6** whom he poured out on us richly[^4] through Jesus Christ our Savior,

**7** so that, having been declared righteous[^5] by his grace, we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.

**8** The saying is trustworthy,[^6] and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works.[^7] These things are excellent and profitable for people.

**9** But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, quarrels and disputes about the Law, for they are unprofitable and worthless.

**10** As for a divisive person,[^8] after warning him once and then a second time, have nothing more to do with him,

**11** knowing that such a person is perverted and sinful; he is self-condemned.

**12** When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, do your best to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

**13** Do your best to send Zenas the lawyer[^9] and Apollos on their way, and see that they lack nothing.

**14** And let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works, so as to meet urgent needs, so that they will not be unfruitful.

**15** All who are with me greet you. Greet those who love us in the faith. Grace be with you all.[^10]

[^1]: Greek *hypotasso*, "to place oneself under, to be subject to", as in 2:5 and 2:9.
[^2]: Greek *philanthropia*, "love for human beings", the source of the word "philanthropy".
[^3]: Or "through the washing of rebirth and of renewal by the Holy Spirit", with one washing that brings both. Greek *palingenesia*, "new birth, regeneration".
[^4]: Compare Joel 3:1 (2:28 in most English Bibles).
[^5]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^6]: See 1 Timothy 1:15. The saying is probably the words of verses 4–7.
[^7]: Or "to engage in honorable occupations"; also in verse 14. Greek *proistemi*, "lead, care for, devote oneself to".
[^8]: Greek *hairetikos*, "factious, causing divisions", the source of the word "heretic".
[^9]: Greek *nomikos*, "legal expert", either in Roman law or in the Law of Moses.
[^10]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
