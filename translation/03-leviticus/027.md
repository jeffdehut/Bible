**1** The LORD[^1] said to Moses,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When anyone makes a special vow to give the value of a person to the LORD,[^2]

**3** the value of a male from twenty to sixty years old is to be fifty shekels[^3] of silver, by the sanctuary shekel.

**4** If it is a female, the value is to be thirty shekels.

**5** If the person is from five to twenty years old, the value of a male is to be twenty shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**6** If the person is from a month to five years old, the value of a male is to be five shekels of silver, and the value of a female three shekels of silver.

**7** If the person is sixty years old or more, the value of a male is to be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten shekels.

**8** But if the one making the vow is too poor to pay the value,[^4] the person is to be presented to the priest, and the priest is to set a value for him. The priest is to set the value according to what the one making the vow can afford.

**9** "If the vow concerns an animal of a kind that may be presented as an offering to the LORD, every such animal that is given to the LORD becomes holy.

**10** He must not exchange it or substitute another for it, a good one for a bad one or a bad one for a good one. If he does substitute one animal for another, both it and its substitute will become holy.

**11** If the vow concerns any unclean animal, of a kind that may not be presented as an offering to the LORD, the animal is to be presented to the priest.

**12** The priest is to set its value, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets, so it is to be.

**13** If he wishes to redeem it, he must add a fifth to its value.

**14** "When anyone consecrates his house as holy to the LORD, the priest is to set its value, whether good or bad. Whatever value the priest sets, so it will stand.

**15** If the one who consecrated it wishes to redeem his house, he must add a fifth to its value in silver, and it will be his again.

**16** "If anyone consecrates to the LORD part of the land that he holds as family property, its value is to be set according to the seed needed to sow it: fifty shekels of silver for a homer[^5] of barley seed.

**17** If he consecrates his field from the year of jubilee onward, it is to stand at the full value.

**18** But if he consecrates his field after the jubilee, the priest is to calculate the price for him according to the years that remain until the year of jubilee, and it is to be deducted from the value.

**19** If the one who consecrated the field wishes to redeem it, he must add a fifth to its value in silver, and it will remain his.

**20** But if he does not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to someone else, it can no longer be redeemed.

**21** When the field is released in the jubilee, it will be holy to the LORD, like a field that has been devoted.[^6] It will become the priest's property.

**22** If anyone consecrates to the LORD a field that he has bought, which is not part of the land he holds as family property,

**23** the priest is to calculate for him the amount of its value up to the year of jubilee, and he must pay its value on that day as a holy gift to the LORD.

**24** In the year of jubilee the field is to return to the person from whom he bought it, the one to whom the land belongs as family property.

**25** Every value is to be set by the sanctuary shekel, twenty gerahs[^7] to the shekel.

**26** "But no one may consecrate the firstborn of an animal, since as a firstborn it already belongs to the LORD. Whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's.

**27** If it is an unclean animal, he may buy it back at its value, adding a fifth to it. If it is not redeemed, it is to be sold at its value.

**28** "But nothing that anyone devotes[^8] to the LORD from all that he has, whether a person or an animal or family land, may be sold or redeemed. Everything devoted is most holy to the LORD.

**29** No person who has been devoted to destruction[^9] may be ransomed. He must be put to death.

**30** "A tenth[^10] of everything from the land, whether grain from the soil or fruit from the trees, belongs to the LORD. It is holy to the LORD.

**31** If anyone wishes to redeem any of his tenth, he must add a fifth to it.

**32** Every tenth of the herd and the flock, every tenth animal that passes under the shepherd's rod,[^11] is to be holy to the LORD.

**33** He must not look to see whether it is good or bad, and he must not substitute another for it. If he does substitute one, both it and its substitute will become holy. It cannot be redeemed."

**34** These are the commandments that the LORD gave Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "makes a special vow, by your valuation of persons, to the LORD". A person vowed to the LORD was released by paying a fixed sum to the sanctuary. The word for "value" (*erekh*, "your valuation") recurs throughout the chapter.
[^3]: A shekel was a weight of silver, about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams), so fifty shekels was about 1¼ pounds (575 grams). On the sanctuary shekel, see verse 25.
[^4]: Literally "But if he is poorer than your valuation".
[^5]: A homer was probably about 220 liters (6 bushels). The word means "donkey-load".
[^6]: Hebrew *cherem*: something devoted to the LORD for good, which could not be sold or redeemed (verses 28–29).
[^7]: A gerah was about 1/50 ounce (0.6 gram).
[^8]: Hebrew *charam*, "devote, put under the ban", the verb related to *cherem* (verse 21).
[^9]: That is, a person condemned under the ban (compare Exodus 22:19 (22:20 in most English Bibles); Joshua 6:17–19).
[^10]: Hebrew *ma'aser*, "a tenth", traditionally "tithe". Also in verses 31 and 32.
[^11]: Animals were counted as they passed under the shepherd's staff (compare Jeremiah 33:13; Ezekiel 20:37).
