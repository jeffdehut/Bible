**1** The LORD[^1] called to Moses and spoke to him[^2] from the tent of meeting.[^3] He said,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When any of you brings an offering[^4] to the LORD, you are to bring your offering from the livestock, from the herd or from the flock.[^5]

**3** If his offering is a burnt offering[^6] from the herd, he is to offer a male without defect.[^7] He is to bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, so that he may be accepted[^8] before the LORD.

**4** He is to lay his hand[^9] on the head of the burnt offering, and it will be accepted on his behalf to make atonement[^10] for him.

**5** He is to slaughter the young bull[^11] before the LORD. Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to present the blood and splash the blood against all sides of the altar that is at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**6** He is to skin the burnt offering and cut it into its pieces.

**7** The sons of Aaron the priest are to put fire on the altar and arrange wood on the fire.

**8** Then Aaron's sons, the priests, are to arrange the pieces, with the head and the fat,[^12] on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**9** He is to wash its internal organs and its legs with water, and the priest is to burn[^13] all of it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire,[^14] a pleasing aroma[^15] to the LORD.

**10** "If his offering for a burnt offering is from the flock, from the sheep or from the goats, he is to offer a male without defect.

**11** He is to slaughter it on the north side of the altar before the LORD, and Aaron's sons, the priests, are to splash its blood against all sides of the altar.

**12** He is to cut it into its pieces, with its head and its fat, and the priest is to arrange them on the wood that is on the fire on the altar.

**13** He is to wash the internal organs and the legs with water. Then the priest is to offer all of it and burn it on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

**14** "If his offering to the LORD is a burnt offering of birds, he is to bring his offering from the turtledoves or from the young pigeons.[^16]

**15** The priest is to bring it to the altar, wring off its head[^17] and burn it on the altar. Its blood is to be drained out against the side of the altar.

**16** He is to remove its crop with its contents[^18] and throw it beside the altar, on the east side, in the place for the ashes.[^19]

**17** He is to tear it open by its wings without dividing it in two.[^20] Then the priest is to burn it on the altar, on the wood that is on the fire. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "And he called to Moses, and the LORD spoke to him". The first word, *vayyiqra*, "and he called", is the Hebrew name of the book.
[^3]: The tent where the LORD met with Moses (Exodus 25:22; 29:42–43), set up as described in Exodus 40. It is also called the tabernacle.
[^4]: Hebrew *qorban*, "something brought near", from the verb used throughout these chapters for bringing or offering a sacrifice.
[^5]: The herd is cattle; the flock is sheep and goats (verse 10).
[^6]: Hebrew *olah*, from the verb "go up": the whole animal went up in smoke on the altar, apart from the skin (7:8).
[^7]: Hebrew *tamim*, "whole, complete": without any blemish or defect (22:17–25).
[^8]: Or "so that it may be accepted".
[^9]: Literally "press his hand" or "lean his hand".
[^10]: Hebrew *kipper*, traditionally "make atonement". The word probably means "wipe clean, purge" or "pay a ransom": to remove the effects of sin or impurity.
[^11]: Literally "the son of the herd".
[^12]: Hebrew *peder*, found only in Leviticus (also verse 12 and 8:20): probably the hard fat around the internal organs.
[^13]: Hebrew *hiqtir*, "make go up in smoke", the word used for burning offerings on the altar. A different word (*saraf*) is used for burning things up outside the camp (4:12).
[^14]: Hebrew *ishsheh*, traditionally "an offering made by fire", as if from *esh*, "fire". Some think it means "gift" or "food offering" (compare 3:11).
[^15]: Literally "a smell of soothing": an offering that pleases the LORD. Traditionally "a sweet savor".
[^16]: Literally "sons of the dove": young pigeons or doves.
[^17]: Hebrew *malaq*, perhaps "pinch off" with the fingernail.
[^18]: Or "with its feathers". The meaning of the Hebrew word *notsah* here is uncertain.
[^19]: Hebrew *deshen*, the ashes soaked with fat from the sacrifices.
[^20]: Literally "he shall not divide (it)".
