**1** Yahweh[^1] said to Moses and Aaron,

**2** "When someone has a swelling, a scab or a shiny spot[^2] on the skin of his body, and it could be a mark[^3] of skin disease[^4] on the skin of his body, he is to be brought to Aaron the priest or to one of his sons, the priests.

**3** The priest is to examine the mark on the skin of his body. If the hair in the mark has turned white, and the mark appears to be deeper than the skin of his body, it is a mark of skin disease. When the priest has examined him, he is to pronounce him unclean.

**4** But if the shiny spot on the skin of his body is white, and it does not appear to be deeper than the skin, and the hair in it has not turned white, the priest is to isolate the person with the mark[^5] for seven days.

**5** On the seventh day the priest is to examine him. If in his view the mark has not changed[^6] and the mark has not spread in the skin, the priest is to isolate him for another seven days.

**6** On the seventh day the priest is to examine him again. If the mark has faded and the mark has not spread in the skin, the priest is to pronounce him clean. It is only a scab. He must wash his clothes, and he will be clean.

**7** But if the scab spreads further in the skin after he has shown himself to the priest to be pronounced clean, he must show himself to the priest again.

**8** The priest is to examine him, and if the scab has spread in the skin, the priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a skin disease.

**9** "When someone has a mark of skin disease, he is to be brought to the priest.

**10** The priest is to examine him. If there is a white swelling in the skin, and it has turned the hair white, and there is raw flesh[^7] in the swelling,

**11** it is a chronic[^8] skin disease in the skin of his body. The priest is to pronounce him unclean. He is not to isolate him, because he is already unclean.

**12** If the skin disease breaks out all over the skin, so that the skin disease covers all the skin of the person with the mark from his head to his feet, wherever the priest looks,

**13** the priest is to examine him. If the skin disease has covered his whole body, he is to pronounce the person with the mark clean. He has turned all white; he is clean.

**14** But on the day raw flesh appears on him, he will be unclean.

**15** The priest is to examine the raw flesh and pronounce him unclean. The raw flesh is unclean; it is a skin disease.

**16** But if the raw flesh changes again and turns white, he is to go to the priest.

**17** The priest is to examine him, and if the mark has turned white, the priest is to pronounce the person with the mark clean. He is clean.

**18** "When someone has a boil on the skin of his body and it has healed,

**19** and in the place of the boil there is a white swelling or a reddish-white shiny spot, he must show himself to the priest.

**20** The priest is to examine it. If it appears to be lower than the skin, and its hair has turned white, the priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a mark of skin disease that has broken out in the boil.

**21** But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in it, and it is not lower than the skin, and it has faded, the priest is to isolate him for seven days.

**22** If it spreads further in the skin, the priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a mark of disease.

**23** But if the shiny spot stays where it is and does not spread, it is only the scar of the boil, and the priest is to pronounce him clean.

**24** "Or when someone has a burn on his skin, and the raw flesh of the burn becomes a shiny spot, reddish-white or white,

**25** the priest is to examine it. If the hair in the shiny spot has turned white, and it appears to be deeper than the skin, it is a skin disease that has broken out in the burn. The priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a mark of skin disease.

**26** But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in the shiny spot, and it is not lower than the skin, and it has faded, the priest is to isolate him for seven days.

**27** The priest is to examine him on the seventh day. If it has spread further in the skin, the priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a mark of skin disease.

**28** But if the shiny spot stays where it is and has not spread in the skin, and it has faded, it is the swelling from the burn. The priest is to pronounce him clean, because it is the scar of the burn.

**29** "When a man or a woman has a mark on the head or in the beard,

**30** the priest is to examine the mark. If it appears to be deeper than the skin, and there is thin yellow hair in it, the priest is to pronounce him unclean. It is a scaly patch,[^9] a skin disease of the head or of the beard.

**31** But if the priest examines the mark of the scaly patch, and it does not appear to be deeper than the skin, and there is no black hair in it, the priest is to isolate the person with the scaly patch for seven days.

**32** On the seventh day the priest is to examine the mark. If the scaly patch has not spread, and there is no yellow hair in it, and the scaly patch does not appear to be deeper than the skin,

**33** the person is to shave himself, but he must not shave the scaly patch. Then the priest is to isolate the person with the scaly patch for another seven days.

**34** On the seventh day the priest is to examine the scaly patch. If the scaly patch has not spread in the skin, and it does not appear to be deeper than the skin, the priest is to pronounce him clean. He must wash his clothes, and he will be clean.

**35** But if the scaly patch spreads further in the skin after he has been pronounced clean,

**36** the priest is to examine him. If the scaly patch has spread in the skin, the priest does not need to look for yellow hair. He is unclean.

**37** But if in his view the scaly patch has not changed, and black hair has grown in it, the scaly patch has healed. He is clean, and the priest is to pronounce him clean.

**38** "When a man or a woman has shiny spots on the skin of their body, white shiny spots,

**39** the priest is to examine them. If the shiny spots on the skin of their body are dull white, it is a rash[^10] that has broken out on the skin. That person is clean.

**40** "When a man loses the hair of his head, he is bald, but he is clean.

**41** If he loses the hair from the front of his head, he is bald on the forehead, but he is clean.

**42** But if there is a reddish-white mark on the bald head or the bald forehead, it is a skin disease breaking out on his bald head or his bald forehead.

**43** The priest is to examine him. If the swelling of the mark on his bald head or his bald forehead is reddish-white, like the appearance of skin disease on the skin of the body,

**44** he is a man with a skin disease. He is unclean. The priest must pronounce him unclean. His mark is on his head.

**45** "The person with the skin disease, who has the mark, must wear torn clothes and let the hair of his head hang loose.[^11] He must cover his upper lip and cry out, 'Unclean! Unclean!'

**46** He will remain unclean as long as he has the mark. He is unclean. He must live alone; his home must be outside the camp.

**47** "When a garment has a mark of mold,[^12] whether it is a garment of wool or of linen,

**48** in the warp or the weft[^13] of linen or of wool, or in leather or anything made of leather,

**49** if the mark in the garment or the leather, in the warp or the weft, or in any article of leather, is greenish or reddish, it is a mark of mold, and it must be shown to the priest.

**50** The priest is to examine the mark and isolate the article with the mark for seven days.

**51** On the seventh day he is to examine the mark. If the mark has spread in the garment, in the warp or the weft, or in the leather, whatever the leather is used for, the mark is a persistent mold.[^14] It is unclean.

**52** He must burn up the garment, or the warp or the weft, of wool or of linen, or any article of leather that has the mark, because it is a persistent mold. It must be burned up in the fire.

**53** But if the priest examines it and the mark has not spread in the garment, in the warp or the weft, or in any article of leather,

**54** the priest is to order them to wash the article that has the mark, and he is to isolate it for another seven days.

**55** After the mark has been washed, the priest is to examine it. If the mark has not changed its appearance, even though the mark has not spread, it is unclean. You must burn it up in the fire. It is rot,[^15] whether on its back or on its front.[^16]

**56** But if the priest examines it and the mark has faded after it has been washed, he is to tear the affected part out of the garment, or out of the leather, or out of the warp or the weft.

**57** If it appears again in the garment, or in the warp or the weft, or in any article of leather, it is breaking out. You must burn up in the fire whatever has the mark.

**58** But the garment, or the warp or the weft, or any article of leather, that you wash and from which the mark disappears, is to be washed a second time, and it will be clean.

**59** These are the instructions for a mark of mold in a garment of wool or of linen, in the warp or the weft, or in any article of leather, for pronouncing it clean or unclean."

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: The exact meaning of the Hebrew words for these three signs (*se'et*, *sappachat* and *baheret*) is uncertain.
[^3]: Hebrew *nega*, "touch, stroke, plague": a visible sign of disease. In verses 4, 12, 13 and 17 the same word stands for the person who has it.
[^4]: Hebrew *tsara'at*, traditionally "leprosy". It was not leprosy (Hansen's disease) as known today, but a range of skin conditions that made a person ritually unclean. In verses 47–59 the same word is used for mold in cloth and leather.
[^5]: Literally "shut up the mark". Also in verses 31 and 33.
[^6]: Literally "the mark has stood in his eyes". Also in verse 37.
[^7]: Literally "living flesh": the raw flesh of an open sore. Also in verses 14–16 and 24.
[^8]: Literally "old".
[^9]: Hebrew *neteq*, perhaps from a verb meaning "tear out": an itching or scaly patch where the hair falls out. Traditionally "scall" or "itch".
[^10]: Hebrew *bohaq*, a harmless rash or white patches on the skin.
[^11]: These were signs of mourning (10:6). So also was covering the upper lip (Ezekiel 24:17, 22; Micah 3:7).
[^12]: Hebrew *tsara'at*, the word used for the skin disease in verses 2–46. Also in verses 49, 51, 52 and 59.
[^13]: The threads running lengthwise and crosswise in woven cloth. Some take the words to mean "woven or knitted material".
[^14]: Hebrew *mam'eret*, perhaps "malignant" or "destructive". Also in verse 52.
[^15]: Hebrew *pechetet*, perhaps "an eaten-away spot".
[^16]: Literally "on its back baldness or on its front baldness", using the words for a bald head and a bald forehead in verses 40–43.
