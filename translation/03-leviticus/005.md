**1** "If anyone sins in this way: he hears a public call for witnesses,[^1] and he is a witness, because he has seen or knows about the matter, but he does not speak up, he will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.[^2]

**2** Or if anyone touches anything unclean,[^3] whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, or the carcass of unclean livestock, or the carcass of an unclean swarming creature,[^4] and it escapes his notice that he has become unclean, and then he realizes his guilt;[^5]

**3** or if he touches human uncleanness, any kind of uncleanness by which a person becomes unclean, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt;

**4** or if anyone thoughtlessly swears an oath with his lips to do harm or to do good, in any matter about which people swear thoughtlessly, and it escapes his notice, and then he learns of it and realizes his guilt in any of these matters;

**5** when he realizes his guilt in any one of these matters, he must confess the sin he has committed.

**6** He must bring to the LORD[^6] his penalty[^7] for the sin he has committed: a female from the flock, a lamb or a female goat, as a sin offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for his sin.

**7** "If he cannot afford[^8] a sheep or a goat, he is to bring to the LORD, as the penalty for the sin he has committed, two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering.

**8** He is to bring them to the priest, who is to offer first the one for the sin offering. He is to wring its head at the back of its neck, without severing it.

**9** He is to sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood is to be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

**10** The second he is to offer as a burnt offering, according to the regulation.[^9] So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he will be forgiven.

**11** "But if he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he is to bring as his offering for the sin he has committed a tenth of an ephah[^10] of fine flour as a sin offering. He must not put oil on it or put frankincense on it, because it is a sin offering.

**12** He is to bring it to the priest, and the priest is to take a full handful of it as its memorial portion and burn it on the altar on top of the offerings by fire to the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**13** So the priest will make atonement for him for the sin he has committed in any one of these matters, and he will be forgiven. The rest is to belong to the priest, as with the grain offering."[^11]

**14** The LORD said to Moses,

**15** "When anyone acts unfaithfully[^12] and sins unintentionally in regard to any of the LORD's holy things,[^13] he is to bring to the LORD as his guilt offering[^14] a ram without defect from the flock, valued in silver shekels[^15] by the sanctuary shekel,[^16] as a guilt offering.

**16** He must make good what he has failed to give from the holy things,[^17] add a fifth of its value to it, and give it to the priest. The priest will make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he will be forgiven.

**17** "If anyone sins by doing any one of the things that the LORD has commanded must not be done, even though he does not know it, he is guilty[^18] and will bear the consequences of his wrongdoing.

**18** He is to bring to the priest a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering. So the priest will make atonement for him for the error he committed unintentionally without knowing it, and he will be forgiven.

**19** It is a guilt offering. He was certainly guilty before the LORD."[^19]

**20** The LORD said to Moses,[^20]

**21** "When anyone sins and acts unfaithfully against the LORD by deceiving his neighbor about something entrusted to him or left in his care,[^21] or about something stolen, or if he has cheated his neighbor,

**22** or has found something that was lost and lied about it, and has sworn falsely about any one of the things that people do and so sin,

**23** then, when he has sinned and realizes his guilt, he must give back what he stole, or what he got by cheating, or what was entrusted to him, or the lost thing he found,

**24** or anything at all about which he swore falsely. He must repay it in full and add a fifth to it. He must give it to its owner on the day he presents his guilt offering.[^22]

**25** He is to bring to the priest, as his guilt offering to the LORD, a ram without defect from the flock, at the value you set, as a guilt offering.

**26** So the priest will make atonement for him before the LORD, and he will be forgiven for any one of all the things he may have done that made him guilty."

[^1]: Literally "he hears the voice of a curse": a public demand, backed by a curse, that anyone who knows something about a matter must come forward and testify.
[^2]: Literally "he will bear his iniquity": he is answerable for it and liable to its penalty. Also in verse 17.
[^3]: Hebrew *tamei*, "unclean": in a state of ritual impurity that bars a person or thing from contact with what is holy. It is not a matter of hygiene, nor in itself of moral guilt.
[^4]: Hebrew *sherets*: small creatures that swarm or creep, such as insects, rodents and lizards (11:29–31, 41–42).
[^5]: Or "and he becomes guilty". Hebrew *ashem*, which can mean both being guilty and feeling or recognizing guilt. Also in verses 3, 4, 5 and 23.
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7]: Hebrew *asham*, the word used for the guilt offering in verses 15–26. Here it means the penalty for his guilt, which is a sin offering. Also in verse 7.
[^8]: Literally "if his hand cannot reach what is enough for". Also in verse 11.
[^9]: That is, as in 1:14–17.
[^10]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel), so a tenth of an ephah was about 2 liters (2 quarts).
[^11]: Literally "and it shall be for the priest like the grain offering" (2:3, 10).
[^12]: Hebrew *ma'al*: a breach of faith, especially by taking or misusing what belongs to God.
[^13]: That is, the things dedicated to the LORD, such as offerings, tithes and the priests' portions.
[^14]: Hebrew *asham*, "guilt" or "compensation". Many scholars translate it "reparation offering", because it was brought together with repayment for what had been wrongly taken or withheld (verse 16; verses 21–24).
[^15]: A shekel was a weight of silver, about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams). The Hebrew may mean "at least two shekels".
[^16]: Literally "by the shekel of the sanctuary": the standard weight used at the sanctuary, twenty gerahs (Exodus 30:13; Leviticus 27:25).
[^17]: Literally "And what he sinned from the holy thing he shall repay".
[^18]: Or "and he feels guilty".
[^19]: Literally "He was guilty, guilty to the LORD."
[^20]: Verses 20–26 are 6:1–7 in most English Bibles.
[^21]: Hebrew *tesumet yad*, "a putting of the hand": perhaps a pledge, a deposit or a business partnership.
[^22]: Literally "on the day of his guilt": the day he admits his guilt and brings his guilt offering.
