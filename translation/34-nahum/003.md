**1** Woe to[^1] the city of bloodshed, full of lies, full of plunder, never without prey!

**2** The crack of the whip, the rumble of the wheel, the galloping horse and the jolting chariot!

**3** Horsemen charging, flashing swords and glittering spears! Many slain, heaps of corpses, bodies without end! They stumble over the bodies.

**4** All because of the many acts of prostitution of the prostitute, so charming, the mistress of sorceries, who sells nations by her prostitution and peoples[^2] by her sorceries.[^3]

**5** Look, I am against you, declares Yahweh of Armies.[^4] I will lift your skirts over your face. I will show the nations your nakedness, and the kingdoms your shame.[^5]

**6** I will throw filth[^6] on you, treat you with contempt, and make you a spectacle.

**7** Everyone who sees you will run from you and say, "Nineveh is devastated! Who will mourn for her?" Where can I find anyone to comfort you?[^7]

**8** Are you better than Thebes,[^8] which sat by the Nile, with water all around her, whose rampart was the sea, whose wall was the water?[^9]

**9** Cush and Egypt were her strength, without limit; Put and the Libyans were among her helpers.[^10]

**10** Yet she too went into exile; she went into captivity. Her infants too were dashed to pieces at the head of every street. They cast lots for her nobles, and all her great men were bound in chains.

**11** You too will become drunk;[^11] you will go into hiding.[^12] You too will seek refuge from the enemy.

**12** All your fortresses are like fig trees with the first-ripe figs: if they are shaken, the figs fall into the mouth of the eater.

**13** Look, your troops are women in your midst.[^13] The gates of your land are wide open to your enemies; fire has devoured your gate bars.

**14** Draw water for the siege! Strengthen your fortresses! Go into the clay and tread the mortar! Take hold of the brick mold![^14]

**15** There the fire will devour you; the sword will cut you off. It will devour you as the locust does.[^15] Multiply like the locust! Multiply like the swarming locust!

**16** You have increased your merchants more than the stars of the sky. The locust strips the land[^16] and flies away.

**17** Your guards are like the swarming locusts, your officials[^17] like swarms of locusts that settle on the walls on a cold day. When the sun rises, they fly away, and no one knows where they are.

**18** Your shepherds are asleep, king of Assyria; your nobles lie down to rest.[^18] Your people are scattered on the mountains, and there is no one to gather them.

**19** There is no relief for your injury; your wound is fatal. All who hear the news about you clap their hands over you, for who has not felt your endless cruelty?[^19]

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to".
[^2]: Literally "clans".
[^3]: Nineveh is pictured as a prostitute and a sorceress, who lured nations into her power and sold them into slavery.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares Yahweh* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement.
[^5]: The public shaming of an unfaithful woman (Jeremiah 13:22, 26; Ezekiel 16:37).
[^6]: Hebrew *shiqqutsim*, "detestable things", a word often used of idols.
[^7]: *Comfort* (*nacham*) plays on the name Nahum, "comfort" (1:1).
[^8]: Hebrew *No-amon*, "city of Amon", the great city of Upper Egypt, sacked by the Assyrian king Ashurbanipal in 663 BC.
[^9]: Literally "whose rampart was the sea, from the sea her wall". *The sea* and *the Nile* (literally "the Niles", its channels) are the great river.
[^10]: Literally "were your help". Cush, the land on the upper Nile, ruled Egypt at the time. Put was perhaps a region of Libya.
[^11]: Drunk with the cup of Yahweh's anger (Obadiah 16; Jeremiah 25:15–29).
[^12]: Or "you will faint". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^13]: That is, weak and unable to fight (Isaiah 19:16; Jeremiah 50:37). The Hebrew is feminine throughout verses 11–17, addressing Nineveh.
[^14]: Or "the brick kiln". The walls of Nineveh were built of sun-dried brick. The commands are mockery: no preparations will save the city.
[^15]: Hebrew *yeleq*, one of several words for locusts (Joel 1:4). Also in verse 16. *Swarming locust* is *arbeh*.
[^16]: Or "sheds its skin". *The land* is added.
[^17]: Hebrew *minnezarayikh* and *tafsarayikh*, both rare words for officials, perhaps borrowed from the Assyrian language (Jeremiah 51:27).
[^18]: The rulers of Assyria are asleep in death. *Lie down to rest* is literally "dwell". Some scholars read "sleep", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^19]: Literally "for on whom has your evil not passed continually?" Like the book of Jonah, which also concerns Nineveh, Nahum ends with a question.
