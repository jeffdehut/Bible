**1** Now concerning the coming[^1] of our Lord Jesus Christ and our being gathered together to him, we ask you, brothers and sisters,[^2]

**2** not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed, either by a spirit[^3] or by a spoken word or by a letter, as if from us,[^4] to the effect that the day of the Lord[^5] has come.[^6]

**3** Let no one deceive you in any way. For that day will not come[^7] unless the rebellion[^8] comes first and the man of lawlessness[^9] is revealed, the son of destruction,[^10]

**4** who opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god or object of worship, so that he takes his seat in the temple[^11] of God,[^12] proclaiming himself to be God.[^13]

**5** Do you not remember that I told you these things while I was still with you?

**6** And you know what is restraining[^14] him now, so that he will be revealed in his own time.

**7** For the mystery[^15] of lawlessness is already at work; only the one who now restrains will do so until he is taken out of the way.[^16]

**8** And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill[^17] with the breath of his mouth[^18] and destroy by the appearance of his coming.

**9** The coming[^19] of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan, with every kind of power and false signs and wonders,[^20]

**10** and with every kind of wicked deception for those who are perishing, because they did not accept the love of the truth so as to be saved.

**11** For this reason God sends[^21] them a powerful delusion,[^22] so that they will believe the lie,

**12** so that all who did not believe the truth but delighted in wrongdoing will be judged.

**13** But we ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters loved by the Lord, because God chose you as firstfruits[^23] for salvation, through sanctification by the Spirit[^24] and belief in the truth.

**14** To this he called you through our good news, so that you may obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**15** So then, brothers and sisters, stand firm and hold to the traditions that you were taught, whether by word of mouth or by our letter.

**16** Now may our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God our Father, who loved us and by grace gave us eternal comfort and good hope,

**17** comfort[^25] your hearts and strengthen them in every good work and word.

[^1]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", used for the visit of a ruler; here the coming of the Lord Jesus. In verse 9 the same word is used of the lawless one.
[^2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 13 and 15.
[^3]: That is, an utterance claimed to come from the Spirit, such as a prophecy.
[^4]: "As if from us" may refer to the letter only, or to all three.
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "the day of Christ".
[^6]: Or "is already here". Greek *enesteken*, "has come, is present".
[^7]: The Greek leaves the sentence unfinished; "that day will not come" is supplied from verse 2.
[^8]: Greek *apostasia*, "rebellion, revolt", used both of political revolt and of rebellion against God; traditionally "falling away".
[^9]: Greek *anomia*, "lawlessness"; also in verse 7. Many manuscripts read "the man of sin".
[^10]: A Hebrew way of speaking: one who belongs to destruction or is destined for it. The same phrase is used of Judas in John 17:12.
[^11]: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary of a temple, the building where the deity was thought to dwell.
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "he takes his seat as God in the temple of God".
[^13]: Compare Daniel 11:36 and Ezekiel 28:2.
[^14]: Greek *to katechon*, "that which restrains", neuter; in verse 7, *ho katechon*, "the one who restrains", masculine. Paul does not say who or what this is, since his readers already know (verse 5), and many explanations have been proposed. The verb *katecho* can also mean "hold fast" or "occupy", and some take it in one of those senses. *Now* may also go with "you know": "And now you know what is restraining him".
[^15]: Greek *mysterion*, here something hidden that is already at work but not yet openly revealed.
[^16]: Literally "only the one who now restrains, until he comes out of the midst". The Greek has no main verb.
[^17]: Many manuscripts leave out "Jesus", and many read "will consume" for "will kill".
[^18]: Compare Isaiah 11:4.
[^19]: Greek *parousia*, the word used in verses 1 and 8 of the coming of the Lord.
[^20]: Literally "signs and wonders of falsehood". *False* may describe all three.
[^21]: Many manuscripts read "will send".
[^22]: Literally "a working of error".
[^23]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28. Other editions, and many manuscripts, read "God chose you from the beginning".
[^24]: Or "through the sanctifying of your spirit". Greek *hagiasmos*, "being made holy".
[^25]: The Greek verbs "comfort" and "strengthen" are singular, though they have two subjects (compare 1 Thessalonians 3:11).
