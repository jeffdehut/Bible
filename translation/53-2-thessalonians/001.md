**1** Paul, Silvanus and Timothy, to the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:[^1]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God the Father[^2] and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters,[^3] as is fitting, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing,

**4** so that we ourselves boast about you in the churches of God, for your endurance and faith in all the persecutions and afflictions you are enduring.

**5** This is evidence[^4] of the righteous judgment of God, so that you may be considered worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are also suffering,

**6** since indeed it is just for God to repay with affliction those who afflict you,

**7** and to give relief to you who are afflicted, and to us as well, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels[^5]

**8** in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance on those who do not know God and on those who do not obey the good news of our Lord Jesus.[^6]

**9** They will pay the penalty of eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might,[^7]

**10** when he comes on that day[^8] to be glorified among his holy people[^9] and to be marveled at among all who have believed, because our testimony to you was believed.

**11** To this end we always pray for you, that our God may make you worthy of his calling, and by his power may fulfill every desire for goodness and every work of faith,

**12** so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.[^10]

[^1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "God our Father".
[^3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^4]: Greek *endeigma*, "proof, sign". "This" refers to their endurance and faith in suffering (verse 4).
[^5]: Literally "with the angels of his power".
[^6]: Compare Isaiah 66:15 and Jeremiah 10:25.
[^7]: Or "eternal destruction that comes from the presence of the Lord …". The words echo Isaiah 2:10, 19, 21, as worded in the Septuagint: "from the presence of the fear of the Lord and from the glory of his might". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^8]: In the Greek, "on that day" comes at the end of the verse.
[^9]: Literally "his holy ones", traditionally "his saints".
[^10]: Or "of our God and Lord, Jesus Christ". In the Greek one article governs both "God" and "Lord".
