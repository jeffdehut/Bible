**1** All the kings west of the Jordan[^1] heard of this, those in the hill country, in the Shephelah[^2] and all along the coast of the Great Sea[^3] as far as Lebanon: the Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

**2** They gathered together to fight as one[^4] against Joshua and Israel.

**3** But when the inhabitants of Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and to Ai,

**4** they too acted with cunning. They went and disguised themselves as envoys.[^5] They took worn-out sacks for their donkeys, and wineskins that were worn out, split and patched up,

**5** worn-out, patched sandals on their feet and worn-out clothes on their bodies. All the bread they had brought as provisions was dry and crumbly.[^6]

**6** They went to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal and said to him and to the men of Israel, "We have come from a distant land. Now make a covenant with us."

**7** The men of Israel said to the Hivites,[^7] "Perhaps you live among us. How then can we make a covenant with you?"

**8** They said to Joshua, "We are your servants." Joshua said to them, "Who are you, and where do you come from?"

**9** They said to him, "Your servants have come from a very distant land because of the name of the LORD[^8] your God. For we have heard reports of him, and of everything he did in Egypt,

**10** and everything he did to the two kings of the Amorites east of the Jordan,[^9] Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan, who lived in Ashtaroth.

**11** Our elders and all the inhabitants of our land said to us, 'Take provisions with you for the journey. Go to meet them and say to them, "We are your servants. Now make a covenant with us."'

**12** This bread of ours was warm when we took it from our houses as provisions on the day we set out to come to you. But now look, it is dry and crumbly.

**13** These wineskins were new when we filled them, and look, they are split. And these clothes and sandals of ours are worn out from the very long journey."

**14** The men of Israel took some of their provisions,[^10] but they did not ask for the LORD's direction.[^11]

**15** Joshua made peace with them and made a covenant with them to let them live, and the leaders of the community swore an oath to them.

**16** Three days after they had made a covenant with them, the Israelites heard that they were their neighbors and lived among them.

**17** So the Israelites set out and came to their towns on the third day. Their towns were Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth and Kiriath-jearim.

**18** But the Israelites did not attack them, because the leaders of the community had sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel. The whole community grumbled against the leaders.

**19** But all the leaders said to the whole community, "We have sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel, and now we cannot touch them.

**20** This is what we will do to them: we will let them live, so that wrath does not come on us because of the oath we swore to them."

**21** The leaders said to them, "Let them live." So they became woodcutters and water carriers for the whole community, as the leaders had said about them.

**22** Joshua summoned them and said to them, "Why did you deceive us by saying, 'We live very far from you,' when you live among us?

**23** Now you are cursed. Some of you will always be slaves,[^12] woodcutters and water carriers for the house of my God."

**24** They answered Joshua, "Your servants were told plainly that the LORD your God had commanded his servant Moses to give you the whole land and to destroy all the inhabitants of the land before you. So we were very much afraid for our lives because of you, and we did this.

**25** Now we are in your hands. Do to us whatever seems good and right to you."

**26** So he did this for them: he rescued them from the hands of the Israelites, and they did not kill them.

**27** That day Joshua made them woodcutters and water carriers for the community and for the altar of the LORD at the place he would choose,[^13] as they are to this day.

[^1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", here meaning west of the river.
[^2]: The low hills between the coastal plain and the hill country of Judah.
[^3]: The Mediterranean Sea.
[^4]: Literally "with one mouth".
[^5]: Hebrew *vayitstayyaru*, a rare word, probably "made themselves envoys". The Greek Septuagint reads "they took provisions", a similar Hebrew word, as in verse 12.
[^6]: Hebrew *niqqudim*, "crumbs", or perhaps "spotted with mold". Also in verse 12.
[^7]: The Gibeonites were Hivites (11:19).
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: Literally "beyond the Jordan".
[^10]: Perhaps to examine them, or as a sign of agreement by sharing a meal.
[^11]: Literally "they did not ask the mouth of the LORD".
[^12]: Literally "A slave will not be cut off from you".
[^13]: The language of Deuteronomy 12:5 for the place of worship that the LORD would choose. Literally "to the place that he would choose".
