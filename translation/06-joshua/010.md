**1** Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem heard that Joshua had captured Ai and devoted it to destruction,[^1] doing to Ai and its king as he had done to Jericho and its king, and that the inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were living among them.

**2** He and his people were very much afraid, because Gibeon was a great town, like one of the royal towns. It was greater than Ai, and all its men were warriors.

**3** So Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem sent word to Hoham king of Hebron, Piram king of Jarmuth, Japhia king of Lachish and Debir king of Eglon:

**4** "Come up to me and help me, and let us attack Gibeon, for it has made peace with Joshua and the Israelites."

**5** So the five kings of the Amorites, the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish and the king of Eglon, gathered and went up, they and all their armies. They camped against Gibeon and attacked it.

**6** The men of Gibeon sent word to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal: "Do not abandon your servants.[^2] Come up to us quickly, save us and help us, for all the kings of the Amorites who live in the hill country have gathered against us."

**7** So Joshua went up from Gilgal, he and all the fighting men with him, all the mighty warriors.

**8** The LORD[^3] said to Joshua, "Do not be afraid of them, for I have given them into your hand. Not one of them will be able to stand against you."

**9** Joshua came on them suddenly, having marched all night from Gilgal.

**10** The LORD threw them into confusion before Israel, and he[^4] struck them down in a great slaughter at Gibeon. He chased them along the road that goes up to Beth-horon and struck them down as far as Azekah and Makkedah.

**11** As they fled before Israel, while they were on the way down from Beth-horon, the LORD threw huge stones down on them from the sky all the way to Azekah, and they died. More of them died from the hailstones than the Israelites killed with the sword.

**12** Then Joshua spoke to the LORD, on the day the LORD gave the Amorites over to the Israelites, and he said in the sight of Israel:[^5] "Sun, stand still[^6] over Gibeon, and moon, over the Valley of Aijalon!"[^7]

**13** So the sun stood still, and the moon stopped,[^8] until the nation took vengeance on its enemies. Is this not written in the Book of Jashar?[^9] The sun stopped in the middle of the sky and did not hurry to set for about a whole day.[^10]

**14** There has been no day like it before or since, when the LORD listened to the voice of a man,[^11] for the LORD fought for Israel.

**15** Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.[^12]

**16** Now these five kings had fled and hidden in the cave at Makkedah.

**17** Joshua was told, "The five kings have been found hiding in the cave at Makkedah."

**18** Joshua said, "Roll large stones against the mouth of the cave, and post men by it to guard them.

**19** But you must not stop. Chase your enemies and attack them from the rear.[^13] Do not let them get into their towns, for the LORD your God has given them into your hand."

**20** Joshua and the Israelites finished striking them down in a very great slaughter, until they were wiped out, though some survivors escaped from them and reached the fortified towns.

**21** Then all the people returned safely to Joshua at the camp at Makkedah. No one dared to say a word against the Israelites.[^14]

**22** Joshua said, "Open the mouth of the cave and bring those five kings out of the cave to me."

**23** They did so. They brought those five kings out of the cave to him: the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish and the king of Eglon.

**24** When they had brought these kings out to Joshua, Joshua summoned all the men of Israel and said to the commanders of the fighting men who had gone with him, "Come here and put your feet on the necks of these kings." So they came and put their feet on their necks.

**25** Joshua said to them, "Do not be afraid or dismayed. Be strong and courageous. For this is what the LORD will do to all the enemies you are fighting."

**26** After this Joshua struck them down and put them to death, and he hanged them on five trees. They were left hanging on the trees until evening.

**27** At sunset Joshua gave the command, and they took them down from the trees and threw them into the cave where they had hidden. They put large stones over the mouth of the cave, which are there to this very day.

**28** That day Joshua captured Makkedah and put it and its king to the sword. He devoted them to destruction, with every person[^15] in it. He left no survivor. He did to the king of Makkedah as he had done to the king of Jericho.

**29** Joshua and all Israel with him moved on from Makkedah to Libnah and fought against Libnah.

**30** The LORD gave it also, with its king, into the hand of Israel. He put it and every person in it to the sword. He left no survivor in it. He did to its king as he had done to the king of Jericho.

**31** Joshua and all Israel with him moved on from Libnah to Lachish. He camped against it and attacked it.

**32** The LORD gave Lachish into the hand of Israel, and he captured it on the second day. He put it and every person in it to the sword, just as he had done to Libnah.

**33** Then Horam king of Gezer came up to help Lachish, but Joshua struck him and his people down until he had left him no survivor.

**34** Joshua and all Israel with him moved on from Lachish to Eglon. They camped against it and attacked it.

**35** They captured it that same day and put it to the sword. He devoted to destruction every person in it that day, just as he had done to Lachish.

**36** Joshua and all Israel with him went up from Eglon to Hebron and attacked it.

**37** They captured it and put it to the sword, with its king, all its towns and every person in it. He left no survivor, just as he had done to Eglon. He devoted it and every person in it to destruction.

**38** Then Joshua and all Israel with him turned back to Debir and attacked it.

**39** He captured it, with its king and all its towns. They put them to the sword and devoted to destruction every person in it. He left no survivor. As he had done to Hebron, so he did to Debir and its king, just as he had done to Libnah and its king.

**40** So Joshua struck the whole land, the hill country, the Negev,[^16] the Shephelah[^17] and the slopes,[^18] with all their kings. He left no survivor. He devoted to destruction everything that breathed, as the LORD, the God of Israel, had commanded.

**41** Joshua struck them from Kadesh-barnea to Gaza, and the whole land of Goshen[^19] as far as Gibeon.

**42** Joshua captured all these kings and their land at one time, because the LORD, the God of Israel, fought for Israel.

**43** Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.

[^1]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself. Also in verses 28, 35, 37, 39 and 40.
[^2]: Literally "Do not let your hands drop from your servants".
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: The LORD, or Joshua. The Hebrew says only "he", here and in the next sentence.
[^5]: Joshua's words, and probably the words "So the sun stood still, and the moon stopped, until the nation took vengeance on its enemies" in verse 13, are poetry quoted from the Book of Jashar (verse 13).
[^6]: Hebrew *dom*, "be still, be silent, wait" (compare 1 Samuel 14:9). Verse 13 describes the sun stopping in its course, and that is how the words have usually been read. Some take the command to mean that the sun should stop shining, or be darkened.
[^7]: Gibeon lies east of the Valley of Aijalon. The picture may be of the early morning, with the sun rising in the east and the moon setting in the west.
[^8]: Literally "stood". The verbs for the sun (*damam*) and the moon (*amad*) are both used of standing still or stopping.
[^9]: Or "the Book of the Upright". *Jashar* means "upright". The book is now lost; it is also quoted in 2 Samuel 1:18.
[^10]: Literally "stood in the half of the heavens and did not hasten to go in, about a complete day".
[^11]: Literally "for the LORD to listen to the voice of a man".
[^12]: This verse is repeated in verse 43. According to verse 21, the army was still at Makkedah. The Greek Septuagint has neither this verse nor verse 43.
[^13]: Literally "cut off their tail", that is, attack the stragglers at the back.
[^14]: Literally "No one sharpened his tongue against the Israelites, against anyone".
[^15]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "living being". Also in verses 30, 32, 35, 37 and 39.
[^16]: The dry country in the south of the land.
[^17]: The low hills between the coastal plain and the hill country of Judah.
[^18]: Hebrew *ashedot*, the slopes that fall from the hill country, perhaps toward the Dead Sea.
[^19]: A region in the south of Judah (compare 11:16; 15:51), not the Goshen in Egypt.
