**1** The LORD[^1] said to Joshua, "Do not be afraid or dismayed. Take all the fighting men with you, and get up and go up to Ai. See, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, his people, his town and his land.

**2** Do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king, except that you may take its spoil and its livestock as plunder for yourselves. Set an ambush against the town, behind it."

**3** So Joshua and all the fighting men got up to go up to Ai. Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty warriors and sent them out by night.

**4** He commanded them, "Listen. You are to lie in ambush against the town, behind it. Do not go very far from the town, and all of you be ready.

**5** I and all the people with me will approach the town. When they come out against us as they did the first time, we will flee from them.

**6** They will come out after us until we have drawn them away from the town, for they will say, 'They are fleeing from us as they did the first time.' While we flee from them,

**7** you are to rise up from the ambush and take possession of the town. The LORD your God will give it into your hand.

**8** When you have seized the town, set it on fire. Do as the LORD has said. See, I have given you your orders."

**9** So Joshua sent them out, and they went to the place of ambush and waited between Bethel and Ai, west of Ai. But Joshua spent that night among the people.

**10** Early in the morning Joshua got up and mustered the people, and he and the elders of Israel went up ahead of the people to Ai.

**11** All the fighting men who were with him went up and drew near until they came in front of the town. They camped north of Ai, with the valley between them and Ai.

**12** He took about five thousand men[^2] and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, west of the town.

**13** So they stationed the people, the main camp north of the town and its rear guard[^3] west of the town. That night Joshua went into the middle of the valley.

**14** When the king of Ai saw this, the men of the town hurried and got up early and went out to meet Israel in battle, the king and all his people, at the appointed place[^4] facing the Arabah. But he did not know that an ambush was waiting for him behind the town.

**15** Joshua and all Israel let themselves be beaten back before them, and they fled along the road to the wilderness.

**16** All the people who were in Ai[^5] were called out to chase them. They chased Joshua and were drawn away from the town.

**17** Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel. They left the town open and chased Israel.

**18** The LORD said to Joshua, "Stretch out the javelin[^6] in your hand toward Ai, for I will give it into your hand." So Joshua stretched out the javelin in his hand toward the town.

**19** As soon as he stretched out his hand, the men in ambush rose quickly from their place and ran. They entered the town and captured it, and they quickly set the town on fire.

**20** The men of Ai looked back and saw the smoke of the town rising to the sky, and they had no strength[^7] to flee this way or that. Then the people who had fled toward the wilderness turned back on their pursuers.

**21** When Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had captured the town and that the smoke of the town was rising, they turned back and struck down the men of Ai.

**22** The others came out of the town against them, so that the men of Ai were caught in the middle of Israel, some on this side and some on that. Israel struck them down until not one survivor or fugitive was left.

**23** But they took the king of Ai alive and brought him to Joshua.

**24** When Israel had finished killing all the inhabitants of Ai in the open country, in the wilderness where they had chased them, and every one of them had fallen by the sword,[^8] all Israel returned to Ai and put it to the sword.

**25** All who fell that day, men and women, numbered twelve thousand, all the people of Ai.

**26** Joshua did not draw back the hand with which he held out the javelin until he had devoted to destruction[^9] all the inhabitants of Ai.

**27** Only the livestock and the spoil of that town Israel took as plunder for themselves, in keeping with the word of the LORD that he had commanded Joshua.

**28** So Joshua burned Ai and made it a ruin mound[^10] forever, a desolate place to this day.

**29** He hanged the king of Ai on a tree until evening.[^11] At sunset Joshua gave the command, and they took his body down from the tree and threw it down at the entrance of the town gate. They raised over it a great heap of stones, which is there to this day.

**30** Then Joshua built an altar to the LORD, the God of Israel, on Mount Ebal,[^12]

**31** as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded the Israelites, as it is written in the book of the law of Moses: an altar of uncut stones, on which no one had used an iron tool.[^13] On it they offered burnt offerings[^14] to the LORD and sacrificed fellowship offerings.[^15]

**32** There on the stones he wrote a copy of the law of Moses, which he wrote[^16] in the presence of the Israelites.

**33** All Israel, with their elders, officials and judges, stood on both sides of the ark, facing the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the LORD, foreigners[^17] as well as native-born. Half of them stood in front of Mount Gerizim and half in front of Mount Ebal, as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded earlier, to bless the people of Israel.[^18]

**34** After that, Joshua read all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse, in keeping with everything written in the book of the law.

**35** There was not a word of all that Moses had commanded that Joshua did not read before the whole assembly of Israel, including the women, the little ones and the foreigners who lived among them.[^19]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Verse 3 gives thirty thousand.
[^3]: Literally "its heel".
[^4]: Or "at the appointed time".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "in the town" but read "in Ai".
[^6]: Hebrew *kidon*, a javelin or perhaps a curved sword. Also in verse 26.
[^7]: Literally "no hands".
[^8]: Literally "had fallen to the mouth of the sword until they were finished".
[^9]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^10]: Hebrew *tel*, a mound of ruins. The name Ai means "the ruin".
[^11]: Probably after he had been killed (compare 10:26). See Deuteronomy 21:22–23.
[^12]: In the Greek Septuagint, verses 30–35 come after 9:2.
[^13]: Exodus 20:25; Deuteronomy 27:5–6.
[^14]: Hebrew *olot*: animals burned whole on the altar.
[^15]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers.
[^16]: Or "which Moses had written". See Deuteronomy 27:2–8.
[^17]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 35.
[^18]: Or "as Moses the servant of the LORD had commanded, to bless the people of Israel first". See Deuteronomy 11:29; 27:11–13.
[^19]: Literally "who walked among them".
