**1** All the kings of the Amorites west of the Jordan[^1] and all the kings of the Canaanites along the sea heard how Yahweh[^2] had dried up the water of the Jordan before the Israelites until they had crossed over.[^3] Their hearts melted, and they had no courage left[^4] because of the Israelites.

**2** At that time Yahweh said to Joshua, "Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites again, a second time."

**3** So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at Gibeath-haaraloth.[^5]

**4** This is why Joshua circumcised them. All the people who came out of Egypt, the males, all the men of war, had died in the wilderness on the way after they came out of Egypt.

**5** All the people who came out had been circumcised, but none of the people born in the wilderness on the way after they came out of Egypt had been circumcised.

**6** For the Israelites walked for forty years in the wilderness, until the whole nation, the men of war who came out of Egypt, had died off, because they had not obeyed the voice of Yahweh. Yahweh had sworn to them that he would not let them see the land that Yahweh had sworn to their ancestors to give us,[^6] a land flowing with milk and honey.[^7]

**7** So it was their sons, whom he raised up in their place, that Joshua circumcised. They were uncircumcised, because they had not been circumcised on the way.

**8** When the whole nation had been circumcised, they stayed where they were in the camp until they had healed.

**9** Yahweh said to Joshua, "Today I have rolled away the disgrace of Egypt[^8] from you." So the place has been called Gilgal[^9] to this day.

**10** While the Israelites were camped at Gilgal, they kept the Passover on the evening of the fourteenth day of the month, in the plains of Jericho.

**11** On the day after the Passover, that very day, they ate some of the produce[^10] of the land: unleavened bread and roasted grain.

**12** The manna stopped on the day after,[^11] when they ate some of the produce of the land. The Israelites no longer had manna, and that year they ate from the crops of the land of Canaan.

**13** When Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him with his sword drawn in his hand. Joshua went up to him and said to him, "Are you for us or for our enemies?"

**14** "No,"[^12] he said, "I am the commander of Yahweh's army.[^13] I have come now." Joshua fell with his face to the ground and bowed down,[^14] and he said to him, "What does my lord have to say to his servant?"

**15** The commander of Yahweh's army said to Joshua, "Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy."[^15] And Joshua did so.

[^1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan toward the sea", here meaning west of the river.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "until we had crossed over" but read "until they had crossed over".
[^4]: Literally "there was no spirit in them any more".
[^5]: The name means "Hill of the Foreskins".
[^6]: So the Hebrew. The writer counts himself among the people who received the land.
[^7]: A land rich in pasture for flocks and herds, and in honey, either from bees or a syrup made from dates.
[^8]: Probably the disgrace of having been slaves in Egypt, or the scorn of the Egyptians.
[^9]: *Gilgal* sounds like Hebrew *galloti*, "I have rolled".
[^10]: Hebrew *avur*, a word found only here and in verse 12, probably grain from the previous harvest.
[^11]: Or "on that same day". Literally "on the morrow".
[^12]: The Greek Septuagint reads "He said to him". In Hebrew "no" (*lo'*) and "to him" (*lo*) sound alike.
[^13]: Or "Yahweh's host". Hebrew *tsava*, the word in the title "Yahweh of Armies": the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 15.
[^14]: Or "worshiped".
[^15]: Compare Exodus 3:5.
