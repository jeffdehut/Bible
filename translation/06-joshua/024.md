**1** Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel at Shechem.[^1] He summoned the elders, the leaders,[^2] the judges and the officials of Israel, and they presented themselves before God.

**2** Joshua said to all the people, "This is what the LORD,[^3] the God of Israel, says: Long ago your ancestors, Terah the father of Abraham and of Nahor, lived beyond the River,[^4] and they served other gods.

**3** But I took your father Abraham from beyond the River and led him through all the land of Canaan. I made his descendants many, and I gave him Isaac.

**4** To Isaac I gave Jacob and Esau. To Esau I gave the hill country of Seir to possess, but Jacob and his sons went down to Egypt.

**5** Then I sent Moses and Aaron, and I struck Egypt with plagues by what I did among them, and afterward I brought you out.

**6** I brought your ancestors out of Egypt, and you came to the sea. The Egyptians pursued your ancestors with chariots and horsemen to the Red Sea.[^5]

**7** They cried out to the LORD, and he put darkness between you and the Egyptians. He brought the sea over them and covered them. Your own eyes saw what I did in Egypt. Then you lived in the wilderness for a long time.

**8** I brought you to the land of the Amorites, who lived east of the Jordan.[^6] They fought against you, but I gave them into your hand. You took possession of their land, and I destroyed them before you.

**9** Then Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, set out to fight against Israel. He sent for Balaam son of Beor to curse you.[^7]

**10** But I would not listen to Balaam. He blessed you again and again, and I rescued you from his hand.

**11** Then you crossed the Jordan and came to Jericho. The leaders[^8] of Jericho fought against you, and so did the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Girgashites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, but I gave them into your hand.

**12** I sent the hornet[^9] ahead of you, and it drove them out before you, the two kings of the Amorites.[^10] It was not by your sword or your bow.

**13** I gave you a land you had not toiled over, and towns you had not built, and you live in them. You eat from vineyards and olive groves that you did not plant.

**14** So now fear the LORD and serve him with integrity and faithfulness. Put away the gods your ancestors served beyond the River and in Egypt, and serve the LORD.

**15** But if you think it wrong[^11] to serve the LORD, choose today whom you will serve, whether the gods your ancestors served beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you are living. But as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD."

**16** The people answered, "Far be it from us to abandon the LORD to serve other gods!

**17** For it is the LORD our God who brought us and our ancestors up from the land of Egypt, out of the place of slavery,[^12] and who did those great signs before our eyes. He protected us all along the way we traveled, and among all the peoples through whom we passed.

**18** The LORD drove out before us all the peoples, and the Amorites who lived in the land. We too will serve the LORD, for he is our God."

**19** Joshua said to the people, "You cannot serve the LORD, for he is a holy God.[^13] He is a jealous God.[^14] He will not forgive your rebellion or your sins.

**20** If you abandon the LORD and serve foreign gods, he will turn and bring disaster on you and make an end of you, after he has done you good."

**21** The people said to Joshua, "No! We will serve the LORD."

**22** Joshua said to the people, "You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen the LORD, to serve him." They said, "We are witnesses."

**23** "Then put away the foreign gods that are among you, and turn your hearts to the LORD, the God of Israel."

**24** The people said to Joshua, "We will serve the LORD our God, and we will obey his voice."

**25** So on that day Joshua made a covenant for the people, and there at Shechem he set out for them a decree and a ruling.

**26** Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God. He took a large stone and set it up there, under the oak[^15] that was by the sanctuary of the LORD.

**27** Joshua said to all the people, "Look, this stone will be a witness against us, for it has heard all the words that the LORD spoke to us. It will be a witness against you, so that you do not deny your God."

**28** Then Joshua sent the people away, each to his own inheritance.

**29** After these things, Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the LORD, died at the age of 110.

**30** They buried him within the territory of his inheritance, at Timnath-serah in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

**31** Israel served the LORD all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua and who had known all the work that the LORD had done for Israel.

**32** The bones of Joseph, which the Israelites had brought up from Egypt, they buried at Shechem, in the piece of land that Jacob had bought from the sons of Hamor, the father of Shechem, for a hundred *qesitah*.[^16] It became an inheritance of the descendants of Joseph.

**33** Eleazar son of Aaron died, and they buried him at Gibeah,[^17] the town of his son Phinehas, which had been given to him in the hill country of Ephraim.[^18]

[^1]: The Greek Septuagint reads "Shiloh" here and in verse 25.
[^2]: Literally "heads".
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: The Euphrates. Also in verses 3, 14 and 15.
[^5]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds". The Greek Septuagint calls it "the Red Sea".
[^6]: Literally "beyond the Jordan".
[^7]: Numbers 22–24.
[^8]: Hebrew *ba'alei Yericho*, "the lords of Jericho", or "the citizens of Jericho".
[^9]: Hebrew *tsir'ah*, of uncertain meaning. It is usually taken as "hornet" or "hornets", but some understand it as "panic" or "plague" (compare Exodus 23:28).
[^10]: The Greek Septuagint reads "the twelve kings of the Amorites".
[^11]: Literally "if it is evil in your eyes".
[^12]: Literally "out of the house of slaves" (Exodus 20:2).
[^13]: The Hebrew word for "holy" is plural here, agreeing with *Elohim*, "God", which is plural in form.
[^14]: Hebrew *el qanno*, a God who demands exclusive loyalty. Or "a zealous God".
[^15]: Or "terebinth".
[^16]: A *qesitah* was a unit of money; its value is not known. See Genesis 33:19; 50:25; Exodus 13:19.
[^17]: Or "Gibeath-phinehas", or "the hill of Phinehas".
[^18]: The Greek Septuagint continues the book with further material after this verse.
