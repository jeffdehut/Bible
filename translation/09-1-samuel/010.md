**1** Then Samuel took a flask of olive oil and poured it on Saul's head, and he kissed him and said, "Has not the LORD[^1] anointed you ruler[^2] over his own people?[^3]

**2** When you leave me today, you will meet two men near Rachel's tomb, at Zelzah in the territory of Benjamin. They will say to you, 'The donkeys you went to look for have been found. Now your father has stopped thinking about the donkeys and is worried about you. He is saying, "What shall I do about my son?"'

**3** Then you will go on from there and come to the oak of Tabor. Three men going up to God at Bethel will meet you there. One will be carrying three young goats, another three loaves of bread, and another a skin of wine.

**4** They will greet you[^4] and give you two loaves of bread, which you are to accept from them.

**5** After that you will come to Gibeah of God,[^5] where the Philistine garrison[^6] is. As you come into the town, you will meet a band of prophets coming down from the high place, with harps, tambourines, flutes and lyres[^7] in front of them, and they will be prophesying.[^8]

**6** Then the Spirit of the LORD will rush upon you,[^9] and you will prophesy with them and be changed into a different person.

**7** When these signs have come to you, do whatever the occasion calls for,[^10] for God is with you.

**8** Then go down ahead of me to Gilgal. I will come down to you to offer burnt offerings and to sacrifice fellowship offerings.[^11] Wait seven days until I come to you and tell you what you are to do."

**9** As Saul turned to leave Samuel, God changed his heart,[^12] and all these signs came about that day.

**10** When they came to Gibeah, there was a band of prophets coming to meet him. The Spirit of God rushed upon him, and he prophesied among them.

**11** When all who had known him before[^13] saw him prophesying with the prophets, the people said to one another, "What has happened to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?"

**12** A man who lived there answered, "And who is their father?"[^14] So it became a saying: "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

**13** When he had finished prophesying, he went to the high place.

**14** Saul's uncle asked him and his servant, "Where did you go?" He said, "To look for the donkeys. When we saw they were nowhere to be found, we went to Samuel."

**15** Saul's uncle said, "Please tell me what Samuel said to you."

**16** Saul said to his uncle, "He told us plainly that the donkeys had been found." But he did not tell him what Samuel had said about the kingship.

**17** Samuel called the people together to the LORD at Mizpah.

**18** He said to the Israelites, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'I brought Israel up out of Egypt, and I rescued you from the power of the Egyptians and from the power of all the kingdoms that were oppressing you.'

**19** But today you have rejected your God, who saves you from all your disasters and your troubles. You have said to him,[^15] 'Set a king over us instead.' So now present yourselves before the LORD by your tribes and by your clans."[^16]

**20** Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel forward, and the tribe of Benjamin was chosen by lot.[^17]

**21** Then he brought the tribe of Benjamin forward clan by clan,[^18] and the clan of Matri was chosen. Then Saul son of Kish was chosen. But when they looked for him, he could not be found.

**22** So they inquired of the LORD again, "Has the man come here yet?" The LORD said, "There he is, hiding among the baggage."

**23** They ran and brought him from there. When he stood among the people, he was a head taller than any of them.[^19]

**24** Samuel said to all the people, "Do you see the man the LORD has chosen? There is no one like him among all the people." Then all the people shouted, "Long live the king!"

**25** Samuel told the people the rules of the kingship.[^20] He wrote them in a scroll and placed it before the LORD. Then Samuel sent all the people away, each to his own home.

**26** Saul too went home to Gibeah, and with him went the valiant men[^21] whose hearts God had touched.

**27** But some worthless men[^22] said, "How can this man save us?" They despised him and brought him no gift. But he kept silent.[^23]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, ruler"; see 9:16.
[^3]: Literally "over his inheritance". The Septuagint reads "Has not the LORD anointed you ruler over his people Israel? You will reign over the people of the LORD and save them from the power of their enemies around them. And this will be the sign for you that the LORD has anointed you ruler over his inheritance:"
[^4]: Literally "ask you about *shalom* (well-being)".
[^5]: Hebrew *Giv'at ha-elohim*, "the hill of God".
[^6]: Hebrew *netsiv*, which may mean a garrison, an officer or governor, or a pillar set up as a marker. Compare 13:3.
[^7]: The harp (*nevel*) and the lyre (*kinnor*) were two kinds of stringed instrument.
[^8]: Here the verb describes an ecstatic state, perhaps with music and movement. Also in verses 6, 10, 11 and 13.
[^9]: Hebrew *tsalach*, "rush on, come powerfully on". Also in verse 10.
[^10]: Literally "do for yourself what your hand finds".
[^11]: Hebrew *zivchei shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers.
[^12]: Literally "God turned for him another heart".
[^13]: Literally "from yesterday and the day before".
[^14]: Perhaps meaning that prophets do not become prophets by birth, so anyone may be found among them. The Septuagint reads "his father".
[^15]: Literally "You have said to him, 'For a king you will set over us.'" The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "No" (Hebrew *lo'*) instead of "to him" (*lo*): "You have said, 'No, set a king over us'", as in 8:19.
[^16]: Literally "your thousands".
[^17]: Literally "was taken". Also in verse 21.
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "by its clan" but read "by its clans".
[^19]: Literally "from his shoulders and upward".
[^20]: Hebrew *mishpat ha-melukhah*, the custom or rule of the kingship. Compare 8:9–11 and Deuteronomy 17:14–20.
[^21]: Hebrew *ha-chayil*, "the force, the army", here a band of capable men.
[^22]: Literally "sons of *beliyya'al*"; see 2:12.
[^23]: Literally "and he was like one who keeps silent". With different vowels and one letter changed, the Septuagint reads "about a month later", beginning 11:1. Before those words 4QSam(a), a Dead Sea Scroll of Samuel, has a paragraph not found in the Hebrew text: Nahash king of the Ammonites had been cruelly oppressing the tribes of Gad and Reuben, gouging out the right eye of each of them and letting no one rescue Israel. No Israelite east of the Jordan was left whose right eye Nahash had not gouged out, except seven thousand men who had escaped from the Ammonites and entered Jabesh-gilead. It then continues, "About a month later, Nahash the Ammonite went up ..." (11:1).
