**1** Hannah prayed and said, "My heart rejoices in Yahweh;[^1] my horn[^2] is lifted high in Yahweh. My mouth laughs at my enemies,[^3] for I rejoice in your salvation.

**2** There is no one holy like Yahweh; there is no one besides you. There is no rock like our God.

**3** Do not keep talking so proudly, or let arrogant words come out of your mouth. For Yahweh is a God who knows,[^4] and by him actions are weighed.[^5]

**4** The bows of the mighty are broken, but those who stumbled are clothed with strength.[^6]

**5** Those who were full hire themselves out for bread, but those who were hungry hunger no more.[^7] The woman who could not have children has given birth to seven, but the mother of many sons wastes away.

**6** Yahweh puts to death and brings to life. He brings down to Sheol[^8] and raises up.

**7** Yahweh makes poor and makes rich. He brings low; he also lifts up.

**8** He raises the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap,[^9] to seat them with nobles and give them a seat of honor.[^10] For the pillars[^11] of the earth belong to Yahweh, and he has set the world on them.

**9** He guards the feet of his faithful ones,[^12] but the wicked are silenced in darkness, for no one wins by strength.

**10** Those who oppose Yahweh will be shattered; he will thunder against them from heaven.[^13] Yahweh will judge the ends of the earth. He will give strength to his king and lift high the horn of his anointed."[^14]

**11** Then Elkanah went home to Ramah, but the boy served Yahweh in the presence of Eli the priest.

**12** Now the sons of Eli were worthless men.[^15] They did not know Yahweh.

**13** This was how the priests dealt with the people:[^16] whenever anyone offered a sacrifice, the priest's servant[^17] would come while the meat was boiling, with a three-pronged fork in his hand.

**14** He would plunge it into the pan, or the kettle, or the cauldron, or the pot, and the priest would take for himself whatever the fork brought up.[^18] This is what they did to all the Israelites who came there to Shiloh.

**15** Even before the fat was burned,[^19] the priest's servant would come and say to the man who was sacrificing, "Give the priest some meat to roast. He will not take boiled meat from you, only raw."

**16** If the man said to him, "Let them burn the fat first, and then take whatever you want," he would say, "No,[^20] you must give it now. If not, I will take it by force."

**17** So the sin of the young men was very great in the sight of Yahweh, for the men treated Yahweh's offering[^21] with contempt.

**18** But Samuel was serving before Yahweh, a boy wearing[^22] a linen ephod.[^23]

**19** His mother would make him a little robe and bring it up to him every year, when she went up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.

**20** Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife and say, "May Yahweh give you children[^24] by this woman in place of the one she asked for and gave to Yahweh."[^25] Then they would go home.

**21** Yahweh came to help[^26] Hannah. She became pregnant and gave birth to three sons and two daughters. And the boy Samuel grew up in the presence of Yahweh.

**22** Eli was very old. He heard about everything his sons were doing to all Israel, and how they slept with the women who served[^27] at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**23** He said to them, "Why do you do such things? I hear about your evil deeds from all the people.

**24** No, my sons! The report I hear spreading among Yahweh's people is not good.[^28]

**25** If one person sins against another, God may mediate for him.[^29] But if someone sins against Yahweh, who will intercede for him?" But they would not listen to their father, for Yahweh wanted to put them to death.

**26** And the boy Samuel kept growing, both in stature and in favor[^30] with Yahweh and with people.

**27** A man of God came to Eli and said to him, "This is what Yahweh says: 'Did I not reveal myself to your father's family when they were in Egypt, subject to Pharaoh's household?[^31]

**28** I chose him[^32] out of all the tribes of Israel to be my priest, to go up to my altar, to burn incense, and to wear an ephod in my presence. And I gave your father's family all the offerings by fire[^33] from the Israelites.

**29** Why do you kick at[^34] my sacrifice and my offering, which I commanded for my dwelling?[^35] Why do you honor your sons more than me, by making yourselves fat on the best part of every offering of my people Israel?

**30** Therefore Yahweh, the God of Israel, declares: I did say that your family and your father's family would walk before me for ever. But now Yahweh declares: Far be it from me! For those who honor me I will honor, and those who despise me will be treated with contempt.

**31** The days are coming when I will cut off your strength[^36] and the strength of your father's family, so that there will be no old man in your family.

**32** You will see distress in my dwelling, in spite of all the good done for Israel,[^37] and there will never be an old man in your family.

**33** Any man of yours whom I do not cut off from my altar will be left only to wear out your eyes and grieve your heart.[^38] And all the descendants of your family will die in the prime of life.[^39]

**34** And what happens to your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, will be the sign for you: both of them will die on the same day.

**35** I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who will do what is in my heart and my mind.[^40] I will build him a lasting house,[^41] and he will walk before my anointed[^42] for ever.[^43]

**36** Everyone who is left in your family will come and bow down to him for a piece[^44] of silver and a loaf of bread. He will say, "Please appoint me to one of the priestly offices, so that I may have a scrap of bread to eat."'"

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: The horn, as of a wild ox, is an image of strength and dignity. Also in verse 10.
[^3]: Literally "My mouth is wide over my enemies".
[^4]: Literally "a God of knowledge".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "and actions are not weighed" but read "and by him actions are weighed".
[^6]: Literally "have girded on strength".
[^7]: Literally "the hungry have ceased".
[^8]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^9]: Hebrew *ashpot*, a heap of ashes and rubbish.
[^10]: Literally "and make them inherit a throne of glory".
[^11]: Hebrew *metsuqim*, a rare word, perhaps "foundations".
[^12]: Hebrew *chasidim*, a word related to *chesed*, "faithful love". The Hebrew text is written "his faithful one" but read "his faithful ones".
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "The one who opposes Yahweh will be shattered; he will thunder against him" but read "Those who oppose ... against them". The Septuagint adds at this point words close to Jeremiah 9:22–23 (English 9:23–24): "Let not the wise boast in his wisdom ..."
[^14]: Hebrew *meshicho*, "his anointed one", from *mashiach*, the word behind "Messiah". Here it means the king anointed by Yahweh.
[^15]: Literally "sons of *beliyya'al*", "sons of worthlessness": scoundrels.
[^16]: Literally "the *mishpat* (custom, rule) of the priests with the people".
[^17]: Hebrew *na'ar*, "boy, young man", often a servant or attendant. Also in verse 15; "young men" in verse 17 is the plural of the same word.
[^18]: Literally "whatever the fork brought up, the priest would take with it".
[^19]: Literally "burned to smoke", that is, on the altar as Yahweh's portion (Leviticus 3:16; 7:25). Also in verse 16.
[^20]: The Hebrew text is written "he would say to him" but read "he would say, 'No'".
[^21]: Hebrew *minchah*, often a grain offering, but here offerings in general.
[^22]: Literally "girded with".
[^23]: Hebrew *ephod*. Here a simple linen garment worn by those who served at the sanctuary (22:18; 2 Samuel 6:14). The high priest's ephod, a richer garment, is described in Exodus 28:6–14. Also in verse 28.
[^24]: Literally "seed".
[^25]: Literally "in place of the request which he asked for Yahweh", another play on *sha'al*, "ask, lend" (1:28).
[^26]: Hebrew *paqad*, "attend to, take note of, come to help".
[^27]: Hebrew *tsove'ot*, as in Exodus 38:8. The Septuagint does not have "and how they slept with the women who served at the entrance of the tent of meeting".
[^28]: Or "The report I hear is not good: you are making Yahweh's people sin."
[^29]: Hebrew *elohim*, which can also mean "the judges" (compare Exodus 21:6).
[^30]: Literally "and good".
[^31]: Literally "to the house of Pharaoh". The Septuagint reads "slaves to the house of Pharaoh".
[^32]: That is, your ancestor, Aaron.
[^33]: Hebrew *ishsheh*, traditionally "an offering made by fire". Some take it to mean "a food gift".
[^34]: That is, scorn. The verb is plural: Eli and his sons.
[^35]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "which I commanded, dwelling".
[^36]: Literally "your arm".
[^37]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "And you will look on distress (or "a rival") of dwelling, in all that he does good for Israel."
[^38]: Literally "to make your eyes fail and to make your *nephesh* grieve". The Septuagint reads "his eyes ... his *nephesh*".
[^39]: Literally "will die men", that is, as grown men rather than in old age. The Septuagint reads "will fall by the sword of men".
[^40]: Literally "my heart and my *nephesh*".
[^41]: Literally "a sure house", that is, a family line that will last.
[^42]: Hebrew *meshichi*, "my anointed one", the king. See verse 10.
[^43]: Literally "all the days".
[^44]: Hebrew *agorah*, found only here, probably a small coin or weight.
