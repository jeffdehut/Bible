**1** Then David got up and left, and Jonathan went back to the town.[^1]

**2** David came to Nob, to Ahimelech the priest. Ahimelech came trembling to meet David and said to him, "Why are you alone, with no one with you?"

**3** David said to Ahimelech the priest, "The king has given me a mission,[^2] and he said to me, 'Let no one know anything about the mission I am sending you on and the orders I have given you.' As for my young men, I have told them to meet me at such and such a place.

**4** Now then, what do you have on hand?[^3] Give me five loaves of bread, or whatever can be found."

**5** The priest answered David, "I have no ordinary bread on hand, only holy bread, provided the young men have kept themselves from women."

**6** David answered the priest, "Women have certainly been kept from us, as always when I set out.[^4] The young men's bodies[^5] are holy even on an ordinary journey. How much more today will they be holy in their bodies!"[^6]

**7** So the priest gave him holy bread, for there was no bread there except the bread of the Presence,[^7] which had been removed from before the LORD[^8] so that hot bread could be put in its place on the day it was taken away.

**8** Now one of Saul's servants was there that day, detained before the LORD.[^9] His name was Doeg the Edomite, the chief of Saul's shepherds.[^10]

**9** David said to Ahimelech, "Don't you have a spear or a sword here on hand? I didn't bring my sword or my weapons with me, because the king's business was urgent."

**10** The priest said, "The sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck down in the Valley of Elah, is here, wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod. If you want to take it, take it, for there is no other here." David said, "There is none like it. Give it to me."

**11** That day David got up and fled from Saul, and came to Achish king of Gath.

**12** But the servants of Achish said to him, "Isn't this David, the king of the land? Isn't he the one they sing about in their dances: 'Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his tens of thousands'?"[^11]

**13** David took these words to heart and was very much afraid of Achish king of Gath.

**14** So he changed his behavior[^12] in front of them and acted like a madman in their hands. He scribbled[^13] on the doors of the gate and let his saliva run down his beard.

**15** Achish said to his servants, "Look, you can see the man is mad! Why did you bring him to me?

**16** Am I short of madmen, that you have brought this one to act like a madman in front of me? Must this man come into my house?"

[^1]: Verse 1 is the second half of 20:42 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–16 are 21:1–15.
[^2]: Literally "The king commanded me a matter". Also later in the verse.
[^3]: Literally "under your hand". Also in verses 5 and 9.
[^4]: Literally "as yesterday and the day before when I went out".
[^5]: Literally "vessels", that is, perhaps their bodies, or their gear.
[^6]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult.
[^7]: Hebrew *lechem ha-panim*, "the bread of the face": twelve loaves set out before the LORD in the sanctuary and replaced each Sabbath (Exodus 25:30; Leviticus 24:5–9).
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: Hebrew *ne'tsar*, "held back", perhaps to fulfill a vow or for purification.
[^10]: Literally "the mightiest of the shepherds".
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "his thousand ... his ten thousand" but read "his thousands ... his tens of thousands". Compare 18:7.
[^12]: Literally "changed his sense", that is, pretended to be mad. The title of Psalm 34 refers to this event.
[^13]: Or "made marks". The Septuagint reads "drummed".
