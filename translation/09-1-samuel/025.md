**1** Samuel died, and all Israel gathered and mourned for him. They buried him at his home in Ramah. Then David got up and went down to the wilderness of Paran.[^1]

**2** There was a man in Maon whose business[^2] was in Carmel.[^3] The man was very rich. He had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, and he was shearing his sheep at Carmel.

**3** The man's name was Nabal,[^4] and his wife's name was Abigail. The woman was intelligent and beautiful, but the man was harsh and evil in his dealings. He was a Calebite.[^5]

**4** While David was in the wilderness, he heard that Nabal was shearing his sheep.

**5** So David sent ten young men. David said to the young men, "Go up to Carmel, go to Nabal and greet him in my name.[^6]

**6** Say this:[^7] 'Peace to you, peace to your household, and peace to all that is yours!

**7** I hear that you are shearing. Now your shepherds have been with us. We did not harm them, and nothing of theirs went missing the whole time they were at Carmel.

**8** Ask your young men, and they will tell you. So let my young men find favor in your eyes, for we have come on a festive day.[^8] Please give whatever you have on hand[^9] to your servants and to your son David.'"

**9** When David's young men arrived, they said all this to Nabal in David's name, and then they waited.

**10** Nabal answered David's servants, "Who is David? Who is this son of Jesse? Nowadays there are many servants breaking away from their masters.

**11** Should I take my bread and my water and the meat I have slaughtered for my shearers, and give it to men who come from who knows where?"

**12** So David's young men turned and went back. They came and reported all these words to him.

**13** David said to his men, "Each of you, strap on your sword!" So each of them strapped on his sword, and David also strapped on his sword. About four hundred men went up after David, while two hundred stayed with the supplies.

**14** But one of the young men told Abigail, Nabal's wife, "Look, David sent messengers from the wilderness to greet[^10] our master, but he screamed insults at them.[^11]

**15** Yet the men were very good to us. They did not harm us, and we did not miss anything the whole time we went about with them while we were in the open country.

**16** They were a wall around us both night and day, the whole time we were with them looking after the sheep.

**17** Now think it over and see what you can do, for disaster is determined against our master and against his whole household. He is such a worthless man[^12] that no one can speak to him."

**18** Abigail hurried. She took two hundred loaves of bread, two skins of wine, five sheep already prepared, five seahs[^13] of roasted grain, a hundred clusters of raisins and two hundred cakes of pressed figs, and loaded them on donkeys.

**19** She said to her young men, "Go on ahead of me. I am coming after you." But she did not tell her husband Nabal.

**20** As she was riding on her donkey and coming down under cover of the mountain, there were David and his men coming down toward her, and she met them.

**21** Now David had said, "It was all for nothing that I guarded everything this fellow had in the wilderness, so that nothing of his went missing. He has paid me back evil for good.

**22** May God deal with David's enemies, and more severely,[^14] if by morning I leave alive a single male[^15] of all who belong to him."

**23** When Abigail saw David, she quickly got down from the donkey and fell on her face before David, bowing to the ground.

**24** She fell at his feet and said, "On me, my lord, on me be the guilt! Please let your servant[^16] speak to you,[^17] and listen to the words of your servant.

**25** Please, my lord, pay no attention to this worthless man,[^18] Nabal. He is just like his name. Nabal is his name, and folly[^19] goes with him. But I, your servant, did not see the young men my lord sent.

**26** Now, my lord, as surely as the LORD[^20] lives and as surely as you live,[^21] since the LORD has kept you from bloodshed and from taking vengeance with your own hand,[^22] now let your enemies and those who seek to harm my lord be like Nabal.

**27** And now let this gift[^23] that your servant[^24] has brought to my lord be given to the young men who follow my lord.[^25]

**28** Please forgive your servant's offense. The LORD will certainly make a lasting house[^26] for my lord, because my lord fights the battles of the LORD, and no evil will be found in you as long as you live.

**29** If anyone rises up to pursue you and seek your life, the life of my lord will be bound securely in the bundle of the living[^27] with the LORD your God. But the lives of your enemies he will sling away as from the pocket of a sling.

**30** When the LORD has done for my lord all the good he has promised concerning you, and has appointed you ruler[^28] over Israel,

**31** my lord will not have this as a cause of stumbling or a burden on his conscience,[^29] that he shed blood for no reason or took vengeance for himself. And when the LORD has dealt well with my lord, remember your servant."

**32** David said to Abigail, "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you to meet me today!

**33** Blessed be your good sense, and blessed be you, for keeping me today from bloodshed and from taking vengeance with my own hand.

**34** But as surely as the LORD, the God of Israel, lives, who has kept me from harming you, if you had not come quickly to meet me, by morning light not a single male belonging to Nabal would have been left."

**35** Then David accepted from her what she had brought him, and he said to her, "Go home in peace. See, I have listened to you and granted your request."[^30]

**36** When Abigail came back to Nabal, he was holding a feast in his house, like a king's feast. Nabal was in high spirits[^31] and very drunk, so she told him nothing at all[^32] until morning light.

**37** In the morning, when Nabal had sobered up,[^33] his wife told him all these things, and his heart died within him, and he became like a stone.

**38** About ten days later the LORD struck Nabal, and he died.

**39** When David heard that Nabal was dead, he said, "Blessed be the LORD, who has pleaded my case against Nabal for insulting me, and has kept his servant from doing evil. The LORD has brought Nabal's evil down on his own head." Then David sent word to Abigail, asking to take her as his wife.

**40** David's servants came to Abigail at Carmel and said to her, "David has sent us to you to take you as his wife."

**41** She got up and bowed with her face to the ground and said, "Here is your servant, a slave[^34] ready to wash the feet of my lord's servants."

**42** Abigail quickly got up and mounted a donkey, with her five young women attending her.[^35] She followed David's messengers and became his wife.

**43** David had also married Ahinoam of Jezreel, and both of them became his wives.

**44** But Saul had given his daughter Michal, David's wife, to Palti[^36] son of Laish, who was from Gallim.

[^1]: The Septuagint reads "Maon".
[^2]: Hebrew *ma'aseh*, "work", here his property and flocks.
[^3]: A town in the hill country of Judah (Joshua 15:55), not Mount Carmel. Also later in the verse and in verses 5, 7 and 40.
[^4]: *Nabal* means "fool"; see verse 25.
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written *klbw*, perhaps "like his heart", but read *kalibbi*, "a Calebite", one of the clan of Caleb, which lived around Hebron (Joshua 14:13–14). The word also recalls *kelev*, "dog".
[^6]: Literally "ask him in my name about *shalom* (well-being)".
[^7]: Literally "Say thus: 'To life!'" The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^8]: Literally "a good day".
[^9]: Literally "whatever your hand finds".
[^10]: Literally "to bless".
[^11]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain: literally "he swooped on them".
[^12]: Literally "a son of *beliyya'al*"; see 2:12.
[^13]: A seah is a dry measure of about 7 quarts (7.3 liters), a third of an ephah.
[^14]: Literally "Thus may God do to the enemies of David, and thus may he add"; see 3:17. "David's enemies" probably stands for David himself, to avoid calling down a curse on himself; compare 20:16. The Septuagint reads "May God deal with David".
[^15]: Literally "one who urinates against a wall", a crude expression for a male. Also in verse 34.
[^16]: Hebrew *amah*, a female servant or slave, a humble way of speaking of oneself (1:11). Also in verses 25, 28, 31 and 41.
[^17]: Literally "speak in your ears".
[^18]: Literally "man of *beliyya'al*"; see 2:12.
[^19]: Hebrew *nevalah*, "folly, disgraceful behavior", a play on Nabal's name.
[^20]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^21]: Literally "as your *nephesh* lives".
[^22]: Literally "and your hand saving you". Also in verses 31 and 33.
[^23]: Literally "blessing".
[^24]: Here Hebrew *shiphchah*, "female servant", with the same meaning as *amah*.
[^25]: Literally "who walk at the feet of my lord".
[^26]: Literally "a sure house", that is, a family line of kings; compare 2:35.
[^27]: Literally "the bundle of life", perhaps a pouch in which valuables were kept safe.
[^28]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, ruler"; see 9:16.
[^29]: Literally "a staggering and a stumbling of heart".
[^30]: Literally "lifted up your face".
[^31]: Literally "Nabal's heart was good on him".
[^32]: Literally "nothing small or great".
[^33]: Literally "when the wine had gone out of Nabal".
[^34]: Hebrew *shiphchah*, "female servant, slave".
[^35]: Literally "who walked at her feet".
[^36]: Called Paltiel in 2 Samuel 3:15.
