**1** There was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim,[^1] from the hill country of Ephraim. His name was Elkanah son of Jeroham, son of Elihu, son of Tohu, son of Zuph, an Ephraimite.[^2]

**2** He had two wives. The name of one was Hannah, and the name of the other was Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no children.

**3** Year after year[^3] this man went up from his town to worship and to sacrifice to the LORD of Armies[^4] at Shiloh. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were priests of the LORD there.

**4** On the day Elkanah sacrificed, he would give portions to his wife Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.

**5** But to Hannah he would give a double portion,[^5] because he loved Hannah, though the LORD had closed her womb.

**6** Her rival[^6] would provoke her bitterly, to upset her,[^7] because the LORD had closed her womb.

**7** So it went on year after year.[^8] Whenever she went up to the house of the LORD, her rival would provoke her, and she would weep and not eat.

**8** Her husband Elkanah said to her, "Hannah, why are you weeping? Why aren't you eating? Why are you so sad?[^9] Am I not better to you than ten sons?"

**9** After they had eaten and drunk at Shiloh, Hannah got up. Now Eli the priest was sitting on his seat by the doorpost of the temple[^10] of the LORD.

**10** She was deeply distressed,[^11] and she prayed to the LORD and wept bitterly.

**11** She made a vow and said, "LORD of Armies, if you will only look on the misery of your servant[^12] and remember me, and not forget your servant, but give your servant a son,[^13] then I will give him to the LORD for all the days of his life, and no razor will ever touch his head."[^14]

**12** As she went on praying before the LORD, Eli was watching her mouth.

**13** Hannah was speaking in her heart. Only her lips were moving, and her voice could not be heard. So Eli thought she was drunk.

**14** Eli said to her, "How long are you going to be drunk? Put away your wine!"

**15** Hannah answered, "No, my lord. I am a deeply troubled woman.[^15] I have not drunk wine or beer.[^16] I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD.

**16** Do not take your servant for a worthless woman.[^17] I have been speaking all this time out of my great anguish and grief."[^18]

**17** Eli answered, "Go in peace. May the God of Israel grant you what you have asked of him."

**18** She said, "May your servant[^19] find favor in your eyes." Then the woman went her way and ate, and her face was no longer sad.[^20]

**19** They got up early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD. Then they went back home to Ramah. Elkanah slept with[^21] his wife Hannah, and the LORD remembered her.

**20** In due time[^22] Hannah became pregnant and gave birth to a son. She named him Samuel,[^23] "because I asked the LORD for him."

**21** The man Elkanah went up with all his household to offer to the LORD the yearly sacrifice and to fulfill his vow.[^24]

**22** But Hannah did not go up. She said to her husband, "Not until the boy is weaned. Then I will bring him, so that he may appear before the LORD and stay there forever."[^25]

**23** Her husband Elkanah said to her, "Do what seems best to you. Stay here until you have weaned him. Only, may the LORD confirm his word."[^26] So the woman stayed and nursed her son until she weaned him.

**24** When she had weaned him, she took him up with her, along with three bulls,[^27] an ephah[^28] of flour and a skin of wine, and brought him to the house of the LORD at Shiloh. The boy was young.[^29]

**25** They slaughtered the bull, and they brought the boy to Eli.

**26** She said, "Pardon me, my lord! As surely as you live,[^30] my lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you, praying to the LORD.

**27** I prayed for this boy, and the LORD has given me what I asked of him.

**28** So I in turn have given him to the LORD.[^31] For as long as he lives, he is given over[^32] to the LORD." And he worshiped the LORD there.[^33]

[^1]: Hebrew *ha-Ramatayim Tsophim*, perhaps "Ramathaim of the Zuphites", the clan of Zuph named later in the verse. The town is called Ramah in verse 19 and elsewhere.
[^2]: Hebrew *Ephrati*, here meaning someone from Ephraim (compare Judges 12:5). Elsewhere the same word means someone from Ephrathah, that is, Bethlehem (17:12).
[^3]: Literally "from days to days".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. This is the first time the title appears in the Bible. Also in verse 11.
[^5]: Hebrew *manah achat appayim*, literally "one portion of two faces". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "a double portion", "a special portion", or "only one portion". The Septuagint reads "one portion, because she had no child".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsarah*, a rival wife, from a root meaning "distress". Also in verse 7.
[^7]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Literally "to make her thunder", that is, perhaps, to make her agitated or to make her complain.
[^8]: Literally "And so he would do year after year", perhaps referring to Elkanah's yearly sacrifice.
[^9]: Literally "Why is your heart bad?"
[^10]: Hebrew *heikhal*, "palace, temple", here the sanctuary of the LORD at Shiloh.
[^11]: Literally "bitter of *nephesh*".
[^12]: Hebrew *amah*, a female servant or slave, a humble way of speaking of oneself. Also later in the verse and in verse 16.
[^13]: Literally "seed of men".
[^14]: Literally "a razor will not go up on his head". Compare the Nazirite vow (Numbers 6:5) and Samson (Judges 13:5). The Septuagint adds "and he will not drink wine or beer".
[^15]: Literally "hard of spirit".
[^16]: Or "fermented drink"; brewed from grain. Hebrew *shekhar*.
[^17]: Literally "Do not put your servant before a daughter of *beliyya'al*", that is, a worthless or wicked woman.
[^18]: Literally "out of the greatness of my complaint and my provocation I have spoken until now".
[^19]: Hebrew *shiphchah*, "female servant", with the same meaning as *amah* in verses 11 and 16.
[^20]: Literally "and her face was no longer to her". The Septuagint reads "and her face no longer fell".
[^21]: Literally "knew", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^22]: Literally "at the turning of the days".
[^23]: Hebrew *Shemu'el*. Hannah's explanation plays on the verb *sha'al*, "ask", which sounds like part of the name. The name itself probably means "name of God" or "heard by God".
[^24]: Literally "and his vow", that is, the sacrifice he had vowed.
[^25]: 4QSam(a), a Dead Sea Scroll of Samuel, adds "and I will give him as a Nazirite forever, all the days of his life".
[^26]: 4QSam(a) and the Septuagint read "what has come out of your mouth", that is, Hannah's own vow.
[^27]: 4QSam(a) and the Septuagint read "a three-year-old bull", which fits "the bull" in verse 25.
[^28]: An ephah is a dry measure of about 20 quarts (22 liters).
[^29]: Literally "And the boy was a boy".
[^30]: Literally "By the life of your *nephesh*".
[^31]: Literally "And I also have made him asked to the LORD", that is, lent or given him. The verb is a form of *sha'al*, "ask".
[^32]: Hebrew *sha'ul*, "asked for, lent", the same word as the name Saul.
[^33]: The Hebrew does not say who "he" is: Elkanah, or the boy Samuel.
