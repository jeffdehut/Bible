**1** Saul was ... years old[^1] when he became king, and he reigned over Israel for ... two years.[^2]

**2** Saul chose for himself three thousand men of Israel. Two thousand were with Saul at Michmash and in the hill country of Bethel, and a thousand were with Jonathan at Gibeah of Benjamin. The rest of the people he sent home, each to his tent.

**3** Jonathan struck down the Philistine garrison[^3] at Geba, and the Philistines heard about it. Then Saul had the trumpet[^4] blown throughout the land, saying, "Let the Hebrews hear!"[^5]

**4** All Israel heard the news: "Saul has struck down the Philistine garrison, and now Israel has become a stench to the Philistines." And the people were called out to follow Saul to Gilgal.

**5** The Philistines gathered to fight against Israel: thirty thousand chariots,[^6] six thousand horsemen, and troops as many as the sand on the seashore. They went up and camped at Michmash, east of Beth-aven.

**6** When the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble, because the troops were hard pressed, the people hid in caves and thickets, among the rocks, and in holes[^7] and cisterns.

**7** Some Hebrews even crossed the Jordan[^8] to the land of Gad and Gilead. Saul was still at Gilgal, and all the troops who followed him were trembling.

**8** He waited seven days, until the time Samuel had set,[^9] but Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the troops began to scatter from Saul.

**9** So Saul said, "Bring me the burnt offering and the fellowship offerings."[^10] And he offered the burnt offering.

**10** Just as he finished offering the burnt offering, Samuel arrived. Saul went out to meet him and greet him.[^11]

**11** Samuel said, "What have you done?" Saul said, "When I saw that the troops were scattering from me, and that you did not come at the set time, and that the Philistines were gathering at Michmash,

**12** I thought, 'Now the Philistines will come down against me at Gilgal, and I have not sought the favor of Yahweh.'[^12] So I forced myself to offer the burnt offering."

**13** Samuel said to Saul, "You have acted foolishly. You have not kept the command Yahweh your God gave you. If you had, Yahweh would now have established your kingdom over Israel forever.

**14** But now your kingdom will not last. Yahweh has sought out for himself a man after his own heart, and Yahweh has appointed him ruler[^13] over his people, because you have not kept what Yahweh commanded you."

**15** Then Samuel got up and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin.[^14] Saul counted the troops who were still with him, about six hundred men.

**16** Saul and his son Jonathan, and the troops who were with them, were staying at Geba of Benjamin, while the Philistines camped at Michmash.

**17** Raiders[^15] came out of the Philistine camp in three companies. One company turned toward Ophrah, to the land of Shual;

**18** another company turned toward Beth-horon; and another company turned toward the border that overlooks the Valley of Zeboim, toward the wilderness.

**19** No blacksmith could be found in the whole land of Israel, for the Philistines had said,[^16] "Otherwise the Hebrews will make swords or spears."

**20** So all Israel went down to the Philistines to have each man's plowshare, mattock, axe and sickle[^17] sharpened.

**21** The charge was a pim[^18] for plowshares and mattocks, and a third of a shekel for pitchforks and axes, and for setting goads.[^19]

**22** So on the day of battle no sword or spear was to be found in the hands of any of the troops with Saul and Jonathan. Only Saul and his son Jonathan had them.

**23** Meanwhile, a Philistine outpost[^20] had gone out to the pass at Michmash.

[^1]: The number is missing from the Hebrew text, which reads literally "Saul was a son of a year", the usual way of giving someone's age but without the number. The verse is missing from most manuscripts of the Septuagint; a few later Greek manuscripts read "thirty years old".
[^2]: Literally "and two years he reigned over Israel". Part of the number seems to be missing. Acts 13:21 gives the length of Saul's reign as forty years.
[^3]: Hebrew *netsiv*; see 10:5. Also in verse 4.
[^4]: Hebrew *shofar*, a ram's horn.
[^5]: The Septuagint reads "The slaves have rebelled!"
[^6]: Some manuscripts of the Septuagint, and the Syriac, read "three thousand".
[^7]: Hebrew *tseriachim*, perhaps tombs, cellars or rock shelters.
[^8]: In the Hebrew, "Hebrews" (*ivrim*) and "crossed" (*averu*) sound alike.
[^9]: Literally "for the appointed time which Samuel". A word such as "had set" seems to be missing.
[^10]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings"; see 10:8.
[^11]: Literally "to bless him".
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Literally "I have not soothed the face of Yahweh".
[^13]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, ruler"; see 9:16.
[^14]: The Septuagint reads "went up from Gilgal and went on his way. The rest of the people went up after Saul to meet the soldiers, and they came from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin."
[^15]: Literally "the destroyer", that is, a raiding force.
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "the Philistine had said" but read "the Philistines had said".
[^17]: The Hebrew names of these tools are uncertain. The last word is nearly the same as the word for "plowshare"; the Septuagint reads "sickle".
[^18]: Hebrew *pim*, a weight of about two-thirds of a shekel, about 1/4 ounce (8 grams). The word was not understood until stone weights inscribed with it were found by archaeologists. A shekel is about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams).
[^19]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult, and the meaning of some words is uncertain.
[^20]: Hebrew *matsav*, a garrison or post.
