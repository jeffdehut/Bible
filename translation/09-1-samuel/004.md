**1** And the word of Samuel came to all Israel. Israel went out to fight against the Philistines.[^1] They camped at Ebenezer,[^2] and the Philistines camped at Aphek.

**2** The Philistines drew up their lines to face Israel. The battle spread,[^3] and Israel was defeated by the Philistines, who struck down about four thousand men on the battlefield.

**3** When the troops came back to the camp, the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD[^4] let us be defeated today by the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the LORD's covenant here from Shiloh, so that it may come among us and save us from the power of our enemies."[^5]

**4** So the people sent men to Shiloh, and they brought from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of Armies,[^6] who is enthroned on[^7] the cherubim.[^8] The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.

**5** When the ark of the LORD's covenant came into the camp, all Israel gave a great shout, so that the ground shook.

**6** When the Philistines heard the noise of the shouting, they said, "What is this great shouting in the camp of the Hebrews?" When they learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp,

**7** the Philistines were afraid, for they said, "God has come into the camp." They said, "We are in trouble![^9] Nothing like this has ever happened before.[^10]

**8** We are in trouble! Who will rescue us from the power of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with every kind of plague in the wilderness.

**9** Be strong, Philistines, and act like men, or you will be slaves to the Hebrews as they have been slaves to you. Act like men and fight!"

**10** So the Philistines fought, and Israel was defeated, and every man fled to his tent. The slaughter was very great: thirty thousand foot soldiers of Israel fell.

**11** The ark of God was captured, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died.

**12** A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh that same day, with his clothes torn and dirt on his head.

**13** When he arrived, Eli was sitting on his seat beside the road, watching, because his heart trembled for the ark of God. When the man came into the town and told the news, the whole town cried out.

**14** When Eli heard the sound of the outcry, he asked, "What is this uproar?" The man hurried over and told Eli.

**15** Eli was ninety-eight years old, and his eyes were set,[^11] so that he could not see.

**16** The man said to Eli, "I am the one who has come from the battle line. I fled from the battle line today." Eli said, "What happened, my son?"

**17** The messenger answered, "Israel fled from the Philistines, and there has also been a great slaughter among the troops. Your two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead too, and the ark of God has been captured."

**18** When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward off his seat beside the gate. His neck was broken, and he died, for he was an old man and heavy. He had judged[^12] Israel for forty years.[^13]

**19** His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and about to give birth. When she heard the news that the ark of God had been captured and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she crouched down and gave birth, for her labor pains came on her suddenly.[^14]

**20** As she was dying, the women standing beside her said, "Do not be afraid, for you have given birth to a son." But she did not answer or pay any attention.[^15]

**21** She named the boy Ichabod,[^16] saying, "Glory has gone into exile[^17] from Israel," because the ark of God had been captured, and because of her father-in-law and her husband.

**22** She said, "Glory has gone into exile from Israel, for the ark of God has been captured."

[^1]: The Septuagint reads "In those days the Philistines gathered together for war against Israel, and Israel went out to meet them for battle".
[^2]: Hebrew *ha-even ha-ezer*, "the stone of help". The place is named in 7:12.
[^3]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Literally "the battle was spread out".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Or "so that he may come among us and save us". Literally "from the palm of our enemies".
[^6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^7]: Or "who sits on", "who dwells between".
[^8]: Winged creatures. Two cherubim of gold faced each other on the cover of the ark (Exodus 25:18–22).
[^9]: Literally "Woe to us!" Also in verse 8.
[^10]: Literally "yesterday and the day before".
[^11]: Literally "his eyes stood", that is, he was blind.
[^12]: Or "led". In the book of Judges, the leaders of Israel are said to "judge" Israel.
[^13]: The Septuagint reads "twenty years".
[^14]: Literally "her pains turned on her".
[^15]: Literally "set her heart".
[^16]: *Ichabod* probably means "Where is the glory?" or "No glory".
[^17]: Hebrew *galah*, "depart, go into exile".
