**1** After the Philistines had captured the ark of God, they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

**2** The Philistines took the ark of God, brought it into the temple of Dagon[^1] and set it up beside Dagon.

**3** When the people of Ashdod got up early the next day, there was Dagon, fallen face down on the ground before the ark of Yahweh.[^2] They took Dagon and put him back in his place.

**4** But when they got up early the next morning, there was Dagon, fallen face down on the ground before the ark of Yahweh. Dagon's head and both his hands lay broken off on the threshold. Only Dagon's body was left.[^3]

**5** That is why to this day neither the priests of Dagon nor anyone who enters the temple of Dagon steps on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod.

**6** The hand of Yahweh was heavy on the people of Ashdod. He devastated them and struck them with tumors,[^4] both Ashdod and its territory.[^5]

**7** When the men of Ashdod saw what was happening, they said, "The ark of the God of Israel must not stay with us, for his hand is hard on us and on Dagon our god."

**8** So they sent for all the rulers of the Philistines[^6] and gathered them together, and asked, "What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" They answered, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be moved to Gath." So they moved the ark of the God of Israel there.

**9** But after they had moved it, the hand of Yahweh was against the city, and there was a very great panic. He struck the people of the city, from the least to the greatest, and tumors broke out on them.[^7]

**10** So they sent the ark of God to Ekron. But as the ark of God came into Ekron, the people of Ekron cried out, "They have brought the ark of the God of Israel around to us to kill us and our people!"[^8]

**11** So they sent for all the rulers of the Philistines and gathered them together, and said, "Send the ark of the God of Israel away. Let it go back to its own place, so that it will not kill us and our people." For a deadly panic had spread through the whole city; the hand of God was very heavy there.

**12** Those who did not die were struck with tumors, and the cry of the city went up to heaven.

[^1]: Literally "the house of Dagon", also in verse 5. Dagon was a god worshiped by the Philistines and by other peoples of the region, probably a god of grain.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "only Dagon was left on him".
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written *ofalim*, "swellings", but read *techorim*, "tumors", a less coarse word. Either may refer to the swellings of a plague. Also in verses 9 and 12.
[^5]: The Septuagint adds "And mice sprang up in the middle of their land, and there was a great panic of death in the city." Compare the gold mice in 6:4–5.
[^6]: Hebrew *seranim*, a word used only of the rulers of the five Philistine cities (6:16–17). Also in verse 11.
[^7]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb translated "broke out" is uncertain.
[^8]: Literally "to me ... to kill me and my people". Also in verse 11.
