**1** David thought to himself,[^1] "One of these days I will be swept away by the hand of Saul. There is nothing better for me than to escape to the land of the Philistines. Then Saul will give up searching for me anywhere in the territory of Israel, and I will escape from his hand."

**2** So David set out and crossed over, he and the six hundred men with him, to Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath.

**3** David stayed with Achish at Gath, he and his men, each with his household. David had his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail of Carmel, Nabal's widow.[^2]

**4** When Saul was told that David had fled to Gath, he no longer searched for him.

**5** Then David said to Achish, "If I have found favor in your eyes, let me be given a place in one of the country towns, so that I can live there. Why should your servant live in the royal city with you?"

**6** So that day Achish gave him Ziklag. That is why Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day.

**7** The length of time David lived in the territory of the Philistines was a year and four months.[^3]

**8** David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Gizrites[^4] and the Amalekites. These peoples had lived in the land from ancient times, as you go toward Shur and on to the land of Egypt.

**9** Whenever David attacked the land, he left no man or woman alive, but he took the sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels and clothing, and then came back to Achish.

**10** When Achish asked, "Where did you raid today?"[^5] David would say, "Against the Negev[^6] of Judah," or "Against the Negev of the Jerahmeelites," or "Against the Negev of the Kenites."

**11** David left no man or woman alive to be brought to Gath, for he thought, "They might report about us and say, 'This is what David did.'" This was his practice[^7] the whole time he lived in the territory of the Philistines.

**12** Achish trusted David and thought, "He has made himself utterly hateful[^8] to his people Israel, so he will be my servant forever."

[^1]: Literally "said to his heart".
[^2]: Literally "Nabal's wife".
[^3]: Literally "days and four months". "Days" sometimes means a year. The Septuagint reads "four months".
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "Girzites" but read "Gizrites".
[^5]: Literally "Have you not raided today?" The Septuagint reads "Against whom did you raid today?"
[^6]: The dry country in the south of the land. Also later in the verse.
[^7]: Hebrew *mishpat*, "custom, way of acting". Or the words of the report continue: "This is what David did, and this has been his practice the whole time ..."
[^8]: Literally "He has made himself stink".
