**1** Samuel said to Saul, "The LORD[^1] sent me to anoint you king over his people Israel. So now listen to the words of the LORD.

**2** This is what the LORD of Armies[^2] says: I will punish[^3] what Amalek did to Israel, how they opposed them on the way when they came up out of Egypt.[^4]

**3** Now go and strike Amalek. Devote to destruction[^5] everything that belongs to them. Do not spare them, but put them to death, men and women, children and babies, oxen and sheep, camels and donkeys."

**4** So Saul summoned the troops and counted them at Telaim: two hundred thousand foot soldiers, and ten thousand men of Judah.

**5** Saul came to the city of Amalek and set an ambush[^6] in the valley.

**6** Saul said to the Kenites, "Go, leave, get away from among the Amalekites, so that I do not destroy you along with them. For you showed faithful love to all the Israelites when they came up out of Egypt." So the Kenites withdrew from among the Amalekites.

**7** Then Saul struck down the Amalekites from Havilah all the way to Shur,[^7] which is east of Egypt.

**8** He captured Agag king of the Amalekites alive, and he devoted all the people to destruction with the sword.

**9** But Saul and the troops spared Agag, and the best of the sheep and cattle, the fatlings[^8] and the lambs, and everything that was good. They were unwilling to devote them to destruction. But everything that was despised and worthless they devoted to destruction.

**10** Then the word of the LORD came to Samuel:

**11** "I regret[^9] that I made Saul king, for he has turned back from following me and has not carried out my words." Samuel was angry,[^10] and he cried out to the LORD all night.

**12** Early in the morning Samuel got up to meet Saul. Samuel was told, "Saul went to Carmel, and there he set up a monument[^11] for himself. Then he turned and went on, down to Gilgal."

**13** When Samuel came to Saul, Saul said to him, "May the LORD bless you! I have carried out the word of the LORD."

**14** But Samuel said, "Then what is this bleating of sheep in my ears, and the lowing of cattle that I hear?"

**15** Saul said, "They brought them from the Amalekites. The troops spared the best of the sheep and cattle to sacrifice to the LORD your God, but the rest we devoted to destruction."

**16** Samuel said to Saul, "Stop! Let me tell you what the LORD said to me last night." He said to him,[^12] "Tell me."

**17** Samuel said, "Though you were small in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel? The LORD anointed you king over Israel.

**18** The LORD sent you on a mission[^13] and said, 'Go and devote the sinners, the Amalekites, to destruction. Fight against them until you have wiped them out.'

**19** Why then did you not obey the LORD?[^14] Why did you pounce on the plunder and do what was evil in the sight of the LORD?"

**20** Saul said to Samuel, "But I did obey the LORD. I went on the mission the LORD sent me on. I brought back Agag king of the Amalekites, and I devoted the Amalekites to destruction.

**21** But the troops took sheep and cattle from the plunder, the best of what was devoted to destruction,[^15] to sacrifice to the LORD your God at Gilgal."

**22** Samuel said, "Does the LORD delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as much as in obeying the voice of the LORD? To obey is better than sacrifice, and to pay attention is better than the fat of rams.

**23** For rebellion is like the sin of divination, and stubbornness like wickedness and household gods.[^16] Because you have rejected the word of the LORD, he has rejected you as king."

**24** Saul said to Samuel, "I have sinned. I have gone against the LORD's command[^17] and your words, because I was afraid of the troops and listened to them.

**25** Now, please forgive my sin and come back with me, so that I may worship the LORD."

**26** Samuel said to Saul, "I will not come back with you. For you have rejected the word of the LORD, and the LORD has rejected you from being king over Israel."

**27** As Samuel turned to leave, Saul[^18] seized the hem of his robe, and it tore.

**28** Samuel said to him, "The LORD has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today and given it to a neighbor of yours who is better than you.

**29** And the Glory of Israel[^19] does not lie or change his mind,[^20] for he is not a human being, that he should change his mind."

**30** Saul said, "I have sinned. But please honor me now before the elders of my people and before Israel. Come back with me, so that I may worship the LORD your God."

**31** So Samuel went back with Saul, and Saul worshiped the LORD.

**32** Then Samuel said, "Bring me Agag king of the Amalekites." Agag came to him in chains.[^21] Agag said, "Surely the bitterness of death is past."[^22]

**33** But Samuel said, "As your sword has made women childless, so your mother will be the most childless of women." And Samuel hacked Agag to pieces[^23] before the LORD at Gilgal.

**34** Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his home at Gibeah of Saul.

**35** Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, but Samuel mourned for Saul. And the LORD regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^3]: Hebrew *paqad*, "attend to", here in the sense of punishing. Or "I have taken note of".
[^4]: See Exodus 17:8–16 and Deuteronomy 25:17–19.
[^5]: Hebrew *charam*, "devote to destruction": give up to God by destroying completely. Also in verses 8–9, 15, 18 and 20.
[^6]: Or "fought". The form of the Hebrew verb is unusual.
[^7]: Literally "as you come to Shur".
[^8]: Hebrew *mishnim*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "the second best", or, with a slight change, "the fat ones".
[^9]: Hebrew *nicham*, "regret, be sorry, change one's mind". Also in verses 29 and 35.
[^10]: Or "distressed".
[^11]: Literally "a hand".
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "They said to him" but read "He said to him".
[^13]: Literally "on a way". Also in verse 20.
[^14]: Literally "listen to the voice of the LORD". Also in verse 20.
[^15]: Hebrew *cherem*, what is devoted to destruction.
[^16]: Hebrew *teraphim*, images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19). The Hebrew of this line is difficult; literally "and pushing (presumption) is wickedness and *teraphim*".
[^17]: Literally "the mouth of the LORD".
[^18]: Literally "he". The Septuagint reads "Saul".
[^19]: Hebrew *netsach yisra'el*, perhaps "the Glory of Israel" or "the Eternal One of Israel".
[^20]: Hebrew *nicham*, the verb translated "regret" in verses 11 and 35.
[^21]: Hebrew *ma'adannot*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "in chains", "cheerfully" or "trembling".
[^22]: Or "Surely the bitterness of death has come."
[^23]: The Hebrew verb is found only here; probably "cut to pieces".
