**1** Samuel said to all Israel, "I have listened to you in everything you said to me, and I have set a king over you.

**2** Now here is the king, walking before you.[^1] As for me, I am old and gray, and my sons are here with you. I have walked before you from my youth until this day.

**3** Here I am. Testify against me before the LORD[^2] and before his anointed:[^3] Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken? Whom have I cheated? Whom have I oppressed? From whose hand have I taken a bribe to make me shut my eyes?[^4] If I have, I will pay it back to you."

**4** They said, "You have not cheated us or oppressed us, and you have not taken anything from anyone's hand."

**5** He said to them, "The LORD is witness against you, and his anointed is witness this day, that you have found nothing in my hand." They said,[^5] "He is witness."

**6** Samuel said to the people, "It is the LORD who appointed[^6] Moses and Aaron and brought your ancestors up out of the land of Egypt.

**7** So now stand here, so that I may plead my case against you before the LORD concerning all the righteous acts[^7] of the LORD, which he has done for you and for your ancestors.

**8** When Jacob went into Egypt, your ancestors cried out to the LORD, and the LORD sent Moses and Aaron, who brought your ancestors out of Egypt and settled them in this place.

**9** But they forgot the LORD their God, so he sold them into the power of Sisera, the commander of the army of Hazor, and into the power of the Philistines and of the king of Moab, and they fought against them.

**10** Then they cried out to the LORD and said,[^8] 'We have sinned, for we have forsaken the LORD and served the Baals and the Ashtoreths. But now rescue us from the power of our enemies, and we will serve you.'

**11** So the LORD sent Jerubbaal,[^9] Bedan,[^10] Jephthah and Samuel, and he rescued you from the power of your enemies all around you, and you lived in safety.

**12** But when you saw that Nahash king of the Ammonites was coming against you, you said to me, 'No, a king must reign over us,' though the LORD your God was your king.

**13** Now here is the king you have chosen, the one you asked for. Look, the LORD has set a king over you.

**14** If you fear the LORD and serve him and listen to his voice, and do not rebel against the LORD's command,[^11] then both you and the king who reigns over you will follow the LORD your God.[^12]

**15** But if you do not listen to the LORD's voice, and rebel against the LORD's command, the hand of the LORD will be against you, as it was against your ancestors.[^13]

**16** Now then, stand still and see this great thing the LORD is about to do before your eyes.

**17** Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call on the LORD, and he will send thunder[^14] and rain. Then you will know and see how great is the evil you have done in the sight of the LORD by asking for a king for yourselves."

**18** Then Samuel called on the LORD, and the LORD sent thunder and rain that day. All the people greatly feared the LORD and Samuel.

**19** All the people said to Samuel, "Pray to the LORD your God for your servants, so that we will not die, for we have added to all our sins this evil of asking for a king for ourselves."

**20** Samuel said to the people, "Do not be afraid. You have done all this evil, yet do not turn aside from following the LORD, but serve the LORD with all your heart.

**21** Do not turn aside after empty things,[^15] which cannot help you or rescue you, because they are empty.

**22** For the sake of his great name the LORD will not abandon his people, because the LORD has been pleased to make you his own people.

**23** As for me, far be it from me to sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you. I will teach you the good and right way.

**24** Only fear the LORD and serve him faithfully[^16] with all your heart. Consider what great things he has done for you.

**25** But if you persist in doing evil, both you and your king will be swept away."

[^1]: Or "leading you".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *meshicho*, "his anointed one", here Saul. Also in verse 5; see 2:10.
[^4]: Literally "to blind my eyes with it". The Septuagint reads "a bribe, or even a pair of sandals? Testify against me".
[^5]: Literally "he said", the people speaking as one.
[^6]: Literally "made". The Hebrew begins abruptly, "The LORD, who made Moses and Aaron ..."; the Septuagint reads "The LORD is witness, who made Moses and Aaron ...".
[^7]: Hebrew *tsidqot*, "righteous acts", here the LORD's acts of rescue.
[^8]: The Hebrew text is written "he said" but read "they said".
[^9]: Another name for Gideon (Judges 6:32).
[^10]: No judge by this name is known from the book of Judges. The Septuagint reads "Barak" (Judges 4–5).
[^11]: Literally "against the mouth of the LORD". Also in verse 15.
[^12]: Or "and if both you and the king who reigns over you follow the LORD your God, (all will be well)". Literally "and you will be, both you and the king who reigns over you, after the LORD your God".
[^13]: Literally "against you and against your fathers". The Septuagint reads "against you and against your king".
[^14]: Literally "voices". Also in verse 18. Rain was very rare at the time of the wheat harvest, in early summer.
[^15]: Hebrew *tohu*, "emptiness", the word translated "formless" in Genesis 1:2. Also at the end of the verse.
[^16]: Literally "in truth".
