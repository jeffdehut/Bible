**1** When David had finished speaking with Saul, Jonathan's life was bound up with David's life, and Jonathan loved him as himself.[^1]

**2** From that day Saul kept David with him and did not let him go back to his father's house.

**3** Jonathan and David made a covenant, because Jonathan loved him as himself.

**4** Jonathan took off the robe he was wearing and gave it to David, along with his tunic,[^2] and even his sword, his bow and his belt.

**5** David went out wherever Saul sent him, and he was successful.[^3] So Saul put him in command of the soldiers. This pleased all the troops, and Saul's servants as well.

**6** As they were coming home, when David returned from killing the Philistine, the women came out from all the towns of Israel to meet King Saul, singing and dancing, with tambourines, with songs of joy, and with three-stringed instruments.[^4]

**7** As they danced, the women sang in turn:[^5] "Saul has struck down his thousands,[^6] and David his tens of thousands."

**8** Saul was very angry. This saying displeased him. He said, "They have credited David with tens of thousands, but me with only thousands. What more can he have but the kingdom?"

**9** From that day on Saul kept a suspicious eye on David.[^7]

**10** The next day an evil spirit from God rushed upon Saul, and he raved[^8] inside the house while David was playing the lyre,[^9] as he did day after day. Saul had a spear in his hand.

**11** Saul hurled[^10] the spear, thinking, "I will pin David to the wall." But David escaped from him twice.

**12** Saul was afraid of David, because the LORD[^11] was with David but had left Saul.

**13** So Saul removed him from his presence and made him a commander of a thousand. David led the troops in their campaigns.[^12]

**14** David was successful in all he did,[^13] and the LORD was with him.

**15** When Saul saw how very successful he was, he was in dread of him.

**16** But all Israel and Judah loved David, because he led them in their campaigns.

**17** Saul said to David, "Here is my older daughter Merab. I will give her to you as your wife. Only be a valiant warrior[^14] for me and fight the LORD's battles." For Saul thought, "I will not raise my hand against him. Let the Philistines do it."[^15]

**18** David said to Saul, "Who am I, and what is my family,[^16] my father's clan in Israel, that I should become the king's son-in-law?"

**19** But when the time came for Merab, Saul's daughter, to be given to David, she was given as wife to Adriel the Meholathite.

**20** Now Michal, Saul's daughter, loved David. When Saul was told, he was pleased.[^17]

**21** Saul thought, "I will give her to him, so that she may be a snare to him and the Philistines may strike him down." So Saul said to David a second time,[^18] "Today you will become my son-in-law."

**22** Saul ordered his servants, "Speak to David privately and say, 'Look, the king is pleased with you, and all his servants love you. So now become the king's son-in-law.'"

**23** Saul's servants repeated these words to David. But David said, "Do you think it is a small thing to become the king's son-in-law, when I am a poor man and of no importance?"

**24** Saul's servants told him, "This is what David said."

**25** Saul said, "This is what you are to say to David: 'The king wants no bride-price[^19] except a hundred Philistine foreskins, to take vengeance on the king's enemies.'" For Saul planned to have David fall by the hands of the Philistines.

**26** When his servants told David these words, David was pleased to become the king's son-in-law. Before the time had run out,[^20]

**27** David got up and went, he and his men, and struck down two hundred Philistines.[^21] David brought their foreskins and presented the full number to the king, so that he might become the king's son-in-law. So Saul gave him his daughter Michal as his wife.

**28** When Saul saw and knew that the LORD was with David, and that his daughter Michal loved him,[^22]

**29** Saul became even more afraid of David. And Saul was David's enemy for the rest of his life.[^23]

**30** The commanders of the Philistines went out to battle, and as often as they went out, David had more success than all Saul's servants, and his name became highly honored.

[^1]: Literally "the *nephesh* of Jonathan was bound to the *nephesh* of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own *nephesh*". Also in verse 3.
[^2]: Hebrew *madav*, his garment, probably a military tunic; see 17:38.
[^3]: Hebrew *maskil*, "acting wisely, succeeding". Also in verses 14, 15 and 30.
[^4]: Hebrew *shalishim*, an instrument of uncertain kind, perhaps with three strings or three corners.
[^5]: Literally "answered", that is, sang to one another in turn.
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "his thousand" but read "his thousands".
[^7]: Literally "Saul was eyeing David".
[^8]: Literally "prophesied"; see 10:5.
[^9]: Literally "playing with his hand"; see 16:23.
[^10]: Or "raised".
[^11]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^12]: Literally "he went out and came in before the people". Also in verse 16.
[^13]: Literally "in all his ways".
[^14]: Hebrew *ben chayil*, "a son of strength".
[^15]: Literally "Let my hand not be on him, but let the hand of the Philistines be on him." Also in verse 21.
[^16]: Hebrew *chayyai*, "my life", or perhaps "my kin".
[^17]: Literally "the thing was right in his eyes". Also in verse 26.
[^18]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "By two you will become my son-in-law today."
[^19]: Hebrew *mohar*, the payment a man made to the family of his bride (Exodus 22:15–16; 22:16–17 in most English Bibles).
[^20]: Literally "and the days were not yet full".
[^21]: The Septuagint reads "a hundred"; compare 2 Samuel 3:14.
[^22]: The Septuagint reads "and that all Israel loved him".
[^23]: Literally "all the days".
