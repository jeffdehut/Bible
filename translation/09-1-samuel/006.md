**1** The ark of the LORD[^1] was in the territory[^2] of the Philistines for seven months.

**2** The Philistines called for the priests and the diviners and asked, "What shall we do with the ark of the LORD? Tell us what we should send with it when we return it to its place."

**3** They said, "If you send away the ark of the God of Israel, do not send it away empty. You must give something back to him as a guilt offering.[^3] Then you will be healed, and you will find out why his hand has not turned away from you."[^4]

**4** They asked, "What guilt offering should we give back to him?" They said, "Five gold tumors[^5] and five gold mice,[^6] for the number of the rulers of the Philistines, since the same plague struck all of you[^7] and your rulers.

**5** Make images of your tumors and images of your mice that are ruining the land, and give glory to the God of Israel. Perhaps he will lift his hand from you, from your gods and from your land.[^8]

**6** Why should you harden[^9] your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? When he had dealt harshly with them,[^10] did they not let them go, and they left?

**7** So now, get a new cart ready, and two cows that have calves and have never been yoked. Hitch the cows to the cart, but take their calves away from them, back home.

**8** Take the ark of the LORD and put it on the cart. Put the gold objects that you are giving back to him as a guilt offering in a box beside it. Then send it off and let it go.

**9** Watch it. If it goes up by the road to its own territory, toward Beth-shemesh, then it is he who has brought this great disaster on us. But if not, we will know that it was not his hand that struck us. It happened to us by chance."

**10** The men did so. They took two cows that had calves and hitched them to the cart, and shut up their calves at home.

**11** They put the ark of the LORD on the cart, along with the box, the gold mice and the images of their tumors.

**12** The cows went straight along the road toward Beth-shemesh. They kept to the one highway, lowing as they went, and did not turn aside to the right or to the left. The rulers of the Philistines followed them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh.

**13** The people of Beth-shemesh were harvesting wheat in the valley. When they looked up and saw the ark, they were overjoyed to see it.

**14** The cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh and stopped there. A large stone was there. They split up the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the LORD.

**15** The Levites took down the ark of the LORD and the box that was with it, which held the gold objects, and put them on the large stone. That day the people of Beth-shemesh offered burnt offerings and made sacrifices to the LORD.

**16** The five rulers of the Philistines saw this, and they went back to Ekron that same day.

**17** These are the gold tumors that the Philistines gave back as a guilt offering to the LORD: one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Ashkelon, one for Gath, one for Ekron.

**18** And the gold mice were for the number of all the Philistine towns belonging to the five rulers, from fortified cities to country villages, as far as Great Abel,[^11] where they set down the ark of the LORD, which is in the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh to this day.

**19** He struck down some of the men of Beth-shemesh, because they had looked into[^12] the ark of the LORD. He struck down seventy men among the people, fifty thousand men.[^13] The people mourned because the LORD had struck the people with a great slaughter.

**20** The men of Beth-shemesh said, "Who can stand before the LORD, this holy God? To whom will he go up from here?"[^14]

**21** So they sent messengers to the people of Kiriath-jearim, saying, "The Philistines have returned the ark of the LORD. Come down and take it up to your town."[^15]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "field".
[^3]: Hebrew *asham*, the offering made to make amends for a wrong (Leviticus 5:14–19). Also in verses 4, 8 and 17.
[^4]: In place of "and you will find out", the Septuagint reads "and atonement will be made for you".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written *ofalim*, "swellings", but read *techorim*, "tumors"; see 5:6. Also in verse 5.
[^6]: Hebrew *akhbar*, a mouse or rat. The plague may have been carried by rodents.
[^7]: Literally "all of them".
[^8]: Literally "Perhaps he will lighten his hand from on you".
[^9]: Literally "make heavy", the word used for Pharaoh's heart in Exodus.
[^10]: Or "made fools of them". Hebrew *hit'allel*, as in Exodus 10:2.
[^11]: Or "the great meadow". The Septuagint and the Targum read "the great stone" (Hebrew *even* instead of *avel*), as in verses 14–15.
[^12]: Or "looked at".
[^13]: Literally "seventy men, fifty thousand men", an unusual way of giving a number. The Septuagint begins the verse differently: "But the sons of Jeconiah did not rejoice with the men of Beth-shemesh when they saw the ark of the LORD, and he struck down seventy men of them, and fifty thousand men."
[^14]: Or "To whom will it go up from us?", that is, the ark.
[^15]: Literally "bring it up to you".
