**1** Nahash the Ammonite went up and besieged[^1] Jabesh-gilead. All the men of Jabesh said to Nahash, "Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you."

**2** But Nahash the Ammonite said to them, "I will make a covenant with you on one condition: that I gouge out the right eye of every one of you, and so bring disgrace on all Israel."

**3** The elders of Jabesh said to him, "Give us seven days, so that we may send messengers throughout the territory of Israel. If no one comes to save us, we will come out to you."[^2]

**4** When the messengers came to Gibeah of Saul and told the people this news, all the people wept aloud.

**5** Just then Saul was coming in from the field behind the oxen. Saul asked, "What is wrong with the people? Why are they weeping?" So they told him what the men of Jabesh had said.

**6** When Saul heard these words, the Spirit of God rushed upon him,[^3] and he became very angry.

**7** He took a pair of oxen, cut them into pieces, and sent them throughout the territory of Israel by the messengers, saying, "This is what will be done to the oxen of anyone who does not come out after Saul and after Samuel." Then the dread of the LORD[^4] fell on the people, and they came out as one man.

**8** He counted[^5] them at Bezek: there were three hundred thousand Israelites and thirty thousand men of Judah.[^6]

**9** They said to the messengers who had come, "Tell the men of Jabesh-gilead this: 'Tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, you will be rescued.'"[^7] When the messengers came and told the men of Jabesh, they were glad.

**10** The men of Jabesh said,[^8] "Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you can do to us whatever seems good to you."

**11** The next day Saul divided the troops into three companies. They came into the camp during the morning watch[^9] and struck down the Ammonites until the heat of the day. Those who survived were so scattered that no two of them were left together.

**12** Then the people said to Samuel, "Who was it that said, 'Is Saul to reign over us?'[^10] Hand those men over, so that we may put them to death."

**13** But Saul said, "No one will be put to death today, for today the LORD has brought victory to Israel."

**14** Samuel said to the people, "Come, let us go to Gilgal and renew the kingship there."

**15** So all the people went to Gilgal, and there at Gilgal they made Saul king before the LORD.[^11] There they sacrificed fellowship offerings[^12] before the LORD, and Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly there.

[^1]: Literally "camped against". For the paragraph that 4QSam(a) has before this verse, see the note on 10:27.
[^2]: That is, surrender to you. Also in verse 10.
[^3]: Hebrew *tsalach*, "rush on, come powerfully on"; see 10:6.
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Or "mustered".
[^6]: The Septuagint reads "six hundred thousand ... seventy thousand".
[^7]: Literally "you will have deliverance". Hebrew *teshu'ah*, the word translated "victory" in verse 13.
[^8]: That is, to Nahash and the Ammonites.
[^9]: The last part of the night, before dawn.
[^10]: Literally "Saul will reign over us", said scornfully; see 10:27.
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "and there Samuel anointed Saul as king before the LORD".
[^12]: Hebrew *zevachim shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings"; see 10:8.
