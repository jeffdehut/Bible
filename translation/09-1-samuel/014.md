**1** One day Jonathan son of Saul said to the young man who was his armor-bearer, "Come, let us go over to the Philistine outpost on the other side." But he did not tell his father.

**2** Saul was staying at the edge of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree at Migron. The troops with him numbered about six hundred men.

**3** Ahijah son of Ahitub was there, wearing the ephod.[^1] Ahitub was the brother of Ichabod son of Phinehas, son of Eli, the priest of the LORD[^2] at Shiloh. The troops did not know that Jonathan had gone.

**4** Between the passes by which Jonathan intended to cross over to the Philistine outpost, there was a rocky crag[^3] on one side and a rocky crag on the other. One was called Bozez and the other Seneh.

**5** One crag rose on the north, facing Michmash, and the other on the south, facing Geba.

**6** Jonathan said to the young man who was his armor-bearer, "Come, let us go over to the outpost of these uncircumcised men. Perhaps the LORD will act for us. Nothing can stop the LORD from saving, whether by many or by few."

**7** His armor-bearer said to him, "Do all that is in your heart. Go ahead; I am with you, heart and soul."[^4]

**8** Jonathan said, "Look, we will cross over to the men and let them see us.

**9** If they say to us, 'Wait there until we come to you,' then we will stay where we are and not go up to them.

**10** But if they say, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up, for the LORD has given them into our hands. That will be the sign for us."

**11** So the two of them let the Philistine outpost see them. The Philistines said, "Look, Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they have been hiding."

**12** The men of the outpost called out to Jonathan and his armor-bearer, "Come up to us, and we will teach you a lesson!"[^5] Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, "Come up after me, for the LORD has given them into the hands of Israel."

**13** Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer behind him. The Philistines fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer killed them as he came after him.

**14** In that first attack Jonathan and his armor-bearer killed about twenty men, within about half a furrow's length in a field.[^6]

**15** Terror spread through the camp, in the field and among all the troops. The outpost and the raiders also trembled. The ground shook, and it became a terror from God.[^7]

**16** Saul's lookouts at Gibeah of Benjamin looked, and there was the crowd, melting away and running here and there.[^8]

**17** Saul said to the troops who were with him, "Count and see who has left us." When they counted, Jonathan and his armor-bearer were not there.

**18** Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring the ark of God here." For the ark of God was with the Israelites at that time.[^9]

**19** While Saul was talking to the priest, the uproar in the Philistine camp kept growing louder and louder. So Saul said to the priest, "Withdraw your hand."[^10]

**20** Then Saul and all the troops with him rallied[^11] and went into the battle. There they found the Philistines in total confusion, each man's sword against his neighbor.

**21** The Hebrews who had previously been with the Philistines, and had gone up with them into the camp from all around, also went over to the Israelites who were with Saul and Jonathan.[^12]

**22** Likewise, when all the men of Israel who had been hiding in the hill country of Ephraim heard that the Philistines were fleeing, they too joined the battle and pursued them closely.

**23** So the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle moved on beyond Beth-aven.

**24** The men of Israel were hard pressed that day, for Saul had bound the troops with an oath, saying, "Cursed be anyone who eats food[^13] before evening, before I have taken vengeance on my enemies." So none of the troops tasted food.

**25** All the troops[^14] came into the forest, and there was honey on the ground.

**26** When the troops entered the forest, there was the honey, flowing,[^15] but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the troops were afraid of the oath.

**27** But Jonathan had not heard his father bind the troops with the oath. He reached out the tip of the staff in his hand, dipped it in the honeycomb, and put his hand to his mouth, and his eyes brightened.[^16]

**28** Then one of the troops said, "Your father strictly bound the troops with an oath, saying, 'Cursed be anyone who eats food today.'" And the troops were exhausted.

**29** Jonathan said, "My father has brought trouble on the land. See how my eyes have brightened because I tasted a little of this honey.

**30** How much better if the troops had eaten freely today from the plunder they found from their enemies! The slaughter among the Philistines would have been much greater."

**31** That day they struck down the Philistines from Michmash to Aijalon, and the troops were very exhausted.

**32** So the troops pounced on the plunder.[^17] They took sheep, cattle and calves, slaughtered them on the ground, and ate them with the blood.[^18]

**33** Saul was told, "Look, the troops are sinning against the LORD by eating with the blood." He said, "You have acted faithlessly. Roll a large stone over to me at once."[^19]

**34** Then Saul said, "Go out among the troops and tell them, 'Each of you bring me his ox or his sheep, and slaughter it here and eat. Do not sin against the LORD by eating with the blood.'" So that night all the troops brought their oxen with them,[^20] and they slaughtered them there.

**35** Saul built an altar to the LORD. It was the first altar he built to the LORD.

**36** Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night and plunder them until the morning light. Let us not leave one of them alive." They said, "Do whatever seems good to you." But the priest said, "Let us draw near to God here."

**37** So Saul asked God, "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into the hands of Israel?" But he did not answer him that day.

**38** Saul said, "Come here, all you leaders[^21] of the people, and find out how this sin has happened today.

**39** For as surely as the LORD lives, who saves Israel, even if it is in my son Jonathan, he must die." But not one of all the people answered him.

**40** He said to all Israel, "You stand on one side, and I and my son Jonathan will stand on the other side." The people said to Saul, "Do whatever seems good to you."

**41** Then Saul said to the LORD, the God of Israel, "Give a perfect answer."[^22] Jonathan and Saul were taken by the lot, and the people went free.

**42** Saul said, "Cast the lot between me and my son Jonathan." And Jonathan was taken.

**43** Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done." Jonathan told him, "I only tasted a little honey with the tip of the staff in my hand. Here I am; I must die!"[^23]

**44** Saul said, "May God deal with me, and more severely,[^24] if you do not die, Jonathan."

**45** But the people said to Saul, "Should Jonathan die, who has brought about this great victory in Israel? Far from it! As surely as the LORD lives, not one hair of his head will fall to the ground, for he has acted with God today." So the people ransomed[^25] Jonathan, and he did not die.

**46** Then Saul stopped pursuing the Philistines,[^26] and the Philistines went back to their own territory.

**47** When Saul had taken the kingship over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side: against Moab, the Ammonites, Edom, the kings of Zobah, and the Philistines. Wherever he turned, he defeated them.[^27]

**48** He acted valiantly.[^28] He struck down the Amalekites and rescued Israel from the power of those who plundered them.

**49** Saul's sons were Jonathan, Ishvi and Malchi-shua.[^29] The names of his two daughters were Merab, the firstborn, and Michal, the younger.

**50** Saul's wife was named Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz. The commander of his army was named Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle.

**51** Kish was Saul's father, and Ner, Abner's father, was the son of Abiel.

**52** There was fierce war against the Philistines all the days of Saul. Whenever Saul saw any strong or brave man, he took him into his service.[^30]

[^1]: Literally "carrying an ephod"; see 2:18 and 2:28. Here it was used to seek God's answers.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "a tooth of rock". Also later in the verse and in verse 5.
[^4]: Literally "Turn yourself; here I am with you as your heart."
[^5]: Literally "and we will make known to you a thing".
[^6]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "as in half a furrow of a yoke of field", perhaps half the length of a furrow plowed by a yoke of oxen.
[^7]: Or "a very great terror".
[^8]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and it went, and here".
[^9]: Literally "For the ark of God was on that day and the Israelites". The Septuagint reads "Bring the ephod here. For he was carrying the ephod at that time before Israel."
[^10]: That is, stop what you are doing. The priest was seeking God's answer, probably by means of the lots kept in the ephod.
[^11]: Literally "were called together".
[^12]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult.
[^13]: Literally "bread". Also in verse 28.
[^14]: Literally "All the land".
[^15]: Or "the bees had gone".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "and his eyes saw" but read "and his eyes brightened".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "The troops made plunder" but read "The troops pounced on the plunder".
[^18]: Literally "on the blood". Eating meat with the blood still in it was forbidden (Leviticus 17:10–14; 19:26). Also in verses 33 and 34.
[^19]: Literally "today".
[^20]: Literally "each his ox in his hand".
[^21]: Literally "cornerstones".
[^22]: Hebrew *havah tamim*, "give what is perfect (right)". With different vowels the words can be read "give Thummim", one of the two lots used to learn God's will (Exodus 28:30). The Septuagint has a longer text: "Then Saul said, 'the LORD God of Israel, why have you not answered your servant today? If this guilt is in me or in my son Jonathan, LORD God of Israel, give Urim; but if it is in your people Israel, give Thummim.'"
[^23]: Or "Here I am, ready to die."
[^24]: Literally "Thus may God do and thus may he add"; see 3:17.
[^25]: Hebrew *padah*, "ransom, redeem", that is, they set him free.
[^26]: Literally "Saul went up from after the Philistines".
[^27]: Literally "he made them guilty", that is, he condemned or punished them.
[^28]: Or "He gathered an army". Hebrew *va-ya'as chayil*.
[^29]: Compare the lists of Saul's sons in 31:2 and 1 Chronicles 8:33.
[^30]: Literally "he gathered him to himself".
