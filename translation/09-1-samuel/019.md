**1** Saul told his son Jonathan and all his servants to kill David. But Saul's son Jonathan was very fond of David.

**2** So Jonathan told David, "My father Saul is trying to kill you. So be on your guard in the morning. Stay in a hiding place and keep out of sight.

**3** I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are. I will speak to my father about you, and if I find out anything,[^1] I will tell you."

**4** Jonathan spoke well of David to his father Saul. He said to him, "Let the king not sin against his servant David, for he has not sinned against you, and what he has done has been very good for you.

**5** He took his life in his hands[^2] and struck down the Philistine, and Yahweh[^3] brought about a great victory for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced. Why then would you sin against innocent blood by killing David for no reason?"

**6** Saul listened to Jonathan, and Saul swore, "As surely as Yahweh lives, he will not be put to death."

**7** Jonathan called David and told him all these things. Then Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was with him as before.[^4]

**8** Once again there was war. David went out and fought against the Philistines and struck them with a great slaughter, and they fled before him.

**9** Then an evil spirit from Yahweh came on Saul as he was sitting in his house with his spear in his hand, while David was playing the lyre.[^5]

**10** Saul tried to pin David to the wall with the spear, but David slipped away from Saul, and he drove the spear into the wall. David fled and escaped that night.

**11** Saul sent messengers to David's house to watch it and to kill him in the morning. But Michal, David's wife, warned him, "If you do not escape with your life[^6] tonight, tomorrow you will be put to death."

**12** So Michal let David down through the window, and he went and fled and escaped.

**13** Michal took the household god[^7] and laid it on the bed. She put a mat of goat hair[^8] at its head and covered it with a garment.

**14** When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, "He is sick."

**15** Then Saul sent the messengers back to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me in his bed, so that I may kill him."

**16** When the messengers came in, there was the household god in the bed, with the goat hair at its head.

**17** Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this and let my enemy go, so that he has escaped?" Michal answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go. Why should I kill you?'"

**18** So David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel at Ramah. He told him all that Saul had done to him. Then he and Samuel went and stayed at Naioth.[^9]

**19** Saul was told, "David is at Naioth in Ramah."

**20** Saul sent messengers to take David. But when they saw the company[^10] of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing there as their leader, the Spirit of God came on Saul's messengers, and they too prophesied.[^11]

**21** When Saul was told, he sent other messengers, and they too prophesied. Saul sent messengers a third time, and they too prophesied.

**22** Then he himself went to Ramah. He came to the great cistern at Secu and asked, "Where are Samuel and David?" Someone said, "They are at Naioth in Ramah."

**23** So he went there, to Naioth in Ramah. The Spirit of God came on him too, and he went on, prophesying as he walked, until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

**24** He too stripped off his clothes, and he too prophesied before Samuel. He lay there naked[^12] all that day and all that night. That is why people say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

[^1]: Literally "and I will see what (there is)".
[^2]: Literally "He put his *nephesh* in his palm".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "as yesterday and the day before".
[^5]: Literally "playing with his hand"; see 16:23.
[^6]: Literally "save your *nephesh*".
[^7]: Hebrew *teraphim*, images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19; see 15:23). Here the plural form is used of a single image, apparently the size of a man. Also in verse 16.
[^8]: Hebrew *kevir ha-izzim*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps a quilt, net or tangle of goat hair. Also in verse 16.
[^9]: *Naioth* may mean "dwellings", perhaps the place where the prophets lived. Also in verses 19, 22 and 23.
[^10]: Hebrew *lahaqat*, a word found only here.
[^11]: See 10:5. Also in verses 21, 23 and 24.
[^12]: Or "with only his undergarments".
