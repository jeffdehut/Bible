**1** David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came and said to Jonathan, "What have I done? What is my guilt? What is my sin against your father, that he is seeking my life?"

**2** Jonathan said to him, "Far from it! You will not die. Look, my father does nothing, great or small, without telling me.[^1] Why would my father hide this from me? It is not so!"

**3** But David took an oath again and said, "Your father knows very well that I have found favor in your eyes, and he has thought, 'Jonathan must not know this, or he will be grieved.' But truly, as surely as Yahweh[^2] lives and as surely as you live,[^3] there is only a step between me and death."

**4** Jonathan said to David, "Whatever you want,[^4] I will do for you."

**5** David said to Jonathan, "Look, tomorrow is the new moon,[^5] and I am supposed to sit with the king at the meal. But let me go, and I will hide in the field until the evening of the third day.

**6** If your father misses me at all, say, 'David earnestly asked my permission to hurry to Bethlehem, his town, because the yearly sacrifice is there for the whole clan.'

**7** If he says, 'Good,' your servant is safe.[^6] But if he becomes angry, you will know that he has made up his mind to harm me.[^7]

**8** So show faithful love to your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of Yahweh with you. But if there is guilt in me, kill me yourself. Why should you bring me to your father?"

**9** Jonathan said, "Far be it from you! If I knew for certain that my father had made up his mind to bring harm on you, would I not tell you?"

**10** David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father gives you a harsh answer?"

**11** Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So the two of them went out into the field.

**12** Jonathan said to David, "Yahweh, the God of Israel, is my witness![^8] I will sound out my father about this time tomorrow, or the day after.[^9] If he is well disposed toward David, will I not then send to you and let you know?[^10]

**13** But if my father intends to harm you, may Yahweh deal with Jonathan, and more severely,[^11] if I do not let you know and send you away, so that you may go in peace. May Yahweh be with you as he was with my father.

**14** And if I am still alive, show me the faithful love of Yahweh, so that I do not die.[^12]

**15** And never cut off your faithful love from my family,[^13] not even when Yahweh cuts off every one of David's enemies from the face of the earth."

**16** So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David: "May Yahweh call David's enemies to account."[^14]

**17** Jonathan made David swear again because of his love for him,[^15] for he loved him as he loved himself.

**18** Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be empty.

**19** On the third day, go down quickly[^16] and come to the place where you hid on the day of the earlier affair,[^17] and stay beside the stone Ezel.[^18]

**20** I will shoot three arrows to the side of it, as though I were shooting at a target.

**21** Then I will send the boy: 'Go, find the arrows.' If I say to the boy, 'Look, the arrows are on this side of you, pick them up,' then come, for it is safe for you and there is no danger,[^19] as surely as Yahweh lives.

**22** But if I say to the young man, 'Look, the arrows are beyond you,' then go, for Yahweh has sent you away.

**23** As for the matter you and I have spoken about, Yahweh is between you and me[^20] for ever."

**24** So David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to eat the meal.

**25** The king sat in his seat as usual, the seat by the wall. Jonathan stood up,[^21] and Abner sat beside Saul, but David's place was empty.

**26** Saul did not say anything that day, for he thought, "Something has happened to him. He must be unclean; surely he is unclean."[^22]

**27** But on the day after the new moon, the second day, David's place was empty again. Saul said to his son Jonathan, "Why hasn't the son of Jesse come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

**28** Jonathan answered Saul, "David earnestly asked my permission to go to Bethlehem.

**29** He said, 'Please let me go, for our family has a sacrifice in the town, and my brother has ordered me to be there. So now, if I have found favor in your eyes, let me slip away to see my brothers.' That is why he has not come to the king's table."

**30** Then Saul's anger burned against Jonathan, and he said to him, "You son of a perverse, rebellious woman![^23] Don't I know that you have sided with[^24] the son of Jesse, to your own shame and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?[^25]

**31** For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingship will be secure. Now send and bring him to me, for he deserves to die."[^26]

**32** Jonathan answered his father Saul, "Why should he be put to death? What has he done?"

**33** But Saul hurled his spear at him to strike him. Then Jonathan knew that his father had made up his mind to kill David.

**34** Jonathan got up from the table in fierce anger, and he ate no food on the second day of the new moon, for he was grieved for David, because his father had disgraced him.[^27]

**35** In the morning Jonathan went out into the field for his meeting with David,[^28] and a young boy was with him.

**36** He said to his boy, "Run, find the arrows I am shooting." As the boy ran, he shot an arrow beyond him.

**37** When the boy came to the place where Jonathan's arrow had fallen, Jonathan called out after the boy, "Isn't the arrow beyond you?"

**38** Jonathan called out after the boy, "Hurry! Be quick! Don't stand still!" So Jonathan's boy gathered up the arrows[^29] and came back to his master.

**39** The boy knew nothing; only Jonathan and David knew about the matter.

**40** Then Jonathan gave his weapons to his boy and said to him, "Go, take them to the town."

**41** As soon as the boy had gone, David got up from beside the south side,[^30] fell with his face to the ground and bowed down three times. They kissed each other and wept with each other, but David wept the most.[^31]

**42** Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, for the two of us have sworn in the name of Yahweh, saying, 'Yahweh will be between me and you, and between my descendants and your descendants for ever.'"[^32]

[^1]: Literally "without uncovering my ear".
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "as your *nephesh* lives".
[^4]: Literally "Whatever your *nephesh* says".
[^5]: The first day of the month, marked with sacrifices and a feast (Numbers 10:10; 28:11–15). Also in verses 18, 24, 27 and 34.
[^6]: Literally "*shalom* (peace) to your servant".
[^7]: Literally "that the evil is decided by him". Also in verses 9 and 33.
[^8]: The Hebrew begins abruptly: "Yahweh, the God of Israel!" The words "is my witness" are added to make the sense clear.
[^9]: Literally "tomorrow, the third (day)".
[^10]: Literally "and uncover your ear". Also in verse 13.
[^11]: Literally "Thus may Yahweh do to Jonathan and thus may he add"; see 3:17.
[^12]: The Hebrew of verses 14–15 is difficult.
[^13]: Literally "house".
[^14]: Literally "And Jonathan cut with the house of David, and Yahweh will seek (it) from the hand of David's enemies." "David's enemies" may stand for David himself, to avoid speaking of a curse on him; compare 25:22.
[^15]: The Septuagint reads "Jonathan swore again to David".
[^16]: Literally "And you will do it a third time; go down greatly". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^17]: Literally "on the day of the deed", perhaps the time described in 19:2–3.
[^18]: Hebrew *ha-even ha-azel*, perhaps "the stone of departure". The Septuagint reads "beside that mound".
[^19]: Literally "for there is *shalom* (peace) for you, and there is nothing".
[^20]: That is, as witness. Also in verse 42.
[^21]: The meaning is uncertain.
[^22]: That is, ritually unclean, and so unable to share in the sacred meal (Leviticus 7:20–21).
[^23]: Literally "son of a perverse woman of rebellion".
[^24]: Literally "are choosing". The Septuagint reads "are a companion of".
[^25]: That is, to the shame of the mother who bore you.
[^26]: Literally "for he is a son of death".
[^27]: The Hebrew does not make clear whether "him" is David or Jonathan.
[^28]: Literally "for the appointed time of David".
[^29]: The Hebrew text is written "the arrow" but read "the arrows".
[^30]: Literally "from beside the south". The Septuagint reads "from beside the mound"; see verse 19.
[^31]: Literally "until David made it great".
[^32]: In most English Bibles, 21:1 is the second half of this verse.
