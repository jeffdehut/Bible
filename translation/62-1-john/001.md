**1** What was from the beginning,[^1] what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we have looked at and our hands have touched, concerning the word of life[^2]

**2** (and the life was revealed, and we have seen it, and we testify to it and announce to you the eternal life[^3] that was with the Father and was revealed to us),

**3** what we have seen and heard we announce to you too, so that you also may have fellowship[^4] with us. And our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ.

**4** We are writing these things[^5] so that our joy may be complete.

**5** This is the message that we have heard from him and announce to you: God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all.

**6** If we say that we have fellowship with him and yet walk in the darkness, we are lying and are not doing what is true.[^6]

**7** But if we walk in the light, as he himself is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son[^7] cleanses us from all sin.

**8** If we say that we have no sin, we are deceiving ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

**9** If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous, and will forgive us our sins and cleanse us from all wrongdoing.[^8]

**10** If we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.

[^1]: Compare John 1:1–2. The letter's opening sentence (verses 1–3) is interrupted by verse 2 and resumed in verse 3.
[^2]: Or "the Word of life". Greek *logos*, "word, message", the title given to Jesus in John 1:1, 14.
[^3]: Greek *zoe aionios*, literally "the life of the age", that is, of the age to come (see John 3:15).
[^4]: Greek *koinonia*, "fellowship, partnership, sharing"; also in verses 6–7.
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "We are writing these things to you".
[^6]: Literally "are not doing the truth".
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "Jesus Christ his Son".
[^8]: Greek *adikia*, "unrighteousness", the opposite of *dikaios*, "righteous", earlier in the verse.
