**1** See what great love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are![^1] The reason the world does not know us[^2] is that it did not know him.

**2** Dear friends, we are God's children now, and what we will be has not yet been revealed. We know that when he appears,[^3] we will be like him, because we will see him as he is.

**3** And everyone who has this hope set on him purifies themselves, just as he is pure.[^4]

**4** Everyone who commits sin also commits lawlessness; sin is lawlessness.

**5** You know that he appeared in order to take away sins,[^5] and in him there is no sin.

**6** No one who remains in him sins.[^6] No one who sins has seen him or known him.

**7** Little children, let no one lead you astray. Whoever does what is right is righteous, just as he is righteous.

**8** Whoever commits sin is from the devil,[^7] because the devil has been sinning from the beginning. The Son of God appeared for this purpose: to destroy the works of the devil.

**9** No one who has been born of God commits sin, because God's seed[^8] remains in them; and they cannot sin, because they have been born of God.

**10** This is how the children of God and the children of the devil are made plain: anyone who does not do what is right is not from God, and neither is anyone who does not love their brother or sister.[^9]

**11** For this is the message that you heard from the beginning: that we should love one another.

**12** We must not be like Cain,[^10] who was from the evil one and murdered[^11] his brother. And why did he murder him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's were righteous.

**13** Do not be surprised, brothers and sisters,[^12] if the world hates you.

**14** We know that we have passed out of death into life, because we love our brothers and sisters. Whoever does not love[^13] remains in death.

**15** Everyone who hates their brother or sister is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life remaining in them.

**16** This is how we have come to know love: he laid down his life for us. And we ought to lay down our lives for our brothers and sisters.

**17** But if anyone has the world's goods[^14] and sees their brother or sister in need, yet closes their heart against them,[^15] how does the love of God remain in them?

**18** Little children, let us not love with words or with the tongue, but in action and in truth.

**19** This is how we will know that we are from the truth, and how we will reassure our hearts[^16] before him

**20** whenever our hearts condemn us; for God is greater than our hearts, and he knows everything.[^17]

**21** Dear friends, if our hearts do not condemn us, we have confidence before God,

**22** and we receive from him whatever we ask, because we keep his commands and do what pleases him.[^18]

**23** And this is his command: that we believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ and love one another, just as he commanded us.

**24** Whoever keeps his commands remains in him, and he in them. And this is how we know that he remains in us: by the Spirit whom he has given us.

[^1]: Many manuscripts leave out "and so we are".
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "you".
[^3]: Or "when it is revealed", that is, what we will be.
[^4]: Literally "just as that one is pure", that is, Jesus (see 2:6); also in verses 5, 7 and 16.
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "our sins".
[^6]: The Greek present tense here and in verses 8–9 can mean "keeps on sinning", "goes on sinning". Compare 1:8–10.
[^7]: Or "belongs to the devil". Literally "is of the devil"; and so "from God" (verses 9–10) and "from the evil one" (verse 12).
[^8]: Literally "his seed".
[^9]: Literally "his brother"; also in verses 12, 15 and 17. The letter uses "brother" for any fellow believer, man or woman.
[^10]: Literally "Not as Cain". See Genesis 4:1–16.
[^11]: Greek *sphazo*, "slaughter", the word used for killing a sacrificial animal; also later in the verse.
[^12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 14 and 16.
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "Whoever does not love their brother or sister".
[^14]: Literally "the life of the world". Greek *bios*, "life, livelihood, possessions", as in 2:16.
[^15]: Literally "shuts his inner parts from him", the seat of the feelings, especially of compassion.
[^16]: Literally "persuade our heart". The Greek has the singular "heart" here and in verses 20–21.
[^17]: The Greek of verses 19–20 is difficult. Verse 20 may also be read "that if our hearts condemn us, God is greater than our hearts …", or "in whatever our hearts condemn us, for God is greater …".
[^18]: Literally "do what is pleasing in his sight".
