**1** The LORD[^1] said to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt,

**2** "This month is to be the first month for you. It is to be the first of the months of your year.[^2]

**3** Tell the whole community of Israel: On the tenth day of this month each man is to take a lamb[^3] for his family,[^4] one lamb for each household.

**4** If the household is too small for a whole lamb, he and the neighbor nearest to his house are to take one, according to the number of people. You are to work out how many can share the lamb by how much each person eats.

**5** Your lamb must be a male a year old, without any defect. You may take it from the sheep or from the goats.

**6** You are to keep it until the fourteenth day of this month. Then the whole assembly of the community of Israel is to slaughter it at twilight.[^5]

**7** They are to take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the top of the doorframe of the houses where they eat it.

**8** They are to eat the meat that same night, roasted over the fire. They are to eat it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

**9** Do not eat any of it raw or boiled in water, but roasted over the fire, with its head, its legs and its inner parts.

**10** Do not leave any of it until morning. Whatever is left of it until morning you must burn in the fire.

**11** This is how you are to eat it: with your belt fastened,[^6] your sandals on your feet and your staff in your hand. You are to eat it in haste. It is the LORD's Passover.[^7]

**12** On that night I will pass through the land of Egypt and strike down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both people and animals, and I will carry out judgments on all the gods of Egypt. I am the LORD.

**13** The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are. When I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will come on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.

**14** This day is to be a day for you to remember. You are to celebrate it as a festival to the LORD. Throughout your generations you are to celebrate it as a permanent decree.

**15** For seven days you are to eat unleavened bread. On the very first day you must remove yeast from your houses, for whoever eats anything leavened from the first day to the seventh day, that person must be cut off[^8] from Israel.

**16** On the first day you are to hold a sacred assembly,[^9] and on the seventh day a sacred assembly. No work at all may be done on those days, except to prepare what each person needs to eat. Only that may be done.

**17** You are to observe the Festival of Unleavened Bread,[^10] because on this very day I brought your divisions[^11] out of the land of Egypt. You are to observe this day throughout your generations as a permanent decree.

**18** In the first month, from the evening of the fourteenth day of the month until the evening of the twenty-first day of the month, you are to eat unleavened bread.

**19** For seven days no yeast may be found in your houses. For whoever eats anything leavened, that person must be cut off from the community of Israel, whether a foreigner or someone born in the land.

**20** You must not eat anything leavened. Wherever you live, you are to eat unleavened bread."

**21** Then Moses summoned all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Go and choose lambs[^12] for your families, and slaughter the Passover lamb.

**22** Take a bunch of hyssop,[^13] dip it in the blood that is in the basin,[^14] and touch the top of the doorframe and the two doorposts with some of the blood in the basin. Not one of you is to go out of the door of his house until morning.

**23** When the LORD passes through to strike down the Egyptians, he will see the blood on the top of the doorframe and on the two doorposts. Then the LORD will pass over the door, and he will not let the destroyer come into your houses to strike you down.

**24** You are to observe this as a decree for you and your descendants forever.

**25** When you come into the land that the LORD will give you, as he has promised, you are to observe this ceremony.[^15]

**26** And when your children ask you, 'What does this ceremony mean to you?'

**27** you are to say, 'It is the Passover sacrifice to the LORD, who passed over the houses of the Israelites in Egypt when he struck down the Egyptians, but spared our houses.'" Then the people bowed down and worshiped.

**28** The Israelites went and did it. They did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**29** At midnight the LORD struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh, who sat on his throne, to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon, and every firstborn of the livestock.

**30** Pharaoh got up in the night, he and all his officials[^16] and all the Egyptians. There was a great cry in Egypt, for there was not a house without someone dead.

**31** He summoned Moses and Aaron in the night and said, "Get up, get out from among my people, both you and the Israelites! Go, serve[^17] the LORD, as you have asked.

**32** Take your flocks and your herds too, as you have asked, and go. And bless me too."

**33** The Egyptians pressed[^18] the people to leave the land quickly, for they said, "We will all be dead!"

**34** So the people took their dough before it had risen, with their kneading bowls wrapped in their cloaks on their shoulders.

**35** The Israelites had done as Moses had told them. They had asked the Egyptians for objects of silver and gold and for clothing,

**36** and the LORD had made the Egyptians look with favor on the people,[^19] so they gave them what they asked for. So they plundered the Egyptians.

**37** The Israelites set out from Rameses for Succoth. There were about six hundred thousand[^20] men on foot, not counting the little ones.

**38** A large mixed crowd also went up with them, and a great number of livestock, both flocks and herds.

**39** They baked the dough they had brought out of Egypt into flat loaves of unleavened bread. It had not risen, because they had been driven out of Egypt and could not wait, and they had not prepared any provisions for themselves.

**40** The time that the Israelites had lived in Egypt was 430 years.[^21]

**41** At the end of the 430 years, on that very day, all the divisions of the LORD went out of the land of Egypt.

**42** It was a night of keeping watch for the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This same night belongs to the LORD, a night of keeping watch for all the Israelites throughout their generations.[^22]

**43** The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the decree for the Passover: No outsider[^23] may eat it.

**44** But any man's slave who has been bought with money may eat it after you have circumcised him.

**45** A temporary resident or a hired worker may not eat it.

**46** It must be eaten in one house. You must not take any of the meat outside the house, and you must not break any of its bones.

**47** The whole community of Israel is to keep it.

**48** If a foreigner living among you wants to keep the LORD's Passover, all the males in his household must be circumcised. Then he may come near and keep it, and he will be like someone born in the land. But no uncircumcised man may eat it.

**49** The same law[^24] applies to the person born in the land and to the foreigner who lives among you."

**50** All the Israelites did it. They did just as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron.

**51** On that very day the LORD brought the Israelites out of the land of Egypt, division by division.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: This month is called Abib in 13:4, and later Nisan. It falls in March–April.
[^3]: Hebrew *seh*, a young animal from the flock, either a sheep or a goat (verse 5); also in verses 4 and 5.
[^4]: Literally "for the house of the fathers".
[^5]: Literally "between the two evenings", probably the time between sunset and dark.
[^6]: Literally "with your loins girded", with the long robe tucked into the belt, ready to travel.
[^7]: Hebrew *pesach*. The related verb *pasach*, in verses 13, 23 and 27, means "pass over" or perhaps "protect, stand guard over".
[^8]: That is, removed from the people, either by being excluded or by death. The phrase is not explained here.
[^9]: Literally "a holy calling", a gathering called for worship.
[^10]: Literally "observe the unleavened bread". The same Hebrew consonants can be read "observe the commands".
[^11]: Literally "armies"; also in verses 41 and 51. Hebrew *tsava'ot*; see 6:26.
[^12]: Hebrew *tson*, "flock animals", sheep or goats.
[^13]: A small bushy plant, used in rites of cleansing.
[^14]: Or "on the threshold"; also later in the verse. Hebrew *saph* can mean either.
[^15]: Literally "this service". Hebrew *avodah*, from the verb *avad*, "serve"; also in verse 26.
[^16]: Literally "servants".
[^17]: Or "worship".
[^18]: Hebrew *chazaq*, the verb used for Pharaoh's hardened heart.
[^19]: Literally "The LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of Egypt".
[^20]: Hebrew *elef*, "thousand". Some scholars think the word here means a clan or military unit, which would give a much smaller number.
[^21]: The Greek Septuagint reads "in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan", and the Samaritan Pentateuch "in the land of Canaan and in the land of Egypt". Compare Genesis 15:13 and Galatians 3:17.
[^22]: Or "It was a night when the LORD kept watch to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This same night is to be kept as a vigil to the LORD by all the Israelites throughout their generations."
[^23]: Literally "son of a foreign land", someone who does not belong to the community. In verses 48–49 the word is *ger*, a foreigner who lives among the Israelites.
[^24]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, law".
