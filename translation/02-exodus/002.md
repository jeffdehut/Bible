**1** A man from the house of Levi went and married a Levite woman.[^1]

**2** The woman became pregnant and gave birth to a son. When she saw that he was a fine child,[^2] she hid him for three months.

**3** When she could hide him no longer, she got a basket[^3] made of papyrus for him and coated it with tar and pitch. She put the child in it and set it among the reeds[^4] at the edge of the Nile.

**4** His sister stood some distance away to find out what would happen to him.

**5** Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe in the Nile, while her young women walked along the riverbank. She saw the basket among the reeds and sent her slave woman to get it.

**6** She opened it and saw the child: a baby boy, crying. She felt sorry for him and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

**7** Then his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call one of the Hebrew women to nurse the child for you?"

**8** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." So the girl went and called the child's mother.

**9** Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you your wages." So the woman took the child and nursed him.

**10** When the child grew older, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, "Because I drew him out of the water."[^5]

**11** In those days, after Moses had grown up, he went out to his own people[^6] and saw their forced labor. He saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, one of his own people.

**12** He looked this way and that, and when he saw that no one was there, he struck down[^7] the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**13** He went out the next day, and there were two Hebrew men fighting. He said to the one who was in the wrong, "Why are you hitting your fellow Hebrew?"[^8]

**14** The man said, "Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Are you planning to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian?" Then Moses was afraid. He thought, "So the matter has become known."

**15** When Pharaoh heard about this, he tried to kill Moses. But Moses fled from Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian, and he sat down by a well.

**16** The priest of Midian had seven daughters. They came to draw water and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

**17** Some shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses got up, came to their rescue, and watered their flock.

**18** When they came home to their father Reuel,[^9] he asked, "Why have you come back so early today?"

**19** They said, "An Egyptian rescued us from the shepherds. He even drew water for us and watered the flock."

**20** He said to his daughters, "Where is he? Why did you leave the man behind? Invite him to have something to eat."[^10]

**21** Moses agreed to stay with the man, and he gave Moses his daughter Zipporah as his wife.

**22** She gave birth to a son, and Moses named him Gershom, for he said, "I have become a foreigner in a foreign land."[^11]

**23** During that long time the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery and cried out, and their cry for help because of their slavery went up to God.

**24** God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac and with Jacob.

**25** God saw the Israelites, and God took notice.[^12]

[^1]: Literally "took a daughter of Levi".
[^2]: Literally "that he was good", the words used of God's creation in Genesis 1.
[^3]: Hebrew *tevah*, the word used for Noah's ark (Genesis 6–9). It occurs nowhere else.
[^4]: Hebrew *suph*, the word in *Yam Suph*, the name of the sea the Israelites later cross (13:18; 15:4).
[^5]: The Hebrew name *Mosheh* sounds like *mashah*, "draw out". The name may also be related to an Egyptian word meaning "born" or "son", found in names such as Thutmose.
[^6]: Literally "his brothers"; also later in the verse.
[^7]: The same Hebrew verb as "beating" in verse 11 and "hitting" in verse 13.
[^8]: Literally "your companion".
[^9]: Moses' father-in-law is called Reuel here and in Numbers 10:29, and Jethro in 3:1 and 18:1.
[^10]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^11]: The name *Gershom* sounds like *ger sham*, "a foreigner there".
[^12]: Literally "and God knew". The Greek Septuagint reads "and he made himself known to them".
