**1** These are the names of the sons of Israel[^1] who came to Egypt. They came with Jacob, each man with his household:

**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi and Judah,

**3** Issachar, Zebulun and Benjamin,

**4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**5** All the people descended from Jacob[^2] numbered seventy.[^3] Joseph was already in Egypt.

**6** Then Joseph died, and all his brothers, and that whole generation.

**7** But the Israelites were fruitful and swarmed;[^4] they multiplied and grew very, very strong, so that the land was filled with them.

**8** Then a new king came to power over Egypt, one who did not know about Joseph.

**9** He said to his people, "Look, the Israelite people are more numerous and stronger than we are.[^5]

**10** Come, let us deal shrewdly with them, or they will multiply even more. Then, if war breaks out, they too will join our enemies, fight against us, and go up from the land."[^6]

**11** So they put slave masters[^7] over them to oppress them with forced labor.[^8] They built storage cities for Pharaoh:[^9] Pithom and Rameses.

**12** But the more they oppressed them, the more they multiplied and spread, and the Egyptians came to dread the Israelites.

**13** So the Egyptians made the Israelites work ruthlessly.

**14** They made their lives bitter with hard labor, with mortar and bricks and with every kind of work in the fields. In all the work they made them do, they were ruthless.[^10]

**15** The king of Egypt spoke to the Hebrew midwives,[^11] one of whom was named Shiphrah and the other Puah.

**16** He said, "When you help the Hebrew women give birth, look at the birthstool.[^12] If it is a son, kill him; but if it is a daughter, she may live."

**17** But the midwives feared God, and they did not do what the king of Egypt had told them. They let the boys live.

**18** So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this? Why have you let the boys live?"

**19** The midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women. They are vigorous,[^13] and they give birth before the midwife gets to them."

**20** So God was good to the midwives, and the people multiplied and grew very strong.

**21** Because the midwives feared God, he gave them families of their own.[^14]

**22** Then Pharaoh gave this order to all his people: "Every son that is born[^15] you must throw into the Nile,[^16] but let every daughter live."

[^1]: Hebrew *bene yisra'el*. Here it means Jacob's own sons; from verse 7 on, the same phrase means the Israelites, the people descended from them.
[^2]: Literally "All the persons who came out of Jacob's thigh". Hebrew *nephesh*, "person, living being".
[^3]: The Greek Septuagint and a Dead Sea Scroll of Exodus (4QExod-b) read "seventy-five", the number given in Acts 7:14. Compare Genesis 46:27.
[^4]: Or "teemed". The words *be fruitful*, *swarm*, *multiply* and *fill* echo God's blessing in Genesis 1:20–28 and 9:1, 7.
[^5]: Or "too numerous and too strong for us".
[^6]: Or "gain control of the land". The Hebrew phrase is unusual; it probably means "escape from the land".
[^7]: Literally "officers of forced labor".
[^8]: Literally "with their burdens".
[^9]: *Pharaoh*: the title of the king of Egypt, used in Exodus as if it were a name. The text does not say which king is meant.
[^10]: Verses 13–14 repeat the Hebrew root *avad*, "work, serve", five times.
[^11]: Or "the midwives to the Hebrew women". The Hebrew can be read either way, so it is not certain whether the midwives were Hebrews or Egyptians.
[^12]: Hebrew *ovnayim*, "the two stones", found only here and in Jeremiah 18:3, where it means a potter's wheel. It probably refers to the stones a woman sat or crouched on to give birth.
[^13]: Or "lively", "full of life". The word is related to *chayyah*, "animal", and may hint that they give birth as easily as animals do.
[^14]: Literally "he made houses for them". *He* is most likely God.
[^15]: The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint add "to the Hebrews".
[^16]: Hebrew *ye'or*, "the river", a word used for the Nile.
