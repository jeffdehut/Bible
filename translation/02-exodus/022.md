**1** If a thief is caught breaking in,[^1] and he is struck so that he dies, there is no bloodguilt for him.[^2]

**2** But if the sun has risen on him, there is bloodguilt for him. A thief must make full restitution. If he has nothing, he is to be sold to pay for what he stole.

**3** If the stolen animal is found alive in his possession, whether ox or donkey or sheep,[^3] he must pay back double.

**4** If a man lets his animals graze[^4] in a field or vineyard, and lets his livestock loose so that they graze in someone else's field, he must make restitution from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.

**5** If a fire breaks out and catches in thornbushes, so that stacked grain or standing grain or the field is burned up, the one who started the fire must make full restitution.

**6** If a man gives his neighbor money or goods to keep safe, and they are stolen from that man's house, then if the thief is caught, he must pay back double.

**7** If the thief is not caught, the owner of the house is to come before God[^5] to show that he has not laid his hand on his neighbor's property.

**8** In every case of a disputed wrong, whether it concerns an ox, a donkey, a sheep, a garment, or anything lost of which someone says, "This is mine," the case of both parties is to come before God. The one whom God declares guilty must pay back double to his neighbor.

**9** If a man gives his neighbor a donkey, an ox, a sheep or any other animal to keep safe, and it dies or is injured or is carried off while no one is looking,

**10** an oath before Yahweh[^6] is to settle the matter between them, that he has not laid his hand on his neighbor's property. Its owner must accept this, and he does not have to make restitution.

**11** But if it was actually stolen from him, he must make restitution to its owner.

**12** If it was torn to pieces by a wild animal, he is to bring it as evidence. He does not have to make restitution for what was torn.

**13** If a man borrows an animal from his neighbor, and it is injured or dies while its owner is not with it, he must make full restitution.

**14** If its owner was with it, he does not have to make restitution. If it was hired, the hiring fee covers the loss.[^7]

**15** If a man seduces a virgin who is not engaged to be married and sleeps with her, he must pay the bride-price[^8] for her and make her his wife.

**16** If her father absolutely refuses to give her to him, he must still pay[^9] money equal to the bride-price for virgins.

**17** You must not let a sorceress live.

**18** Whoever has sexual relations with an animal must be put to death.

**19** Whoever sacrifices to any god other than Yahweh alone must be destroyed.[^10]

**20** You must not mistreat or oppress a foreigner,[^11] for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

**21** You must not mistreat[^12] any widow or orphan.

**22** If you do mistreat them, and they cry out to me, I will certainly hear their cry.

**23** My anger will burn, and I will kill you with the sword. Then your wives will become widows and your children orphans.

**24** If you lend money to my people, to a poor person among you, do not act like a moneylender toward him. You must not charge him interest.

**25** If you ever take your neighbor's cloak as security for a debt, give it back to him before sunset,

**26** because it is his only covering.[^13] It is the cloak for his body. What else will he sleep in? When he cries out to me, I will listen, for I am gracious.

**27** You must not curse God,[^14] and you must not curse a leader of your people.

**28** You must not hold back the best of your harvest and of your wine and oil.[^15] You must give me the firstborn of your sons.

**29** You must do the same with your cattle and your sheep. The firstborn may stay with its mother seven days, but on the eighth day you must give it to me.

**30** You are to be holy people for me. You must not eat the meat of an animal torn by wild animals in the field. Throw it to the dogs.

[^1]: Verses 1–30 are 22:2–31 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "there is no blood for him": the one who killed him is not guilty of murder. In verse 2, "there is blood for him".
[^3]: Hebrew *seh*, a sheep or a goat; also in verses 8 and 9.
[^4]: Hebrew *ba'ar*, which can mean "graze" or "burn". Some understand verse 4 as "If a man burns off a field or vineyard and lets the fire spread …", to match verse 5.
[^5]: Or "the judges". Hebrew *ha-elohim*, "God"; also twice in verse 8. See the note on 21:6.
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^7]: Literally "it came for its hire". Or "If he is a hired worker, the loss is covered by his wages".
[^8]: Hebrew *mohar*, the payment a man gave to the family of the woman he married.
[^9]: Literally "weigh out".
[^10]: Hebrew *charam*, "be devoted to destruction": given up to God by being destroyed.
[^11]: Hebrew *ger*, a foreigner who lives among the Israelites.
[^12]: The Hebrew verb *anah*, "mistreat", is the one used for Egypt's oppression of Israel (1:11–12); also in verse 22.
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "its covering" but read "his covering".
[^14]: Or "the judges". The Greek Septuagint reads "gods". The second half of the verse is quoted in Acts 23:5.
[^15]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "your fullness and your juice".
