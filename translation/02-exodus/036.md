**1** "So Bezalel and Oholiab and all the skilled people[^1] to whom the LORD[^2] has given skill and understanding, to know how to do all the work for the service of the sanctuary, are to do the work just as the LORD has commanded."[^3]

**2** Then Moses called Bezalel and Oholiab and all the skilled people to whom the LORD had given skill,[^4] everyone whose heart stirred him to come and do the work.

**3** They received from Moses all the contributions that the Israelites had brought for doing the work for the service of the sanctuary. But the people kept bringing him more freewill offerings, morning after morning.

**4** So all the skilled workers who were doing all the work on the sanctuary left the work each of them was doing and came

**5** and said to Moses, "The people are bringing much more than enough for doing the work that the LORD commanded us to do."

**6** So Moses gave an order, and they sent a message throughout the camp: "No man or woman is to make anything more as a contribution for the sanctuary." So the people were stopped from bringing more.

**7** What they had was enough for doing all the work, and more than enough.

**8** All the skilled people among those doing the work made the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twisted linen and blue, purple and scarlet yarn.[^5] He made them with cherubim worked into them by a skilled designer.[^6]

**9** Each curtain was 28 cubits[^7] long and 4 cubits wide. All the curtains were the same size.

**10** He joined five of the curtains to one another, and he joined the other five curtains to one another.

**11** He made loops of blue yarn on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and he did the same on the edge of the end curtain in the second set.

**12** He made fifty loops on the one curtain, and he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain at the end of the second set, with the loops opposite one another.

**13** He made fifty gold clasps and joined the curtains to one another with the clasps, so that the tabernacle became a single whole.

**14** He made curtains of goats' hair for a tent over the tabernacle. He made eleven of these curtains.

**15** Each curtain was 30 cubits long and 4 cubits wide. All eleven curtains were the same size.

**16** He joined five of the curtains together by themselves, and six of the curtains by themselves.

**17** He made fifty loops on the edge of the end curtain in the first set, and he made fifty loops on the edge of the curtain in the second set.

**18** He made fifty bronze clasps to join the tent together, so that it became a single whole.

**19** He made a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and over that a covering of fine leather.[^8]

**20** He made upright frames[^9] of acacia wood for the tabernacle.

**21** Each frame was 10 cubits long and 1½ cubits wide.

**22** Each frame had two tenons, joined to each other.[^10] He did this for all the frames of the tabernacle.

**23** He made the frames for the tabernacle: twenty frames for the south side,

**24** and he made forty silver bases to go under the twenty frames, two bases under one frame for its two tenons, and two bases under the next frame for its two tenons.

**25** For the second side of the tabernacle, the north side, he made twenty frames,

**26** with their forty silver bases, two bases under one frame and two bases under the next frame.

**27** For the back of the tabernacle, toward the west,[^11] he made six frames.

**28** He made two frames for the corners of the tabernacle at the back.

**29** They were double at the bottom, and together they were complete up to the top, to the first ring.[^12] He made both of them this way, for the two corners.

**30** So there were eight frames, with their silver bases, sixteen bases: two bases under each frame.[^13]

**31** He made crossbars of acacia wood: five for the frames of one side of the tabernacle,

**32** five crossbars for the frames of the second side of the tabernacle, and five crossbars for the frames of the tabernacle at the back, toward the west.

**33** He made the middle crossbar to run halfway up the frames, from one end to the other.

**34** He overlaid the frames with gold, and he made their rings of gold to hold the crossbars. He overlaid the crossbars with gold as well.

**35** He made the veil of blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen. He made it with cherubim worked into it by a skilled designer.

**36** He made four posts of acacia wood for it and overlaid them with gold, with gold hooks, and he cast four silver bases for them.

**37** For the entrance of the tent he made a screen of blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer,

**38** with its five posts and their hooks. He overlaid their tops and their bands with gold, and their five bases were of bronze.

[^1]: Literally "every man wise of heart". Also in verses 2, 4 and 8.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "So Bezalel and Oholiab and all the skilled people … did the work just as the LORD had commanded." The verse may end Moses' words of 35:30–35, or begin the account of the work.
[^4]: Literally "had put wisdom in his heart".
[^5]: Literally "blue and purple and crimson worm": wools dyed violet-blue, red-purple and crimson. See 25:4.
[^6]: Literally "cherubim, the work of a designer, he made them". From here to 39:31 the Hebrew verbs are mostly singular, "he made". They probably refer to Bezalel (37:1), who led the work.
[^7]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm).
[^8]: Hebrew *tachash*, of uncertain meaning. See 25:5.
[^9]: Hebrew *qerashim*, traditionally "boards". See 26:15.
[^10]: See 26:17.
[^11]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean. Also in verse 32.
[^12]: The meaning of this verse is uncertain. See 26:24.
[^13]: Literally "two bases, two bases under the one frame".
