**1** When the people saw that Moses was taking a long time to come down from the mountain, they gathered around Aaron and said to him, "Come, make us gods[^1] who will go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has happened to him."

**2** Aaron said to them, "Take off the gold rings that are in the ears of your wives, your sons and your daughters, and bring them to me."

**3** So all the people took off the gold rings that were in their ears and brought them to Aaron.

**4** He took the gold from them, shaped it with a tool[^2] and made it into a calf of cast metal. Then they said, "These are your gods,[^3] Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt."

**5** When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of it. Aaron made a proclamation: "Tomorrow there will be a festival to the LORD."[^4]

**6** Early the next day they got up and offered burnt offerings and brought fellowship offerings. The people sat down to eat and drink, and then got up to revel.[^5]

**7** The LORD said to Moses, "Go down at once, because your people, whom you brought up out of the land of Egypt, have acted corruptly.

**8** They have been quick to turn away from the way I commanded them. They have made themselves a calf of cast metal. They have bowed down to it and sacrificed to it, and they have said, 'These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.'"

**9** The LORD said to Moses, "I have seen this people, and they are a stiff-necked people.[^6]

**10** Now leave me alone, so that my anger may burn against them and I may destroy them. Then I will make you into a great nation."

**11** But Moses pleaded with the LORD his God.[^7] He said, "LORD, why should your anger burn against your people, whom you brought out of the land of Egypt with great power and a strong hand?

**12** Why should the Egyptians say, 'He brought them out with evil intent, to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth'? Turn from your fierce anger, and relent[^8] from bringing disaster on your people.

**13** Remember Abraham, Isaac and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by yourself. You said to them, 'I will make your descendants as numerous as the stars of the sky, and I will give your descendants all this land that I have spoken of, and they will possess it forever.'"

**14** So the LORD relented from the disaster he had said he would bring on his people.

**15** Moses turned and went down the mountain with the two tablets of the testimony in his hands. The tablets were written on both sides. They were written on the front and on the back.

**16** The tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, engraved on the tablets.

**17** When Joshua heard the noise of the people shouting,[^9] he said to Moses, "There is a sound of war in the camp."

**18** But Moses said, "It is not the sound of victors shouting, and it is not the sound of the defeated crying out. It is the sound of singing that I hear."[^10]

**19** As soon as he came near the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, Moses' anger burned. He threw the tablets from his hands[^11] and broke them at the foot of the mountain.

**20** He took the calf they had made and burned it in the fire. Then he ground it until it was powder, scattered it on the water and made the Israelites drink it.

**21** Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you have brought such a great sin on them?"

**22** Aaron said, "Do not let my lord's anger burn. You know the people yourself, how set on evil they are.[^12]

**23** They said to me, 'Make us gods who will go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has happened to him.'

**24** So I said to them, 'Whoever has any gold, take it off.' They gave it to me, and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf!"

**25** Moses saw that the people were running wild, for Aaron had let them run wild, and they had become an object of scorn among their enemies.[^13]

**26** So Moses stood at the gate of the camp and said, "Whoever is on the LORD's side, come to me!" And all the Levites[^14] gathered around him.

**27** He said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Each of you, strap your sword to your side.[^15] Go back and forth through the camp from gate to gate, and each of you kill his brother, his friend and his neighbor.'"

**28** The Levites did as Moses said, and about three thousand of the people died[^16] that day.

**29** Moses said, "Dedicate yourselves to the LORD today,[^17] because each of you has gone against his own son and his own brother. So he will give you a blessing today."

**30** The next day Moses said to the people, "You have committed a great sin. But now I will go up to the LORD. Perhaps I can make atonement for your sin."

**31** So Moses went back to the LORD and said, "Oh, this people has committed a great sin! They have made themselves gods of gold.

**32** But now, if you will forgive their sin…[^18] But if not, please wipe me out of the book you have written."

**33** The LORD said to Moses, "Whoever has sinned against me, I will wipe out of my book.

**34** Now go, lead the people to the place I told you about. Look, my angel[^19] will go ahead of you. But when the day comes for me to punish, I will punish them for their sin."[^20]

**35** And the LORD struck the people with a plague because they had made the calf, the one Aaron made.[^21]

[^1]: Or "a god". Hebrew *elohim* can mean "God" or "gods". The verb "will go" is plural.
[^2]: Or "tied it up in a bag". The Hebrew can be understood either way.
[^3]: Or "This is your god". In the Hebrew, "these" and "brought you up" are plural. Also in verse 8.
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Hebrew *letsacheq*, "to laugh, play, make merry". It can suggest sexual play.
[^6]: Literally "hard of neck", that is, stubborn, like an ox that will not bend its neck to the yoke.
[^7]: Literally "softened the face of the LORD his God".
[^8]: Or "change your mind". Hebrew *hinnachem*, "relent, be sorry". The same verb is in verse 14.
[^9]: The Hebrew word translated "shouting" is unusual.
[^10]: Moses' answer plays on the verb *anah*, "answer, sing". Literally "It is not the sound of the answering of might, and it is not the sound of the answering of weakness; the sound of singing I hear." The meaning of the last word is uncertain.
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "his hand" but read "his hands".
[^12]: Literally "that it is in evil".
[^13]: The meaning of the Hebrew word translated "object of scorn" is uncertain. Literally "for a whisper among those who rise against them".
[^14]: Literally "the sons of Levi". Also in verse 28.
[^15]: Literally "on his thigh".
[^16]: Literally "fell".
[^17]: Literally "Fill your hand today for the LORD", the idiom used for ordaining priests (28:41). Some understand it as "You have ordained yourselves today".
[^18]: The sentence is left unfinished in the Hebrew.
[^19]: Or "my messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh*.
[^20]: Literally "on the day of my visiting, I will visit their sin upon them".
[^21]: Literally "because they made the calf that Aaron made".
