**1** Then God spoke all these words:[^1]

**2** "I am Yahweh[^2] your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the place of slavery.[^3]

**3** You must have no other gods besides me.[^4]

**4** You must not make for yourself an idol,[^5] or an image of anything in the heavens above, or on the earth below, or in the waters under the earth.

**5** You must not bow down to them or serve them,[^6] for I, Yahweh your God, am a jealous God.[^7] I punish children for the wrongdoing of their parents,[^8] to the third and the fourth generation of those who hate me,

**6** but I show faithful love[^9] to thousands[^10] of those who love me and keep my commands.

**7** You must not misuse the name of Yahweh your God,[^11] for Yahweh will not leave unpunished anyone who misuses his name.

**8** Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy.

**9** For six days you are to work and do all your work,

**10** but the seventh day is a Sabbath to Yahweh your God. On it you must not do any work, neither you, nor your son or your daughter, nor your male or female slave,[^12] nor your livestock, nor the foreigner who lives in your towns.[^13]

**11** For in six days Yahweh made the heavens and the earth, the sea and everything in them, and he rested on the seventh day. That is why Yahweh blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.

**12** Honor[^14] your father and your mother, so that you may live long in the land that Yahweh your God is giving you.

**13** You must not murder.[^15]

**14** You must not commit adultery.

**15** You must not steal.

**16** You must not give false testimony against your neighbor.

**17** You must not covet[^16] your neighbor's house. You must not covet your neighbor's wife, or his male or female slave, or his ox or his donkey, or anything that belongs to your neighbor."[^17]

**18** All the people saw the thunder and the lightning,[^18] the sound of the ram's horn and the mountain smoking. When the people saw it, they trembled and stood at a distance.

**19** They said to Moses, "You speak to us, and we will listen. But do not let God speak to us, or we will die."

**20** Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. God has come to test you, so that the fear of him will be with you[^19] and keep you from sinning."

**21** So the people stood at a distance, while Moses went near to the thick darkness where God was.

**22** Then Yahweh said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'You have seen for yourselves that I have spoken to you from heaven.

**23** You must not make anything to set beside me. You must not make for yourselves gods of silver or gods of gold.[^20]

**24** Make an altar of earth for me, and sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your fellowship offerings,[^21] your sheep and goats and your cattle. In every place where I cause my name to be remembered, I will come to you and bless you.

**25** If you make me an altar of stones, do not build it of cut stones, for if you use a tool[^22] on it, you will defile it.

**26** And do not go up to my altar by steps, so that your nakedness is not exposed on it.'"

[^1]: Verses 2–17 are called "the ten words" (34:28; Deuteronomy 4:13; 10:4), traditionally the Ten Commandments. Traditions count them differently. In Jewish tradition verse 2 is the first word and verses 3–6 the second; in many Christian traditions verse 3 is the first commandment and verses 4–6 the second; in Catholic and Lutheran tradition verses 3–6 are the first and verse 17 is divided into two. In verses 2–17 the Hebrew text carries two sets of accents. One divides the passage into verses as numbered here, which is the numbering of the Leningrad Codex and of most English Bibles. The other, used for public reading, treats each commandment as a single unit, so some Jewish editions number the verses of this passage differently.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "out of the house of slaves".
[^4]: Or "before me", "in my presence". Hebrew *al panai*, literally "on my face".
[^5]: Hebrew *pesel*, a carved image.
[^6]: Or "worship them". Hebrew *avad*.
[^7]: Hebrew *el qanna*, a God who demands exclusive loyalty. Or "a zealous God".
[^8]: Literally "visiting the wrongdoing of fathers on sons".
[^9]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^10]: Or "to a thousand generations".
[^11]: Literally "You must not lift up the name of Yahweh your God for emptiness", traditionally "take the name … in vain". This includes using God's name in a false oath or for no good purpose; likewise later in the verse.
[^12]: Or "servant". Hebrew *eved*, *amah*; also in verse 17.
[^13]: Literally "within your gates". Hebrew *ger*, a foreigner who lives among the Israelites.
[^14]: Hebrew *kabbed*, from *kaved*, "be heavy": to give weight to, to respect.
[^15]: Hebrew *ratsach*, the unlawful killing of a person. Traditionally "You shall not kill."
[^16]: Hebrew *chamad*, "desire, crave": to want for oneself what belongs to someone else; also later in the verse.
[^17]: The Samaritan Pentateuch adds here a command to set up stones and build an altar on Mount Gerizim (compare Deuteronomy 11:29–30; 27:2–7).
[^18]: Literally "the voices and the torches".
[^19]: Literally "on your faces".
[^20]: Or "You must not make gods of silver to be with me, and you must not make gods of gold for yourselves." The Hebrew can be divided either way.
[^21]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings", sacrifices shared in a meal by the worshipers.
[^22]: Literally "your sword".
