**1** The LORD[^1] said to Moses,

**2** "Tell the Israelites to turn back and camp in front of Pi-hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea. You are to camp by the sea, in front of Baal-zephon, opposite it.

**3** Pharaoh will say of the Israelites, 'They are wandering around the land in confusion. The wilderness has shut them in.'

**4** I will harden[^2] Pharaoh's heart, and he will pursue them. Then I will gain glory[^3] for myself through Pharaoh and his whole army, and the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD." And they did so.

**5** When the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled, Pharaoh and his officials[^4] changed their minds about the people. They said, "What have we done? We have let Israel go, and they will no longer serve us!"

**6** So he had his chariot harnessed and took his army[^5] with him.

**7** He took six hundred select chariots, and all the other chariots of Egypt, with officers[^6] over all of them.

**8** The LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and he pursued the Israelites, who were going out boldly.[^7]

**9** The Egyptians pursued them, all Pharaoh's chariot horses, his horsemen[^8] and his army, and they overtook them as they were camped by the sea, near Pi-hahiroth, in front of Baal-zephon.

**10** As Pharaoh came near, the Israelites looked up, and there were the Egyptians marching after them! They were terrified, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.

**11** They said to Moses, "Was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you have taken us away to die in the wilderness? What have you done to us, bringing us out of Egypt?

**12** Isn't this what we told you in Egypt: 'Leave us alone, and let us serve the Egyptians'? It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness."

**13** Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. Stand firm and see how the LORD will save you today.[^9] The Egyptians you see today you will never see again.

**14** The LORD will fight for you. You need only to be still."

**15** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Why are you crying out to me? Tell the Israelites to move on.

**16** As for you, raise your staff and stretch out your hand over the sea and split it, so that the Israelites can go through the middle of the sea on dry ground.

**17** And I will harden the hearts of the Egyptians, and they will go in after them. Then I will gain glory for myself through Pharaoh and his whole army, his chariots and his horsemen.

**18** The Egyptians will know that I am the LORD when I have gained glory for myself through Pharaoh, his chariots and his horsemen."

**19** Then the angel[^10] of God, who had been going ahead of the camp of Israel, moved and went behind them. The pillar of cloud moved from in front of them and stood behind them.

**20** It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel. There was the cloud and the darkness, and it lit up the night.[^11] So neither came near the other all night.

**21** Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and all that night the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind and turned the sea into dry land. The waters were split,

**22** and the Israelites went through the middle of the sea on dry ground, with the waters forming a wall for them on their right and on their left.

**23** The Egyptians pursued them and went in after them into the middle of the sea, all Pharaoh's horses, his chariots and his horsemen.

**24** In the morning watch,[^12] the LORD looked down on the Egyptian army from the pillar of fire and cloud, and he threw the Egyptian army into confusion.

**25** He made the wheels of their chariots come off,[^13] so that they drove with difficulty.[^14] The Egyptians said, "Let us flee from Israel, for the LORD is fighting for them against Egypt!"

**26** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, so that the waters may come back over the Egyptians, over their chariots and their horsemen."

**27** So Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and at daybreak the sea went back to its normal place. The Egyptians were fleeing toward it, and the LORD swept the Egyptians into the middle of the sea.

**28** The waters came back and covered the chariots and the horsemen, the whole army of Pharaoh that had gone into the sea after them. Not one of them was left.

**29** But the Israelites had walked on dry ground through the middle of the sea, with the waters forming a wall for them on their right and on their left.

**30** That day the LORD saved Israel from the power[^15] of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians lying dead on the seashore.

**31** When Israel saw the great power[^16] that the LORD had shown against the Egyptians, the people feared the LORD, and they believed in the LORD and in his servant Moses.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *chazaq*, "make strong, firm"; also in verses 8 and 17. On the verbs used for Pharaoh's heart, see the note on 4:21.
[^3]: Hebrew *ikkavedah*, from *kaved*, "be heavy, be honored", the verb used elsewhere for Pharaoh's stubborn heart; also in verses 17 and 18.
[^4]: Literally "servants".
[^5]: Literally "his people".
[^6]: Hebrew *shalishim*, perhaps "third men", the third soldier in a chariot.
[^7]: Literally "with a raised hand".
[^8]: Or "charioteers"; also in verses 17, 18, 23, 26 and 28.
[^9]: Literally "see the salvation of the LORD, which he will do for you today".
[^10]: Or "messenger".
[^11]: The Hebrew is difficult. It may mean that the cloud brought darkness to one side and light to the other. The Greek Septuagint reads "and there was darkness and gloom, and the night passed".
[^12]: The last part of the night, before dawn.
[^13]: The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "He clogged the wheels of their chariots".
[^14]: Literally "with heaviness". Hebrew *kevedut*, from *kaved*.
[^15]: Literally "hand".
[^16]: Literally "the great hand".
