**1** Moses was tending the flock of his father-in-law Jethro, the priest of Midian. He led the flock to the far side of the wilderness[^1] and came to Horeb,[^2] the mountain of God.

**2** There the angel[^3] of Yahweh[^4] appeared to him in a flame of fire from the middle of a bush.[^5] Moses looked, and the bush was burning with fire, but the bush was not burned up.

**3** So Moses said, "I will go over and look at this amazing sight. Why isn't the bush burning up?"

**4** When Yahweh saw that he had gone over to look, God called to him from the middle of the bush: "Moses, Moses!" He answered, "Here I am."

**5** God said, "Do not come any closer. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy ground."

**6** He said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Then Moses hid his face, because he was afraid to look at God.

**7** Yahweh said, "I have seen, I have truly seen, the misery of my people in Egypt. I have heard them crying out because of their slave drivers. I know how they are suffering.

**8** I have come down to rescue them from the power[^6] of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and spacious land, a land flowing with milk and honey,[^7] the home of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites.

**9** And now, the cry of the Israelites has reached me, and I have also seen how the Egyptians are oppressing them.

**10** So now, go. I am sending you to Pharaoh. Bring my people, the Israelites, out of Egypt."

**11** But Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh and bring the Israelites out of Egypt?"

**12** God said, "I will be with you.[^8] And this will be the sign for you that it is I who have sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you will all serve[^9] God on this mountain."

**13** Moses said to God, "Suppose I go to the Israelites and say to them, 'The God of your ancestors[^10] has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' What shall I tell them?"

**14** God said to Moses, "I am who I am."[^11] And he said, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'I Am[^12] has sent me to you.'"

**15** God also said to Moses, "This is what you are to say to the Israelites: 'Yahweh,[^13] the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.' This is my name forever, and this is how I am to be remembered from generation to generation.

**16** Go and gather the elders of Israel, and say to them, 'Yahweh, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, appeared to me and said: I have paid close attention[^14] to you and to what has been done to you in Egypt.

**17** And I have promised that I will bring you up out of your misery in Egypt to the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.'

**18** They will listen to you. Then you and the elders of Israel are to go to the king of Egypt and say to him, 'Yahweh, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. Now please let us go on a three-day journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to Yahweh our God.'

**19** But I know that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless he is forced by a mighty hand.[^15]

**20** So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all the wonders that I will do among them. After that he will let you go.

**21** I will make the Egyptians look with favor on this people,[^16] so that when you go, you will not go empty-handed.

**22** Every woman is to ask her neighbor, and any woman living in her house, for objects of silver and gold and for clothing. You will put them on your sons and your daughters. So you will plunder[^17] the Egyptians."

[^1]: Or "to the west side of the wilderness". Literally "behind the wilderness".
[^2]: The mountain is called Horeb here and in 17:6, and Sinai in chapter 19.
[^3]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh*. In the next verses it is Yahweh and God who speak from the bush.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Hebrew *seneh*, a thorny bush. The word sounds like *Sinai*.
[^6]: Literally "hand".
[^7]: A land rich in pasture for flocks and herds and in honey, either from bees or a syrup made from dates.
[^8]: Hebrew *ehyeh immakh*. *Ehyeh*, "I will be", is the same word that God uses of himself in verse 14.
[^9]: Or "worship". Hebrew *avad*, the verb also used for the Israelites' slave labor in Egypt (1:13–14).
[^10]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 15 and 16.
[^11]: Hebrew *ehyeh asher ehyeh*. *Ehyeh* is a form of the verb *hayah*, "be", that can mean "I am" or "I will be", and *asher* can mean "who", "what" or "that". So the phrase can also be translated "I will be who I will be" or "I will be what I will be". The Greek Septuagint reads "I am the one who is". It has been understood as a statement of God's own being, as a promise of his presence (compare "I will be with you" in verse 12), or as a refusal to be defined by any name. The text does not explain it.
[^12]: Hebrew *ehyeh*, "I am" or "I will be".
[^13]: The name YHWH appears to come from the same verb, *hayah* (in an older form *hawah*), "be". In verse 14 God speaks of himself as *ehyeh*, "I am"; the name *Yahweh* is probably the matching form, "he is" or "he will be", by which others speak of him. Some scholars take it to mean "he causes to be". Traditionally "the LORD"; see the note on verse 2.
[^14]: Hebrew *paqod paqadti*, "I have surely visited" or "attended to", the words Joseph used in Genesis 50:24–25.
[^15]: Literally "and not by a mighty hand", which may mean "not even when he is forced by a mighty hand". The Greek Septuagint reads "unless by a mighty hand".
[^16]: Literally "I will give this people favor in the eyes of Egypt".
[^17]: The same Hebrew verb, *natsal*, as "rescue" in verse 8.
