**1** Moses answered, "But what if they do not believe me or listen to what I say? They may say, 'Yahweh[^1] did not appear to you.'"

**2** Yahweh said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A staff."

**3** He said, "Throw it on the ground." So he threw it on the ground, and it became a snake, and Moses ran away from it.

**4** Then Yahweh said to Moses, "Reach out your hand and take hold of it by the tail." So he reached out his hand and grabbed it, and it became a staff in his hand.

**5** "This is so that they may believe that Yahweh, the God of their ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you."

**6** Yahweh also said to him, "Put your hand inside your cloak." So he put his hand inside his cloak, and when he took it out, his hand had a skin disease,[^2] white as snow.

**7** Then he said, "Put your hand back inside your cloak." So he put his hand back inside his cloak, and when he took it out of his cloak, it had become like the rest of his body again.

**8** "If they do not believe you or pay attention to the first sign,[^3] they may believe the second sign.

**9** And if they do not believe even these two signs or listen to you, take some water from the Nile and pour it on the dry ground. The water you take from the Nile will become blood on the dry ground."

**10** Moses said to Yahweh, "Please, Lord, I have never been a man of words, not in the past[^4] and not since you have spoken to your servant. I am slow of speech and slow of tongue."[^5]

**11** Yahweh said to him, "Who gave human beings their mouths? Who makes a person unable to speak or unable to hear, able to see or blind? Is it not I, Yahweh?

**12** Now go. I will be[^6] with your mouth, and I will teach you what to say."

**13** But Moses said, "Please, Lord, send someone else."[^7]

**14** Then Yahweh's anger burned against Moses, and he said, "What about your brother Aaron the Levite? I know that he can speak well. And look, he is already coming out to meet you, and when he sees you, he will be glad in his heart.

**15** You will speak to him and put the words in his mouth. I will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you both what to do.

**16** He will speak to the people for you. He will be your mouth, and you will be like God to him.[^8]

**17** And take this staff in your hand, so that you can perform the signs with it."

**18** Then Moses went back to his father-in-law Jether[^9] and said to him, "Please let me go back to my own people[^10] in Egypt and see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace."

**19** Yahweh said to Moses in Midian, "Go back to Egypt, for all the men who wanted to kill you are dead."[^11]

**20** So Moses took his wife and his sons, put them on a donkey, and went back to the land of Egypt. Moses took the staff of God in his hand.

**21** Yahweh said to Moses, "When you go back to Egypt, see that you perform in front of Pharaoh all the wonders I have put in your power.[^12] But I will harden his heart,[^13] and he will not let the people go.

**22** Then say to Pharaoh, 'This is what Yahweh says: Israel is my son, my firstborn.

**23** I told you, "Let my son go, so that he may serve[^14] me," but you refused to let him go. So now I will kill your son, your firstborn.'"

**24** On the way, at the place where they stopped for the night, Yahweh met him and tried to kill him.[^15]

**25** But Zipporah took a flint knife, cut off her son's foreskin, and touched his feet[^16] with it. She said, "Surely you are a bridegroom of blood to me."

**26** So he let him alone. At that time she said "a bridegroom of blood" because of the circumcision.[^17]

**27** Yahweh said to Aaron, "Go into the wilderness to meet Moses." So he went and met him at the mountain of God, and he kissed him.

**28** Moses told Aaron everything that Yahweh had sent him to say, and all the signs he had commanded him to perform.

**29** Then Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the Israelites.

**30** Aaron told them everything that Yahweh had said to Moses, and he performed the signs in front of the people.

**31** The people believed. When they heard that Yahweh had paid attention[^18] to the Israelites and had seen their misery, they bowed down and worshiped.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *metsora'at*, traditionally "leprous". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy. *White as snow* may describe the color or the flaking of the skin.
[^3]: Literally "listen to the voice of the first sign". Likewise "believe the voice of the second sign".
[^4]: Literally "neither yesterday nor the day before".
[^5]: Literally "heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue".
[^6]: Hebrew *ehyeh*, the word God uses in 3:12 and 3:14; also in verse 15.
[^7]: Literally "send by the hand of whomever you will send".
[^8]: Literally "you will be God to him": Moses will tell Aaron what to say, as God tells a prophet. Compare 7:1.
[^9]: Hebrew *Yeter*, a shorter form of *Yitro*, Jethro, the name used later in the verse.
[^10]: Literally "my brothers".
[^11]: Literally "all the men who were seeking your life are dead". Hebrew *nephesh*, "life".
[^12]: Literally "in your hand".
[^13]: Hebrew *chazaq*, "make strong, firm". Exodus describes Pharaoh's heart with three different verbs: *chazaq*, "be strong, firm" (in this translation "harden", "hard"); *kaved*, "be heavy" ("make stubborn", "stubborn"); and *qashah*, "be hard, harsh" ("make unyielding"). In Hebrew the heart is the center of thought and will, not only of feeling.
[^14]: Or "worship". Hebrew *avad*, the verb also used for Israel's slave labor (1:13–14).
[^15]: Literally "him … him". The Hebrew does not say whether Moses or his son is meant.
[^16]: The Hebrew does not say whose feet are meant: Moses', the boy's, or Yahweh's. *Feet* may also be a polite way of referring to the genitals.
[^17]: Or "with regard to circumcision". The meaning of these verses is uncertain. Hebrew *chatan*, "bridegroom", is related to an Arabic word meaning "circumcise".
[^18]: Hebrew *paqad*; see 3:16.
