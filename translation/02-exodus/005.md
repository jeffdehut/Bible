**1** Afterward Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh and said, "This is what the LORD,[^1] the God of Israel, says: 'Let my people go, so that they may hold a festival for me in the wilderness.'"

**2** Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should listen to him and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and besides, I will not let Israel go."

**3** They said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Please let us go on a three-day journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to the LORD our God, or he may strike us with plague or with the sword."

**4** But the king of Egypt said to them, "Moses and Aaron, why are you taking the people away from their work? Get back to your labor!"

**5** Pharaoh also said, "Look, the people of the land are now many,[^2] and you are making them stop their labor."

**6** That same day Pharaoh gave orders to the slave drivers over the people and to their foremen:[^3]

**7** "You are no longer to give the people straw for making bricks, as you did before.[^4] Let them go and gather straw for themselves.

**8** But require them to make the same number of bricks as they were making before. Do not reduce it. They are lazy. That is why they are crying out, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God.'

**9** Make the work heavier for the men, so that they keep at it and pay no attention to lies."

**10** So the people's slave drivers and their foremen went out and said to the people, "This is what Pharaoh says: 'I will not give you any straw.

**11** Go and get straw for yourselves wherever you can find it. But your work will not be reduced at all.'"

**12** So the people scattered throughout the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.

**13** The slave drivers kept pressing them: "Finish your work, the amount required each day, just as when there was straw."

**14** The Israelite foremen, whom Pharaoh's slave drivers had put over them, were beaten and asked, "Why didn't you finish your quota of bricks yesterday or today, as before?"

**15** Then the Israelite foremen went and cried out to Pharaoh, "Why do you treat your servants this way?

**16** No straw is given to your servants, yet we are told, 'Make bricks!' Now your servants are being beaten, but the fault is your own people's."[^5]

**17** He said, "You are lazy, lazy! That is why you are saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'

**18** Now go and work. No straw will be given to you, but you must still deliver the full quota of bricks."

**19** The Israelite foremen saw that they were in trouble when they were told, "You must not reduce your daily quota of bricks."

**20** As they came out from Pharaoh, they met Moses and Aaron, who were standing there waiting for them.

**21** They said to them, "May the LORD look on you and judge you! You have made us repulsive to Pharaoh and his officials,[^6] and you have put a sword in their hand to kill us."

**22** Moses went back to the LORD and said, "Lord, why have you brought trouble on this people? Why did you ever send me?

**23** Ever since I came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has brought trouble on this people, and you have not rescued your people at all."

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: The Samaritan Pentateuch reads "Look, they are now more numerous than the people of the land".
[^3]: Hebrew *shoterim*, officials, perhaps record keepers. Verse 14 shows that they were Israelites placed over their own people.
[^4]: Literally "as yesterday and the day before"; also in verses 8 and 14.
[^5]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and your people sins" or "and the sin is your people's". The Greek Septuagint reads "so you will wrong your people".
[^6]: Literally "You have made our smell stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants".
