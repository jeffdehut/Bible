**1** He made the altar of burnt offering of acacia wood. It was square, five cubits[^1] long and five cubits wide, and it was three cubits high.

**2** He made horns for it on its four corners. Its horns were of one piece with it, and he overlaid it with bronze.

**3** He made all the utensils of the altar: the pots, the shovels, the bowls, the forks and the firepans. He made all its utensils of bronze.

**4** He made a grating for the altar, a network of bronze, under its ledge, below it, reaching halfway up.

**5** He cast four rings for the four corners of the bronze grating, to hold the poles.

**6** He made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with bronze.

**7** He put the poles through the rings on the sides of the altar, for carrying it. He made it hollow, out of boards.

**8** He made the bronze basin and its bronze stand from the mirrors of the women who served[^2] at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**9** He made the courtyard. On the south side the hangings of the courtyard were of fine twisted linen, a hundred cubits long,

**10** with twenty posts and twenty bronze bases. The hooks of the posts and their bands were of silver.

**11** On the north side the hangings were a hundred cubits long, with twenty posts and twenty bronze bases. The hooks of the posts and their bands were of silver.

**12** On the west side[^3] there were hangings fifty cubits long, with ten posts and ten bases. The hooks of the posts and their bands were of silver.

**13** On the east side, toward the sunrise, the courtyard was fifty cubits wide.

**14** There were hangings fifteen cubits long on one side of the entrance,[^4] with three posts and three bases,

**15** and on the other side, so that there were hangings on both sides of the gate of the courtyard, hangings fifteen cubits long, with three posts and three bases.

**16** All the hangings around the courtyard were of fine twisted linen.

**17** The bases for the posts were of bronze. The hooks of the posts and their bands were of silver, and their tops were overlaid with silver. All the posts of the courtyard had silver bands.

**18** The screen for the gate of the courtyard was the work of an embroiderer, of blue, purple and scarlet yarn[^5] and fine twisted linen. It was twenty cubits long and five cubits high,[^6] matching the hangings of the courtyard.

**19** It had four posts and four bronze bases. Their hooks were of silver, and the overlay of their tops and their bands were of silver.

**20** All the tent pegs for the tabernacle and for the courtyard around it were of bronze.

**21** This is the inventory[^7] of the tabernacle, the tabernacle of the testimony, which was recorded at Moses' command, as the work of the Levites under the direction of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest.

**22** Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made everything the LORD[^8] had commanded Moses.

**23** With him was Oholiab son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, a craftsman and designer, and an embroiderer in blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine linen.

**24** All the gold that was used for the work, in all the work on the sanctuary, the gold of the wave offering, came to 29 talents and 730 shekels,[^9] by the sanctuary shekel.[^10]

**25** The silver from those of the community who were counted came to 100 talents and 1,775 shekels, by the sanctuary shekel.[^11]

**26** That was a beka[^12] for each person, that is, half a shekel by the sanctuary shekel, for everyone who crossed over to those counted, from twenty years old and over: 603,550 in all.[^13]

**27** The hundred talents of silver were used to cast the bases of the sanctuary and the bases of the veil: a hundred bases from the hundred talents, a talent for each base.

**28** From the 1,775 shekels he made hooks for the posts, overlaid their tops and made bands for them.

**29** The bronze of the wave offering came to 70 talents and 2,400 shekels.[^14]

**30** With it he made the bases for the entrance of the tent of meeting, the bronze altar with its bronze grating, and all the utensils of the altar,

**31** the bases around the courtyard and the bases for the gate of the courtyard, and all the tent pegs of the tabernacle and all the tent pegs around the courtyard.

[^1]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the altar was about 7½ feet square and 4½ feet high (2.3 by 2.3 by 1.4 m).
[^2]: Mirrors were made of polished bronze. The Hebrew verb *tsava'* is used of service at the sanctuary (Numbers 4:23; 1 Samuel 2:22), but the text does not say what these women did.
[^3]: Literally "the side of the sea", that is, toward the Mediterranean.
[^4]: Literally "toward the shoulder", the side wing of the entrance. Also in verse 15.
[^5]: Literally "blue and purple and crimson worm": wools dyed violet-blue, red-purple and crimson. See 25:4.
[^6]: Literally "and the height, in width, five cubits", that is, the width of the cloth, which formed its height.
[^7]: Hebrew *pequdei*, "the things counted", the same root as "counted" in verses 25–26.
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), and a shekel about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams). A talent was 3,000 shekels (see verses 25–26), so the gold weighed about 2,200 pounds (1,000 kg).
[^10]: Literally "the shekel of the holy place", the standard weight used at the sanctuary (30:13).
[^11]: About 7,500 pounds (3,400 kg).
[^12]: Hebrew *beqa*, "a split": half a shekel, about ⅕ ounce (5.7 grams).
[^13]: Half a shekel each from 603,550 people makes 301,775 shekels, that is, 100 talents and 1,775 shekels (verse 25). Compare Numbers 1:46.
[^14]: About 5,300 pounds (2,400 kg).
