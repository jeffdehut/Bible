**1** Then the LORD[^1] said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and tell him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go, so that they may serve[^2] me.

**2** If you refuse to let them go and keep holding on to them,[^3]

**3** the hand of the LORD will bring a very severe plague[^4] on your livestock in the fields: on the horses, the donkeys, the camels, the cattle, and the sheep and goats.

**4** But the LORD will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt, so that nothing belonging to the Israelites will die.'"

**5** The LORD set a time and said, "Tomorrow the LORD will do this in the land."

**6** And the next day the LORD did it. All the livestock of Egypt died, but not one animal of the Israelites' livestock died.

**7** Pharaoh sent to find out, and not even one of the livestock of Israel had died. But Pharaoh's heart was stubborn,[^5] and he did not let the people go.

**8** Then the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "Take handfuls of soot from a kiln, and let Moses throw it into the air in front of Pharaoh.

**9** It will become fine dust over the whole land of Egypt, and it will cause boils[^6] that break out into sores on people and animals throughout the land of Egypt."

**10** So they took soot from a kiln and stood in front of Pharaoh. Moses threw it into the air, and it caused boils that broke out into sores on people and animals.

**11** The magicians could not stand in front of Moses because of the boils, for the boils were on the magicians and on all the Egyptians.

**12** But the LORD hardened[^7] Pharaoh's heart, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said to Moses.

**13** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Get up early in the morning, stand in front of Pharaoh, and say to him, 'This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Let my people go, so that they may serve me.

**14** For this time I am going to send all my plagues against you yourself,[^8] and against your officials[^9] and your people, so that you may know that there is no one like me in all the earth.

**15** For by now I could have stretched out my hand and struck you and your people with plague, and you would have been wiped off the earth.

**16** But this is why I have let you live:[^10] to show you my power, and so that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth.

**17** You are still setting yourself up against my people and not letting them go.

**18** So at this time tomorrow I will send down a very heavy hail, such as has never been in Egypt from the day it was founded until now.

**19** Now send word, and bring your livestock and everything you have in the fields to shelter. Every person and animal that is found in the fields and not brought into the house will die when the hail comes down on them.'"

**20** Those of Pharaoh's officials who feared the word of the LORD hurried their slaves[^11] and their livestock into the houses.

**21** But those who paid no attention to the word of the LORD left their slaves and their livestock in the fields.

**22** Then the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, so that there will be hail throughout the land of Egypt, on people and on animals and on every plant of the field in the land of Egypt."

**23** Moses stretched out his staff toward the sky, and the LORD sent thunder[^12] and hail, and fire came down to the earth. So the LORD rained hail on the land of Egypt.

**24** There was hail, and fire flashing[^13] in the middle of the hail. It was very heavy, such as had never been in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.

**25** Throughout the land of Egypt the hail struck down everything that was in the fields, both people and animals. The hail struck down every plant of the field and shattered every tree of the field.

**26** Only in the land of Goshen, where the Israelites were, was there no hail.

**27** Then Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron and said to them, "This time I have sinned. The LORD is in the right, and I and my people are in the wrong.

**28** Pray to the LORD. We have had enough of God's thunder[^14] and hail. I will let you go; you will not have to stay any longer."

**29** Moses said to him, "As soon as I have gone out of the city, I will spread out my hands to the LORD. The thunder will stop, and there will be no more hail, so that you may know that the earth belongs to the LORD.

**30** But as for you and your officials, I know that you do not yet fear the LORD God."

**31** The flax and the barley were struck down, because the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bud.

**32** But the wheat and the spelt were not struck down, because they ripen later.

**33** Moses left Pharaoh and went out of the city, and he spread out his hands to the LORD. The thunder and the hail stopped, and the rain no longer poured down on the earth.

**34** When Pharaoh saw that the rain, the hail and the thunder had stopped, he sinned again: he made his heart stubborn,[^15] he and his officials.

**35** So Pharaoh's heart grew hard,[^16] and he did not let the Israelites go, just as the LORD had said through Moses.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "worship"; also in verse 13.
[^3]: Hebrew *machaziq*, a form of *chazaq*, the verb used for Pharaoh's hardened heart (verses 12 and 35).
[^4]: Literally "a very heavy plague". Hebrew *dever*, a deadly epidemic.
[^5]: Literally "heavy". Hebrew *kaved*. On the verbs used for Pharaoh's heart, see the note on 4:21.
[^6]: Hebrew *shechin*, an inflamed swelling of the skin. The exact disease is not known.
[^7]: Hebrew *chazaq*, "make strong, firm".
[^8]: Literally "against your heart".
[^9]: Literally "servants"; also in verses 20, 30 and 34.
[^10]: Literally "I have caused you to stand". Or "I have raised you up", the sense in which Paul quotes this verse in Romans 9:17.
[^11]: Literally "servants". The Hebrew uses the same word, *avadim*, for Pharaoh's officials and for their slaves.
[^12]: Literally "voices"; also in verses 28, 29, 33 and 34.
[^13]: Or "lightning flashing". Literally "fire taking hold of itself".
[^14]: Literally "voices of God", which may mean "mighty thunder".
[^15]: Literally "made his heart heavy". Hebrew *kaved*.
[^16]: Hebrew *chazaq*, "be strong, firm".
