**1** "These are the rulings[^1] you are to set before them.

**2** If you buy a Hebrew slave,[^2] he is to serve for six years, but in the seventh year he is to go free, without paying anything.

**3** If he came in single, he is to go out single. If he had a wife when he came, his wife is to go out with him.

**4** If his master gives him a wife and she bears him sons or daughters, the wife and her children will belong to her master, and he is to go out alone.

**5** But if the slave declares, 'I love my master, my wife and my children. I will not go free,'

**6** then his master must bring him to God.[^3] He is to bring him to the door or the doorpost, and his master is to pierce his ear with an awl. Then he will be his slave for life.

**7** If a man sells his daughter as a slave woman,[^4] she is not to go free as the male slaves do.

**8** If she does not please her master, who has designated her for himself,[^5] he must let her be bought back. He has no right to sell her to foreigners, since he has broken faith with her.

**9** If he designates her for his son, he must treat her as he would a daughter.[^6]

**10** If he takes another wife for himself, he must not reduce her food, her clothing or her marital rights.[^7]

**11** If he does not provide her with these three things, she is to go free, without paying any money.

**12** Whoever strikes a person so that he dies must be put to death.

**13** But if he did not lie in wait for him, and God let him fall into his hands,[^8] I will appoint a place for you to which he may flee.

**14** But if someone plots against his neighbor and kills him by treachery, you must take him even from my altar to be put to death.

**15** Whoever strikes his father or his mother must be put to death.

**16** Whoever kidnaps someone[^9] must be put to death, whether he has sold him or the person is still found in his possession.

**17** Whoever curses his father or his mother must be put to death.

**18** If men quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or with his fist, and he does not die but has to stay in bed,

**19** then if he gets up and walks around outside with the help of a staff, the one who struck him will not be held liable. He must only pay for the time he lost and see that he is fully healed.

**20** If a man strikes his male or female slave with a rod, and the slave dies at his hand, the slave must be avenged.[^10]

**21** But if the slave survives a day or two, he is not to be avenged, for the slave is his property.[^11]

**22** If men are fighting and they hit a pregnant woman so that her children come out,[^12] but there is no further harm,[^13] the one responsible must be fined whatever the woman's husband demands of him, and he is to pay as the judges decide.[^14]

**23** But if there is further harm, you are to give life[^15] for life,

**24** eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,

**25** burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.

**26** If a man strikes the eye of his male or female slave and destroys it, he must let the slave go free to make up for the eye.

**27** And if he knocks out the tooth of his male or female slave, he must let the slave go free to make up for the tooth.

**28** If an ox gores a man or a woman to death, the ox must be stoned, and its meat must not be eaten, but the owner of the ox will not be held liable.

**29** But if the ox has been in the habit of goring,[^16] and its owner has been warned but has not kept it under control, and it kills a man or a woman, the ox must be stoned, and its owner too must be put to death.

**30** If a ransom is set for him instead, he must pay whatever is set for him to redeem his life.

**31** If it gores a son or a daughter, the same ruling applies.[^17]

**32** If the ox gores a male or female slave, the owner must pay thirty shekels[^18] of silver to the slave's master, and the ox must be stoned.

**33** If a man opens a pit, or digs a pit and does not cover it, and an ox or a donkey falls into it,

**34** the owner of the pit must make good the loss. He must pay money to its owner, and the dead animal will be his.

**35** If a man's ox injures his neighbor's ox and it dies, they are to sell the live ox and divide the money, and they are to divide the dead animal as well.

**36** But if it was known that the ox had been in the habit of goring, and its owner did not keep it under control, he must pay in full, ox for ox, and the dead animal will be his.

**37** If a man steals an ox or a sheep[^19] and slaughters it or sells it, he must repay five cattle for the ox and four sheep for the sheep.[^20]

[^1]: Hebrew *mishpatim*, "rulings, judgments": laws for deciding cases. God's words to Moses that began in 20:22 continue to the end of chapter 23.
[^2]: Or "servant". Hebrew *eved*, a man who works for a master, usually to pay off a debt; also in verses 5–7, 20–21, 26–27 and 32.
[^3]: Or "to the judges". Hebrew *ha-elohim*, "God". It may mean a sanctuary, or judges acting in God's name.
[^4]: Hebrew *amah*, "slave woman, servant"; also in verses 20, 26, 27 and 32.
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "who has not designated her" but read "who has designated her for himself". The two words sound alike.
[^6]: Literally "he must do for her according to the rights of daughters".
[^7]: The meaning of the Hebrew word *onah*, translated "marital rights", is uncertain. Some understand it as "oil" or "shelter".
[^8]: Literally "and God caused it to happen to his hand".
[^9]: Literally "Whoever steals a man".
[^10]: Or "punished". The Hebrew does not say how.
[^11]: Literally "his silver", "his money".
[^12]: That is, she gives birth early or loses the child. The Hebrew can mean either.
[^13]: Hebrew *ason*, serious injury or death; also in verse 23. The text does not say whether the harm is to the woman, to the child, or to both.
[^14]: The meaning of the Hebrew word translated "as the judges decide" is uncertain.
[^15]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "life".
[^16]: Literally "if the ox has been goring yesterday and the day before"; also in verse 36.
[^17]: Literally "it is to be done to him according to this ruling".
[^18]: Thirty shekels is about 12 ounces (345 grams).
[^19]: Hebrew *seh*, a sheep or a goat; likewise "four sheep" (*tson*, "flock animals") later in the verse.
[^20]: Verse 37 is 22:1 in most English Bibles.
