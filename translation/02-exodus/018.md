**1** Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law, heard about everything God had done for Moses and for his people Israel, how Yahweh[^1] had brought Israel out of Egypt.

**2** So Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses' wife, after Moses had sent her away,[^2]

**3** and her two sons. One of them was named Gershom, for Moses said, "I have become a foreigner in a foreign land."[^3]

**4** The other was named Eliezer,[^4] for he said, "The God of my father was my help; he rescued me from Pharaoh's sword."

**5** Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, came with Moses' sons and wife to Moses in the wilderness, where he was camped at the mountain of God.

**6** He sent word to Moses, "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you, with your wife and her two sons."[^5]

**7** Moses went out to meet his father-in-law. He bowed down and kissed him, and they asked each other how they were.[^6] Then they went into the tent.

**8** Moses told his father-in-law everything Yahweh had done to Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel's sake, all the hardships that had come on them along the way, and how Yahweh had rescued them.

**9** Jethro rejoiced over all the good that Yahweh had done for Israel in rescuing them from the power[^7] of the Egyptians.

**10** Jethro said, "Blessed be Yahweh, who has rescued you from the power of the Egyptians and from the power of Pharaoh, who has rescued the people from under the power of the Egyptians.

**11** Now I know that Yahweh is greater than all the gods, because of what happened when they acted arrogantly against them."[^8]

**12** Then Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, brought a burnt offering and sacrifices to God, and Aaron came with all the elders of Israel to eat a meal[^9] with Moses' father-in-law in the presence of God.

**13** The next day Moses sat down to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from morning until evening.

**14** When Moses' father-in-law saw all that he was doing for the people, he said, "What is this you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone, while all the people stand around you from morning until evening?"

**15** Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.[^10]

**16** Whenever they have a dispute, it comes to me, and I decide between one person and another. And I make known to them God's decrees and his instructions."[^11]

**17** Moses' father-in-law said to him, "What you are doing is not good.

**18** You will wear yourself out, both you and these people who are with you, for the task is too heavy for you. You cannot do it alone.

**19** Now listen to me. I will give you advice, and may God be with you. You are to represent the people before God and bring their cases to God.

**20** Teach them the decrees and the instructions, and show them the way they are to live[^12] and the things they are to do.

**21** But look among all the people for capable men who fear God, trustworthy men who hate dishonest gain. Appoint them over the people as officials over thousands, officials over hundreds, officials over fifties and officials over tens.

**22** Let them judge the people at all times. Every major case they are to bring to you, but every minor case they can decide themselves. That will make your load lighter, and they will carry it with you.

**23** If you do this, and God so commands you, you will be able to stand the strain, and all these people too will go home in peace."[^13]

**24** Moses listened to his father-in-law and did everything he had said.

**25** Moses chose capable men from all Israel and made them heads over the people, officials over thousands, officials over hundreds, officials over fifties and officials over tens.

**26** They judged the people at all times. The difficult cases they brought to Moses, but every minor case they decided themselves.

**27** Then Moses let his father-in-law go, and he went back to his own land.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "after her sending away". The earlier chapters do not tell when this happened; compare 4:20–26.
[^3]: See 2:22. *Gershom* sounds like *ger sham*, "a foreigner there".
[^4]: *Eliezer* means "My God is help".
[^5]: The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Greek Septuagint and the Syriac read "Someone said to Moses, 'Look, your father-in-law Jethro is coming to you …'".
[^6]: Literally "asked each other about peace". Hebrew *shalom*, "peace, well-being".
[^7]: Literally "hand"; also in verse 10.
[^8]: The Hebrew of this clause is difficult. Literally "for in the thing in which they acted arrogantly against them". *They* is probably the Egyptians and *them* the Israelites.
[^9]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^10]: That is, to ask God for a decision.
[^11]: Hebrew *torot*, "instructions, laws"; also in verse 20.
[^12]: Literally "the way they are to walk in".
[^13]: Literally "will come to their place in peace".
