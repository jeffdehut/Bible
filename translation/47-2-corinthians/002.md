**1** For I made up my mind[^1] not to come to you again in grief.[^2]

**2** For if I cause you grief, who is there to make me glad but the one whom I have grieved?

**3** And I wrote as I did[^3] so that when I came I would not be grieved by those who ought to make me rejoice, for I am confident about all of you that my joy is the joy of all of you.

**4** For I wrote to you out of much affliction and anguish of heart and with many tears, not to grieve you, but to let you know the love that I have especially for you.

**5** If anyone has caused grief, he has not grieved me, but to some extent, not to put it too heavily, he has grieved all of you.[^4]

**6** For such a person, this punishment by the majority is enough,

**7** so that now, instead, you should rather forgive and comfort him, so that he will not be overwhelmed[^5] by excessive grief.

**8** So I urge you to reaffirm your love for him.

**9** For this is why I wrote: to test you and know whether you are obedient in everything.

**10** Anyone you forgive, I also forgive. Indeed, what I have forgiven, if I have forgiven anything, has been for your sake in the presence of Christ,[^6]

**11** so that we may not be outwitted by Satan, for we are not unaware of his schemes.

**12** When I came to Troas for the good news of Christ, even though a door was opened for me in the Lord,

**13** I had no rest in my spirit because I did not find my brother Titus[^7] there. So I said goodbye to them and went on to Macedonia.

**14** But thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumphal procession[^8] in Christ, and through us spreads in every place the fragrance of the knowledge of him.

**15** For we are the pleasing aroma[^9] of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing,

**16** to the one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life. And who is adequate for these things?

**17** For we are not, like so many,[^10] peddlers[^11] of the word of God; but as people of sincerity, as people sent from God, we speak in Christ before God.

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "But I made up my mind".
[^2]: Or "not to make you another painful visit". Greek *lype*, "grief, sorrow, pain", and the related verb *lypeo*, "grieve, cause grief"; so throughout verses 1–7.
[^3]: Literally "I wrote this very thing". Paul refers to a letter written "with many tears" (verse 4; 7:8). Whether it is 1 Corinthians, a letter now lost, or a letter partly preserved in chapters 10–13 is debated.
[^4]: Or "but in part, so that I may not burden all of you".
[^5]: Literally "swallowed up".
[^6]: Or "as Christ's representative". Literally "in the face of Christ".
[^7]: One of Paul's fellow workers (7:6; 8:23; Galatians 2:1).
[^8]: Greek *thriambeuo*, used of a Roman general's victory procession, in which conquered captives were led through the city. Paul may picture himself as one of the captives, or as sharing in the triumph. Incense was burned along the route.
[^9]: Greek *euodia*, the word the Septuagint uses for the "pleasing aroma" of sacrifices offered to God (for example Genesis 8:21; Leviticus 1:9).
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "like the rest".
[^11]: Greek *kapeleuo*, to sell goods as a retailer, often with the suggestion of cheating.
