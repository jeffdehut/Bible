**1** I wish you would put up with a little foolishness from me. Indeed, you are putting up with me.[^1]

**2** For I am jealous for you with a godly jealousy,[^2] for I promised you in marriage to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ.

**3** But I am afraid that, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning,[^3] your thoughts may be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion[^4] to Christ.

**4** For if someone comes and proclaims another Jesus than the one we proclaimed, or you receive a different spirit from the one you received, or a different good news from the one you accepted, you put up with it well enough.[^5]

**5** For I consider that I am not in the least inferior to these super-apostles.[^6]

**6** Even if I am unskilled[^7] in speaking, I am not so in knowledge; indeed, in every way we have made this plain to you in all things.

**7** Or did I commit a sin by humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I proclaimed the good news of God to you free of charge?

**8** I robbed other churches by accepting support[^8] from them in order to serve you.

**9** And when I was with you and was in need, I was not a burden to anyone, for the brothers[^9] who came from Macedonia supplied my need. In every way I have kept myself from being a burden to you, and I will continue to do so.

**10** As the truth of Christ is in me, this boast of mine will not be silenced in the regions of Achaia.

**11** Why? Because I do not love you? God knows that I do![^10]

**12** And I will go on doing what I am doing, in order to cut off the opportunity from those who want an opportunity to be regarded as our equals in the things they boast about.

**13** For such people are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ.

**14** And no wonder, for Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light.

**15** So it is not surprising if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness. Their end will match their deeds.

**16** I say again, let no one think me a fool. But if you do, then accept me even as a fool, so that I too may boast a little.

**17** What I am saying in this confident boasting,[^11] I am not saying as the Lord would,[^12] but as in foolishness.

**18** Since many boast according to the flesh,[^13] I too will boast.

**19** For you gladly put up with fools, since you are so wise!

**20** For you put up with it if someone enslaves you, or devours you, or takes advantage of you,[^14] or puts on airs, or strikes you in the face.

**21** To my shame, I must say that we were too weak for that![^15] But whatever anyone dares to boast about (I am speaking in foolishness), I also dare to boast about.

**22** Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they descendants of Abraham?[^16] So am I.

**23** Are they servants of Christ? I am talking like a madman: I am more so. I have worked much harder, been in prison far more often, been beaten far more severely, and been near death many times.[^17]

**24** Five times I received from the Jews the forty lashes minus one.[^18]

**25** Three times I was beaten with rods.[^19] Once I was stoned.[^20] Three times I was shipwrecked. A night and a day I spent in the open sea.

**26** I have been on frequent journeys, in danger from rivers, danger from robbers, danger from my own people, danger from Gentiles, danger in the city, danger in the wilderness, danger at sea, danger among false brothers,[^21]

**27** in labor and hardship, through many sleepless nights, in hunger and thirst, often without food,[^22] in cold and without enough clothing.[^23]

**28** And besides other things, there is the pressure on me every day: my anxiety for all the churches.

**29** Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn with indignation?[^24]

**30** If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness.

**31** The God and Father of the Lord Jesus, he who is blessed forever, knows that I am not lying.

**32** In Damascus, the governor[^25] under King Aretas was guarding the city of the Damascenes in order to seize me,

**33** but I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall, and escaped his hands.

[^1]: Or "Do put up with me!"
[^2]: Literally "with a jealousy of God".
[^3]: Compare Genesis 3:13.
[^4]: Many manuscripts leave out "and pure". *Sincere*: Greek *haplotes* (see 8:2).
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "you would put up with it well enough".
[^6]: Greek *hyperlian apostoloi*, "the extra-special apostles", probably Paul's ironic name for his rivals in Corinth; also in 12:11.
[^7]: Greek *idiotes*, an ordinary person without special training (compare 1 Corinthians 14:16).
[^8]: Greek *opsonion*, "wages", especially a soldier's pay.
[^9]: Greek *adelphoi*, here probably believers sent from Macedonia with a gift (compare Philippians 4:15–16).
[^10]: Literally "God knows."
[^11]: Greek *hypostasis* (see 9:4).
[^12]: Literally "not according to the Lord".
[^13]: Greek *kata sarka*, "according to the flesh" (see 1:17), here of boasting about merely human advantages (compare verse 22).
[^14]: Literally "takes", perhaps in the sense "takes you in" or "exploits you".
[^15]: Said with irony. Literally "I speak according to dishonor, as that we have been weak."
[^16]: Literally "the seed of Abraham".
[^17]: Literally "in deaths often".
[^18]: Deuteronomy 25:3 limits a flogging to forty lashes. The practice was to give one fewer, so as not to exceed the limit.
[^19]: A Roman punishment (compare Acts 16:22–23).
[^20]: Compare Acts 14:19.
[^21]: Greek *pseudadelphoi*, people who falsely claimed to be fellow believers.
[^22]: Or "in many fastings".
[^23]: Literally "in cold and nakedness".
[^24]: Literally "burn". The word may express indignation, or distress.
[^25]: Greek *ethnarches*, "ruler of a people", an official of Aretas IV, king of the Nabateans (9 BC–AD 40). Compare Acts 9:23–25.
