**1** Since we have these promises, dear friends,[^1] let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and spirit,[^2] bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.

**2** Make room for us.[^3] We have wronged no one, we have ruined no one, we have taken advantage of no one.

**3** I do not say this to condemn you, for I have said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.

**4** I speak to you with great frankness;[^4] I have great reason to boast about you. I am filled with comfort; in all our affliction I am overflowing with joy.

**5** For even when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no rest.[^5] We were afflicted in every way: fights outside, fears inside.

**6** But God, who comforts the downcast,[^6] comforted us by the arrival of Titus,

**7** and not only by his arrival but also by the comfort with which he had been comforted by you, as he told us of your longing, your mourning, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced still more.

**8** For even if I grieved you with my letter,[^7] I do not regret it. Though I did regret it (I see that that letter grieved you, though only for a while),

**9** now I rejoice, not because you were grieved, but because your grief led to repentance. For you were grieved as God intended,[^8] so that you suffered no loss through us.

**10** For grief as God intends produces a repentance that leads to salvation and brings no regret,[^9] but the grief of the world produces death.

**11** For see what earnestness this very thing, being grieved as God intends, has produced in you: what eagerness to clear yourselves, what indignation, what alarm, what longing, what zeal, what readiness to see justice done! In everything you have shown yourselves to be innocent in this matter.

**12** So although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did wrong, nor for the sake of the one who was wronged, but so that your earnestness for us might be made plain to you before God.

**13** Therefore we have been comforted. And besides our own comfort,[^10] we rejoiced still more at the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you.

**14** For if I have boasted to him about you in anything, I was not put to shame. But just as everything we said to you was true, so our boasting to Titus has also proved true.

**15** And his affection[^11] for you is all the greater as he remembers the obedience of all of you, how you welcomed him with fear and trembling.

**16** I rejoice, because I have complete confidence in you.

[^1]: Literally "beloved".
[^2]: That is, of body and spirit. *Flesh* (Greek *sarx*) here means the body, not the flesh in Paul's moral sense. This verse ends the passage that began at 6:14 (see the note on 6:14).
[^3]: Literally "Make room for us", that is, in your hearts (compare 6:11–13).
[^4]: Or "I have great confidence in you". Greek *parresia* (see 3:12).
[^5]: That is, we ourselves, as weak human beings, had no rest. Compare "no rest in my spirit" in 2:13.
[^6]: Compare Isaiah 49:13.
[^7]: The letter written "with many tears" (2:3–4).
[^8]: Literally "according to God"; also in verses 10 and 11.
[^9]: "Brings no regret" may describe the repentance or the salvation. The Greek plays on *metanoia*, "repentance", and *ametameletos*, "not to be regretted".
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "your comfort".
[^11]: Greek *splanchna*, literally "inward parts", thought of as the seat of the emotions (see 6:12).
