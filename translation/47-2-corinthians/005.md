**1** For we know that if the earthly tent we live in[^1] is torn down, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.

**2** For in this one we groan, longing to put on over it[^2] our dwelling from heaven,

**3** if indeed, when we have put it on,[^3] we will not be found naked.

**4** For while we are in this tent, we groan under our burden, because we do not want to be unclothed but to be clothed over, so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life.

**5** The one who has prepared us for this very thing is God, who has given us the Spirit as a down payment.[^4]

**6** So we are always confident, and we know that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord,

**7** for we live[^5] by faith, not by sight—

**8** yes, we are confident, and we would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord.

**9** So whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to please him.

**10** For all of us must appear[^6] before the judgment seat[^7] of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or bad.

**11** Therefore, since we know the fear of the Lord, we try to persuade people. But what we are is plain to God, and I hope it is also plain to your consciences.

**12** We are not commending ourselves to you again, but giving you an opportunity to boast about us, so that you may have an answer for those who boast about outward appearance[^8] and not about what is in the heart.

**13** For if we are out of our minds, it is for God; if we are in our right mind, it is for you.

**14** For the love of Christ[^9] controls us,[^10] because we have concluded this: that one has died for all;[^11] therefore all have died.

**15** And he died for all, so that those who live would no longer live for themselves but for him who died for them and was raised.

**16** So from now on we know no one according to the flesh.[^12] Even if we have known Christ according to the flesh, we no longer know him so.

**17** So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation.[^13] The old has passed away; look, the new has come.[^14]

**18** All this is from God, who reconciled us to himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation,

**19** that is, in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself,[^15] not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation.

**20** So we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making his appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: be reconciled to God.

**21** He made him who knew no sin to be sin[^16] for our sake, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.[^17]

[^1]: Literally "our earthly house of the tent", a picture of the present body.
[^2]: Greek *ependyomai*, "put on over", as one garment over another; also in verse 4.
[^3]: Some manuscripts read "when we have taken it off".
[^4]: Greek *arrabon* (see 1:22).
[^5]: Literally "walk". *By sight*: or "by what we see"; literally "through appearance".
[^6]: Or "be revealed", "be made known". Greek *phaneroo*, "make visible", translated "plain" twice in verse 11.
[^7]: Greek *bema*, the raised platform from which a Roman official heard cases (compare Acts 18:12).
[^8]: Literally "in face".
[^9]: Most interpreters take this as Christ's love for us; it can also mean our love for Christ.
[^10]: Or "compels us", "holds us fast". Greek *synecho*.
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "that if one has died for all".
[^12]: Greek *kata sarka*, "according to the flesh": by human, worldly standards (see 1:17); also later in the verse. Whether "according to the flesh" describes the way of knowing (a worldly way of knowing Christ) or Christ himself (Christ as a human being) is debated; most take it in the first way.
[^13]: Or "he is a new creation". Compare Isaiah 43:18–19 and 65:17.
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "look, all things have become new".
[^15]: Or "that God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself".
[^16]: "He" is God (verse 20). The phrase "made him … to be sin" is much discussed. Some interpreters understand "to be a sin offering": the Hebrew *chatta't* means both "sin" and "sin offering", and the Septuagint sometimes uses *hamartia*, "sin", in the second sense (compare Romans 8:3, "as a sin offering"). Others take it to mean that God treated Christ as a sinner, or that Christ bore sin and its consequences on behalf of others. The phrase "knew no sin" means that he had no sin of his own.
[^17]: Greek *dikaiosyne theou*. As in Romans 1:17, it may mean God's own righteousness, his justice and faithfulness, or the right standing that comes from God. Either way, Paul says that "we might become" it "in him".
