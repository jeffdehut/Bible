**1** As we work together with him,[^1] we also urge you not to receive the grace of God in vain.

**2** For he says, "At an acceptable time I listened to you, and on a day of salvation I helped you."[^2] Look, now is the truly acceptable time; look, now is the day of salvation.

**3** We put no obstacle in anyone's way, so that our ministry will not be discredited,

**4** but as servants of God we commend ourselves in every way: through great endurance, in afflictions, in hardships, in distresses,

**5** in beatings, in imprisonments, in riots, in labors, in sleepless nights, in hunger,[^3]

**6** by purity, by knowledge, by patience, by kindness, by the Holy Spirit,[^4] by sincere love,

**7** by truthful speech,[^5] by the power of God; with the weapons of righteousness for the right hand and for the left,[^6]

**8** through glory and dishonor, through slander and praise. We are treated as deceivers, and yet are true;

**9** as unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and look, we live; as disciplined, and yet not put to death;[^7]

**10** as grieving, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.

**11** We have spoken openly to you,[^8] Corinthians; our heart is wide open.

**12** You are not restricted by us, but you are restricted in your own affections.[^9]

**13** In return (I speak as to children), open wide your hearts too.

**14** Do not be unequally yoked[^10] with unbelievers. For what partnership does righteousness have with lawlessness? Or what fellowship does light have with darkness?[^11]

**15** What agreement does Christ have with Beliar?[^12] Or what share does a believer have with an unbeliever?

**16** What agreement does the temple of God have with idols? For we are the temple of the living God;[^13] as God said, "I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they will be my people."[^14]

**17** Therefore, "Come out from among them and be separate, says the Lord, and touch nothing unclean; then I will welcome you,[^15]

**18** and I will be a father to you, and you will be sons and daughters to me, says the Lord Almighty."[^16]

[^1]: Literally "working together". "With him", that is, with God (5:20), is implied; some take it as "with you".
[^2]: Isaiah 49:8, following the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "In a time of favor I have answered you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you."
[^3]: Or "in fastings".
[^4]: Or "by holiness of spirit".
[^5]: Or "by the message of truth". Literally "by the word of truth".
[^6]: Perhaps a weapon in the right hand for attack and a shield in the left for defense.
[^7]: Compare Psalm 118:18.
[^8]: Literally "Our mouth has been opened to you".
[^9]: Literally "in your inward parts", thought of as the seat of the emotions.
[^10]: Literally "Do not become mismatched in a yoke", a picture drawn from the law against yoking different kinds of animals together (Leviticus 19:19; Deuteronomy 22:10).
[^11]: Verses 6:14–7:1 raise a well-known question about the unity of the letter. The passage interrupts Paul's appeal to the Corinthians to open their hearts, which 7:2 ("Make room for us") seems to continue directly from 6:13, and it uses several words found nowhere else in Paul's letters, such as "Beliar". Some scholars think it is part of another letter (perhaps the one mentioned in 1 Corinthians 5:9) or a piece by another author that was later added here; others think Paul himself wrote it here as part of his appeal. The question arises from the content of the passage, not from differences in the manuscripts recorded in the SBLGNT apparatus.
[^12]: A name for Satan, found here only in the New Testament. It comes from the Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthlessness" (compare Deuteronomy 13:14, 13:13 in most English Bibles), and is used in Jewish writings of the time, including the Dead Sea Scrolls.
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "you are the temple of the living God".
[^14]: Combining Leviticus 26:11–12 and Ezekiel 37:27.
[^15]: Isaiah 52:11 ("Touch nothing unclean. Go out from the middle of her; purify yourselves"), close to the Septuagint. "I will welcome you" recalls Ezekiel 20:34 in the Septuagint, where the Hebrew has "I will gather you".
[^16]: Compare 2 Samuel 7:14 ("I will be his father") and Isaiah 43:6 ("my sons … my daughters"). *The Lord Almighty*: Greek *kyrios pantokrator*, the Septuagint's usual rendering of "Yahweh of Armies" (as in 2 Samuel 7:8). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
