**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus[^1] by the will of God, and our brother Timothy,[^2] to the church of God in Corinth, together with all God's holy people[^3] throughout Achaia:[^4]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort,[^5]

**4** who comforts us in all our affliction,[^6] so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.

**5** For just as the sufferings of Christ overflow to us, so also through Christ our comfort overflows.

**6** If we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which is at work when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we also suffer.

**7** And our hope for you is firm,[^7] because we know that just as you share in the sufferings, so also you share in the comfort.

**8** For we do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters,[^8] of the affliction we experienced in Asia.[^9] We were so utterly weighed down, beyond our strength, that we despaired even of life.

**9** Indeed, we felt within ourselves that we had received the sentence of death, so that we would rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead.

**10** He rescued us from so deadly a peril,[^10] and he will rescue us. On him we have set our hope that he will rescue us again,

**11** as you also join in helping us by your prayers, so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the gift granted to us through the prayers of many.[^11]

**12** For this is our boast, the testimony of our conscience: that we have conducted ourselves in the world, and especially toward you, with the holiness[^12] and sincerity that come from God, not by worldly wisdom[^13] but by the grace of God.

**13** For we write you nothing other than what you can read and understand,[^14] and I hope that you will understand fully,[^15]

**14** just as you have understood us in part, that we are your reason for boasting, as you also are ours, on the day of our Lord Jesus.

**15** Because I was confident of this, I wanted to come to you first, so that you might have a second benefit.[^16]

**16** I wanted to visit you on my way to Macedonia, and to come back to you from Macedonia, and to be sent on my way by you to Judea.

**17** So when I wanted to do this, did I act lightly? Or do I make my plans according to the flesh,[^17] so that with me it is "Yes, yes" and "No, no" at the same time?

**18** As surely as God is faithful, our word to you is not "Yes" and "No".

**19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, whom we proclaimed among you, that is, I and Silvanus[^18] and Timothy, was not "Yes" and "No"; in him it has always been "Yes".

**20** For all the promises of God find their "Yes" in him. That is why it is also through him that the "Amen"[^19] is spoken through us to the glory of God.

**21** Now it is God who establishes us with you in Christ and has anointed us,[^20]

**22** who has also put his seal on us and given us the Spirit in our hearts as a down payment.[^21]

**23** I call on God as my witness, on my life,[^22] that it was to spare you that I did not come again to Corinth.

**24** Not that we lord it over your faith, but we are workers with you for your joy, for you stand firm by faith.

[^1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name. Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^2]: Literally "Timothy the brother".
[^3]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^4]: The Roman province of southern Greece, with Corinth as its capital.
[^5]: Greek *paraklesis*, "comfort, encouragement, appeal", and the related verb *parakaleo*; so throughout verses 3–7.
[^6]: Greek *thlipsis*, "pressure, affliction, trouble"; also in verses 6 and 8.
[^7]: Many manuscripts arrange verses 6–7 differently: "(6) If we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation, which is at work when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we also suffer. (7) And our hope for you is firm; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort and salvation, because we know …"
[^8]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^9]: The Roman province of Asia, with Ephesus as its chief city.
[^10]: Literally "from so great a death".
[^11]: The Greek is compressed; literally "so that from many faces the gift to us through many may be given thanks for on our behalf".
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "with frankness" (or "simplicity", "single-mindedness"), Greek *haplotes*.
[^13]: Literally "fleshly wisdom".
[^14]: The Greek plays on *anaginosko*, "read", and *epiginosko*, "understand, recognize"; also in verse 14.
[^15]: Or "to the end".
[^16]: Literally "a second grace", perhaps the benefit of a second visit. Greek *charis*. Some manuscripts read "a second joy".
[^17]: Greek *sarx*, here in Paul's moral sense: human nature in its weakness and opposition to God. That is, by merely human, self-interested standards.
[^18]: Probably the Silas of Acts 15:40–18:5. *Silvanus* is a Latin form of the name.
[^19]: A Hebrew word meaning "it is firm, it is true", said to affirm a prayer or a promise.
[^20]: *Anointed* (Greek *chrio*) is related to *Christos*, "Christ", "Anointed One", earlier in the verse.
[^21]: Greek *arrabon*, a first payment that guarantees the rest (also 5:5).
[^22]: Literally "upon my soul", a form of oath.
