**1** I must boast. It is not profitable, but I will go on to visions and revelations of the Lord.[^1]

**2** I know a man in Christ[^2] who fourteen years ago—whether in the body I do not know, or out of the body I do not know; God knows—was caught up to the third heaven.[^3]

**3** And I know that this man—whether in the body or apart from the body I do not know; God knows—

**4** was caught up into paradise[^4] and heard things too sacred for words,[^5] which no human being is permitted to speak.

**5** On behalf of such a man I will boast, but on my own behalf I will not boast, except of my weaknesses.

**6** For if I should want to boast, I would not be a fool, for I would be speaking the truth. But I refrain, so that no one may think more of me than what he sees in me or hears from me,

**7** even because of the extraordinary character of the revelations.[^6] Therefore, to keep me from becoming conceited, I was given a thorn in the flesh,[^7] a messenger of Satan, to torment me,[^8] to keep me from becoming conceited.[^9]

**8** Three times I appealed to the Lord about this, that it would leave me.

**9** But he said to me, "My grace is enough for you, for power[^10] is made perfect in weakness." So I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may rest on me.[^11]

**10** That is why, for the sake of Christ, I am content with weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions and distress. For when I am weak, then I am strong.

**11** I have become a fool! You forced me to it. I ought to have been commended by you. For I am not in the least inferior to the super-apostles, even though I am nothing.

**12** The signs of an apostle were performed among you with all endurance, by signs and wonders and miracles.

**13** For in what way were you treated worse than the other churches, except that I myself was not a burden to you? Forgive me this wrong!

**14** Look, I am ready to come to you this third time,[^12] and I will not be a burden, for I do not want what is yours but you. For children ought not to save up for their parents, but parents for their children.

**15** I will most gladly spend and be spent for your sakes.[^13] If I love you more, am I to be loved less?

**16** Be that as it may, I did not burden you. But, crafty as I am, I took you in by deceit![^14]

**17** Did I take advantage of you through any of those I sent to you?

**18** I urged Titus to go, and I sent the brother with him. Titus did not take advantage of you, did he? Did we not walk in the same spirit?[^15] Did we not walk in the same steps?

**19** Have you been thinking all along[^16] that we have been defending ourselves to you? We are speaking before God in Christ. Everything, dear friends,[^17] is for your building up.

**20** For I am afraid that when I come I may find you not as I wish, and that you may find me not as you wish. I fear there may be quarreling, jealousy, outbursts of anger, selfish ambition, slander, gossip, arrogance[^18] and disorder.

**21** I fear that when I come again[^19] my God may humble me before you, and I may have to mourn over many who sinned earlier and have not repented of the impurity, sexual immorality and debauchery that they have practiced.

[^1]: Or "visions and revelations from the Lord". Many manuscripts read "Boasting is certainly not profitable for me. For I will go on to visions …".
[^2]: Most interpreters take this as Paul speaking of himself (compare verse 7).
[^3]: Perhaps the highest heaven. Jewish writings of the time speak of three, or of seven, heavens.
[^4]: Greek *paradeisos*, "garden, park", the word the Septuagint uses for the garden of Eden. Here it seems to be the same place as "the third heaven" (verse 2). Compare Luke 23:43.
[^5]: Literally "unspeakable words".
[^6]: The SBLGNT's punctuation joins these words to verse 6. Many translations begin a new sentence with them: "To keep me from becoming conceited because of the extraordinary character of the revelations, I was given a thorn in the flesh …". Many manuscripts leave out "Therefore", which suits that reading.
[^7]: Greek *skolops*, "a pointed stake, a thorn"; *in the flesh* may also be read "for the flesh". Paul does not say what it was. Suggestions include a physical illness or disability (compare Galatians 4:13–15), persistent opposition or persecution, and a temptation or spiritual trial. *Flesh* (Greek *sarx*) may here mean the body, or Paul's human weakness more generally.
[^8]: Literally "to strike me with the fist" (compare 1 Corinthians 4:11). *Messenger*: Greek *angelos*, "messenger, angel".
[^9]: Some manuscripts leave out these last words, which repeat the beginning of the sentence.
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "my power".
[^11]: Literally "may pitch its tent over me".
[^12]: Or "This is the third time I am ready to come to you". Compare 13:1–2.
[^13]: Literally "for your souls".
[^14]: Paul seems to be repeating, with irony, what some were saying about him.
[^15]: Or "by the same Spirit".
[^16]: Or, as a statement, "You have been thinking all along".
[^17]: Literally "beloved".
[^18]: Greek *physioseis*, "puffings up" (compare 1 Corinthians 4:6).
[^19]: Or "that when I come my God may humble me again before you".
