**1** I, Paul, myself appeal to you[^1] by the meekness and gentleness of Christ, I who am humble when face to face with you, but bold toward you when I am away.[^2]

**2** I beg you that when I am present I may not have to be bold with the confidence with which I expect to dare to act against some who regard us as living[^3] according to the flesh.[^4]

**3** For though we live in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh—

**4** for the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but powerful for God[^5] to tear down strongholds[^6]—we tear down arguments

**5** and every proud obstacle raised up against the knowledge of God, and we take every thought captive to obey Christ,

**6** and we are ready to punish every disobedience, once your obedience is complete.

**7** Look at what is in front of your eyes.[^7] If anyone is confident that he belongs to Christ, let him remind himself of this: that just as he belongs to Christ, so do we.

**8** For even if I boast a little too much about our authority, which the Lord gave for building you up and not for tearing you down, I will not be ashamed,

**9** so that I may not seem to be trying to frighten you with my letters.

**10** For they say,[^8] "His letters are weighty and strong, but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech is contemptible."

**11** Let such a person understand that what we are in word by letters when absent, we are also in action when present.

**12** For we do not dare to class or compare ourselves[^9] with some of those who commend themselves. But when they measure themselves by themselves and compare themselves with themselves, they do not understand.

**13** But we will not boast beyond limits, but according to the measure of the field[^10] that God assigned to us as a measure, to reach even as far as you.

**14** For we are not overextending ourselves, as though we did not reach you; we were the first to come all the way to you with the good news of Christ.

**15** We do not boast beyond limits in the labors of others, but we hope that, as your faith grows, we may be greatly enlarged among you, within our field,

**16** so that we may proclaim the good news in the regions beyond you, without boasting of work already done in someone else's field.

**17** But "let the one who boasts boast in the Lord."[^11]

**18** For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.

[^1]: Chapters 10–13 raise a well-known question about the unity of the letter. After the reconciliation and joy of chapter 7 and the appeal for the collection in chapters 8–9, Paul now defends his apostleship sharply against rivals and fears what he will find when he comes. Many scholars think these chapters were once a separate letter: either the earlier "tearful letter" (2:3–4; 7:8), or a later letter written after fresh trouble arose. Others hold that the whole of 2 Corinthians is a single letter, and explain the change of tone in other ways, for example by new reports reaching Paul while he wrote. The question arises from the content of the letter, not from differences in the manuscripts recorded in the SBLGNT apparatus.
[^2]: Paul seems to be repeating what some were saying about him (compare verse 10).
[^3]: Literally "walking"; also in verse 3.
[^4]: Greek *sarx*, here in Paul's moral sense (see 1:17). In verses 2–4 Paul plays on two senses of the word: living "in the flesh", as a human being, and acting "according to the flesh", by worldly standards.
[^5]: Or "divinely powerful", "powerful in God's sight".
[^6]: Compare Proverbs 21:22.
[^7]: Or "You are looking at outward appearances." Literally "You look at the things according to face."
[^8]: Literally "he says", or "one says", perhaps of a particular opponent.
[^9]: The Greek plays on *enkrino*, "class with", and *synkrino*, "compare"; *synkrino* is used again later in the verse.
[^10]: Greek *kanon*, "measuring rod, rule", here the area of work assigned to Paul; also in verses 15 and 16.
[^11]: Jeremiah 9:23 (9:24 in most English Bibles), in brief, as in 1 Corinthians 1:31. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
