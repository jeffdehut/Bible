**1** We want you to know, brothers and sisters,[^1] about the grace of God that has been given in the churches of Macedonia:

**2** in a severe test of affliction, their abundant joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity[^2] on their part.

**3** For they gave,[^3] I testify, as much as they were able, and beyond what they were able, of their own accord,

**4** begging us earnestly for the privilege[^4] of sharing[^5] in this service to God's holy people[^6]—

**5** and not as we expected, but they gave themselves first of all to the Lord, and to us, by the will of God.

**6** So we urged Titus that, just as he had already made a beginning, he should also complete among you this generous undertaking.

**7** But just as you excel in everything, in faith and speech and knowledge and all earnestness, and in the love from us that is in you,[^7] see that you excel in this generous undertaking too.

**8** I do not say this as a command, but to test the genuineness of your love by the earnestness of others.

**9** For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: though he was rich, for your sake he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich.

**10** And in this matter I give my opinion: this is to your advantage, since last year you were the first not only to act but also to want to do it.

**11** So now finish doing it, so that your eagerness to want it may be matched by your completing it, out of what you have.

**12** For if the eagerness is there, the gift is acceptable according to what one has, not according to what one does not have.

**13** For I do not mean that others should be relieved while you are burdened, but that there should be equality.

**14** At the present time your abundance will supply their need, so that their abundance may also supply your need, so that there may be equality.

**15** As it is written, "The one who gathered much had nothing left over, and the one who gathered little did not have too little."[^8]

**16** But thanks be to God, who puts into the heart of Titus the same earnestness for you.

**17** For he not only welcomed our appeal, but being very earnest he is going to you[^9] of his own accord.

**18** With him we are sending the brother[^10] who is praised in all the churches for his work in the good news.

**19** And not only that, but he has also been appointed by the churches to travel with us in this generous undertaking that we are administering for the glory of the Lord himself and to show our eagerness.

**20** We are taking this precaution so that no one will blame us about this generous gift that we are administering,

**21** for we intend what is honorable not only in the Lord's sight but also in the sight of people.[^11]

**22** And with them we are sending our brother, whom we have often tested in many matters and found earnest, but who is now much more earnest because of his great confidence in you.

**23** As for Titus, he is my partner and fellow worker for you; as for our brothers, they are messengers[^12] of the churches, the glory of Christ.

**24** So show them the proof of your love and of our boasting about you, openly before the churches.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^2]: Greek *haplotes*, "single-mindedness, sincerity", and so "generosity"; also in 9:11 and 9:13.
[^3]: The Greek of verses 3–6 is one long sentence; its verb, "they gave", comes in verse 5.
[^4]: Greek *charis*, "grace, favor, gift". In chapters 8–9 Paul uses the word in several senses: "grace" (8:1, 9; 9:8, 14), "privilege" (here), "generous undertaking" (8:6, 7, 19) and "thanks" (8:16; 9:15).
[^5]: Greek *koinonia*, "sharing, participation, fellowship" (compare 1 Corinthians 1:9).
[^6]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints": here the believers in Jerusalem (compare 1 Corinthians 16:1–3).
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "and in your love for us".
[^8]: Exodus 16:18. Literally "The one with much … the one with little".
[^9]: Or "he has set out for you".
[^10]: The brother is not named; nor is the one in verse 22.
[^11]: Compare Proverbs 3:4.
[^12]: Greek *apostoloi*, "apostles", here in the general sense of envoys or delegates sent by the churches.
