**1** Now the Spirit says explicitly that in later times some will abandon the faith, devoting themselves to deceiving spirits and teachings of demons,

**2** through the hypocrisy of liars whose consciences are seared,[^1]

**3** who forbid marriage and demand abstinence from foods[^2] that God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth.

**4** For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving,

**5** for it is made holy by the word of God and prayer.

**6** If you put these things before the brothers and sisters,[^3] you will be a good servant[^4] of Christ Jesus, nourished on the words of the faith and of the good teaching that you have followed.

**7** Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths.[^5] Rather, train yourself for godliness;

**8** for physical training is of some value,[^6] but godliness is of value in every way, holding promise for the present life and for the life to come.

**9** The saying is trustworthy and deserves full acceptance.[^7]

**10** For to this end we toil and suffer reproach,[^8] because we have set our hope on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe.

**11** Command and teach these things.

**12** Let no one look down on you because you are young, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love,[^9] in faith, in purity.

**13** Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of scripture,[^10] to encouragement, to teaching.

**14** Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you through prophecy when the council of elders[^11] laid their hands on you.

**15** Practice these things, give yourself wholly to them, so that your progress may be evident to all.

**16** Pay close attention to yourself and to your teaching; persevere in these things, for by doing this you will save both yourself and those who hear you.

[^1]: Greek *kausteriazo*, "brand with a hot iron" or "cauterize", that is, made insensitive, or marked as belonging to another.
[^2]: Literally "forbidding to marry, to abstain from foods".
[^3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^4]: Greek *diakonos*, "servant", traditionally "minister".
[^5]: Literally "profane and old-womanish myths".
[^6]: Literally "is useful for a little", which may mean "for a little" in degree or in time.
[^7]: See 1:15. The saying may be the words of verse 8, or of verse 10.
[^8]: The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "we toil and strive".
[^9]: Many manuscripts add "in spirit".
[^10]: Literally "to the reading", the reading aloud of scripture in the gathering of believers.
[^11]: Greek *presbyterion*, the elders (*presbyteroi*) as a body.
