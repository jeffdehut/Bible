**1** First of all, then, I urge that petitions, prayers, intercessions and thanksgivings be made for all people,

**2** for kings and for all who are in authority, so that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life in all godliness and dignity.

**3** This is good, and it pleases God our Savior,

**4** who wants all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

**5** For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and humankind, the human being[^1] Christ Jesus,

**6** who gave himself as a ransom[^2] for all. This was the testimony given at the proper time.[^3]

**7** For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle (I am telling the truth,[^4] I am not lying), a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

**8** So I want the men[^5] in every place to pray, lifting up holy hands without anger or argument.

**9** Likewise I want the women to adorn themselves in respectable clothing,[^6] with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold or pearls or expensive clothes,

**10** but with what is fitting for women who profess godliness: with good works.

**11** A woman should learn in quietness[^7] with full submission.[^8]

**12** I do not permit a woman to teach or to have authority[^9] over a man; she is to be in quietness.

**13** For Adam was formed first, then Eve;[^10]

**14** and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and fell into transgression.[^11]

**15** But she will be saved through childbearing, if they continue in faith and love and holiness, with self-control.[^12]

[^1]: Greek *anthropos*, "human being", the same word as "humankind" (*anthropoi*) earlier in the verse and "people" in verses 1 and 4.
[^2]: Greek *antilytron*, "ransom, price paid for release". Compare Mark 10:45.
[^3]: Literally "the testimony at its own times".
[^4]: Many manuscripts add "in Christ".
[^5]: Greek *andres*. In verses 8–15, *aner* means "man" or "husband" and *gyne* "woman" or "wife". Interpreters differ over whether these verses concern men and women in general, or husbands and wives; the passage moves between plural (verses 9–10) and singular (verses 11–12), and between Adam and Eve (verses 13–14) and "she" and "they" (verse 15).
[^6]: The verb "pray" in verse 8 may also carry over: "Likewise I want the women to pray …, adorning themselves in respectable clothing".
[^7]: Greek *hesychia*, "quietness, calm", also "silence"; also in verse 12. The related adjective is used in verse 2 ("a quiet life").
[^8]: Greek *hypotage*, "subjection, submission", from the same root as *hypotasso* (see Ephesians 5:21). The text does not say to whom.
[^9]: Greek *authenteo*, a rare verb found only here in the New Testament. Its sense is debated; proposed meanings include "have authority over", "exercise authority", "dominate" and "assume authority". Whether "over a man" belongs with "teach" as well as with this verb is also debated. *I do not permit*: the present tense can also mean "I am not permitting".
[^10]: Compare Genesis 2:7, 21–22.
[^11]: Literally "was thoroughly deceived and has come into transgression". Compare Genesis 3:13.
[^12]: The Greek has "she" (singular) with the first verb and "they" (plural) with the second. "She" may be the woman of verse 14 (Eve) or any woman; "they" may be women, or the woman and her husband, or her children. "Through childbearing" (in Greek, "the childbearing") has been understood as "by bearing children", "kept safe through childbirth", or "through the birth of the Child" (compare Genesis 3:15). *Saved* may mean salvation or being kept safe.
