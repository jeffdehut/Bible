**1** All who are under the yoke as slaves[^1] should regard their own masters as worthy of all honor, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be slandered.[^2]

**2** Those who have believing masters must not look down on them because they are brothers;[^3] rather they must serve them all the better, because those who benefit from their good service[^4] are believers and beloved. Teach and urge these things.

**3** If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound[^5] words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness,

**4** he is conceited and understands nothing, but has a sickly craving for controversies and disputes about words, from which come envy, strife, slanders, evil suspicions,

**5** and constant friction among people who are corrupted in mind and deprived of the truth, who think that godliness is a means of gain.[^6]

**6** But godliness with contentment[^7] is great gain,

**7** for we brought nothing into the world, so that we cannot take anything out of it either.[^8]

**8** But if we have food and clothing,[^9] with these we will be content.

**9** But those who want to be rich fall into temptation and a trap and many senseless and harmful desires, which plunge people into ruin and destruction.

**10** For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.[^10] Some, by craving it, have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pains.

**11** But you, man of God,[^11] flee these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness.

**12** Fight the good fight[^12] of the faith; take hold of the eternal life to which you were called; and you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.

**13** I charge you in the presence of God, who gives life to all things, and of Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate[^13] made the good confession,

**14** to keep the commandment without stain or reproach until the appearing[^14] of our Lord Jesus Christ,

**15** which he will display at the proper time,[^15] he who is the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords,[^16]

**16** who alone has immortality, who dwells in unapproachable light, whom no human being has seen or can see. To him be honor and eternal dominion. Amen.

**17** As for those who are rich in this present age, command them not to be arrogant, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on God,[^17] who richly provides us with everything to enjoy;

**18** to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share,

**19** storing up for themselves a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of the life that is truly life.[^18]

**20** Timothy, guard what has been entrusted to you.[^19] Avoid the irreverent, empty chatter and the contradictions of what is falsely called knowledge,[^20]

**21** which some have professed and so have swerved from the faith. Grace be with you all.[^21]

[^1]: Greek *douloi*, "slaves". *Masters* in verses 1–2 is *despotai*, "owners". Slavery was part of the social order of the Roman world, and some members of the churches were slaves or owned slaves (compare Ephesians 6:5–9).
[^2]: Compare Isaiah 52:5, quoted in Romans 2:24. *Slandered*: Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^3]: Greek *adelphoi*, here fellow believers.
[^4]: Or "because those who devote themselves to doing good", taking the words to describe the masters' kindness. Greek *euergesia*, "a good deed, benefit".
[^5]: Greek *hygiaino*, "be healthy"; compare "sickly" (*noseo*, "be sick") in verse 4.
[^6]: Many manuscripts add "Withdraw from such people."
[^7]: Greek *autarkeia*, "self-sufficiency, contentment". Compare Philippians 4:11.
[^8]: Literally "because we cannot take anything out either"; the connection of the two clauses is difficult. Many manuscripts read "and it is clear that we cannot take anything out". Compare Job 1:21; Ecclesiastes 5:14 (5:15 in most English Bibles).
[^9]: Or "covering", which may include shelter as well as clothing.
[^10]: Or "the root of all evils".
[^11]: A title used in the Old Testament for Moses and the prophets (Deuteronomy 33:1; 1 Kings 17:18).
[^12]: Or "Compete well in the contest". The words come from athletic contests.
[^13]: Or "in the time of Pontius Pilate".
[^14]: Greek *epiphaneia*, "appearance, manifestation".
[^15]: Literally "at his own times".
[^16]: Literally "the King of those who reign and Lord of those who rule as lords".
[^17]: Many manuscripts read "on the living God".
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "take hold of eternal life".
[^19]: Greek *paratheke*, "a deposit", something entrusted to another for safekeeping.
[^20]: Greek *gnosis*, "knowledge".
[^21]: "You" is plural in Greek. Many manuscripts read "Grace be with you" (singular), followed by "Amen".
