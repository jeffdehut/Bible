**1** The saying is trustworthy:[^1] whoever aspires to the office of overseer[^2] desires a noble task.

**2** An overseer, then, must be above reproach, the husband of one wife,[^3] sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach,

**3** not a drunkard, not violent[^4] but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money.

**4** He must manage his own household well, keeping his children under control with all dignity

**5** (for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he take care of God's church?).

**6** He must not be a recent convert, so that he may not become conceited and fall into the condemnation of the devil.[^5]

**7** Moreover, he must have a good reputation with outsiders, so that he may not fall into disgrace and the snare of the devil.

**8** Deacons[^6] likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not given to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain.

**9** They must hold to the mystery[^7] of the faith with a clear conscience.

**10** And let them also be tested first; then let them serve if they prove blameless.

**11** Women[^8] likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, sober-minded, faithful in all things.

**12** Deacons must each be the husband of one wife, managing their children and their own households well.

**13** For those who have served well gain a good standing for themselves and great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus.

**14** I am writing these things to you, hoping to come to you soon,

**15** but if I am delayed, I write so that you may know how people ought to conduct themselves[^9] in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, a pillar and foundation of the truth.

**16** And undeniably great is the mystery of godliness: He[^10] was revealed in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit,[^11] seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations, believed in the world, taken up in glory.[^12]

[^1]: See 1:15. Some take "The saying is trustworthy" with the end of 2:15 instead.
[^2]: Greek *episkope*, "oversight". *Overseer* in verse 2 is *episkopos*, traditionally "bishop".
[^3]: Literally "a man of one woman"; also in verse 12. The phrase may mean married only once, faithful to his wife, or not having more than one wife.
[^4]: Many manuscripts add "not greedy for dishonest gain".
[^5]: Or "the judgment that the devil fell under"; or "the judgment brought by the devil". Greek *diabolos* means "slanderer"; compare "slanderers" (*diaboloi*) in verse 11.
[^6]: Greek *diakonoi*, "servants", here people holding a recognized role in the church; traditionally "deacons"; also in verse 12. The verb "serve" in verses 10 and 13 is *diakoneo*.
[^7]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it; also in verse 16.
[^8]: Greek *gynaikes*, "women" or "wives". This may mean the wives of deacons, or women who serve as deacons (compare Phoebe, called a *diakonos* of the church in Romans 16:1). The Greek has no "their".
[^9]: Or "how you ought to conduct yourself". The Greek does not name the subject.
[^10]: Literally "Who". Many manuscripts read "God was revealed in the flesh". In Greek capital letters the two readings look alike: ΟΣ, "who", and ΘΣ, the usual abbreviation for "God".
[^11]: Or "shown to be in the right in the spirit". Greek *dikaioo*, which in Paul's argument about salvation is translated "declare righteous" (traditionally "justify"); here it means "vindicate, prove right". *By the Spirit* may also mean "in the spirit", in contrast with "in the flesh".
[^12]: The six short lines of this verse appear to quote an early Christian hymn or creed.
