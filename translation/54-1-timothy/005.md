**1** Do not rebuke an older man,[^1] but appeal to him as a father, to younger men as brothers,

**2** to older women as mothers, to younger women as sisters, in all purity.

**3** Honor[^2] widows who are truly widows.

**4** But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let them[^3] first learn to show godliness toward their own household and to repay their parents,[^4] for this is pleasing before God.

**5** The one who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God and continues in petitions and prayers night and day,

**6** but she who lives for pleasure is dead even while she lives.

**7** Command these things as well, so that they may be above reproach.

**8** But if anyone does not provide for their own relatives, and especially for members of their household, they have denied the faith and are worse than an unbeliever.

**9** Let a widow be put on the list if she is not less than sixty years old, the wife of one husband,[^5]

**10** and has a reputation for good works: if she has brought up children, if she has shown hospitality, if she has washed the feet of God's holy people,[^6] if she has helped those in distress, if she has devoted herself to every good work.

**11** But refuse to enroll younger widows, for when their desires draw them away from Christ,[^7] they want to marry,

**12** and so they incur condemnation for having set aside their first pledge.[^8]

**13** Besides that, they learn to be idle, going around from house to house, and not only idle but also gossips and busybodies, saying what they should not.

**14** So I want younger widows[^9] to marry, bear children, manage their households, and give the adversary[^10] no occasion for reproach.

**15** For some have already turned aside to follow Satan.

**16** If any believing woman[^11] has widows in her family, let her help them, and let the church not be burdened, so that it may help those who are truly widows.

**17** Let the elders[^12] who lead well be considered worthy of double honor,[^13] especially those who labor in preaching and teaching.

**18** For the scripture says, "Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain,"[^14] and, "The worker deserves his wages."[^15]

**19** Do not accept an accusation against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses.[^16]

**20** Those who sin, rebuke in the presence of all, so that the rest also may be afraid.

**21** I solemnly charge you before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen angels to keep these instructions without prejudice, doing nothing out of favoritism.

**22** Do not be hasty in laying hands on anyone, and do not share in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.

**23** No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent illnesses.

**24** The sins of some people are obvious, going before them to judgment, but the sins of others follow after them.

**25** So also good works are obvious, and those that are not cannot remain hidden.

[^1]: Greek *presbyteros*, "older man", the word translated "elder" in verses 17 and 19.
[^2]: Greek *timao*, "honor", which here may include material support (compare verses 4, 16 and 17).
[^3]: That is, the children and grandchildren. Some take "they" to be widows.
[^4]: Literally "those who went before them": parents and grandparents.
[^5]: Literally "a woman of one man". Compare 3:2.
[^6]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^7]: Or "when they grow restless against Christ". Greek *katastreniao*, "feel strong desire, grow wanton against".
[^8]: Or "their first faith". Greek *pistis*, "faith, faithfulness, pledge".
[^9]: Literally "younger women".
[^10]: Or "the opponent". This may be a human opponent or Satan (compare verse 15).
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "any believing man or woman".
[^12]: Greek *presbyteroi*, "elders", leaders of the church; also in verse 19.
[^13]: Greek *time*, "honor", also "price, payment"; compare verse 18.
[^14]: Deuteronomy 25:4.
[^15]: These words are found in Luke 10:7; compare Matthew 10:10.
[^16]: Compare Deuteronomy 19:15.
