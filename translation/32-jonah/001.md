**1** The word of the LORD[^1] came to Jonah son of Amittai:[^2]

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh,[^3] the great city, and cry out against it, for their evil has come up before me."

**3** But Jonah got up to run away to Tarshish,[^4] away from the presence of the LORD. He went down to Joppa[^5] and found a ship going to Tarshish. He paid the fare[^6] and went down into the ship to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD.

**4** But the LORD hurled[^7] a great wind on the sea, and there was a great storm on the sea, and the ship was about to break up.[^8]

**5** The sailors were afraid, and each one cried out to his own god. They hurled the cargo[^9] that was in the ship into the sea to lighten it for themselves. But Jonah had gone down into the hold of the ship, and he lay there, fast asleep.[^10]

**6** The captain[^11] came to him and said to him, "How can you be sleeping?[^12] Get up, call on your god! Perhaps the god will give a thought to us, and we will not perish."

**7** Then they said to one another, "Come, let us cast lots, so that we may know on whose account this disaster has come on us." So they cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.

**8** They said to him, "Tell us, you on whose account this disaster has come on us:[^13] what is your work? Where do you come from? What is your country? From what people are you?"

**9** He said to them, "I am a Hebrew. I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land."

**10** The men were terrified[^14] and said to him, "What is this you have done?" For the men knew that he was running away from the presence of the LORD, because he had told them.

**11** They said to him, "What should we do to you, so that the sea will calm down for us?" For the sea was growing more and more stormy.

**12** He said to them, "Pick me up and hurl me into the sea, and the sea will calm down for you. For I know that it is on my account that this great storm has come on you."

**13** But the men rowed hard[^15] to get back to dry land, and they could not, because the sea was growing more and more stormy against them.

**14** Then they called out to the LORD: "Please, LORD, do not let us perish for this man's life, and do not hold us guilty of innocent blood.[^16] For you, LORD, have done as you pleased."

**15** Then they picked Jonah up and hurled him into the sea, and the sea stopped raging.

**16** The men greatly feared the LORD. They offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows.[^17]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: A prophet from Gath-hepher in Galilee, who foretold the victories of Jeroboam II king of Israel in the eighth century BC (2 Kings 14:25). *Jonah* means "dove".
[^3]: The capital of the Assyrian empire, on the Tigris River opposite modern Mosul in Iraq. Assyria destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel in 722 BC.
[^4]: A distant port in the far west, perhaps in Spain. Nineveh lay to the east.
[^5]: A port on the Mediterranean coast, modern Jaffa, now part of Tel Aviv.
[^6]: Literally "he gave its hire", perhaps the hire of the whole ship.
[^7]: The verb *hetil*, "hurl", is repeated in verses 5, 12 and 15. So also *went down* in verses 3 and 5 traces Jonah's descent, which continues in 2:7 (2:6 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: Literally "the ship thought it would be broken".
[^9]: Or "the gear". Hebrew *kelim*, "vessels, objects".
[^10]: Hebrew *nirdam*, "in a deep sleep", the word used of Adam's sleep in Genesis 2:21. Also in verse 6.
[^11]: Literally "the chief of the sailors".
[^12]: Literally "What is with you, sleeping?"
[^13]: Literally "Tell us, on account of whom is this disaster on us?" The lot has already fallen on Jonah, so the question may ask him to explain himself.
[^14]: Literally "feared a great fear". The same words are used in verse 16, where they fear the LORD.
[^15]: Literally "dug".
[^16]: The guilt of killing an innocent man (Deuteronomy 21:8).
[^17]: In most English Bibles chapter 1 has a verse 17, which is 2:1 in the Hebrew.
