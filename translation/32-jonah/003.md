**1** The word of the LORD[^1] came to Jonah a second time:

**2** "Get up, go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim to it the message that I tell you."

**3** So Jonah got up and went to Nineveh, as the LORD had said. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city,[^2] a three days' walk across.[^3]

**4** Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's walk, and he proclaimed, "Forty days more, and Nineveh will be overthrown!"[^4]

**5** The people of Nineveh believed God. They proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least.

**6** When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he got up from his throne, took off his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in the ashes.

**7** He had this proclaimed in Nineveh: "By the decree of the king and his nobles: No human being or animal, no herd or flock, is to taste anything. They are not to graze or drink water.

**8** Let human beings and animals be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out to God with all their strength. Let each one turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands.

**9** Who knows? God may turn and relent,[^5] and turn from his burning anger, so that we do not perish."

**10** When God saw what they did, that they turned from their evil way, God relented concerning the disaster[^6] that he had said he would bring on them, and he did not do it.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "a great city to God", an idiom for something very great. Or "a great city in God's sight".
[^3]: Literally "a walk of three days". The walls of ancient Nineveh were about 7½ miles (12 km) around, so the phrase may include the whole district around the city, or mean the time needed to go through all of it.
[^4]: Hebrew *nehpakhet*, the word used of the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:25; Amos 4:11). It can also mean "turned around, changed", which is what happens to Nineveh. The Septuagint reads "Three days more".
[^5]: Or "change his mind". Also in verse 10. Compare Joel 2:14.
[^6]: Hebrew *ra'ah*, the same word as *evil* earlier in the verse and in verse 8. When the people turn from their evil, God turns from the evil he planned for them. The word is used again in 4:1.
