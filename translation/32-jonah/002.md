**1** The LORD[^1] appointed[^2] a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.[^3]

**2** Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish.

**3** He said:[^4] "I called to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me. From the belly of Sheol[^5] I cried for help, and you heard my voice.

**4** You threw me into the deep, into the heart of the seas, and the current[^6] surrounded me. All your breakers and your waves swept over me.[^7]

**5** I said, 'I have been driven away from your sight. Yet I will look again toward your holy temple.'[^8]

**6** The waters closed around me up to my neck;[^9] the deep surrounded me; seaweed was wrapped around my head.

**7** I went down to the roots of the mountains;[^10] the earth with its bars closed behind me forever. But you brought my life up from the pit,[^11] LORD my God.

**8** When my life was ebbing away,[^12] I remembered the LORD, and my prayer came to you, to your holy temple.

**9** Those who cling to worthless idols[^13] abandon their faithful love.[^14]

**10** But I, with a song of thanksgiving, will sacrifice to you. What I have vowed I will pay. Salvation belongs to the LORD!"[^15]

**11** Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto the dry land.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "provided". The same verb is used of the plant, the worm and the wind in 4:6–8.
[^3]: Verse 1 is 1:17 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–11 are 2:1–10. Jesus speaks of the sign of Jonah and his three days and nights in the fish in Matthew 12:39–41.
[^4]: Verses 3–10 are a psalm of thanksgiving, sung by someone already rescued. Many of its lines echo the book of Psalms.
[^5]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. *I called … and he answered me* echoes Psalm 120:1.
[^6]: Hebrew *nahar*, "river, current", here the currents of the sea.
[^7]: The same line is found in Psalm 42:8 (42:7 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: Some scholars read "How will I ever look again toward your holy temple?", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^9]: Literally "up to the *nephesh*", the throat or the life (Psalm 69:2, 69:1 in most English Bibles).
[^10]: Literally "to the cuttings of the mountains", probably their foundations at the bottom of the sea. *I went down* continues the descent of 1:3 and 1:5.
[^11]: Hebrew *shachat*, the pit of the grave.
[^12]: Literally "When my *nephesh* fainted within me".
[^13]: Literally "those who keep vanities of emptiness", as in Psalm 31:7 (31:6 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Hebrew *chesed*. Perhaps "abandon the God who is faithful to them", or "abandon their loyalty".
[^15]: Compare Psalm 3:9 (3:8 in most English Bibles).
