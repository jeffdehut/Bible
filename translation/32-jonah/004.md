**1** But to Jonah this seemed very wrong,[^1] and he was angry.

**2** He prayed to Yahweh[^2] and said, "Please, Yahweh, isn't this what I said while I was still in my own country? That is why I hurried to run away to Tarshish. For I knew that you are a gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger and abounding in faithful love,[^3] and that you relent from sending disaster.[^4]

**3** So now, Yahweh, please take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live."[^5]

**4** Yahweh said, "Is it right for you to be angry?"[^6]

**5** Jonah went out of the city and sat down to the east of the city. He made a shelter for himself there and sat under it in the shade, until he could see what would happen to the city.

**6** Then Yahweh God[^7] appointed a plant,[^8] and it grew up over Jonah to be a shade over his head, to save him from his misery.[^9] Jonah was very glad about the plant.

**7** But at dawn the next day God appointed a worm, and it attacked the plant, so that it withered.

**8** When the sun rose, God appointed a scorching[^10] east wind, and the sun beat down on Jonah's head, so that he grew faint. He asked to die,[^11] saying, "It is better for me to die than to live."

**9** But God said to Jonah, "Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?" He said, "It is right for me to be angry, angry enough to die!"

**10** Yahweh said, "You were concerned about[^12] the plant, for which you did not labor and which you did not make grow. It came up in a night and perished in a night.[^13]

**11** And should I not be concerned about Nineveh, the great city, in which there are more than 120,000 people who do not know their right hand from their left,[^14] and many animals as well?"

[^1]: Literally "It was evil to Jonah, a great evil". The Hebrew uses the word *ra'ah*, "evil, disaster", of 3:10 again.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love. The words echo Yahweh's description of himself in Exodus 34:6.
[^4]: Literally "and relenting concerning the disaster". The same words are found in Joel 2:13.
[^5]: Elijah, in despair, asked to die in similar words (1 Kings 19:4).
[^6]: Or "Are you very angry?" Also in verse 9.
[^7]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohim*, the form of the divine name used in Genesis 2–3.
[^8]: Hebrew *qiqayon*, probably the castor-oil plant, which grows fast and has large leaves. Traditionally "gourd".
[^9]: Hebrew *ra'ah*, "evil, misery", again the word of verse 1 and 3:10.
[^10]: Hebrew *charishit*, found only here. The meaning is uncertain.
[^11]: Literally "He asked for his *nephesh* to die".
[^12]: Or "You had pity on". Hebrew *chus*, "to pity, to spare". Also in verse 11.
[^13]: Literally "which was the son of a night and perished as the son of a night".
[^14]: Perhaps young children, or people who do not know right from wrong. The book ends with Yahweh's question.
