**1** Where has your beloved gone, most beautiful of women? Where has your beloved turned, that we may seek him with you?[^1]

**2** My beloved has gone down to his garden, to the beds of spices, to graze in the gardens and to gather lilies.[^2]

**3** I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine; he grazes among the lilies.

**4** You are beautiful, my darling, as Tirzah,[^3] lovely as Jerusalem, awe-inspiring as armies with banners.[^4]

**5** Turn your eyes away from me, for they overwhelm me. Your hair is like a flock of goats streaming down from Gilead.

**6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes that have come up from the washing; all of them bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**7** Your temples are like a slice of pomegranate behind your veil.

**8** There are sixty queens and eighty concubines, and young women without number.

**9** But my dove, my perfect one, is the only one, the only one of her mother, the favorite of the one who bore her. The young women saw her and called her blessed; the queens and concubines saw her and praised her:[^5]

**10** Who is this who looks down like the dawn, beautiful as the moon, bright as the sun,[^6] awe-inspiring as armies with banners?

**11** I went down to the walnut grove to look at the green shoots of the valley,[^7] to see whether the vine had budded, whether the pomegranates were in bloom.[^8]

**12** Before I knew it, my desire set me among the chariots of my noble people.[^9]

[^1]: The daughters of Jerusalem speak ("your" is feminine).
[^2]: The woman speaks in verses 2–3.
[^3]: An early capital of the northern kingdom of Israel (1 Kings 14:17; 16:23). The name means "pleasing". The man speaks in verses 4–10 ("you" is feminine).
[^4]: Hebrew *ka-nidgalot*, "like those with banners": perhaps armies, or the stars. Also in verse 10.
[^5]: Literally "and they praised her". Verse 10 may give the words of their praise.
[^6]: Hebrew *levanah*, "the white one", and *chammah*, "the hot one", poetic words for the moon and the sun.
[^7]: Hebrew *nachal*, a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season.
[^8]: Who speaks in verses 11–12 is not clear: perhaps the woman, perhaps the man.
[^9]: The Hebrew is very obscure. Literally "I did not know, my soul set me chariots of my people, noble". Or "the chariots of Amminadib", taking the last words as a name.
