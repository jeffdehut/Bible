**1** I have come into my garden, my sister, my bride;[^1] I have gathered my myrrh with my spice, I have eaten my honeycomb[^2] with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk. Eat, friends, drink, and be drunk with love![^3]

**2** I was asleep, but my heart was awake.[^4] Listen! My beloved is knocking.[^5] "Open to me, my sister, my darling, my dove, my perfect one, for my head is wet with dew, my locks with the drops of the night."

**3** I have taken off my robe; how could I put it on again? I have washed my feet; how could I soil them?

**4** My beloved put his hand in through the opening,[^6] and my heart[^7] trembled for him.

**5** I rose to open to my beloved, and my hands dripped with myrrh, my fingers with flowing myrrh, on the handles of the bolt.

**6** I opened to my beloved, but my beloved had turned and gone. My soul went out when he spoke.[^8] I sought him, but did not find him; I called him, but he did not answer me.

**7** The watchmen found me as they went around the city. They struck me, they wounded me; they took my shawl from me, those watchmen of the walls.

**8** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem: if you find my beloved, what will you tell him? That I am sick with love.

**9** What is your beloved more than any other beloved, most beautiful of women? What is your beloved more than any other beloved, that you make us swear like this?[^9]

**10** My beloved is radiant and ruddy,[^10] outstanding among ten thousand.

**11** His head is the finest gold; his locks are wavy,[^11] black as a raven.

**12** His eyes are like doves beside streams of water, bathed in milk, sitting by brimming pools.[^12]

**13** His cheeks are like beds of spices, towers of perfume.[^13] His lips are lilies, dripping with flowing myrrh.

**14** His arms[^14] are rods of gold set with jewels of Tarshish.[^15] His body is polished ivory, covered with sapphires.[^16]

**15** His legs are pillars of alabaster,[^17] set on bases of fine gold. His appearance is like Lebanon, choice as the cedars.

**16** His mouth[^18] is sweetness itself, and he is altogether desirable. This is my beloved and this is my friend,[^19] daughters of Jerusalem.

[^1]: The man speaks ("my sister, my bride"); see 4:9.
[^2]: Hebrew *ya'ar*, usually "forest", here "honeycomb", as in 1 Samuel 14:27.
[^3]: Or "drink deeply, you lovers!" Hebrew *dodim* can mean "love" (1:2) or "lovers". Who speaks this line is not clear: the man, or another voice.
[^4]: The woman speaks in verses 2–8.
[^5]: Literally "The voice of my beloved knocking!"
[^6]: Literally "the hole", perhaps the opening for the key or latch.
[^7]: Literally "my inner parts".
[^8]: Literally "My soul (*nephesh*) went out at his speaking". Or "My heart sank at his leaving".
[^9]: The daughters of Jerusalem speak ("your" is feminine, and "us").
[^10]: The woman answers in verses 10–16.
[^11]: Hebrew *taltallim*, a word found only here, perhaps "wavy", "curly", or "like clusters of dates".
[^12]: Literally "sitting on fullness": perhaps by full pools, or set like gems in their setting.
[^13]: With different vowels, the Hebrew can be read "that produce perfume", as the Septuagint reads.
[^14]: Literally "His hands".
[^15]: A precious stone, perhaps named after the faraway land of Tarshish: perhaps beryl or topaz.
[^16]: Hebrew *sappirim*, probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.
[^17]: Or "marble". Hebrew *shesh*.
[^18]: Literally "His palate".
[^19]: Hebrew *re'i*, the masculine form of *ra'yati*, "my darling" (1:9).
