**1** I am a crocus[^1] of Sharon, a lily of the valleys.

**2** Like a lily among thorns, so is my darling among the young women.[^2]

**3** Like an apple tree[^3] among the trees of the forest, so is my beloved among the young men. In his shade I delighted to sit, and his fruit was sweet to my taste.[^4]

**4** He brought me to the house of wine, and his banner over me was love.[^5]

**5** Sustain me with raisin cakes, refresh me with apples,[^6] for I am sick with love.

**6** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.

**7** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field:[^7] do not stir up or awaken love until it is ready.[^8]

**8** Listen! My beloved![^9] Look, here he comes, leaping over the mountains, bounding over the hills.

**9** My beloved is like a gazelle or a young stag. Look, there he stands behind our wall, gazing in through the windows, peering through the lattice.

**10** My beloved spoke and said to me, "Arise, my darling, my beautiful one, and come away.

**11** For look, the winter is past; the rain is over and gone.

**12** The blossoms have appeared in the land; the time of pruning[^10] has come, and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land.

**13** The fig tree ripens its early figs, and the vines in blossom give out their fragrance. Arise, my darling, my beautiful one, and come away."

**14** My dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the hiding places of the cliff,[^11] let me see your form, let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet, and your form is lovely.

**15** Catch the foxes for us, the little foxes that ruin the vineyards, for our vineyards are in blossom.[^12]

**16** My beloved is mine and I am his; he grazes among the lilies.[^13]

**17** Until the day breathes[^14] and the shadows flee, turn, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag on the cleft mountains.[^15]

[^1]: Hebrew *chavatselet*, a meadow flower, perhaps a crocus or an asphodel; traditionally "rose". Sharon is the coastal plain south of Mount Carmel. The woman probably speaks.
[^2]: The man speaks.
[^3]: Hebrew *tappuach*, perhaps an apricot. Also in verse 5.
[^4]: The woman speaks in verses 3–13.
[^5]: Or "and his look upon me was love".
[^6]: The commands are plural: she speaks to the young women.
[^7]: An oath by the animals of love. In Hebrew *bi-tsva'ot o be-ayelot ha-sadeh* sounds like the oaths "by (Yahweh of) Armies", *tseva'ot*, and "by God Almighty", *El Shaddai*. The same refrain comes in 3:5, and in a shorter form in 8:4.
[^8]: Literally "until it pleases".
[^9]: Literally "The voice of my beloved!"
[^10]: Or "the time of singing". Hebrew *zamir* can mean either.
[^11]: The man's words go on, either still quoted by the woman or spoken by him ("your" is feminine). Literally "in the hiding place of the steep slope".
[^12]: Who speaks is not clear; the command is plural.
[^13]: Or "he pastures his flock among the lilies".
[^14]: That is, until the cool breeze of evening, or until the dawn.
[^15]: Or "on the mountains of Bether". Hebrew *bater* means "division, cleft". Compare 8:14.
