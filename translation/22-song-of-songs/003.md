**1** On my bed, night after night,[^1] I sought the one my soul loves;[^2] I sought him, but did not find him.

**2** I will get up now and go around the city, through the streets and through the squares; I will seek the one my soul loves. I sought him, but did not find him.

**3** The watchmen found me as they went around the city. "Have you seen the one my soul loves?"

**4** Scarcely had I passed them when I found the one my soul loves. I held him and would not let him go until I had brought him into my mother's house, into the room of the one who conceived me.

**5** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem, by the gazelles or by the does of the field:[^3] do not stir up or awaken love until it is ready.

**6** Who is this coming up from the wilderness like columns of smoke,[^4] perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with all the powders of the merchant?

**7** Look, it is Solomon's couch![^5] Sixty warriors surround it, from the warriors of Israel.

**8** All of them hold swords and are trained in battle, each with his sword at his side,[^6] against the terrors of the night.

**9** King Solomon made himself a carriage[^7] from the wood of Lebanon.

**10** He made its posts of silver, its back of gold, its seat of purple; its inside was inlaid with love by the daughters of Jerusalem.[^8]

**11** Go out and look, daughters of Zion, at King Solomon, at the crown with which his mother crowned him on the day of his wedding, on the day of his heart's joy.

[^1]: Literally "in the nights". The woman speaks in verses 1–5.
[^2]: Literally "whom my soul (*nephesh*) loves"; see 1:7. Also in verses 2–4.
[^3]: See 2:7.
[^4]: "Who" is feminine in the Hebrew. It may refer to the woman, or to Solomon's couch (verse 7), a feminine word.
[^5]: Hebrew *mittah*, a bed or couch, here one carried in procession.
[^6]: Literally "on his thigh".
[^7]: Hebrew *appiryon*, a word found only here, probably borrowed from another language: a sedan chair or palanquin.
[^8]: Literally "its inside inlaid, love, from the daughters of Jerusalem". Or "inlaid with love for the daughters of Jerusalem".
