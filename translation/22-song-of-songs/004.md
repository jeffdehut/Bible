**1** How beautiful you are, my darling![^1] How beautiful you are! Your eyes are doves behind your veil. Your hair is like a flock of goats streaming down from Mount Gilead.

**2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes that have come up from the washing; all of them bear twins, and not one among them has lost its young.

**3** Your lips are like a scarlet thread, and your mouth is lovely. Your temples[^2] are like a slice of pomegranate behind your veil.

**4** Your neck is like the tower of David, built in courses,[^3] on which hang a thousand shields, all the shields of warriors.

**5** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, that graze among the lilies.

**6** Until the day breathes[^4] and the shadows flee, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.

**7** You are altogether beautiful, my darling; there is no flaw in you.

**8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride, come with me from Lebanon. Look down[^5] from the peak of Amana, from the peak of Senir and Hermon,[^6] from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.

**9** You have stolen my heart,[^7] my sister,[^8] my bride; you have stolen my heart with one glance of your eyes, with one jewel of your necklace.

**10** How beautiful is your love,[^9] my sister, my bride! How much better is your love than wine, and the fragrance of your oils than any spice!

**11** Your lips drip honey from the comb, my bride; honey and milk are under your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**12** A garden locked is my sister, my bride, a pool[^10] locked, a fountain sealed.

**13** Your shoots[^11] are a park[^12] of pomegranates with choice fruits, henna with nard,

**14** nard and saffron, cane and cinnamon, with every kind of incense tree, myrrh and aloes, with all the finest spices.

**15** You are a fountain in the gardens, a well of living water,[^13] streams flowing down from Lebanon.

**16** Awake, north wind, and come, south wind! Blow on my garden, let its spices flow.[^14] Let my beloved come into his garden and eat its choice fruits.

[^1]: The man speaks ("you" is feminine) in verses 1–15.
[^2]: Or "cheeks".
[^3]: Hebrew *le-talpiyyot*, a word found only here, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "in courses (of stone)", "for an armory", or "with turrets".
[^4]: See 2:17.
[^5]: Or "Come down", or "Journey".
[^6]: Amana is a peak of the Anti-Lebanon range. Senir is another name for Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9), or a peak near it.
[^7]: Hebrew *libbavtini*, from *lev*, "heart". Or "You have made my heart beat faster".
[^8]: An affectionate name for a beloved woman, as in ancient Egyptian love songs. Also in verses 10 and 12.
[^9]: Hebrew *dodim*; see 1:2. Also later in the verse.
[^10]: Hebrew *gal*, perhaps "pool" or "spring". The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "a garden", as in the first line.
[^11]: Or "Your channels". Hebrew *shelachayikh*, perhaps the shoots of a plant or the channels that water a garden.
[^12]: Hebrew *pardes*, a Persian word for an enclosed park, the source of the English word "paradise".
[^13]: That is, fresh, flowing water.
[^14]: Who speaks the first half of the verse is not clear: the man, or the woman calling her body "my garden". In the second half the woman speaks ("my beloved").
