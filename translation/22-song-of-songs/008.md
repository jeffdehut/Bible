**1** Oh, that you were like a brother to me,[^1] who nursed at my mother's breasts! If I found you outside, I would kiss you, and no one would despise me.

**2** I would lead you, I would bring you into my mother's house; you would teach me.[^2] I would give you spiced wine to drink, the juice of my pomegranates.

**3** His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me.

**4** I make you swear, daughters of Jerusalem: do not stir up or awaken love until it is ready.[^3]

**5** Who is this coming up from the wilderness, leaning on her beloved?[^4] Under the apple tree I awakened you;[^5] there your mother was in labor with you; there she who bore you was in labor.

**6** Set me as a seal on your heart, as a seal on your arm, for love is as strong as death, its passion[^6] as unyielding as Sheol.[^7] Its flashes are flashes of fire, a flame of the LORD.[^8]

**7** Many waters cannot quench love, and rivers cannot sweep it away. If a man gave all the wealth of his house for love, he would be utterly despised.[^9]

**8** We have a little sister, and she has no breasts.[^10] What shall we do for our sister on the day she is spoken for?

**9** If she is a wall, we will build on her a battlement of silver; but if she is a door, we will enclose her with boards of cedar.

**10** I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers; so I have become in his eyes like one who finds peace.[^11]

**11** Solomon had a vineyard at Baal-hamon;[^12] he gave the vineyard to keepers. Each was to bring for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver.[^13]

**12** My vineyard, my very own, is before me.[^14] The thousand are yours, Solomon, and two hundred for those who keep its fruit.

**13** You who sit in the gardens, companions are listening for your voice; let me hear it.[^15]

**14** Flee, my beloved, and be like a gazelle or a young stag on the mountains of spices.[^16]

[^1]: The woman speaks in verses 1–4 ("you" is masculine). Literally "Who would make you like a brother to me".
[^2]: Or "she would teach me", that is, my mother. The Septuagint reads "and into the room of the one who conceived me", as in 3:4.
[^3]: Literally "why would you stir up and why would you awaken love until it pleases?" Here the refrain has no oath by the gazelles and does (compare 2:7; 3:5).
[^4]: Others speak, perhaps the daughters of Jerusalem (compare 3:6). In the rest of verse 5 and in verses 6–7 the woman speaks ("you" is masculine).
[^5]: Or "I aroused you".
[^6]: Or "jealousy". Hebrew *qin'ah*, "ardor, zeal, jealousy".
[^7]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^8]: Hebrew *shalhevetyah*, written as one word in the Leningrad Codex. The ending *-yah* may be *Yah*, the short form of the name YHWH (as in *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah"; Exodus 15:2), giving "a flame of Yah", "the very flame of the LORD". Or it may express a superlative, "a mighty flame", "the hottest flame", as words for God sometimes do in Hebrew (compare "mountains of God", that is, mighty mountains, in Psalm 36:7, 36:6 in most English Bibles). Some Hebrew manuscripts write it as two words, *shalhevet yah*, "a flame of Yah". If the divine name is here, it is the only place it appears in the Song.
[^9]: Or "it would be utterly scorned".
[^10]: Perhaps her brothers speak in verses 8–9 (compare 1:6).
[^11]: Hebrew *shalom*, "peace", perhaps a play on *Shelomoh*, "Solomon" (verses 11–12), and on "Shulammite" (7:1). The woman speaks.
[^12]: The place is not known. The name means "owner of a crowd" or "owner of wealth".
[^13]: Literally "a thousand of silver", probably shekels: about 25 pounds (11.5 kg) of silver.
[^14]: Compare 1:6. The speaker is probably the woman, perhaps the man.
[^15]: The man speaks ("you" is feminine).
[^16]: The woman speaks. Compare 2:17.
