**1** Turn back, turn back, O Shulammite![^shulammite] Turn back, turn back, that we may look at you! Why would you look at the Shulammite, as at the dance of the two camps?[^camps]

**2** How beautiful are your feet in sandals, O noble daughter![^noble] The curves of your thighs are like jewels, the work of a craftsman's hands.

**3** Your navel is a rounded bowl; may it never lack mixed wine.[^wine] Your belly is a heap of wheat, encircled with lilies.

**4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

**5** Your neck is like a tower of ivory. Your eyes are pools in Heshbon,[^heshbon] by the gate of Bath-rabbim.[^bathrabbim] Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, looking out toward Damascus.

**6** Your head crowns you like Carmel,[^carmel] and the flowing hair of your head is like purple;[^purple] a king is held captive in its tresses.[^tresses]

**7** How beautiful you are, and how pleasant, O love, with its delights!

**8** Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts are like its clusters.

**9** I said, "I will climb the palm tree; I will take hold of its fruit." Oh, may your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your breath[^nose] like apples,

**10** and your mouth[^palate] like the best wine, going down smoothly for my beloved,[^mybeloved] gliding over the lips of sleepers.[^sleepers]

**11** I am my beloved's, and his desire[^desire] is for me.

**12** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the fields; let us spend the night in the villages.[^villages]

**13** Let us go early to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has budded, whether its blossoms have opened, whether the pomegranates are in bloom. There I will give you my love.[^dodim]

**14** The mandrakes[^mandrakes] give out their fragrance, and at our doors are all kinds of choice fruits, new as well as old, which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

[^shulammite]: Verse 1 is 6:13 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–14 are 7:1–13. *Shulammite* may mean "woman of Shulem", perhaps Shunem (1 Kings 1:3), or it may be a feminine form of the name Solomon (*Shelomoh*). Others ("we") speak in the first half of the verse; who answers in the second half is not clear.
[^camps]: Or "the dance of Mahanaim", a place whose name means "two camps" (Genesis 32:3, 32:2 in English Bibles).
[^noble]: Literally "daughter of a noble". The man probably speaks in verses 2–10 ("you" is feminine).
[^wine]: Wine mixed with spices or water.
[^heshbon]: A city east of the Jordan, known for its pools.
[^bathrabbim]: The name means "daughter of many".
[^carmel]: The wooded mountain range on the coast. The same consonants can be read *karmil*, "crimson" (2 Chronicles 2:6).
[^purple]: The costly dark red-purple dye worn by kings.
[^tresses]: Hebrew *rehatim*, elsewhere "watering troughs" (Genesis 30:38), here the flowing locks of her hair.
[^nose]: Literally "your nose".
[^palate]: Literally "your palate".
[^mybeloved]: "My beloved" is the woman's name for the man (1:13). Here she seems to take up the man's words and finish them.
[^sleepers]: The Septuagint reads "over my lips and teeth".
[^desire]: Hebrew *teshuqah*, found elsewhere only in Genesis 3:16 and 4:7.
[^villages]: Or "among the henna bushes". Hebrew *kefarim* can mean either (compare 1:14).
[^dodim]: Hebrew *dodim*; see 1:2.
[^mandrakes]: Hebrew *duda'im*, a plant believed to stir love and help conception (Genesis 30:14–16). The word sounds like *dodim*, "love", and *dodi*, "my beloved".
