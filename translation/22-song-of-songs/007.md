**1** Turn back, turn back, O Shulammite![^1] Turn back, turn back, that we may look at you! Why would you look at the Shulammite, as at the dance of the two camps?[^2]

**2** How beautiful are your feet in sandals, O noble daughter![^3] The curves of your thighs are like jewels, the work of a craftsman's hands.

**3** Your navel is a rounded bowl; may it never lack mixed wine.[^4] Your belly is a heap of wheat, encircled with lilies.

**4** Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

**5** Your neck is like a tower of ivory. Your eyes are pools in Heshbon,[^5] by the gate of Bath-rabbim.[^6] Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon, looking out toward Damascus.

**6** Your head crowns you like Carmel,[^7] and the flowing hair of your head is like purple;[^8] a king is held captive in its tresses.[^9]

**7** How beautiful you are, and how pleasant, O love, with its delights!

**8** Your stature is like a palm tree, and your breasts are like its clusters.

**9** I said, "I will climb the palm tree; I will take hold of its fruit." Oh, may your breasts be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your breath[^10] like apples,

**10** and your mouth[^11] like the best wine, going down smoothly for my beloved,[^12] gliding over the lips of sleepers.[^13]

**11** I am my beloved's, and his desire[^14] is for me.

**12** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the fields; let us spend the night in the villages.[^15]

**13** Let us go early to the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has budded, whether its blossoms have opened, whether the pomegranates are in bloom. There I will give you my love.[^16]

**14** The mandrakes[^17] give out their fragrance, and at our doors are all kinds of choice fruits, new as well as old, which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

[^1]: Verse 1 is 6:13 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–14 are 7:1–13. *Shulammite* may mean "woman of Shulem", perhaps Shunem (1 Kings 1:3), or it may be a feminine form of the name Solomon (*Shelomoh*). Others ("we") speak in the first half of the verse; who answers in the second half is not clear.
[^2]: Or "the dance of Mahanaim", a place whose name means "two camps" (Genesis 32:3, 32:2 in English Bibles).
[^3]: Literally "daughter of a noble". The man probably speaks in verses 2–10 ("you" is feminine).
[^4]: Wine mixed with spices or water.
[^5]: A city east of the Jordan, known for its pools.
[^6]: The name means "daughter of many".
[^7]: The wooded mountain range on the coast.
[^8]: The costly dark red-purple dye worn by kings.
[^9]: Hebrew *rehatim*, elsewhere "watering troughs" (Genesis 30:38), here the flowing locks of her hair.
[^10]: Literally "your nose".
[^11]: Literally "your palate".
[^12]: "My beloved" is the woman's name for the man (1:13). Here she seems to take up the man's words and finish them.
[^13]: The Septuagint reads "over my lips and teeth".
[^14]: Hebrew *teshuqah*, found elsewhere only in Genesis 3:16 and 4:7.
[^15]: Or "among the henna bushes". Hebrew *kefarim* can mean either (compare 1:14).
[^16]: Hebrew *dodim*; see 1:2.
[^17]: Hebrew *duda'im*, a plant believed to stir love and help conception (Genesis 30:14–16). The word sounds like *dodim*, "love", and *dodi*, "my beloved".
