**1** The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's.[^1]

**2** Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth![^2] For your love[^3] is better than wine.

**3** Your oils have a pleasing fragrance; your name is oil poured out.[^4] That is why the young women love you.

**4** Draw me after you; let us run! The king has brought me into his rooms. We will rejoice and be glad in you;[^5] we will praise your love more than wine. How rightly they love you![^6]

**5** I am dark, and lovely,[^7] daughters of Jerusalem, like the tents of Kedar,[^8] like the curtains of Solomon.

**6** Do not stare at me because I am dark, because the sun has gazed on me. My mother's sons were angry with me; they made me keep the vineyards, but my own vineyard I have not kept.[^9]

**7** Tell me, you whom my soul loves,[^10] where do you pasture your flock? Where do you make it lie down at noon? For why should I be like a woman who veils herself[^11] beside the flocks of your companions?

**8** If you do not know,[^12] most beautiful of women, follow the tracks of the flock, and pasture your young goats beside the shepherds' tents.

**9** To a mare[^13] among Pharaoh's chariots I compare you, my darling.[^14]

**10** Your cheeks are lovely with ornaments, your neck with strings of beads.

**11** We will make you ornaments of gold, studded with silver.

**12** While the king was at his couch,[^15] my nard[^16] gave out its fragrance.

**13** My beloved[^17] is to me a pouch of myrrh that lies between my breasts.[^18]

**14** My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms in the vineyards of En-gedi.[^19]

**15** How beautiful you are, my darling! How beautiful you are! Your eyes are doves.[^20]

**16** How handsome you are, my beloved, how delightful![^21] Our bed is green with leaves.

**17** The beams of our house are cedars, our rafters are junipers.[^22]

[^1]: "Song of Songs" is a Hebrew superlative: the finest of songs. *Which is Solomon's* may also mean "for Solomon" or "about Solomon".
[^2]: The Song is a series of love poems in which a woman and a man speak to each other and about each other, with other voices (the "daughters of Jerusalem" and others). The Hebrew does not say who is speaking, but its forms of "you", "I" and "my" often show whether a man or a woman speaks or is addressed. The notes give the probable speaker where it matters. Here the woman speaks, to the man ("you" is masculine), through verse 7.
[^3]: Hebrew *dodim*, love shown in caresses and lovemaking. Also in verse 4.
[^4]: A play on words: *shemen*, "oil", and *shem*, "name".
[^5]: "We" may be the young women of verse 3, or the woman and the man together. "You" is masculine.
[^6]: Literally "With uprightness they love you".
[^7]: Or "but lovely".
[^8]: A nomadic people of the Arabian desert, whose tents were made of black goats' hair.
[^9]: Probably a picture of herself.
[^10]: Literally "whom my soul (*nephesh*) loves", that is, whom I love with all my being.
[^11]: Perhaps a woman taken for a prostitute (compare Genesis 38:14–15). The Vulgate reads "one who wanders".
[^12]: "You" is feminine. The man answers her, or the young women do.
[^13]: Literally "my mare". Chariot horses were stallions, so a mare among them would draw every eye.
[^14]: Hebrew *ra'yati*, "my companion, my friend", the man's name for the woman throughout the Song. The man speaks in verses 9–11.
[^15]: Or "at his table", where guests reclined on couches. The woman speaks in verses 12–14.
[^16]: A costly perfume made from a plant of the Himalayas.
[^17]: Hebrew *dodi*, "my beloved", the woman's name for the man throughout the Song. It comes from the same root as *dodim*, "love", in verse 2.
[^18]: Or "that spends the night between my breasts".
[^19]: An oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea.
[^20]: The man speaks ("you" is feminine).
[^21]: The woman speaks ("you" is masculine) in verses 16–17.
[^22]: Hebrew *berotim*, perhaps junipers or cypresses.
