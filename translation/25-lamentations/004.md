**1** How[^1] the gold has grown dim, how the pure gold has changed! The holy stones[^2] lie scattered at the head of every street.

**2** The precious children of Zion, worth their weight in fine gold, how they are regarded as clay jars, the work of a potter's hands!

**3** Even jackals[^3] offer the breast and nurse their young, but my dear people[^4] has become cruel, like ostriches in the wilderness.[^5]

**4** The tongue of the nursing infant sticks to the roof of its mouth for thirst. Little children ask for bread, but no one breaks it for them.

**5** Those who used to eat delicacies are desolate in the streets. Those brought up in scarlet now cling to ash heaps.

**6** The punishment[^6] of my dear people is greater than the punishment of Sodom, which was overthrown in a moment, with no hand laid on it.[^7]

**7** Her princes[^8] were purer than snow, whiter than milk; their bodies were ruddier than coral,[^9] their form like sapphire.[^10]

**8** Now their appearance is blacker than soot; they are not recognized in the streets. Their skin has shriveled on their bones; it has become as dry as wood.

**9** Better off were those killed by the sword than those killed by hunger, who waste away, pierced through by the lack of the produce of the field.[^11]

**10** The hands of compassionate women have boiled their own children; they became their food in the destruction of my dear people.

**11** Yahweh[^12] has spent his wrath; he has poured out his burning anger. He has kindled a fire in Zion, and it has consumed her foundations.

**12** The kings of the earth did not believe, nor did any of the inhabitants of the world, that foe and enemy could enter the gates of Jerusalem.

**13** It happened because of the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests,[^13] who shed in her midst the blood of the righteous.

**14** They wandered blind through the streets, so defiled with blood that no one could touch their garments.

**15** "Go away! Unclean!" people cried to them. "Go away, go away! Do not touch!" So they fled and wandered, and people among the nations said, "They shall not stay here any longer."[^14]

**16** Yahweh himself[^15] has scattered them; he will no longer look on them. No one showed respect to the priests, or favor to the elders.

**17** Our eyes still wear themselves out, looking in vain[^16] for help. From our watchtower we watched for a nation that could not save us.

**18** They dogged our steps so that we could not walk in our streets. Our end drew near; our days were numbered,[^17] for our end had come.

**19** Our pursuers were swifter than the eagles[^18] in the sky. They chased us on the mountains; they lay in wait for us in the wilderness.

**20** The breath of our nostrils, Yahweh's anointed,[^19] was caught in their pits, the one of whom we said, "Under his shadow we will live among the nations."

**21** Rejoice and be glad, Daughter Edom,[^20] you who live in the land of Uz![^21] To you also the cup will pass;[^22] you will become drunk and strip yourself naked.

**22** Your punishment is complete, Daughter Zion; he will not keep you in exile any longer. But he will punish your iniquity, Daughter Edom; he will expose your sins.

[^1]: Hebrew *eikhah*, "How!"; see 1:1. This chapter is an acrostic poem: its twenty-two verses begin with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, with *pe* (verse 16) before *ayin* (verse 17), as in chapters 2 and 3. Each verse here has two lines.
[^2]: Perhaps the stones of the temple, or precious stones, a picture of the "precious children" of verse 2.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written *tannin*, which can mean "sea monster" or be another plural form of "jackals", but read *tannim*, "jackals".
[^4]: Literally "the daughter of my people"; see 2:11. Also in verses 6 and 10.
[^5]: The ostrich was thought to neglect its young (Job 39:13–16).
[^6]: Or "iniquity". Hebrew *avon* means both the wrong and its punishment, and *chatta't*, "sin", later in the verse, can also mean its punishment. Also in verse 22.
[^7]: Literally "and no hands whirled against it". Or "and no hands were wrung over it".
[^8]: Or "Her Nazirites". Hebrew *nezireha*, "her consecrated ones".
[^9]: Hebrew *peninim*, perhaps red coral, or pearls.
[^10]: Hebrew *sappir*, probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone.
[^11]: Literally "who flow away, pierced, from the produce of the field".
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^13]: Literally "From the sins of her prophets, the iniquities of her priests".
[^14]: Or "They shall not live among us as foreigners any longer".
[^15]: Literally "The face of Yahweh".
[^16]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", the key word of Ecclesiastes; here "in vain".
[^17]: Literally "our days were full".
[^18]: Or "vultures". Hebrew *nesher*.
[^19]: That is, the king of Judah, probably Zedekiah, who was captured as he fled (2 Kings 25:4–7).
[^20]: A poetic name for Edom and its people, like "Daughter Zion" for Jerusalem (1:6). Also in verse 22.
[^21]: A land to the east or southeast of Israel; compare Job 1:1.
[^22]: The cup of God's wrath, which the nations must drink (Jeremiah 25:15–29).
