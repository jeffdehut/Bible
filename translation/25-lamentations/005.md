**1** Remember, LORD,[^1] what has happened to us; look and see our disgrace.[^2]

**2** Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers, our houses to foreigners.

**3** We have become orphans, without a father; our mothers are like widows.

**4** We must pay silver for the water we drink; our wood comes to us at a price.

**5** Our pursuers are at our necks;[^3] we are weary and are given no rest.

**6** We have given our hand to Egypt, and to Assyria, to get enough bread.[^4]

**7** Our ancestors sinned and are no more, and we bear their iniquities.

**8** Slaves[^5] rule over us; there is no one to rescue us from their hand.

**9** We get our bread at the risk of our lives, because of the sword in the wilderness.

**10** Our skin is hot as an oven from the burning heat of hunger.

**11** Women have been raped in Zion, young women in the towns of Judah.

**12** Princes have been hung up by their hands;[^6] elders are shown no respect.

**13** Young men carry the millstone,[^7] and boys stagger under loads of wood.

**14** The elders have left the city gate; the young men have stopped their music.

**15** The joy of our hearts has ceased; our dancing has turned to mourning.

**16** The crown has fallen from our head. Woe to us, for we have sinned!

**17** Because of this our heart is faint; because of these things our eyes have grown dim:

**18** because Mount Zion lies desolate, with foxes prowling over it.

**19** But you, LORD, reign forever;[^8] your throne endures from generation to generation.

**20** Why do you forget us forever? Why do you forsake us for so long?

**21** Bring us back to you, LORD, so that we may return; renew our days as of old,

**22** unless you have utterly rejected us, and are angry with us beyond measure.[^9]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: This chapter has twenty-two verses, as many as the letters of the Hebrew alphabet, but unlike chapters 1–4 it is not an acrostic. Its lines are shorter, and the whole chapter is a prayer of the people ("we").
[^3]: Literally "On our necks we are pursued".
[^4]: To give the hand means to submit, or to make a treaty.
[^5]: Or "Servants".
[^6]: Or "by their hand", that is, by the enemy.
[^7]: Literally "carry the grinding".
[^8]: Literally "sit enthroned".
[^9]: Or "For you have utterly rejected us; you are angry with us beyond measure", or "Or have you utterly rejected us? Are you angry with us beyond measure?" When the book is read aloud in the synagogue, verse 21 is repeated after this verse, so that the reading does not end on this note.
