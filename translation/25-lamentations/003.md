**1** I am the man who has seen affliction under the rod of his wrath.[^1]

**2** He has driven me away and made me walk in darkness, not light.

**3** Surely against me he turns his hand again and again, all day long.

**4** He has worn away my flesh and my skin; he has broken my bones.

**5** He has besieged me and surrounded me with bitterness[^2] and hardship.

**6** He has made me live in dark places, like those long dead.[^3]

**7** He has walled me in so that I cannot get out; he has made my chains heavy.[^4]

**8** Though I call out and cry for help, he shuts out my prayer.

**9** He has walled up my ways with hewn stone; he has made my paths crooked.

**10** He is to me a bear lying in wait, a lion in hiding.

**11** He has turned aside my ways and torn me to pieces; he has made me desolate.

**12** He bent his bow and set me up as a target for his arrow.

**13** He has shot into my heart[^5] the arrows of his quiver.[^6]

**14** I have become a laughingstock to all my people,[^7] their mocking song all day long.

**15** He has filled me with bitter herbs; he has made me drink my fill of wormwood.

**16** He has broken my teeth with gravel; he has pressed me down in the ashes.

**17** My soul has been rejected from peace;[^8] I have forgotten what good is.

**18** So I said, "My strength has perished, and my hope from the LORD."[^9]

**19** Remember my affliction and my homelessness, the wormwood and the bitterness.[^10]

**20** My soul remembers it continually and is bowed down within me.[^11]

**21** But this I call to mind, and therefore I have hope:

**22** Because of the LORD's faithful love[^12] we are not finished,[^13] for his compassion does not fail.

**23** It is new every morning; great is your faithfulness.

**24** "The LORD is my portion," says my soul, "therefore I will hope in him."

**25** The LORD is good to those who wait for him, to the soul who seeks him.

**26** It is good to wait in silence for the salvation of the LORD.

**27** It is good for a man to bear the yoke in his youth.

**28** Let him sit alone in silence, for he has laid it on him.[^14]

**29** Let him put his mouth in the dust; perhaps there may yet be hope.

**30** Let him offer his cheek to the one who strikes him; let him be filled with disgrace.

**31** For the Lord will not reject forever.

**32** Though he brings grief, he will have compassion according to the greatness of his faithful love.

**33** For he does not afflict willingly,[^15] or bring grief to human beings.

**34** To crush underfoot all the prisoners of the land,

**35** to deny a man his rights before the Most High,[^16]

**36** to wrong a person in his lawsuit: the Lord does not approve.[^17]

**37** Who has spoken and it came to pass, unless the Lord has commanded it?

**38** Is it not from the mouth of the Most High that both evil and good come?

**39** Why should any living person complain, any man, about his sins?[^18]

**40** Let us search and examine our ways, and return to the LORD.

**41** Let us lift up our hearts with our hands to God in heaven:

**42** We have rebelled and disobeyed, and you have not forgiven.

**43** You have wrapped yourself in anger and pursued us; you have killed without pity.

**44** You have wrapped yourself in a cloud, so that no prayer can pass through.

**45** You have made us scum and refuse among the peoples.

**46** All our enemies have opened their mouths against us.

**47** Panic and pit have come upon us,[^19] devastation and destruction.

**48** My eye runs down with streams of water because of the destruction of my dear people.[^20]

**49** My eye flows and does not stop, without any rest,

**50** until the LORD looks down from heaven and sees.

**51** My eye brings grief to my soul because of all the daughters of my city.

**52** Those who are my enemies without cause have hunted me down like a bird.

**53** They tried to end my life in a pit,[^21] and they threw stones at me.[^22]

**54** Water flowed over my head; I said, "I am cut off!"

**55** I called on your name, LORD, from the depths of the pit.

**56** You heard my voice; do not close your ear to my cry for relief, to my cry for help!

**57** You came near on the day I called to you; you said, "Do not fear!"

**58** You have taken up my case, Lord; you have redeemed my life.

**59** You have seen the wrong done to me, LORD; judge my case!

**60** You have seen all their vengeance, all their plots against me.

**61** You have heard their taunts, LORD, all their plots against me,

**62** the lips of those who rise against me, and their muttering against me all day long.

**63** Look at them, sitting or standing:[^23] I am their mocking song.

**64** Pay them back, LORD,[^24] according to the work of their hands.

**65** Give them a hardened heart;[^25] may your curse be on them!

**66** Pursue them in anger and destroy them from under the heavens of the LORD.[^26]

[^1]: This chapter is a triple acrostic. It has sixty-six verses, and each letter of the Hebrew alphabet begins three verses in a row: verses 1–3 begin with *alef*, verses 4–6 with *bet*, and so on to *tav* in verses 64–66. As in chapters 2 and 4, *pe* (verses 46–48) comes before *ayin* (verses 49–51). The speaker is one man, who speaks of God as "he" until verse 18.
[^2]: Hebrew *rosh*, a bitter, poisonous plant. Also in verse 19.
[^3]: The same words are in Psalm 143:3.
[^4]: Literally "my bronze", that is, my bronze chains.
[^5]: Literally "my kidneys", the inmost being.
[^6]: Literally "the sons of his quiver".
[^7]: Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Syriac read "all peoples".
[^8]: Or "You have rejected my soul from peace".
[^9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^10]: Or "The memory of my affliction and my homelessness is wormwood and bitterness".
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "and my soul muses (or complains)" but read "and my soul is bowed down". Or "You will surely remember, and my soul is bowed down within me".
[^12]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Here and in verse 32 the word is plural, "acts of faithful love".
[^13]: Literally "The faithful love of the LORD, for we are not finished". The Syriac and the Targum read "it is not finished", matching the next line; this reading is followed by many translations.
[^14]: Or "when it has been laid on him".
[^15]: Literally "from his heart".
[^16]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God. Also in verse 38.
[^17]: Literally "the Lord does not see". Or "does the Lord not see?"
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "his sin" but read "his sins". Or "Why should a living person complain? Let each one complain about his own sins".
[^19]: A play on sounds: *pachad va-fachat*. Compare Isaiah 24:17; Jeremiah 48:43.
[^20]: Literally "the daughter of my people"; see 2:11.
[^21]: Hebrew *bor*, a pit or cistern, also a picture of the grave. Also in verse 55.
[^22]: Or "they set a stone over me".
[^23]: Literally "their sitting and their rising".
[^24]: Or "You will pay them back, LORD"; also in verses 65–66.
[^25]: Hebrew *meginnat lev*, a phrase found only here, perhaps "a covering of the heart", that is, a hardened or anguished heart.
[^26]: The Septuagint reads "under heaven, O Lord".
