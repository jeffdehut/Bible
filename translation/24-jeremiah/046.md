**1** The word of the LORD[^1] that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the nations.[^2]

**2** Concerning Egypt. About the army of Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt, which was by the Euphrates River at Carchemish, and which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon defeated in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah:[^3]

**3** Prepare shield and buckler,[^4] and advance to battle!

**4** Harness the horses; mount, you horsemen![^5] Take your positions with helmets on! Polish the spears; put on your armor!

**5** Why do I see them terrified, falling back? Their warriors are beaten down; they flee headlong and do not look back. Terror on every side![^6] declares the LORD.

**6** The swift cannot flee, nor the warrior escape. In the north, by the Euphrates River, they stumble and fall.

**7** Who is this, rising like the Nile,[^7] like rivers whose waters surge?

**8** Egypt rises like the Nile, like rivers whose waters surge. He says, "I will rise and cover the earth; I will destroy cities and those who live in them."

**9** Go up, you horses! Drive furiously, you chariots! Let the warriors go out: Cush and Put, who carry the shield, and the Ludim, who draw the bow.[^8]

**10** That day belongs to the Lord GOD of Armies,[^9] a day of vengeance, to avenge himself on his foes. The sword will devour and be satisfied, and drink its fill of their blood. For the Lord GOD of Armies holds a sacrifice in the land of the north, by the Euphrates River.

**11** Go up to Gilead and get balm,[^10] virgin daughter Egypt! In vain you have used many medicines;[^11] there is no healing for you.

**12** The nations have heard of your shame; your cry fills the earth. For warrior has stumbled against warrior; both have fallen together.

**13** The word that the LORD spoke to Jeremiah the prophet about the coming of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon to strike the land of Egypt:[^12]

**14** Declare it in Egypt, proclaim it in Migdol, proclaim it in Memphis and in Tahpanhes.[^13] Say: Take your stand and get ready, for the sword devours those around you.

**15** Why has your mighty one been swept away?[^14] He did not stand, because the LORD drove him down.

**16** He made many stumble; they fell, each against the other. They said, "Get up, let us go back to our own people and to the land of our birth, away from the sword of the oppressor."[^15]

**17** There they cried out, "Pharaoh king of Egypt is only a noise; he has let the appointed time pass by."[^16]

**18** As surely as I live, declares the King, whose name is the LORD of Armies, one will come like Tabor among the mountains, and like Carmel by the sea.[^17]

**19** Pack your bags for exile, you who live there, daughter Egypt, for Memphis will become a desolation, burned[^18] and empty of inhabitants.

**20** Egypt is a beautiful heifer, but a gadfly[^19] from the north has come, has come!

**21** Even her hired soldiers in her midst are like fattened calves. They too have turned and fled together; they did not stand. For the day of their calamity has come upon them, the time of their punishment.

**22** Her sound is like a snake slithering away,[^20] for they march on in force; they come against her with axes, like those who cut down trees.

**23** They will cut down her forest, declares the LORD, though it cannot be searched through,[^21] for they are more numerous than locusts; they cannot be counted.

**24** Daughter Egypt is put to shame; she is given into the hand of the people of the north.

**25** The LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says: I am going to punish Amon of Thebes,[^22] and Pharaoh and Egypt, her gods and her kings, Pharaoh and those who trust in him.

**26** I will give them into the hand of those who seek their lives, into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and his servants. But afterward Egypt will be inhabited as in days of old, declares the LORD.

**27** As for you, do not be afraid, Jacob my servant; do not be dismayed, Israel. For I am going to save you from far away, and your descendants from the land of their captivity. Jacob will return and be at peace and secure, and no one will make him afraid.[^23]

**28** As for you, do not be afraid, Jacob my servant, declares the LORD, for I am with you. I will make a full end of all the nations where I have driven you, but of you I will not make a full end. I will discipline you with justice,[^24] and I will by no means leave you unpunished.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Verse 1 is the heading of the oracles against the nations in chapters 46–51. In the Septuagint they come after 25:13 (see the note there), and in a different order: Elam (49:34–39), Egypt (46), Babylon (50–51), the Philistines (47), Edom (49:7–22), Ammon (49:1–6), Kedar (49:28–33), Damascus (49:23–27) and Moab (48).
[^3]: In 605 BC. Carchemish was on the upper Euphrates, on the present border of Turkey and Syria. Four years earlier Neco had killed King Josiah at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29).
[^4]: Hebrew *magen*, a small round shield, and *tsinnah*, a large shield that covered the whole body.
[^5]: Or "mount the stallions".
[^6]: Hebrew *magor missaviv*: see 6:25.
[^7]: Hebrew *ye'or*, the Nile, which rose in flood each year. Also in verse 8.
[^8]: Literally "who grasp, who bend the bow". Cush was the land south of Egypt, and Put was probably Libya. The Ludim may be Lydians from Asia Minor. All served in Egypt's army.
[^9]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH tseva'ot*, "my Lord YHWH of Armies". *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *The LORD of Armies* is also in verses 18 and 25.
[^10]: See 8:22.
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "I have used many medicines" but read "you have used many medicines". The written form may be an old spelling of "you have used" (compare 2:20, 33).
[^12]: Compare 43:8–13.
[^13]: See 44:1.
[^14]: Hebrew *abbirekha*, "your mighty one", a word also used of a bull. The Septuagint divides the letters differently and reads "Why has Apis fled? Your chosen calf did not stand." Apis was the sacred bull worshiped at Memphis.
[^15]: Or "the cruel sword". Hebrew *ha-yonah*, perhaps "the oppressor" (25:38; 50:16).
[^16]: Or, with the same consonants, "Call Pharaoh king of Egypt by the name 'Big Noise, who let the appointed time pass by'." The Hebrew *sha'on*, "noise, uproar", may mock a royal title.
[^17]: Mount Tabor rises alone above the plain of Jezreel, and Mount Carmel above the Mediterranean coast. The one who comes is the king of Babylon.
[^18]: Or "laid waste".
[^19]: Hebrew *qerets*, a rare word, perhaps "gadfly" or "horsefly".
[^20]: Literally "Her sound goes like a snake". Egypt hisses and slips away as the enemy advances.
[^21]: Or "though it is impenetrable".
[^22]: Amon was the chief god of Thebes (Hebrew *No*), the great city of Upper Egypt.
[^23]: Verses 27–28 repeat 30:10–11 with small differences.
[^24]: Or "in due measure". Hebrew *mishpat*, "justice, judgment".
