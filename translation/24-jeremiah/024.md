**1** Yahweh[^1] showed me two baskets of figs set in front of the temple of Yahweh. This was after Nebuchadnezzar[^2] king of Babylon had taken into exile from Jerusalem Jeconiah[^3] son of Jehoiakim king of Judah, the officials of Judah, the craftsmen and the metalworkers,[^4] and had brought them to Babylon.

**2** One basket had very good figs, like figs that ripen early, and the other basket had very bad figs, so bad they could not be eaten.

**3** Yahweh said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" I said, "Figs. The good figs are very good, and the bad ones are very bad, so bad they cannot be eaten."

**4** Then the word of Yahweh came to me:

**5** "This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: Like these good figs, so I will regard as good the exiles of Judah, whom I have sent away from this place to the land of the Babylonians.[^5]

**6** I will set my eyes on them for good, and I will bring them back to this land. I will build them up and not tear them down; I will plant them and not uproot them.

**7** I will give them a heart to know me, that I am Yahweh. They will be my people, and I will be their God, for they will return to me with all their heart.

**8** But like the bad figs, which are so bad they cannot be eaten, this is what Yahweh says, so I will treat Zedekiah king of Judah, his officials, and the remnant of Jerusalem who remain in this land and those who live in the land of Egypt.

**9** I will make them a horror, a disaster, to all the kingdoms of the earth, a disgrace and a byword, a taunt and a curse, in all the places where I will drive them.

**10** I will send the sword, famine and plague against them until they are wiped out from the land I gave to them and their ancestors."

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *Nevukhadre'tsar*: see 21:2.
[^3]: Also called Jehoiachin, and Coniah (22:24). He was taken to Babylon in 597 BC (2 Kings 24:10–16).
[^4]: Hebrew *masger*, perhaps "smith" or "locksmith" (2 Kings 24:14).
[^5]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon.
