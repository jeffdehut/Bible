**1** Hear the word that the LORD[^1] speaks to you, house of Israel.

**2** This is what the LORD says: Do not learn the way of the nations, and do not be terrified by the signs in the heavens, though the nations are terrified by them.

**3** For the customs[^2] of the peoples are worthless.[^3] A tree is cut from the forest and shaped with a chisel by a craftsman's hands.

**4** They adorn it with silver and gold; they fasten it with nails and hammers so that it will not totter.

**5** Like a scarecrow in a cucumber field,[^4] they cannot speak. They have to be carried, because they cannot walk. Do not be afraid of them, for they can do no harm, and they cannot do any good either.[^5]

**6** There is no one like you, LORD. You are great, and your name is great in might.

**7** Who would not fear you, King of the nations? It is your due. For among all the wise of the nations and in all their kingdoms there is no one like you.

**8** They are all stupid and foolish alike; the teaching of worthless idols[^6] is only wood!

**9** Hammered silver is brought from Tarshish,[^7] and gold from Uphaz,[^8] the work of a craftsman and of a goldsmith's hands. Their clothing is violet and purple;[^9] they are all the work of skilled workers.

**10** But the LORD is the true God; he is the living God and the everlasting King. At his wrath the earth quakes, and the nations cannot endure his fury.

**11** This is what you are to say to them:[^10] The gods who did not make the heavens and the earth will perish from the earth and from under these heavens.

**12** He made the earth by his power; he established the world by his wisdom, and by his understanding he stretched out the heavens.[^11]

**13** When he thunders,[^12] there is a roar of waters in the heavens; he makes the clouds rise from the ends of the earth. He makes lightning for the rain, and brings out the wind from his storehouses.

**14** Every human being is stupid and without knowledge; every goldsmith is put to shame by his idol. For his cast images are a lie; there is no breath[^13] in them.

**15** They are worthless, a work of mockery. At the time of their punishment they will perish.

**16** The Portion of Jacob[^14] is not like these, for he is the maker of all things, and Israel is the tribe of his inheritance. The LORD of Armies[^15] is his name.

**17** Gather up your bundle from the ground,[^16] you who live under siege!

**18** For this is what the LORD says: This time I am going to sling out the inhabitants of the land. I will bring distress on them, so that they will feel it.[^17]

**19** Woe is me because of my injury! My wound is incurable. But I said, "Surely this is my sickness, and I must bear it."[^18]

**20** My tent is destroyed, and all my tent ropes are snapped. My children have gone from me and are no more. There is no one left to pitch my tent again or to set up my tent curtains.

**21** For the shepherds are stupid; they have not sought the LORD. So they have not prospered,[^19] and all their flock is scattered.

**22** Listen! A report is coming, a great commotion from the land of the north, to make the cities of Judah a desolation, a haunt of jackals.

**23** I know, LORD, that a human being's way is not in his own control;[^20] it is not for anyone who walks to direct his own steps.

**24** Correct me, LORD, but only with justice, not in your anger, or you will reduce me to nothing.

**25** Pour out your wrath on the nations that do not know you, and on the clans that do not call on your name. For they have devoured Jacob; they have devoured him and consumed him, and laid waste his homeland.[^21]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *chuqqot*, "decrees, customs".
[^3]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": see 2:5. Also in verses 8 and 15.
[^4]: Hebrew *tomer miqshah*, perhaps "a palm post in a cucumber field". Compare the Letter of Jeremiah, verse 70 (Baruch 6:70), which uses the same picture.
[^5]: The Septuagint does not have verses 6–8 and 10, and it places verse 9 within verse 5. A Hebrew manuscript of Jeremiah from Qumran (4QJer-b) has the same shorter text.
[^6]: Literally "the instruction of vapors".
[^7]: A distant land rich in metals, probably in the far west of the Mediterranean.
[^8]: An unknown place, perhaps Ophir, the land of gold (1 Kings 9:28).
[^9]: Hebrew *tekhelet* and *argaman*, costly dyed wool, violet-blue and red-purple.
[^10]: This verse is in Aramaic, the international language of the time, not in Hebrew; Hebrew resumes in verse 12. It is the only Aramaic in Jeremiah. It contains a wordplay: the gods who did not *avadu*, "make", the heavens will *ye'vadu*, "perish".
[^11]: Verses 12–16 are repeated in 51:15–19.
[^12]: Literally "At the sound of his giving".
[^13]: Hebrew *ruach*, "breath, spirit".
[^14]: A title of God (51:19; Psalm 16:5): the one who belongs to Jacob as his share, as the land belongs to the other tribes.
[^15]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^16]: Or "from the land". The meaning of the Hebrew word *kin'ah*, "bundle", is uncertain. Jerusalem, pictured as a woman, is told to get ready for exile.
[^17]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "so that they may find".
[^18]: Jerusalem, pictured as a mother, speaks in verses 19–20.
[^19]: Or "they have not acted wisely". The Hebrew verb can mean either.
[^20]: Literally "is not to him", that is, it does not belong to him to decide.
[^21]: Verse 25 is nearly the same as Psalm 79:6–7.
