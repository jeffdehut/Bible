**1** The word that came to Jeremiah from Yahweh:[^1]

**2** This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: Write in a scroll all the words I have spoken to you.

**3** For look, the days are coming, declares Yahweh, when I will restore the fortunes[^2] of my people Israel and Judah, says Yahweh. I will bring them back to the land I gave their ancestors, and they will take possession of it.

**4** These are the words Yahweh spoke concerning Israel and Judah.

**5** For this is what Yahweh says: We have heard a cry of terror, of dread and not of peace.

**6** Ask now, and see: can a male give birth? Why then do I see every man with his hands on his loins like a woman in labor? Why has every face turned deathly pale?

**7** Woe! For that day is great; there is none like it. It is a time of distress for Jacob, yet he will be saved out of it.

**8** On that day, declares Yahweh of Armies,[^3] I will break his yoke from off your neck,[^4] and I will tear off your bonds. Foreigners will no longer make him their slave.

**9** Instead they will serve Yahweh their God and David their king, whom I will raise up for them.[^5]

**10** As for you, do not be afraid, Jacob my servant, declares Yahweh; do not be dismayed, Israel. For I am going to save you from far away, and your descendants from the land of their captivity. Jacob will return and be at peace and secure, and no one will make him afraid.[^6]

**11** For I am with you, declares Yahweh, to save you. I will make a full end of all the nations among which I have scattered you, but of you I will not make a full end. I will discipline you with justice,[^7] and I will by no means leave you unpunished.

**12** For this is what Yahweh says: Your injury is incurable;[^8] your wound is beyond healing.

**13** There is no one to plead your cause, no remedy for your sore, no new skin for you.[^9]

**14** All your lovers have forgotten you; they do not seek you. For I have struck you as an enemy strikes, with the punishment of a cruel one, because your guilt is great and your sins are many.

**15** Why do you cry out over your injury? Your pain is incurable. Because your guilt is great and your sins are many, I have done these things to you.

**16** Therefore all who devour you will be devoured, and all your foes, every one of them, will go into captivity. Those who plunder you will become plunder, and all who prey on you I will give up as prey.

**17** For I will restore you to health[^10] and heal you of your wounds, declares Yahweh, because they have called you an outcast: "That is Zion; no one seeks her."

**18** This is what Yahweh says: I am going to restore the fortunes of the tents of Jacob and have compassion on his dwellings. The city will be rebuilt on its mound,[^11] and the palace will stand in its rightful place.

**19** From them will come songs of thanksgiving and the sound of people celebrating. I will multiply them, and they will not decrease; I will bring them honor, and they will not be made small.

**20** His children will be as they were long ago, and his community will be established before me. I will punish all who oppress him.

**21** His leader[^12] will be one of their own, and his ruler will come out from among them. I will bring him near, and he will approach me, for who would otherwise dare to approach me?[^13] declares Yahweh.

**22** So you will be my people, and I will be your God.

**23** Look, the storm of Yahweh! Wrath has gone out, a sweeping[^14] storm. It will whirl down on the head of the wicked.[^15]

**24** The fierce anger of Yahweh will not turn back until he has carried out and accomplished the purposes of his heart. In the days to come[^16] you will understand this.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Chapters 30–33 are often called the Book of Consolation, because they promise the return of Israel and Judah from exile.
[^2]: Literally "turn the turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back the captives". Also in verse 18.
[^3]: *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^4]: The yoke is that of the king of Babylon (27:8–11; 28:10–14). The Hebrew moves between "you" and "him", both meaning Jacob.
[^5]: That is, a king from David's line (23:5; Ezekiel 34:23–24; Hosea 3:5).
[^6]: Verses 10–11 are repeated with small differences in 46:27–28. The Septuagint does not have them here.
[^7]: Or "in due measure". Hebrew *mishpat*, "justice, judgment".
[^8]: Hebrew *shever*, "breaking, fracture". Also in verse 15. In verses 12–17 Zion is addressed as a woman (verse 17).
[^9]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "There is no one judging your judgment for a sore; remedies, new flesh, there are none for you".
[^10]: Literally "bring up new flesh for you".
[^11]: Hebrew *tel*, the mound formed by the ruins of earlier settlements.
[^12]: Literally "his mighty one".
[^13]: Literally "for who is he who would pledge his heart to approach me?" To come near to God without being called was to risk one's life (Numbers 17:28, which is 17:13 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Hebrew *mitgorer*, a rare word, perhaps "sweeping" or "gathering". The same lines in 23:19 have *mitcholel*, "whirling".
[^15]: Verses 23–24 are nearly the same as 23:19–20.
[^16]: Literally "in the latter end of the days".
