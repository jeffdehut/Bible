**1** If a man sends his wife away,[^1] and she leaves him and becomes another man's wife, will he go back to her again? Would that land not be utterly defiled?[^2] But you have prostituted yourself with many lovers, and would you return to me?[^3] declares the LORD.[^4]

**2** Lift up your eyes to the bare heights and look: where have you not been lain with?[^5] You sat by the roads waiting for them, like a nomad[^6] in the desert. You have defiled the land with your prostitution and your wickedness.

**3** So the showers have been held back, and the spring rain[^7] has not come. Yet you have the brazen look of a prostitute;[^8] you refuse to be ashamed.

**4** Have you not just now called to me,[^9] "My father, you are the companion of my youth!

**5** Will he stay angry forever? Will he keep his anger to the end?" Look, that is how you have spoken, yet you have done all the evil you could.

**6** The LORD said to me in the days of King Josiah: Have you seen what Faithless Israel[^10] has done? She went up on every high mountain and under every leafy tree, and prostituted herself there.

**7** I thought, "After she has done all this, she will return to me." But she did not return, and her treacherous sister Judah saw it.

**8** I saw that, though I had sent Faithless Israel away and given her a certificate of divorce for all her adulteries, her treacherous sister Judah had no fear. She too went and prostituted herself.

**9** Because her prostitution meant so little to her,[^11] she defiled the land, committing adultery with stone and tree.[^12]

**10** Yet for all this her treacherous sister Judah did not return to me with all her heart, but only in pretense, declares the LORD.

**11** The LORD said to me: Faithless Israel has shown herself more righteous than treacherous Judah.

**12** Go and proclaim these words toward the north, and say: Return, Faithless Israel,[^13] declares the LORD. I will not look on you in anger,[^14] for I am faithful,[^15] declares the LORD; I will not stay angry forever.

**13** Only acknowledge your guilt, that you have rebelled against the LORD your God. You have spread your favors[^16] to strangers under every leafy tree, and you have not listened to my voice, declares the LORD.

**14** Return, faithless children,[^17] declares the LORD, for I am your master.[^18] I will take you, one from a city and two from a clan, and bring you to Zion.

**15** I will give you shepherds after my own heart, and they will feed you with knowledge and understanding.

**16** When you have multiplied and become fruitful in the land in those days, declares the LORD, people will no longer say, "The ark of the covenant of the LORD." It will not come to mind. They will not remember it or miss it, and another will not be made.

**17** At that time they will call Jerusalem "The Throne of the LORD," and all the nations will gather to it, to the name of the LORD, to Jerusalem. They will no longer follow the stubbornness of their evil hearts.

**18** In those days the house of Judah will join the house of Israel, and together they will come from the land of the north to the land I gave your ancestors as an inheritance.

**19** I myself said: How I would set you among my sons,[^19] and give you a pleasant land, the most beautiful inheritance among the nations![^20] I thought you would call me "My father," and would not turn back from following me.

**20** But as a woman is unfaithful to her lover, so you have been unfaithful to me, house of Israel, declares the LORD.

**21** A voice is heard on the bare heights, the weeping and pleading of the Israelites, because they have twisted their way; they have forgotten the LORD their God.

**22** Return, faithless children; I will heal your faithlessness. "Here we are;[^21] we come to you, for you are the LORD our God.

**23** Surely the hills are a delusion, the uproar on the mountains.[^22] Surely in the LORD our God is the salvation of Israel.

**24** But the Shameful Thing[^23] has devoured what our ancestors worked for since our youth: their flocks and their herds, their sons and their daughters.

**25** Let us lie down in our shame, and let our disgrace cover us. For we have sinned against the LORD our God, we and our ancestors, from our youth until this day, and we have not listened to the voice of the LORD our God."

[^1]: The Hebrew begins with *lemor*, "saying", perhaps left from an introduction like the one in 2:1. The law in view is Deuteronomy 24:1–4, which forbids a man to take back a wife he has divorced after she has married another man.
[^2]: Literally "Would that land not be greatly polluted?"
[^3]: Or "yet return to me!"
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written with a coarser verb, *shagal*, "been ravished", but read "been lain with".
[^6]: Hebrew *aravi*, "an Arab", a desert nomad, who waits by the road for travelers.
[^7]: Hebrew *malqosh*, the "latter rain" of March and April, which the crops need before harvest.
[^8]: Literally "the forehead of a prostitute".
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "I have called" but read "you have called", and in verse 5 is written "I have spoken" but read "you have spoken". The written forms are probably an old spelling of "you" (compare 2:20, 33).
[^10]: Hebrew *meshuvah yisra'el*, "Turning-away Israel", as though it were her name. The northern kingdom of Israel had been destroyed by Assyria about a century before (2 Kings 17).
[^11]: Literally "from the lightness of her prostitution".
[^12]: That is, with idols of stone and wood (2:27).
[^13]: A wordplay on *shuv*, "turn, return": *shuvah meshuvah yisra'el*, "Turn back, Turning-away Israel". Verses 14 and 22 play on the same root.
[^14]: Literally "I will not make my face fall on you".
[^15]: Hebrew *chasid*, related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^16]: Literally "you have scattered your ways".
[^17]: Literally "Return, turning-away sons" (Hebrew *shuvu banim shovavim*). Also in verse 22.
[^18]: Or "your husband". Hebrew *ba'alti*, from *ba'al*, "lord, owner, husband", the same word as the name of the god Baal.
[^19]: Israel, pictured as a daughter, is given a son's share of the inheritance.
[^20]: Hebrew *nachalat tsevi tsiv'ot goyim*, literally "an inheritance of beauty, of the beauties of the nations".
[^21]: The people's answer, to the end of verse 25.
[^22]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Surely for a lie from the hills, the tumult of the mountains". The hills and mountains were places of worship of other gods (verse 6).
[^23]: Hebrew *ha-boshet*, "the shame", a scornful name for Baal (11:13).
