**1** The word of the LORD[^1] came to me:

**2** Go and proclaim in the hearing of Jerusalem:[^2] This is what the LORD says: I remember the faithful love[^3] of your youth, your love as a bride, how you followed me in the wilderness, in a land not sown.

**3** Israel was holy to the LORD, the firstfruits of his harvest.[^4] All who ate of it were held guilty; disaster came on them, declares the LORD.

**4** Hear the word of the LORD, house of Jacob, and all the clans of the house of Israel.

**5** This is what the LORD says: What wrong did your ancestors find in me, that they went far from me? They followed what is worthless and became worthless themselves.[^5]

**6** They did not say, "Where is the LORD, who brought us up from the land of Egypt, who led us through the wilderness, through a land of deserts and pits, through a land of drought and deep darkness,[^6] a land that no one travels through, where no human being lives?"

**7** I brought you into a fruitful land,[^7] to eat its fruit and its good things. But you came in and defiled my land, and made my inheritance detestable.

**8** The priests did not say, "Where is the LORD?" Those who handle the law did not know me. The shepherds[^8] rebelled against me. The prophets prophesied by Baal and followed things that do not profit.[^9]

**9** Therefore I will still bring charges against you,[^10] declares the LORD, and against your children's children I will bring charges.

**10** Cross over to the coasts of Kittim and look; send to Kedar and consider carefully;[^11] see if there has ever been anything like this.

**11** Has a nation ever exchanged its gods, even though they are not gods? But my people have exchanged their Glory[^12] for what does not profit.

**12** Be appalled at this, you heavens; shudder and be utterly desolate, declares the LORD.

**13** For my people have done two evils: they have abandoned me, the spring of living water,[^13] to dig out cisterns for themselves, cracked cisterns that cannot hold water.

**14** Is Israel a slave? Was he born a slave in the household?[^14] Then why has he become plunder?

**15** Young lions have roared over him; they have raised their voices. They have made his land a desolation; his cities are burned,[^15] with no one living in them.

**16** The people of Memphis and Tahpanhes[^16] have also shaved your head.[^17]

**17** Have you not brought this on yourself by abandoning the LORD your God, when he was leading you on the way?

**18** So now, what good is it for you to take the road to Egypt, to drink the waters of the Shihor?[^18] What good is it for you to take the road to Assyria, to drink the waters of the River?

**19** Your own evil will discipline you, and your faithlessness[^19] will rebuke you. Know and see how evil and bitter it is for you to abandon the LORD your God, and to have no fear of me,[^20] declares the Lord GOD of Armies.[^21]

**20** For long ago I broke your yoke[^22] and tore off your bonds, and you said, "I will not transgress."[^23] Yet on every high hill and under every leafy tree you lay down as a prostitute.

**21** I had planted you as a choice vine, wholly of true seed. How then have you turned against me into the wild shoots of a foreign vine?

**22** Even if you wash yourself with lye and use much soap,[^24] the stain of your guilt is still before me, declares the Lord GOD.[^25]

**23** How can you say, "I am not defiled; I have not gone after the Baals"?[^26] Look at your way in the valley;[^27] know what you have done, you swift young camel running here and there,[^28]

**24** a wild donkey used to the wilderness, sniffing the wind in her desire![^29] In her season of heat, who can turn her back? None who look for her need to tire themselves; in her month they will find her.

**25** Keep your feet from going bare and your throat from thirst![^30] But you said, "It's hopeless! No! For I love strangers,[^31] and I will go after them."

**26** As a thief is put to shame when he is caught, so the house of Israel is put to shame: they, their kings, their officials, their priests and their prophets.

**27** They say to a tree, "You are my father," and to a stone, "You gave birth to us."[^32] For they have turned their backs to me, not their faces. Yet in the time of their trouble they say, "Get up and save us!"

**28** But where are your gods that you made for yourself? Let them get up, if they can save you in the time of your trouble! For you have as many gods as you have cities, Judah.

**29** Why do you bring charges against me? All of you have rebelled against me, declares the LORD.

**30** In vain I have struck your children; they accepted no discipline. Your own sword has devoured your prophets like a ravaging lion.

**31** You people of this generation, consider the word of the LORD![^33] Have I been a wilderness to Israel, or a land of thick darkness? Why do my people say, "We roam free;[^34] we will not come to you again"?

**32** Does a young woman forget her jewelry, or a bride her sash? Yet my people have forgotten me for days without number.

**33** How well you set your course to seek love! So you have even taught your ways to wicked women.[^35]

**34** Even on your skirts[^36] is found the lifeblood of the innocent poor. You did not catch them breaking in.[^37] Yet in spite of all this[^38]

**35** you say, "I am innocent; surely his anger has turned away from me." Look, I will bring you to judgment because you say, "I have not sinned."

**36** How lightly you go about changing your way! You will be put to shame by Egypt, as you were put to shame by Assyria.

**37** From there too you will go out with your hands on your head,[^39] for the LORD has rejected those you trust, and you will not prosper with them.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Jerusalem, and in much of this chapter Israel, is addressed as a woman. In the oracles of this book, the words of the LORD and of the prophet are not put in quotation marks, because the speaker often changes without being marked. Quotation marks are kept for conversations, and for words quoted within an oracle.
[^3]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Here it is the bride's devotion.
[^4]: The firstfruits of a harvest belonged to the LORD, and anyone else who ate them was guilty (Leviticus 22:10–16).
[^5]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", a word for what is empty and worthless, often used of idols. "Became worthless" is the verb from the same word: literally "they went after vapor and became vapor".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death".
[^7]: Hebrew *erets ha-karmel*, "the land of the orchard, garden land".
[^8]: That is, the rulers.
[^9]: Hebrew *lo yo'ilu*, "what does not profit", perhaps a play on the sound of *Baal*. Also in verse 11.
[^10]: Hebrew *riv*, to bring a case against someone, as in a court of law. Also in verse 29.
[^11]: Kittim (Cyprus) lay to the west, and Kedar, the tribes of the Arabian desert, to the east: search the world from one side to the other.
[^12]: Literally "his glory", meaning the glorious God who was theirs (compare Psalm 106:20). Jewish scribal tradition counts this among the places where the scribes changed the wording out of reverence, and says that it once read "my glory".
[^13]: Fresh, flowing water from a spring, as opposed to water stored in a cistern.
[^14]: A slave born in the master's house belonged to him for life.
[^15]: Or "laid in ruins".
[^16]: Hebrew *Noph*: Memphis, the old capital of Lower Egypt. Tahpanhes was a fortress city in the eastern Nile delta (43:7–9). The Hebrew text is written "Tahpanes" but read "Tahpanhes".
[^17]: Literally "will graze the crown of your head". Or "have cracked your skull".
[^18]: A branch of the Nile in the eastern delta; here, the Nile. "The River" is the Euphrates.
[^19]: Hebrew *meshuvah*, "turning away", from *shuv*, "turn, return". Jeremiah plays on this word often, especially in chapter 3.
[^20]: Literally "and my dread is not on you".
[^21]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH tseva'ot*, "my Lord YHWH of Armies". *The LORD of Armies* is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^22]: Or "you broke your yoke". The Hebrew is pointed "I broke", referring to the LORD's freeing Israel from Egypt, but the ending can be an old form of "you" (compare verse 33), and the Septuagint reads "you broke".
[^23]: The Hebrew text is written "I will not serve" but read "I will not transgress".
[^24]: Hebrew *neter* (natron, a mineral used for washing) and *borit* (a soap made from plant ash).
[^25]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^26]: Local forms of the Canaanite god Baal.
[^27]: Probably the Valley of Ben-hinnom, outside Jerusalem, where children were sacrificed (7:31).
[^28]: Literally "crisscrossing her ways".
[^29]: The Hebrew text is written "his desire" but read "her desire".
[^30]: That is, don't wear out your sandals and parch your throat running after your lovers.
[^31]: That is, foreign gods.
[^32]: The tree and the stone are probably the wooden pole of Asherah and the standing stone of Canaanite worship. The Hebrew text is written "You gave birth to me" but read "You gave birth to us".
[^33]: The Hebrew is unusual. Literally "The generation, you, see the word of the LORD".
[^34]: Hebrew *radnu*, a rare verb, perhaps "we roam freely" or "we are free".
[^35]: Or "you have trained your ways in wickedness". The Hebrew text is written "I have taught" but read "you have taught". The written form may be an old spelling of "you have taught" (compare verse 20).
[^36]: Literally "on your wings", the edges of a robe.
[^37]: A householder who killed a burglar caught breaking in was not guilty of murder (Exodus 22:1). These victims had done nothing of the kind.
[^38]: The Hebrew of this line is obscure. Literally "for on all these".
[^39]: A gesture of grief and shame (2 Samuel 13:19).
