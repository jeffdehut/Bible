**1** When the priest Pashhur son of Immer, who was chief officer in the house of the LORD,[^1] heard Jeremiah prophesying these things,

**2** Pashhur struck Jeremiah the prophet and put him in the stocks[^2] at the Upper Gate of Benjamin in the house of the LORD.

**3** The next day, when Pashhur released Jeremiah from the stocks, Jeremiah said to him, "The LORD has not named you Pashhur, but Terror-on-Every-Side.[^3]

**4** For this is what the LORD says: I am going to make you a terror to yourself and to all your friends. They will fall by the sword of their enemies before your eyes. I will hand all Judah over to the king of Babylon,[^4] and he will carry them into exile to Babylon or strike them down with the sword.

**5** I will hand over to their enemies all the wealth of this city, all the fruit of its labor, all its valuables and all the treasures of the kings of Judah. They will plunder them, seize them and carry them off to Babylon.

**6** And you, Pashhur, and everyone who lives in your house will go into captivity. You will go to Babylon; there you will die, and there you will be buried, you and all your friends to whom you have prophesied lies."

**7** You deceived me,[^5] LORD, and I was deceived; you overpowered me, and you prevailed. I have become a laughingstock all day long; everyone mocks me.

**8** For whenever I speak, I cry out; I shout, "Violence and destruction!" For the word of the LORD has brought me insult and scorn all day long.

**9** If I say, "I will not mention him or speak any more in his name," then it is in my heart like a burning fire, shut up in my bones. I am worn out holding it in, and I cannot.

**10** For I hear many whispering, "Terror on every side! Denounce him! Let us denounce him!" All my close friends[^6] are watching for me to stumble: "Perhaps he will be deceived, and then we will prevail over him and take our revenge on him."

**11** But the LORD is with me like a mighty warrior. So my persecutors will stumble and not prevail. They will be utterly put to shame, for they will not succeed. Their disgrace will never be forgotten.

**12** LORD of Armies,[^7] you who test the righteous, who see the heart and mind,[^8] let me see your vengeance on them, for to you I have committed my cause.

**13** Sing to the LORD! Praise the LORD! For he has rescued the life of the needy from the hand of evildoers.

**14** Cursed be the day I was born![^9] May the day my mother gave birth to me not be blessed!

**15** Cursed be the man who brought the news to my father, "A son is born to you, a boy!" and made him very glad.

**16** May that man be like the cities that the LORD overthrew without relenting.[^10] May he hear a cry in the morning and a battle shout at noon,

**17** because he did not kill me in the womb, so that my mother would have been my grave, her womb pregnant forever.

**18** Why did I ever come out of the womb to see trouble and sorrow, and to end my days in shame?

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. "The house of the LORD" is the temple. Pashhur's office was to keep order in the temple (compare 29:26).
[^2]: Hebrew *mahpekhet*, from a root meaning "to turn", perhaps a frame that held the body twisted in a painful position. Also in verse 3.
[^3]: Hebrew *Magor-missaviv*, the phrase Jeremiah uses in 6:25 and in verse 10.
[^4]: This is the first time Babylon is named in the book.
[^5]: Or "You enticed me". Hebrew *pittitani*, a verb used of seducing a young woman (Exodus 22:15, 22:16 in most English Bibles) and of luring someone into a trap (1 Kings 22:20–22). It is used again in verse 10. Verses 7–18 are the last of Jeremiah's personal laments (see 11:18).
[^6]: Literally "Every man of my peace".
[^7]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. This verse repeats 11:20 with small differences.
[^8]: Literally "the kidneys and the heart". In Hebrew thought the kidneys were the seat of the emotions and the heart of thought and will.
[^9]: Compare Job 3:1–19, which curses the day of birth in similar words.
[^10]: Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:24–25).
