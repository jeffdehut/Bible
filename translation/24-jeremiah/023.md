**1** Woe to the shepherds[^1] who destroy and scatter the sheep of my pasture! declares the LORD.[^2]

**2** Therefore this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says about the shepherds who shepherd my people: You have scattered my flock and driven them away, and you have not cared for them. So I am going to deal with you[^3] for the evil of your deeds, declares the LORD.

**3** I myself will gather the remnant of my flock from all the lands where I have driven them, and I will bring them back to their pasture, and they will be fruitful and multiply.

**4** I will raise up shepherds over them who will shepherd them, and they will no longer be afraid or dismayed, and none will be missing, declares the LORD.

**5** Look, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will raise up for David a righteous Branch.[^4] He will reign as king and act wisely, and he will do justice and righteousness in the land.

**6** In his days Judah will be saved, and Israel will live in safety. And this is the name by which he will be called: The LORD Is Our Righteousness.[^5]

**7** Therefore, look, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when people will no longer say, "As surely as the LORD lives, who brought the Israelites up out of the land of Egypt,"[^6]

**8** but, "As surely as the LORD lives, who brought up and led the descendants of the house of Israel out of the land of the north and out of all the lands where I had driven them."[^7] Then they will live on their own land.

**9** Concerning the prophets: My heart is broken within me; all my bones tremble. I am like a drunk man, like a man overcome by wine, because of the LORD and because of his holy words.

**10** For the land is full of adulterers; because of the curse the land mourns, and the pastures of the wilderness are dried up. Their course is evil, and their might is not right.

**11** Both prophet and priest are godless; even in my house I have found their wickedness, declares the LORD.

**12** Therefore their way will be to them like slippery paths in the darkness; they will be driven on and fall there. For I will bring disaster on them, the year of their punishment, declares the LORD.

**13** Among the prophets of Samaria I saw something offensive: they prophesied by Baal and led my people Israel astray.

**14** But among the prophets of Jerusalem I have seen something horrible: they commit adultery and walk in lies. They strengthen the hands of evildoers, so that no one turns from his wickedness. All of them have become like Sodom to me, and its inhabitants like Gomorrah.

**15** Therefore this is what the LORD of Armies[^8] says about the prophets: I am going to feed them wormwood[^9] and make them drink poisoned water, for from the prophets of Jerusalem godlessness has spread throughout the land.

**16** This is what the LORD of Armies says: Do not listen to the words of the prophets who prophesy to you. They are filling you with empty hopes.[^10] They speak visions from their own minds, not from the mouth of the LORD.

**17** They keep saying to those who despise me,[^11] "The LORD has said, 'You will have peace.'" And to everyone who follows the stubbornness of his heart they say, "No disaster will come on you."

**18** But which of them has stood in the council of the LORD, to see and hear his word? Who has paid attention to his word[^12] and listened?

**19** Look, the storm of the LORD! Wrath has gone out, a whirling tempest; it will whirl down on the heads of the wicked.

**20** The anger of the LORD will not turn back until he has done and carried out the purposes of his heart. In the days to come you will understand it clearly.[^13]

**21** I did not send these prophets, yet they ran. I did not speak to them, yet they prophesied.

**22** But if they had stood in my council, they would have proclaimed my words to my people and turned them back from their evil way and from the evil of their deeds.

**23** Am I a God who is near, declares the LORD, and not a God far off?

**24** Can anyone hide in secret places so that I cannot see him? declares the LORD. Do I not fill heaven and earth? declares the LORD.

**25** I have heard what the prophets say who prophesy lies in my name: "I had a dream! I had a dream!"

**26** How long? Is there anything in the minds[^14] of the prophets who prophesy lies, the prophets of the deceit of their own minds?

**27** They plan to make my people forget my name by the dreams they tell one another, just as their ancestors forgot my name for Baal.

**28** Let the prophet who has a dream tell his dream, but let the one who has my word speak my word faithfully. What does straw have to do with grain? declares the LORD.

**29** Is not my word like fire, declares the LORD, and like a hammer that shatters rock?

**30** Therefore I am against the prophets, declares the LORD, who steal my words from one another.

**31** I am against the prophets, declares the LORD, who use their own tongues and declare, "He declares."[^15]

**32** I am against those who prophesy lying dreams, declares the LORD, who tell them and lead my people astray with their lies and their recklessness. I did not send them or command them, and they do this people no good at all, declares the LORD.

**33** When this people, or a prophet or a priest, asks you, "What is the burden[^16] of the LORD?" say to them, "What burden?[^17] I will cast you off, declares the LORD."

**34** And if a prophet or a priest or anyone else says, "The burden of the LORD," I will punish that man and his household.

**35** This is what each of you is to say to his neighbor and to his brother: "What has the LORD answered?" or "What has the LORD spoken?"

**36** But you must no longer mention "the burden of the LORD," for each man's own word will become his burden, and you twist the words of the living God, the LORD of Armies, our God.

**37** This is what you are to say to the prophet: "What has the LORD answered you?" or "What has the LORD spoken?"

**38** But if you say, "The burden of the LORD," then this is what the LORD says: Because you have used these words, "The burden of the LORD," though I sent word to you, "You must not say, 'The burden of the LORD,'"

**39** therefore I will surely forget you[^18] and cast you away from my presence, you and the city I gave to you and your ancestors.

**40** I will bring on you everlasting disgrace and everlasting shame that will never be forgotten.
[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*: see 22:13. The shepherds are the kings of Judah (22:22).
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: A wordplay on *paqad*, "attend to, care for, punish": "you have not attended to them, so I will attend to you". In verse 4, *none will be missing* is from the same verb.
[^4]: Hebrew *tsemach*, "sprout, branch", a new shoot from the stump of David's family tree. It became a title for the promised king of David's line (33:15; Zechariah 3:8; 6:12), and later Jewish and Christian readers heard the Messiah here.
[^5]: Hebrew *YHWH tsidqenu*, perhaps a play on the name of Zedekiah, *Tsidqiyyahu*, "The LORD is my righteousness", the last king of Judah. Verses 5–6 are repeated, with differences, in 33:15–16.
[^6]: The Septuagint places verses 7–8 after verse 40. They repeat 16:14–15 with small differences.
[^7]: The Hebrew has "I", as though the LORD were speaking. 16:15 has "he".
[^8]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 16 and 36.
[^9]: Hebrew *la'anah*, a plant with a very bitter taste (9:14; 9:15 in most English Bibles).
[^10]: Hebrew *mahbilim*, from *hevel*, "breath, vapor" (2:5): "they make you empty".
[^11]: The same consonants can be read "to those who despise the word of the LORD", as the Septuagint reads.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "my word" but read "his word".
[^13]: Verses 19–20 are repeated, with small differences, in 30:23–24.
[^14]: Literally "in the heart". The Hebrew of this verse is difficult.
[^15]: Literally "and declare a declaration", using the word *ne'um* of the formula "declares the LORD" (see 1:8).
[^16]: Hebrew *massa*, a word that means both "oracle", a message from the LORD, and "burden", a load to be carried (17:21). In verses 33–40 Jeremiah plays on both senses, and "the burden of the LORD" seems to have become a mocking catchword for his prophecies.
[^17]: Hebrew *et-mah-massa*. The Septuagint and the Vulgate divide the consonants differently, *attem ha-massa*, "You are the burden".
[^18]: Hebrew *nashiti … nasho*, "I will surely forget". The Vulgate reads "I will lift you up", as though from *nasa*, "lift, carry", continuing the play on *massa*, "burden".
