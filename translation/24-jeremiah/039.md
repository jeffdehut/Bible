**1** In the ninth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, in the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came against Jerusalem with his whole army and besieged it.[^1]

**2** In the eleventh year of Zedekiah, in the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month,[^2] the city wall was broken through.

**3** Then all the officials of the king of Babylon came in and sat in the Middle Gate: Nergal-sharezer, Samgar-nebo, Sarsechim the Rab-saris, Nergal-sharezer the Rab-mag, and all the rest of the officials of the king of Babylon.[^3]

**4** When Zedekiah king of Judah and all the soldiers saw them, they fled.[^4] They went out of the city by night by way of the king's garden, through the gate between the two walls, and he went off in the direction of the Arabah.[^5]

**5** But the Babylonian[^6] army pursued them and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho. They captured him and brought him up to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath,[^7] and he passed sentence on him.

**6** The king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah's sons at Riblah before his eyes, and the king of Babylon slaughtered all the nobles of Judah.

**7** Then he put out Zedekiah's eyes and bound him in bronze shackles to take him to Babylon.

**8** The Babylonians burned the king's palace and the houses of the people with fire,[^8] and they tore down the walls of Jerusalem.

**9** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard[^9] deported to Babylon the rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to him, and the rest of the people who were left.

**10** But Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard left in the land of Judah some of the poor people, who owned nothing, and at that time he gave them vineyards and fields.

**11** Now Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had given orders about Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard:

**12** "Take him and look after him. Do him no harm, but do for him whatever he tells you."

**13** So Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard, Nebushazban[^10] the Rab-saris, Nergal-sharezer the Rab-mag and all the chief officers of the king of Babylon sent

**14** and had Jeremiah taken from the courtyard of the guard. They entrusted him to Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan, to take him home. So he stayed among the people.[^11]

**15** While Jeremiah was still confined in the courtyard of the guard, the word of the LORD[^12] had come to him:

**16** "Go and say to Ebed-melech the Cushite: 'This is what the LORD of Armies,[^13] the God of Israel, says: I am going to bring my words against this city for harm and not for good, and they will take place before your eyes on that day.

**17** But I will rescue you on that day, declares the LORD, and you will not be given into the hand of the men you fear.

**18** For I will surely save you, and you will not fall by the sword. You will escape with your life,[^14] because you have trusted in me, declares the LORD.'"

[^1]: In January 588 BC. Verses 1–2 and 4–10 are also told, more fully, in 52:4–16 and 2 Kings 25:1–12.
[^2]: In July 587 or 586 BC.
[^3]: The names and titles are uncertain, and the Hebrew may be divided differently. *Nergal-sharezer* is the Babylonian name Nergal-shar-usur; a man of that name later became king of Babylon (560–556 BC). *Samgar* may be a Babylonian title, and *Sarsechim* a name. *Rab-saris* means "chief official" (2 Kings 18:17), and *Rab-mag* is another high title, of uncertain meaning. Verse 3 completes the sentence begun in 38:28.
[^4]: The Septuagint does not have verses 4–13.
[^5]: The Jordan Valley.
[^6]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verse 8.
[^7]: Riblah was on the Orontes River in Syria, about 200 miles (320 km) north of Jerusalem, where Nebuchadnezzar had his headquarters.
[^8]: Literally "the house of the people". 52:13 and 2 Kings 25:9 read "and all the houses of Jerusalem".
[^9]: Hebrew *rav-tabbachim*, literally "chief of the butchers" or "chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal bodyguard. Also in verses 10–11 and 13.
[^10]: In the Hebrew text one letter of this name is written small, a scribal mark.
[^11]: 40:1–6 tells of Jeremiah's release in a different way: he was taken in chains with the other captives as far as Ramah, where Nebuzaradan set him free.
[^12]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^13]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^14]: Literally "your life will be to you as plunder" (21:9).
