**1** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king,[^1] and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal[^2] daughter of Jeremiah, from Libnah.

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh,[^3] just as Jehoiakim had done.[^4]

**3** For it was because of Yahweh's anger that all this happened to Jerusalem and Judah, until he cast them out of his presence. And Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

**4** So in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month,[^5] Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came against Jerusalem with his whole army. They camped against it, and they built siege works all around it.

**5** The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**6** In the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month,[^6] the famine in the city was so severe that there was no food for the people of the land.

**7** Then the city wall was broken through, and all the soldiers fled and went out of the city by night by way of the gate between the two walls, near the king's garden, though the Babylonians[^7] were all around the city. They went off in the direction of the Arabah.[^8]

**8** But the Babylonian army pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**9** They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath,[^9] and he passed sentence on him.

**10** The king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah's sons before his eyes, and he also slaughtered all the officials of Judah at Riblah.[^10]

**11** Then he put out Zedekiah's eyes and bound him in bronze shackles, and the king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in prison[^11] until the day of his death.

**12** In the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month,[^12] which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard,[^13] who served the king of Babylon,[^14] came to Jerusalem.

**13** He burned the house of Yahweh,[^15] the king's palace and all the houses of Jerusalem. Every great house he burned with fire.

**14** The whole Babylonian army that was with the commander of the guard tore down all the walls around Jerusalem.

**15** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard deported some of the poorest of the people,[^16] the rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the craftsmen.[^17]

**16** But Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard left some of the poorest people of the land to work the vineyards and the fields.

**17** The Babylonians broke up the bronze pillars that belonged to the house of Yahweh, and the stands and the bronze Sea that were in the house of Yahweh, and they carried all the bronze to Babylon.

**18** They also took away the pots, the shovels, the wick trimmers, the sprinkling bowls, the dishes and all the bronze articles used in the temple service.

**19** The commander of the guard took away the basins, the firepans, the sprinkling bowls, the pots, the lampstands, the dishes and the bowls for drink offerings,[^18] whatever was made of gold and whatever was made of silver.

**20** As for the two pillars, the one Sea, and the twelve bronze bulls that were under the stands,[^19] which King Solomon had made for the house of Yahweh, the bronze of all these articles was beyond weighing.

**21** As for the pillars, each pillar was eighteen cubits[^20] high, and a cord of twelve cubits went around it. It was four fingers thick,[^21] and hollow.

**22** The bronze capital on top of it was five cubits high,[^22] with a network and pomegranates all around the capital, all of bronze. The second pillar, with its pomegranates, was the same.

**23** There were ninety-six pomegranates on the sides;[^23] all the pomegranates around the network numbered a hundred.

**24** The commander of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest and the three doorkeepers.[^24]

**25** From the city he took an official who was in charge of the soldiers, and seven of the king's advisers[^25] who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army,[^26] who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.

**26** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**27** The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah went into exile from its land.

**28** These are the people whom Nebuchadnezzar deported:[^27] in the seventh year, 3,023 Judeans;[^28]

**29** in the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, 832 people from Jerusalem;[^29]

**30** in the twenty-third year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard deported 745 Judeans.[^30] In all there were 4,600 people.

**31** In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fifth day of the month,[^31] Evil-merodach[^32] king of Babylon, in the year he became king, showed favor to Jehoiachin king of Judah and brought him out of prison.[^33]

**32** He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.

**33** So Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes, and for the rest of his life he regularly ate at the king's table.

**34** As for his allowance, a regular allowance was given him by the king of Babylon, a portion for each day, until the day of his death, for as long as he lived.[^34]

[^1]: This chapter is also found, with small differences, in 2 Kings 24:18–25:30, except for verses 28–30, which only Jeremiah has. Verses 4–16 are also told, more briefly, in 39:1–10. The differences that change the sense are given in the notes.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "Hamital" but read "Hamutal". Her father is not Jeremiah the prophet.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: The Septuagint does not have verses 2–3.
[^5]: In January 588 BC.
[^6]: In July 587 or 586 BC. 2 Kings 25:3 does not have "In the fourth month".
[^7]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verses 8, 14 and 17.
[^8]: The Jordan Valley. 2 Kings 25:4 has no verb for the soldiers, and reads "he went off", meaning the king.
[^9]: Riblah was on the Orontes River in Syria, where Nebuchadnezzar had his headquarters. 2 Kings 25:6 does not have "in the land of Hamath", and reads "they passed sentence on him".
[^10]: 2 Kings 25:7 does not mention the killing of the officials.
[^11]: Literally "the house of the punishments". 2 Kings 25:7 does not mention his imprisonment or his death.
[^12]: In the summer of 587 or 586 BC. 2 Kings 25:8 reads "on the seventh day of the month".
[^13]: Hebrew *rav-tabbachim*, literally "chief of the butchers" or "chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal bodyguard. Also in verses 14–16, 19, 24, 26 and 30.
[^14]: Literally "who stood before the king of Babylon". 2 Kings 25:8 reads "an officer of the king of Babylon".
[^15]: "The house of Yahweh" is the temple.
[^16]: These words are not in 2 Kings 25:11, and they seem to conflict with verse 16. The Septuagint does not have verse 15.
[^17]: Hebrew *ha-amon*, perhaps "the craftsmen". 2 Kings 25:11 reads *he-hamon*, "the population".
[^18]: 2 Kings 25:15 names only the firepans and the sprinkling bowls.
[^19]: 2 Kings 25:16 does not mention the bulls. According to 1 Kings 7:25 the twelve bulls stood under the Sea, not under the stands, and King Ahaz had already taken the Sea down from them (2 Kings 16:17).
[^20]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm). Eighteen cubits was about 27 feet (8.1 meters), and twelve cubits about 18 feet (5.4 meters). Verses 21–23 are not in 2 Kings 25, which gives only the height of the pillars and their capitals. Compare 1 Kings 7:15–22.
[^21]: About 3 inches (7.5 cm).
[^22]: About 7½ feet (2.3 meters), as in 1 Kings 7:16. 2 Kings 25:17 reads "three cubits".
[^23]: Hebrew *ruchah*, "toward the wind", perhaps "on the outside" or "exposed to view". The meaning is uncertain.
[^24]: Literally "the keepers of the threshold".
[^25]: Literally "of those who saw the king's face". 2 Kings 25:19 reads "five".
[^26]: Or "the secretary, the commander of the army".
[^27]: Verses 28–30 are found only here, and the Septuagint does not have them.
[^28]: In 597 BC. 2 Kings 24:12 dates this deportation to Nebuchadnezzar's eighth year, and 2 Kings 24:14–16 gives larger numbers. Hebrew *yehudim*, the people of Judah. Also in verse 30.
[^29]: In 587 or 586 BC, when Jerusalem fell. Verse 12 calls that year Nebuchadnezzar's nineteenth.
[^30]: In 582 or 581 BC. This deportation is not mentioned anywhere else.
[^31]: In 561 BC. 2 Kings 25:27 reads "on the twenty-seventh day of the month".
[^32]: Amel-Marduk, son of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon from 562 to 560 BC.
[^33]: Literally "lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah, and brought him out of the prison house" (compare Genesis 40:13, 20). 2 Kings 25:27 does not have "and brought him out".
[^34]: Literally "all the days of his life". 2 Kings 25:30 reads "by the king", and does not have "until the day of his death".
