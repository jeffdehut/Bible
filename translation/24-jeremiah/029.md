**1** These are the words of the letter that Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem to the surviving elders of the exiles,[^1] to the priests, to the prophets and to all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had deported from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**2** This was after King Jeconiah,[^2] the queen mother,[^3] the court officials,[^4] the officials of Judah and Jerusalem, the craftsmen and the metalworkers[^5] had left Jerusalem.

**3** He sent it by Elasah son of Shaphan and Gemariah son of Hilkiah, whom Zedekiah king of Judah sent to Babylon, to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon. It said:

**4** This is what the LORD of Armies,[^6] the God of Israel, says to all the exiles whom I deported from Jerusalem to Babylon:

**5** Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat their produce.

**6** Take wives and have sons and daughters. Take wives for your sons and give your daughters to husbands, so that they may bear sons and daughters. Multiply there; do not decrease.

**7** Seek the well-being[^7] of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the LORD for it, for in its well-being you will have well-being.

**8** For this is what the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says: Do not let your prophets who are among you and your diviners deceive you, and do not listen to the dreams that you dream.[^8]

**9** For they are prophesying lies to you in my name. I did not send them, declares the LORD.

**10** For this is what the LORD says: When seventy years are completed for Babylon,[^9] I will come to you,[^10] and I will fulfill for you my good promise to bring you back to this place.

**11** For I know the plans I have for you, declares the LORD, plans for well-being and not for disaster, to give you a future and a hope.[^11]

**12** Then you will call on me and come and pray to me, and I will listen to you.

**13** You will seek me and find me, when you search for me with all your heart.

**14** I will let myself be found by you, declares the LORD. I will restore your fortunes[^12] and gather you from all the nations and from all the places where I have driven you, declares the LORD, and I will bring you back to the place from which I sent you into exile.

**15** Because you have said, "The LORD has raised up prophets for us in Babylon,"[^13]

**16** this is what the LORD says about the king who sits on David's throne, and about all the people who live in this city, your relatives who did not go out with you into exile:[^14]

**17** This is what the LORD of Armies says: I am going to send against them the sword, famine and plague, and I will make them like rotten[^15] figs that are too bad to be eaten.

**18** I will pursue them with the sword, famine and plague, and I will make them a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth, and a curse, a horror,[^16] a thing to hiss at and a disgrace among all the nations where I have driven them,

**19** because they did not listen to my words, declares the LORD, which I sent to them through my servants the prophets, sending them again and again.[^17] But you did not listen, declares the LORD.

**20** So you, hear the word of the LORD, all you exiles whom I sent from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**21** This is what the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says about Ahab son of Kolaiah and about Zedekiah son of Maaseiah, who are prophesying lies to you in my name: I am going to give them into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar[^18] king of Babylon, and he will strike them down before your eyes.

**22** Because of them all the exiles of Judah who are in Babylon will use this curse:[^19] "May the LORD make you like Zedekiah and Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire,"

**23** because they have done an outrageous thing in Israel. They have committed adultery with their neighbors' wives, and they have spoken lies in my name, words I did not command them. I am the one who knows, and I am a witness, declares the LORD.

**24** To Shemaiah the Nehelamite[^20] you are to say:

**25** This is what the LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, says: Because you sent letters in your own name to all the people in Jerusalem, to Zephaniah son of Maaseiah the priest and to all the priests, saying,[^21]

**26** "The LORD has made you priest in place of Jehoiada the priest, to have overseers in the house of the LORD for every madman who prophesies, to put him in the stocks and the collar.[^22]

**27** So now, why have you not rebuked Jeremiah of Anathoth, who is prophesying to you?

**28** For he has sent word to us in Babylon, saying, 'It will be a long time. Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat their produce.'"

**29** Zephaniah the priest read this letter in the hearing of Jeremiah the prophet.

**30** Then the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah:

**31** Send to all the exiles: This is what the LORD says about Shemaiah the Nehelamite: Because Shemaiah has prophesied to you, though I did not send him, and has made you trust in a lie,

**32** therefore this is what the LORD says: I am going to punish Shemaiah the Nehelamite and his descendants. He will have no one left living among this people, and he will not see the good that I am going to do for my people, declares the LORD, for he has preached rebellion against the LORD.[^23]

[^1]: Literally "to the rest of the elders of the exile".
[^2]: That is, Jehoiachin, taken into exile with his court in 597 BC (2 Kings 24:8–16).
[^3]: Hebrew *gevirah*, "great lady", the king's mother, Nehushta (2 Kings 24:8, 15; Jeremiah 13:18).
[^4]: Hebrew *sarisim*, court officials. The word can also mean eunuchs.
[^5]: Hebrew *masger*, perhaps "smith" or "locksmith" (2 Kings 24:14).
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 8, 17, 21 and 25.
[^7]: Hebrew *shalom*, "peace, well-being", three times in this verse. Also in verse 11.
[^8]: Literally "your dreams that you cause to be dreamed". The Hebrew form is unusual.
[^9]: Compare 25:11–12. Daniel 9:2 and 2 Chronicles 36:21 refer back to this prophecy.
[^10]: Hebrew *paqad*, "visit, attend to". The same verb often means "punish" (verse 32).
[^11]: Literally "an outcome and a hope".
[^12]: Literally "turn your turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back your captives".
[^13]: Verse 15 leads into verses 21–23, which deal with the prophets in Babylon. The Septuagint does not have verses 16–20.
[^14]: The king is Zedekiah, and the city Jerusalem. Verses 16–19 take up the picture of the figs in chapter 24.
[^15]: Hebrew *sho'arim*, a rare word, perhaps "split open" or "disgusting".
[^16]: Two different Hebrew words are translated "horror" in this verse, *za'avah* and *shammah*. Hissing or whistling was a sound of shock or scorn.
[^17]: Literally "rising early and sending".
[^18]: Hebrew *Nevukhadre'tsar*: see 21:2. Verses 1 and 3 have *Nevukhadne'tsar*, as in chapters 27–28.
[^19]: Literally "From them a curse will be taken by all the exiles of Judah". A wordplay: *qelalah*, "curse", sounds like *qalam*, "roasted them".
[^20]: Perhaps "from Nehelam", a place otherwise unknown. The name sounds like *chalam*, "to dream" (verse 8).
[^21]: Shemaiah's letter is quoted in verses 26–28. The sentence that begins with "Because" is not completed; the sentence against Shemaiah is given in verses 31–32.
[^22]: Hebrew *mahpekhet*, "stocks" (20:2), and *tsinoq*, a rare word, perhaps an iron collar.
[^23]: The same words are used of Hananiah in 28:16. Compare Deuteronomy 13:6 (13:5 in most English Bibles).
