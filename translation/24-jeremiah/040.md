**1** The word that came to Jeremiah from Yahweh,[^1] after Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard[^2] had released him at Ramah. He had taken him there in chains, among all the exiles of Jerusalem and Judah who were being taken into exile to Babylon.[^3]

**2** The commander of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, "Yahweh your God pronounced this disaster against this place.

**3** Now Yahweh has brought it about; he has done as he said. Because you people sinned against Yahweh and did not obey his voice, this has happened to you.

**4** But now, look, today I am freeing you from the chains on your hands. If you wish to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you. But if you do not wish to come with me to Babylon, then don't. See, the whole land is before you; go wherever you think good and right."

**5** When Jeremiah still had not turned to go, he said,[^4] "Go back to Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan, whom the king of Babylon has appointed over the cities of Judah, and stay with him among the people, or go wherever you think right." Then the commander of the guard gave him provisions and a gift, and let him go.

**6** So Jeremiah went to Gedaliah son of Ahikam at Mizpah[^5] and stayed with him among the people who were left in the land.

**7** All the commanders of the armed forces who were in the open country, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam over the land, and that he had put in his charge men, women and children, those of the poorest of the land who had not been deported to Babylon.[^6]

**8** So they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan and Jonathan the sons of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth, the sons of Ephai[^7] the Netophathite, and Jezaniah son of the Maacathite, they and their men.

**9** Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan swore an oath to them and their men, and he said, "Do not be afraid to serve the Babylonians.[^8] Live in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you.

**10** As for me, I will stay at Mizpah to represent you before the Babylonians who come to us.[^9] But you, gather wine, summer fruit and oil, store them in your jars, and live in the towns you have taken over."

**11** Likewise all the Judeans[^10] who were in Moab, among the Ammonites, in Edom and in all the other lands heard that the king of Babylon had left a remnant in Judah, and that he had appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan over them.

**12** Then all the Judeans returned from all the places where they had been scattered and came to the land of Judah, to Gedaliah at Mizpah. And they gathered wine and summer fruit in great abundance.

**13** Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the armed forces who were in the open country came to Gedaliah at Mizpah

**14** and said to him, "Do you know that Baalis king of the Ammonites has sent Ishmael son of Nethaniah to take your life?" But Gedaliah son of Ahikam did not believe them.

**15** Then Johanan son of Kareah said to Gedaliah in secret at Mizpah, "Let me go and kill Ishmael son of Nethaniah, and no one will know. Why should he take your life, so that all the Judeans who have gathered around you would be scattered and the remnant of Judah would perish?"

**16** But Gedaliah son of Ahikam said to Johanan son of Kareah, "Do not do this thing, for what you are saying about Ishmael is a lie."

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. No word of Yahweh to Jeremiah follows at once. The heading may introduce the whole account of chapters 40–44, in which such a word comes in 42:7.
[^2]: Hebrew *rav-tabbachim*, literally "chief of the butchers" or "chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal bodyguard. Also in verses 2 and 5.
[^3]: Ramah was about 5 miles (8 km) north of Jerusalem (31:15). Compare 39:11–14.
[^4]: The Hebrew of this sentence is difficult. Literally "And he still would not turn back, 'And turn back to Gedaliah …'". "He said" is supplied.
[^5]: A town about 8 miles (13 km) north of Jerusalem, where the Babylonians now set their governor.
[^6]: Verses 7–9 are also told, more briefly, in 2 Kings 25:22–24, and 40:7–41:18 is summarized in 2 Kings 25:22–26.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "Ophai" but read "Ephai". 2 Kings 25:23 has "Johanan son of Kareah", "Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite" and "Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite". Netophah was a town near Bethlehem.
[^8]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verse 10. 2 Kings 25:24 reads "Do not be afraid of the Babylonian officials".
[^9]: Literally "to stand before the Babylonians who come to us".
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-yehudim*, the people of Judah. Also in verses 12 and 15.
