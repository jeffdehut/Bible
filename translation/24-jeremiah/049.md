**1** Concerning the Ammonites. This is what the LORD[^1] says: Has Israel no sons? Has he no heir? Why then has Milcom[^2] taken possession of Gad, and why do his people live in its cities?

**2** Therefore, look, the days are coming, declares the LORD, when I will make the battle cry heard against Rabbah of the Ammonites.[^3] It will become a desolate mound, and its villages[^4] will be burned with fire. Then Israel will take possession of those who took possession of him, says the LORD.

**3** Wail, Heshbon, for Ai is devastated![^5] Cry out, daughters of Rabbah! Put on sackcloth and mourn; run back and forth among the walls,[^6] for Milcom will go into exile, together with his priests and his officials.

**4** Why do you boast of your valleys, your valley overflowing,[^7] faithless daughter, who trusted in her treasures, saying, "Who will come against me?"

**5** I am going to bring terror on you, declares the Lord GOD of Armies,[^8] from all who are around you. You will be driven away, each one headlong, with no one to gather the fugitives.

**6** But afterward I will restore the fortunes[^9] of the Ammonites, declares the LORD.

**7** Concerning Edom. This is what the LORD of Armies says: Is there no longer wisdom in Teman?[^10] Has counsel perished from the prudent? Has their wisdom gone bad?

**8** Flee, turn back, hide deep down,[^11] you who live in Dedan! For I will bring disaster on Esau, the time when I punish him.

**9** If grape pickers came to you, they would leave no gleanings. If thieves came by night, they would destroy all they wanted.[^12]

**10** But I have stripped Esau bare; I have uncovered his hiding places, and he cannot hide himself. His offspring are destroyed, his brothers and his neighbors, and he is no more.

**11** Leave your orphans; I will keep them alive. Let your widows trust in me.

**12** For this is what the LORD says: If those who did not deserve to drink the cup must drink it,[^13] will you go unpunished? You will not go unpunished, for you will surely drink it.

**13** For I have sworn by myself, declares the LORD, that Bozrah[^14] will become a horror, a disgrace, a ruin and a curse, and all her cities will be ruins forever.

**14** I have heard a report from the LORD; an envoy has been sent among the nations: "Gather together and come against her; rise up for battle!"

**15** For look, I will make you small among the nations, despised among human beings.

**16** The terror you inspire[^15] and the pride of your heart have deceived you, you who live in the clefts of the rock,[^16] who hold the height of the hill. Though you build your nest as high as the eagle's, from there I will bring you down, declares the LORD.

**17** Edom will become a horror; everyone who passes by it will be appalled and hiss at all its wounds.

**18** As when Sodom and Gomorrah and their neighbors were overthrown, says the LORD, no one will live there; no human being will stay in it.[^17]

**19** Look, like a lion coming up from the thickets of the Jordan[^18] to a pasture that is always green,[^19] I will suddenly chase them from it, and I will appoint over it whoever is chosen.[^20] For who is like me? Who can summon me? What shepherd can stand before me?

**20** Therefore hear the plan that the LORD has made against Edom, and the purposes he has formed against the inhabitants of Teman: Surely the little ones of the flock will be dragged away; surely their pasture will be made desolate because of them.

**21** At the sound of their fall the earth quakes; their cry is heard at the Red Sea.[^21]

**22** Look, like an eagle he will rise and swoop down and spread his wings over Bozrah. On that day the hearts of Edom's warriors will be like the heart of a woman in labor.

**23** Concerning Damascus. Hamath and Arpad[^22] are put to shame, for they have heard bad news. They melt in fear; there is anxiety like the sea,[^23] which cannot be calm.

**24** Damascus has grown weak; she has turned to flee, and panic has seized her. Distress and pangs have gripped her like a woman in labor.

**25** How is it that the city of praise has not been abandoned, the town of my joy?[^24]

**26** Therefore her young men will fall in her squares, and all her soldiers will be silenced on that day, declares the LORD of Armies.[^25]

**27** I will set fire to the wall of Damascus, and it will consume the fortresses of Ben-hadad.[^26]

**28** Concerning Kedar and the kingdoms of Hazor, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck down.[^27] This is what the LORD says: Rise up, go up against Kedar, and destroy the people of the east.

**29** Their tents and their flocks will be taken, their tent curtains and all their goods; their camels will be carried off from them. People will shout to them, "Terror on every side!"

**30** Flee, wander far away, hide deep down, you who live in Hazor, declares the LORD. For Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon has made a plan against you; he has formed a purpose against you.[^28]

**31** Rise up, go up against a nation at ease, that lives in security, declares the LORD, that has no gates or bars; they live alone.

**32** Their camels will become plunder, and their great herds spoil. I will scatter to every wind those who clip the hair at their temples,[^29] and I will bring disaster on them from every side, declares the LORD.

**33** Hazor will become a haunt of jackals, a desolation forever. No one will live there; no human being will stay in it.

**34** The word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning Elam,[^30] at the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah king of Judah:

**35** This is what the LORD of Armies says: I am going to break the bow of Elam, the mainstay of their might.[^31]

**36** I will bring against Elam the four winds from the four ends of the heavens, and I will scatter them to all these winds. There will be no nation to which the outcasts of Elam[^32] will not come.

**37** I will shatter Elam before their enemies, before those who seek their lives. I will bring disaster on them, my fierce anger, declares the LORD. I will send the sword after them until I have made an end of them.

**38** I will set my throne in Elam and destroy from there its king and officials, declares the LORD.

**39** But in the days to come[^33] I will restore the fortunes of Elam, declares the LORD.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: The Hebrew is pointed *malkam*, "their king". The same consonants can be read *Milkom*, Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5, 33), as the Septuagint reads. Also in verse 3, where Milcom goes into exile with his priests, as Chemosh does in 48:7.
[^3]: The Ammonite capital, modern Amman.
[^4]: Literally "its daughters".
[^5]: This Ai, in Ammon, is not the town near Bethel. Heshbon lay near the border of Ammon and Moab (48:2).
[^6]: Hebrew *gederot*, "walls" or "sheepfolds". The meaning of the line is uncertain.
[^7]: Literally "your valley flows".
[^8]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH tseva'ot*, "my Lord YHWH of Armies". *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *The LORD of Armies* is also in verses 7, 26 and 35.
[^9]: Literally "turn the turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back the captives". Also in verse 39.
[^10]: A region or town of Edom, known for its wise men (Job 2:11). Verses 7–22 have many lines in common with Obadiah 1–9.
[^11]: Literally "go deep to dwell", that is, hide in caves. Also in verse 30. Dedan was an Arabian people southeast of Edom (25:23).
[^12]: Or, as questions, as in Obadiah 5: "would they not leave gleanings? … would they not destroy only what they wanted?"
[^13]: The cup of the LORD's wrath (25:15–29).
[^14]: A chief city of Edom (Isaiah 34:6; Amos 1:12). Also in verse 22.
[^15]: Hebrew *tiflatstekha*, a rare word, perhaps "the horror you cause".
[^16]: Hebrew *sela*, "rock", perhaps also the Edomite stronghold Sela (2 Kings 14:7).
[^17]: Repeated in 50:40.
[^18]: Literally "the pride of the Jordan" (12:5). Verses 19–21 are repeated, of Babylon, in 50:44–46.
[^19]: Hebrew *neveh etan*, "an enduring pasture".
[^20]: The Hebrew of this sentence is difficult. Literally "for I will in a moment make him run from it, and who is chosen I will appoint over it".
[^21]: Hebrew *yam suf*, "Sea of Reeds", here probably the Gulf of Aqaba, south of Edom.
[^22]: Cities of northern Syria (Isaiah 10:9).
[^23]: Literally "in the sea, anxiety".
[^24]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "How is it not abandoned". Perhaps the citizens lament that they did not leave the city in time.
[^25]: Verse 26 is repeated in 50:30.
[^26]: The name of several kings of Damascus (1 Kings 15:18; 2 Kings 13:3). Compare Amos 1:4.
[^27]: Kedar was a people of the Arabian desert (2:10). This Hazor is not the city in Galilee but probably a group of Arab settlements. Nebuchadnezzar campaigned against the Arabs in 599–598 BC.
[^28]: The Hebrew text is written "against them" but read "against you".
[^29]: See 9:25.
[^30]: Elam lay east of Babylonia, in what is now southwestern Iran. Zedekiah began to reign in 597 BC.
[^31]: Literally "the first of their might". The Elamites were famous archers (Isaiah 22:6).
[^32]: The Hebrew text is written *olam*, "forever", but read *Elam*.
[^33]: Literally "in the latter end of the days".
