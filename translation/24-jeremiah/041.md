**1** In the seventh month,[^1] Ishmael son of Nethaniah son of Elishama, who was of royal descent and one of the king's chief officers,[^2] came with ten men to Gedaliah son of Ahikam at Mizpah. As they were eating a meal together there at Mizpah,

**2** Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the ten men who were with him got up and struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan with the sword and killed him, the man the king of Babylon had appointed over the land.

**3** Ishmael also struck down all the Judeans[^3] who were with Gedaliah at Mizpah, and the Babylonians[^4] who were there, the soldiers.

**4** On the day after Gedaliah was killed, before anyone knew of it,

**5** eighty men came from Shechem, from Shiloh and from Samaria, with their beards shaved off, their clothes torn and their bodies gashed,[^5] bringing grain offerings and frankincense to present at the house of the LORD.[^6]

**6** Ishmael son of Nethaniah went out from Mizpah to meet them, weeping as he went. When he met them, he said to them, "Come to Gedaliah son of Ahikam."

**7** But when they came into the city, Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the men who were with him slaughtered them and threw them into a cistern.[^7]

**8** But there were ten men among them who said to Ishmael, "Do not kill us, for we have stores of wheat and barley, oil and honey hidden in the field." So he held back and did not kill them with their companions.

**9** Now the cistern into which Ishmael threw all the corpses of the men he had struck down because of Gedaliah[^8] was the one King Asa had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel.[^9] Ishmael son of Nethaniah filled it with the slain.

**10** Then Ishmael took captive all the rest of the people who were in Mizpah, the king's daughters and all the people who were left at Mizpah, whom Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard[^10] had put in the charge of Gedaliah son of Ahikam. Ishmael son of Nethaniah took them captive and set out to cross over to the Ammonites.

**11** When Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the armed forces who were with him heard of all the evil Ishmael son of Nethaniah had done,

**12** they took all their men and went to fight against Ishmael son of Nethaniah. They found him by the great pool[^11] in Gibeon.

**13** When all the people who were with Ishmael saw Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the armed forces who were with him, they were glad.

**14** All the people Ishmael had taken captive from Mizpah turned around and went back to Johanan son of Kareah.

**15** But Ishmael son of Nethaniah escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the Ammonites.

**16** Then Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the armed forces who were with him took all the rest of the people from Mizpah whom he had recovered from Ishmael son of Nethaniah, after Ishmael had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam: the men who were soldiers, the women, the children and the court officials[^12] whom he had brought back from Gibeon.

**17** They went and stayed at Geruth-chimham,[^13] near Bethlehem, intending to go on into Egypt

**18** to escape the Babylonians. They were afraid of them, because Ishmael son of Nethaniah had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had appointed over the land.

[^1]: The seventh month fell in September–October. The year is not given; it may have been the year Jerusalem fell (39:2; 52:12). After the exile a fast was kept in the seventh month, probably in memory of Gedaliah's murder (Zechariah 7:5; 8:19). Verses 1–3 are told more briefly in 2 Kings 25:25.
[^2]: The words "and one of the king's chief officers" are not in 2 Kings 25:25.
[^3]: Hebrew *ha-yehudim*, the people of Judah.
[^4]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verse 18.
[^5]: Signs of mourning (16:6; 48:37), probably for the destruction of the temple.
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. "The house of the LORD" is the temple, now in ruins.
[^7]: Literally "slaughtered them into the midst of the cistern".
[^8]: Literally "by the hand of Gedaliah". The Septuagint reads "was a great cistern".
[^9]: King Asa of Judah fortified Mizpah against Baasha of Israel (1 Kings 15:22).
[^10]: Hebrew *rav-tabbachim*, literally "chief of the butchers" or "chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal bodyguard.
[^11]: Literally "the great waters" (compare 2 Samuel 2:13).
[^12]: Hebrew *sarisim*, court officials. The word can also mean eunuchs.
[^13]: Perhaps "the lodging place of Chimham". The Hebrew text is written "Kimoham" but read "Chimham". Chimham may be the son of Barzillai to whom David showed kindness (2 Samuel 19:38–41, 19:37–40 in most English Bibles).
