**1** This is what Yahweh[^1] says: Go down to the palace of the king of Judah and speak this word there.

**2** Say: Hear the word of Yahweh, king of Judah, you who sit on David's throne, you and your servants and your people who come in through these gates.

**3** This is what Yahweh says: Do justice and righteousness. Rescue the one who has been robbed from the hand of the oppressor. Do not wrong or mistreat the foreigner, the fatherless or the widow, and do not shed innocent blood in this place.

**4** For if you truly carry out this word, then kings who sit on David's throne will come in through the gates of this palace, riding in chariots and on horses, each with his servants[^2] and his people.

**5** But if you do not listen to these words, I swear by myself, declares Yahweh, that this palace will become a ruin.

**6** For this is what Yahweh says about the palace of the king of Judah: You are like Gilead to me, like the summit of Lebanon,[^3] yet I will surely make you a wilderness, cities where no one lives.

**7** I will set apart destroyers against you,[^4] each with his weapons, and they will cut down your choicest cedars and throw them into the fire.

**8** Many nations will pass by this city, and they will say to one another, "Why has Yahweh done such a thing to this great city?"

**9** And they will answer, "Because they abandoned the covenant of Yahweh their God, and worshiped other gods and served them."

**10** Do not weep for the dead,[^5] or mourn for him. Weep bitterly instead for the one who is going away, for he will never come back to see his native land.

**11** For this is what Yahweh says about Shallum[^6] son of Josiah king of Judah, who became king in place of his father Josiah and who has gone away from this place: He will never come back here.

**12** He will die in the place where they have taken him into exile, and he will never see this land again.

**13** Woe to[^7] him who builds his house without righteousness and his upper rooms without justice, who makes his neighbor work for nothing and does not pay him his wages,

**14** who says, "I will build myself a spacious house with roomy upper rooms," who cuts out windows for it, panels it with cedar and paints it red.[^8]

**15** Does it make you a king, that you compete in cedar? Did your father not eat and drink, and do justice and righteousness? Then it went well with him.

**16** He defended the cause of the poor and needy; then it went well. Is that not what it means to know me? declares Yahweh.

**17** But your eyes and your heart are set only on your dishonest gain, on shedding innocent blood, on oppression and extortion.

**18** Therefore this is what Yahweh says about Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah: They will not mourn for him, "Alas,[^9] my brother! Alas, sister!" They will not mourn for him, "Alas, master! Alas, his majesty!"

**19** He will be buried with the burial of a donkey, dragged off and thrown out beyond the gates of Jerusalem.[^10]

**20** Go up to Lebanon and cry out; raise your voice in Bashan; cry out from Abarim,[^11] for all your lovers are crushed.

**21** I spoke to you when you were at ease, but you said, "I will not listen." This has been your way from your youth: you have not listened to my voice.

**22** The wind will shepherd away all your shepherds,[^12] and your lovers will go into captivity. Then you will be ashamed and humiliated because of all your wickedness.

**23** You who live in Lebanon, nestled among the cedars,[^13] how pitiable you will be[^14] when pangs come on you, pain like that of a woman in labor!

**24** As surely as I live, declares Yahweh, even if Coniah[^15] son of Jehoiakim king of Judah were the signet ring on my right hand, I would still pull you off it.

**25** I will hand you over to those who seek your life, to those you fear, to Nebuchadnezzar[^16] king of Babylon and to the Babylonians.[^17]

**26** I will hurl you and the mother who bore you into another country, where neither of you was born, and there you will die.

**27** They will never return to the land they long to return to.

**28** Is this man Coniah a despised, broken pot, a jar that no one wants? Why have he and his children been hurled out and thrown into a land they do not know?

**29** Land, land, land, hear the word of Yahweh!

**30** This is what Yahweh says: Record this man as childless,[^18] a man who will not prosper in his lifetime. For none of his descendants will prosper; none will sit on David's throne or rule again in Judah.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "his servant" but read "his servants".
[^3]: Gilead and Lebanon were famous for their forests. The palace was built with so much cedar that part of it was called the House of the Forest of Lebanon (1 Kings 7:2).
[^4]: Literally "I will consecrate destroyers against you", as soldiers were consecrated for a holy war (6:4).
[^5]: King Josiah, killed in battle in 609 BC (2 Kings 23:29–30). The one who is going away is his son Shallum (verse 11).
[^6]: Shallum was the personal name of Jehoahaz, who reigned three months in 609 BC before Pharaoh Neco took him to Egypt (2 Kings 23:30–34; 1 Chronicles 3:15).
[^7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead (verse 18; 1 Kings 13:30), used by the prophets to announce disaster. The king meant in verses 13–19 is Jehoiakim (verse 18), who reigned from 609 to 598 BC.
[^8]: Hebrew *shashar*, a red pigment, probably vermilion.
[^9]: Hebrew *hoy*, the cry of mourning translated "Woe to" in verse 13. The mourners address the dead as family and as lord.
[^10]: That is, his body will be thrown out unburied. 2 Kings 24:6 says only that he "slept with his ancestors".
[^11]: The mountains of Moab, east of the Dead Sea (Numbers 27:12). Lebanon in the north, Bashan in the northeast and Abarim in the southeast: Jerusalem, pictured as a woman, is to cry out from every height.
[^12]: A wordplay: *kol ro'ayikh tir'eh ruach*. Shepherds were rulers; the wind will drive them away as a shepherd drives a flock.
[^13]: Jerusalem, and especially its cedar-built palace (verse 6). The Hebrew text is written "I live … I am nested" but read "you live … you are nested". The written forms are probably an old spelling of "you" (compare 2:33).
[^14]: The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "how you will groan".
[^15]: A short form of Jeconiah (24:1), also called Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:8–15). He reigned three months in 597 BC and was taken to Babylon with his mother Nehushta.
[^16]: Hebrew *Nevukhadre'tsar*: see 21:2.
[^17]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon.
[^18]: Hebrew *ariri*, "stripped, childless". Jehoiachin had sons (1 Chronicles 3:17–18), but the verse goes on to say that none of them would reign.
