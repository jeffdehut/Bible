**1** Shephatiah son of Mattan, Gedaliah son of Pashhur, Jucal son of Shelemiah and Pashhur son of Malchiah[^1] heard the words that Jeremiah was speaking to all the people:

**2** "This is what Yahweh[^2] says: Whoever stays in this city will die by the sword, famine and plague, but whoever goes out to the Babylonians[^3] will live. He will escape with his life,[^4] and live.

**3** This is what Yahweh says: This city will certainly be given into the hand of the army of the king of Babylon, and he will capture it."

**4** Then the officials said to the king, "This man must be put to death, for he is discouraging[^5] the soldiers who are left in this city, and all the people, by speaking such words to them. This man is not seeking the well-being of this people, but their harm."

**5** King Zedekiah said, "He is in your hands, for the king can do nothing against you."

**6** So they took Jeremiah and threw him into the cistern of Malchiah the king's son, which was in the courtyard of the guard. They let Jeremiah down by ropes. There was no water in the cistern, only mud, and Jeremiah sank into the mud.

**7** Ebed-melech the Cushite,[^6] an official[^7] in the king's palace, heard that they had put Jeremiah into the cistern. The king was sitting in the Benjamin Gate.

**8** Ebed-melech went out from the king's palace and spoke to the king:

**9** "My lord the king, these men have done evil in all they have done to Jeremiah the prophet. They have thrown him into the cistern, and he will die where he is of hunger, for there is no more bread in the city."

**10** Then the king commanded Ebed-melech the Cushite, "Take thirty men with you from here, and pull Jeremiah the prophet up out of the cistern before he dies."

**11** So Ebed-melech took the men with him and went into the king's palace, to a place under the storeroom.[^8] He took from there some worn-out rags and worn-out clothes, and let them down by ropes to Jeremiah in the cistern.

**12** Ebed-melech the Cushite said to Jeremiah, "Put these worn-out rags and clothes under your armpits, under the ropes." Jeremiah did so.

**13** Then they pulled Jeremiah up with the ropes and brought him out of the cistern. And Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard.

**14** King Zedekiah sent and had Jeremiah the prophet brought to him at the third entrance of the house of Yahweh. The king said to Jeremiah, "I am going to ask you something. Do not hide anything from me."

**15** Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "If I tell you, will you not surely put me to death? And if I give you advice, you will not listen to me."

**16** But King Zedekiah swore an oath to Jeremiah in secret: "As surely as Yahweh lives, who has given us this life,[^9] I will not put you to death or hand you over to these men who seek your life."

**17** Then Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "This is what Yahweh, the God of Armies,[^10] the God of Israel, says: If you surrender[^11] to the officials of the king of Babylon, your life will be spared and this city will not be burned with fire; you and your household will live.

**18** But if you do not surrender to the officials of the king of Babylon, this city will be given into the hand of the Babylonians, and they will burn it with fire, and you yourself will not escape from their hand."

**19** King Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "I am afraid of the Judeans[^12] who have deserted to the Babylonians, that I may be handed over to them and they will mistreat me."

**20** Jeremiah said, "They will not hand you over. Please obey the voice of Yahweh in what I am telling you, and it will go well with you, and your life will be spared.

**21** But if you refuse to surrender, this is what Yahweh has shown me:

**22** All the women who are left in the palace of the king of Judah will be brought out to the officials of the king of Babylon, and they will say, 'Your trusted friends[^13] misled you and overcame you. Now that your feet are sunk in the mud, they have turned back.'

**23** All your wives and your children will be brought out to the Babylonians, and you yourself will not escape from their hand. You will be seized by the hand of the king of Babylon, and this city will be burned with fire."[^14]

**24** Then Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "Let no one know about this conversation, and you will not die.

**25** If the officials hear that I have spoken with you, and they come to you and say to you, 'Tell us what you said to the king. Do not hide it from us, and we will not put you to death. And what did the king say to you?'

**26** then say to them, 'I was presenting my plea to the king not to send me back to the house of Jonathan to die there.'"

**27** All the officials did come to Jeremiah and question him, and he told them just what the king had commanded. So they said no more to him, for no one had heard the conversation.

**28** And Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard until the day Jerusalem was captured. This is what happened when Jerusalem was captured:[^15]

[^1]: Jucal is the Jehucal of 37:3, and Pashhur son of Malchiah is the official of 21:1.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" in verse 14 is the temple.
[^3]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verses 18–19 and 23.
[^4]: Literally "his life will be to him as plunder" (21:9).
[^5]: Literally "weakening the hands of".
[^6]: The name means "servant of the king". A Cushite was a man from Cush, the land on the upper Nile, south of Egypt (13:23).
[^7]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official. The word can also mean a eunuch.
[^8]: Literally "under the treasury".
[^9]: Literally "who made for us this soul".
[^10]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD God of hosts". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^11]: Literally "go out". Also in verses 18 and 21.
[^12]: Hebrew *ha-yehudim*, the people of Judah.
[^13]: Literally "the men of your peace", your allies (compare Obadiah 7). The women's words sound like a lament, perhaps a mocking one. Jeremiah had sunk into the mud of the cistern (verse 6); now the king will sink.
[^14]: Literally "and you will burn this city with fire", that is, you will be the cause of its burning. The same consonants can be read "and this city will be burned with fire".
[^15]: The second half of this verse leads into 39:3, after the dates given in 39:1–2.
