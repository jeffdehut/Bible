**1** Zedekiah son of Josiah reigned as king in place of Coniah[^1] son of Jehoiakim; Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon made him king in the land of Judah.

**2** But neither he nor his servants nor the people of the land listened to the words of the LORD[^2] that he spoke through Jeremiah the prophet.

**3** King Zedekiah sent Jehucal son of Shelemiah and Zephaniah son of Maaseiah the priest to Jeremiah the prophet to say, "Please pray to the LORD our God for us."

**4** Now Jeremiah was still coming and going among the people, for they had not yet put him in prison.

**5** Pharaoh's army had come out of Egypt,[^3] and when the Babylonians[^4] who were besieging Jerusalem heard the report about them, they withdrew from Jerusalem.

**6** Then the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the prophet:

**7** "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: This is what you are to say to the king of Judah, who sent you to me to inquire of me: 'Pharaoh's army, which has come out to help you, is going back to its own land, to Egypt.

**8** The Babylonians will come back and fight against this city. They will capture it and burn it with fire.

**9** This is what the LORD says: Do not deceive yourselves, saying: The Babylonians will surely go away from us. For they will not go away.

**10** Even if you were to strike down the whole army of the Babylonians who are fighting against you, and only wounded men were left among them, each in his tent, they would get up and burn this city with fire.'"

**11** When the army of the Babylonians had withdrawn from Jerusalem because of Pharaoh's army,

**12** Jeremiah set out from Jerusalem to go to the land of Benjamin, to receive his share of property there among the people.[^5]

**13** When he reached the Benjamin Gate, a captain of the guard named Irijah son of Shelemiah son of Hananiah was there, and he arrested Jeremiah the prophet, saying, "You are deserting to the Babylonians!"

**14** Jeremiah said, "That is a lie! I am not deserting to the Babylonians." But he would not listen to him. Irijah arrested Jeremiah and brought him to the officials.

**15** The officials were furious with Jeremiah. They beat him and put him in prison in the house of Jonathan the secretary, for they had made it into a prison.

**16** So Jeremiah was put into the cistern house, into the vaulted cells,[^6] and Jeremiah remained there many days.

**17** Then King Zedekiah sent for him and had him brought, and the king questioned him secretly in his palace. He said, "Is there any word from the LORD?" Jeremiah said, "There is." And he said, "You will be given into the hand of the king of Babylon."

**18** Then Jeremiah said to King Zedekiah, "What wrong have I done to you or your servants or this people, that you have put me in prison?

**19** Where are your prophets who prophesied to you, 'The king of Babylon will not come against you or against this land'?

**20** Now please listen, my lord the king. Let my plea come before you:[^7] do not send me back to the house of Jonathan the secretary, or I will die there."

**21** So King Zedekiah gave orders, and they committed Jeremiah to the courtyard of the guard.[^8] He was given a loaf of bread each day from the bakers' street, until all the bread in the city was gone. So Jeremiah remained in the courtyard of the guard.

[^1]: That is, Jehoiachin (22:24), taken into exile in 597 BC (2 Kings 24:12–17).
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Probably the army of Pharaoh Hophra (44:30), in 588 BC.
[^4]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verses 8–11 and 13–14.
[^5]: The meaning of the Hebrew *lachaloq*, "to divide, to take a share", is uncertain here. It may concern a family inheritance in Anathoth (compare 32:6–15).
[^6]: Hebrew *beit ha-bor*, "the house of the pit" or "cistern", and *chanuyot*, a rare word, perhaps "vaults" or "cells".
[^7]: Literally "Let my plea fall before you".
[^8]: See 32:2.
