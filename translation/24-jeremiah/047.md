**1** The word of the LORD[^1] that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the Philistines, before Pharaoh struck Gaza.[^2]

**2** This is what the LORD says: Look, waters are rising from the north; they will become an overflowing torrent. They will overflow the land and everything in it, the city and those who live in it. People will cry out, and all who live in the land will wail.

**3** At the sound of the stamping hooves of his stallions, at the rumble of his chariots and the clatter of their wheels, fathers will not turn back for their children, because their hands hang limp.[^3]

**4** For the day is coming to destroy all the Philistines, to cut off from Tyre and Sidon every helper who survives. For the LORD is destroying the Philistines, the remnant of the coastland of Caphtor.[^4]

**5** Baldness has come upon Gaza;[^5] Ashkelon is silenced. Remnant of their valley,[^6] how long will you gash yourself?

**6** Ah,[^7] sword of the LORD! How long until you are quiet? Go back into your sheath; rest and be still.

**7** How can it be quiet,[^8] when the LORD has given it a command? Against Ashkelon and against the seacoast, there he has appointed it.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: The occasion is not known. Gaza, Ashkelon and the other Philistine cities lay on the coastal plain southwest of Judah.
[^3]: Literally "because of the slackness of hands".
[^4]: Caphtor is probably Crete, from which the Philistines had come (Amos 9:7; Deuteronomy 2:23). Hebrew *i*, "coastland, island".
[^5]: The head was shaved in mourning (16:6; 48:37).
[^6]: Hebrew *imqam*, "their valley", perhaps the coastal plain. The Septuagint reads "the remnant of the Anakim", the giants of old who had lived in the Philistine cities (Joshua 11:22).
[^7]: Hebrew *hoy*, translated "Woe to" in 22:13, here a cry of lament.
[^8]: Literally "How can you be quiet".
