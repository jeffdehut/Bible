**1** Come now, you rich people, weep and wail over the miseries that are coming on you.

**2** Your wealth has rotted, and your clothes have become moth-eaten.

**3** Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion[^1] will be evidence against you, and it will eat your flesh. You have stored up treasure that is like fire, in the last days.[^2]

**4** Look, the wages you withheld from the workers who mowed your fields are crying out, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of Armies.[^3]

**5** You have lived on the earth in luxury and self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.[^4]

**6** You have condemned and murdered the righteous one. Does he not oppose you?[^5]

**7** Be patient, then, brothers and sisters,[^6] until the coming of the Lord. See how the farmer waits for the precious crop from the land, being patient over it until it receives the early and the late rains.[^7]

**8** You too must be patient. Strengthen your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is near.

**9** Do not complain against one another, brothers and sisters, so that you will not be judged. Look, the Judge is standing at the door!

**10** As an example of suffering and patience, brothers and sisters, take the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord.

**11** Look, we call blessed those who endured. You have heard of the endurance of Job, and you have seen the outcome the Lord brought about,[^8] for the Lord is full of compassion and mercy.[^9]

**12** Above all, my brothers and sisters, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or with any other oath. Let your "Yes" be yes and your "No" be no, so that you may not fall under judgment.[^10]

**13** Is anyone among you suffering? Let them pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let them sing praise.

**14** Is anyone among you sick? Let them call for the elders of the church, and let the elders pray over them, anointing them with oil in the name of the Lord.

**15** And the prayer offered in faith will save[^11] the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise them up. And if they have committed sins, they will be forgiven.

**16** So confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it works.[^12]

**17** Elijah was a human being just like us. He prayed earnestly[^13] that it would not rain, and it did not rain on the land for three years and six months.[^14]

**18** Then he prayed again, and the sky gave rain, and the land produced its crops.[^15]

**19** My brothers and sisters, if anyone among you wanders from the truth and someone brings them back,

**20** let that person know that whoever brings a sinner back from the error of their way will save their soul from death[^16] and will cover a multitude of sins.[^17]

[^1]: Greek *ios*, "rust", the word translated "poison" in 3:8.
[^2]: Or, punctuated differently, "and it will eat your flesh like fire. You have stored up treasure in the last days."
[^3]: Greek *Kyrios Sabaoth*, from the Hebrew title *Yahweh tseva'ot*, "Yahweh of Armies", traditionally "the Lord of hosts". Compare Isaiah 5:9, "Yahweh of Armies has sworn in my hearing", and Deuteronomy 24:14–15.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "as in a day of slaughter". Compare Jeremiah 12:3, "set them apart for the day of killing".
[^5]: The SBLGNT punctuates this as a question. *He* may be God, who "opposes the proud" (4:6, the same verb), or the righteous one, whose cry rises against them. Many translations read it as a statement: "who does not resist you".
[^6]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 9, 10, 12 and 19.
[^7]: Literally "the early and the late", the autumn and spring rains (Deuteronomy 11:14). Many manuscripts add "rain".
[^8]: Literally "the end of the Lord": the purpose he had and the good end he gave (Job 42:10–17).
[^9]: Compare Psalm 103:8, "Yahweh is compassionate and gracious".
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "fall into hypocrisy". Compare Matthew 5:34–37.
[^11]: Or "heal". Greek *sozo* means both.
[^12]: Or "The prayer of a righteous person, when it is put into action, can do much."
[^13]: Literally "he prayed with prayer", a Hebrew way of giving emphasis.
[^14]: 1 Kings 17:1; 18:1, where the rain comes "in the third year". Compare Luke 4:25, "three years and six months".
[^15]: 1 Kings 18:41–45.
[^16]: Literally "will save his soul from death": the sinner's soul, or perhaps the soul of the one who brings the sinner back. Many manuscripts read "will save a soul from death".
[^17]: Compare Proverbs 10:12, "love covers all offenses", and 1 Peter 4:8.
