**1** My brothers and sisters,[^1] can you hold faith in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ[^2] and show favoritism?[^3]

**2** Suppose a man comes into your assembly[^4] wearing gold rings and fine clothes, and a poor man in dirty clothes also comes in,

**3** and you pay attention to the one wearing the fine clothes and say, "You sit here in a good place," but say to the poor man, "You stand, or sit there on the floor by my footstool."[^5]

**4** Have you not made distinctions among yourselves[^6] and become judges with evil thoughts?

**5** Listen, my dear brothers and sisters. Has not God chosen those who are poor in the eyes of the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom that he has promised to those who love him?

**6** But you have dishonored the poor. Is it not the rich who oppress you, and they who drag you into court?

**7** Is it not they who blaspheme the honorable name that was spoken over you?[^7]

**8** If you really fulfill the royal law[^8] according to the scripture, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself,"[^9] you are doing well.

**9** But if you show favoritism, you commit sin and are convicted by the law as lawbreakers.

**10** For whoever keeps the whole law but stumbles at one point has become guilty of breaking all of it.

**11** For he who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder."[^10] If you do not commit adultery but you do murder, you have become a lawbreaker.

**12** Speak and act as those who are going to be judged by the law of freedom.

**13** For judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment.

**14** What good is it, my brothers and sisters, if someone says they have faith but they do not have works?[^11] Can such faith save them?

**15** Suppose a brother or sister has no clothes[^12] and lacks daily food,

**16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace; keep warm and eat your fill," but you do not give them what the body needs. What good is that?

**17** So too faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.

**18** But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without works,[^13] and I will show you my faith by my works.[^14]

**19** You believe that God is one?[^15] You do well. Even the demons believe that, and they shudder.

**20** Do you want to be shown, you foolish person,[^16] that faith without works is useless?[^17]

**21** Was not Abraham our ancestor[^18] declared righteous[^19] by works when he offered his son Isaac on the altar?[^20]

**22** You see that faith was working together with his works, and faith was made complete by the works.

**23** And the scripture was fulfilled that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness,"[^21] and he was called God's friend.[^22]

**24** You see that a person is declared righteous by works and not by faith alone.

**25** In the same way, was not Rahab the prostitute also declared righteous by works when she welcomed the messengers and sent them out by another road?[^23]

**26** For just as the body without the spirit[^24] is dead, so faith without works is dead.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 5 and 14.
[^2]: Literally "faith of our Lord Jesus Christ of glory": perhaps "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory", or "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Glory". *Faith in*: or "the faithfulness of".
[^3]: The SBLGNT punctuates the verse as a question expecting the answer "No". Many translations read it as a command: "do not hold the faith … while showing favoritism".
[^4]: Greek *synagoge*, "synagogue, gathering", used here of the believers' meeting.
[^5]: Literally "under my footstool".
[^6]: Or "been divided in your minds". Greek *diakrino*, the verb translated "doubt" in 1:6.
[^7]: Literally "the good name that was called upon you", the name of the one to whom they belong. Compare Amos 9:12, "the nations that are called by my name".
[^8]: Perhaps the law of the kingdom (verse 5), or the supreme law.
[^9]: Leviticus 19:18 (see Mark 12:31).
[^10]: Exodus 20:13–14; Deuteronomy 5:17–18 (see Mark 10:19).
[^11]: Greek *erga*, "works, deeds": what a person does. Also in verses 17–26.
[^12]: Literally "naked", that is, poorly clothed.
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "by your works". It is uncertain where the words of the "someone" end: after "I have works", as here, or at the end of verse 18, or later.
[^14]: The Greek has "my works" and "the faith"; many manuscripts read "my faith".
[^15]: Or "that there is one God". Compare Deuteronomy 6:4 (see Mark 12:29).
[^16]: Literally "empty man".
[^17]: Greek *arge*, "idle, useless", from *a-ergos*, "without work": a play on *erga*, "works". Many manuscripts read "dead".
[^18]: Literally "our father".
[^19]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justified"; also in verses 24 and 25. James may mean that God declared Abraham righteous on the basis of what he did, or that his actions showed him to be righteous (vindicated him). Paul uses the same verb and the same example of Abraham (Genesis 15:6) when he writes that "a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28; compare Romans 4:1–5; Galatians 2:16). Interpreters differ on how the two passages relate. Many hold that they use the words in different senses: Paul speaks of works of the law as a ground of standing with God, and James of the deeds that true faith produces; and Paul speaks of the beginning of a right relationship with God, James of its proof or of the final judgment. Others see a real difference between them, or James answering a misunderstanding of Paul's teaching.
[^20]: Genesis 22:9–12.
[^21]: Genesis 15:6, as in the Septuagint, which has "God" and the passive "it was credited". The Hebrew reads "Abram believed Yahweh, and he credited it to him as righteousness."
[^22]: Compare 2 Chronicles 20:7; Isaiah 41:8.
[^23]: Joshua 2:1–21. *The messengers*: the two spies Joshua sent.
[^24]: Or "breath".
