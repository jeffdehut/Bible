**1** James,[^1] a servant[^2] of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the Dispersion:[^3] Greetings.[^4]

**2** Consider it nothing but joy, my brothers and sisters,[^5] whenever you meet trials[^6] of many kinds,

**3** because you know that the testing of your faith produces endurance.

**4** And let endurance have its full effect,[^7] so that you may be mature and complete, lacking in nothing.

**5** If any of you lacks wisdom, let them ask God, who gives to all generously[^8] and without reproach, and it will be given to them.

**6** But they must ask in faith, without doubting, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.

**7** That person must not suppose that they will receive anything from the Lord.

**8** Such a person is double-minded,[^9] unstable in all their ways.

**9** Let the believer who is lowly[^10] boast in being lifted up,

**10** and the one who is rich in being brought low,[^11] because the rich will pass away like a flower in the grass.[^12]

**11** For the sun rises with its scorching heat[^13] and withers the grass; its flower falls, and the beauty of its appearance perishes. In the same way the rich will fade away in the midst of their pursuits.

**12** Blessed is the one who endures trial, because when they have stood the test they will receive the crown of life that God has promised[^14] to those who love him.

**13** When tempted, no one should say, "I am being tempted by God." For God cannot be tempted by evil,[^15] and he himself tempts no one.

**14** But each person is tempted when they are dragged away and enticed by their own desire.[^16]

**15** Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin, and when sin is fully grown, it gives birth to death.

**16** Do not be deceived, my dear brothers and sisters.

**17** Every good gift and every perfect present is from above,[^17] coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no change or shadow caused by turning.[^18]

**18** By his own choice he gave birth[^19] to us by the word of truth, so that we would be a kind of firstfruits[^20] of all he created.

**19** Know this,[^21] my dear brothers and sisters: everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to anger,

**20** for human anger[^22] does not produce the righteousness of God.

**21** So get rid of all filth and the evil that is everywhere,[^23] and humbly accept the word planted in you, which is able to save your souls.

**22** Be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.

**23** For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, they are like a man who looks at his own face[^24] in a mirror.

**24** He looks at himself, goes away, and at once forgets what he was like.

**25** But the one who looks closely into the perfect law, the law of freedom, and continues in it, not as a hearer who forgets but as a doer who acts, will be blessed in what they do.

**26** If anyone thinks they are religious but does not keep a tight rein[^25] on their tongue, they deceive their own heart, and their religion is worthless.

**27** Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress, and to keep oneself unstained by the world.

[^1]: Greek *Iakobos*, the same name as Jacob. Traditionally the writer is identified as James the brother of the Lord (Mark 6:3; Acts 15:13; Galatians 1:19).
[^2]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^3]: Greek *diaspora*, "scattering", the usual term for the Jews living outside the land of Israel. Here it may describe believers scattered among the nations (compare 1 Peter 1:1).
[^4]: Greek *chairein*, literally "rejoice", the ordinary greeting at the start of a letter (Acts 15:23). It leads into "joy" (*chara*) in verse 2.
[^5]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 16 and 19.
[^6]: Greek *peirasmos*, "trial, test" or "temptation"; also in verse 12. The related verb *peirazo* in verses 13–14 has the sense "tempt".
[^7]: Literally "a perfect work". Greek *teleios*, "complete, perfect, mature", the word translated "mature" later in the verse.
[^8]: Greek *haplos*, "simply": without reserve, with a single mind (compare "double-minded" in verse 8).
[^9]: Literally "a two-souled man".
[^10]: Literally "the lowly brother": the poor and of low standing.
[^11]: It is not clear whether the rich person is a believer, with "brother" understood from verse 9, or not.
[^12]: Compare Isaiah 40:6–8, "All people are grass … the grass withers, the flower fades". Literally "like a flower of the grass".
[^13]: Or "the scorching wind". Greek *kauson*, used of the hot east wind.
[^14]: Literally "that he promised". Many manuscripts read "that the Lord promised".
[^15]: Or "God has no experience of evil".
[^16]: The Greek words picture a fish or an animal drawn out and lured with bait.
[^17]: In Greek the first part of the verse forms a line of verse, perhaps a familiar saying.
[^18]: *The Father of lights* is the creator of the sun, moon and stars (Genesis 1:14–18). The terms *change* and *turning* may be drawn from the movements of the heavenly bodies, whose light varies and whose shadows shift; God does not change. The Greek of the last phrase is unusual, and its exact sense is uncertain.
[^19]: Greek *apokyeo*, the verb used of sin in verse 15.
[^20]: The first and best part of a harvest, which belonged to God (Leviticus 23:10).
[^21]: Or "You know this". Many manuscripts read "So then, my dear brothers and sisters, everyone should be …".
[^22]: Literally "the anger of a man".
[^23]: Literally "the abundance of evil".
[^24]: Literally "the face of his birth", his natural face.
[^25]: Greek *chalinagogeo*, "guide with a bridle" (compare 3:2–3).
