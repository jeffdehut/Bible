**1** Comfort, comfort my people, says your God.[^1]

**2** Speak tenderly to Jerusalem,[^2] and call out to her that her hard service is over,[^3] that her guilt has been paid for, that she has received from the hand of the LORD[^4] double for all her sins.

**3** A voice is shouting: "In the wilderness prepare the way for the LORD; make straight in the desert a highway for our God.[^5]

**4** Every valley will be raised up, and every mountain and hill made low. The uneven ground will become level, and the rugged places a plain.

**5** Then the glory of the LORD will be revealed, and all people[^6] will see it together. For the mouth of the LORD has spoken."

**6** A voice says, "Shout!" And I said,[^7] "What shall I shout?" All people are grass, and all their faithfulness[^8] is like the flower of the field.

**7** The grass withers, the flower fades, when the breath[^9] of the LORD blows on it. Surely the people are grass.

**8** The grass withers, the flower fades, but the word of our God will stand forever.[^10]

**9** Go up on a high mountain, you who bring good news to Zion![^11] Raise your voice with strength, you who bring good news to Jerusalem! Raise it; do not be afraid! Say to the towns of Judah, "Here is your God!"

**10** Look, the Lord GOD comes with might, and his arm rules for him. Look, his reward is with him, and his recompense goes before him.[^12]

**11** Like a shepherd he tends his flock. He gathers the lambs in his arm and carries them close to his heart;[^13] he gently leads the ewes that have young.

**12** Who has measured the waters in the hollow of his hand, and marked off the heavens with the span of his hand?[^14] Who has held the dust of the earth in a measure, and weighed the mountains on scales and the hills in a balance?

**13** Who has measured the Spirit[^15] of the LORD, or instructed him as his counselor?[^16]

**14** Whom did he consult to give him understanding? Who taught him the path of justice, taught him knowledge, and showed him the way of understanding?

**15** Look, the nations are like a drop from a bucket; they are counted as dust on the scales. Look, he lifts up the coastlands[^17] like fine dust.

**16** Lebanon is not enough for fuel, nor are its animals enough for a burnt offering.

**17** All the nations are as nothing before him; he counts them as less than nothing and emptiness.[^18]

**18** To whom, then, will you compare God?[^19] What likeness will you set beside him?

**19** An idol? A craftsman casts it, and a goldsmith overlays it with gold and casts silver chains for it.

**20** One too poor for such an offering[^20] chooses wood that will not rot. He looks for a skilled craftsman to set up an idol that will not topple.

**21** Do you not know? Have you not heard? Has it not been told you from the beginning? Have you not understood how the earth was founded?[^21]

**22** He is the one who sits enthroned above the circle of the earth, and its inhabitants are like grasshoppers. He stretches out the heavens like a curtain[^22] and spreads them out like a tent to live in.

**23** He brings rulers to nothing and makes the judges of the earth like emptiness.

**24** Scarcely are they planted, scarcely are they sown, scarcely has their stem taken root in the ground, when he blows on them and they wither, and the storm carries them off like straw.

**25** To whom, then, will you compare me, that I should be like him? says the Holy One.

**26** Lift up your eyes on high and see: who created these? He brings out their army[^23] by number and calls them all by name. Because of his great strength and mighty power, not one of them is missing.

**27** Why do you say, Jacob, and why do you declare, Israel, "My way is hidden from the LORD, and my God pays no attention to my cause"?[^24]

**28** Do you not know? Have you not heard? The LORD is the everlasting God, the Creator of the ends of the earth. He does not grow tired or weary; his understanding cannot be searched out.

**29** He gives strength to the weary, and to those who have no power he gives great might.

**30** Even young men grow tired and weary, and strong young men stumble and fall.

**31** But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will rise up on wings like eagles;[^25] they will run and not grow weary; they will walk and not grow tired.

[^1]: *Comfort* is plural: the command is given to more than one, perhaps the prophets or the heavenly council (6:8). The Septuagint adds "Priests" at the start of verse 2. Chapters 40–66 speak to the people of Judah in exile in Babylon and after their return. They name Cyrus of Persia, who conquered Babylon in 539 BC (44:28; 45:1). Many scholars attribute them to a later prophet or prophets in the tradition of Isaiah; traditionally they have been read as the words of Isaiah son of Amoz.
[^2]: Literally "Speak to the heart of Jerusalem", as one speaks to win someone's love or to comfort them (Genesis 34:3; Ruth 2:13).
[^3]: Literally "that her term of service is fulfilled". Hebrew *tsava*, "army, military service, forced labor" (Job 7:1).
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: The Hebrew accents join "in the wilderness" to "prepare". The Septuagint joins it to the voice: "A voice of one shouting in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord; make straight the paths of our God." The Gospels quote this verse from the Septuagint, applying it to John the Baptizer (Matthew 3:3; Mark 1:3; Luke 3:4–6; John 1:23). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^6]: Literally "all flesh". Also in verse 6. The Septuagint reads "and all flesh will see the salvation of God", quoted in Luke 3:6.
[^7]: The vowels of the Hebrew text read "And he said" or "And someone said". The same consonants can be read "And I said", as the Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) and the Septuagint understood them.
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed*, "faithful love", here perhaps the loyalty or the goodness of human beings. The Septuagint reads "all the glory of a human being". 1 Peter 1:24–25 quotes verses 6–8 from the Septuagint.
[^9]: Or "wind". Hebrew *ruach*. The Septuagint has the line "The grass withers, the flower fades" only once, and does not have "when the breath of the LORD blows on it. Surely the people are grass."
[^10]: Or "will rise up and stand".
[^11]: Or "Zion, you who bring good news". The messenger is feminine: either a woman who brings the news, or Zion herself sent to the towns of Judah. Hebrew *mevasseret*, from *basar*, "bring good news" (52:7; 61:1), translated in the Septuagint with the Greek verb from which the New Testament word for "good news" comes. Also in the next line.
[^12]: The *reward* and *recompense* are the people he has won back, whom he brings with him (62:11; compare Revelation 22:12).
[^13]: Literally "in his bosom", the fold of the garment over the chest.
[^14]: Hebrew *zeret*, the width of a spread hand, from thumb to little finger. A *measure* is literally "a third", perhaps a third of an ephah.
[^15]: Or "mind". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^16]: Romans 11:34 and 1 Corinthians 2:16 quote this verse from the Septuagint: "Who has known the mind of the Lord? Who has been his counselor?" In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^17]: The islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands.
[^18]: Hebrew *tohu*, the word for the "formless" earth in Genesis 1:2. Also in verse 23.
[^19]: Hebrew *El*, "God".
[^20]: Hebrew *ha-mesukkan terumah*, of uncertain meaning. Perhaps "the one who is impoverished in offering". Some scholars connect *mesukkan* with an Akkadian word for a kind of hard wood, *musukkannu*: "As an offering, he chooses *musukkannu*, wood that will not rot."
[^21]: Literally "Have you not understood the foundations of the earth?" Or "since the foundations of the earth".
[^22]: Hebrew *doq*, "fine cloth, gauze", found only here.
[^23]: The stars, pictured as an army (34:4). Hebrew *tseva*, as in the title *YHWH tseva'ot*, "the LORD of Armies".
[^24]: Literally "and from my God my right passes away".
[^25]: Or "They will grow new feathers like eagles" (compare Psalm 103:5). *Eagle* is Hebrew *nesher*, which may also mean the griffon vulture.
