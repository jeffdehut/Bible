**1** Woe to the proud crown of the drunkards of Ephraim,[^1] to the fading flower of its glorious beauty, which is on the head of the fertile valley,[^2] of those overcome by wine!

**2** Look, the Lord has one who is mighty and strong. Like a storm of hail, a destroying wind, like a storm of mighty, flooding waters, he will throw it down to the ground with his hand.

**3** The proud crown of the drunkards of Ephraim will be trampled underfoot.

**4** And the fading flower of its glorious beauty, which is on the head of the fertile valley, will be like a fig that ripens before the summer harvest: as soon as someone sees it, he swallows it while it is still in his hand.

**5** On that day the LORD of Armies[^3] will be a crown of glory and a diadem of beauty for the remnant of his people,

**6** a spirit of justice for the one who sits in judgment, and strength for those who turn back the battle at the gate.

**7** But these too reel from wine and stagger from beer: priest and prophet reel from beer; they are swallowed up by wine; they stagger from beer; they reel when they see visions; they stumble when they give judgment.

**8** For all the tables are covered with vomit; no place is without filth.

**9** Whom is he trying to teach? To whom is he explaining his message? To children just weaned from milk, just taken from the breast?[^4]

**10** For it is *tsav la-tsav, tsav la-tsav, qav la-qav, qav la-qav*, a little here, a little there.[^5]

**11** Very well, then: with stammering lips and in a foreign tongue he will speak to this people,[^6]

**12** to whom he said, "This is the place of rest; give rest to the weary; this is the place of repose." But they would not listen.

**13** So the word of the LORD will be to them *tsav la-tsav, tsav la-tsav, qav la-qav, qav la-qav*, a little here, a little there, so that they will go and fall backward, be broken, be snared and be captured.

**14** Therefore hear the word of the LORD, you scoffers who rule this people in Jerusalem![^7]

**15** For you have said, "We have made a covenant with death,[^8] and with Sheol[^9] we have made an agreement. When the overwhelming scourge passes through,[^10] it will not reach us, for we have made lies our refuge and hidden ourselves in falsehood."

**16** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: Look, I am laying in Zion a stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a firm foundation.[^11] Whoever trusts will not panic.[^12]

**17** I will make justice the measuring line and righteousness the plumb line. Hail will sweep away the refuge of lies, and water will flood the hiding place.

**18** Your covenant with death will be annulled,[^13] and your agreement with Sheol will not stand. When the overwhelming scourge passes through, you will be trampled down by it.

**19** As often as it passes through, it will take you; morning after morning it will pass through, by day and by night. To understand the message will be sheer terror.

**20** For the bed is too short to stretch out on, and the blanket too narrow to wrap around you.

**21** For the LORD will rise up as at Mount Perazim; he will rage as in the valley of Gibeon,[^14] to do his work, his strange work, and to perform his task, his alien task.

**22** So now, stop your scoffing, or your chains will be made heavier. For I have heard from the Lord GOD of Armies a decree of destruction against the whole land.[^15]

**23** Listen and hear my voice; pay attention and hear what I say.

**24** Does the plowman plow all day to plant seed? Does he keep on breaking up and harrowing his ground?

**25** When he has leveled its surface, does he not scatter dill and sow cumin? Does he not put wheat in rows, barley in its place, and spelt at its border?[^16]

**26** His God instructs him and teaches him the right way.[^17]

**27** Dill is not threshed with a threshing sledge, and a cartwheel is not rolled over cumin. Dill is beaten out with a stick, and cumin with a rod.

**28** Grain for bread is crushed, but not endlessly; he does not keep threshing it forever. He drives the wheel of his cart and his horses over it, but he does not crush it.

**29** This also comes from the LORD of Armies. His counsel is wonderful, and his wisdom is great.[^18]

[^1]: Samaria, capital of the northern kingdom (Ephraim), stood on a hill above a fertile valley like a crown or garland on a drinker's head. It fell to Assyria in 722 BC.
[^2]: Literally "the valley of fatness". Also in verse 4.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^4]: Verses 9–10 are probably the words of the drunken priests and prophets, mocking Isaiah's teaching as fit only for small children.
[^5]: The Hebrew is a string of short sounds, perhaps baby talk, or the drunken mockery of a teacher. It may also be understood as "command upon command, command upon command, line upon line, line upon line", since *tsav* may come from the word for "command" and *qav* means "measuring line" (verse 17). Some think of the letters *tsade* and *qof* recited by children learning the alphabet. Also in verse 13.
[^6]: The foreign speech of Assyrian soldiers. Paul quotes verses 11–12 in 1 Corinthians 14:21.
[^7]: Or "you scoffers who make proverbs for this people". Hebrew *moshle* can mean "rulers" or "speakers of sayings".
[^8]: Hebrew *mavet*. In Canaanite stories *Mot*, "Death", was also the name of a god of the underworld.
[^9]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. Also in verse 18. *Agreement* is literally "vision" or "seer" (*chozeh*), perhaps a pact made through a seer.
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "passed" but read "passes".
[^11]: Literally "Look, I have laid". The form of the Hebrew verb is unusual.
[^12]: Or "will not be in a hurry". The Septuagint reads "will not be put to shame", and Romans 9:33; 10:11 and 1 Peter 2:6 quote the verse in that form.
[^13]: Literally "covered over", the verb used for atonement, here perhaps "wiped out".
[^14]: Where David defeated the Philistines (2 Samuel 5:20, 25; 1 Chronicles 14:16), and where the LORD fought for Joshua at Gibeon (Joshua 10:10–14).
[^15]: Or "against the whole earth". Compare 10:23.
[^16]: The words translated *in rows* and *in its place* are uncertain.
[^17]: Literally "for judgment", that is, the proper way to do things.
[^18]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, practical wisdom that brings success.
