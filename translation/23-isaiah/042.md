**1** Here is my servant,[^1] whom I uphold, my chosen one, in whom I delight.[^2] I have put my Spirit[^3] on him; he will bring justice[^4] to the nations.

**2** He will not cry out or raise his voice, or make his voice heard in the street.

**3** A bruised reed he will not break, and a smoldering wick he will not snuff out. He will faithfully bring justice.[^5]

**4** He will not grow dim or be bruised[^6] until he has established justice on the earth, and the coastlands[^7] wait for his instruction.[^8]

**5** This is what God, the LORD,[^9] says, who created the heavens and stretched them out, who spread out the earth and all that comes from it, who gives breath to the people on it and spirit to those who walk on it:

**6** I, the LORD, have called you in righteousness;[^10] I will take hold of your hand. I will keep you[^11] and make you a covenant for the people,[^12] a light for the nations,

**7** to open eyes that are blind, to bring prisoners out of the dungeon, and those who sit in darkness out of the prison.

**8** I am the LORD; that is my name. I will not give my glory to another, or my praise to idols.

**9** Look, the former things have come to pass, and new things I now declare. Before they spring up, I tell you of them.

**10** Sing to the LORD a new song,[^13] his praise from the end of the earth, you who go down to the sea, and all that is in it, you coastlands and those who live in them.

**11** Let the wilderness and its towns raise their voices, the villages where Kedar[^14] lives. Let the people of Sela[^15] sing for joy; let them shout from the tops of the mountains.

**12** Let them give glory to the LORD and declare his praise in the coastlands.

**13** The LORD goes out like a warrior; like a man of war he stirs up his zeal. He shouts, yes, he roars; he shows his might against his enemies.

**14** For a long time I have kept silent; I have been still and held myself back. Now, like a woman in labor, I will cry out; I will gasp and pant.

**15** I will lay waste mountains and hills and dry up all their plants. I will turn rivers into dry land[^16] and dry up the pools.

**16** I will lead the blind by a way they do not know; I will guide them on paths they have not known. I will turn the darkness before them into light, and the rough places into level ground. These are the things I will do,[^17] and I will not abandon them.

**17** They will be turned back and utterly put to shame, those who trust in idols, who say to cast images, "You are our gods."

**18** Listen, you deaf! Look, you blind, so that you may see!

**19** Who is blind but my servant, or deaf like my messenger whom I send? Who is blind like the one at peace with me,[^18] blind like the servant of the LORD?

**20** You see many things,[^19] but you do not take note of them; his ears are open, but he does not hear.

**21** The LORD was pleased, for the sake of his righteousness, to make his instruction great and glorious.

**22** But this is a people plundered and looted, all of them trapped in holes[^20] and hidden away in prisons. They have become plunder with no one to rescue them, loot with no one to say, "Give it back!"

**23** Who among you will listen to this, pay attention and hear it for the time to come?

**24** Who handed Jacob over to be looted, and Israel to plunderers? Was it not the LORD, against whom we have sinned? They would not walk in his ways, and they did not obey his instruction.

**25** So he poured out on him his burning anger and the fury of battle. It set him on fire all around, but he did not understand; it burned him, but he did not take it to heart.

[^1]: Hebrew *avdi*, "my servant". Verses 1–9 are the first of four passages about the servant of the LORD that are often called the "Servant Songs" (42:1–9; 49:1–13; 50:4–11; 52:13–53:12; scholars count their limits differently). In the chapters around them the servant is Israel (41:8–9; 44:1–2), and 49:3 calls him "Israel", yet he also has a task toward Israel (49:5–6). He has been identified with the people of Israel, a faithful part of the people, the prophet himself, a king, or a figure still to come. The Septuagint reads "Jacob my servant … Israel my chosen one". The Aramaic Targum reads "my servant, the anointed one (Messiah)". In the New Testament the servant passages are applied to Jesus: Matthew 12:18–21 quotes verses 1–4, and the words from heaven at Jesus' baptism echo verse 1 (Mark 1:11).
[^2]: Literally "in whom my *nephesh* delights".
[^3]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^4]: Hebrew *mishpat*, "justice, judgment", the right order that God decrees. Also in verses 3–4.
[^5]: Or "In faithfulness he will bring out justice".
[^6]: A play on words: *lo yikheh*, "he will not grow dim", and *lo yaruts*, "he will not be bruised", echo the "smoldering" (*kehah*) wick and the "bruised" (*ratsuts*) reed of verse 3.
[^7]: Hebrew *iyyim*, the islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands. Also in verses 10 and 12.
[^8]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law". Also in verses 21 and 24. Matthew 12:21 quotes the Septuagint: "and in his name the nations will hope".
[^9]: Hebrew *ha-el YHWH*, "the God, the LORD". *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^10]: Or "for a righteous purpose". Hebrew *tsedeq*.
[^11]: Or "I will form you". The Hebrew form can come from either verb.
[^12]: Hebrew *berit am*, "a covenant of people", perhaps "a covenant for the people of Israel" or "a covenant for humankind". The phrase is repeated in 49:8. Simeon's words in Luke 2:32, "a light for revelation to the nations", echo this verse and 49:6.
[^13]: Compare Psalms 96:1 and 98:1.
[^14]: A tribe of the Arabian desert (21:16–17).
[^15]: Hebrew *sela*, "rock", perhaps the Edomite city of Sela (16:1), or "those who live among the rocks".
[^16]: Hebrew *iyyim*, elsewhere "coastlands" or "islands" (verse 10). Here it seems to mean dry land.
[^17]: Literally "I have done them". The future is spoken of as if it were already done.
[^18]: Hebrew *meshullam*, perhaps "the one in a covenant of peace with me", "the one made complete" or "the one paid for". The meaning is uncertain.
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "You have seen many things" but read "Seeing many things".
[^20]: Hebrew *ba-churim*. The same consonants can be read *bachurim*, "young men": "all their young men are trapped".
