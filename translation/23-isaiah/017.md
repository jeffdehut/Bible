**1** An oracle[^1] concerning Damascus: Look, Damascus will cease to be a city; it will become a heap of ruins.

**2** The cities of Aroer[^2] are abandoned; they will be for flocks, which will lie down with no one to frighten them.

**3** The fortress will disappear from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus. The remnant of Aram will be like the glory of the Israelites, declares Yahweh of Armies.[^3]

**4** On that day the glory of Jacob will fade, and the fat of his body will waste away.

**5** It will be as when a reaper gathers the standing grain and his arm harvests the ears, as when someone gleans ears of grain in the Valley of Rephaim.[^4]

**6** Gleanings will be left in it, as when an olive tree is beaten: two or three olives at the very top, four or five on its fruitful branches, declares Yahweh, the God of Israel.

**7** On that day people will look to their Maker, and their eyes will turn to the Holy One of Israel.

**8** They will not look to the altars, the work of their hands, or regard what their fingers have made, the Asherah poles[^5] and the incense altars.[^6]

**9** On that day their strong cities will be like the abandoned places of the woods and the hilltops,[^7] which they abandoned before the Israelites, and there will be desolation.

**10** For you have forgotten the God of your salvation and have not remembered the Rock of your refuge. Therefore, though you plant pleasant plants[^8] and set out cuttings for a foreign god,[^9]

**11** though on the day you plant them you make them grow, and in the morning you make your seed blossom, the harvest will vanish[^10] on the day of sickness and incurable pain.

**12** Woe to[^11] the many peoples who roar, who roar like the roaring of the seas, and the nations who rage, who rage like the raging of mighty waters!

**13** The nations rage like the raging of many waters, but he rebukes them, and they flee far away, chased like chaff on the mountains before the wind, like whirling dust before a storm.

**14** At evening, look, sudden terror! Before morning, they are gone. This is the portion of those who loot us, the lot of those who plunder us.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*. See 13:1. The oracle concerns both Damascus, capital of Aram, and its ally Ephraim, the northern kingdom of Israel (7:1–9).
[^2]: Aroer is known as a town on the Arnon in Moab (Deuteronomy 2:36). The name here may stand for towns near Damascus. The Septuagint reads "abandoned forever".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^4]: A fertile valley southwest of Jerusalem (2 Samuel 5:18).
[^5]: Hebrew *asherim*: wooden poles or trees that were symbols of the goddess Asherah.
[^6]: Hebrew *chammanim*, probably small altars for burning incense.
[^7]: The Septuagint reads "like the abandoned places of the Amorites and the Hivites", peoples who lived in Canaan before Israel.
[^8]: Hebrew *na'amanim*, "pleasant things". The word may refer to gardens planted in honor of a dying and rising god of fertility, whose title *na'aman*, "the pleasant one", was used of the god Adonis.
[^9]: Literally "a stranger".
[^10]: Or "the harvest will be a heap". The meaning of the Hebrew word *ned* here is uncertain.
[^11]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning, used by the prophets to announce disaster (see 1:4).
