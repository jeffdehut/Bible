**1** Woe to Ariel, Ariel,[^1] the city where David camped! Add year to year; let the festivals run their round.

**2** Yet I will bring distress on Ariel. There will be mourning and lamenting,[^2] and she will be to me like an altar hearth.[^3]

**3** I will camp against you all around;[^4] I will besiege you with towers and set up siege works against you.

**4** Brought low, you will speak from the ground, and your words will come muffled from the dust. Your voice will come from the ground like a ghost's,[^5] and your words will whisper from the dust.

**5** But the horde of your foes[^6] will be like fine dust, the horde of the ruthless like chaff blowing away. Suddenly, in an instant,

**6** you will be visited by Yahweh of Armies[^7] with thunder and earthquake and great noise, with whirlwind and storm and the flame of a devouring fire.

**7** The horde of all the nations that fight against Ariel, all who attack her and her stronghold and bring distress on her, will be like a dream, a vision in the night.

**8** It will be as when a hungry man dreams that he is eating, but he wakes up, and his hunger is not satisfied, or as when a thirsty man dreams that he is drinking, but he wakes up faint, and his thirst is not quenched.[^8] So it will be with the horde of all the nations that fight against Mount Zion.

**9** Be stunned and stay stunned; blind yourselves and be blind! Be drunk, but not with wine; stagger, but not with beer![^9]

**10** For Yahweh has poured out on you a spirit of deep sleep. He has shut your eyes, the prophets, and covered your heads, the seers.[^10]

**11** For you this whole vision has become like the words of a sealed scroll. When they give it to someone who can read[^11] and say, "Read this, please," he answers, "I cannot, for it is sealed."

**12** Or when they give the scroll to someone who cannot read and say, "Read this, please," he answers, "I cannot read."

**13** The Lord said: Because this people draw near with their mouth and honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me, and their fear of me is a human command learned by rote,[^12]

**14** therefore, look, I will again do amazing things with this people, wonder upon wonder. The wisdom of their wise will perish, and the understanding of their discerning will vanish.[^13]

**15** Woe to those who go to great depths to hide their plans from Yahweh, whose deeds are done in the dark, and who say, "Who sees us? Who knows us?"

**16** How you turn things upside down! Should the potter be thought of as the clay? Should what is made say of its maker, "He did not make me"? Should the pot say of the potter, "He has no understanding"?[^14]

**17** In a very little while, will not Lebanon be turned into a fruitful field, and the fruitful field be thought of as a forest?[^15]

**18** On that day the deaf will hear the words of a scroll, and out of gloom and darkness the eyes of the blind will see.

**19** The humble will again rejoice in Yahweh, and the poorest of people will exult in the Holy One of Israel.

**20** For the ruthless will be gone, the scoffer will come to an end, and all who watch for a chance to do evil will be cut off,

**21** those who make a person guilty with a word,[^16] who lay a trap for the one who argues for justice at the gate,[^17] and with empty claims[^18] turn aside the innocent.

**22** Therefore this is what Yahweh, who redeemed Abraham, says to the house of Jacob: Jacob will no longer be ashamed; his face will no longer grow pale.

**23** For when he sees his children, the work of my hands, in his midst, they will treat my name as holy. They will treat the Holy One of Jacob as holy and stand in awe of the God of Israel.

**24** Those who go astray in spirit will gain understanding, and those who grumble will accept instruction.

[^1]: A name for Jerusalem. Hebrew *ari'el* can mean "lion of God", but in Ezekiel 43:15–16 it is the "altar hearth" where sacrifices were burned (verse 2).
[^2]: A play on sounds: *ta'aniyyah va-aniyyah*.
[^3]: Hebrew *ari'el*. Jerusalem will become like the place of burning on the altar.
[^4]: Literally "like a circle". The Septuagint reads "like David", whose camp is named in verse 1.
[^5]: Hebrew *ov*, the spirit of a dead person called up by a medium (8:19), thought to speak in a faint whisper.
[^6]: Literally "your strangers".
[^7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^8]: Literally "his *nephesh* is empty … his *nephesh* is longing". Hebrew *nephesh* here means "throat, appetite".
[^9]: Plays on sounds: *hitmahmehu u-temahu, hishta'ash'u va-sho'u*.
[^10]: *The prophets* and *the seers* may explain what *your eyes* and *your heads* mean.
[^11]: Literally "who knows writing". The Hebrew text is written "who knows the scroll" but read "who knows writing".
[^12]: Literally "a commandment of human beings, taught". Jesus quotes verse 13 in Mark 7:6–7 and Matthew 15:8–9, following the Septuagint: "They worship me in vain, teaching human rules as if they were doctrines."
[^13]: Paul quotes the last part of this verse in 1 Corinthians 1:19, following the Septuagint.
[^14]: Compare 45:9 and Romans 9:20.
[^15]: Hebrew *karmel*, "garden land, orchard" (10:18). The great forest will become farmland, and farmland will grow wild, a reversal of the present order.
[^16]: Or "who condemn a person in a lawsuit". Hebrew *davar* means "word" or "case".
[^17]: The town gate was where cases were heard and judged.
[^18]: Hebrew *tohu*, "emptiness" (24:10).
