**1** The Spirit[^1] of the Lord GOD[^2] is on me, because the LORD has anointed me[^3] to bring good news[^4] to the poor. He has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim freedom[^5] for the captives and release for the prisoners,[^6]

**2** to proclaim the year of the LORD's favor and the day of vengeance of our God,[^7] to comfort all who mourn,

**3** to provide for those who mourn in Zion: to give them a garland instead of ashes,[^8] the oil of gladness instead of mourning, a garment of praise instead of a fainting spirit. They will be called oaks of righteousness, the planting of the LORD, so that he may be glorified.

**4** They will rebuild the ancient ruins and raise up the places long desolate. They will renew the ruined cities, the places desolate for generations.

**5** Strangers will stand and tend your flocks, and foreigners will be your plowmen and vinedressers.

**6** But you will be called priests of the LORD; you will be named ministers of our God.[^9] You will eat the wealth of the nations, and you will glory in their riches.

**7** Instead of your shame you will have a double portion, and instead of disgrace they will rejoice in their lot.[^10] Therefore in their land they will possess a double portion; everlasting joy will be theirs.

**8** For I, the LORD, love justice; I hate robbery and wrongdoing.[^11] I will faithfully give them their reward and make an everlasting covenant with them.

**9** Their offspring will be known among the nations, and their descendants among the peoples. All who see them will recognize that they are offspring the LORD has blessed.

**10** I will greatly rejoice in the LORD; my soul will exult in my God. For he has clothed me with garments of salvation and wrapped me in a robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom puts on a garland like a priest,[^12] and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.

**11** For as the earth brings out its sprouts, and as a garden makes what is sown in it spring up, so the Lord GOD will make righteousness and praise spring up before all the nations.

[^1]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath". Also in verse 3, "spirit".
[^2]: *The Lord GOD*: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". God's personal name, YHWH, is written GOD in capitals after "Lord", and "the LORD" elsewhere, as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *mashach*, "anoint", the verb from which *mashiach*, "anointed one" (45:1), comes. Kings and priests were anointed with oil; here the speaker is anointed by the Spirit for his task. The speaker is not named; he may be the prophet or the servant of the earlier chapters (42:1; 48:16; 50:4). In Luke 4:16–21 Jesus reads verses 1–2 in the synagogue at Nazareth, mostly from the Septuagint, and says, "Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing." He stops before "and the day of vengeance of our God".
[^4]: Hebrew *basar*, "bring good news" (40:9; 52:7).
[^5]: Hebrew *deror*, "release, liberty", the word used for the release proclaimed in the year of Jubilee (Leviticus 25:10).
[^6]: Hebrew *peqach-qoach*, probably "opening", that is, the opening of the prison, or of eyes long in darkness. The Septuagint reads "and recovery of sight to the blind", the reading in Luke 4:18, which also adds "to set the oppressed free" from 58:6.
[^7]: The *year of favor* recalls the Jubilee year (Leviticus 25:8–17), and the *day of vengeance* is the day when God puts things right (34:8; 63:4).
[^8]: A play on words: *pe'er*, "headdress, garland", and *efer*, "ashes", which mourners put on their heads (2 Samuel 13:19).
[^9]: Compare Exodus 19:6: "You will be to me a kingdom of priests."
[^10]: The Hebrew moves from "you" to "they" in this verse.
[^11]: The vowels of the Hebrew text read "robbery with a burnt offering", that is, offerings brought from stolen goods. The same consonants can be read *avlah*, "wrongdoing", as the Septuagint understood them.
[^12]: Literally "as a bridegroom acts as priest with a headdress", wearing a garland like the turban of a priest.
