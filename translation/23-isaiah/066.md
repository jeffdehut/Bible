**1** This is what Yahweh[^1] says: Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool. Where is the house you would build for me? Where is the place of my rest?

**2** My hand made all these things, and so they came to be,[^2] declares Yahweh. But this is the one I will look to: the one who is humble and contrite in spirit, and who trembles at my word.

**3** Whoever slaughters an ox is like one who kills a person; whoever sacrifices a lamb, like one who breaks a dog's neck; whoever presents a grain offering, like one who offers pig's blood; whoever burns incense as a memorial, like one who blesses an idol.[^3] They have chosen their own ways, and they delight in their detestable things.[^4]

**4** So I too will choose harsh treatment for them and bring on them what they dread. For I called, and no one answered; I spoke, and they did not listen. They did what was evil in my eyes and chose what does not please me.[^5]

**5** Hear the word of Yahweh, you who tremble at his word: Your brothers who hate you and cast you out for my name's sake have said, "Let Yahweh show his glory, so that we may see your joy!" But they will be put to shame.

**6** Listen! An uproar from the city! A sound from the temple! It is the sound of Yahweh repaying his enemies what they deserve.

**7** Before she went into labor, she gave birth; before her pains came on her, she delivered a son.

**8** Who has heard of such a thing? Who has seen such things? Can a land be born in one day, or a nation be brought forth in a moment? Yet as soon as Zion went into labor, she gave birth to her children.

**9** Shall I bring to the moment of birth[^6] and not cause delivery? says Yahweh. Shall I, who cause delivery, close the womb? says your God.

**10** Rejoice with Jerusalem and be glad for her, all you who love her! Rejoice greatly with her, all you who mourn over her,

**11** so that you may nurse and be satisfied at her comforting breasts, so that you may drink deeply and delight in her overflowing abundance.[^7]

**12** For this is what Yahweh says: Look, I will extend peace to her like a river, and the wealth of the nations like an overflowing stream. You will nurse and be carried on her hip and bounced on her knees.

**13** As a mother comforts her child,[^8] so I will comfort you; you will be comforted in Jerusalem.

**14** You will see, and your heart will rejoice; your bones will flourish like grass. The hand of Yahweh will be made known to his servants, but his fury to his enemies.

**15** For look, Yahweh will come in fire, and his chariots like the whirlwind, to repay his anger with fury and his rebuke with flames of fire.

**16** For with fire Yahweh will execute judgment, and with his sword, on all people,[^9] and those slain by Yahweh will be many.

**17** Those who consecrate and purify themselves to go into the gardens, following one in the middle,[^10] who eat the flesh of pigs and vile things and mice, will come to an end together, declares Yahweh.

**18** As for me, I know their deeds and their thoughts, and the time is coming to gather all nations and languages.[^11] They will come and see my glory.

**19** I will set a sign among them, and I will send survivors from them to the nations: to Tarshish, Pul and Lud (who draw the bow), to Tubal and Javan,[^12] to the distant coastlands[^13] that have not heard of my fame or seen my glory. They will declare my glory among the nations.

**20** They will bring all your brothers from all the nations as an offering[^14] to Yahweh, on horses, in chariots and wagons, on mules and on camels,[^15] to my holy mountain Jerusalem, says Yahweh, just as the Israelites bring a grain offering in a clean vessel to the house of Yahweh.

**21** And I will also take some of them to be priests and Levites,[^16] says Yahweh.

**22** For as the new heavens and the new earth that I am making will endure before me, declares Yahweh, so your offspring and your name will endure.

**23** From new moon to new moon, and from Sabbath to Sabbath, all people will come to bow down before me, says Yahweh.

**24** They will go out and look on the corpses of the people who rebelled against me. For their worm will not die, and their fire will not be quenched,[^17] and they will be a horror to all people.[^18]

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Stephen quotes verses 1–2 in Acts 7:49–50.
[^2]: The Septuagint reads "and all these things are mine".
[^3]: The Hebrew sets each pair side by side without "like": literally "one who slaughters the ox, one who strikes a man; one who sacrifices the lamb, one who breaks a dog's neck …". It may mean that their sacrifices are as unacceptable as murder and unclean offerings, or that the same people who sacrifice also commit these acts. *Idol* is Hebrew *aven*, "wickedness, nothingness" (41:29).
[^4]: Literally "their *nephesh* delights in their detestable things". Hebrew *shiqqutsim*, a word for idols and unclean things.
[^5]: The last two sentences repeat 65:12.
[^6]: Literally "Shall I bring to the breaking point", the word used in 37:3 for the moment of birth.
[^7]: Hebrew *ziz*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "abundance" or "breast". Literally "the abundance of her glory".
[^8]: Literally "As a man whom his mother comforts".
[^9]: Literally "all flesh". Also in verses 23 and 24.
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written with a masculine form but read with a feminine form of "one". The leader of the rites in the gardens (65:3) may have been a priest or priestess. The meaning is uncertain.
[^11]: The Hebrew is incomplete. Literally "And I, their deeds and their thoughts, coming to gather all the nations and languages". The verb "know" is not in the Hebrew, but is found in the Septuagint. *The time* is supplied: the Hebrew has only the feminine form "is coming".
[^12]: Distant peoples. *Tarshish* was a port in the far west (2:16); *Pul* is otherwise unknown as a land, and the Septuagint reads "Put", that is, Libya (Jeremiah 46:9); *Lud* is Lydia in Asia Minor, or a people of Africa; *Tubal* was in eastern Asia Minor; *Javan* is Greece (Ionia). For "who draw the bow" the Septuagint reads "Meshech", another people of Asia Minor (Ezekiel 27:13).
[^13]: Hebrew *iyyim*, the islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands.
[^14]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering, as later in this verse.
[^15]: Hebrew *kirkarot*, a word found only here, probably camels or other swift animals.
[^16]: *Some of them* may be the nations of verses 18–19 or the brothers they bring back in verse 20.
[^17]: Jesus quotes this line in Mark 9:48.
[^18]: Hebrew *dera'on*, "horror, abhorrence", found also in Daniel 12:2, of those who will rise "to everlasting contempt". When this chapter is read aloud in the synagogue, verse 23 is repeated after this verse, so that the reading, and the book, do not end on this note. The same is done at the end of Malachi, Lamentations and Ecclesiastes.
