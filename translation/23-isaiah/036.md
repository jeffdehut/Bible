**1** In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the fortified cities of Judah and captured them.[^1]

**2** The king of Assyria sent the Rabshakeh[^2] with a large army from Lachish[^3] to King Hezekiah at Jerusalem. He stood by the channel of the Upper Pool, on the road to the Launderer's Field.[^4]

**3** Eliakim son of Hilkiah, the palace administrator,[^5] Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder,[^6] went out to him.

**4** The Rabshakeh said to them, "Tell Hezekiah, 'This is what the great king, the king of Assyria, says: What is this confidence of yours? What are you trusting in?

**5** I say that your strategy and strength for war are mere words.[^7] Now, on whom are you relying, that you have rebelled against me?

**6** Look, you are relying on Egypt, that broken reed of a staff, which goes into the hand of anyone who leans on it and pierces it. That is what Pharaoh king of Egypt is to all who rely on him.

**7** But if you say to me, "We are trusting in the LORD[^8] our God," isn't he the one whose high places[^9] and altars Hezekiah has removed, saying to Judah and Jerusalem, "You must worship before this altar"?[^10]

**8** So now, make a wager with my master, the king of Assyria: I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able to put riders on them!

**9** How then can you drive back even one officer,[^11] one of the least of my master's servants, when you rely on Egypt for chariots and horsemen?

**10** And besides, have I come up against this land to destroy it without the LORD? The LORD himself said to me, "Go up against this land and destroy it."'"[^12]

**11** Then Eliakim, Shebna and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, "Please speak to your servants in Aramaic,[^13] for we understand it. Do not speak to us in the language of Judah[^14] in the hearing of the people on the wall."

**12** But the Rabshakeh said, "Was it to your master and to you that my master sent me to speak these words? Wasn't it to the men sitting on the wall, who, like you, will have to eat their own dung and drink their own urine?"[^15]

**13** Then the Rabshakeh stood and called out in a loud voice in the language of Judah. He said, "Hear the words of the great king, the king of Assyria!

**14** This is what the king says: Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to rescue you.[^16]

**15** Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD by saying, 'The LORD will surely rescue us; this city will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.'

**16** Do not listen to Hezekiah, for this is what the king of Assyria says: Make peace with me[^17] and come out to me. Then each of you will eat from his own vine and his own fig tree, and each of you will drink water from his own cistern,

**17** until I come and take you to a land like your own land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards.[^18]

**18** Do not let Hezekiah mislead you by saying, 'The LORD will rescue us.' Has any of the gods of the nations rescued his land from the hand of the king of Assyria?

**19** Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim?[^19] Did they rescue Samaria from my hand?

**20** Who among all the gods of these lands has rescued his land from my hand, that the LORD should rescue Jerusalem from my hand?"

**21** But they kept silent and did not answer him a word,[^20] for the king had commanded, "Do not answer him."

**22** Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, the palace administrator, Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder, came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn, and they told him the words of the Rabshakeh.

[^1]: In 701 BC. Chapters 36–39 are also found, with small differences, in 2 Kings 18:13–20:19, and parts of the story are told in 2 Chronicles 32. Where the Hebrew is the same, the English here follows the translation of 2 Kings. 2 Kings 18:14–16, Hezekiah's payment of tribute to Sennacherib, is not in Isaiah, and Hezekiah's psalm (38:9–20) is not in Kings.
[^2]: Hebrew *rav-shaqeh*, from the Assyrian title *rab shaqe*, "chief cupbearer", a high official who here speaks for the king. Also in verses 4, 11–13 and 22. 2 Kings 18:17 says that the Tartan (the commander-in-chief) and the Rab-saris (the chief official) were sent with him.
[^3]: A fortified city of Judah about 30 miles (50 km) southwest of Jerusalem, which Sennacherib was besieging.
[^4]: Traditionally "the Fuller's Field", a field outside the city where cloth was washed and bleached. It is the place where Isaiah met Ahaz (7:3).
[^5]: Literally "who was over the house", the title of the king's chief minister (22:15, 20–22). Also in verse 22.
[^6]: Hebrew *mazkir*, "one who brings to remembrance": a senior royal official, perhaps the king's herald or the keeper of records. Also in verse 22.
[^7]: Literally "I say: only a word of lips, counsel and strength for war." 2 Kings 18:20 reads "You say", that is, "You think that mere words are strategy and strength for war."
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^10]: 2 Kings 18:22 reads "before this altar in Jerusalem".
[^11]: Hebrew *pechah*, "governor".
[^12]: 2 Kings 18:25 reads "against this place" in the first sentence.
[^13]: The international language of diplomacy and trade in the Assyrian empire.
[^14]: Literally "in Judean", that is, Hebrew. Also in verse 13.
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written with coarse words for "dung" and "urine", but read with more polite ones, literally "excrement" and "the water of their feet".
[^16]: 2 Kings 18:29 reads "from his hand", that is, from the hand of the king of Assyria.
[^17]: Literally "Make a blessing with me", that is, surrender on terms.
[^18]: 2 Kings 18:32 continues: "a land of olive trees and honey, so that you may live and not die. Do not listen to Hezekiah, for he is misleading you when he says, 'the LORD will rescue us.'"
[^19]: Cities of Syria conquered by Assyria (10:9). 2 Kings 18:34 adds "Hena and Ivvah".
[^20]: 2 Kings 18:36 reads "But the people kept silent".
