**1** Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness, you who seek the LORD:[^1] Look to the rock from which you were cut and to the quarry from which you were dug.[^2]

**2** Look to your ancestor Abraham[^3] and to Sarah who gave birth to you. For he was only one when I called him, and I blessed him and made him many.

**3** For the LORD will comfort Zion; he will comfort all her ruins. He will make her wilderness like Eden, her desert like the garden of the LORD. Joy and gladness will be found in her, thanksgiving and the sound of singing.

**4** Pay attention to me, my people; listen to me, my nation. For instruction[^4] will go out from me, and I will set my justice as a light for the peoples.[^5]

**5** My righteousness draws near; my salvation has gone out, and my arms will judge the peoples. The coastlands[^6] will hope in me and wait for my arm.

**6** Lift up your eyes to the heavens, and look at the earth beneath. For the heavens will vanish like smoke, the earth will wear out like a garment, and its inhabitants will die like gnats.[^7] But my salvation will last forever, and my righteousness will never fail.

**7** Listen to me, you who know what is right, you people who have my instruction in your hearts. Do not fear the reproach of mere mortals, or be dismayed by their insults.

**8** For the moth will eat them like a garment, and the worm will eat them like wool. But my righteousness will last forever, and my salvation through all generations.

**9** Awake, awake, put on strength, arm of the LORD! Awake, as in days of old, in generations long ago. Was it not you who cut Rahab to pieces,[^8] who pierced the dragon?[^9]

**10** Was it not you who dried up the sea, the waters of the great deep, who made the depths of the sea a road for the redeemed to cross?[^10]

**11** Those whom the LORD has ransomed will return and come to Zion with singing. Everlasting joy will be on their heads. Gladness and joy will overtake them, and sorrow and sighing will flee away.[^11]

**12** I, I am he[^12] who comforts you. Who are you that you are afraid of mortals who die, of human beings who are made like grass?

**13** You have forgotten the LORD your Maker, who stretched out the heavens and laid the foundations of the earth. You live in constant fear all day long because of the fury of the oppressor, as he prepares to destroy. But where is the fury of the oppressor?

**14** The one bowed down in chains[^13] will soon be set free. He will not die and go down to the Pit,[^14] and he will not lack bread.

**15** For I am the LORD your God, who stirs up the sea so that its waves roar.[^15] The LORD of Armies[^16] is his name.

**16** I have put my words in your mouth and covered you with the shadow of my hand, to plant the heavens and lay the foundations of the earth, and to say to Zion, "You are my people."

**17** Wake up, wake up! Stand up, Jerusalem, you who have drunk from the hand of the LORD the cup of his fury, you who have drained to the dregs the bowl, the cup that makes people stagger.[^17]

**18** There is no one to guide her among all the children she bore; there is no one to take her by the hand among all the children she brought up.

**19** These two things have happened to you (who will grieve with you?): ruin and destruction, famine and sword. How can I comfort you?[^18]

**20** Your children have fainted; they lie at the head of every street like an antelope caught in a net. They are full of the fury of the LORD, the rebuke of your God.

**21** Therefore hear this, you afflicted one, drunk, but not with wine.

**22** This is what your Lord, the LORD, says, your God, who defends his people: Look, I have taken from your hand the cup that makes people stagger, the bowl, the cup of my fury. You will never drink it again.

**23** I will put it into the hands of your tormentors, who said to you,[^19] "Bow down, so that we may walk over you." And you made your back like the ground, like a street for them to walk over.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "to the hollow of the pit from which you were dug". The rock and the quarry are Abraham and Sarah (verse 2).
[^3]: Literally "Abraham your father".
[^4]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law". Also in verse 7.
[^5]: Hebrew *argia*, perhaps "I will establish" or "in a moment", joined to the next verse.
[^6]: Hebrew *iyyim*, the islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands.
[^7]: Hebrew *kemo-khen*, perhaps "like gnats" (*ken*) or "in the same way".
[^8]: Hebrew *Rahav*, a sea monster, a figure of the chaos that God overcame (Job 9:13; 26:12; Psalm 89:11; 89:10 in most English Bibles), and a poetic name for Egypt (30:7; Psalm 87:4). It is not the Rahab of Joshua 2, whose name is spelled differently in Hebrew.
[^9]: Hebrew *tannin*, "sea monster, dragon" (27:1). The defeat of the monsters of the sea is joined in verse 10 with the crossing of the Red Sea.
[^10]: Hebrew *ge'ulim*, those whom the LORD has rescued as next of kin (41:14).
[^11]: Verse 11 repeats 35:10.
[^12]: Hebrew *ani hu* (41:4). *You* is plural, then feminine singular ("Who are you"), then masculine singular in verse 13.
[^13]: Hebrew *tso'eh*, "one who stoops", perhaps a prisoner bent under his chains.
[^14]: Hebrew *shachat*, "pit, grave", the world of the dead.
[^15]: Or "who stills the sea when its waves roar". The same line is found in Jeremiah 31:35.
[^16]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^17]: Literally "the bowl of the cup of reeling you have drunk, you have drained". The cup stands for the judgment God gives his people to drink (Jeremiah 25:15–17; Psalm 75:9; 75:8 in most English Bibles).
[^18]: Literally "Who, I will comfort you?" The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) and the Septuagint read "Who will comfort you?"
[^19]: Literally "to your *nephesh*".
