**1** In the year that the commander-in-chief,[^1] sent by Sargon king of Assyria, came to Ashdod and fought against it and captured it,[^2]

**2** at that time the LORD[^3] spoke through Isaiah son of Amoz: "Go, take off the sackcloth from your waist and remove the sandals from your feet." And he did so, walking around stripped[^4] and barefoot.

**3** Then the LORD said, "Just as my servant Isaiah has walked stripped and barefoot for three years as a sign and a portent against Egypt and Cush,[^5]

**4** so the king of Assyria will lead away the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Cush, young and old, stripped and barefoot, with buttocks bared, to Egypt's shame.

**5** They will be dismayed and ashamed because of Cush, their hope, and Egypt, their pride.

**6** On that day the people who live on this coast will say, 'Look, this is what has happened to those we hoped in, to whom we fled for help, to be rescued from the king of Assyria! How then can we escape?'"

[^1]: Hebrew *tartan*, an Assyrian title for the army's chief commander (2 Kings 18:17).
[^2]: Ashdod, a Philistine city, rebelled against Assyria and was captured by Sargon II's army in 711 BC.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *arom*, "naked", here probably wearing only a loincloth, like a prisoner of war. Also in verses 3–4.
[^5]: The region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia), whose kings then ruled Egypt.
