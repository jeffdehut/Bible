**1** Let me sing for my beloved a song of my beloved about his vineyard:[^1] My beloved had a vineyard on a very fertile hill.[^2]

**2** He dug it up and cleared it of stones, and planted it with choice vines.[^3] He built a watchtower in the middle of it and also cut out a winepress in it. He waited for it to produce grapes, but it produced rotten grapes.[^4]

**3** So now, you who live in Jerusalem and you people of Judah, judge between me and my vineyard.

**4** What more could I have done for my vineyard that I have not done in it? When I waited for it to produce grapes, why did it produce rotten grapes?

**5** Now let me tell you what I am going to do to my vineyard: I will take away its hedge, and it will be grazed bare; I will break down its wall, and it will be trampled.

**6** I will make it a wasteland. It will not be pruned or hoed, and briers and thorns will grow up there. I will also command the clouds not to rain on it.

**7** For the vineyard of Yahweh[^5] of Armies is the house of Israel, and the people of Judah are the planting he delighted in. He waited for justice, but saw bloodshed; for righteousness, but heard an outcry.[^6]

**8** Woe to[^7] those who join house to house and add field to field, until there is no room left, and you are left to live alone in the middle of the land!

**9** Yahweh of Armies has sworn in my hearing:[^8] Many houses will surely become desolate, large and fine houses, with no one living in them.

**10** For ten acres[^9] of vineyard will produce only one bath,[^10] and a homer of seed will produce only an ephah.[^11]

**11** Woe to those who rise early in the morning to chase after beer, who stay up late at night till wine sets them on fire!

**12** They have lyre and harp, tambourine and flute, and wine at their feasts, but they pay no attention to the deeds of Yahweh, and they do not see the work of his hands.

**13** Therefore my people go into exile for lack of knowledge.[^12] Their nobles are starving,[^13] and their masses are parched with thirst.

**14** Therefore Sheol[^14] has opened its throat wide[^15] and opened its mouth beyond measure. Down go her splendor and her masses, her noisy crowd and all who revel in her.[^16]

**15** So humankind is brought low, and everyone is humbled, and the eyes of the proud are humbled.

**16** But Yahweh of Armies is exalted by justice, and the holy God shows himself holy by righteousness.

**17** Lambs will graze there as in their own pasture, and strangers will eat among the ruins of the rich.[^17]

**18** Woe to those who drag guilt along with cords of falsehood, and sin as if with cart ropes,

**19** who say, "Let him hurry; let him be quick with his work, so that we may see it! Let the plan of the Holy One of Israel come near and come true, so that we may know it!"

**20** Woe to those who call evil good and good evil, who make darkness light and light darkness, who make bitter sweet and sweet bitter!

**21** Woe to those who are wise in their own eyes and clever in their own sight!

**22** Woe to those who are heroes at drinking wine, and champions at mixing strong drinks,

**23** who acquit the guilty for a bribe and deny justice to the innocent!

**24** Therefore, as a tongue of fire devours straw and dry grass sinks down in the flame, their root will be like rot, and their blossom will blow away like dust. For they have rejected the instruction[^18] of Yahweh of Armies and despised the word of the Holy One of Israel.

**25** That is why the anger of Yahweh burns against his people. He has stretched out his hand against them and struck them down. The mountains shook, and their corpses were like garbage in the middle of the streets. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.[^19]

**26** He will raise a signal flag for the nations far away and whistle for them[^20] from the ends of the earth. And look, swiftly, speedily they come!

**27** None of them is weary, and none stumbles; none slumbers or sleeps. Not a belt is loose at the waist, not a sandal strap is broken.

**28** Their arrows are sharp, and all their bows are strung. Their horses' hooves seem like flint, and their chariot wheels like a whirlwind.

**29** Their roar is like a lion's; they roar like young lions. They growl and seize their prey and carry it off, and no one can rescue it.

**30** On that day they will growl over it like the growling of the sea. If anyone looks at the land, there is darkness and distress, and the light is darkened by its clouds.

[^1]: Or "my love song about his vineyard". Hebrew *dod*, "beloved", is the word used in the Song of Songs. The prophet sings as the friend of the vineyard's owner, who is revealed in verse 7.
[^2]: Literally "on a horn, a son of oil".
[^3]: Hebrew *soreq*, a choice variety of red grape (Jeremiah 2:21).
[^4]: Hebrew *be'ushim*, "stinking things": sour, rotten or wild grapes. Also in verse 4.
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^6]: A play on words that English cannot reproduce: he waited for *mishpat*, "justice", but saw *mispach*; for *tsedaqah*, "righteousness", but heard *tse'aqah*, the cry of the oppressed (Exodus 3:7, 9). *Mispach* is found only here; it probably means "bloodshed" or "lawlessness".
[^7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning, used by the prophets to announce disaster (see 1:4). It begins six woes in this chapter (verses 8, 11, 18, 20, 21 and 22).
[^8]: Literally "In my ears, Yahweh of Armies". The verb "has sworn" is not in the Hebrew but is implied by the oath that follows.
[^9]: Literally "ten yokes": the land that ten teams of oxen could plow in a day.
[^10]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters).
[^11]: A homer was ten ephahs (Ezekiel 45:11), probably about 220 liters (6 bushels). The harvest will be only a tenth of the seed sown.
[^12]: Or "without knowing it".
[^13]: Literally "Their glory are men of hunger". The same consonants can be read "dead from hunger".
[^14]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead, pictured here as a monster with an open mouth.
[^15]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here "throat, appetite".
[^16]: *Her*: Jerusalem's.
[^17]: Literally "the fat ones". The Septuagint reads "lambs" instead of "strangers". The meaning of the verse is uncertain.
[^18]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^19]: This refrain returns in 9:11, 16 and 20 (9:12, 17 and 21 in most English Bibles) and in 10:4.
[^20]: Literally "for it". The invader is singular in the Hebrew of verses 26–30, a single nation pictured as one army.
