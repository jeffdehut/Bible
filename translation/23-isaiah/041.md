**1** Be silent before me, you coastlands![^1] Let the peoples renew their strength! Let them come near and then speak; let us come together for judgment.[^2]

**2** Who has stirred up one from the east, whom victory meets at every step?[^3] He hands nations over to him and makes him trample kings.[^4] His sword makes them like dust, his bow like driven straw.

**3** He pursues them and passes on unharmed, by a path his feet have never traveled.[^5]

**4** Who has done this and carried it out, calling the generations from the beginning? I, Yahweh,[^6] am the first, and with the last I am he.[^7]

**5** The coastlands have seen it and are afraid; the ends of the earth tremble. They have drawn near and come.

**6** Each one helps his neighbor and says to his brother, "Be strong!"

**7** The craftsman encourages the goldsmith, and the one who smooths with the hammer encourages the one who strikes the anvil. He says of the soldering, "It is good." He fastens the idol with nails so that it will not topple.[^8]

**8** But you, Israel, my servant,[^9] Jacob, whom I have chosen, offspring of Abraham my friend,[^10]

**9** you whom I took hold of from the ends of the earth and called from its farthest corners, and to whom I said, "You are my servant; I have chosen you and have not rejected you":

**10** Do not be afraid, for I am with you; do not be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you; I will help you; I will uphold you with my righteous right hand.[^11]

**11** Look, all who rage against you will be put to shame and disgraced. Those who contend with you will be as nothing and will perish.

**12** You will look for those who quarrel with you, but you will not find them. Those who make war against you will be as nothing, nothing at all.

**13** For I, Yahweh your God, take hold of your right hand and say to you, "Do not be afraid; I will help you."

**14** Do not be afraid, you worm Jacob, you little band of Israel![^12] I will help you, declares Yahweh; your Redeemer[^13] is the Holy One of Israel.

**15** Look, I will make you into a threshing sledge,[^14] new and sharp, with many teeth. You will thresh the mountains and crush them, and make the hills like chaff.

**16** You will winnow them, and the wind will carry them away, and the storm will scatter them. But you will rejoice in Yahweh; you will glory in the Holy One of Israel.

**17** The poor and needy look for water, but there is none; their tongues are parched with thirst. I, Yahweh, will answer them; I, the God of Israel, will not abandon them.

**18** I will open rivers on the bare heights, and springs in the middle of the valleys. I will turn the wilderness into pools of water, and the dry land into springs of water.

**19** I will put in the wilderness the cedar, the acacia, the myrtle and the olive. I will set in the desert the juniper, the plane tree and the pine together,[^15]

**20** so that people may see and know, may consider and understand together, that the hand of Yahweh has done this, and the Holy One of Israel has created it.

**21** Present your case, says Yahweh; bring forward your arguments,[^16] says the King of Jacob.

**22** Let them come forward and tell us what is going to happen.[^17] Tell us the former things, what they were, so that we may consider them and know how they turned out. Or announce to us the things to come.

**23** Tell us what is coming hereafter, so that we may know that you are gods. Yes, do something, good or bad, so that we may be dismayed and see it together.[^18]

**24** Look, you are less than nothing, and your work is less than nothing.[^19] Whoever chooses you is detestable.

**25** I stirred up one from the north, and he has come, one from the rising of the sun who calls on my name.[^20] He will tread on rulers[^21] as on mortar, as a potter treads clay.

**26** Who announced this from the beginning, so that we could know, and beforehand, so that we could say, "He is right"? No one announced it; no one proclaimed it; no one heard any words from you.

**27** I was the first to say to Zion, "Look, here they are!" and I give Jerusalem a messenger of good news.[^22]

**28** But I look, and there is no one; among these there is no counselor who can give an answer when I ask them.

**29** Look, all of them are a delusion;[^23] their works are nothing; their cast images are wind and emptiness.[^24]

[^1]: Hebrew *iyyim*, the islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands. Also in verse 5.
[^2]: The nations and their gods are summoned to a court of law. The trial scene returns in verses 21–29.
[^3]: Literally "whom righteousness calls to his foot". Hebrew *tsedeq*, "righteousness", here the right that wins out, God's saving purpose carried out through the conqueror. Or "whom he calls in righteousness to his service". The conqueror from the east (and the north, verse 25) is Cyrus of Persia, named in 44:28 and 45:1.
[^4]: Or "subdues kings before him". The meaning of the Hebrew verb *yard* is uncertain.
[^5]: Literally "a path with his feet he does not come", perhaps "so swiftly that his feet hardly touch the path".
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^7]: Hebrew *ani hu*, "I am he", a solemn self-declaration of God found often in these chapters (43:10, 13; 46:4; 48:12; compare Deuteronomy 32:39). The Septuagint translates it here as *ego eimi*, "I am" (compare John 8:24, 28).
[^8]: Literally "fastens it". Verses 6–7 describe the makers of idols, who encourage one another as the nations draw near.
[^9]: Hebrew *eved*, "servant". In chapters 41–53 Yahweh often calls Israel "my servant" (44:1–2, 21; 45:4; 48:20). On the servant passages, see 42:1.
[^10]: Literally "who loved me". 2 Chronicles 20:7 and James 2:23 also call Abraham God's friend.
[^11]: Literally "the right hand of my righteousness". Or "my victorious right hand".
[^12]: Literally "you men of Israel". Hebrew *metim*, a word used of a small number of people (Deuteronomy 4:27). *You* is feminine in the Hebrew, matching *worm*.
[^13]: Hebrew *go'el*, a close relative with the duty to protect a family member in need: to buy back land a relative had sold, to free a relative sold into slavery, and to avenge a killing (Leviticus 25:25, 47–49; Numbers 35:19; Ruth 2:20). In chapters 41–63 it is a title of God, who acts as Israel's next of kin.
[^14]: A heavy board with sharp stones or iron set into its underside, dragged over grain to separate it from the husks.
[^15]: The identification of several of these trees is uncertain. *Olive* is literally "tree of oil", perhaps the wild olive or another tree that gives oil or resin; *juniper* (Hebrew *berosh*) may be the cypress (14:8); *plane tree* (*tidhar*) and *pine* (*te'ashur*) may be the elm and the cypress or box tree. The three trees are named together again in 60:13.
[^16]: Literally "your strong things".
[^17]: Yahweh challenges the gods of the nations to foretell the future.
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written with letters that may be read "and be afraid", but is read "and see".
[^19]: Hebrew *me'afa*, found only here, probably a form of *me'efes*, "less than nothing" (40:17).
[^20]: Or "who will be called by my name".
[^21]: Literally "He will come upon rulers". Hebrew *seganim*, "governors, officials", a word borrowed from Akkadian.
[^22]: The Hebrew of this verse is very short and difficult. Literally "First to Zion: Look, look at them! And to Jerusalem I give a bringer of good news." *Messenger of good news* is the word used in 40:9.
[^23]: Hebrew *aven*, "wickedness, trouble, nothingness".
[^24]: Hebrew *ruach va-tohu*, "wind and emptiness" (40:17).
