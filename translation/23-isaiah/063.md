**1** Who is this coming from Edom,[^1] in crimson garments from Bozrah,[^2] this one splendid in his clothing, striding forward in the greatness of his strength? It is I, speaking in righteousness, mighty to save.[^3]

**2** Why is your clothing red, and your garments like those of one who treads in the winepress?

**3** I have trodden the winepress alone; from the peoples no one was with me. I trod them in my anger and trampled them in my fury. Their lifeblood[^4] spattered my garments, and I stained all my clothing.[^5]

**4** For the day of vengeance was in my heart, and the year of my redemption had come.[^6]

**5** I looked, but there was no one to help; I was appalled that there was no one to support me. So my own arm brought me salvation, and my fury upheld me.[^7]

**6** I trampled the peoples in my anger and made them drunk in my fury, and I poured out their lifeblood on the ground.

**7** I will tell of the faithful love[^8] of Yahweh,[^9] the praises of Yahweh, for all that Yahweh has done for us, the great goodness to the house of Israel that he has shown them in his compassion and in his abundant faithful love.

**8** He said, "Surely they are my people, children who will not be false." So he became their Savior.

**9** In all their distress he too was distressed,[^10] and the angel of his presence[^11] saved them. In his love and in his pity he redeemed them; he lifted them up and carried them all the days of old.

**10** But they rebelled and grieved his holy Spirit.[^12] So he turned and became their enemy; he himself fought against them.

**11** Then his people remembered the days of old, the days of Moses:[^13] Where is he who brought them up out of the sea, with the shepherds of his flock? Where is he who put his holy Spirit among them,

**12** who sent his glorious arm to go at the right hand of Moses, who split the waters before them to make for himself an everlasting name,

**13** who led them through the depths? Like a horse in the wilderness, they did not stumble.

**14** Like cattle that go down into the valley, the Spirit of Yahweh gave them rest.[^14] So you led your people, to make for yourself a glorious name.

**15** Look down from heaven and see, from your holy and glorious dwelling. Where are your zeal and your mighty deeds? The yearning of your heart[^15] and your compassion are held back from me.

**16** For you are our Father, though Abraham does not know us and Israel does not acknowledge us. You, Yahweh, are our Father; "Our Redeemer from of old" is your name.[^16]

**17** Why, Yahweh, do you make us wander from your ways and harden our hearts so that we do not fear you? Return for the sake of your servants, the tribes of your inheritance.

**18** Your holy people possessed it for only a little while;[^17] our adversaries have trampled down your sanctuary.

**19** We have become like those you have never ruled, like those who were never called by your name. If only you would tear open the heavens and come down, so that the mountains would quake at your presence![^18]

[^1]: The land south and east of the Dead Sea (34:5–6). *Edom* sounds like *adom*, "red" (verse 2).
[^2]: The chief city of Edom (34:6). *Crimson* is literally "stained" or "bright red".
[^3]: The speaker who comes from Edom is Yahweh, and in verses 1–6 the prophet's questions and Yahweh's answers alternate.
[^4]: Hebrew *netsach*, here "juice, lifeblood", the red juice of the grapes. Also in verse 6.
[^5]: Revelation 14:19–20 and 19:13–15 echo verses 1–6.
[^6]: Or "the year of my redeemed ones had come". Hebrew *ge'ulai*.
[^7]: Compare 59:16.
[^8]: Hebrew *chasdei*, the plural of *chesed*, "acts of faithful love". Also at the end of the verse.
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^10]: Literally "to him was distress". The Hebrew text is written *lo*, "not", but read *lo*, "to him": the written text may mean "In all their distress he did not afflict them". The Septuagint reads "not an envoy or an angel, but he himself saved them".
[^11]: Literally "the angel of his face". Or "the messenger of his presence". Compare Exodus 23:20–23; 33:14–15.
[^12]: Literally "the spirit of his holiness" (Psalm 51:13, 51:11 in most English Bibles). Also in verse 11. Ephesians 4:30 echoes this line.
[^13]: Literally "And he remembered the days of old, Moses, his people". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^14]: Literally "gave him rest", that is, gave rest to the people.
[^15]: Literally "the stirring of your inner parts".
[^16]: Hebrew *go'el* (41:14).
[^17]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "For a little while they possessed your holy people".
[^18]: The second half of this verse, "If only you would tear open the heavens …", is 64:1 in most English Bibles, and 64:1–11 are 64:2–12. Compare Judges 5:5 and Psalm 18:10 (18:9 in most English Bibles).
