**1** For look, the Lord[^1] Yahweh of Armies[^2] is about to take away from Jerusalem and from Judah every support and supply,[^3] all supply of bread and all supply of water:

**2** the warrior and the soldier, the judge and the prophet, the diviner and the elder,

**3** the commander of fifty and the man of rank, the counselor, the skilled craftsman[^4] and the expert in charms.

**4** I will make boys their rulers, and the reckless[^5] will govern them.

**5** The people will oppress one another, each one his neighbor. The young will be insolent to the old, and the nobody to the honored.

**6** A man will take hold of his brother in his father's house and say, "You have a cloak; you be our ruler! Take charge of this heap of ruins!"[^6]

**7** On that day he will cry out, "I will not be a healer![^7] There is no bread and no cloak in my house. Do not make me ruler of the people."

**8** For Jerusalem has stumbled, and Judah has fallen, because their words and their deeds are against Yahweh, defying his glorious presence.[^8]

**9** The look on their faces testifies against them.[^9] They parade their sin like Sodom; they do not hide it. Woe to them![^10] They have brought disaster on themselves.

**10** Tell the righteous that it will go well with them, for they will eat the fruit of their deeds.

**11** Woe to the wicked! It will go badly with them, for they will be paid back for what their hands have done.

**12** My people! Children are their oppressors,[^11] and women rule over them.[^12] My people, your guides lead you astray; they have erased the path you should walk.[^13]

**13** Yahweh takes his place to bring charges; he stands to judge the peoples.[^14]

**14** Yahweh enters into judgment with the elders and leaders of his people: It is you who have stripped the vineyard bare.[^15] What you have stolen from the poor is in your houses.

**15** What do you mean by crushing my people and grinding the faces of the poor? declares the Lord Yahweh of Armies.

**16** Yahweh said: Because the daughters of Zion are proud and walk with outstretched necks, flirting with their eyes, tripping along with mincing steps, jingling the bangles on their feet,

**17** the Lord will cover the heads of the daughters of Zion with scabs, and Yahweh will lay bare their foreheads.[^16]

**18** On that day the Lord will take away their finery:[^17] the anklets, the headbands, the crescent ornaments,

**19** the pendants, the bracelets, the veils,

**20** the headdresses, the ankle chains, the sashes, the perfume boxes,[^18] the amulets,

**21** the signet rings, the nose rings,

**22** the festal robes, the capes, the shawls, the purses,

**23** the mirrors,[^19] the linen garments, the turbans and the veils.

**24** Instead of perfume there will be rot; instead of a sash, a rope; instead of well-set hair, baldness; instead of a rich robe, a wrapping of sackcloth; instead of beauty, branding.[^20]

**25** Your men will fall by the sword, and your warriors in battle.[^21]

**26** Her gates will lament and mourn; stripped bare, she will sit on the ground.

[^1]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name Yahweh. "The Lord" in verses 17 and 18 is *Adonai*.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 15.
[^3]: Hebrew *mash'en u-mash'enah*, the masculine and feminine forms of the same word, "staff": every kind of support.
[^4]: Or "the expert in magic arts".
[^5]: Hebrew *ta'alulim*, perhaps "caprice, whims", or "infants".
[^6]: Literally "and this ruin will be under your hand".
[^7]: Literally "a binder of wounds".
[^8]: Literally "rebelling against the eyes of his glory".
[^9]: Or "Their partiality testifies against them". The Hebrew phrase can mean "regard for faces", that is, favoritism in judgment.
[^10]: Hebrew *oy*, a cry of distress close in meaning to *hoy* (1:4). Literally "Woe to their souls!"
[^11]: Or "a child is their oppressor". The Hebrew word may also mean "one who gleans": "their oppressors strip them bare".
[^12]: The same consonants can be read "creditors" (*noshim*) instead of "women" (*nashim*), as the Septuagint understood them.
[^13]: Literally "they have swallowed up the way of your paths".
[^14]: The Septuagint reads "his people".
[^15]: Literally "burned" or "grazed bare".
[^16]: Or "their private parts". The meaning of the Hebrew word is uncertain.
[^17]: Many of the words in verses 18–23 are rare, and some of the items cannot be identified with certainty.
[^18]: Literally "houses of the *nephesh*", probably small bottles of perfume worn at the throat.
[^19]: Or "the papyrus garments", perhaps "the see-through garments".
[^20]: The Hebrew is short and abrupt: "for instead of beauty, branding". The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads "for instead of beauty, shame".
[^21]: *Your* is feminine singular: Zion is addressed as a woman.
