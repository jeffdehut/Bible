**1** The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light. On those who lived in the land of deep darkness[^1] a light has shone.

**2** You have made the nation great; you have increased its joy.[^2] They rejoice before you as people rejoice at harvest, as warriors rejoice when they divide the plunder.

**3** For the yoke that weighed them down, the bar across their shoulders, the rod of their oppressor, you have shattered, as on the day of Midian.[^3]

**4** For every boot of the warrior that tramped in the battle,[^4] and every garment rolled in blood, will be burned as fuel for the fire.

**5** For a child has been born to us, a son has been given to us, and the government will be on his shoulder. His name will be called[^5] Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.[^6]

**6** His government will grow,[^7] and there will be no end to peace, on the throne of David and over his kingdom, to establish it and uphold it with justice and righteousness from now on and forever. The zeal of the LORD[^8] of Armies will do this.

**7** The Lord has sent a word against Jacob, and it has fallen on Israel.

**8** All the people will know it, Ephraim and those who live in Samaria, who say in their pride and arrogance of heart,

**9** "The bricks have fallen, but we will rebuild with dressed stone. The sycamore-figs have been cut down, but we will put cedars in their place."

**10** So the LORD has raised up the adversaries of Rezin against him[^9] and stirred up his enemies:

**11** Aram from the east and the Philistines from the west[^10] have devoured Israel with open mouth. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.[^11]

**12** But the people have not turned back to him who struck them, nor have they sought the LORD of Armies.

**13** So the LORD cut off from Israel head and tail, palm branch and reed,[^12] in a single day.

**14** The elder and the man of rank are the head, and the prophet who teaches lies is the tail.

**15** Those who guide this people lead them astray, and those who are guided are swallowed up.[^13]

**16** That is why the Lord takes no pleasure in their young men and has no compassion on their fatherless and widows, for everyone is godless and an evildoer, and every mouth speaks folly. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.

**17** For wickedness burns like a fire; it devours briers and thorns. It sets the thickets of the forest ablaze, and they roll upward in a column of smoke.

**18** By the fury of the LORD of Armies the land is scorched,[^14] and the people are like fuel for the fire. No one spares his brother.

**19** They slice meat on the right but are still hungry; they eat on the left but are not satisfied. Each of them devours the flesh of his own arm.[^15]

**20** Manasseh devours Ephraim, and Ephraim Manasseh; together they turn against Judah. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.

[^1]: Verses 1–20 are 9:2–21 in most English Bibles. *Deep darkness* is Hebrew *tsalmavet*, traditionally "the shadow of death" (Psalm 23:4). Matthew 4:15–16 quotes this verse with 8:23 (9:1–2 in most English Bibles).
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "you have not increased the joy" but read "you have increased its joy". The two words sound the same (*lo*).
[^3]: When Gideon defeated Midian with a small band (Judges 7; Isaiah 10:26).
[^4]: Hebrew *se'on*, a word found only here, probably the boot of a soldier. Literally "every boot of one who tramps with noise".
[^5]: Or "has been called". The verbs in this verse are in the past tense in Hebrew: either a birth that has just happened, or the future described as if it had already taken place. Literally "and he has called his name", that is, "one has named him".
[^6]: Hebrew *pele yo'ets el gibbor avi'ad sar-shalom*. Each name is made of two words: "a wonder of a counselor", "a mighty God" or "God the warrior" (*el gibbor*, used of God in 10:21), "a father forever", "a prince of peace". In the ancient Near East a king could be given throne names at his crowning. Some read the words as one long name in the form of a sentence, like the names of Isaiah's sons (7:3; 8:3), describing God who gives the child. In Jewish interpretation the child was often taken to be King Hezekiah, while the Aramaic Targum takes the first names as names of God, who names the child "the anointed one (Messiah), in whose days peace will increase". Christian tradition reads the names as titles of Jesus the Messiah. The Septuagint reads "and his name is called Messenger of Great Counsel".
[^7]: Literally "to the increase of the government". The Hebrew text is written as two words, *lm rbh*, the first ending in the final form of the letter *mem*, but read as one word, *lemarbeh*, "to the increase".
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^9]: *Him* is Israel. Rezin of Aram was Israel's ally (7:1), but now the enemies of both are sent against them. The Hebrew is difficult.
[^10]: Literally "from in front … from behind": a person faced east to name the directions.
[^11]: This refrain first appears in 5:25 and returns in verses 16 and 20 and in 10:4.
[^12]: The top and bottom of society, as verse 14 explains.
[^13]: A play on words: *me'ashrim*, "those who guide", and *me'ushar*, "those who are guided" (3:12).
[^14]: Hebrew *ne'tam*, a word found only here. Perhaps "is burned" or "is shaken".
[^15]: That is, his own kin, as verse 20 shows.
