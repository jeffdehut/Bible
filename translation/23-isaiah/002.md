**1** This is the word that Isaiah son of Amoz saw concerning Judah and Jerusalem.

**2** In the days to come,[^1] the mountain of the LORD's[^2] house will be established as the highest of the mountains;[^3] it will be raised above the hills, and all the nations will stream to it.[^4]

**3** Many peoples will come and say, "Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob, so that he may teach us his ways and we may walk in his paths." For instruction[^5] will go out from Zion, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

**4** He will judge between the nations and settle disputes for many peoples. They will beat their swords into plowshares[^6] and their spears into pruning knives. Nation will not lift up sword against nation, and they will not learn war anymore.

**5** House of Jacob, come, let us walk in the light of the LORD!

**6** For you have abandoned your people, the house of Jacob, because they are full of things from the east[^7] and of fortune-tellers like the Philistines, and they strike hands with the children of foreigners.[^8]

**7** Their land is filled with silver and gold, and there is no end to their treasures. Their land is filled with horses, and there is no end to their chariots.

**8** Their land is filled with idols.[^9] They bow down to the work of their hands, to what their own fingers have made.

**9** So humankind is brought low, and everyone is humbled. Do not forgive them![^10]

**10** Go into the rock and hide in the dust from the terror of the LORD and from the splendor of his majesty.[^11]

**11** The proud eyes of humankind will be brought low, and human arrogance will be humbled. The LORD alone will be exalted on that day.

**12** For the LORD of Armies[^12] has a day against all that is proud and lofty, against all that is lifted up, and it will be brought low,

**13** against all the cedars of Lebanon, high and lifted up, and against all the oaks of Bashan,

**14** against all the high mountains and against all the lofty hills,

**15** against every high tower and against every fortified wall,

**16** against all the ships of Tarshish[^13] and against all the beautiful vessels.[^14]

**17** Human pride will be humbled, and human arrogance brought low. The LORD alone will be exalted on that day,

**18** and the idols will vanish completely.

**19** People will go into caves in the rocks and holes in the ground, from the terror of the LORD and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to make the earth tremble.[^15]

**20** On that day people will throw away to the moles[^16] and the bats their idols of silver and their idols of gold, which they made for themselves to worship,

**21** and go into the clefts of the rocks and the crevices of the cliffs, from the terror of the LORD and from the splendor of his majesty, when he rises to make the earth tremble.

**22** Stop trusting in human beings, who have only a breath in their nostrils. For what are they worth?[^17]

[^1]: Literally "at the end of the days". Verses 2–4 are found also in Micah 4:1–3, with small differences.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "at the head of the mountains".
[^4]: Or "will flow to it", like a river.
[^5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^6]: Hebrew *ittim*, probably iron plow points or hoes. Joel 4:10 (3:10 in most English Bibles) turns the image around: "Beat your plowshares into swords."
[^7]: Literally "they are full from the east", perhaps of practices of divination from the east. The Hebrew is difficult.
[^8]: Perhaps "they make deals with foreigners". The meaning of the verb is uncertain.
[^9]: Hebrew *elilim*, "worthless things, nothings", a word that sounds like *elohim*, "gods". Isaiah uses it often for idols.
[^10]: Literally "Do not lift them up". Or "You cannot raise them up".
[^11]: The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a), the oldest complete manuscript of Isaiah, does not have the last words of verse 9 ("Do not forgive them!") or verse 10.
[^12]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^13]: Large seagoing ships, of the kind that sailed to Tarshish, a distant port in the west, perhaps in Spain.
[^14]: Hebrew *sekhiyyot*, a word found only here. Perhaps "beautiful craft" or "fine images".
[^15]: A play on words: *la'arots ha'arets*, "to make the earth tremble". Also in verse 21.
[^16]: Hebrew *lachpor perot*, found only here, perhaps "to the burrowers": moles, rats or shrews.
[^17]: The Septuagint does not have this verse.
