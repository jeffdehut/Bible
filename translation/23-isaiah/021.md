**1** An oracle[^1] concerning the wilderness of the sea:[^2] Like whirlwinds sweeping through the Negev,[^3] it comes from the wilderness, from a terrifying land.

**2** A harsh vision has been shown to me: the traitor betrays, and the destroyer destroys. Go up, Elam! Lay siege, Media![^4] I put an end to all the groaning she caused.[^5]

**3** Therefore my body[^6] is filled with anguish; pains have seized me like the pains of a woman in labor. I am bent double by what I hear; I am terrified by what I see.

**4** My mind reels; horror overwhelms me. The twilight I longed for has become a terror to me.

**5** They set the table, they spread the rugs,[^7] they eat, they drink! Get up, you officers, oil the shields!

**6** For this is what the Lord said to me: "Go, post a lookout; let him report what he sees.

**7** When he sees riders, horsemen in pairs, riders on donkeys, riders on camels, let him pay attention, close attention."

**8** Then he called out like a lion:[^8] "Lord,[^9] I stand on the watchtower continually by day, and I am stationed at my post every night.

**9** Look, here comes a man in a chariot, with horsemen in pairs!" Then he answered, "Fallen, fallen is Babylon! All the images of her gods lie shattered on the ground!"[^10]

**10** My people, threshed and winnowed on my threshing floor,[^11] what I have heard from Yahweh of Armies,[^12] the God of Israel, I have told you.

**11** An oracle concerning Dumah:[^13] Someone calls to me from Seir, "Watchman, what is left of the night? Watchman, what is left of the night?"

**12** The watchman says, "Morning is coming, but also the night. If you want to ask, ask; come back again."[^14]

**13** An oracle concerning Arabia:[^15] You caravans of Dedanites, who camp in the thickets of Arabia,

**14** bring water to meet the thirsty! You who live in the land of Tema, meet the fugitives with bread.

**15** For they have fled from the swords, from the drawn sword, from the bent bow, and from the heat of battle.

**16** For this is what the Lord said to me: "Within one year, counted as a hired worker counts it,[^16] all the glory of Kedar[^17] will come to an end.

**17** The bows of Kedar's warriors that remain will be few." For Yahweh, the God of Israel, has spoken.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*. See 13:1. Also in verses 11 and 13.
[^2]: Hebrew *midbar-yam*. The oracle concerns Babylon (verse 9), which lay near the marshes at the head of the Persian Gulf, a region the Assyrians called "the Sealand". The Septuagint reads only "the wilderness".
[^3]: The dry southern region of Judah.
[^4]: Elam and Media, peoples to the east of Babylon (11:11; 13:17).
[^5]: Literally "all her groaning", that is, the groaning Babylon has caused, or perhaps Babylon's own groaning.
[^6]: Literally "my loins".
[^7]: Or "they set the watch". The meaning of the Hebrew phrase *tsafoh ha-tsafit* is uncertain.
[^8]: Literally "And a lion called out". The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads "And the lookout called out".
[^9]: Or "my lord". Hebrew *Adonai*.
[^10]: Compare Revelation 14:8; 18:2.
[^11]: Literally "my threshed one and son of my threshing floor": Israel, beaten and sifted by its enemies.
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^13]: *Dumah*, "silence", is an oasis in northern Arabia (Genesis 25:14). Here it may also be a play on *Edom*, since Seir is the mountain country of Edom. The Septuagint reads "Idumea", that is, Edom.
[^14]: The verbs "ask" and "come" are in forms close to Aramaic, perhaps imitating the speech of the questioner.
[^15]: Or "An oracle in the steppe". Hebrew *ba'rav*. Dedan and Tema were oasis towns of northern Arabia.
[^16]: Literally "like the years of a hired worker", that is, exactly (16:14).
[^17]: A tribe of the Arabian desert (Genesis 25:13; Psalm 120:5).
