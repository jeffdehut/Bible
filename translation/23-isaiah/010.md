**1** Woe to[^1] those who make unjust laws,[^2] and to the writers who keep writing oppression,

**2** to deprive the poor of justice and rob the needy among my people of their rights, so that widows become their plunder and they prey on the fatherless!

**3** What will you do on the day of reckoning,[^3] when ruin comes from far away? To whom will you run for help, and where will you leave your wealth?[^4]

**4** Nothing will be left but to crouch among the prisoners or to fall among the slain. Yet for all this his anger has not turned away; his hand is still stretched out.[^5]

**5** Woe to Assyria, the rod of my anger! The club in their hand is my fury.

**6** I send him against a godless nation; I command him to go against the people who provoke my wrath,[^6] to take spoil and seize plunder, and to trample them like mud in the streets.

**7** But this is not what he intends; this is not what he has in mind. His purpose is to destroy, to cut off nations, and not a few.

**8** For he says, "Are not my commanders all kings?

**9** Is not Calno like Carchemish? Is not Hamath like Arpad? Is not Samaria like Damascus?[^7]

**10** As my hand has reached the kingdoms of the idols,[^8] whose images were greater than those of Jerusalem and Samaria,

**11** shall I not do to Jerusalem and her images as I have done to Samaria and her idols?"

**12** When the Lord has finished all his work on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, I will punish the fruit of the king of Assyria's proud heart and the haughty look in his eyes.[^9]

**13** For he says: "By the strength of my hand I have done this, and by my wisdom, for I have understanding. I have removed the boundaries of peoples and plundered their treasures; like a mighty one I have brought down those who sat on thrones.[^10]

**14** My hand has found the wealth of the peoples like a nest, and as one gathers abandoned eggs, I have gathered all the earth. Not one flapped a wing or opened its mouth to chirp."

**15** Does the ax boast over the one who chops with it? Does the saw make itself greater than the one who saws with it? As if a rod could wield the one who lifts it, or a staff could lift the one who is not wood!

**16** Therefore the Lord[^11] GOD of Armies[^12] will send a wasting disease among his stout warriors, and under his glory a fire will be kindled, burning like a fire.

**17** The light of Israel will become a fire, and his Holy One a flame. It will burn and devour his thorns and his briers in a single day.

**18** The glory of his forest and his fruitful land[^13] it will destroy, both soul and body,[^14] and it will be as when a sick man wastes away.[^15]

**19** The trees left in his forest will be so few that a child could write them down.

**20** On that day the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob will no longer lean on the one who struck them, but will truly lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel.

**21** A remnant will return,[^16] the remnant of Jacob, to the Mighty God.[^17]

**22** For even if your people, Israel, were like the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. Destruction has been decreed, overflowing with righteousness.[^18]

**23** For the Lord GOD of Armies will carry out the destruction he has decreed in the middle of all the earth.[^19]

**24** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD of Armies says: My people who live in Zion, do not be afraid of Assyria, though he strikes you with a rod and lifts up his staff against you, as Egypt did.[^20]

**25** For in a very little while my fury will come to an end, and my anger will turn to their destruction.

**26** The LORD of Armies will raise a whip against him, as when he struck Midian at the rock of Oreb.[^21] His staff will be over the sea, and he will lift it up as he did in Egypt.[^22]

**27** On that day his burden will be lifted from your shoulder, and his yoke from your neck, and the yoke will be broken because of fatness.[^23]

**28** He has come to Aiath; he has passed through Migron; at Michmash he leaves his baggage.[^24]

**29** They have crossed the pass: "Geba is our camp for the night!" Ramah trembles; Gibeah of Saul has fled.

**30** Cry aloud, Daughter Gallim! Listen, Laishah! Poor Anathoth![^25]

**31** Madmenah is in flight; the people of Gebim take refuge.

**32** This very day he will halt at Nob. He shakes his fist at the mount of Daughter Zion,[^26] the hill of Jerusalem.

**33** Look, the Lord GOD of Armies will lop off the branches with terrifying power. The tallest trees will be cut down, and the lofty will be brought low.

**34** He will cut down the thickets of the forest with an ax,[^27] and Lebanon will fall before the Mighty One.[^28]

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning, used by the prophets to announce disaster (see 1:4). Also in verse 5.
[^2]: Literally "who decree decrees of wickedness".
[^3]: Literally "the day of visitation", when God comes to call to account.
[^4]: Literally "your glory".
[^5]: The refrain of 5:25 and 9:11, 16 and 20 (9:12, 17 and 21 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Literally "the people of my fury".
[^7]: Cities of Syria and northern Mesopotamia that Assyria conquered in the eighth century BC. Samaria fell in 722 BC.
[^8]: Hebrew *elil*, "worthless thing" (see 2:8). Also in verse 11.
[^9]: The LORD speaks of himself as *the Lord* and then as *I*.
[^10]: Or "I have brought down the inhabitants". Hebrew *yoshevim* means both "those who sit" and "those who live".
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name YHWH. Also in verse 33. "The Lord" in verses 12, 23 and 24 is *Adonai*.
[^12]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^13]: Hebrew *karmel*, "garden land, orchard".
[^14]: Literally "from *nephesh* to flesh", that is, completely.
[^15]: Hebrew *kimsos noses*, words found only here. Perhaps "as a standard-bearer faints".
[^16]: Hebrew *she'ar yashuv*, the name of Isaiah's son Shear-jashub (7:3). *Return* can also mean "turn back" to God.
[^17]: Hebrew *el gibbor*, the title given to the child in 9:5 (9:6 in most English Bibles).
[^18]: Romans 9:27–28 quotes verses 22–23 from the Septuagint.
[^19]: Or "of all the land".
[^20]: Literally "in the way of Egypt", as Egypt oppressed Israel before the exodus.
[^21]: Where the Midianite leader Oreb was killed (Judges 7:25).
[^22]: As Moses' staff was raised over the Red Sea (Exodus 14:16).
[^23]: Or "because of the oil". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain: perhaps the ox has grown too fat for the yoke.
[^24]: Verses 28–32 trace an enemy army marching on Jerusalem from the north. The towns all lie within about 10 miles (16 km) of the city.
[^25]: A play on words: *aniyyah anatot*, "poor Anathoth". *Gallim* sounds like "waves", and *Laishah* like "lion".
[^26]: The Hebrew text is written "the house of Zion" but read "Daughter Zion".
[^27]: Literally "with iron".
[^28]: Or "by a mighty one". *Lebanon*, with its great cedars, stands for the proud army of Assyria.
