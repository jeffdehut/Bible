**1** Look, the LORD[^1] is about to empty the earth and lay it waste.[^2] He will twist its surface and scatter its inhabitants.

**2** It will be the same for people and priest, for servant and master, for maid and mistress, for buyer and seller, for lender and borrower, for creditor and debtor.

**3** The earth will be utterly emptied and utterly plundered, for the LORD has spoken this word.

**4** The earth dries up[^3] and withers; the world languishes and withers; the highest of the people of the earth languish.

**5** The earth is defiled under its inhabitants, for they have broken the laws, violated the decree and broken the everlasting covenant.[^4]

**6** Therefore a curse devours the earth, and its people bear their guilt. Therefore the inhabitants of the earth are burned up,[^5] and few people are left.

**7** The new wine dries up; the vine languishes. All the merrymakers groan.

**8** The joyful sound of tambourines has stopped; the noise of the revelers has ended; the joyful sound of the lyre has stopped.

**9** They no longer drink wine with a song; beer is bitter to those who drink it.

**10** The city of chaos[^6] is broken down; every house is shut up so that no one can enter.

**11** In the streets there is an outcry over wine; all joy has turned to gloom; the gladness of the earth has gone into exile.

**12** Desolation is left in the city, and its gate is battered to ruins.

**13** For so it will be in the middle of the earth, among the peoples: as when an olive tree is beaten, as at the gleaning when the grape harvest is over.

**14** They raise their voices and shout for joy; they cry out from the west[^7] over the majesty of the LORD.

**15** Therefore in the east[^8] give glory to the LORD; in the coastlands of the sea, give glory to the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

**16** From the ends of the earth we hear songs: "Glory[^9] to the Righteous One!" But I said, "I waste away, I waste away![^10] Woe is me! The traitors betray; with treachery the traitors betray!"[^11]

**17** Terror and pit and snare are upon you, you who live on the earth![^12]

**18** Whoever flees at the sound of terror will fall into the pit, and whoever climbs out of the pit will be caught in the snare. For the windows of heaven are opened,[^13] and the foundations of the earth shake.

**19** The earth is utterly broken; the earth is split apart; the earth is violently shaken.

**20** The earth staggers like a drunkard; it sways like a hut.[^14] Its rebellion lies heavy on it; it falls and will not rise again.

**21** On that day the LORD will punish the host of heaven in heaven,[^15] and the kings of the earth on the earth.

**22** They will be gathered together like prisoners in a pit; they will be shut up in a prison, and after many days they will be punished.[^16]

**23** The moon will be ashamed and the sun abashed,[^17] for the LORD of Armies[^18] will reign on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, and his glory will be before his elders.[^19]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Chapters 24–27 are often called the "Isaiah Apocalypse": poems about the judgment of the whole earth and the final triumph of God.
[^2]: A play on sounds: *boqeq … u-voleqah*, "empty … and lay waste". Similar sound-plays run through verses 3, 4, 16, 17 and 19.
[^3]: Or "mourns". Hebrew *avlah*, also in verse 7.
[^4]: Perhaps the covenant with Noah and all living things (Genesis 9:16). *Laws* is Hebrew *torot*, "instructions"; *decree* is *choq*.
[^5]: Or "dwindle". The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain.
[^6]: Hebrew *tohu*, the word for the "formless" earth in Genesis 1:2. The city is not named.
[^7]: Literally "from the sea", the Mediterranean, to the west of Israel.
[^8]: Hebrew *ba'urim*, perhaps "in the regions of light", where the sun rises. The meaning is uncertain.
[^9]: Or "Beauty". Hebrew *tsevi*.
[^10]: Hebrew *razi li*, a phrase of uncertain meaning, perhaps "leanness to me". The Vulgate reads "My secret is mine".
[^11]: A play on words: *bogdim bagadu u-veged bogdim bagadu*.
[^12]: A play on sounds: *pachad va-pachat va-fach*. Jeremiah 48:43–44 repeats verses 17–18.
[^13]: As in the flood (Genesis 7:11).
[^14]: A watchman's hut in a field (1:8).
[^15]: Literally "the army of the height in the height": the heavenly powers, perhaps the stars, or the beings thought to rule the nations.
[^16]: Or "visited". Hebrew *paqad* (verse 21).
[^17]: Hebrew *levanah*, "the white one", and *chammah*, "the hot one", poetic names for the moon and the sun.
[^18]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^19]: Literally "and before his elders, glory". Compare the elders who saw the God of Israel on Mount Sinai (Exodus 24:9–11).
