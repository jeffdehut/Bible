**1** Awake, awake, put on your strength, Zion! Put on your beautiful garments, Jerusalem, the holy city! For the uncircumcised and the unclean will never enter you again.

**2** Shake yourself free from the dust; rise up and take your seat, Jerusalem![^1] Loose the bonds from your neck,[^2] captive Daughter Zion!

**3** For this is what the LORD[^3] says: You were sold for nothing, and you will be redeemed without silver.

**4** For this is what the Lord GOD says: At first my people went down to Egypt to live there as foreigners; then Assyria oppressed them for nothing.

**5** And now what do I have here?[^4] declares the LORD. For my people have been taken away for nothing. Their rulers wail,[^5] declares the LORD, and all day long my name is continually despised.[^6]

**6** Therefore my people will know my name; therefore on that day they will know that I am he[^7] who speaks: Here I am.

**7** How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of the one who brings good news,[^8] who proclaims peace, who brings good news of good things, who proclaims salvation, who says to Zion, "Your God reigns!"[^9]

**8** Listen! Your watchmen raise their voices; together they shout for joy. For with their own eyes[^10] they will see the LORD return to Zion.

**9** Break into singing together, you ruins of Jerusalem! For the LORD has comforted his people; he has redeemed Jerusalem.

**10** The LORD has bared his holy arm before the eyes of all the nations, and all the ends of the earth will see the salvation of our God.

**11** Depart, depart, go out from there! Touch nothing unclean. Go out from the middle of her; purify yourselves, you who carry the vessels of the LORD.[^11]

**12** But you will not go out in haste or leave in flight.[^12] For the LORD will go before you, and the God of Israel will be your rear guard.

**13** Look, my servant will act wisely;[^13] he will be raised and lifted up and greatly exalted.[^14]

**14** Just as many were appalled at you,[^15] so disfigured was his appearance, beyond that of any man, and his form beyond that of human beings,[^16]

**15** so he will sprinkle many nations.[^17] Kings will shut their mouths because of him. For what they were not told, they will see, and what they have not heard, they will understand.[^18]

[^1]: Hebrew *shevi*, "sit", that is, sit enthroned. The same Hebrew word can mean "captivity", and many translate "rise up, captive Jerusalem", matching "captive Daughter Zion" at the end of the verse.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "the bonds of your neck have come loose" but read "Loose the bonds from your neck".
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "Who do I have here?" but read "What do I have here?"
[^5]: Or "Their rulers boast" or "mock". Hebrew *yehelilu*.
[^6]: Romans 2:24 quotes this line from the Septuagint: "Because of you my name is despised among the nations."
[^7]: Hebrew *ani hu* (41:4).
[^8]: Hebrew *mevasser*, from *basar*, "bring good news" (40:9). Also later in this verse. Romans 10:15 quotes the first part of this verse.
[^9]: Or "Your God has become king!" Hebrew *malakh*, as in "The LORD reigns!" (Psalm 93:1; 96:10; 97:1; 99:1).
[^10]: Literally "eye to eye".
[^11]: The vessels of the temple, which Nebuchadnezzar had carried off to Babylon (Ezra 1:7–11). 2 Corinthians 6:17 quotes this verse.
[^12]: Unlike the exodus from Egypt, when Israel left in haste (Exodus 12:11; Deuteronomy 16:3).
[^13]: Or "will prosper". Hebrew *yaskil* means both "act with insight" and "succeed". 52:13–53:12 is the fourth of the servant passages (see 42:1). The Aramaic Targum begins "Look, my servant the anointed one (Messiah) will prosper", but it applies the suffering in the passage to Israel and the nations rather than to the Messiah. In the New Testament the passage is applied to the suffering and death of Jesus, and many Jewish interpreters have understood the servant as Israel, the righteous within Israel, or the prophet.
[^14]: Literally "he will be high and lifted up and very lofty". The words "high and lifted up" are used of the LORD on his throne in 6:1 and 57:15.
[^15]: The Syriac and the Aramaic Targum read "at him". The Hebrew moves between "you" and "him".
[^16]: Hebrew *mishchat*, a word found only here, probably "disfigurement". The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) has *mashachti*, which may be read "I anointed". The meaning of verses 14–15 is uncertain in places.
[^17]: Hebrew *yazzeh*, "he will sprinkle", the verb used for the priest's sprinkling of blood or water to cleanse (Leviticus 4:6; 16:14; Numbers 19:18). The Septuagint reads "so many nations will marvel at him".
[^18]: Romans 15:21 quotes the end of this verse from the Septuagint.
