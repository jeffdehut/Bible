**1** In those days Hezekiah became ill and was at the point of death. The prophet Isaiah son of Amoz came to him and said to him, "This is what the LORD[^1] says: Give instructions to your household,[^2] for you are going to die. You will not recover."

**2** Then Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD.

**3** He said, "Please, LORD, remember how I have walked before you faithfully and with my whole heart, and have done what is good in your eyes." And Hezekiah wept bitterly.

**4** Then the word of the LORD came to Isaiah:[^3]

**5** "Go and say to Hezekiah, 'This is what the LORD, the God of your ancestor[^4] David, says: I have heard your prayer. I have seen your tears. I am going to add fifteen years to your life.

**6** I will rescue you and this city from the power of the king of Assyria, and I will defend this city.[^5]

**7** This is the sign to you from the LORD that the LORD will do what he has promised:[^6]

**8** I am going to make the shadow on the stairway of Ahaz,[^7] which has gone down with the sun, go back ten steps.'" So the sun went back ten steps on the stairway it had gone down.

**9** A writing[^8] of Hezekiah king of Judah, after he had been ill and had recovered from his illness:

**10** I said, "In the prime of my days[^9] I must go through the gates of Sheol;[^10] I am robbed of the rest of my years."[^11]

**11** I said, "I will not see the LORD,[^12] the LORD in the land of the living. I will no longer look on any human being, when I am among those who live where life has ceased."[^13]

**12** My dwelling[^14] is pulled up and taken away from me like a shepherd's tent. Like a weaver I have rolled up my life; he cuts me off from the loom.[^15] From day to night you bring me to an end.[^16]

**13** I composed myself until morning,[^17] but like a lion he breaks all my bones. From day to night you bring me to an end.

**14** Like a swift or a thrush[^18] I chirp; I moan like a dove. My eyes grow weak from looking upward. Lord,[^19] I am oppressed; be my security![^20]

**15** What can I say? He has spoken to me, and he himself has done it. I will walk slowly[^21] all my years because of the bitterness of my soul.

**16** Lord, by these things people live, and in all of them is the life of my spirit. You restore my health and let me live![^22]

**17** Look, it was for my good that I had such bitter grief.[^23] In your love you have kept my life from the pit of destruction,[^24] for you have thrown all my sins behind your back.

**18** For Sheol cannot thank you; death cannot praise you. Those who go down to the Pit[^25] cannot hope for your faithfulness.

**19** The living, the living, they thank you, as I do today. Fathers make known your faithfulness to their children.

**20** The LORD will save me,[^26] and we will play my songs on stringed instruments all the days of our lives in the house of the LORD.

**21** Isaiah said, "Let them take a cake of figs and spread it on the boil, and he will recover."[^27]

**22** And Hezekiah said, "What is the sign that I will go up to the house of the LORD?"

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Verses 1–8 and 21–22 are also found, with differences, in 2 Kings 20:1–11. Hezekiah's psalm (verses 9–20) is found only in Isaiah.
[^2]: Literally "Command your house".
[^3]: 2 Kings 20:4 reads "Isaiah had not yet gone out of the middle courtyard when the word of the LORD came to him".
[^4]: Literally "your father". 2 Kings 20:5–6 adds "the ruler of my people" after "Hezekiah", and "I am going to heal you. On the third day you will go up to the house of the LORD" before "I am going to add".
[^5]: 2 Kings 20:6 adds "for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David" (as in 37:35).
[^6]: In 2 Kings 20:8–11 Hezekiah first asks for a sign (compare verse 22), and Isaiah lets him choose whether the shadow will go forward or back.
[^7]: Literally "the shadow of the steps, which has gone down on the steps of Ahaz". The "steps" were probably those of a stairway whose shadow marked the time of day. The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads "the steps of the upper room of Ahaz" (compare 2 Kings 23:12).
[^8]: Hebrew *mikhtav*. Some scholars read *miktam*, a term found in the titles of Psalms 16 and 56–60, with a small change to the Hebrew. The psalm in verses 10–20 has many rare words, and its meaning is uncertain in places.
[^9]: Hebrew *bidmi yamai*, perhaps "in the quiet of my days" or "in the middle of my days" (compare Psalm 102:25; 102:24 in most English Bibles).
[^10]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. Also in verse 18.
[^11]: Or "I am consigned there for the rest of my years".
[^12]: *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH (12:2).
[^13]: Literally "with the inhabitants of cessation". Some scholars read *cheled*, "the world", with a small change to the Hebrew: "with those who live in the world".
[^14]: Hebrew *dor*, usually "generation", here probably "dwelling".
[^15]: Literally "from the thrum", the threads that held the woven cloth to the loom. The weaver cut them when the cloth was finished.
[^16]: That is, between morning and evening, before the day is out. Also in verse 13.
[^17]: Or "I waited quietly until morning". Some scholars read *shivva'ti*, "I cried for help", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^18]: Hebrew *sus agur*. *Sus* (written for *sis*) and *agur* are birds named together in Jeremiah 8:7. Their identification is uncertain.
[^19]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH. Also in verse 16.
[^20]: Literally "Lord, oppression is mine; stand surety for me!", that is, take up my debt and my cause.
[^21]: Hebrew *eddaddeh*, perhaps "I will walk humbly" or "I will wander". The meaning is uncertain.
[^22]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "Lord, on them they live, and for all in them the life of my spirit, and you restore me and make me live." It may also be read as a prayer: "Restore my health and let me live!"
[^23]: Literally "Look, for peace it was bitter to me, bitter".
[^24]: Literally "you loved my life from the pit of nothingness".
[^25]: Hebrew *bor*, "pit, cistern", a name for the grave and the world of the dead.
[^26]: Literally "The LORD, to save me", perhaps "The LORD is ready to save me".
[^27]: Verses 21–22 are found in 2 Kings 20:7–8, where they come before the sign of the shadow, after verse 6 here. 2 Kings 20:7 reads "Then Isaiah said, 'Bring a cake of figs.' They brought it and put it on the boil, and he recovered." 2 Kings 20:8 reads "What will be the sign that the LORD will heal me and that I will go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?"
