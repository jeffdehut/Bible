**1** When King Hezekiah heard it, he tore his clothes, covered himself with sackcloth and went into the house of Yahweh.[^1]

**2** He sent Eliakim the palace administrator,[^2] Shebna the secretary and the senior priests, all wearing sackcloth, to the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz.

**3** They said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: Today is a day of distress, of rebuke and of disgrace. Children have come to the point of birth, and there is no strength to deliver them.

**4** Perhaps Yahweh your God will hear the words of the Rabshakeh,[^3] whom his master the king of Assyria has sent to mock the living God, and will rebuke him for the words that Yahweh your God has heard. So pray for the remnant that is left."

**5** When King Hezekiah's servants came to Isaiah,

**6** Isaiah said to them, "Say this to your master: 'This is what Yahweh says: Do not be afraid of the words you have heard, with which the king of Assyria's lackeys[^4] have insulted me.

**7** I am going to put a spirit in him, so that he will hear a rumor and go back to his own land, and I will make him fall by the sword in his own land.'"

**8** The Rabshakeh went back and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah, for he had heard that the king had left Lachish.

**9** Then the king heard that Tirhakah king of Cush[^5] had set out to fight against him. When he heard it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,[^6]

**10** "Say this to Hezekiah king of Judah: Do not let your God, in whom you trust, deceive you by saying, 'Jerusalem will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.'

**11** Look, you have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, destroying them completely.[^7] And will you be rescued?

**12** Did the gods of the nations that my predecessors[^8] destroyed rescue them, the nations of Gozan, Haran and Rezeph, and the people of Eden who were in Telassar?

**13** Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, and the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena and of Ivvah?"

**14** Hezekiah took the letter[^9] from the hand of the messengers and read it. Then he went up to the house of Yahweh, and Hezekiah spread it out before Yahweh.

**15** Hezekiah prayed to Yahweh and said,

**16** "Yahweh of Armies,[^10] the God of Israel, enthroned above the cherubim,[^11] you alone are God over all the kingdoms of the earth. You made the heavens and the earth.

**17** Turn your ear, Yahweh, and hear. Open your eyes, Yahweh, and see. Hear all the words that Sennacherib has sent to mock the living God.

**18** It is true, Yahweh, that the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the countries and their lands.[^12]

**19** They have thrown their gods into the fire, for they were not gods but only the work of human hands, wood and stone. So they destroyed them.

**20** Now, Yahweh our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you alone are Yahweh."[^13]

**21** Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent this message to Hezekiah: "This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: Because you have prayed to me about Sennacherib king of Assyria,[^14]

**22** this is the word that Yahweh has spoken against him: The virgin daughter Zion[^15] despises you and mocks you. Daughter Jerusalem tosses her head behind your back.

**23** Whom have you mocked and insulted? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes in pride? Against the Holy One of Israel!

**24** Through your servants you have mocked the Lord.[^16] You have said, 'With my many chariots I have gone up to the heights of the mountains, to the farthest reaches of Lebanon. I cut down its tallest cedars, its choicest junipers. I came to its farthest heights, its densest forest.[^17]

**25** I dug wells and drank water,[^18] and with the soles of my feet I dried up all the streams of Egypt.'[^19]

**26** Have you not heard? Long ago I planned it.[^20] From days of old I formed it. Now I have brought it about, that you should turn fortified cities into heaps of ruins.

**27** Their people, drained of strength,[^21] are dismayed and put to shame. They are like plants of the field and tender green shoots, like grass on the rooftops, like a field before the grain is grown.[^22]

**28** I know when you sit down, when you go out and when you come in, and how you rage against me.

**29** Because you rage against me and your arrogance has reached my ears, I will put my hook in your nose and my bit in your mouth, and I will turn you back by the way you came.

**30** And this will be the sign for you:[^23] This year you will eat what grows by itself, and in the second year what springs up from that. But in the third year, sow and reap, plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**31** The survivors of the house of Judah who are left will again take root below and bear fruit above.

**32** For from Jerusalem a remnant will go out, and survivors from Mount Zion. The zeal of Yahweh of Armies will do this.

**33** Therefore this is what Yahweh says concerning the king of Assyria: He will not come into this city or shoot an arrow there. He will not come before it with a shield or build a siege ramp against it.

**34** By the way that he came, he will go back. He will not come into this city, declares Yahweh.

**35** I will defend this city and save it, for my own sake and for the sake of my servant David."

**36** Then the angel[^24] of Yahweh went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of Assyria. When people got up early in the morning, there were all the dead bodies.

**37** So Sennacherib king of Assyria broke camp and went back home, and he stayed in Nineveh.

**38** One day, while he was worshiping in the temple of Nisroch his god, his sons Adrammelech and Sharezer struck him down with the sword and escaped to the land of Ararat.[^25] And his son Esarhaddon became king in his place.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple. This chapter is also found in 2 Kings 19:1–37.
[^2]: Literally "who was over the house" (36:3).
[^3]: On this title, see 36:2. 2 Kings 19:4 reads "all the words".
[^4]: Hebrew *na'arim*, "young men, servants", here probably said with contempt.
[^5]: The region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia). Its kings then also ruled Egypt.
[^6]: 2 Kings 19:9 reads "So he again sent messengers".
[^7]: Hebrew *charam*: to destroy completely, as something devoted to a god (34:2).
[^8]: Literally "my fathers".
[^9]: Literally "the letters". The Hebrew then says "and read it … and spread it out".
[^10]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 32. 2 Kings 19:15 reads only "Yahweh".
[^11]: Winged creatures whose images were on the cover of the ark and in the inner sanctuary of the temple (Exodus 25:18–22; 1 Kings 6:23–28).
[^12]: Literally "all the lands and their land". 2 Kings 19:17 reads "the nations and their land".
[^13]: 2 Kings 19:19 reads "that you, Yahweh, alone are God".
[^14]: 2 Kings 19:20 reads "I have heard what you prayed to me about Sennacherib king of Assyria."
[^15]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman (1:8).
[^16]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name Yahweh. 2 Kings 19:23 reads "Through your messengers".
[^17]: Literally "the forest of its *karmel*", its fruitful land or orchard. 2 Kings 19:23 reads "its farthest lodging place" instead of "its farthest heights".
[^18]: 2 Kings 19:24 reads "foreign waters".
[^19]: Hebrew *Matsor*, a poetic name for Egypt (19:6). Literally "all the Nile streams of Matsor".
[^20]: Literally "From far off I did it".
[^21]: Literally "short of hand".
[^22]: Hebrew *shedemah*, "field" or "terrace". 2 Kings 19:26 reads *shedefah*, "scorched", which differs by one letter: "scorched before it is grown".
[^23]: The prophet now speaks to Hezekiah.
[^24]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. 2 Kings 19:35 begins "That night".
[^25]: Urartu, a kingdom in what is now Armenia and eastern Turkey. Sennacherib was killed in 681 BC.
