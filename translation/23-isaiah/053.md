**1** Who has believed what we have heard?[^1] And to whom has the arm of the LORD[^2] been revealed?

**2** He grew up before him like a young plant, like a root out of dry ground. He had no form or majesty that we should look at him, no appearance that we should desire him.

**3** He was despised and rejected by others,[^3] a man of pains, who knew what sickness was.[^4] Like one from whom people hide their faces,[^5] he was despised, and we held him in no regard.

**4** Surely he has carried our sicknesses and borne our pains.[^6] Yet we thought of him as stricken, struck down by God, and afflicted.

**5** But he was pierced[^7] for our rebellious acts; he was crushed for our wrongdoings. The punishment that brought us peace[^8] was on him, and by his wounds[^9] we are healed.

**6** We all, like sheep, have gone astray; we have each turned to his own way. And the LORD has laid on him the guilt of us all.[^10]

**7** He was oppressed, and he let himself be afflicted,[^11] yet he did not open his mouth. Like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that is silent before its shearers, he did not open his mouth.[^12]

**8** By oppression and judgment he was taken away.[^13] And who of his generation considered[^14] that he was cut off from the land of the living, struck down for the rebellion of my people?[^15]

**9** They made his grave with the wicked, and with a rich man in his death,[^16] although he had done no violence, and there was no deceit in his mouth.[^17]

**10** Yet it was the LORD's will to crush him; he made him sick. When you make his life a guilt offering,[^18] he will see offspring and prolong his days, and the will of the LORD will prosper in his hand.

**11** Out of the anguish of his soul he will see[^19] and be satisfied. By his knowledge[^20] my righteous servant will make many righteous,[^21] and he will bear their wrongdoings.

**12** Therefore I will give him a portion among the many,[^22] and he will divide the plunder with the strong, because he poured out his life to death and was counted among the rebels.[^23] Yet he bore the sin of many and made intercession for the rebels.[^24]

[^1]: Or "our message", that is, the message we bring. Hebrew *shemu'ah*, "what is heard, report". John 12:38 and Romans 10:16 quote this verse from the Septuagint: "Lord, who has believed our message?" The speakers in verses 1–10 are not named: the nations and kings of 52:15, or Israel, or the prophet with those who share his view.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "lacking men", that is, abandoned by people, or "the lowest of men".
[^4]: Literally "a man of pains, knowing sickness". Traditionally "a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief". Hebrew *choli*, "sickness", as in verse 4.
[^5]: Or "Like one who hides his face from us", as a leper covered his face (Leviticus 13:45).
[^6]: Matthew 8:17 quotes this line, applying it to Jesus' healing of the sick: "He took our weaknesses and carried our diseases."
[^7]: Or "wounded". Hebrew *mecholal*, from the verb "pierce".
[^8]: Literally "The discipline of our peace". Hebrew *musar*, "discipline, correction, chastisement"; *shalom*, "peace, well-being, wholeness".
[^9]: Hebrew *chabburah*, "wound, welt, bruise" (1:6). 1 Peter 2:24–25 echoes verses 5–6.
[^10]: Literally "has caused to fall on him the guilt of us all". The same verb is translated "made intercession" in verse 12.
[^11]: Or "he humbled himself". Hebrew *na'aneh*.
[^12]: Acts 8:32–33 quotes verses 7–8 from the Septuagint: "Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer is silent, so he does not open his mouth. In his humiliation justice was denied him. Who can describe his generation? For his life is taken away from the earth." Philip begins from this passage to tell the Ethiopian official the good news about Jesus (Acts 8:35).
[^13]: Or "Without restraint and without justice he was taken away", or "From prison and from judgment he was taken away". Hebrew *otser* means "restraint, oppression" or "custody".
[^14]: Or "And who could speak of his descendants?" or "Who could think about his fate?" Hebrew *dor* means "generation", the people living at one time, or perhaps "fate, dwelling". The Hebrew of this line is difficult.
[^15]: Literally "for the rebellion of my people, a blow to them" (or "to him"). The Septuagint reads "for the lawless acts of my people he was led to death".
[^16]: Literally "in his deaths". *They made* is literally "he gave" or "one gave". The *rich* stand in parallel with the *wicked*, perhaps as oppressors. Compare the burial of Jesus in the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea, a rich man (Matthew 27:57–60).
[^17]: 1 Peter 2:22 quotes this line.
[^18]: Or "When his life makes a guilt offering". The Hebrew verb can mean "you make" or "it makes". Hebrew *asham*, the guilt offering (Leviticus 5:14–26; 5:14–6:7 in most English Bibles), also called the reparation offering, which made up for a wrong done. *Life* is Hebrew *nephesh*, also in verses 11–12.
[^19]: The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a), two other Isaiah scrolls from Qumran and the Septuagint read "he will see light".
[^20]: Or "By knowing him", that is, by the knowledge others gain of him.
[^21]: Hebrew *yatsdiq*, "make righteous, declare righteous, bring justice to" (50:8). *Many* (Hebrew *rabbim*) is repeated from 52:14–15 and is found again twice in verse 12.
[^22]: Or "among the great". Hebrew *rabbim*, "many" or "great". *The strong* (Hebrew *atsumim*) may also mean "the numerous".
[^23]: Luke 22:37 quotes this line, which Jesus applies to himself. *Poured out* is literally "laid bare".
[^24]: Or "and intervened for the rebels". Hebrew *yafgia*, the verb translated "laid on him" in verse 6.
