**1** Yahweh[^1] will have compassion on Jacob and will again choose Israel and settle them in their own land. Foreigners will join them and attach themselves to the house of Jacob.

**2** Peoples will take them and bring them to their own place, and the house of Israel will possess them as male and female servants in Yahweh's land. They will take captive those who took them captive, and rule over those who oppressed them.

**3** On the day Yahweh gives you rest from your pain and turmoil and from the hard labor forced on you,

**4** you will take up this taunt[^2] against the king of Babylon: "How the oppressor has come to an end! How his fury has ended![^3]

**5** Yahweh has broken the staff of the wicked, the scepter of rulers,

**6** that struck peoples in fury with blows that never stopped, that ruled nations in anger with relentless persecution.

**7** The whole earth is at rest and at peace; they break into song.

**8** Even the junipers[^4] and the cedars of Lebanon rejoice over you: 'Since you were laid low, no woodcutter comes up against us.'

**9** Sheol[^5] below is stirred up to meet you when you come. It rouses the shades[^6] to greet you, all who were leaders[^7] of the earth. It makes all the kings of the nations rise from their thrones.

**10** All of them answer and say to you: 'You too have become as weak as we are! You have become like us!'

**11** Your pomp has been brought down to Sheol, with the music of your harps. Maggots are spread out as your bed, and worms are your covering.

**12** How you have fallen from heaven, shining one, son of the dawn![^8] How you have been cut down to the ground, you who laid the nations low!

**13** You said in your heart, 'I will go up to heaven; above the stars of God I will set my throne on high. I will sit on the mount of assembly, in the far reaches of the north.[^9]

**14** I will go up above the heights of the clouds; I will make myself like the Most High.'[^10]

**15** But you are brought down to Sheol, to the depths of the Pit.

**16** Those who see you will stare at you; they will look closely at you: 'Is this the man who made the earth tremble, who shook kingdoms,

**17** who made the world like a wilderness and overthrew its cities, who would not let his prisoners go home?'

**18** All the kings of the nations, all of them, lie in honor, each in his own tomb.[^11]

**19** But you are thrown out of your grave like a rejected branch,[^12] covered with the slain, those pierced by the sword, who go down to the stones of the Pit, like a corpse trampled underfoot.

**20** You will not join them in burial, for you have destroyed your land and killed your people. May the offspring of evildoers never be named again!

**21** Prepare a place to slaughter his sons for the guilt of their ancestors, so that they may not rise and possess the earth and fill the face of the world with cities."

**22** I will rise up against them, declares Yahweh of Armies.[^13] I will cut off from Babylon name and remnant, offspring and descendants, declares Yahweh.

**23** I will make it a place for owls[^14] and pools of water, and I will sweep it with the broom of destruction, declares Yahweh of Armies.

**24** Yahweh of Armies has sworn: Surely, as I have planned, so it will be, and as I have purposed, so it will stand:

**25** I will break Assyria in my land and trample him on my mountains. His yoke will be removed from them, and his burden removed from their shoulder.

**26** This is the plan that is planned for the whole earth, and this is the hand that is stretched out over all the nations.

**27** For Yahweh of Armies has planned, and who can stop him? His hand is stretched out, and who can turn it back?

**28** In the year King Ahaz died,[^15] this oracle[^16] came:

**29** Do not rejoice, all you Philistines,[^17] because the rod that struck you is broken. For from the root of the snake will come a viper, and its fruit will be a flying serpent.[^18]

**30** The firstborn of the poor will graze,[^19] and the needy will lie down in safety. But I will kill your root with famine, and it will slay your survivors.[^20]

**31** Wail, you gate! Cry out, you city! Melt in fear, all you Philistines! For smoke comes from the north, and there is no straggler in its ranks.[^21]

**32** What answer will be given to the messengers of the nation? That Yahweh has founded Zion, and in her the afflicted of his people will find refuge.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *mashal*, "saying, proverb, taunt song". The song runs from here to verse 21.
[^3]: Hebrew *madhevah*, a word found only here, of uncertain meaning. The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads *marhevah*, "onslaught" or "arrogance", which differs by one similar letter.
[^4]: Or "cypresses". Hebrew *berosh*.
[^5]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^6]: Hebrew *repha'im*, the spirits of the dead (26:14, 19).
[^7]: Literally "he-goats", the leaders of the flock.
[^8]: Hebrew *helel ben shachar*, "shining one, son of the dawn", probably the morning star, the planet Venus, which rises brightly before dawn and then fades in the light of the sun. The poem may draw on an old story of a heavenly being who tried to rise above the gods and was thrown down. The Septuagint translated *helel* as "the morning star", and the Latin Vulgate as *lucifer*, "light-bearer", its word for the morning star; from this came the traditional rendering "Lucifer" (as in the King James Version). Some early Christian writers applied the verse to the fall of Satan (compare Luke 10:18), and "Lucifer" became a name for him. In the poem the words are a taunt against the king of Babylon (verse 4).
[^9]: Hebrew *yarkete tsafon*, "the far reaches of Zaphon". In Canaanite stories the gods met on Mount Zaphon in the north. Psalm 48:3 (English 48:2) uses the same words of Mount Zion.
[^10]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^11]: Literally "in his house".
[^12]: Hebrew *netser*, the word for the "branch" from the roots of Jesse in 11:1.
[^13]: *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^14]: Hebrew *qippod*, perhaps a hedgehog or a kind of owl.
[^15]: About 715 BC, though the date of Ahaz's death is uncertain.
[^16]: Hebrew *massa*. See 13:1.
[^17]: Literally "Philistia, all of you", the land addressed as a woman. Also in verse 31.
[^18]: Hebrew *saraf*, the word used for the *seraphim* of 6:2.
[^19]: That is, the poorest of the poor.
[^20]: Hebrew *she'erit*, "remnant".
[^21]: Literally "there is no one alone in his appointed places". The meaning is uncertain.
