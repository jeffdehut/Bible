**1** Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help, who rely on horses, who trust in chariots because they are many and in horsemen because they are very strong, but do not look to the Holy One of Israel or seek Yahweh![^1]

**2** Yet he too is wise and brings disaster; he does not take back his words. He will rise up against the house of evildoers and against the helpers of those who do wrong.

**3** The Egyptians are human beings and not God;[^2] their horses are flesh and not spirit. When Yahweh stretches out his hand, the helper will stumble and the one who is helped will fall; they will all perish together.

**4** For this is what Yahweh has said to me: As a lion or a young lion growls over its prey, and though a whole band of shepherds is called out against it, it is not terrified by their shouting or daunted by their noise, so Yahweh of Armies[^3] will come down to wage war on Mount Zion and on its hill.[^4]

**5** Like birds hovering overhead, so Yahweh of Armies will shield Jerusalem; he will shield and rescue it, pass over and deliver it.[^5]

**6** Return to the one against whom the Israelites have so deeply rebelled.

**7** For on that day each of you will reject your idols of silver and your idols of gold, which your hands have made for you in sin.[^6]

**8** Assyria will fall by a sword not of man; a sword not of a human being will devour him. He will flee from the sword,[^7] and his young men will be put to forced labor.

**9** His rock will pass away in terror,[^8] and his officers will be dismayed at the signal flag,[^9] declares Yahweh, whose fire is in Zion and whose furnace is in Jerusalem.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *el*, "God, a god".
[^3]: *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *To wage war* (*litsbo*) comes from the same root.
[^4]: Or "against Mount Zion". The Hebrew can mean either; verse 5 shows Yahweh protecting the city.
[^5]: Hebrew *pasoach*, the verb of the Passover, when Yahweh "passed over" the houses of Israel (Exodus 12:13, 23, 27).
[^6]: Literally "which your hands have made for you, a sin".
[^7]: Literally "he will flee for himself from before the sword".
[^8]: Perhaps his stronghold, or his king. Hebrew *sal'o*, "his rock".
[^9]: Or "will desert the standard".
