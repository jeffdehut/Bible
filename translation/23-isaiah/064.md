**1** As when fire kindles brushwood, and fire makes water boil, make your name known to your adversaries, so that the nations may tremble at your presence![^1]

**2** When you did awesome things that we did not expect, you came down; the mountains quaked at your presence.

**3** From ages past no one has heard, no ear has perceived, no eye has seen any God besides you, who acts for those who wait for him.[^2]

**4** You meet those who gladly do what is right, who remember you in your ways. Look, you were angry, and we sinned. We have been in them a long time; can we be saved?[^3]

**5** We have all become like one who is unclean, and all our righteous deeds are like a filthy cloth.[^4] We all wither like a leaf, and our wrongdoings carry us away like the wind.

**6** No one calls on your name or rouses himself to take hold of you. For you have hidden your face from us and made us melt away in the grip of our wrongdoings.[^5]

**7** But now, LORD,[^6] you are our Father. We are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand.

**8** Do not be so very angry, LORD, and do not remember our wrongdoing forever. Look, please, we are all your people.

**9** Your holy cities have become a wilderness; Zion has become a wilderness, Jerusalem a desolation.

**10** Our holy and beautiful house, where our ancestors praised you, has been burned with fire, and all that we treasured has become ruins.[^7]

**11** After all this, LORD, will you hold back? Will you keep silent and afflict us so very much?

[^1]: Verses 1–11 are 64:2–12 in most English Bibles. The prayer that began in 63:15 continues to the end of this chapter. *Brushwood* is Hebrew *hamasim*, a word found only here.
[^2]: Paul echoes this verse in 1 Corinthians 2:9: "What no eye has seen, nor ear heard … God has prepared for those who love him."
[^3]: The Hebrew of the last line is very difficult. Literally "in them forever, and we will be saved". *Them* may be the LORD's ways or the people's sins. Some take the line as a statement: "In them we will be saved forever."
[^4]: Literally "a garment of menstruation", a cloth made unclean by menstrual blood (Leviticus 15:19–24).
[^5]: Literally "made us melt in the hand of our wrongdoings".
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7]: Literally "all our precious things have become a ruin", perhaps the city's treasured places or the temple's treasures. The temple was burned by the Babylonians in 587 or 586 BC (2 Kings 25:9).
