**1** But now listen, Jacob my servant, Israel whom I have chosen.

**2** This is what the LORD[^1] says, who made you, who formed you from the womb and will help you: Do not be afraid, Jacob my servant, Jeshurun[^2] whom I have chosen.

**3** For I will pour water on the thirsty land and streams on the dry ground. I will pour out my Spirit[^3] on your offspring and my blessing on your descendants.

**4** They will spring up among the grass,[^4] like willows by streams of water.

**5** One will say, "I belong to the LORD"; another will call himself by the name of Jacob; another will write on his hand, "The LORD's,"[^5] and take the name of Israel as his title.

**6** This is what the LORD says, the King of Israel and its Redeemer,[^6] the LORD of Armies:[^7] I am the first and I am the last; apart from me there is no God.[^8]

**7** Who is like me? Let him proclaim it; let him declare it and set it out before me, since I established my ancient people.[^9] Let them declare what is coming, and what is going to happen.

**8** Do not tremble or be afraid. Did I not tell you long ago and declare it? You are my witnesses. Is there any God apart from me? There is no other Rock; I know of none.

**9** All who make idols are nothing,[^10] and the things they delight in do no good. Their witnesses[^11] neither see nor know, so they will be put to shame.

**10** Who would fashion a god or cast an idol that can do no good?

**11** Look, all who join themselves to it[^12] will be put to shame; the craftsmen are only human. Let them all gather and take their stand; they will be terrified and put to shame together.

**12** The blacksmith takes a tool and works it over the coals. He shapes the idol with hammers and forges it with his strong arm. He also grows hungry and loses his strength; he drinks no water and grows faint.

**13** The carpenter stretches out a line and marks it out with a stylus. He shapes it with chisels and marks it with compasses. He makes it in the form of a man, with human beauty, to live in a shrine.[^13]

**14** He cuts down cedars for himself, or takes a cypress[^14] or an oak. He lets it grow strong among the trees of the forest. He plants a laurel,[^15] and the rain makes it grow.

**15** Then it becomes fuel for people to burn. He takes some of it and warms himself; he kindles a fire and bakes bread. He also makes a god and worships it; he makes it an idol and bows down to it.

**16** Half of it he burns in the fire. Over that half he eats meat; he roasts a roast and is satisfied. He also warms himself and says, "Ah! I am warm; I have seen the fire!"

**17** And the rest of it he makes into a god, his idol. He bows down to it and worships; he prays to it and says, "Save me, for you are my god!"

**18** They do not know or understand, for their eyes are plastered over so that they cannot see, and their minds[^16] so that they cannot understand.

**19** No one stops to think;[^17] no one has the knowledge or understanding to say, "Half of it I burned in the fire; I also baked bread on its coals; I roast meat and eat it. Shall I make the rest of it a detestable thing? Shall I bow down to a block of wood?"

**20** He feeds on ashes.[^18] A deceived heart has led him astray, and he cannot save himself[^19] or say, "Is not this thing in my right hand a lie?"

**21** Remember these things, Jacob, Israel, for you are my servant. I formed you; you are my servant. Israel, you will not be forgotten by me.[^20]

**22** I have wiped out your rebellious acts like a cloud, and your sins like mist. Return to me, for I have redeemed you.

**23** Sing for joy, you heavens, for the LORD has done it! Shout aloud, you depths of the earth! Break into song, you mountains, you forest and every tree in it! For the LORD has redeemed Jacob, and he shows his glory in Israel.

**24** This is what the LORD says, your Redeemer, who formed you from the womb: I am the LORD, who made all things, who alone stretched out the heavens, who spread out the earth by myself,[^21]

**25** who frustrates the signs of false prophets[^22] and makes fools of diviners, who turns back the wise and makes their knowledge foolish,

**26** who carries out the word of his servant and fulfills the counsel of his messengers, who says of Jerusalem, "She will be inhabited," and of the towns of Judah, "They will be rebuilt, and I will raise up her ruins,"

**27** who says to the deep, "Be dry! I will dry up your rivers,"[^23]

**28** who says of Cyrus,[^24] "He is my shepherd, and he will carry out all that I desire," saying of Jerusalem, "She will be rebuilt," and of the temple, "Its foundations will be laid."

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: A poetic name for Israel, perhaps "the upright one" (Deuteronomy 32:15; 33:5, 26).
[^3]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^4]: Literally "in between grass".
[^5]: Literally "will write his hand: To the LORD". Or "will write with his hand". The mark would show to whom he belongs, as a slave's or a soldier's mark showed his master.
[^6]: Hebrew *go'el* (41:14). Also in verse 24.
[^7]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^8]: Compare 41:4; 48:12. Revelation 1:17; 2:8 and 22:13 echo these words.
[^9]: Literally "since I set up a people of long ago". The meaning is uncertain.
[^10]: Hebrew *tohu*, "emptiness" (40:17).
[^11]: In the Hebrew text, dots are written over the next word (*hemmah*, "they"), a scribal mark of doubt.
[^12]: Literally "all its companions", that is, its worshipers.
[^13]: Literally "to sit in a house".
[^14]: Hebrew *tirzah*, a word found only here, perhaps a kind of cypress or holm oak.
[^15]: Hebrew *oren*, perhaps a laurel, pine or cedar. In the Hebrew text one letter of this word (*nun*) is written small, a scribal mark.
[^16]: Literally "their hearts".
[^17]: Literally "No one brings it back to his heart".
[^18]: Or "He tends ashes", as a shepherd tends a flock. Hebrew *ro'eh efer*.
[^19]: Literally "his *nephesh*".
[^20]: Or "you must not forget me". The Hebrew form is unusual.
[^21]: The Hebrew text is written "Who was with me?" but read "by myself".
[^22]: Hebrew *baddim*, "babblers", probably prophets who speak empty words, or diviners.
[^23]: The *deep* (Hebrew *tsulah*) may be the sea or the river of Babylon, and the words recall the drying of the Red Sea (51:10; Exodus 14:21).
[^24]: Cyrus II, king of Persia (559–530 BC). He conquered Babylon in 539 BC and allowed the exiles to return to Judah and rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:1–4). Isaiah names him here and in 45:1.
