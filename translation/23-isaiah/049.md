**1** Listen to me, you coastlands;[^1] pay attention, you peoples far away! The LORD[^2] called me before I was born; from my mother's body he named me.[^3]

**2** He made my mouth like a sharp sword; in the shadow of his hand he hid me. He made me a polished arrow; in his quiver he concealed me.[^4]

**3** He said to me, "You are my servant, Israel, in whom I will show my glory."[^5]

**4** But I said, "I have labored in vain; I have spent my strength for nothing and emptiness. Yet surely my cause[^6] is with the LORD, and my reward with my God."

**5** And now the LORD says, he who formed me from the womb to be his servant, to bring Jacob back to him, so that Israel might be gathered to him[^7] (for I am honored in the eyes of the LORD, and my God has become my strength),

**6** he says, "It is too small a thing for you to be my servant, to raise up the tribes of Jacob and bring back the survivors of Israel. I will also make you a light for the nations, so that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth."[^8]

**7** This is what the LORD says, the Redeemer[^9] of Israel and his Holy One, to the one who is deeply despised,[^10] abhorred by the nation, the servant of rulers: Kings will see and rise up; princes will bow down, because of the LORD, who is faithful, the Holy One of Israel, who has chosen you.

**8** This is what the LORD says: In a time of favor I have answered you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you.[^11] I will keep you and make you a covenant for the people,[^12] to restore the land and to give out again the desolate inheritances,

**9** saying to the prisoners, "Come out," and to those in darkness, "Show yourselves." They will graze along the roads, and their pasture will be on all the bare heights.

**10** They will not hunger or thirst; the scorching heat and the sun will not strike them. For he who has compassion on them will lead them and guide them to springs of water.[^13]

**11** I will make all my mountains into a road, and my highways will be raised up.

**12** Look, these will come from far away; look, these from the north and from the west,[^14] and these from the land of Sinim.[^15]

**13** Shout for joy, you heavens! Rejoice, you earth! Break into song, you mountains! For the LORD has comforted his people and will have compassion on his afflicted ones.

**14** But Zion said, "The LORD has abandoned me; the Lord has forgotten me."

**15** Can a woman forget her nursing child, or have no compassion on the son of her womb? Even if these forget, I will not forget you.[^16]

**16** Look, I have engraved you on the palms of my hands; your walls are always before me.

**17** Your children hurry back;[^17] those who destroyed you and laid you waste go away from you.

**18** Lift up your eyes and look around: they all gather and come to you. As surely as I live, declares the LORD, you will put them all on like jewelry; you will tie them on as a bride does.

**19** For your ruins and your desolate places and your devastated land—now you will be too small for your inhabitants, and those who swallowed you up will be far away.

**20** The children born in your time of bereavement will yet say in your hearing, "This place is too cramped for me; make room for me to live."

**21** Then you will say in your heart, "Who bore me these? I was bereaved and barren, exiled and turned away. Who brought these up? Look, I was left all alone; where have these come from?"

**22** This is what the Lord GOD says: Look, I will lift up my hand to the nations and raise my signal flag to the peoples. They will bring your sons in their arms,[^18] and your daughters will be carried on their shoulders.

**23** Kings will be your foster fathers, and their queens your nursing mothers. They will bow down to you with their faces to the ground and lick the dust of your feet. Then you will know that I am the LORD; those who hope in me will not be put to shame.

**24** Can plunder be taken from a warrior, or captives be rescued from a righteous one?[^19]

**25** But this is what the LORD says: Even the captives of the warrior will be taken, and the plunder of the tyrant will be rescued. I will contend with those who contend with you, and I will save your children.

**26** I will make your oppressors eat their own flesh, and they will be drunk on their own blood as on new wine. Then all people[^20] will know that I, the LORD, am your Savior, your Redeemer, the Mighty One of Jacob.[^21]

[^1]: Hebrew *iyyim*, the islands and coasts of the Mediterranean (11:11), and in these chapters the most distant lands. Verses 1–13 are the second of the servant passages (see 42:1). In verses 1–6 the servant himself speaks.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "from the womb he called me; from the inner parts of my mother he made mention of my name". Compare Jeremiah 1:5; Galatians 1:15.
[^4]: The sword and the arrow are kept ready and hidden until the time to use them. Revelation 1:16 and 2:12 echo the first line.
[^5]: Here the servant is named *Israel*, as in 41:8 and 44:1–2. Yet in verses 5–6 he has a task toward Jacob and Israel. Some have taken him to be the true Israel, the faithful within the people; others an individual who embodies Israel. *Show my glory* is Hebrew *etpa'ar*, "I will be glorified" or "I will glory".
[^6]: Hebrew *mishpat*, "justice, right, judgment". *Reward* is literally "wage".
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "and Israel is not gathered" but read "and Israel to him is gathered". The two words for "not" and "to him" sound alike (*lo*).
[^8]: Literally "to be my salvation to the end of the earth", that is, "so that you may be my salvation" or "so that my salvation may be". Acts 13:47 quotes this sentence from the Septuagint, and Simeon's words in Luke 2:32 echo it.
[^9]: Hebrew *go'el* (41:14). Also in verse 26.
[^10]: Literally "to one despised of *nephesh*". Or "to the one whom people despise".
[^11]: 2 Corinthians 6:2 quotes this sentence from the Septuagint: "At an acceptable time I listened to you, and on a day of salvation I helped you."
[^12]: Hebrew *berit am* (42:6).
[^13]: Revelation 7:16–17 echoes this verse.
[^14]: Literally "from the sea", the Mediterranean, to the west of Israel.
[^15]: An unknown land. The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads *Sewenim*, that is, Syene (modern Aswan) in southern Egypt (Ezekiel 29:10), where there was a colony of Jews in the Persian period.
[^16]: *You* is feminine singular in verses 15–26: Zion is addressed as a woman and a mother.
[^17]: The same consonants can be read *bonayikh*, "your builders", the reading of the Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a).
[^18]: Literally "in their bosom", the fold of the garment over the chest.
[^19]: The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) reads "from a tyrant", as in verse 25.
[^20]: Literally "all flesh".
[^21]: Hebrew *avir ya'aqov*, a title of God (Genesis 49:24; Isaiah 1:24; 60:16).
