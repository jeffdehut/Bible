**1** An oracle[^1] concerning Babylon, which Isaiah son of Amoz saw.

**2** On a bare hill raise a signal flag! Shout to them; wave your hand for them to enter the gates of the nobles.

**3** I myself have given orders to my consecrated ones;[^2] I have summoned my warriors to carry out my anger, those who exult in my triumph.[^3]

**4** Listen! A tumult on the mountains, like that of a great people! Listen! An uproar of kingdoms, of nations gathering! Yahweh of Armies[^4] is mustering an army for battle.

**5** They come from a distant land, from the ends of the heavens, Yahweh and the weapons of his wrath, to destroy the whole earth.[^5]

**6** Wail, for the day of Yahweh is near! It comes like destruction from the Almighty.[^6]

**7** Therefore all hands will go limp, and every human heart will melt.

**8** They will be terrified. Pains and pangs will seize them; they will writhe like a woman in labor. They will look at one another in horror, their faces aflame.

**9** Look, the day of Yahweh is coming, cruel, with fury and burning anger, to make the earth a desolation and to destroy its sinners from it.

**10** For the stars of the heavens and their constellations[^7] will not give their light. The sun will be dark when it rises, and the moon will not shed its light.[^8]

**11** I will punish the world for its evil and the wicked for their guilt. I will put an end to the pride of the arrogant and bring low the haughtiness of tyrants.

**12** I will make human beings scarcer than fine gold, humankind scarcer than the gold of Ophir.[^9]

**13** Therefore I will make the heavens tremble, and the earth will be shaken from its place at the fury of Yahweh of Armies, on the day of his burning anger.

**14** Like a hunted gazelle, like sheep with no one to gather them, each will turn to his own people, each will flee to his own land.

**15** Whoever is found will be stabbed, and whoever is caught will fall by the sword.

**16** Their infants will be dashed to pieces before their eyes, their houses will be plundered, and their wives raped.[^10]

**17** Look, I am stirring up the Medes[^11] against them, who have no regard for silver and take no delight in gold.

**18** Their bows will cut down the young men. They will have no pity on the fruit of the womb; their eyes will not spare children.

**19** Babylon, the jewel of kingdoms, the glory and pride of the Chaldeans,[^12] will be like Sodom and Gomorrah when God overthrew them.

**20** It will never be inhabited or lived in from generation to generation. No Arab will pitch his tent there, and no shepherds will rest their flocks there.

**21** But desert creatures will lie down there, and its houses will be full of howling creatures. Ostriches will live there, and wild goats will dance there.[^13]

**22** Hyenas will howl in its fortresses,[^14] and jackals in its luxurious palaces. Its time is near, and its days will not be prolonged.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden of Babylon". The word heads most of the oracles against the nations in chapters 13–23, and is translated "oracle" throughout.
[^2]: Soldiers set apart for a holy war.
[^3]: Or "my proudly exulting ones".
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Here Yahweh of Armies musters an army (*tseva*).
[^5]: Or "the whole land".
[^6]: A play on words: *shod*, "destruction", and *Shaddai*, "the Almighty", a title of God whose meaning is uncertain. The same line is found in Joel 1:15.
[^7]: Hebrew *kesilim*, plural of *kesil*, the constellation Orion.
[^8]: Compare Mark 13:24–25.
[^9]: A land famous for its gold, perhaps in southern Arabia or East Africa (1 Kings 9:28).
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written with a coarse word for sexual violation but read with a milder one, "lain with".
[^11]: A people of what is now northwestern Iran. With the Persians they conquered Babylon in 539 BC.
[^12]: The people of southern Babylonia, whose kings ruled the Babylonian empire.
[^13]: Several of the animals in verses 21–22 cannot be identified with certainty. *Wild goats* (Hebrew *se'irim*, "hairy ones") may be goat-demons thought to haunt ruins (34:14; Leviticus 17:7).
[^14]: Hebrew *almenotav*, literally "its widows", probably a form of *armenotav*, "its fortresses".
