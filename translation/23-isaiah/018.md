**1** Woe to[^1] the land of whirring wings,[^2] beyond the rivers of Cush,[^3]

**2** which sends envoys by sea, in papyrus boats over the waters! Go, swift messengers, to a nation tall and smooth-skinned,[^4] to a people feared far and wide,[^5] a nation strong and conquering,[^6] whose land the rivers divide.

**3** All you inhabitants of the world, you who live on the earth, when a signal flag is raised on the mountains, you will see it; when a ram's horn is blown, you will hear it.

**4** For this is what the LORD[^7] has said to me: I will be still and look on from my dwelling place, like the shimmering heat in the sunshine, like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest.

**5** For before the harvest, when the blossom is over and the flower becomes a ripening grape, he will cut off the shoots with pruning knives and lop off and clear away the spreading branches.

**6** They will all be left to the birds of prey on the mountains and to the wild animals of the earth. The birds of prey will spend the summer on them, and all the wild animals of the earth the winter.

**7** At that time gifts will be brought to the LORD of Armies[^8] from a people tall and smooth-skinned,[^9] from a people feared far and wide, a nation strong and conquering, whose land the rivers divide, to the place of the name of the LORD of Armies, Mount Zion.

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning, used by the prophets to announce disaster (see 1:4).
[^2]: Hebrew *tsiltsal kenafayim*, perhaps the buzzing of insects, or the sails of ships.
[^3]: The region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia). In Isaiah's time its kings also ruled Egypt and sought allies against Assyria.
[^4]: Literally "drawn out and polished". Also in verse 7.
[^5]: Literally "from it and beyond".
[^6]: Hebrew *qav-qav u-mevusah*, literally perhaps "line-line and trampling". The meaning is uncertain; perhaps "of strange speech".
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^9]: Literally "a people tall and smooth-skinned, and from a people feared far and wide".
