**1** An oracle[^1] concerning Moab: Ar of Moab is devastated in a night, destroyed![^2] Kir of Moab is devastated in a night, destroyed!

**2** Dibon goes up to its temple, to its high places, to weep.[^3] Over Nebo and over Medeba Moab wails. Every head is shaved bald, every beard cut off.[^4]

**3** In its streets they wear sackcloth; on its rooftops and in its squares everyone wails, streaming with tears.

**4** Heshbon and Elealeh cry out; their voice is heard as far as Jahaz. Therefore the armed men of Moab cry out; his soul trembles within him.

**5** My heart cries out for Moab. Her fugitives flee as far as Zoar, as far as Eglath-shelishiyah.[^5] For they go up the ascent of Luhith weeping; on the road to Horonaim they raise a cry of destruction.

**6** For the waters of Nimrim are a desolation; the grass is withered, the new growth is gone, and nothing green is left.

**7** So they carry the wealth they have gained and stored up away over the Wadi of the Willows.[^6]

**8** For the cry has gone around the borders of Moab; her wailing reaches as far as Eglaim, her wailing as far as Beer-elim.

**9** For the waters of Dimon[^7] are full of blood. Yet I will bring still more on Dimon: a lion for the survivors of Moab and for the remnant of the land.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*. See 13:1. Much of chapters 15–16 is found also in Jeremiah 48.
[^2]: Or "silenced". Ar and Kir were chief cities of Moab, east of the Dead Sea.
[^3]: Literally "He goes up, the house and Dibon, the high places, to weep". The Hebrew is difficult; *the house* may be a temple or a place named Bayith.
[^4]: Shaving the head and cutting the beard were signs of mourning.
[^5]: Or "as far as Zoar, like a three-year-old heifer".
[^6]: Or "the Wadi of the Poplars". Probably the Zered, the southern border of Moab.
[^7]: Probably Dibon (verse 2), spelled to sound like *dam*, "blood". The Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsa-a) and the Vulgate read "Dibon".
