**1** He made a bronze altar,[^1] twenty cubits[^2] long, twenty cubits wide and ten cubits high.

**2** He made the Sea[^3] of cast metal, ten cubits from rim to rim, round all around and five cubits high. A line of thirty cubits measured around it.

**3** Under it were figures of oxen[^4] encircling it all around, ten to a cubit, going all around the Sea. The oxen were in two rows, cast in one piece with it.

**4** It stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west,[^5] three facing south and three facing east. The Sea rested on top of them, and all their hindquarters were toward the center.

**5** It was a handbreadth[^6] thick, and its rim was made like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It could hold three thousand baths.[^7]

**6** He made ten basins[^8] and put five on the right and five on the left,[^9] for washing. In them they rinsed the things used for the burnt offering,[^10] but the Sea was for the priests to wash in.

**7** He made ten gold lampstands as prescribed for them[^11] and put them in the main hall,[^12] five on the right and five on the left.

**8** He made ten tables and placed them in the main hall, five on the right and five on the left. And he made a hundred gold bowls.[^13]

**9** He made the courtyard of the priests and the great court,[^14] and doors for the court, and he overlaid their doors with bronze.

**10** He put the Sea on the south side, toward the southeast.[^15]

**11** Huram[^16] made the pots,[^17] the shovels and the bowls. So Huram finished the work that he did for King Solomon in the house of God:

**12** the two pillars; the bowls[^18] and the capitals on top of the pillars, two of them; the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on top of the pillars;

**13** the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the pillars;[^19]

**14** he made the stands,[^20] and he made the basins on the stands;

**15** the one Sea, and the twelve oxen under it;

**16** and the pots, the shovels, the forks and all their utensils. Huram-abi[^21] made them for King Solomon for the house of the LORD,[^22] of polished bronze.

**17** The king cast them in the plain of the Jordan, in the clay ground[^23] between Succoth and Zeredah.[^24]

**18** Solomon made all these utensils in such great quantities that the weight of the bronze was never determined.

**19** Solomon also made all the utensils that were in the house of God: the gold altar;[^25] the tables on which was the bread of the Presence;[^26]

**20** the lampstands and their lamps, to burn as prescribed in front of the inner sanctuary,[^27] of pure gold;[^28]

**21** the flowers,[^29] the lamps and the tongs, of gold, the finest gold;[^30]

**22** the wick trimmers, the bowls, the dishes[^31] and the firepans, of pure gold; and the entrance[^32] of the house, its inner doors to the most holy place[^33] and the doors of the house to the main hall, of gold.

[^1]: This altar of burnt offering is not described in 1 Kings 7, but see 1 Kings 8:64. Verses 2–22 parallel 1 Kings 7:23–26 and 38–50.
[^2]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the altar was about 30 feet square and 15 feet high (9 by 9 by 4.5 m), and the Sea about 15 feet (4.5 m) across, 7½ feet (2.3 m) high and 45 feet (13.5 m) around.
[^3]: A huge basin of bronze, called "the Sea" for its size (verse 6).
[^4]: 1 Kings 7:24 has *peqa'im*, "gourds", where this verse has *beqarim*, "oxen", a word that looks similar in Hebrew.
[^5]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean.
[^6]: About 3 inches (7.5 cm).
[^7]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters), so the Sea held about 18,000 gallons (66,000 liters). 1 Kings 7:26 reads "two thousand baths".
[^8]: Hebrew *kiyyor*, the word used for the bronze basin of the tabernacle (Exodus 30:18). Also in verse 14.
[^9]: That is, on the south and on the north. Directions were given facing east, so the right side is the south. Also in verses 7 and 8.
[^10]: Literally "the work of the burnt offering".
[^11]: Hebrew *mishpat*, here "prescribed pattern". Also in verse 20.
[^12]: Hebrew *heikhal*, the large main room of the house, in front of the inner sanctuary (3:17). Also in verses 8 and 22.
[^13]: Hebrew *mizraqim*: bowls, probably for sprinkling the blood of the sacrifices (Exodus 27:3). Also in verses 11 and 22.
[^14]: Hebrew *azarah*, a word for a courtyard found in Chronicles and Ezekiel. Also later in the verse.
[^15]: Literally "from the right shoulder, eastward, opposite the south".
[^16]: The craftsman Huram-abi (2:12; verse 16). The Hebrew text is written "Hiram" in the second half of the verse but read "Huram".
[^17]: Hebrew *sirot*. 1 Kings 7:40 has *kiyyorot*, "basins". Also in verse 16.
[^18]: Hebrew *gullot*, "bowls": the rounded parts of the capitals. Also later in the verse and in verse 13.
[^19]: Literally "on the face of the pillars".
[^20]: Hebrew *mekhonot*: bronze stands on wheels, each carrying a basin (1 Kings 7:27–38).
[^21]: Hebrew *Huram aviv*, "Huram his father". Compare 2:12.
[^22]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^23]: Literally "in the thickness of the ground", probably clay ground suitable for molds.
[^24]: 1 Kings 7:46 has Zarethan.
[^25]: The altar of incense (Exodus 30:1–6; 39:38).
[^26]: Literally "bread of the face": bread set out in the LORD's presence (Exodus 25:30; Leviticus 24:5–9).
[^27]: Hebrew *devir*: the innermost room, the most holy place (3:16).
[^28]: Hebrew *zahav sagur*, perhaps "refined gold" or "solid gold". Also in verse 22.
[^29]: Hebrew *perach*, "blossom": flower-shaped ornaments on the lampstands (Exodus 25:31).
[^30]: Literally "it was the perfection of gold". The meaning is uncertain.
[^31]: Hebrew *kappot*, "palms": small dishes, perhaps for incense (Exodus 25:29).
[^32]: 1 Kings 7:50 has "the sockets" (Hebrew *potot*), a word that looks similar to *petach*, "entrance".
[^33]: Literally "the holy of holies".
