**1** The inhabitants of Jerusalem made Ahaziah, his youngest son, king in his place, for the raiding band that came with the Arabs to the camp had killed all the older sons. So Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah became king.[^1]

**2** Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he became king,[^2] and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Athaliah daughter of Omri.[^3]

**3** He too walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counselor in doing evil.

**4** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh,[^4] as the house of Ahab had done, for after his father's death they were his counselors, to his ruin.

**5** He also followed their advice and went with Jehoram son of Ahab king of Israel to war against Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead, and the Arameans wounded Joram.[^5]

**6** He went back to Jezreel to recover from the wounds they had given him at Ramah,[^6] when he fought against Hazael king of Aram. And Azariah[^7] son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to Jezreel to see Jehoram son of Ahab, because he was sick.

**7** Ahaziah's downfall came from God, through his visit to Joram. When he arrived, he went out with Jehoram to meet Jehu son of Nimshi,[^8] whom Yahweh had anointed to cut off the house of Ahab.

**8** While Jehu was executing judgment on the house of Ahab, he found the leaders of Judah and the sons of Ahaziah's brothers, who were serving Ahaziah, and he killed them.[^9]

**9** He searched for Ahaziah, and they captured him while he was hiding in Samaria. They brought him to Jehu and put him to death.[^10] They buried him, for they said, "He is the grandson[^11] of Jehoshaphat, who sought Yahweh with all his heart." So there was no one in the house of Ahaziah strong enough to rule the kingdom.

**10** When Athaliah, Ahaziah's mother, saw that her son was dead, she set out to destroy[^12] the whole royal family[^13] of the house of Judah.[^14]

**11** But Jehoshabeath,[^15] the king's daughter, took Joash son of Ahaziah and stole him away from among the king's sons who were being put to death. She put him and his nurse in the bedroom.[^16] So Jehoshabeath, the daughter of King Jehoram and the wife of Jehoiada the priest, since she was Ahaziah's sister, hid him from Athaliah, and Athaliah did not put him to death.

**12** He remained with them, hidden in the house of God, for six years, while Athaliah reigned over the land.

[^1]: Verses 1–6 parallel 2 Kings 8:24–29.
[^2]: 2 Kings 8:26 gives his age as twenty-two. According to 21:20, his father died at the age of forty.
[^3]: In Hebrew "daughter" can mean any female descendant. Athaliah was a daughter of Ahab (21:6), Omri's son.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Jehoram son of Ahab. *Joram* is a shorter form of the same name. Also in verse 7.
[^6]: That is, Ramoth-gilead (verse 5).
[^7]: That is, Ahaziah. The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "Ahaziah", as in 2 Kings 8:29.
[^8]: Jehu was the son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi (2 Kings 9:2, 14).
[^9]: Compare 2 Kings 10:12–14.
[^10]: 2 Kings 9:27–28 tells his death differently: he was struck down near Ibleam, fled to Megiddo and died there, and his servants buried him in Jerusalem.
[^11]: Literally "son".
[^12]: Hebrew *va-tedabber*, an unusual word. 2 Kings 11:1 has *va-te'abbed*, "and she destroyed".
[^13]: Literally "all the offspring of the kingdom".
[^14]: Verses 10–12 parallel 2 Kings 11:1–3.
[^15]: Called Jehosheba in 2 Kings 11:2. Also later in the verse.
[^16]: Literally "the room of the beds", perhaps a storeroom for bedding.
