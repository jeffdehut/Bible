**1** The Spirit of God came on Azariah son of Oded.

**2** He went out to meet Asa and said to him, "Listen to me, Asa and all Judah and Benjamin. Yahweh[^1] is with you when you are with him. If you seek him, he will be found by you, but if you abandon him, he will abandon you.

**3** For a long time Israel was without the true God, without a priest to teach, and without instruction.[^2]

**4** But in their distress they turned to Yahweh, the God of Israel. They sought him, and he was found by them.

**5** In those times there was no peace for anyone who went out or came in, for great turmoil afflicted all the inhabitants of the lands.

**6** They were crushed, nation against nation and city against city, for God troubled them with every kind of distress.

**7** But as for you, be strong and do not lose heart,[^3] for your work will be rewarded."

**8** When Asa heard these words and the prophecy of Oded the prophet,[^4] he took courage. He removed the detestable idols from the whole land of Judah and Benjamin and from the cities he had captured in the hill country of Ephraim. And he restored the altar of Yahweh that was in front of the porch of the house of Yahweh.[^5]

**9** He gathered all Judah and Benjamin, and those from Ephraim, Manasseh and Simeon who were living among them, for many had deserted to him from Israel when they saw that Yahweh his God was with him.

**10** They gathered in Jerusalem in the third month of the fifteenth year of Asa's reign.

**11** On that day they sacrificed to Yahweh, from the plunder they had brought, seven hundred cattle and seven thousand sheep and goats.

**12** They entered into a covenant to seek Yahweh, the God of their ancestors, with all their heart and with all their soul.

**13** Anyone who would not seek Yahweh, the God of Israel, was to be put to death, whether small or great, man or woman.

**14** They swore an oath to Yahweh with a loud voice, with shouting, and with trumpets and rams' horns.

**15** All Judah rejoiced over the oath, for they had sworn with all their heart and sought him with all their desire, and he was found by them. So Yahweh gave them rest on every side.

**16** King Asa even removed Maacah, his mother,[^6] from being queen mother,[^7] because she had made an obscene image[^8] for Asherah.[^9] Asa cut down her obscene image, crushed it and burned it in the Kidron Valley.

**17** But the high places[^10] were not taken away from Israel. Yet Asa's heart was fully devoted[^11] all his days.

**18** He brought into the house of God the things his father had dedicated and the things he himself had dedicated: silver, gold and utensils.

**19** There was no more war until the thirty-fifth year of Asa's reign.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, law". The Hebrew has no verb here, and verses 3–6 can also be read as a description of the future: "For a long time Israel will be without …".
[^3]: Literally "do not let your hands drop".
[^4]: The Hebrew reads "and the prophecy, Oded the prophet". The Vulgate reads "the prophecy of Azariah son of Oded the prophet", as in verse 1.
[^5]: Literally "in front of the porch of Yahweh".
[^6]: Maacah was the mother of Abijah, Asa's father (11:20–22). In Hebrew "mother" can also mean grandmother. Verses 16–18 parallel 1 Kings 15:13–15.
[^7]: Hebrew *gevirah*, "great lady": the title of the king's mother, who held an official position at court.
[^8]: Hebrew *mifletset*, "a horrible thing". Its exact nature is unknown. Also later in the verse.
[^9]: A Canaanite goddess, whose symbol was a wooden pole or tree.
[^10]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops. Compare 14:2, 4.
[^11]: Literally "was whole". 1 Kings 15:14 adds "with Yahweh".
