**1** After these acts of faithfulness,[^1] Sennacherib king of Assyria came and invaded Judah.[^2] He besieged the fortified cities and intended to break into them for himself.

**2** When Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib had come and that he intended to make war on Jerusalem,

**3** he consulted with his officials and his warriors about stopping up the water of the springs outside the city, and they helped him.

**4** A large number of people gathered and stopped up all the springs and the stream that flowed through the land. "Why should the kings of Assyria come and find plenty of water?" they said.

**5** He worked hard and rebuilt all the wall that had been broken down, and raised towers on it,[^3] and he built another wall outside it. He strengthened the Millo[^4] in the City of David, and he made weapons and shields in great numbers.

**6** He appointed military commanders over the people and gathered them to him in the square at the city gate, and he spoke encouragingly to them:[^5]

**7** "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or dismayed before the king of Assyria and all the horde that is with him, for there is more with us than with him.

**8** With him is an arm of flesh,[^6] but with us is Yahweh[^7] our God to help us and to fight our battles." And the people took confidence from the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.

**9** After this, while Sennacherib king of Assyria was at Lachish with all his forces, he sent his servants to Jerusalem, to Hezekiah king of Judah and to all the people of Judah who were in Jerusalem, saying,

**10** "This is what Sennacherib king of Assyria says: On what are you relying, that you stay in Jerusalem under siege?

**11** Is not Hezekiah misleading you, to hand you over to die by hunger and thirst, when he says, 'Yahweh our God will rescue us from the hand of the king of Assyria'?

**12** Is not this Hezekiah the one who removed his high places[^8] and his altars and said to Judah and Jerusalem, 'You must worship before one altar, and on it you must burn incense'?

**13** Do you not know what I and my ancestors have done to all the peoples of the lands? Were the gods of the nations of the lands at all able to rescue their land from my hand?

**14** Who among all the gods of those nations that my ancestors destroyed[^9] was able to rescue his people from my hand, that your God should be able to rescue you from my hand?

**15** So now do not let Hezekiah deceive you or mislead you like this. Do not believe him, for no god of any nation or kingdom has been able to rescue his people from my hand or from the hand of my ancestors. How much less will your God rescue you from my hand!"

**16** His servants said still more against Yahweh God and against his servant Hezekiah.

**17** He also wrote letters to insult Yahweh, the God of Israel, and to speak against him, saying, "Just as the gods of the nations of the lands did not rescue their people from my hand, so the God of Hezekiah will not rescue his people from my hand."

**18** They called out in a loud voice in the language of Judah[^10] to the people of Jerusalem who were on the wall, to frighten them and terrify them, so that they could capture the city.

**19** They spoke about the God of Jerusalem as they spoke about the gods of the peoples of the earth, the work of human hands.

**20** Then King Hezekiah and Isaiah the prophet son of Amoz prayed about this and cried out to heaven.

**21** Yahweh sent an angel,[^11] who wiped out every mighty warrior, leader and commander in the camp of the king of Assyria. So he returned in disgrace to his own land. When he went into the house of his god, some of his own sons[^12] struck him down there with the sword.

**22** So Yahweh saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from the hand of all others, and he guided them on every side.[^13]

**23** Many brought offerings to Yahweh in Jerusalem and precious gifts to Hezekiah king of Judah, and from then on he was exalted in the eyes of all the nations.

**24** In those days Hezekiah became sick and was at the point of death. He prayed to Yahweh, who spoke to him and gave him a sign.[^14]

**25** But Hezekiah did not respond in keeping with the kindness done to him, for his heart was proud. So wrath came on him and on Judah and Jerusalem.

**26** Then Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, both he and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so that the wrath of Yahweh did not come on them in the days of Hezekiah.

**27** Hezekiah had very great riches and honor. He made himself treasuries for silver, gold, precious stones, spices, shields and all kinds of costly articles,

**28** storehouses for the produce of grain, new wine and olive oil, stalls for all kinds of livestock, and folds for the flocks.[^15]

**29** He made cities for himself, and he had flocks and herds in abundance, for God had given him very great wealth.

**30** It was Hezekiah who stopped up the upper outlet of the waters of the Gihon and channeled them down to the west side of the City of David.[^16] Hezekiah prospered in all his works.

**31** But in the matter of the envoys of the officials of Babylon, who had been sent to him to inquire about the sign that had happened in the land, God left him to himself, to test him and to know all that was in his heart.[^17]

**32** The rest of the acts of Hezekiah and his deeds of faithful love[^18] are written in the vision of Isaiah the prophet son of Amoz, in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.[^19]

**33** Hezekiah slept with his ancestors,[^20] and they buried him on the ascent[^21] to the tombs of the descendants of David. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem honored him at his death. And his son Manasseh became king in his place.

[^1]: Literally "After these things and this faithfulness".
[^2]: Verses 1–23 give a shorter account of the events in 2 Kings 18:13–19:37 and Isaiah 36–37, which took place in 701 BC.
[^3]: Literally "and he went up on the towers". The meaning is uncertain.
[^4]: Hebrew *millo*, "filling": probably a terrace or earthwork supporting buildings in the City of David.
[^5]: Literally "spoke to their heart".
[^6]: That is, mere human strength.
[^7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^9]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to a god, which in war meant destroying it.
[^10]: Literally "in Judean", that is, Hebrew (2 Kings 18:26, 28).
[^11]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both.
[^12]: Literally "some of those who came out of his own body". See 2 Kings 19:37.
[^13]: The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "and he gave them rest on every side".
[^14]: See 2 Kings 20:1–11 and Isaiah 38.
[^15]: Literally "and flocks for folds".
[^16]: Probably the tunnel that still carries water from the Gihon spring to the Pool of Siloam in Jerusalem (2 Kings 20:20).
[^17]: See 2 Kings 20:12–19 and Isaiah 39.
[^18]: Hebrew *chasadim*, plural of *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^19]: Compare Isaiah 1:1.
[^20]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^21]: Or "in the upper part of".
