**1** At the end of twenty years, in which Solomon had built the house of the LORD[^1] and his own house,

**2** Solomon built up the cities that Huram[^2] had given him, and he settled Israelites there.

**3** Solomon went to Hamath-zobah and seized it.[^3]

**4** He built Tadmor[^4] in the wilderness and all the storage cities that he built in Hamath.

**5** He built Upper Beth-horon and Lower Beth-horon, fortified cities with walls, gates and bars,

**6** and Baalath, and all the storage cities that Solomon had, all the cities for the chariots and the cities for the horsemen,[^5] and whatever Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon and in all the land under his rule.

**7** As for all the people who were left of the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, who were not of Israel,

**8** their descendants who were left after them in the land, whom the Israelites had not wiped out, Solomon conscripted them as forced labor,[^6] as they are to this day.

**9** But Solomon did not make slaves of any of the Israelites for his work. They were his soldiers, the commanders of his officers,[^7] and the commanders of his chariots and his horsemen.

**10** These were King Solomon's chief officers,[^8] 250 of them,[^9] who directed the people.

**11** Solomon brought Pharaoh's daughter up from the City of David to the house he had built for her,[^10] for he said, "My wife must not live in the house of David king of Israel, for the places to which the ark of the LORD has come are holy."

**12** Then Solomon offered burnt offerings to the LORD on the altar of the LORD that he had built in front of the porch,

**13** as each day required, offering them according to the command of Moses for the Sabbaths, the new moons and the appointed festivals three times a year: the Festival of Bread Made without Yeast, the Festival of Weeks and the Festival of Shelters.

**14** Following the ruling of his father David, he appointed the divisions of the priests for their service, and the Levites for their duties of praising and ministering beside the priests, as each day required, and the gatekeepers by their divisions for each gate, for this was what David the man of God had commanded.

**15** They did not turn aside from the king's command to the priests and the Levites in any matter, including the treasuries.

**16** So all Solomon's work was carried out, from the day the foundation of the house of the LORD was laid until it was finished.[^11] The house of the LORD was complete.

**17** Then Solomon went to Ezion-geber and to Eloth on the seashore, in the land of Edom.

**18** Huram sent him ships by his servants, with servants who knew the sea. They went with Solomon's servants to Ophir[^12] and took from there 450 talents of gold,[^13] which they brought to King Solomon.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Verses 1–18 parallel 1 Kings 9:10–28.
[^2]: Hiram king of Tyre (2:2; 2:3 in most English Bibles). Also in verse 18. According to 1 Kings 9:11–13, Solomon gave Hiram twenty towns in Galilee.
[^3]: Literally "and was strong against it".
[^4]: The oasis city later called Palmyra, in the Syrian desert (1 Kings 9:18).
[^5]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*. Also in verse 9.
[^6]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs conscripted to labor for the king (1 Kings 4:6; 5:27, which is 5:13 in most English Bibles).
[^7]: Hebrew *shalishim*, an officer's title of uncertain meaning, perhaps from "third", the third man in a chariot.
[^8]: Literally "the officers of the officials". The Hebrew text is written *netsivim* but read *nitsavim*; both mean "officials" or "governors".
[^9]: 1 Kings 9:23 gives 550.
[^10]: See 1 Kings 3:1; 9:24.
[^11]: Literally "until the day of the foundation of the house of the LORD and until its completion".
[^12]: A land famous for its gold, perhaps in southern Arabia or on the coast of East Africa.
[^13]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so 450 talents was about 34,000 pounds (15,300 kg). 1 Kings 9:28 gives 420 talents.
