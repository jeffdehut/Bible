**1** When Solomon finished praying, fire came down from heaven and consumed the burnt offering and the sacrifices, and the glory of the LORD[^1] filled the house.

**2** The priests could not enter the house of the LORD, because the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.

**3** When all the Israelites saw the fire come down and the glory of the LORD on the house, they bowed down on the pavement with their faces to the ground. They worshiped and gave thanks to the LORD, "For he is good, for his faithful love[^2] endures forever."

**4** Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices before the LORD.[^3]

**5** King Solomon offered as a sacrifice 22,000 cattle and 120,000 sheep and goats. So the king and all the people dedicated the house of God.

**6** The priests stood at their posts, and so did the Levites with the instruments for music to the LORD that King David had made for giving thanks to the LORD, "for his faithful love endures forever," whenever David offered praise through them.[^4] Opposite them the priests blew trumpets, and all Israel was standing.

**7** Solomon consecrated the middle of the courtyard that was in front of the house of the LORD, for there he offered the burnt offerings and the fat of the fellowship offerings,[^5] because the bronze altar that Solomon had made could not hold the burnt offering, the grain offering and the fat.

**8** At that time Solomon held the festival[^6] for seven days, and all Israel with him, a very great assembly from Lebo-hamath[^7] to the Wadi of Egypt.[^8]

**9** On the eighth day they held a closing assembly,[^9] for they had celebrated the dedication of the altar for seven days and the festival for seven days.

**10** On the twenty-third day of the seventh month he sent the people away to their homes,[^10] joyful and glad of heart for the good that the LORD had done for David and for Solomon and for his people Israel.

**11** So Solomon finished the house of the LORD and the king's house.[^11] He succeeded in carrying out all that Solomon had in mind to do in the house of the LORD and in his own house.

**12** Then the LORD appeared to Solomon at night and said to him, "I have heard your prayer, and I have chosen this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.

**13** When I shut up the heavens so that there is no rain, or command the locusts to devour the land, or send plague among my people,

**14** and my people who are called by my name[^12] humble themselves, pray, seek my face and turn from their evil ways, then I will hear from heaven, forgive their sin and heal their land.

**15** Now my eyes will be open and my ears attentive to the prayer made in this place.[^13]

**16** Now I have chosen and consecrated this house so that my name may be there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there for all time.

**17** As for you, if you walk before me as your father David walked, doing all that I have commanded you, and if you keep my decrees and my rulings,

**18** then I will establish the throne of your kingdom, as I covenanted with your father David[^14] when I said, 'You will never lack a man to rule over Israel.'[^15]

**19** But if you[^16] turn away and abandon my decrees and my commands that I have set before you, and go and serve other gods and bow down to them,

**20** then I will uproot them[^17] from my land that I have given them, and this house that I have consecrated for my name I will cast out of my sight. I will make it a byword and an object of ridicule[^18] among all the peoples.

**21** And as for this house, which was so exalted,[^19] everyone who passes by it will be appalled and will say, 'Why has the LORD done this to this land and to this house?'

**22** And people will answer, 'Because they abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who brought them out of the land of Egypt, and they embraced other gods and bowed down to them and served them. That is why he has brought all this disaster on them.'"

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Verses 1–3 are not in 1 Kings 8, which tells instead how Solomon rose from his knees and blessed the assembly (1 Kings 8:54–61).
[^2]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 6.
[^3]: Verses 4–10 parallel 1 Kings 8:62–66.
[^4]: Literally "when David praised by their hand".
[^5]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings": sacrifices shared in a meal by the worshipers.
[^6]: The Festival of Shelters (5:3).
[^7]: Or "the entrance to Hamath", at the northern limit of the land (Numbers 34:8).
[^8]: Hebrew *nachal Mitsrayim*, a seasonal stream on the border of Egypt, probably the Wadi el-Arish in northern Sinai.
[^9]: Hebrew *atseret*, a solemn gathering, here the one that closed the festival (Leviticus 23:36).
[^10]: Literally "to their tents".
[^11]: Verses 11–22 parallel 1 Kings 9:1–9. Verses 12b–15 are not in Kings.
[^12]: Literally "over whom my name is called", a phrase that marks ownership.
[^13]: Literally "to the prayer of this place", as in 6:40.
[^14]: Literally "as I cut for David your father", the idiom for making a covenant.
[^15]: Literally "A man will not be cut off for you, ruling in Israel." 1 Kings 9:5 has "on the throne of Israel".
[^16]: Here "you" is plural.
[^17]: That is, the Israelites.
[^18]: Literally "a proverb and a sharp saying".
[^19]: Literally "which was high". Compare 1 Kings 9:8.
