**1** Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor, and he made a marriage alliance with Ahab.[^1]

**2** Some years later he went down to Ahab in Samaria.[^2] Ahab slaughtered sheep and cattle in abundance for him and for the people who were with him, and he persuaded him to go up against Ramoth-gilead.

**3** Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, "Will you go with me to Ramoth-gilead?" He answered him, "I am as you are, my people as your people. We will be with you in the battle."

**4** But Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "Please inquire first for the word of the LORD."[^3]

**5** So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, four hundred men, and said to them, "Shall we go to battle against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?" They said, "Go up, for God will hand it over to the king."

**6** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no other prophet of the LORD here, so that we can inquire of him?"

**7** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man through whom we can inquire of the LORD, but I hate him, because he never prophesies anything good about me, but always disaster. He is Micaiah son of Imla." Jehoshaphat said, "The king should not say that."

**8** So the king of Israel called an officer[^4] and said, "Bring Micaiah son of Imla quickly."

**9** The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting each on his throne, dressed in their robes. They were sitting at the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.

**10** Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had made himself horns of iron, and he said, "This is what the LORD says: With these you will gore the Arameans until they are destroyed."

**11** All the prophets were prophesying the same, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-gilead and succeed. The LORD will hand it over to the king."

**12** The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him, "Look, the prophets with one voice are speaking favorably to the king.[^5] Please let your word be like that of one of them, and speak favorably."

**13** But Micaiah said, "As the LORD lives, whatever my God says, that is what I will speak."

**14** When he came to the king, the king said to him, "Micah,[^6] shall we go to battle against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?" He said, "Go up and succeed. They will be handed over to you."

**15** But the king said to him, "How many times must I make you swear to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?"

**16** Then he said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains, like sheep that have no shepherd. And the LORD said, 'These have no master. Let each of them go home in peace.'"

**17** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Didn't I tell you he would not prophesy anything good about me, only disaster?"

**18** Micaiah said,[^7] "Therefore hear the word of the LORD. I saw the LORD sitting on his throne, with the whole host of heaven[^8] standing on his right and on his left.

**19** And the LORD said, 'Who will entice Ahab king of Israel to go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?' One said one thing, and another said another.

**20** Then a spirit[^9] came forward and stood before the LORD and said, 'I will entice him.' The LORD said to him, 'How?'

**21** He said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' The LORD said, 'You will entice him, and you will succeed. Go and do it.'

**22** So now, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of these prophets of yours. The LORD has pronounced disaster against you."[^10]

**23** Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the Spirit of the LORD go when he left me to speak to you?"

**24** Micaiah said, "You will see on the day when you go into an inner room[^11] to hide."

**25** The king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and bring him back to Amon the governor of the city and to Joash the king's son,[^12]

**26** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this fellow in prison and feed him on scant bread and water[^13] until I come back safely.'"

**27** Micaiah said, "If you ever come back safely, the LORD has not spoken through me." And he said, "Listen, all you peoples!"[^14]

**28** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**29** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself, and they went into battle.

**30** Now the king of Aram had commanded his chariot commanders, "Do not fight with anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel alone."

**31** When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "That is the king of Israel," and they turned to fight against him. But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him. God drew them away from him.[^15]

**32** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.

**33** But a man drew his bow at random[^16] and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armor.[^17] He said to his chariot driver, "Turn around[^18] and get me out of the fighting,[^19] for I am wounded."

**34** The battle grew fierce that day, and the king of Israel propped himself up in his chariot facing the Arameans until evening. At sunset he died.

[^1]: Jehoshaphat's son Jehoram married Ahab's daughter (21:6; 2 Kings 8:18).
[^2]: Verses 2–34 parallel 1 Kings 22:2–35.
[^3]: Literally "Please seek, as the day, the word of the LORD", that is, today, before going. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official; the word can mean "eunuch".
[^5]: Literally "the words of the prophets are one mouth good to the king".
[^6]: A short form of the name Micaiah.
[^7]: Literally "He said".
[^8]: The heavenly beings who serve the LORD.
[^9]: Literally "the spirit". Hebrew *ruach* can also mean "wind" or "breath".
[^10]: Literally "The LORD has spoken evil concerning you."
[^11]: Literally "room within room".
[^12]: "The king's son" may be a title for a royal official rather than a son of Ahab.
[^13]: Literally "bread of oppression and water of oppression".
[^14]: The same words begin the book of Micah (Micah 1:2).
[^15]: The words "and the LORD helped him. God drew them away from him" are not in 1 Kings 22:32.
[^16]: Literally "in his innocence", that is, without aiming at anyone in particular.
[^17]: Literally "between the attachments and the coat of mail": a gap in the armor.
[^18]: Literally "Turn your hand". The Hebrew text is written "your hands" but read "your hand".
[^19]: Literally "out of the camp", here the army in battle.
