**1** When Rehoboam's kingdom was established and he had become strong, he abandoned the law of Yahweh,[^1] and all Israel with him.

**2** In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak[^2] king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had been unfaithful[^3] to Yahweh,

**3** with 1,200 chariots and 60,000 horsemen.[^4] The people who came with him from Egypt were beyond number: Libyans, Sukkites[^5] and Cushites.[^6]

**4** He captured the fortified cities of Judah and came as far as Jerusalem.

**5** Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and the leaders of Judah, who had gathered at Jerusalem because of Shishak, and he said to them, "This is what Yahweh says: You have abandoned me, so I have abandoned you into the hand of Shishak."

**6** Then the leaders of Israel and the king humbled themselves and said, "Yahweh is righteous."

**7** When Yahweh saw that they had humbled themselves, the word of Yahweh came to Shemaiah: "They have humbled themselves. I will not destroy them. I will soon give them some deliverance,[^7] and my wrath will not be poured out on Jerusalem by the hand of Shishak.

**8** But they will become his servants, so that they may learn the difference between serving me and serving the kingdoms of other lands."[^8]

**9** So Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem. He took away the treasures of the house of Yahweh and the treasures of the king's house. He took everything. He also took the gold shields that Solomon had made.

**10** So King Rehoboam made bronze shields to replace them, and he entrusted them to the commanders of the guard[^9] who kept watch at the entrance of the king's house.

**11** Whenever the king went into the house of Yahweh, the guards came and carried them, and then they brought them back to the guardroom.

**12** Because he humbled himself, the anger of Yahweh turned away from him, so as not to destroy him completely. Indeed, there was still some good in Judah.[^10]

**13** King Rehoboam established himself firmly in Jerusalem and reigned. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city Yahweh had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite.

**14** He did what was evil, because he did not set his heart on seeking Yahweh.

**15** As for the acts of Rehoboam, from first to last, are they not written in the records[^11] of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer, in the genealogies?[^12] There were wars between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.[^13]

**16** Rehoboam slept with his ancestors[^14] and was buried in the City of David. And his son Abijah[^15] became king in his place.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Verses 2–16 parallel 1 Kings 14:21–31, with additions.
[^2]: Pharaoh Sheshonq I, who led a campaign into Judah and Israel about 925 BC.
[^3]: Hebrew *ma'al*: to break faith, to betray a trust. A key word in Chronicles.
[^4]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*.
[^5]: A people not otherwise known, perhaps from Libya.
[^6]: People from Cush, the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia).
[^7]: Or "I will give them deliverance in a little while".
[^8]: Literally "and they will know my service and the service of the kingdoms of the lands".
[^9]: Literally "the runners": the king's bodyguard, who ran ahead of his chariot. Also in verse 11.
[^10]: Literally "and also in Judah there were good things".
[^11]: Literally "the words".
[^12]: Literally "to be enrolled by genealogy". The meaning here is uncertain.
[^13]: Literally "all the days".
[^14]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^15]: Called Abijam in 1 Kings 14:31 and 15:1–8.
