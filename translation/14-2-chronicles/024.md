**1** Joash was seven years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Zibiah, from Beersheba.[^1]

**2** Joash did what was right in the eyes of the LORD[^2] all the days of Jehoiada the priest.

**3** Jehoiada took two wives for him, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**4** Some time later Joash set his heart on restoring the house of the LORD.

**5** He gathered the priests and the Levites and said to them, "Go out to the cities of Judah and collect money from all Israel to repair the house of your God year by year. Do it quickly." But the Levites did not act quickly.

**6** So the king summoned Jehoiada the chief priest[^3] and said to him, "Why have you not required the Levites to bring in from Judah and Jerusalem the tax that Moses the servant of the LORD and the assembly of Israel imposed,[^4] for the tent of the testimony?"[^5]

**7** For the sons of that wicked woman Athaliah had broken into the house of God and had even used all the holy things of the house of the LORD for the Baals.[^6]

**8** At the king's command they made a chest and set it outside, at the gate of the house of the LORD.[^7]

**9** A proclamation was issued in Judah and Jerusalem to bring to the LORD the tax that Moses the servant of God had imposed on Israel in the wilderness.

**10** All the officials and all the people rejoiced, and they brought their money and threw it into the chest until it was full.[^8]

**11** Whenever the chest was brought by the Levites to the king's officials, and they saw that there was a large amount of money, the king's secretary and the officer of the chief priest would come and empty the chest, then carry it back to its place. They did this day after day and collected a great deal of money.

**12** The king and Jehoiada gave it to those who did the work of the service of the house of the LORD. They hired stonecutters and carpenters to restore the house of the LORD, and also workers in iron and bronze to repair the house of the LORD.

**13** The workmen did their work, and the repairs went forward in their hands.[^9] They restored the house of God to its proper design and strengthened it.

**14** When they had finished, they brought the rest of the money before the king and Jehoiada, and with it utensils were made for the house of the LORD: utensils for the service and for the burnt offerings, dishes,[^10] and articles of gold and silver.[^11] They offered burnt offerings in the house of the LORD continually, all the days of Jehoiada.

**15** Jehoiada grew old and full of days, and he died. He was 130 years old when he died.

**16** They buried him in the City of David with the kings, because he had done good in Israel, for God and for his house.

**17** After the death of Jehoiada the officials of Judah came and bowed down to the king. Then the king listened to them.

**18** They abandoned the house of the LORD, the God of their ancestors, and served the Asherah poles[^12] and the idols. Because of this guilt of theirs, wrath came on Judah and Jerusalem.

**19** Yet he sent prophets among them to bring them back to the LORD. They warned them, but they would not listen.

**20** Then the Spirit of God clothed[^13] Zechariah[^14] son of Jehoiada the priest. He stood up above the people and said to them, "This is what God says: Why do you transgress the commands of the LORD, so that you cannot prosper? Because you have abandoned the LORD, he has abandoned you."

**21** But they conspired against him, and at the king's command they stoned him to death in the courtyard of the house of the LORD.

**22** King Joash did not remember the faithful love[^15] that Zechariah's father Jehoiada[^16] had shown him, but killed his son. As he was dying, he said, "May the LORD see and call you to account!"

**23** At the turn of the year[^17] the army of Aram came up against Joash. They came to Judah and Jerusalem and destroyed all the leaders of the people from among the people, and they sent all their plunder to the king of Damascus.

**24** Although the army of Aram came with only a few men, the LORD handed over to them a very great army, because Judah had abandoned the LORD, the God of their ancestors. So they executed judgment on Joash.

**25** When they withdrew from him, they left him severely wounded. His servants conspired against him because of the blood of the sons[^18] of Jehoiada the priest, and they killed him on his bed, and he died. They buried him in the City of David, but they did not bury him in the tombs of the kings.

**26** These were the ones who conspired against him: Zabad son of Shimeath the Ammonite woman, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith the Moabite woman.[^19]

**27** As for his sons, the many oracles against him,[^20] and the rebuilding of the house of God, they are written in the commentary[^21] on the book of the kings. And his son Amaziah became king in his place.

[^1]: This chapter parallels 2 Kings 12 (11:21–12:21 in most English Bibles), with many differences.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "the head".
[^4]: See Exodus 30:12–16.
[^5]: That is, the tabernacle, named after the tablets of the covenant kept in the ark (Hebrew *edut*, "testimony"; Exodus 25:16; Numbers 1:50).
[^6]: Plural of *Baal*, "lord, master": the Canaanite god Baal, worshiped in many local forms.
[^7]: Compare 2 Kings 12:10 (12:9 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: Or "until they had all given".
[^9]: Literally "and healing went up for the work by their hand".
[^10]: Hebrew *kappot*, "palms": small dishes, perhaps for incense (4:22).
[^11]: 2 Kings 12:14 (12:13 in most English Bibles) says that no articles of gold or silver were made from this money.
[^12]: Hebrew *asherim*: wooden poles or trees that were symbols of the goddess Asherah.
[^13]: Literally "clothed itself with". Compare Judges 6:34.
[^14]: Probably the Zechariah mentioned in Matthew 23:35 and Luke 11:51.
[^15]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^16]: Literally "Jehoiada his father".
[^17]: That is, in the spring.
[^18]: The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "son".
[^19]: 2 Kings 12:22 (12:21 in most English Bibles) has "Jozabad son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer".
[^20]: Or "the heavy tribute laid on him". Hebrew *massa* can mean "oracle" or "burden". The Hebrew text is written "and the abundance of the oracle" but read "and the oracle was great".
[^21]: Hebrew *midrash*, "study" or "account" (13:22).
