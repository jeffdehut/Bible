**1** Jehoshaphat slept with his ancestors[^1] and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. And his son Jehoram became king in his place.

**2** He had brothers, sons of Jehoshaphat: Azariah, Jehiel, Zechariah, Azariah,[^2] Michael and Shephatiah. All these were sons of Jehoshaphat king of Israel.[^3]

**3** Their father gave them many gifts of silver, gold and precious things, along with fortified cities in Judah, but he gave the kingdom to Jehoram, because he was the firstborn.

**4** When Jehoram had taken over his father's kingdom and had made himself strong, he killed all his brothers with the sword, and also some of the leaders of Israel.

**5** Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.[^4]

**6** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for a daughter of Ahab was his wife. He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh.[^5]

**7** But Yahweh was not willing to destroy the house of David, because of the covenant he had made with David, and since he had promised to give a lamp[^6] to him and to his descendants forever.

**8** In his days Edom rebelled against Judah's rule[^7] and set up a king of their own.

**9** So Jehoram crossed over with his commanders, and all his chariots with him.[^8] He rose by night and struck the Edomites who had surrounded him and the commanders of the chariots.

**10** So Edom has been in rebellion against Judah's rule to this day. Libnah also rebelled against his rule at that time, because he had abandoned Yahweh, the God of his ancestors.

**11** He also made high places[^9] in the hills of Judah, and he led the inhabitants of Jerusalem to be unfaithful[^10] and led Judah astray.

**12** A letter came to him from Elijah the prophet, saying, "This is what Yahweh, the God of your ancestor David,[^11] says: Because you have not walked in the ways of your father Jehoshaphat or in the ways of Asa king of Judah,

**13** but have walked in the way of the kings of Israel, and have led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem to be unfaithful, as the house of Ahab did, and have also killed your brothers, your own father's family, who were better than you,

**14** Yahweh is about to strike your people, your sons, your wives and all your possessions with a great blow.

**15** And you yourself will suffer severe sickness, a disease of your bowels, until your bowels come out because of the disease, day after day."

**16** Yahweh stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the Philistines and of the Arabs who live near the Cushites.[^12]

**17** They came up against Judah, broke into it and carried off all the possessions found in the king's house, and also his sons and his wives. Not a son was left to him except Jehoahaz,[^13] his youngest son.

**18** After all this Yahweh struck him in his bowels with an incurable disease.

**19** In the course of time, at the end of two years,[^14] his bowels came out because of his disease, and he died in great pain. His people made no fire in his honor like the fire made for his ancestors.[^15]

**20** He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. He passed away, and no one was sorry.[^16] They buried him in the City of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

[^1]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^2]: The Hebrew has two forms of the name, *Azaryah* and *Azaryahu*.
[^3]: Chronicles sometimes uses "Israel" for Judah (12:6).
[^4]: Verses 5–10 parallel 2 Kings 8:17–22.
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: That is, a descendant on the throne to continue his line (1 Kings 11:36; 15:4).
[^7]: Literally "from under the hand of Judah". Also in verse 10.
[^8]: 2 Kings 8:21 reads "crossed over to Zair with all his chariots", and adds at the end of the verse "and the army fled to their homes".
[^9]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^10]: Literally "caused the inhabitants of Jerusalem to prostitute themselves", a common image for unfaithfulness to Yahweh. Also in verse 13.
[^11]: Literally "your father David".
[^12]: Literally "who are beside the Cushites". Compare 14:8.
[^13]: Called Ahaziah in 22:1. The two names have the same parts in reverse order and mean "Yahweh has taken hold".
[^14]: Literally "and at the time when the end came out, two days". "Days" here probably means years.
[^15]: See 16:14.
[^16]: Literally "He went without desire."
