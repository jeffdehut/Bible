**1** The queen of Sheba[^1] heard of Solomon's fame, and she came to Jerusalem to test Solomon with hard questions.[^2] She came with a very great retinue, with camels carrying spices, a great quantity of gold, and precious stones. When she came to Solomon, she talked with him about everything that was on her mind.[^3]

**2** Solomon answered all her questions.[^4] Nothing was hidden from Solomon that he could not explain to her.

**3** When the queen of Sheba saw Solomon's wisdom, the house he had built,

**4** the food on his table, the seating of his officials,[^5] the attendance of his servants and their clothing, his cupbearers and their clothing, and the ascent by which he went up to the house of Yahweh,[^6] it took her breath away.[^7]

**5** She said to the king, "The report I heard in my own country about your words[^8] and your wisdom was true.

**6** But I did not believe their reports until I came and saw it with my own eyes. And look, not even half the greatness of your wisdom was told me. You go beyond the report I heard.

**7** How happy your men must be! How happy these servants of yours, who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom!

**8** Blessed be Yahweh your God, who has delighted in you and set you on his throne as king for Yahweh your God![^9] Because your God loves Israel and will establish them forever, he has made you king over them, to do justice and what is right."[^10]

**9** Then she gave the king 120 talents[^11] of gold, a very great quantity of spices, and precious stones. There were never such spices as those the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon.

**10** The servants of Huram[^12] and the servants of Solomon, who brought gold from Ophir, also brought algum wood[^13] and precious stones.

**11** The king used the algum wood to make steps[^14] for the house of Yahweh and for the king's house, and lyres and harps for the singers. Nothing like them had been seen before in the land of Judah.

**12** King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she desired, whatever she asked for, besides what she had brought to the king.[^15] Then she turned and went back to her own country, she and her servants.

**13** The weight of the gold that came to Solomon in one year was 666 talents of gold,[^16]

**14** besides what the traders and the merchants brought.[^17] All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land also brought gold and silver to Solomon.

**15** King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold. Six hundred shekels[^18] of hammered gold went into each shield.

**16** He also made three hundred small shields of hammered gold. Three hundred shekels of gold went into each shield.[^19] The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.[^20]

**17** The king also made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with pure gold.

**18** The throne had six steps, and a footstool of gold[^21] was attached to the throne. There were armrests on each side of the seat, with two lions standing beside the armrests.

**19** Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, one on each side.[^22] Nothing like it had ever been made in any kingdom.

**20** All King Solomon's drinking vessels were gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold.[^23] Silver was considered of no value in Solomon's days.

**21** For the king had ships that went to Tarshish[^24] with Huram's servants. Once every three years the Tarshish ships came, bringing gold and silver, ivory, apes and baboons.[^25]

**22** So King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.

**23** All the kings of the earth sought an audience with Solomon[^26] to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.

**24** Each of them brought his gift: articles of silver and articles of gold, garments, weapons[^27] and spices, horses and mules, so much year by year.

**25** Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen,[^28] and he stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.[^29]

**26** He ruled over all the kings from the River[^30] to the land of the Philistines, as far as the border of Egypt.

**27** The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and he made cedar as plentiful as the sycamore-fig trees in the Shephelah.[^31]

**28** Horses were imported for Solomon from Egypt and from all the lands.

**29** As for the rest of the acts of Solomon,[^32] from first to last, are they not written in the records of Nathan the prophet,[^33] in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo[^34] the seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nebat?

**30** Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years.

**31** Solomon slept with his ancestors,[^35] and they buried him in the city of his father David. And his son Rehoboam became king in his place.

[^1]: A kingdom in southwest Arabia, in what is now Yemen. Verses 1–24 parallel 1 Kings 10:1–25.
[^2]: Hebrew *chidot*, "riddles".
[^3]: Literally "with her heart".
[^4]: Literally "told her all her words".
[^5]: Or "servants". Hebrew *avadim*. Also in verse 7.
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Hebrew *aliyyato*, "his ascent". 1 Kings 10:5 has *olato*, "his burnt offerings".
[^7]: Literally "there was no more spirit in her".
[^8]: Or "your affairs". Hebrew *devarim* means both "words" and "matters".
[^9]: 1 Kings 10:9 has "on the throne of Israel".
[^10]: Literally "justice and righteousness".
[^11]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so 120 talents was about 9,000 pounds (4,100 kg).
[^12]: Hiram king of Tyre (2:2). The Hebrew text is written "Hiram" but read "Huram".
[^13]: Hebrew *algummim*, a costly wood that cannot be identified with certainty (2:7). 1 Kings 10:11–12 has *almuggim*. Also in verse 11.
[^14]: Hebrew *mesillot*, "raised paths", perhaps steps or terraces. 1 Kings 10:12 has *mis'ad*, "supports".
[^15]: 1 Kings 10:13 reads "besides what he gave her out of his royal generosity".
[^16]: About 50,000 pounds (22,600 kg).
[^17]: Literally "besides the men of the travelers and the merchants bringing". The meaning of "travelers" is uncertain.
[^18]: Literally "six hundred of hammered gold". The word "shekels" is understood. A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so six hundred shekels was about 15 pounds (6.9 kg).
[^19]: Literally "three hundred of gold", that is, shekels: about 7½ pounds (3.5 kg). 1 Kings 10:17 has "three minas", that is, 150 shekels.
[^20]: Solomon's great hall, probably named for its many cedar pillars (1 Kings 7:2–5). Also in verse 20.
[^21]: Hebrew *kevesh*, a word found only here, probably "footstool". 1 Kings 10:19 mentions instead a rounded top at the back of the throne.
[^22]: Literally "on this side and on that". Also in verse 18.
[^23]: Hebrew *zahav sagur*, perhaps "refined gold" or "solid gold".
[^24]: A distant port in the west, perhaps in Spain. 1 Kings 10:22 has "a fleet of Tarshish ships", that is, large seagoing ships of the kind that sailed to Tarshish.
[^25]: Or "peacocks". Hebrew *tukkiyyim*, of uncertain meaning.
[^26]: Literally "sought the face of Solomon".
[^27]: Hebrew *neshek*, "weapons". Some take it as a word meaning "myrrh".
[^28]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*.
[^29]: 1 Kings 5:6 (English 4:26) has "forty thousand stalls". Verses 25–28 are close to 1:14–17 and 1 Kings 5:1, 6 (English 4:21, 26) and 10:26–29.
[^30]: The Euphrates.
[^31]: The low hills between the hill country of Judah and the coastal plain.
[^32]: Verses 29–31 parallel 1 Kings 11:41–43.
[^33]: Literally "the words of Nathan the prophet". These records have not survived.
[^34]: Hebrew *Ye'do*, probably another form of the name Iddo (12:15; 13:22).
[^35]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
