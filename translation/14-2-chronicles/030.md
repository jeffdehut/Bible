**1** Hezekiah sent word to all Israel and Judah, and he also wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh, for them to come to the house of the LORD[^1] in Jerusalem to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**2** The king and his officials and the whole assembly in Jerusalem had decided to keep the Passover in the second month,[^2]

**3** for they had not been able to keep it at the proper time,[^3] because not enough priests had consecrated themselves, and the people had not gathered in Jerusalem.

**4** The plan seemed right to the king and to the whole assembly.

**5** So they decided to send a proclamation throughout all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, for the people to come to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel, in Jerusalem, for they had not kept it in great numbers,[^4] as it is written.

**6** The couriers[^5] went throughout all Israel and Judah with letters from the king and his officials, as the king had commanded, saying, "Israelites, return to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel, so that he may return to those of you who are left, who have escaped from the hand of the kings of Assyria.

**7** Do not be like your ancestors and your brothers, who were unfaithful[^6] to the LORD, the God of their ancestors, so that he made them an object of horror, as you see.

**8** Now do not be stiff-necked, as your ancestors were. Submit to the LORD[^7] and come to his sanctuary, which he has consecrated forever, and serve the LORD your God, so that his burning anger may turn away from you.

**9** For if you return to the LORD, your brothers and your children will find compassion with their captors and will return to this land. For the LORD your God is gracious and compassionate, and he will not turn his face away from you if you return to him."

**10** So the couriers went from city to city through the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, as far as Zebulun, but people laughed at them and mocked them.

**11** Yet some men from Asher, Manasseh and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem.

**12** In Judah also the hand of God was at work, giving them one heart to carry out the command of the king and the officials, by the word of the LORD.

**13** A large crowd gathered in Jerusalem to keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread in the second month, a very great assembly.

**14** They set to work and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem. They also removed all the incense altars and threw them into the Kidron Valley.

**15** They slaughtered the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the second month. The priests and the Levites were ashamed, and they consecrated themselves and brought burnt offerings to the house of the LORD.

**16** They took their places according to their regulations, following the Law of Moses the man of God. The priests splashed the blood that they received from the Levites.

**17** For many in the assembly had not consecrated themselves, so the Levites were in charge of slaughtering the Passover lambs for everyone who was not clean, to consecrate them to the LORD.

**18** For most of the people, many from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar and Zebulun, had not purified themselves, yet they ate the Passover, contrary to what is written. But Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, "May the LORD, who is good, provide atonement[^8] for

**19** everyone who has set his heart on seeking God, the LORD, the God of his ancestors, even if he is not clean by the rules of purity for the holy place."

**20** The LORD listened to Hezekiah and healed the people.

**21** The Israelites who were present in Jerusalem kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread for seven days with great joy. The Levites and the priests praised the LORD day after day with loud instruments for the LORD.[^9]

**22** Hezekiah spoke encouragingly[^10] to all the Levites who showed good understanding in the service of the LORD. They ate the festival meals for the seven days, offering fellowship offerings[^11] and giving thanks[^12] to the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**23** Then the whole assembly decided to keep the festival for another seven days, and they kept another seven days with joy.

**24** For Hezekiah king of Judah gave the assembly a thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep and goats, and the officials gave the assembly a thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep and goats. And a great number of priests consecrated themselves.

**25** The whole assembly of Judah rejoiced, together with the priests and the Levites, and the whole assembly that came from Israel, and the foreigners[^13] who came from the land of Israel and those who lived in Judah.

**26** There was great joy in Jerusalem, for since the days of Solomon son of David king of Israel there had been nothing like this in Jerusalem.

**27** Then the Levitical priests[^14] stood up and blessed the people. Their voice was heard, and their prayer reached his holy dwelling place in heaven.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Numbers 9:9–11 allows those who are unclean or away on a journey to keep the Passover in the second month.
[^3]: Literally "at that time", that is, in the first month (Exodus 12:2–6).
[^4]: Or "for a long time".
[^5]: Literally "the runners". Also in verse 10.
[^6]: Hebrew *ma'al*: to break faith, to betray a trust (12:2).
[^7]: Literally "Give a hand to the LORD."
[^8]: Hebrew *kipper*, "make atonement".
[^9]: Literally "with instruments of strength for the LORD".
[^10]: Literally "spoke to the heart of".
[^11]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings": sacrifices shared in a meal by the worshipers.
[^12]: Or "making confession".
[^13]: Hebrew *gerim*: foreigners living among the Israelites.
[^14]: Literally "the priests, the Levites".
