**1** Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem.[^1]

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh,[^2] following the detestable practices of the nations whom Yahweh had driven out before the Israelites.

**3** He rebuilt the high places[^3] that his father Hezekiah had torn down. He set up altars for the Baals[^4] and made Asherah poles.[^5] He bowed down to all the host of heaven[^6] and served them.

**4** He built altars in the house of Yahweh, of which Yahweh had said, "My name will be in Jerusalem forever."

**5** He built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courtyards of the house of Yahweh.

**6** He made his sons pass through the fire[^7] in the Valley of Ben-hinnom.[^8] He told fortunes, interpreted omens and practiced sorcery, and he appointed mediums and spiritists.[^9] He did much evil in the eyes of Yahweh, provoking him to anger.

**7** He put the carved image of the idol[^10] that he had made in the house of God, of which God had said to David and to his son Solomon, "In this house, and in Jerusalem, which I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel, I will put my name forever.

**8** I will not again remove the feet of Israel from the land that I appointed for your ancestors, if only they will be careful to do all that I have commanded them, all the law, the decrees and the rulings given through Moses."

**9** But Manasseh led Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem astray, so that they did more evil than the nations whom Yahweh had destroyed before the Israelites.

**10** Yahweh spoke to Manasseh and to his people, but they paid no attention.

**11** So Yahweh brought against them the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria. They captured Manasseh with hooks,[^11] bound him with bronze chains and took him to Babylon.[^12]

**12** In his distress he sought the favor of Yahweh his God,[^13] and he humbled himself greatly before the God of his ancestors.

**13** He prayed to him, and God was moved by his entreaty and heard his plea, and he brought him back to Jerusalem, to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that Yahweh is God.[^14]

**14** After this he built an outer wall for the City of David, west of the Gihon in the valley, as far as the entrance at the Fish Gate. He encircled the Ophel[^15] with it and made it very high. He put army commanders in all the fortified cities of Judah.

**15** He removed the foreign gods and the idol from the house of Yahweh, and all the altars he had built on the mountain of the house of Yahweh and in Jerusalem, and he threw them out of the city.

**16** He rebuilt[^16] the altar of Yahweh and sacrificed on it fellowship offerings[^17] and thank offerings,[^18] and he told Judah to serve Yahweh, the God of Israel.

**17** But the people still sacrificed at the high places, though only to Yahweh their God.

**18** The rest of the acts of Manasseh, his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of Yahweh, the God of Israel, are in the records[^19] of the kings of Israel.

**19** His prayer, and how God was moved by his entreaty, all his sin and his unfaithfulness,[^20] and the sites where he built high places and set up the Asherah poles and the carved images before he humbled himself, are written in the records of Hozai.[^21]

**20** Manasseh slept with his ancestors,[^22] and they buried him in his house.[^23] And his son Amon became king in his place.

**21** Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem.

**22** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, as his father Manasseh had done. Amon sacrificed to all the carved images that his father Manasseh had made, and he served them.

**23** He did not humble himself before Yahweh as his father Manasseh had humbled himself, but this same Amon increased his guilt.

**24** His officials conspired against him and killed him in his palace.

**25** But the people of the land[^24] struck down all who had conspired against King Amon, and the people of the land made his son Josiah king in his place.

[^1]: Verses 1–9 and 18–25 parallel 2 Kings 21:1–9 and 17–24.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops. Also in verses 17 and 19.
[^4]: Plural of *Baal*, "lord, master": the Canaanite god Baal, worshiped in many local forms.
[^5]: Hebrew *asherot*, plural of *Asherah*: wooden poles or trees that were symbols of the goddess Asherah.
[^6]: The sun, moon and stars, worshiped as gods. Also in verse 5.
[^7]: That is, he burned them as sacrifices (28:3; Deuteronomy 18:10).
[^8]: Literally "the valley of the son of Hinnom", south and west of Jerusalem (28:3).
[^9]: Hebrew *ov* and *yidde'oni*: people who claimed to call up the spirits of the dead, or the spirits themselves.
[^10]: Hebrew *semel*, "image, statue". Also in verse 15. 2 Kings 21:7 has "the carved image of Asherah".
[^11]: Or "in the thickets". Hebrew *choachim*.
[^12]: Babylon was then under Assyrian rule.
[^13]: Literally "he soothed the face of Yahweh his God".
[^14]: The Prayer of Manasseh, found among the books of the Apocrypha, was later composed as the prayer mentioned in verses 12–13 and 18–19.
[^15]: The hill on which the City of David stood, south of the temple (27:3).
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "he prepared" but read "he built".
[^17]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings": sacrifices shared in a meal by the worshipers.
[^18]: Hebrew *todah*: a sacrifice given in thanksgiving (Leviticus 7:12).
[^19]: Literally "the words". Also in verse 19.
[^20]: Hebrew *ma'al*: breaking faith, betraying a trust (12:2).
[^21]: Or "the seers". The Septuagint reads "the seers".
[^22]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^23]: 2 Kings 21:18 has "in the garden of his palace, the garden of Uzza".
[^24]: Hebrew *am ha-arets*, the landowning citizens of Judah, who often took part in choosing the king (23:13).
