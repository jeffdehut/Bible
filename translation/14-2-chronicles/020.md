**1** After this the Moabites and the Ammonites, and with them some of the Ammonites,[^1] came to make war against Jehoshaphat.

**2** Messengers came and told Jehoshaphat, "A great multitude is coming against you from beyond the sea,[^2] from Aram.[^3] They are already in Hazazon-tamar," that is, En-gedi.

**3** Jehoshaphat was afraid, and he set himself to seek Yahweh[^4] and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah.

**4** Judah gathered to seek help from Yahweh. From all the cities of Judah they came to seek Yahweh.

**5** Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of Yahweh, in front of the new courtyard,

**6** and said, "Yahweh, God of our ancestors, are you not God in heaven? You rule over all the kingdoms of the nations. Power and might are in your hand, and no one can stand against you.

**7** Did you not, our God, drive out the inhabitants of this land before your people Israel, and give it forever to the descendants of Abraham your friend?[^5]

**8** They have lived in it and have built you a sanctuary in it for your name, saying,

**9** 'If disaster comes on us, sword, judgment,[^6] plague or famine, we will stand before this house and before you, for your name is in this house, and we will cry out to you in our distress, and you will hear and save us.'

**10** Now here are the Ammonites, Moab and Mount Seir, whom you did not let Israel invade when they came from the land of Egypt. They turned away from them and did not destroy them.[^7]

**11** Look how they are repaying us by coming to drive us out of your possession, which you gave us to inherit.

**12** Our God, will you not judge them? For we have no strength to face this great multitude that is coming against us. We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on you."

**13** All Judah was standing before Yahweh, with their little ones, their wives and their children.

**14** Then the Spirit of Yahweh came on Jahaziel son of Zechariah, son of Benaiah, son of Jeiel, son of Mattaniah, a Levite of the descendants of Asaph, in the middle of the assembly.

**15** He said, "Listen, all Judah and you inhabitants of Jerusalem, and King Jehoshaphat. This is what Yahweh says to you: Do not be afraid or dismayed because of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours but God's.

**16** Tomorrow go down against them. They will be coming up by the Ascent of Ziz, and you will find them at the end of the valley,[^8] facing the wilderness of Jeruel.

**17** You will not need to fight in this battle. Take your positions, stand still and see the deliverance Yahweh will give you, Judah and Jerusalem. Do not be afraid or dismayed. Tomorrow go out to face them, and Yahweh will be with you."

**18** Jehoshaphat bowed down with his face to the ground, and all Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before Yahweh to worship Yahweh.

**19** Then the Levites, from the Kohathites and the Korahites, stood up to praise Yahweh, the God of Israel, with a very loud voice.

**20** Early in the morning they went out to the wilderness of Tekoa. As they went out, Jehoshaphat stood and said, "Listen to me, Judah and you inhabitants of Jerusalem. Believe in Yahweh your God, and you will be established.[^9] Believe his prophets, and you will succeed."

**21** After consulting with the people, he appointed singers to Yahweh, and those who praised him in holy splendor,[^10] to go out ahead of the army, saying, "Give thanks to Yahweh, for his faithful love[^11] endures forever."

**22** As soon as they began to sing and praise, Yahweh set ambushes against the Ammonites, Moab and Mount Seir, who had come against Judah, and they were defeated.

**23** The Ammonites and Moab rose up against the inhabitants of Mount Seir to destroy them completely[^12] and wipe them out. When they had finished off the inhabitants of Seir, they turned on one another and destroyed each other.[^13]

**24** When Judah came to the watchtower overlooking the wilderness and looked toward the multitude, there were dead bodies lying on the ground. No one had escaped.

**25** When Jehoshaphat and his people came to take their plunder, they found among them a great amount of goods, corpses[^14] and precious things. They stripped them for themselves until they could carry no more. They spent three days taking the plunder, because there was so much.

**26** On the fourth day they assembled in the Valley of Beracah,[^15] for there they blessed Yahweh. That is why that place is called the Valley of Beracah to this day.

**27** Then all the men of Judah and Jerusalem, with Jehoshaphat at their head, returned to Jerusalem with joy, for Yahweh had given them joy over their enemies.

**28** They came to Jerusalem with harps, lyres and trumpets, to the house of Yahweh.

**29** The fear of God fell on all the kingdoms of the lands when they heard that Yahweh had fought against the enemies of Israel.

**30** So the kingdom of Jehoshaphat was at peace, for his God gave him rest on every side.

**31** Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah.[^16] He was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.

**32** He walked in the way of his father Asa and did not turn aside from it, doing what was right in the eyes of Yahweh.

**33** But the high places[^17] were not removed, and the people had not yet set their hearts on the God of their ancestors.

**34** The rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, from first to last, are written in the records[^18] of Jehu son of Hanani, which are included in the book of the kings of Israel.

**35** After this Jehoshaphat king of Judah allied himself with Ahaziah king of Israel, who acted wickedly.[^19]

**36** He joined with him in building ships to go to Tarshish, and they built the ships at Ezion-geber.

**37** Then Eliezer son of Dodavahu of Mareshah prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying, "Because you have allied yourself with Ahaziah, Yahweh has broken down what you have made." The ships were wrecked and were not able to go to Tarshish.[^20]

[^1]: The Septuagint reads "some of the Meunites", a people from the region of Edom (26:7).
[^2]: That is, the Dead Sea.
[^3]: Many interpreters read *Edom*, which differs from *Aram* by one letter in Hebrew.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "who loved you". Compare Isaiah 41:8.
[^6]: Or "the sword of judgment".
[^7]: See Deuteronomy 2:4–19.
[^8]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows only in the rainy season.
[^9]: In Hebrew, "believe" (*ha'aminu*) and "you will be established" (*te'amenu*) come from the same root. Compare Isaiah 7:9.
[^10]: Or "in holy attire". Literally "for the splendor of holiness".
[^11]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^12]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^13]: Literally "each man helped against his neighbor, to destroy".
[^14]: Hebrew *pegarim*. The Vulgate reads "garments" (Hebrew *begadim*).
[^15]: *Beracah* means "blessing". Also later in the verse.
[^16]: Verses 31–37 are close to 1 Kings 22:41–51 (English 22:41–50).
[^17]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops. Compare 17:6.
[^18]: Literally "the words".
[^19]: Or "and in doing so he acted wickedly", referring to Jehoshaphat.
[^20]: Compare 1 Kings 22:49–50 (English 22:48–49).
