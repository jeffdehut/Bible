**1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah became king over Judah.[^1]

**2** He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah.[^2] There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**3** Abijah went into battle with an army of valiant warriors, four hundred thousand chosen men, and Jeroboam drew up his battle line against him with eight hundred thousand chosen men, mighty warriors.[^3]

**4** Abijah stood up on Mount Zemaraim, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, and said, "Listen to me, Jeroboam and all Israel!

**5** Don't you know that Yahweh,[^4] the God of Israel, gave the kingship over Israel to David forever, to him and his sons, by a covenant of salt?[^5]

**6** Yet Jeroboam son of Nebat, a servant of Solomon son of David, rose up and rebelled against his master.

**7** Worthless men, scoundrels,[^6] gathered around him and prevailed against Rehoboam son of Solomon, when Rehoboam was young and inexperienced[^7] and could not stand up to them.

**8** And now you think you can stand up to the kingdom of Yahweh, which is in the hands of the sons of David. You are a great multitude, and you have with you the gold calves that Jeroboam made to be your gods.

**9** Haven't you driven out the priests of Yahweh, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and made priests for yourselves like the peoples of other lands? Whoever comes to be ordained[^8] with a young bull and seven rams becomes a priest of what are not gods.

**10** But as for us, Yahweh is our God, and we have not abandoned him. Priests who are sons of Aaron minister to Yahweh, and the Levites are at their work.

**11** Every morning and every evening they burn burnt offerings and fragrant incense to Yahweh. They set out the rows of bread on the pure table,[^9] and they light the gold lampstand with its lamps every evening. For we keep the charge of Yahweh our God,[^10] but you have abandoned him.

**12** Look, God is with us at our head, and his priests with their battle trumpets to sound the alarm against you. Israelites, do not fight against Yahweh, the God of your ancestors, for you will not succeed."

**13** But Jeroboam had sent an ambush around to come up behind them, so that his troops were in front of Judah and the ambush was behind them.

**14** When Judah turned, there was the battle, in front of them and behind them. They cried out to Yahweh, and the priests blew the trumpets.

**15** Then the men of Judah raised the battle cry. And when the men of Judah raised the battle cry, God struck down Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah.

**16** The Israelites fled before Judah, and God handed them over to them.

**17** Abijah and his people struck them with a great slaughter, and five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell slain.

**18** So the Israelites were subdued at that time, and the people of Judah prevailed, because they relied on Yahweh, the God of their ancestors.

**19** Abijah pursued Jeroboam and took cities from him: Bethel and its villages,[^11] Jeshanah and its villages, and Ephrain[^12] and its villages.

**20** Jeroboam did not regain his strength in the days of Abijah. Yahweh struck him down, and he died.

**21** But Abijah grew strong. He married fourteen wives, and he fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

**22** The rest of the acts of Abijah, his ways and his sayings, are written in the commentary[^13] of the prophet Iddo.

**23** Abijah slept with his ancestors,[^14] and they buried him in the City of David. And his son Asa became king in his place. In his days the land had peace for ten years.[^15]

[^1]: Verses 1–2 and 22–23 parallel 1 Kings 15:1–2 and 7–8, where he is called Abijam.
[^2]: 11:20 and 1 Kings 15:2 call his mother Maacah daughter of Absalom (Abishalom). The Septuagint here reads Maacah.
[^3]: Hebrew *eleph*, "thousand", may also mean a military unit or a clan (Numbers 1:16), in which case the armies here and in verse 17 were much smaller.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: That is, a lasting covenant that cannot be broken (Leviticus 2:13; Numbers 18:19).
[^6]: Literally "empty men, sons of worthlessness (*beliyya'al*)".
[^7]: Literally "a youth and soft of heart". Rehoboam was forty-one when he became king (12:13).
[^8]: Literally "to fill his hand", the idiom for ordaining a priest (Exodus 28:41).
[^9]: The bread of the Presence, set out in rows on the gold table (Leviticus 24:5–8).
[^10]: That is, we carry out the duties he has given us.
[^11]: Literally "its daughters". Also later in the verse.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "Ephron" but read "Ephrain".
[^13]: Hebrew *midrash*, "study" or "account". In the Bible the word appears only here and in 24:27.
[^14]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^15]: Verse 23 is 14:1 in most English Bibles.
