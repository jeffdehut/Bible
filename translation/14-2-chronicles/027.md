**1** Jotham was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jerushah daughter of Zadok.[^1]

**2** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD,[^2] just as his father Uzziah had done, except that he did not enter the temple of the LORD.[^3] But the people still acted corruptly.

**3** He built the Upper Gate of the house of the LORD, and he did much building on the wall of Ophel.[^4]

**4** He built cities in the hill country of Judah, and in the wooded areas he built fortresses and towers.

**5** He fought with the king of the Ammonites and prevailed over them. That year the Ammonites gave him a hundred talents[^5] of silver, ten thousand kors[^6] of wheat and ten thousand of barley. The Ammonites paid him the same in the second and the third years.

**6** Jotham became strong, because he ordered his ways before the LORD his God.

**7** The rest of the acts of Jotham, all his wars and his ways, are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

**8** He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem.

**9** Jotham slept with his ancestors,[^7] and they buried him in the City of David. And his son Ahaz became king in his place.

[^1]: Verses 1–3 and 7–9 parallel 2 Kings 15:33–38.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: As his father had done (26:16).
[^4]: The hill on which the City of David stood, south of the temple.
[^5]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so a hundred talents was about 7,500 pounds (3,400 kg).
[^6]: A kor was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters).
[^7]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
