**1** So I, the prisoner in the Lord, urge you to live[^1] in a way worthy of the calling to which you have been called,

**2** with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love,

**3** making every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

**4** There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were also called to one hope when you were called;

**5** one Lord, one faith, one baptism;

**6** one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all.[^2]

**7** But to each one of us grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift.

**8** Therefore it says,[^3] "When he went up on high, he led captives away;[^4] he gave gifts to people."[^5]

**9** Now what does "he went up" mean, except that he also went down[^6] into the lower parts of the earth?[^7]

**10** The one who went down is the same one who also went up far above all the heavens, so that he might fill all things.

**11** And he gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists,[^8] some shepherds[^9] and teachers,

**12** to equip God's holy people[^10] for the work of service, for building up the body of Christ,[^11]

**13** until we all reach the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, mature adulthood,[^12] the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ,

**14** so that we may no longer be infants, tossed by the waves and carried around by every wind of teaching, by human trickery,[^13] by craftiness in the service of deceitful scheming.

**15** Instead, speaking the truth[^14] in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, Christ.

**16** From him the whole body, joined and held together by every ligament that supplies it,[^15] according to the working of each part in its measure, makes the body grow, building itself up in love.

**17** So I say this and insist on it in the Lord: you must no longer live as the Gentiles[^16] live, in the futility of their minds.

**18** They are darkened in their understanding and alienated from the life of God, because of the ignorance that is in them, because of the hardness of their hearts.

**19** Having lost all feeling, they have given themselves over to sensuality, to practice every kind of impurity with greed.[^17]

**20** But that is not how you learned Christ,

**21** assuming that you have heard him[^18] and were taught in him, as the truth is in Jesus:

**22** that you were to put off your old self,[^19] which belonged to your former way of life and is being ruined by its deceitful desires,[^20]

**23** and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds,

**24** and to put on the new self, created to be like God[^21] in the righteousness and holiness that come from the truth.

**25** So, putting away falsehood, speak the truth, each of you, with your neighbor,[^22] because we are members of one another.

**26** Be angry, and do not sin.[^23] Do not let the sun go down on your anger,

**27** and do not give the devil a foothold.[^24]

**28** Whoever steals must not steal any longer. Instead, let them work hard, doing what is good with their own hands, so that they will have something to share with anyone in need.

**29** Let no rotten[^25] word come out of your mouths, but only what is good for building up, as the need requires, so that it may give grace to those who hear.

**30** And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God,[^26] with whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.

**31** Let all bitterness and rage and anger and shouting and slander be put away from you, along with all malice.

**32** Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, just as God in Christ forgave you.[^27]

[^1]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life; also in verse 17.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "in us all".
[^3]: Or "he says", that is, God in scripture.
[^4]: Literally "he took captivity captive".
[^5]: Psalm 68:19 (68:18 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew and the Septuagint read "You went up … you received gifts from people"; here the verb is "he gave". The Aramaic Targum of the Psalms also reads "gave".
[^6]: Many manuscripts add "first".
[^7]: Or "into the lower regions, that is, the earth".
[^8]: Greek *euangelistai*, "people who proclaim the good news".
[^9]: Or "pastors". Greek *poimenes*, "shepherds".
[^10]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^11]: Or "to equip God's holy people, for the work of service, for building up …": the three phrases may all describe the purpose of the gifts in verse 11, rather than the work of service being done by God's holy people.
[^12]: Literally "to a complete man".
[^13]: Literally "by the dice-playing of people".
[^14]: Or "holding to the truth". Greek *aletheuo*, "to speak or live the truth".
[^15]: Literally "through every joint of the supply".
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "the rest of the Gentiles".
[^17]: Greek *pleonexia*, the desire to have more and more.
[^18]: Or "heard about him".
[^19]: Literally "the old man"; also "the new man" in verse 24. The Greek can also be read as a statement: "you were taught … that you have put off your old self".
[^20]: Literally "the desires of deceit".
[^21]: Literally "according to God".
[^22]: Zechariah 8:16.
[^23]: Psalm 4:5 (4:4 in most English Bibles), as worded in the Septuagint. The Hebrew can be read "Tremble, and do not sin".
[^24]: Literally "a place".
[^25]: Or "harmful".
[^26]: Compare Isaiah 63:10.
[^27]: Many manuscripts read "us".
