**1** Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness

**2** for forty days, being tested[^1] by the devil. He ate nothing during those days, and when they were over, he was hungry.

**3** The devil said to him, "If you are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread."

**4** Jesus answered him, "It is written: 'People do not live on bread alone.'"[^2]

**5** Then he led him up and in an instant showed him all the kingdoms of the world.[^3]

**6** The devil said to him, "I will give you all this authority and their glory, for it has been handed over to me, and I give it to anyone I wish.

**7** So if you worship me,[^4] it will all be yours."

**8** Jesus answered him,[^5] "It is written: 'Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.'"[^6]

**9** Then he brought him to Jerusalem, set him on the highest point[^7] of the temple, and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down from here.

**10** For it is written: 'He will command his angels concerning you, to guard you,'

**11** and, 'They will lift you up in their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.'"[^8]

**12** Jesus answered him, "It is said: 'Do not put the Lord your God to the test.'"[^9]

**13** When the devil had finished every test, he left him until an opportune time.

**14** Jesus returned to Galilee in the power of the Spirit, and news about him spread through the whole surrounding region.

**15** He taught in their synagogues, and everyone praised him.

**16** He came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up, and on the Sabbath day he went into the synagogue, as was his custom. He stood up to read,

**17** and the scroll of the prophet Isaiah was handed to him. He unrolled the scroll and found the place where it was written:

**18** "The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me[^10] to proclaim freedom for the captives and recovery of sight for the blind, to set the oppressed free,

**19** to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor."[^11]

**20** Then he rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. The eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed on him.

**21** He began by saying to them, "Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing."

**22** Everyone spoke well of him[^12] and was amazed at the gracious words that came from his mouth. They said, "Isn't this Joseph's son?"

**23** He said to them, "No doubt you will quote this proverb to me: 'Doctor, heal yourself! Do here in your hometown the things we have heard you did in Capernaum.'"

**24** He went on, "Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted[^13] in his hometown.

**25** But I tell you the truth: there were many widows in Israel in the days of Elijah, when the sky was shut for three years and six months and a great famine came over the whole land.[^14]

**26** Yet Elijah was not sent to any of them, but only to a widow at Zarephath in the region of Sidon.

**27** And there were many in Israel with skin diseases[^15] in the time of the prophet Elisha, yet none of them was made clean except Naaman the Syrian."[^16]

**28** When they heard this, everyone in the synagogue was filled with rage.

**29** They got up, drove him out of the town, and led him to the brow of the hill on which their town was built, in order to throw him down the cliff.

**30** But he passed through the middle of them and went on his way.

**31** He went down to Capernaum, a town in Galilee, and on the Sabbath he was teaching them.

**32** They were amazed at his teaching, because his word had authority.

**33** In the synagogue there was a man who had the spirit of an unclean demon, and he cried out with a loud voice,

**34** "Ha![^17] What do you want with us,[^18] Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God!"

**35** Jesus rebuked him: "Be quiet, and come out of him!" The demon threw the man down in front of them all and came out of him without doing him any harm.

**36** Everyone was amazed, and they said to one another, "What is this word?[^19] With authority and power he gives orders to the unclean spirits, and they come out!"

**37** And news about him spread to every place in the surrounding region.

**38** Jesus left the synagogue and went into Simon's house. Simon's mother-in-law was suffering from a high fever, and they asked him to help her.[^20]

**39** He stood over her and rebuked the fever, and it left her. At once she got up and began to serve them.

**40** As the sun was setting, all who had people sick with various diseases brought them to him. He laid his hands on each one of them and healed them.

**41** Demons also came out of many people, shouting, "You are the Son of God!"[^21] But he rebuked them and would not let them speak, because they knew he was the Messiah.

**42** At daybreak Jesus left and went to a deserted place. The crowds were looking for him, and when they came to him, they tried to keep him from leaving them.

**43** But he said to them, "I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other towns too, because that is why I was sent."

**44** So he kept proclaiming the message in the synagogues of Judea.[^22]

[^1]: Or "tempted". The Greek word means both; also in verse 13.
[^2]: Deuteronomy 8:3. Many manuscripts add "but on every word of God".
[^3]: Many manuscripts read "Then the devil led him up on a high mountain and in an instant showed him all the kingdoms of the world."
[^4]: Literally "worship before me".
[^5]: Many manuscripts add "Get behind me, Satan!"
[^6]: Deuteronomy 6:13. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). The Hebrew reads "Fear" where Luke has "Worship", and does not have "only".
[^7]: Literally "the little wing", perhaps a corner of the temple walls high above the valley.
[^8]: Psalm 91:11–12, leaving out "in all your ways".
[^9]: Deuteronomy 6:16. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^10]: Many manuscripts add "to heal the brokenhearted".
[^11]: Isaiah 61:1–2, mostly following the Greek translation, with "to set the oppressed free" from 58:6. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). The Hebrew also has "to bind up the brokenhearted" after "He has sent me", and where Luke has "recovery of sight for the blind", it has "release for the prisoners". The reading stops before Isaiah's next words, "and the day of vengeance of our God".
[^12]: Or "testified about him".
[^13]: Greek *dektos*, the word translated "favor" in verse 19.
[^14]: 1 Kings 17:1–16; 18:1.
[^15]: Greek *lepros*, traditionally "leper". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy.
[^16]: 2 Kings 5:1–14.
[^17]: Greek *ea*, a cry of surprise or displeasure. Or "Leave us alone!"
[^18]: Literally "What to us and to you?", an idiom: "What do we have to do with you?"
[^19]: Or "What kind of message is this?"
[^20]: Literally "they asked him about her".
[^21]: Many manuscripts read "You are the Messiah, the Son of God!"
[^22]: Luke sometimes uses "Judea" for the whole land of the Jews (1:5; 7:17; 23:5). Many manuscripts read "Galilee".
