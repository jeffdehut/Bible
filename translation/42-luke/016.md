**1** Jesus also said to the disciples, "There was a rich man who had a manager,[^1] and this manager was accused to him of squandering his possessions.

**2** So he called him in and said to him, 'What is this I hear about you? Give an account of your management, because you can no longer serve as manager.'

**3** The manager said to himself, 'What shall I do, now that my master is taking the management away from me? I am not strong enough to dig, and I am ashamed to beg.

**4** I know what I will do, so that when I am removed from the management, people will welcome me into their homes.'

**5** So he summoned each one of his master's debtors. He said to the first, 'How much do you owe my master?'

**6** He said, 'A hundred baths[^2] of olive oil.' The manager said to him, 'Take your bill, sit down quickly, and write fifty.'

**7** Then he said to another, 'And how much do you owe?' He said, 'A hundred kors[^3] of wheat.' The manager said to him, 'Take your bill and write eighty.'

**8** And the master[^4] praised the dishonest manager because he had acted shrewdly. For the people of this age are shrewder in dealing with their own kind than the people of the light.[^5]

**9** And I tell you, make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest money,[^6] so that when it is gone,[^7] they will welcome you into the eternal dwellings.[^8]

**10** Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much, and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.

**11** So if you have not been faithful with dishonest money, who will trust you with the true riches?

**12** And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to someone else, who will give you what is your own?[^9]

**13** No household servant can serve[^10] two masters. Either they will hate the one and love the other, or they will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and money."

**14** The Pharisees, who loved money, heard all this, and they sneered at Jesus.

**15** He said to them, "You are the ones who declare yourselves righteous[^11] in the sight of others, but God knows your hearts. For what is highly valued among people is detestable in the sight of God.

**16** The Law and the Prophets lasted until John.[^12] From then on the good news of the kingdom of God is being proclaimed, and everyone is forcing their way into it.[^13]

**17** But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one stroke of a letter[^14] of the Law to fall away.

**18** "Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery, and whoever marries a woman divorced from her husband commits adultery.

**19** "There was a rich man who dressed in purple and fine linen and lived in luxury every day.

**20** At his gate lay a poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores,

**21** who longed to eat his fill of what fell[^15] from the rich man's table. Even the dogs came and licked his sores.

**22** The poor man died and was carried away by the angels to Abraham's side.[^16] The rich man also died and was buried.

**23** In Hades,[^17] where he was in torment, he looked up and saw Abraham far away, with Lazarus at his side.

**24** He called out, 'Father Abraham, have mercy on me! Send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am in agony in this flame.'

**25** But Abraham said, 'My child, remember that during your lifetime you received your good things, while Lazarus in the same way received bad things. But now he is comforted here, and you are in agony.

**26** Besides all this, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that those who want to cross over from here to you cannot, and no one can cross from there to us.'

**27** He said, 'Then I beg you, father, send him to my father's house,

**28** for I have five brothers. Let him warn them, so that they will not also come to this place of torment.'

**29** But Abraham said, 'They have Moses and the Prophets. Let them listen to them.'

**30** He said, 'No, father Abraham, but if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent.'

**31** Abraham said to him, 'If they do not listen to Moses and the Prophets, they will not be convinced even if someone rises from the dead.'"

[^1]: Greek *oikonomos*, the manager of a household, often a trusted slave; also in verses 3 and 8.
[^2]: Greek *batos*, the Hebrew *bath*. A bath was probably about 22 liters (6 gallons).
[^3]: Greek *koros*, the Hebrew *kor*. A kor was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters).
[^4]: Or "the Lord", that is, Jesus. Greek *kyrios*.
[^5]: Literally "the sons of this age are shrewder toward their own generation than the sons of the light".
[^6]: Literally "the mammon of unrighteousness". Greek *mamonas*, from the Aramaic *mamon*, "wealth, property"; also in verses 11 and 13.
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "when you fail", that is, when you die.
[^8]: Literally "tents".
[^9]: Some manuscripts read "what is ours".
[^10]: Or "be a slave to"; also later in the verse.
[^11]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^12]: The Greek has no verb: "The Law and the Prophets until John".
[^13]: Or "everyone is urged to enter it". The meaning of the Greek verb is debated.
[^14]: Greek *keraia*, "horn", a tiny stroke that tells one letter from another.
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "the crumbs that fell".
[^16]: Literally "to the bosom of Abraham", a picture of closeness and honor, like a guest reclining next to the host at a banquet; also in verse 23.
[^17]: Greek *Hades*, the realm of the dead, like the Hebrew Sheol.
