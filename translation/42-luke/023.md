**1** Then the whole assembly got up and brought Jesus to Pilate.

**2** They began to accuse him, saying, "We found this man misleading our nation, forbidding us to pay tribute to Caesar, and saying that he himself is the Messiah, a king."[^1]

**3** So Pilate asked him, "Are you the king of the Jews?" He answered him, "You say so."[^2]

**4** Then Pilate said to the chief priests and the crowds, "I find no basis for a charge against this man."

**5** But they insisted, "He stirs up the people, teaching throughout all Judea,[^3] beginning from Galilee and coming all the way here."

**6** When Pilate heard this, he asked whether the man was a Galilean.

**7** When he learned that Jesus was under Herod's jurisdiction, he sent him to Herod, who was also in Jerusalem at that time.

**8** When Herod saw Jesus, he was very glad, for he had been wanting to see him for a long time, because he had heard about him, and he was hoping to see him perform some sign.[^4]

**9** He questioned him at some length, but Jesus gave him no answer.

**10** The chief priests and the scribes stood there, accusing him vehemently.

**11** Herod too,[^5] with his soldiers, treated him with contempt and mocked him. Then he put a splendid robe on him and sent him back to Pilate.

**12** That very day Herod and Pilate became friends with each other; before this they had been enemies.

**13** Pilate called together the chief priests, the leaders, and the people,

**14** and said to them, "You brought this man to me as one who was leading the people astray. I have examined him in your presence and have found no basis in this man for the charges you bring against him.

**15** Neither has Herod, for he sent him back to us.[^6] Look, he has done nothing to deserve death.

**16** So I will have him flogged[^7] and then release him."[^8]

**18** But they all shouted together, "Away with this man! Release Barabbas for us!"

**19** Barabbas had been thrown into prison for an uprising that had taken place in the city, and for murder.

**20** Pilate, wanting to release Jesus, addressed them again.

**21** But they kept shouting, "Crucify him! Crucify him!"

**22** A third time he said to them, "Why? What wrong has this man done? I have found in him no grounds for the death penalty. So I will have him flogged and then release him."

**23** But they kept pressing with loud shouts, demanding that he be crucified, and their shouts prevailed.[^9]

**24** So Pilate decided that their demand should be granted.

**25** He released the man they asked for, who had been thrown into prison for an uprising and murder, and he handed Jesus over to their will.

**26** As they led him away, they seized a man, Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the country, and they put the cross on him to carry behind Jesus.

**27** A large crowd of people followed him, including women who were beating their breasts and wailing for him.

**28** Jesus turned to them and said, "Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for me. Weep instead for yourselves and for your children.

**29** For look, the days are coming when people will say, 'Blessed are the women who are childless, the wombs that never gave birth and the breasts that never nursed!'

**30** Then they will begin to say to the mountains, 'Fall on us!' and to the hills, 'Cover us!'[^10]

**31** For if they do these things when the wood is green, what will happen when it is dry?"[^11]

**32** Two others, who were criminals, were also led away with him to be put to death.

**33** When they came to the place called the Skull,[^12] they crucified him there, along with the criminals, one on his right and one on his left.

**34** And Jesus said, "Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."[^13] They divided his clothes by casting lots.[^14]

**35** The people stood by, watching. The leaders also sneered at him,[^15] saying, "He saved others; let him save himself, if he is the Messiah of God, the Chosen One!"

**36** The soldiers also mocked him. They came up and offered him sour wine[^16]

**37** and said, "If you are the king of the Jews, save yourself!"

**38** There was also an inscription over him:[^17] "This is the King of the Jews."

**39** One of the criminals hanging there insulted him, saying, "Aren't you the Messiah? Save yourself and us!"

**40** But the other rebuked him, saying, "Don't you even fear God, since you are under the same sentence?

**41** We are being punished justly, for we are getting what our deeds deserve. But this man has done nothing wrong."

**42** Then he said, "Jesus, remember me when you come in your kingdom."[^18]

**43** Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise."[^19]

**44** It was now about noon,[^20] and darkness came over the whole land[^21] until three in the afternoon,

**45** because the sun's light failed.[^22] And the veil[^23] of the temple was torn down the middle.

**46** Jesus called out with a loud voice, "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit."[^24] Having said this, he breathed his last.

**47** When the centurion saw what had happened, he glorified God and said, "Certainly this man was righteous."[^25]

**48** When all the crowds who had gathered for this spectacle saw what had happened, they returned home, beating their breasts.

**49** But all who knew him, including the women who had followed him from Galilee, stood at a distance, watching these things.[^26]

**50** Now there was a man named Joseph, a member of the council,[^27] a good and righteous man—

**51** he had not agreed with their plan and their action—who came from Arimathea, a town of the Jews, and was waiting for the kingdom of God.

**52** This man went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus.

**53** Then he took it down, wrapped it in a linen cloth, and laid him in a tomb cut out of the rock, where no one had yet been laid.

**54** It was the day of Preparation, and the Sabbath was about to begin.[^28]

**55** The women who had come with Jesus from Galilee followed, and they saw the tomb and how his body was laid.

**56** Then they went back and prepared spices and perfumes. On the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.[^29]

[^1]: Or "an anointed king". Greek *christos*.
[^2]: Or "You have said it." The reply neither simply affirms nor denies.
[^3]: Here "Judea" probably means the whole land of the Jews; see 4:44.
[^4]: Compare 9:9.
[^5]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28; other editions leave out "too".
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "for I sent you to him".
[^7]: Greek *paideuo*, "discipline", probably a lesser beating than the flogging that came before crucifixion; also in verse 22.
[^8]: Many manuscripts add verse 17: "Now he was obliged to release one man for them at the festival." The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave it out, so this translation has no verse 17.
[^9]: Many manuscripts add "and those of the chief priests".
[^10]: Hosea 10:8, where the two cries are in the opposite order.
[^11]: Literally "if they do these things in the green wood, what will happen in the dry?", a proverb.
[^12]: Greek *Kranion*, "skull"; traditionally "Calvary", from the Latin word for skull.
[^13]: Some manuscripts leave out the first sentence of this verse. The SBLGNT prints it as part of the text, without brackets.
[^14]: See Psalm 22:19 (22:18 in most English Bibles).
[^15]: Compare Psalm 22:8 (22:7 in most English Bibles).
[^16]: Cheap wine vinegar, a common drink of soldiers and laborers. See Psalm 69:22 (69:21 in most English Bibles).
[^17]: Many manuscripts add "written in Greek, Latin, and Hebrew letters".
[^18]: Or "when you come as king". Some manuscripts read "into your kingdom". Many manuscripts read "And he said to Jesus, 'Lord, remember me …'"
[^19]: Greek *paradeisos*, "garden, park", the word used in the Greek Old Testament for the garden of Eden (Genesis 2:8). Or, with different punctuation, "Truly I tell you today, you will be with me in paradise."
[^20]: Literally "the sixth hour"; "three in the afternoon" is literally "the ninth hour".
[^21]: Or "the whole earth".
[^22]: Or "was eclipsed". Many manuscripts read "and the sun was darkened".
[^23]: Greek *katapetasma*, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the veil that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33).
[^24]: Psalm 31:6 (31:5 in most English Bibles).
[^25]: Or "innocent".
[^26]: Compare Psalm 38:12 (38:11 in most English Bibles); 88:9 (88:8 in most English Bibles).
[^27]: Greek *bouleutes*, a member of the Sanhedrin.
[^28]: Literally "was dawning". The Sabbath began at sunset.
[^29]: See Exodus 20:8–10.
