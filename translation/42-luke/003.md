**1** In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar,[^1] when Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea,[^2] Herod was tetrarch[^3] of Galilee, his brother Philip tetrarch of the region of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene,

**2** during the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas,[^4] the word of God came to John son of Zechariah in the wilderness.

**3** He went into all the region around the Jordan, proclaiming a baptism of repentance[^5] for the forgiveness of sins,

**4** as it is written in the book of the words of the prophet Isaiah: "A voice is shouting in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight.

**5** Every valley will be filled, and every mountain and hill made low. The crooked ways will become straight, and the rough places smooth roads,

**6** and all people[^6] will see the salvation of God.'"[^7]

**7** So John said to the crowds who came out to be baptized by him, "You brood of vipers![^8] Who warned you to flee from the coming wrath?

**8** Produce fruit, then, that fits your repentance,[^9] and don't begin to say to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father.' For I tell you, God can raise up children for Abraham from these stones.

**9** Even now the ax is lying at the root of the trees. So every tree that does not produce good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire."

**10** The crowds asked him, "What then should we do?"

**11** He answered them, "Whoever has two tunics should share with the one who has none, and whoever has food should do the same."

**12** Tax collectors also came to be baptized, and they said to him, "Teacher, what should we do?"

**13** He told them, "Collect no more than you have been ordered to."

**14** Soldiers also asked him, "And what should we do?" He told them, "Don't take money from anyone by force or by false accusation, and be content with your wages."

**15** The people were waiting expectantly, and all of them were wondering in their hearts about John, whether he might be the Messiah.

**16** John answered them all, "I baptize you with water, but someone more powerful than I am is coming. I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.

**17** His winnowing fork[^10] is in his hand, to clear his threshing floor and to gather the wheat into his barn, but he will burn up the chaff with fire that never goes out."

**18** So with many other appeals he proclaimed the good news to the people.

**19** But Herod the tetrarch had been rebuked by John because of Herodias, his brother's wife, and because of all the evil things Herod had done.

**20** He added this to them all: he locked John up in prison.

**21** When all the people were being baptized, Jesus also was baptized. And while he was praying, heaven was opened,

**22** and the Holy Spirit came down on him in bodily form like a dove. And a voice came from heaven: "You are my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with you."[^11]

**23** Jesus was about thirty years old when he began his work.[^12] He was the son, as people supposed,[^13] of Joseph, the son of Heli,[^14]

**24** the son of Matthat, the son of Levi, the son of Melchi, the son of Jannai, the son of Joseph,

**25** the son of Mattathias, the son of Amos, the son of Nahum, the son of Esli, the son of Naggai,

**26** the son of Maath, the son of Mattathias, the son of Semein, the son of Josech,[^15] the son of Joda,

**27** the son of Joanan, the son of Rhesa, the son of Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel,[^16] the son of Neri,

**28** the son of Melchi, the son of Addi, the son of Cosam, the son of Elmadam, the son of Er,

**29** the son of Joshua,[^17] the son of Eliezer, the son of Jorim, the son of Matthat, the son of Levi,

**30** the son of Simeon, the son of Judah, the son of Joseph, the son of Jonam, the son of Eliakim,

**31** the son of Melea, the son of Menna, the son of Mattatha, the son of Nathan,[^18] the son of David,

**32** the son of Jesse, the son of Obed,[^19] the son of Boaz, the son of Sala,[^20] the son of Nahshon,

**33** the son of Amminadab, the son of Admin, the son of Arni,[^21] the son of Hezron, the son of Perez, the son of Judah,

**34** the son of Jacob, the son of Isaac, the son of Abraham, the son of Terah, the son of Nahor,

**35** the son of Serug, the son of Reu, the son of Peleg, the son of Eber, the son of Shelah,

**36** the son of Cainan,[^22] the son of Arpachshad, the son of Shem, the son of Noah, the son of Lamech,

**37** the son of Methuselah, the son of Enoch, the son of Jared, the son of Mahalalel, the son of Kenan,[^23]

**38** the son of Enosh, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God.

[^1]: Tiberius became emperor in AD 14, so his fifteenth year was about AD 28.
[^2]: Pilate was the Roman prefect of Judea from AD 26 to 36.
[^3]: Literally "ruler of a fourth", a title for a ruler of part of a kingdom under Roman control. This Herod is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great, who ruled Galilee and Perea from 4 BC to AD 39. Philip was another son of Herod the Great.
[^4]: Literally "in the time of the high priest Annas and Caiaphas". Annas was high priest from AD 6 to 15, and his son-in-law Caiaphas from about AD 18 to 36 (John 18:13). Annas kept great influence and is still called high priest in Acts 4:6.
[^5]: A baptism expressing a change of heart and life. Greek *metanoia*, a turning around of mind and life.
[^6]: Literally "all flesh".
[^7]: Isaiah 40:3–5, following the Greek translation. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). Where verse 6 has "and all people will see the salvation of God", the Hebrew reads "Then the glory of the LORD will be revealed, and all people will see it together."
[^8]: Literally "offspring of vipers".
[^9]: Literally "fruits worthy of repentance".
[^10]: A wooden fork for tossing threshed grain into the air, so that the wind would blow the chaff away.
[^11]: Or "You are my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in you." Compare Psalm 2:7; Isaiah 42:1.
[^12]: Literally "Jesus himself was beginning, about thirty years old".
[^13]: Literally "as was thought".
[^14]: In verses 23–38 the Greek says only "of Heli, of Matthat …"; "the son" is understood. Names found in the Old Testament are spelled here as in this translation's Old Testament, though Luke gives them in Greek forms, for example *Phares* for Perez and *Ragau* for Reu.
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "Joseph".
[^16]: Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel led the return from exile (Ezra 3:2; Haggai 1:1). In 1 Chronicles 3:17–19 Shealtiel is a son of King Jeconiah, as in Matthew 1:12, and Zerubbabel is a son of Shealtiel's brother Pedaiah. Here Shealtiel's father is Neri.
[^17]: Greek *Iesous*, the same name as Jesus; Joshua is its Hebrew form. Many manuscripts read "Jose".
[^18]: Nathan was a son of David born in Jerusalem (2 Samuel 5:14; 1 Chronicles 3:5). From David to Joseph, Luke traces the line through Nathan, while Matthew 1:6–16 traces it through Solomon and the kings of Judah. The two lists meet only at Shealtiel and Zerubbabel, and they name different fathers for Joseph: Heli here, Jacob in Matthew 1:16.
[^19]: The SBLGNT spells the name *Iobel*; in Matthew 1:5 it is *Iobed*.
[^20]: Called Salmah and Salmon in Ruth 4:20–21, and Salma in 1 Chronicles 2:11. Many manuscripts read "Salmon".
[^21]: Ruth 4:19 and 1 Chronicles 2:9–10 have only Ram between Hezron and Amminadab. Many manuscripts read "the son of Amminadab, the son of Aram" (a Greek form of Ram), and some leave out Amminadab: "the son of Admin, the son of Arni".
[^22]: Cainan is not in the Hebrew text of Genesis 11:12 or 1 Chronicles 1:18, 24, where Arpachshad is the father of Shelah. The Septuagint includes him (Genesis 10:24; 11:12–13).
[^23]: Greek *Kainam*, the same form as in verse 36.
