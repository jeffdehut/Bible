**1** Jesus entered Jericho and was passing through it.

**2** There was a man there named Zacchaeus. He was a chief tax collector, and he was rich.

**3** He was trying to see who Jesus was, but he could not because of the crowd, since he was short.

**4** So he ran on ahead and climbed a sycamore-fig tree[^1] to see him, because Jesus was going to pass that way.

**5** When Jesus reached the place, he looked up and said to him, "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house."

**6** So he hurried down and welcomed him joyfully.

**7** All who saw it began to grumble, saying, "He has gone in to be the guest of a sinner."

**8** But Zacchaeus stood there and said to the Lord, "Look, Lord, I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have cheated anyone out of anything, I pay back four times as much."[^2]

**9** Jesus said to him, "Today salvation has come to this house, because he too is a son of Abraham.

**10** For the Son of Man came to seek and to save what was lost."[^3]

**11** As they were listening to this, Jesus went on to tell a parable, because he was near Jerusalem and they thought that the kingdom of God was going to appear immediately.

**12** So he said, "A man of noble birth went to a distant country to receive royal power[^4] for himself and then return.

**13** He called ten of his servants,[^5] gave them ten minas,[^6] and said to them, 'Do business with this until I come back.'

**14** But the people of his country hated him, and they sent a delegation after him to say, 'We do not want this man to be king over us.'

**15** When he returned, having received royal power, he ordered those servants to whom he had given the money to be called to him, so that he could find out what they had gained by doing business.

**16** The first came and said, 'Master, your mina has earned ten more minas.'

**17** He said to him, 'Well done, good servant! Because you have been faithful in a very small matter, you are to have authority over ten cities.'

**18** The second came and said, 'Master, your mina has made five minas.'

**19** He said to this one too, 'And you are to be over five cities.'

**20** Then another came and said, 'Master, here is your mina. I kept it put away in a cloth.

**21** For I was afraid of you, because you are a harsh man. You take out what you did not put in, and reap what you did not sow.'

**22** He said to him, 'I will judge you by your own words, you wicked servant! You knew, did you, that I am a harsh man, taking out what I did not put in and reaping what I did not sow?

**23** Why then didn't you put my money in the bank? Then when I came back, I could have collected it with interest.'

**24** And he said to those standing by, 'Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has ten minas'—

**25** and they said to him, 'Master, he has ten minas!'—

**26** 'I tell you, everyone who has will be given more, but from the one who does not have, even what they have will be taken away.

**27** But as for these enemies of mine who did not want me to be king over them, bring them here and slaughter them in front of me.'"

**28** After he had said this, Jesus went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.

**29** As he approached Bethphage and Bethany, at the hill called the Mount of Olives,[^7] he sent two of the disciples,

**30** telling them, "Go into the village ahead of you. As you enter it, you will find a colt tied there that no one has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it.

**31** If anyone asks you, 'Why are you untying it?' say this: 'The Lord[^8] needs it.'"

**32** Those who were sent went and found it just as he had told them.

**33** As they were untying the colt, its owners said to them, "Why are you untying the colt?"

**34** They said, "The Lord needs it."

**35** They brought the colt to Jesus, threw their cloaks on it, and put Jesus on it.

**36** As he rode along, people were spreading their cloaks on the road.

**37** As he was now approaching the descent from the Mount of Olives, the whole crowd of disciples began to praise God joyfully with a loud voice for all the mighty works they had seen,

**38** saying, "Blessed is the king who comes in the name of the Lord![^9] Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest heaven!"

**39** Some of the Pharisees in the crowd said to him, "Teacher, rebuke your disciples!"

**40** He answered, "I tell you, if these keep silent, the stones will shout out."

**41** As he came near and saw the city, he wept over it,

**42** saying, "If only you had recognized on this day, even you, the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.

**43** For the days will come upon you when your enemies will build a siege wall against you, surround you, and hem you in on every side.

**44** They will dash you to the ground, you and your children within you, and they will not leave one stone on another within you, because you did not recognize the time when God came to you."[^10]

**45** Jesus went into the temple and began to drive out those who were selling,[^11]

**46** saying to them, "It is written: 'My house will be a house of prayer,'[^12] but you have made it a den of robbers."[^13]

**47** Every day he was teaching in the temple. The chief priests, the scribes, and the leaders of the people were trying to kill him,

**48** but they could not find any way to do it, because all the people were hanging on his every word.

[^1]: Greek *sykomorea*, a kind of fig tree common in the region, not the sycamore of Europe or North America.
[^2]: Or "I will give … I will pay back". The Greek present tense can express a resolve.
[^3]: Compare Ezekiel 34:16.
[^4]: Or "a kingdom"; also in verse 15. Greek *basileia*.
[^5]: Or "slaves"; also in verses 15–22. Greek *douloi*.
[^6]: A mina was worth about a hundred days' wages for a laborer.
[^7]: Or "the hill called Olivet". Greek *Elaion*, "olives" or "olive grove".
[^8]: Or "Its master"; also in verse 34. Greek *kyrios* means "lord, master, owner"; the "owners" in verse 33 are its *kyrioi*.
[^9]: Psalm 118:26. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^10]: Literally "the time of your visitation", the time of God's coming to save or to judge.
[^11]: Many manuscripts add "and buying there".
[^12]: Isaiah 56:7.
[^13]: Jeremiah 7:11.
