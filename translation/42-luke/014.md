**1** One Sabbath, when Jesus went to eat a meal[^1] at the house of one of the leaders of the Pharisees, they were watching him closely.

**2** There in front of him was a man whose body was swollen with fluid.[^2]

**3** Jesus responded by asking the experts in the Law[^3] and the Pharisees, "Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath or not?"

**4** But they kept silent. So Jesus took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him away.

**5** Then he said to them, "If one of you has a son or an ox that falls into a well, won't you pull him out at once on the Sabbath day?"

**6** And they could not answer him about these things.

**7** When he noticed how the guests were choosing the places of honor, he told those who had been invited a parable. He said to them,

**8** "When someone invites you to a wedding feast, don't take the place of honor. Someone more distinguished than you may have been invited by your host,

**9** and the host who invited both of you will come and say to you, 'Give this man your place.' Then, in shame, you will have to take the lowest place.

**10** Instead, when you are invited, go and sit in the lowest place, so that when your host comes, he will say to you, 'Friend, move up higher.' Then you will be honored in front of all who are at the table with you.[^4]

**11** For everyone who exalts themselves will be humbled, and whoever humbles themselves will be exalted."

**12** He also said to the man who had invited him, "When you give a lunch or a dinner, don't invite your friends, your brothers, your relatives, or your rich neighbors. Otherwise they may invite you in return, and so you will be repaid.

**13** Instead, when you give a banquet, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind.

**14** Then you will be blessed, because they have nothing to repay you with. You will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous."

**15** When one of those at the table with him heard this, he said to Jesus, "Blessed is anyone who will eat bread in the kingdom of God!"

**16** Jesus said to him, "A man was giving a great banquet, and he invited many people.

**17** At the time for the banquet he sent his servant[^5] to tell those who had been invited, 'Come, because it is ready now.'

**18** But they all alike began to make excuses. The first said to him, 'I have bought a field, and I must go out and see it. Please consider me excused.'

**19** Another said, 'I have bought five pairs of oxen, and I am going to try them out. Please consider me excused.'

**20** Another said, 'I have just married a wife, and so I cannot come.'

**21** The servant came back and reported these things to his master. Then the owner of the house became angry and said to his servant, 'Go out quickly into the streets and alleys of the town, and bring in here the poor, the crippled, the blind, and the lame.'

**22** The servant said, 'Master, what you ordered has been done, and there is still room.'

**23** Then the master said to the servant, 'Go out to the roads and country lanes[^6] and make people come in, so that my house will be full.

**24** For I tell you,[^7] not one of those men who were invited will taste my banquet.'"[^8]

**25** Large crowds were traveling with Jesus. He turned and said to them,

**26** "If anyone comes to me and does not hate[^9] father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and even their own life,[^10] they cannot be my disciple.

**27** Whoever does not carry their own cross and come after me cannot be my disciple.

**28** For which of you, if you want to build a tower, will not first sit down and estimate the cost, to see whether you have enough to finish it?

**29** Otherwise, when you have laid the foundation and are not able to finish, everyone who sees it will begin to ridicule you,

**30** saying, 'This man began to build and was not able to finish.'

**31** Or what king, setting out to go to war against another king, will not first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand to oppose the one coming against him with twenty thousand?

**32** If he cannot, he sends a delegation while the other is still far away and asks for terms of peace.

**33** In the same way, then, none of you can be my disciple if you do not give up everything you have.

**34** "Salt, then, is good. But if even salt loses its taste,[^11] how can it be made salty again?

**35** It is of no use either for the soil or for the manure pile; people throw it out. Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear."

[^1]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^2]: Greek *hydropikos*, suffering from dropsy (edema), a swelling of the body caused by fluid.
[^3]: Greek *nomikoi*, experts in the Law of Moses.
[^4]: Compare Proverbs 25:6–7.
[^5]: Or "slave"; also in verses 21–23. Greek *doulos*.
[^6]: Literally "the roads and hedges", the paths outside the town.
[^7]: "You" is plural in Greek. The master's words seem to merge here with Jesus' own words to his hearers.
[^8]: Many manuscripts add "For many are called, but few are chosen."
[^9]: In biblical idiom, "hate" can mean "love less" or "put second" (compare Genesis 29:30–31).
[^10]: Greek *psyche*, life, soul, self.
[^11]: The Greek verb also means "become foolish".
