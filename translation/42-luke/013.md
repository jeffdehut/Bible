**1** At that very time some people who were there told Jesus about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.[^1]

**2** He answered them, "Do you think that these Galileans were worse sinners than all the other Galileans because they suffered these things?

**3** No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish as they did.

**4** Or those eighteen who were killed when the tower in Siloam fell on them: do you think they were worse offenders[^2] than all the other people living in Jerusalem?

**5** No, I tell you. But unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way."

**6** Then he told this parable: "A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard. He came looking for fruit on it but found none.

**7** So he said to the man who tended the vineyard, 'Look, for three years now I have come looking for fruit on this fig tree and have found none. Cut it down! Why should it use up the soil as well?'

**8** The man answered him, 'Sir,[^3] leave it alone for this year too, until I dig around it and put manure on it.

**9** If it bears fruit next year, well and good.[^4] But if not, you can cut it down.'"

**10** Jesus was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.

**11** A woman was there who for eighteen years had had a spirit that disabled her.[^5] She was bent over and could not stand up straight at all.[^6]

**12** When Jesus saw her, he called her to him and said to her, "Woman, you are set free from your disability."

**13** He placed his hands on her, and immediately she stood up straight and began to praise God.

**14** But the synagogue leader, indignant that Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, responded by saying to the crowd, "There are six days on which work should be done. So come and be healed on those days, and not on the Sabbath day."

**15** The Lord answered him, "You hypocrites! Doesn't each of you untie your ox or your donkey from the manger on the Sabbath and lead it out to give it water?

**16** And this woman, a daughter of Abraham whom Satan has kept bound for eighteen long years,[^7] shouldn't she be set free[^8] from this bond on the Sabbath day?"

**17** As he said this, all his opponents were put to shame, and the whole crowd rejoiced at all the wonderful things he was doing.

**18** So he said, "What is the kingdom of God like? What shall I compare it to?

**19** It is like a mustard seed that a man took and planted in his garden. It grew and became a tree,[^9] and the birds of the sky nested in its branches."[^10]

**20** Again he said, "What shall I compare the kingdom of God to?

**21** It is like yeast that a woman took and mixed[^11] into three seahs[^12] of flour until all of it was leavened."

**22** Jesus went through towns and villages, teaching as he made his way to Jerusalem.

**23** Someone asked him, "Lord, are only a few people being saved?" He said to them,

**24** "Strive to enter through the narrow door.[^13] For many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able to.

**25** Once the owner of the house has gotten up and shut the door, and you begin to stand outside and knock on the door, saying, 'Lord, open up for us!'[^14] he will answer you, 'I don't know you or where you come from.'

**26** Then you will begin to say, 'We ate and drank with you,[^15] and you taught in our streets.'

**27** But he will tell you, 'I don't know where you come from. Depart from me, all you evildoers!'[^16]

**28** There will be weeping and grinding of teeth there, when you see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, while you yourselves are thrown out.

**29** People will come from east and west, and from north and south, and will recline at table in the kingdom of God.

**30** And indeed, some who are last will be first, and some who are first will be last."

**31** At that very hour[^17] some Pharisees came and said to him, "Get away from here and go somewhere else, because Herod wants to kill you."

**32** He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'Look, I am driving out demons and performing healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I will reach my goal.'[^18]

**33** Yet I must keep going today, tomorrow, and the next day, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.

**34** "Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those sent to you! How often I have wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you were not willing!

**35** Look, your house is left to you.[^19] And I tell you, you will not see me until the time comes when you say, 'Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord!'"[^20]

[^1]: Apparently Pilate had them killed while they were offering sacrifices. The incident is not recorded elsewhere.
[^2]: Literally "debtors", a common way of speaking of sinners.
[^3]: Greek *kyrios*, "lord, master, sir".
[^4]: The Greek breaks off: "And if it bears fruit in the future …" The words "well and good" are implied.
[^5]: Literally "having a spirit of weakness". The word translated "disability" in verse 12 is the same word.
[^6]: Or "could not stand fully upright".
[^7]: Literally "has bound, look, eighteen years".
[^8]: Or "untied", the same verb as "untie" in verse 15.
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "a large tree".
[^10]: Compare Ezekiel 17:23; Daniel 4:9, 18 (4:12, 21 in most English Bibles).
[^11]: Literally "hid".
[^12]: Greek *sata*, from the Hebrew *seah*. A seah is about 7 quarts (7 liters), so three seahs is about 20 quarts (22 liters).
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "the narrow gate".
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "Lord, Lord".
[^15]: Literally "in front of you".
[^16]: Psalm 6:9 (6:8 in most English Bibles).
[^17]: Many manuscripts read "On that very day".
[^18]: Or "I will be finished", "I will be made complete". Greek *teleioumai*.
[^19]: Or "is abandoned to you". *Your house* may mean the temple or the city; compare Jeremiah 12:7; 22:5. Many manuscripts add "desolate".
[^20]: Psalm 118:26. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
