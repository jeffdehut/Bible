**1** In those days a decree went out from Caesar Augustus[^1] that the whole world[^2] should be registered.

**2** (This was the first registration, made while Quirinius was governor of Syria.)[^3]

**3** Everyone went to be registered, each to his own town.

**4** Joseph also went up from the town of Nazareth in Galilee to Judea, to the town of David called Bethlehem,[^4] because he was of the house and family line of David.

**5** He went to be registered with Mary, who was engaged to him[^5] and was expecting a child.

**6** While they were there, the time came for her to give birth,

**7** and she gave birth to her firstborn son. She wrapped him in strips of cloth[^6] and laid him in a manger,[^7] because there was no room for them in the guest room.[^8]

**8** In the same region there were shepherds living out in the fields, keeping watch over their flock at night.

**9** An angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified.[^9]

**10** But the angel said to them, "Don't be afraid. Look, I am bringing you good news of great joy, which will be for all the people.

**11** Today in the town of David a Savior has been born for you. He is the Messiah, the Lord.[^10]

**12** This will be the sign for you: you will find a baby wrapped in strips of cloth and lying in a manger."

**13** Suddenly a great company of the heavenly army[^11] appeared with the angel, praising God and saying,

**14** "Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace among people with whom he is pleased!"[^12]

**15** When the angels had left them and gone back into heaven, the shepherds said to one another, "Let us go over to Bethlehem and see this thing that has happened, which the Lord has made known to us."

**16** So they hurried off and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger.

**17** When they had seen him, they made known what they had been told about this child.

**18** All who heard it were amazed at what the shepherds told them.

**19** But Mary treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart.

**20** The shepherds went back, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, just as they had been told.

**21** When eight days had passed and it was time to circumcise him, he was named Jesus, the name the angel had given him before he was conceived in the womb.

**22** When the days of their purification[^13] according to the Law of Moses were completed, they brought him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord

**23** (as it is written in the Law of the Lord, "Every male who first opens the womb will be called holy to the Lord"),[^14]

**24** and to offer a sacrifice according to what is said in the Law of the Lord: "a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons."[^15]

**25** Now there was a man in Jerusalem named Simeon. This man was righteous and devout, and he was waiting for the comfort of Israel.[^16] The Holy Spirit was on him,

**26** and it had been revealed to him by the Holy Spirit that he would not see death before he had seen the Lord's Messiah.[^17]

**27** Led by the Spirit,[^18] he came into the temple. When the parents brought in the child Jesus to do for him what the Law required,

**28** Simeon took him in his arms, praised God, and said:

**29** "Now, Master, you are letting your servant go[^19] in peace, as you promised,[^20]

**30** for my eyes have seen your salvation,

**31** which you have prepared in the presence of all peoples:

**32** a light for revelation to the Gentiles,[^21] and glory for your people Israel."

**33** The child's father and mother were amazed at what was being said about him.[^22]

**34** Then Simeon blessed them and said to his mother Mary, "Look, this child is destined to cause the falling and rising of many in Israel, and to be a sign that will be opposed,[^23]

**35** and a sword will pierce your own soul too, so that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed."

**36** There was also a prophet, Anna, the daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Asher. She was very old. After her marriage[^24] she had lived with her husband seven years,

**37** and then she had been a widow until she was eighty-four.[^25] She never left the temple, but worshiped night and day with fasting and prayer.

**38** At that very hour she came up and began to give thanks to God and to speak about the child to all who were waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem.[^26]

**39** When they had done everything the Law of the Lord required, they returned to Galilee, to their own town of Nazareth.

**40** The child grew and became strong,[^27] filled with wisdom, and the favor[^28] of God was on him.

**41** Every year his parents went to Jerusalem for the festival of the Passover.

**42** When he was twelve years old, they went up as usual for the festival.[^29]

**43** When the days of the festival were over, as they were returning, the boy Jesus stayed behind in Jerusalem, but his parents did not know it.[^30]

**44** Thinking he was in their group of travelers, they went a day's journey. Then they began looking for him among their relatives and friends.

**45** When they did not find him, they went back to Jerusalem to search for him.

**46** After three days they found him in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions.

**47** Everyone who heard him was astonished at his understanding and his answers.

**48** When his parents saw him, they were overwhelmed, and his mother said to him, "Son,[^31] why have you treated us like this? Look, your father and I have been searching for you in great distress."

**49** He said to them, "Why were you searching for me? Didn't you know that I had to be in my Father's house?"[^32]

**50** But they did not understand what he was saying to them.

**51** Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and he was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.

**52** And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature,[^33] and in favor with God and people.

[^1]: Augustus was the first Roman emperor, ruling from 27 BC to AD 14.
[^2]: Greek *oikoumene*, "the inhabited world", meaning the Roman Empire. *Registered*: listed in a census.
[^3]: Quirinius was governor of Syria from AD 6, when he carried out a census of Judea recorded by the historian Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.1–2). Luke places the birth of Jesus in the reign of Herod the Great (1:5), who died in 4 BC. How Luke's dating relates to these dates is debated. Some take the Greek to mean "This registration took place before Quirinius was governor of Syria."
[^4]: David's hometown (1 Samuel 17:12).
[^5]: Many manuscripts read "Mary, his engaged wife".
[^6]: Literally "swaddled him", wrapping a newborn baby tightly in bands of cloth; also in verse 12.
[^7]: A feeding trough for animals; also in verses 12 and 16.
[^8]: Or "the inn". Greek *katalyma*, a place to stay; in 22:11 it is the guest room of a house.
[^9]: Literally "they feared a great fear".
[^10]: Greek *christos kyrios*, "Messiah and Lord", or "the Lord Messiah"; traditionally "Christ the Lord".
[^11]: Traditionally "the heavenly host", the angels as God's army.
[^12]: Literally "among people of good pleasure", that is, people on whom God's favor rests. Many manuscripts read "and on earth peace, goodwill among people". In Greek the difference is a single letter (*eudokias*, *eudokia*).
[^13]: The Law of Moses set a time of purification for a mother after childbirth (Leviticus 12:1–8). The Greek says "their purification".
[^14]: Exodus 13:2, 12. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^15]: Leviticus 12:8, the offering of a mother who could not afford a lamb.
[^16]: That is, the time when God would comfort his people (Isaiah 40:1; 49:13).
[^17]: Or "the Lord's Anointed". Greek *christos*.
[^18]: Literally "in the Spirit".
[^19]: Or "releasing your slave", that is, letting him die. The words *Master* (Greek *despotes*) and *servant* (*doulos*) are those used of an owner and a slave. The song (verses 29–32) is known as the Nunc Dimittis, from its first words in Latin.
[^20]: Literally "according to your word".
[^21]: Or "the nations". Compare Isaiah 42:6; 49:6; 52:10.
[^22]: Many manuscripts read "Joseph and his mother were amazed".
[^23]: Literally "is set for the fall and rising of many in Israel, and for a sign spoken against".
[^24]: Literally "from her virginity".
[^25]: Or "she had been a widow for eighty-four years".
[^26]: Many manuscripts read "redemption in Jerusalem".
[^27]: Many manuscripts add "in spirit".
[^28]: Or "grace".
[^29]: Many manuscripts add "to Jerusalem".
[^30]: Many manuscripts read "Joseph and his mother did not know it".
[^31]: Literally "Child".
[^32]: Or "about my Father's business". Literally "in the things of my Father".
[^33]: Or "in years". Greek *helikia* means both age and height.
