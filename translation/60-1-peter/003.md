**1** In the same way, wives, submit to your own husbands, so that even if some of them do not obey the word, they may be won over without a word by the way their wives live,

**2** when they see your pure and reverent conduct.[^1]

**3** Do not let your beauty be the outward kind, such as braided hair, gold jewelry or fine clothes.[^2]

**4** Instead, let it be the inner person of the heart, with the lasting beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is very precious in God's sight.

**5** For this is how the holy women of the past who put their hope in God used to make themselves beautiful. They submitted to their own husbands,

**6** as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord.[^3] You have become her daughters if you do what is right and do not give way to fear.[^4]

**7** Husbands, in the same way, live with your wives with understanding, showing them honor as the weaker partner[^5] and as heirs with you of the gracious gift of life, so that your prayers will not be hindered.

**8** Finally, all of you, be of one mind, sympathetic, loving one another as brothers and sisters, compassionate and humble.[^6]

**9** Do not repay evil for evil or insult for insult, but on the contrary, bless, for you were called to this, so that you may inherit a blessing.

**10** For "Whoever wants to love life and see good days must keep their tongue from evil and their lips from speaking deceit.[^7]

**11** They must turn away from evil and do good; they must seek peace and pursue it.

**12** For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer. But the face of the Lord is against those who do evil."[^8]

**13** Who is going to harm you if you are eager to do good?[^9]

**14** But even if you should suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear,[^10] and do not be troubled,

**15** but in your hearts regard Christ the Lord as holy.[^11] Always be ready to give an answer to anyone who asks you for the reason for the hope that is in you,

**16** yet do it with gentleness and respect,[^12] keeping a good conscience, so that when you are slandered,[^13] those who abuse your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.

**17** For it is better, if it is God's will, to suffer for doing good than for doing evil.

**18** For Christ also suffered[^14] for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring you[^15] to God. He was put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit,[^16]

**19** in which[^17] he also went and made a proclamation to the spirits in prison,[^18]

**20** who had once disobeyed, when God waited patiently in the days of Noah while the ark was being built. In it a few people, eight in all,[^19] were brought safely through the water.[^20]

**21** And baptism, which corresponds to this,[^21] now saves you[^22] also, not as a removal of dirt from the body, but as the pledge of a good conscience toward God,[^23] through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,

**22** who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God,[^24] with angels, authorities and powers made subject to him.

[^1]: Literally "your pure conduct in fear", perhaps the fear of God.
[^2]: Literally "Let yours be not the outward adorning of braiding hair, putting on gold or wearing clothes."
[^3]: Compare Genesis 18:12, where Sarah calls Abraham "my lord".
[^4]: Literally "and do not fear any terror". Compare Proverbs 3:25, "Do not be afraid of sudden terror".
[^5]: Literally "as with the weaker vessel, the female". Greek *skeuos*, "vessel, container", is used of the body or the person.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "courteous".
[^7]: Psalm 34:13–17 (34:12–16 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 10–12 from the Septuagint, with the psalm's question ("Who is the one who delights in life …?") turned into a statement and its commands into the third person.
[^8]: In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). Peter leaves out the end of the psalm verse, "to cut off the memory of them from the earth".
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "if you imitate what is good".
[^10]: Or "Do not be afraid of them", literally "do not fear their fear". Verses 14–15 echo Isaiah 8:12–13, in a form close to the Septuagint.
[^11]: Or "regard Christ as Lord and as holy". Isaiah 8:13 reads "The LORD of Armies: he is the one you must regard as holy." Many manuscripts read "regard the Lord God as holy".
[^12]: Literally "fear", perhaps the fear of God (compare 2:17–18).
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "when they speak against you as evildoers" (compare 2:12).
[^14]: Some manuscripts read "died".
[^15]: Some manuscripts read "us".
[^16]: Or "by the Spirit". The contrast may be between two spheres of existence, the human and the spiritual, or between Jesus' body and the Spirit by whom he was raised.
[^17]: Or "in whom", that is, in the Spirit; or "and so".
[^18]: *Made a proclamation*: Greek *kerysso*, "proclaim, preach", which does not say what was proclaimed. This passage is one of the most difficult in the New Testament, and there are three main readings of it. (1) After his death or resurrection, Christ went and announced his victory, or God's judgment, to rebellious spirits held in prison since the days of Noah: the "sons of God" of Genesis 6:1–4, whom later Jewish writings (such as 1 Enoch) describe as fallen angels kept for judgment (compare 2 Peter 2:4–5; Jude 6). On this view he may have gone as he ascended (the same verb "went" is used in verse 22). (2) Between his death and resurrection, Christ went to the realm of the dead and preached to the spirits of those who had died, including those who perished in the flood (compare 4:6). This reading is linked with the creeds' words "he descended to the dead (or into hell)". (3) Christ, in the Spirit, preached through Noah (compare 1:11; 2 Peter 2:5) to the people of Noah's day, who refused to listen and are now spirits in prison.
[^19]: Literally "eight souls": Noah, his wife, his three sons and their wives (Genesis 7:13).
[^20]: Or "by means of water".
[^21]: Literally "which, as an antitype, now also saves you, baptism". The water of the flood corresponds to the water of baptism.
[^22]: Many manuscripts read "us".
[^23]: Greek *eperotema*, "request, appeal", or "pledge, answer" (in legal use, the answer given to a formal question). It may mean an appeal to God for a good conscience, or the pledge to God that comes from a good conscience.
[^24]: Compare Psalm 110:1.
