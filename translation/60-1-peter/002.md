**1** So get rid of all malice and all deceit, hypocrisy, envy and all slander.

**2** Like newborn babies, long for the pure spiritual milk,[^1] so that by it you may grow up into salvation,[^2]

**3** since you have tasted that the Lord is good.[^3]

**4** As you come to him, a living stone, rejected by people but chosen and precious in God's sight,

**5** you yourselves also, like living stones, are being built[^4] into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

**6** For it stands in scripture: "Look, I am laying in Zion a stone, a chosen and precious cornerstone, and whoever believes in him[^5] will never be put to shame."[^6]

**7** So the honor is for you who believe.[^7] But for those who do not believe,[^8] "The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone,"[^9]

**8** and, "A stone that makes people stumble and a rock that makes them fall."[^10] They stumble because they disobey the word, as they were destined to do.[^11]

**9** But you are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's own possession,[^12] so that you may proclaim the excellent deeds[^13] of the one who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.

**10** Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people. Once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy.[^14]

**11** Dear friends,[^15] I urge you, as foreigners and resident aliens,[^16] to abstain from the desires of the flesh,[^17] which wage war against the soul.

**12** Keep your conduct among the Gentiles good, so that, though they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good works and glorify God on the day he comes.[^18]

**13** For the Lord's sake submit to every human authority,[^19] whether to the emperor[^20] as supreme,

**14** or to governors, as those sent by him to punish those who do wrong and to praise those who do right.

**15** (For it is God's will that by doing good you should silence the ignorance of foolish people.)

**16** Live as free people, but do not use your freedom as a cover for evil; live as servants[^21] of God.

**17** Honor everyone. Love the family of believers.[^22] Fear God. Honor the emperor.

**18** Household slaves,[^23] submit to your masters with all respect,[^24] not only to those who are good and gentle but also to those who are harsh.

**19** For it is commendable[^25] if someone endures the pain of suffering unjustly because they are mindful of God.

**20** For what credit is it if you endure when you are beaten for doing wrong? But if you endure when you suffer for doing good, this is commendable before God.

**21** This is what you were called to, because Christ also suffered for you,[^26] leaving you an example,[^27] so that you should follow in his steps.

**22** He committed no sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth.[^28]

**23** When he was insulted, he did not insult in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but entrusted himself[^29] to the one who judges justly.

**24** He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree,[^30] so that we might die to sins[^31] and live for righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed.[^32]

**25** For you were going astray like sheep,[^33] but now you have returned to the shepherd and overseer[^34] of your souls.

[^1]: Greek *logikos*, "reasonable, spiritual", perhaps also "of the word" (*logos*, 1:23). *Pure*: Greek *adolos*, "without deceit", in contrast to "deceit" (*dolos*) in verse 1.
[^2]: Many manuscripts leave out "into salvation".
[^3]: Compare Psalm 34:9 (34:8 in most English Bibles), "Taste and see that the LORD is good." In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^4]: Or "let yourselves be built", a command.
[^5]: Or "in it", the stone.
[^6]: Isaiah 28:16, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "a stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a firm foundation. Whoever trusts will not panic."
[^7]: Or "So for you who believe, he is precious." Greek *time*, "honor, preciousness", from the same root as "precious" (*entimos*) in verses 4 and 6. The honor is the opposite of being "put to shame" (verse 6).
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "for those who disobey".
[^9]: Psalm 118:22 (see Mark 12:10). Literally "the head of the corner".
[^10]: Isaiah 8:14.
[^11]: Literally "to which they were also appointed". *Because they disobey the word*: or "they stumble at the word, because they disobey".
[^12]: Isaiah 43:20–21 and Exodus 19:5–6, in the wording of the Septuagint. The Hebrew of Exodus 19:6 reads "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation", and of 19:5 "my own treasured possession".
[^13]: Greek *aretai*, "excellences, praises", the Septuagint's word in Isaiah 43:21, where the Hebrew reads "so that they may declare my praise".
[^14]: Hosea 1:6, 9; 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles), where Hosea's children are named Lo-ammi, "not my people", and Lo-ruhamah, "not shown compassion".
[^15]: Literally "Beloved".
[^16]: Greek *paroikoi kai parepidemoi*, the words of Genesis 23:4 in the Septuagint, where Abraham calls himself "a foreigner and a resident alien".
[^17]: Literally "fleshly desires": the desires of human nature in its weakness.
[^18]: Literally "on the day of visitation", the day when God comes, in judgment or in mercy (compare Isaiah 10:3).
[^19]: Literally "every human creation": perhaps every human institution, or every human being.
[^20]: Literally "the king", the title used in the eastern provinces for the Roman emperor; also in verse 17.
[^21]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^22]: Literally "the brotherhood".
[^23]: Greek *oiketai*, slaves who served in a household.
[^24]: Literally "in all fear", perhaps the fear of God (verse 17).
[^25]: Literally "this is grace", what wins favor with God; also in verse 20.
[^26]: Many manuscripts read "for us".
[^27]: Greek *hypogrammos*, a pattern of letters for a child to trace in learning to write.
[^28]: Isaiah 53:9. The Hebrew reads "although he had done no violence, and there was no deceit in his mouth". Verses 22–25 echo Isaiah 53:4–12.
[^29]: Literally "he handed over", perhaps himself or his cause. Compare Isaiah 53:7.
[^30]: Literally "the wood", the cross (compare Deuteronomy 21:22–23; Acts 5:30). *Bore our sins*: compare Isaiah 53:11–12, "he will bear their wrongdoings … he bore the sin of many".
[^31]: Greek *apoginomai*, "be away from, have no part in", here "die to".
[^32]: Isaiah 53:5, "by his wounds we are healed".
[^33]: Isaiah 53:6, "We all, like sheep, have gone astray".
[^34]: Greek *episkopos*, "overseer, guardian".
