**1** So I appeal to the elders among you, as a fellow elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and one who also shares in the glory that is going to be revealed:

**2** Shepherd God's flock that is among you, watching over it[^1] not because you must but willingly, as God wants;[^2] not for shameful gain but eagerly;

**3** not lording it over those entrusted to you,[^3] but being examples to the flock.

**4** And when the chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the crown of glory that will never fade.

**5** In the same way, you who are younger, submit to the elders.[^4] All of you, clothe yourselves with humility toward one another,[^5] because "God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble."[^6]

**6** So humble yourselves under God's mighty hand, so that he may lift you up at the right time,

**7** casting all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you.[^7]

**8** Be sober-minded; be watchful. Your adversary[^8] the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour.

**9** Resist him, firm in your faith, knowing that the same kinds of suffering are being experienced by your family of believers[^9] throughout the world.

**10** And after you have suffered a little while, the God of all grace, who called you to his eternal glory in Christ, will himself restore you, strengthen you, make you firm and establish you.[^10]

**11** To him be the power[^11] forever and ever.[^12] Amen.

**12** Through Silvanus, whom I regard as a faithful brother, I have written to you briefly,[^13] to encourage you and to testify that this is the true grace of God. Stand firm in it.

**13** She who is in Babylon,[^14] chosen together with you, sends you greetings, and so does Mark, my son.[^15]

**14** Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace to all of you who are in Christ.[^16]

[^1]: Greek *episkopeo*, "watch over, oversee", the verb related to *episkopos*, "overseer" (2:25). Some manuscripts leave it out.
[^2]: Literally "according to God". Many manuscripts leave out these words.
[^3]: Literally "the lots", the people allotted to your care.
[^4]: Or "the older men", in contrast to "the younger".
[^5]: Greek *enkombomai*, "tie on", as a servant ties on an apron. Many manuscripts read "All of you, submitting to one another, clothe yourselves with humility".
[^6]: Proverbs 3:34, as in the Septuagint (see James 4:6). The Hebrew reads "He mocks the mockers, but he gives favor to the humble."
[^7]: Compare Psalm 55:23 (55:22 in most English Bibles), "Cast your burden on the LORD", where the Septuagint reads "your care".
[^8]: Greek *antidikos*, an opponent in a lawsuit, an accuser.
[^9]: Literally "the brotherhood".
[^10]: Some manuscripts leave out "establish you".
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "the glory and the power".
[^12]: Some manuscripts read "forever".
[^13]: Silvanus (Silas) may have carried the letter, or helped to write it (compare Acts 15:22, 27; 2 Corinthians 1:19; 1 Thessalonians 1:1).
[^14]: Probably the church in the city from which the letter was written. *Babylon* was used as a name for Rome (compare Revelation 17–18); some take it as the city in Mesopotamia.
[^15]: Probably John Mark (Acts 12:12), a "son" in the faith.
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "in Christ Jesus. Amen."
