**1** Again I saw all the oppression that is done under the sun. Look, the tears of the oppressed, and they have no one to comfort them! Power is on the side of their oppressors,[^1] and they have no one to comfort them.

**2** So I praised the dead, who have already died, more than the living, who are still alive.

**3** But better than both is the one who has not yet been, who has not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

**4** I saw that all toil and all skill in work comes from one person's envy of another.[^2] This too is vapor[^3] and chasing the wind.[^4]

**5** The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh.[^5]

**6** Better one handful with rest than two fistfuls with toil and chasing the wind.

**7** Again I saw vapor under the sun:

**8** There is someone who is alone, without a second;[^6] he has neither son nor brother. Yet there is no end to all his toil, and his eye[^7] is never satisfied with riches. "For whom am I toiling, and depriving myself of good things?"[^8] This too is vapor and an unhappy task.

**9** Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.

**10** For if they fall, one will lift up his companion. But pity the one who falls alone,[^9] with no second to lift him up!

**11** Again, if two lie down together, they keep warm; but how can one keep warm alone?

**12** And if someone overpowers one who is alone, two can stand against him.[^10] A three-stranded cord is not quickly broken.

**13** Better a poor but wise youth than an old but foolish king who no longer knows how to take advice.

**14** For he came out of prison[^11] to become king, though he was born poor in his kingdom.[^12]

**15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun following[^13] the youth, the second one,[^14] who would stand in his place.

**16** There was no end to all the people, to all whom he led,[^15] yet those who come later will not rejoice in him. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.[^16]

**17** Guard your steps[^17] when you go to the house of God. To draw near to listen[^18] is better than for fools to offer a sacrifice, for they do not know that they are doing evil.[^19]

[^1]: Literally "and from the hand of their oppressors, power".
[^2]: Literally "that it is the envy of a man from his neighbor". Or "the rivalry between one person and another".
[^3]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^4]: See 1:14.
[^5]: That is, he ruins himself through idleness.
[^6]: Literally "There is one, and there is no second".
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "his eyes" but read "his eye".
[^8]: The Hebrew moves into the first person without saying who is speaking.
[^9]: Literally "But woe to him, the one who falls".
[^10]: Literally "And if someone overpowers him, the one, the two will stand against him".
[^11]: Literally "from the house of prisoners".
[^12]: The Hebrew does not make clear who "he" is in each part of this verse. It may be the youth, who rose from prison to the throne though born poor; "in his kingdom" may mean in the old king's kingdom. Or it may be the king, who came out of prison to reign, while the poor youth was born in his kingdom.
[^13]: Literally "with".
[^14]: Perhaps the youth of verse 13, who succeeds the old king, or another youth who takes his place in turn.
[^15]: Literally "to all before whom he was". Or "to all who were before them", that is, earlier generations.
[^16]: Hebrew *ra'yon ruach*; see 1:17.
[^17]: Literally "Guard your foot". The Hebrew text is written "your feet" but read "your foot". Verse 17 is 5:1 in most English Bibles.
[^18]: To listen often means to obey.
[^19]: Literally "for they do not know to do evil".
