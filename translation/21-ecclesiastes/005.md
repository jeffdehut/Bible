**1** Do not be quick with your mouth, and do not let your heart hurry to bring out a word before God.[^1] For God is in heaven and you are on earth, so let your words be few.

**2** For a dream comes with much busyness,[^2] and the voice of a fool with many words.

**3** When you make a vow to God, do not delay to fulfill it, for he has no pleasure in fools. Fulfill what you vow.[^3]

**4** It is better not to vow than to vow and not fulfill it.

**5** Do not let your mouth make your flesh[^4] sin, and do not say before the messenger[^5] that it was a mistake.[^6] Why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?

**6** For when dreams are many, so are vapors[^7] and words.[^8] But fear God.

**7** If you see the poor oppressed, and justice and righteousness robbed in the province, do not be amazed at the matter, for one high official is watched by a higher one, and there are higher ones over them.[^9]

**8** But the gain[^10] of a land in every way is this: a king for the cultivated field.[^11]

**9** Whoever loves money[^12] will never be satisfied with money, and whoever loves wealth will never be satisfied with income. This too is vapor.

**10** When good things increase, those who eat them increase. And what advantage is there to their owner, except to see them with his eyes?

**11** Sweet is the sleep of the laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the abundance of the rich[^13] will not let him sleep.

**12** There is a grievous evil[^14] I have seen under the sun: riches kept by their owner to his harm.

**13** Those riches are lost in some bad venture,[^15] and he has fathered a son, but there is nothing in his hand.

**14** As he came from his mother's womb, so he will go again, naked as he came.[^16] He will take nothing for his toil that he can carry in his hand.

**15** This too is a grievous evil: exactly as he came, so he will go. And what gain does he have, who toils for the wind?

**16** All his days, too, he eats in darkness, with much grief, sickness and anger.[^17]

**17** Look, this is what I have seen to be good and fitting:[^18] to eat and drink and enjoy[^19] all the toil at which one toils under the sun during the few days of life[^20] that God has given him, for this is his portion.

**18** Also, whenever God gives anyone riches and possessions, and enables him to eat from them, to accept his portion and to rejoice in his toil, this is the gift of God.

**19** For he will not dwell much on the days of his life,[^21] because God keeps him busy[^22] with the joy of his heart.

[^1]: Verses 1–19 are 5:2–20 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *inyan*, the word translated "task" in 1:13.
[^3]: Compare Deuteronomy 23:22–24 (23:21–23 in English Bibles).
[^4]: That is, yourself.
[^5]: Hebrew *mal'akh*, "messenger", perhaps a priest or temple official (compare Malachi 2:7). The Septuagint reads "before God".
[^6]: Hebrew *shegagah*, the word used for an unintentional sin in Leviticus 4 and Numbers 15.
[^7]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^8]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "For in a multitude of dreams and vapors and many words; for fear God."
[^9]: Literally "for a high one watches over a high one, and high ones are over them". This may mean that officials protect one another, or that each is watched by another above him.
[^10]: Hebrew *yitron*; see 1:3.
[^11]: The Hebrew is obscure. Literally "And the gain of a land in all, it is, a king for a field that is worked". Perhaps "a king who cares for the farmland", or "even a king is served by the field".
[^12]: Literally "silver". Also later in the verse.
[^13]: Or "the full stomach of the rich".
[^14]: Literally "a sick evil". Also in verse 15.
[^15]: Hebrew *inyan ra*, the phrase translated "an unhappy task" in 1:13 and 4:8.
[^16]: Compare Job 1:21.
[^17]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and he has much grief, and his sickness, and wrath".
[^18]: Literally "what I have seen, I: good that is beautiful".
[^19]: Literally "to see good in".
[^20]: Literally "the number of the days of his life".
[^21]: Or "For he will hardly notice the days of his life passing". Literally "For he will not much remember the days of his life".
[^22]: Or "God answers him".
