**1** There is an evil I have seen under the sun, and it lies heavy on human beings:[^1]

**2** a man to whom God gives riches, possessions and honor, so that he lacks nothing he desires, yet God does not give him the power to eat from them, but a stranger eats them. This is vapor[^2] and a grievous sickness.[^3]

**3** If a man fathers a hundred children[^4] and lives many years, however many the days of his years may be, if he is not satisfied with good things,[^5] and he has no burial either,[^6] I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.

**4** For it comes in vapor and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered.

**5** It has not even seen the sun or known anything, yet it has more rest than he.

**6** Even if he lived a thousand years twice over, but did not enjoy[^7] good things, do not all go to one place?

**7** All the toil of human beings is for their mouth, yet the appetite[^8] is never filled.

**8** For what advantage does the wise have over the fool? What does the poor person gain by knowing how to conduct himself before the living?[^9]

**9** Better is what the eyes see than the wandering of desire.[^10] This too is vapor and chasing the wind.[^11]

**10** Whatever exists has already been named, and it is known what a human being is:[^12] he cannot contend with one who is stronger than he.[^13]

**11** The more words, the more vapor;[^14] and what advantage is that to anyone?

**12** For who knows what is good for a person in life, during the few days of his life of vapor,[^15] which he passes like a shadow? For who can tell anyone what will happen after him under the sun?

[^1]: Or "and it is common among human beings".
[^2]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^3]: Literally "an evil sickness".
[^4]: Literally "a hundred".
[^5]: Literally "if his soul is not satisfied with good".
[^6]: To lack a proper burial was a great disgrace.
[^7]: Literally "see".
[^8]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here "appetite"; also "life, self".
[^9]: Literally "What to the poor man who knows how to walk before the living?"
[^10]: Literally "than the going of the *nephesh*", the appetite that roams after what it does not have.
[^11]: See 1:14.
[^12]: Or "and it is known that he is human (*adam*)", perhaps recalling that the first human was named from the ground, *adamah* (Genesis 2:7).
[^13]: Perhaps God.
[^14]: Literally "For there are many words that increase vapor".
[^15]: Literally "the number of the days of the life of his vapor".
