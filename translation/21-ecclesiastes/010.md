**1** Dead flies[^1] make the perfumer's oil stink and ferment; a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor.[^2]

**2** The heart of the wise is at his right hand, but the heart of the fool at his left.[^3]

**3** Even when a fool[^4] walks along the road, he lacks sense,[^5] and he shows everyone that he is a fool.[^6]

**4** If the ruler's anger[^7] rises against you, do not leave your place, for calmness lays great offenses to rest.

**5** There is an evil I have seen under the sun, like a mistake[^8] that comes from the ruler:

**6** folly is set in many high places, while the rich sit in low places.

**7** I have seen servants[^9] on horses, and princes walking on foot like servants.

**8** Whoever digs a pit may fall into it, and whoever breaks through a wall may be bitten by a snake.

**9** Whoever quarries stones may be hurt by them, and whoever splits logs may be endangered by them.

**10** If the iron is blunt and one does not sharpen its edge, he must use more strength; but wisdom has the advantage of bringing success.[^10]

**11** If the snake bites before it is charmed, there is no gain[^11] for the charmer.[^12]

**12** The words of a wise person's mouth win favor,[^13] but the lips of a fool swallow him up.

**13** The words of his mouth begin in folly, and his talk ends in evil madness.

**14** The fool multiplies words, though no one knows what will happen; and what will come after him, who can tell him?

**15** The toil of fools wears them out, for they do not even know the way to the city.[^14]

**16** Woe to you, O land, whose king is a youth,[^15] and whose princes feast in the morning!

**17** Happy are you, O land, whose king is the son of nobles, and whose princes eat at the proper time, for strength and not for drunkenness!

**18** Through laziness the roof sinks in, and through idle hands the house leaks.

**19** For laughter they make a feast,[^16] and wine gladdens life, and money answers everything.[^17]

**20** Even in your thoughts do not curse the king, and in your bedroom do not curse the rich, for a bird of the air may carry your voice, and a winged creature may tell the matter.

[^1]: Literally "flies of death". Or "deadly flies".
[^2]: Literally "Weightier than wisdom, than honor, is a little folly".
[^3]: The right hand stood for strength and favor, the left for misfortune.
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "the fool" but read "a fool".
[^5]: Literally "his heart is lacking".
[^6]: Literally "and he says to everyone, he is a fool". Or "and he says of everyone that they are fools".
[^7]: Literally "spirit". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^8]: Hebrew *shegagah*, an unintentional wrong; see 5:5.
[^9]: Or "slaves". Also later in the verse.
[^10]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "and the advantage of making successful is wisdom".
[^11]: Hebrew *yitron*; see 1:3.
[^12]: Literally "the master of the tongue".
[^13]: Literally "are grace".
[^14]: Perhaps a saying for someone who cannot do even the simplest thing.
[^15]: Or "a servant". Hebrew *na'ar* can mean either, and the contrast in verse 17 is with a king of noble birth.
[^16]: Literally "make bread".
[^17]: Or "money provides for everything", or "money keeps everyone busy".
