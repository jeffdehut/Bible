**1** For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter[^1] under heaven:[^2]

**2** a time to be born and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted;

**3** a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to break down and a time to build up;

**4** a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance;

**5** a time to throw stones away and a time to gather stones,[^3] a time to embrace and a time to refrain from embracing;

**6** a time to seek and a time to lose, a time to keep and a time to throw away;

**7** a time to tear and a time to sew, a time to be silent and a time to speak;

**8** a time to love and a time to hate, a time for war and a time for peace.

**9** What gain[^4] does the worker have from his toil?

**10** I have seen the task that God has given human beings to keep them busy.

**11** He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also set eternity[^5] in their hearts, yet so that[^6] no one can find out the work that God has done from beginning to end.

**12** I know that there is nothing better for them than to be glad and to do good[^7] while they live.

**13** And also, when anyone eats and drinks and enjoys[^8] all his toil, that is the gift of God.

**14** I know that whatever God does will last forever. Nothing can be added to it, and nothing taken from it. God has done it so that people will fear him.[^9]

**15** What has been is already here, and what is to be has already been, and God seeks out what has passed by.[^10]

**16** And I saw something else under the sun: in the place of justice, there was wickedness, and in the place of righteousness, there was wickedness.

**17** I said to myself,[^11] "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every matter and for every deed there."[^12]

**18** I said to myself about human beings, "God is testing them,[^13] to show them that they are animals, nothing more."[^14]

**19** For the fate[^15] of human beings and the fate of animals is one and the same: as one dies, so dies the other. They all have the same breath,[^16] and humans have no advantage over animals, for everything is vapor.[^17]

**20** All go to one place. All came from the dust, and all return to the dust.

**21** Who knows whether the spirit of human beings goes upward, and whether the spirit of animals goes down into the earth?[^18]

**22** So I saw that there is nothing better than for people to rejoice in their works, for that is their portion. For who can bring them to see what will happen after them?

[^1]: Hebrew *chefets*, "delight, desire", and in later Hebrew "matter, affair". Also in verse 17.
[^2]: See 1:13.
[^3]: The meaning of the stones is uncertain: perhaps clearing a field and gathering stones for building, or ruining an enemy's field with stones (2 Kings 3:19, 25).
[^4]: Hebrew *yitron*; see 1:3.
[^5]: Hebrew *ha-olam*, "eternity", the distant past and future. Others suggest "a sense of the ages", "the world", or, from a root meaning "to hide", "darkness, ignorance".
[^6]: Or "without which".
[^7]: Or "to enjoy themselves". Literally "to do good in his life".
[^8]: Literally "sees good in".
[^9]: Literally "fear before him".
[^10]: Literally "God seeks what is pursued". The sense may be that God brings back what has gone by, or that God looks after those who are pursued.
[^11]: Literally "I said, I, in my heart". Also in verse 18.
[^12]: The sense of "there" is uncertain: perhaps with God, or at the judgment.
[^13]: Or "God has set them apart".
[^14]: Literally "that they are animals, they themselves".
[^15]: Hebrew *miqreh*, "what befalls"; see 2:14.
[^16]: Hebrew *ruach*, "breath, spirit, wind". Also in verse 21, where it is "spirit".
[^17]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^18]: Or "Who knows the spirit of human beings, which goes upward, and the spirit of animals, which goes down into the earth?" The Hebrew vowel points read it this way. The same consonants can be read as questions, as followed here and in the Septuagint.
