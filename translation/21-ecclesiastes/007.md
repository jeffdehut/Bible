**1** A good name is better than good oil,[^1] and the day of death than the day of one's birth.

**2** It is better to go to a house of mourning than to go to a house of feasting, for that is the end of every person, and the living will take it to heart.

**3** Grief[^2] is better than laughter, for when the face is sad the heart may be glad.[^3]

**4** The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of pleasure.

**5** It is better to listen to the rebuke of the wise than to listen to the song of fools.

**6** For like the crackling of thorns under a pot,[^4] so is the laughter of the fool. This too is vapor.[^5]

**7** Surely oppression[^6] makes the wise foolish, and a bribe corrupts the heart.

**8** Better is the end of a thing than its beginning; better a patient spirit than a proud spirit.[^7]

**9** Do not be quick in your spirit to become angry,[^8] for anger lodges in the lap of fools.

**10** Do not say, "Why were the former days better than these?" For it is not from wisdom that you ask about this.

**11** Wisdom is good with an inheritance,[^9] and an advantage to those who see the sun.

**12** For to be in the shade of wisdom is to be in the shade of money,[^10] and the advantage of knowledge is this: wisdom gives life to those who have it.

**13** Consider the work of God: who can make straight what he has made crooked?

**14** In the day of prosperity, be glad,[^11] and in the day of adversity, consider: God has made the one as well as the other, so that no one can find out anything that will come after him.

**15** In my days of vapor[^12] I have seen everything: there is a righteous person who perishes in his righteousness, and there is a wicked person who lives long in his wickedness.

**16** Do not be too righteous, and do not make yourself too wise. Why destroy yourself?

**17** Do not be too wicked, and do not be a fool. Why die before your time?

**18** It is good that you should take hold of the one and not let go of the other,[^13] for whoever fears God will come through with both.[^14]

**19** Wisdom makes the wise stronger than ten rulers in a city.

**20** Surely there is no one on earth so righteous that he does good and never sins.[^15]

**21** Also, do not take to heart all the things that people say, or you may hear your servant[^16] cursing you.

**22** For your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others.

**23** All this I tested by wisdom. I said, "I will be wise," but it was far from me.

**24** What exists is far off, and deep, very deep. Who can find it out?

**25** I turned, I and my heart, to know and to explore and to seek wisdom and the sum of things,[^17] and to know that wickedness is folly and that foolishness is madness.

**26** And I find more bitter than death the woman who is a snare, whose heart is a net and whose hands are chains. The one who pleases God[^18] escapes her, but the sinner is caught by her.[^19]

**27** "See, this is what I have found," says the Teacher,[^20] "adding one thing to another to find the sum,

**28** which my soul still seeks but I have not found: one man[^21] among a thousand I have found, but a woman among all these I have not found.

**29** Only see, this I have found: that God made human beings upright, but they have sought out many schemes."[^22]

[^1]: A play on words: *shem*, "name", and *shemen*, "oil", here fragrant oil or perfume.
[^2]: Hebrew *ka'as*, "vexation, grief, anger", as in 1:18.
[^3]: Literally "for by sadness of face the heart is made good". Or "the heart is made better".
[^4]: A play on words: *sirim*, "thorns", under the *sir*, "pot".
[^5]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2.
[^6]: Or "extortion".
[^7]: Literally "better long of spirit than high of spirit".
[^8]: The verb is from *ka'as*, translated "grief" in verse 3.
[^9]: Or "Wisdom is as good as an inheritance".
[^10]: Literally "For in the shade of wisdom, in the shade of silver". Shade is a picture of protection.
[^11]: Literally "In the day of good, be in good".
[^12]: Literally "in the days of my vapor", that is, in my brief life.
[^13]: Literally "and also from this do not rest your hand".
[^14]: Literally "will go out with all of them". Or "will avoid all extremes".
[^15]: Compare 1 Kings 8:46.
[^16]: Or "slave".
[^17]: Hebrew *cheshbon*, "reckoning, account, explanation". Also in verse 27.
[^18]: Literally "who is good before God".
[^19]: Some see here the dangerous woman of Proverbs 5 and 7, or Folly pictured as a woman (Proverbs 9:13–18).
[^20]: Here, as in 1:2 and 12:8–10, the narrator speaks of the Teacher. In the Hebrew the verb "says" is feminine, perhaps matching the feminine form of *Qohelet* (see 1:1).
[^21]: Hebrew *adam*, usually "human being", here set against "woman".
[^22]: Hebrew *chishvonot*, a play on *cheshbon*, "sum", in verses 25 and 27.
