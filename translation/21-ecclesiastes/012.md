**1** Remember your Creator[^1] in the days of your youth, before the days of trouble come and the years draw near when you will say, "I have no pleasure in them";

**2** before the sun and the light, the moon and the stars grow dark, and the clouds return after the rain;

**3** in the day when the keepers of the house tremble, and the strong men are bent, and the women who grind stop because they are few, and those who look through the windows grow dim;[^2]

**4** when the doors to the street are shut, as the sound of the mill grows low, and one rises at the sound of a bird,[^3] and all the daughters of song[^4] are brought low;

**5** when people are afraid of what is high, and terrors are on the road; when the almond tree blossoms, and the grasshopper drags itself along, and the caper berry fails;[^5] for a person is going to his eternal home,[^6] and the mourners go about in the street;

**6** before the silver cord is snapped[^7] and the golden bowl is broken, and the jar is shattered at the spring, and the wheel is broken at the cistern;[^8]

**7** and the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit[^9] returns to God who gave it.

**8** "Vapor of vapors,"[^10] says the Teacher,[^11] "everything is vapor."

**9** Besides being wise, the Teacher also taught the people knowledge. He weighed,[^12] searched out and arranged many proverbs.

**10** The Teacher sought to find pleasing words, and to write words of truth correctly.[^13]

**11** The words of the wise are like goads, and like nails firmly fixed are the collected sayings;[^14] they are given by one shepherd.[^15]

**12** Beyond these, my son, be warned. Of making many books there is no end, and much study wearies the body.[^16]

**13** The end of the matter; all has been heard. Fear God and keep his commands, for this is the whole of a human being.[^17]

**14** For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every hidden thing, whether good or evil.

[^1]: Hebrew *bor'ekha*, "your Creator", written in a plural form, like the plural of majesty in *Elohim*. Some hear a play on *bor*, "cistern" (compare Proverbs 5:15 and verse 6) or "pit, grave".
[^2]: Verses 2–6 picture old age and death. Many take the images as the failing body (the keepers as arms, the women who grind as teeth, those at the windows as eyes); others as a great house or a town falling into silence and decay.
[^3]: Or "and the sound of the bird rises".
[^4]: That is, the singing voices, or the notes of song.
[^5]: Hebrew *aviyyonah*, "caper berry", which was eaten to stir the appetite. The sense is probably that desire fails.
[^6]: Literally "the house of his eternity".
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "is removed" but read "is snapped".
[^8]: Hebrew *bor*, perhaps echoing "your Creator" (*bor'ekha*) in verse 1.
[^9]: Or "breath". Hebrew *ruach*. Compare Genesis 2:7; 3:19.
[^10]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": the book ends its words as it began them (1:2).
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-qohelet*, "the Teacher", here with the article; see 1:1. Verses 9–14 speak about the Teacher, perhaps in the voice of the one who collected his words.
[^12]: Or "He listened".
[^13]: Or "and what was written was upright, words of truth".
[^14]: Literally "the masters of collections": the collected sayings, or those who collect them. Or "the masters of assemblies".
[^15]: The shepherd may be God, or the teacher who gathered the sayings.
[^16]: Literally "the flesh".
[^17]: Literally "for this is all the human being". That is, this is the whole duty of everyone, or this is what every person is for.
