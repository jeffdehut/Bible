**1** The words of the Teacher,[^1] the son of David, king in Jerusalem.

**2** "Vapor of vapors,"[^2] says the Teacher, "vapor of vapors! Everything is vapor."

**3** What gain[^3] do people have from all their toil, all their toiling under the sun?[^4]

**4** A generation goes and a generation comes, but the earth stands forever.

**5** The sun rises and the sun goes down, and it hurries, panting,[^5] back to its place, and there it rises again.

**6** It goes to the south and turns to the north; round and round goes the wind, and on its rounds the wind returns.

**7** All the streams flow to the sea, yet the sea is never full. To the place where the streams flow, there they keep on flowing.[^6]

**8** All things are weary,[^7] more than anyone can say. The eye is never satisfied with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

**9** What has been is what will be, and what has been done is what will be done. There is nothing new under the sun.

**10** There is a thing of which people say, "Look, this is new!" It was here already, in the ages before us.

**11** There is no remembrance of those who came before,[^8] and of those who are yet to come there will be no remembrance either, among those who come after them.

**12** I, the Teacher, was king over Israel in Jerusalem.

**13** I set my heart[^9] to seek and to explore by wisdom all that is done under heaven.[^10] It is an unhappy task[^11] that God has given human beings to keep them busy.[^12]

**14** I have seen all the deeds that are done under the sun, and look, everything is vapor and chasing the wind.[^13]

**15** What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is missing cannot be counted.

**16** I said to myself,[^14] "Look, I have grown great and gained more wisdom than all who ruled Jerusalem before me,[^15] and my heart has seen much wisdom and knowledge."

**17** So I set my heart to know wisdom, and to know madness and folly. I learned that this too is chasing the wind.[^16]

**18** For with much wisdom comes much grief, and whoever increases knowledge increases pain.

[^1]: Hebrew *Qohelet*, a title or pen name, from the verb *qahal*, "to gather, assemble": one who gathers or addresses an assembly, or perhaps one who gathers sayings (12:9). The Greek title of the book, *Ekklesiastes*, means a member or speaker of an assembly; the traditional English rendering is "the Preacher". The word is feminine in form, perhaps as the name of an office. It is "the Teacher" throughout (1:2, 12; 7:27; 12:8–10). The book does not name Solomon, though the description fits him.
[^2]: Hebrew *havel havalim*. *Hevel* means "breath" or "vapor": something real but without substance, which cannot be grasped and soon vanishes. It is also Abel's name (Genesis 4:2). Ecclesiastes uses the word thirty-eight times for what is fleeting, futile, empty or beyond understanding. Traditionally it is translated "vanity", and in some modern versions "meaningless"; here it is "vapor" every time, so that readers can follow the pattern. "Vapor of vapors" is a superlative, like "Song of Songs": the merest vapor, utterly fleeting. The book ends with the same words (12:8).
[^3]: Hebrew *yitron*, "gain, profit, what is left over", a word found only in Ecclesiastes. It is "gain" or "advantage" throughout.
[^4]: "Under the sun" is the book's phrase for this world and human life in it, as people experience it. It occurs twenty-nine times, always translated the same way.
[^5]: Hebrew *sho'ef*, "panting, gasping", or "hurrying": the sun is pictured as a weary runner.
[^6]: Literally "there they return to go". Or "from there they go back again".
[^7]: Or "All words are weary". Hebrew *devarim* means both "things" and "words".
[^8]: Or "of the things that came before … of the things yet to come".
[^9]: Literally "I gave my heart", that is, I applied my mind. In Hebrew the heart is the seat of thought and will. Also in verse 17.
[^10]: Literally "under the heavens", a variation on "under the sun" (verse 3). Also in 2:3 and 3:1.
[^11]: Hebrew *inyan ra*, "a bad business, an evil occupation".
[^12]: Literally "to be busied with it". Or "to be afflicted with". *Inyan*, "task", and the verb here come from the same root.
[^13]: Hebrew *re'ut ruach*, "pursuit of wind", or perhaps "shepherding the wind" (from *ra'ah*, "to tend, to herd"), something that can never be caught or held. Traditionally "vexation of spirit". The phrase occurs seven times, and a related form, *ra'yon ruach* (verse 17; 4:16), twice; both are "chasing the wind" throughout.
[^14]: Literally "I spoke, I, with my heart".
[^15]: Literally "all who were before me over Jerusalem".
[^16]: Hebrew *ra'yon ruach*, a form of the phrase in verse 14.
