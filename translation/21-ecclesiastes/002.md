**1** I said to myself,[^1] "Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy what is good."[^2] But look, this too was vapor.[^3]

**2** Of laughter I said, "It is madness," and of pleasure, "What does it accomplish?"

**3** I explored with my heart how to cheer my body with wine,[^4] while my heart still guided me with wisdom, and how to take hold of folly, until I could see what is good for human beings to do under heaven during the few days of their life.[^5]

**4** I did great things. I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.

**5** I made myself gardens and parks,[^6] and I planted every kind of fruit tree in them.

**6** I made myself pools of water, to water from them a forest of growing trees.

**7** I bought male and female slaves, and I had slaves born in my house.[^7] I also had great herds and flocks, more than all who were in Jerusalem before me.

**8** I also gathered for myself silver and gold, and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got myself male and female singers, and the delights of human beings: many concubines.[^8]

**9** So I became great, greater than all who were in Jerusalem before me. My wisdom also stayed with me.

**10** Whatever my eyes asked for, I did not keep from them. I did not hold back my heart from any pleasure, for my heart took pleasure in all my toil, and this was my portion[^9] from all my toil.

**11** Then I turned to look at all the works my hands had done, and at the toil I had toiled to do, and look, everything was vapor and chasing the wind.[^10] There was no gain under the sun.

**12** Then I turned to consider wisdom, and madness and folly. For what can the one do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done.[^11]

**13** I saw that wisdom has an advantage[^12] over folly, as light has an advantage over darkness.

**14** The wise have eyes in their heads, but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I also knew that one fate[^13] befalls them all.

**15** So I said to myself,[^14] "What befalls the fool will befall me too. Why then have I been so very wise?" And I said to myself that this too was vapor.

**16** For there is no lasting remembrance of the wise, any more than of the fool, since in the days to come everything will already be forgotten. How the wise die, just like the fool!

**17** So I hated life, because the work that is done under the sun was grievous to me; for everything is vapor and chasing the wind.

**18** I hated all my toil at which I toil under the sun, because I must leave it to the one who will come after me.

**19** And who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all my toil, all I toiled at and was wise in under the sun. This too is vapor.

**20** So I turned and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil at which I had toiled under the sun.

**21** For there is a person whose toil is done with wisdom, knowledge and skill, and he must give it as a portion to someone who did not toil for it. This too is vapor, and a great evil.

**22** For what does a person get from all his toil and the striving of his heart[^15] at which he toils under the sun?

**23** For all his days are pain, and his task is grief; even at night his heart does not rest. This too is vapor.

**24** There is nothing better for a person than to eat and drink and let himself enjoy his toil.[^16] This too, I saw, is from the hand of God.

**25** For who can eat, and who can find enjoyment,[^17] apart from me?[^18]

**26** For to the person who pleases him[^19] God gives wisdom, knowledge and joy, but to the sinner he gives the task of gathering and collecting, only to give it to the one who pleases God. This too is vapor and chasing the wind.

[^1]: Literally "I said, I, in my heart".
[^2]: Literally "and see good".
[^3]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": what is fleeting, futile or beyond understanding; see 1:2.
[^4]: Literally "how to draw my flesh with wine". The meaning of the verb is uncertain: perhaps "to cheer", "to stimulate" or "to indulge".
[^5]: Literally "the number of the days of their life".
[^6]: Hebrew *pardesim*, a Persian loanword for an enclosed park. It is the source of the English word "paradise".
[^7]: Literally "sons of the house".
[^8]: Hebrew *shiddah ve-shiddot*, words found only here, of uncertain meaning. They are usually understood as "a concubine, and concubines" (perhaps from *shad*, "breast"); others take them as "a chest, and chests" of treasure. The singular and plural together express a great number.
[^9]: Hebrew *cheleq*, "portion, share": what is allotted to someone. A key word in Ecclesiastes, always "portion".
[^10]: See 1:14.
[^11]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "For what the man who will come after the king, what they have already done". Or "For what is the man who comes after the king? He is the one whom they made king long ago."
[^12]: Hebrew *yitron*, the word translated "gain" in verse 11; see 1:3.
[^13]: Hebrew *miqreh*, "what happens, what befalls someone": here death.
[^14]: Literally "in my heart". Also later in the verse.
[^15]: Hebrew *ra'yon libbo*. *Ra'yon* is the word in "chasing the wind" (*ra'yon ruach*) in 1:17.
[^16]: Literally "There is no good in a person that he should eat and drink and make his soul see good in his toil". Most take it to mean "nothing better than", as in 3:12, 22 and 8:15.
[^17]: Hebrew *yachush*, a word of uncertain meaning: perhaps "enjoy", or "worry".
[^18]: Or "more than I". The Septuagint reads "apart from him", that is, apart from God.
[^19]: Literally "who is good before him". Also later in the verse.
