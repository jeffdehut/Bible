**1** All this I took to heart, and I sought to make all this clear:[^1] that the righteous and the wise and their deeds are in the hand of God. Whether it is love or hate, no one knows; everything lies before them.[^2]

**2** Everything is the same for everyone:[^3] one fate comes to the righteous and the wicked, to the good,[^4] to the clean and the unclean, to the one who offers sacrifices and the one who does not. As it is with the good, so it is with the sinner; as it is with the one who swears an oath, so it is with the one who fears an oath.

**3** This is an evil in all that is done under the sun: that one fate comes to all. The hearts of human beings are full of evil, too, and there is madness in their hearts while they live, and after that they go to the dead.[^5]

**4** But whoever is joined[^6] with all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion.

**5** For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing at all. They have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten.[^7]

**6** Their love and their hatred and their envy have already perished, and they will never again have a portion in anything that is done under the sun.

**7** Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a glad heart, for God has already approved what you do.

**8** Let your clothes always be white, and let your head never lack oil.

**9** Enjoy life[^8] with the wife whom you love, all the days of your life of vapor[^9] that he has given you under the sun, all your days of vapor. For that is your portion in life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.

**10** Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your strength, for there is no work or planning or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol,[^10] where you are going.

**11** Again I saw under the sun that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to the intelligent, nor favor to the skilled, for time and chance happen to them all.

**12** For no one knows his time. Like fish caught in a cruel net, and like birds caught in a snare, so human beings are trapped at an evil time, when it falls on them suddenly.

**13** This too I saw as wisdom under the sun, and it seemed great to me.

**14** There was a small city with few people in it, and a great king came against it, surrounded it, and built great siegeworks[^11] against it.

**15** Now there was found in it a poor wise man, and he saved the city by his wisdom. Yet no one remembered that poor man.[^12]

**16** So I said, "Wisdom is better than strength, but the poor man's wisdom is despised, and his words are not heard."

**17** The words of the wise, heard in quiet, are better than the shouting of a ruler among fools.

**18** Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good.

[^1]: Literally "and to examine all this".
[^2]: Or "everything that lies before them is vapor". The Septuagint reads this, with "vapor" (Greek *mataiotes*, its word for *hevel*) in place of the first word of verse 2, "everything".
[^3]: Literally "Everything as to everyone".
[^4]: The Septuagint adds "and to the bad".
[^5]: Literally "and after him, to the dead".
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "whoever is chosen" but read "whoever is joined".
[^7]: A play on words: *sakhar*, "reward", and *zikhram*, "the memory of them".
[^8]: Literally "See life".
[^9]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor"; see 1:2. Here, as in 6:12 and 7:15, the sense is "fleeting".
[^10]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^11]: Hebrew *metsodim*, "nets" or "strongholds", here the works built to besiege a city.
[^12]: Or "and he could have saved the city by his wisdom, but no one remembered that poor man".
