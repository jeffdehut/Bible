**1** Look, I am sending my messenger,[^1] and he will prepare the way before me. Then suddenly the Lord[^2] whom you seek will come to his temple, the messenger of the covenant, in whom you delight. Look, he is coming, says the LORD of Armies.[^3]

**2** But who can endure the day of his coming? Who can stand when he appears? For he will be like a refiner's fire and like launderers' soap.[^4]

**3** He will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, and he will purify the sons of Levi[^5] and refine them like gold and silver. Then they will bring offerings[^6] to the LORD in righteousness.

**4** Then the offering of Judah and Jerusalem will be pleasing to the LORD, as in the days of old and as in former years.

**5** I will come near to you for judgment. I will be quick to testify against the sorcerers, the adulterers and those who swear falsely, against those who cheat hired workers of their wages[^7] and oppress the widow and the fatherless, and against those who deprive the foreigner of justice and do not fear me, says the LORD of Armies.

**6** For I, the LORD, do not change; and you, children of Jacob, have not come to an end.[^8]

**7** From the days of your ancestors you have turned aside from my decrees and have not kept them. Return to me, and I will return to you,[^9] says the LORD of Armies. But you say, "How shall we return?"

**8** Will a human being rob God?[^10] Yet you are robbing me. But you say, "How have we robbed you?" In tithes and contributions.[^11]

**9** You are cursed with a curse, yet you are robbing me, the whole nation of you.

**10** Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, so that there may be food in my house. Test me in this, says the LORD of Armies, and see if I will not open for you the windows of heaven[^12] and pour out for you a blessing until there is no more need.[^13]

**11** I will rebuke the devourer[^14] for you, so that it will not destroy the fruit of your ground, and your vine in the field will not lose its fruit, says the LORD of Armies.

**12** Then all the nations will call you blessed, for you will be a land of delight, says the LORD of Armies.

**13** Your words have been harsh against me,[^15] says the LORD. But you say, "What have we said against you?"

**14** You have said, "It is useless to serve God. What did we gain by keeping his charge[^16] and walking about in mourning before the LORD of Armies?

**15** So now we call the arrogant blessed. Those who do evil not only prosper; they test God and escape."

**16** Then those who feared the LORD spoke with one another. The LORD paid attention and heard, and a book of remembrance[^17] was written before him for those who feared the LORD and honored his name.[^18]

**17** They will be mine, says the LORD of Armies, my treasured possession,[^19] on the day that I act. I will spare them as a man spares his son who serves him.

**18** Then you will again see the difference between the righteous and the wicked, between the one who serves God and the one who does not serve him.

**19** For look, the day is coming, burning like an oven.[^20] All the arrogant and all who do evil will be stubble, and the day that is coming will set them on fire, says the LORD of Armies, so that it will leave them neither root nor branch.

**20** But for you who fear my name, the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings.[^21] You will go out and leap like calves let out of the stall.

**21** You will trample the wicked, for they will be ashes under the soles of your feet on the day that I act, says the LORD of Armies.

**22** Remember the Law[^22] of Moses my servant, the decrees and rulings that I commanded him at Horeb[^23] for all Israel.

**23** Look, I am going to send you Elijah the prophet[^24] before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.

**24** He will turn the hearts of fathers to their children and the hearts of children to their fathers,[^25] so that I will not come and strike the land with a curse of destruction.[^26]

[^1]: Hebrew *mal'akhi*, "my messenger", the same word as the name Malachi (1:1). Mark 1:2 quotes this line together with Exodus 23:20, "Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you. He will prepare your way," and the Gospels apply it to John the Baptist (Matthew 11:10; Luke 7:27). *Prepare* is the verb of Isaiah 40:3, "prepare the way for the LORD".
[^2]: Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name YHWH.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^4]: Hebrew *borit*, lye, an alkali used for washing clothes.
[^5]: The priests and Levites (2:4–9).
[^6]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering. Also in verse 4.
[^7]: Compare Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15.
[^8]: Or "you have not stopped being children of Jacob", that is, as deceitful as their ancestor (Genesis 27:36).
[^9]: The same words are found in Zechariah 1:3.
[^10]: Hebrew *qava*, "rob", a rare verb (Proverbs 22:23). The Septuagint reads "Will a person deceive God?" Also later in the verse and in verse 9.
[^11]: Hebrew *terumah*, the portion set apart from the offerings for the priests (Leviticus 7:14; Numbers 18:8–19). The tithes supported the Levites (Numbers 18:21–32; Nehemiah 13:10–12).
[^12]: The openings through which the rain was pictured as falling (Genesis 7:11; 2 Kings 7:2).
[^13]: Literally "until there is no more enough".
[^14]: Probably the locust.
[^15]: Literally "Your words have been strong against me".
[^16]: Hebrew *mishmeret*, "charge, duty" (Zechariah 3:7). *In mourning* is literally "in black", the clothing of mourners.
[^17]: A record of those who belong to the LORD (compare Exodus 32:32; Psalm 69:29; 69:28 in most English Bibles; Daniel 12:1). The kings of Persia kept such books (Esther 6:1–2).
[^18]: Literally "thought on his name".
[^19]: Hebrew *segullah*, a king's private treasure, the word used of Israel at Sinai (Exodus 19:5; Deuteronomy 7:6). Or "on the day when I make up my treasured possession".
[^20]: Verses 19–24 are 4:1–6 in most English Bibles.
[^21]: In the ancient Near East the sun was often pictured as a disk with wings. *Wings* may also mean the rays of the sun. *Righteousness* is Hebrew *tsedaqah*, here the justice that puts right what was wrong.
[^22]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law", here the Law given through Moses as a whole.
[^23]: Another name for Mount Sinai (Deuteronomy 4:10; 5:2).
[^24]: Elijah had been taken up to heaven without dying (2 Kings 2:11). The Gospels see this promise fulfilled in John the Baptist (Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13; Mark 9:11–13; Luke 1:17). *The great and terrible day of the LORD* is also in Joel 3:4 (2:31 in most English Bibles).
[^25]: Luke 1:17 quotes the first half of this line.
[^26]: Hebrew *cherem*, the ban by which something was devoted to destruction (Zechariah 14:11). These verses close the Prophets, the second division of the Hebrew Bible, and in most Christian Bibles they close the Old Testament. In the synagogue reading, verse 23 is read again after verse 24, so that the book does not end with a curse.
