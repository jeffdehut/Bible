**1** An oracle.[^1] The word of Yahweh[^2] to Israel through Malachi.[^3]

**2** I have loved you, says Yahweh. But you say, "How have you loved us?" Was not Esau Jacob's brother? declares Yahweh.[^4] Yet I loved Jacob,

**3** but Esau I hated.[^5] I have made his mountains a desolation and left his inheritance to the jackals of the desert.

**4** If Edom says, "We have been crushed, but we will rebuild the ruins," this is what Yahweh of Armies[^6] says: They may build, but I will tear down. They will be called the Wicked Country, the people with whom Yahweh is angry forever.

**5** Your own eyes will see it, and you will say, "Great is Yahweh beyond the borders of Israel!"

**6** A son honors his father, and a servant his master. If I am a father, where is the honor due to me? If I am a master, where is the respect due to me? says Yahweh of Armies to you, priests, who despise my name. But you say, "How have we despised your name?"

**7** By offering defiled food[^7] on my altar. But you say, "How have we defiled you?" By saying that the table of Yahweh[^8] may be despised.

**8** When you offer blind animals as sacrifices, is that not wrong? When you offer the lame and the sick, is that not wrong?[^9] Try offering them to your governor![^10] Would he be pleased with you or show you favor?[^11] says Yahweh of Armies.

**9** So now, plead with God[^12] to be gracious to us! With such offerings from your hands, will he show favor to any of you? says Yahweh of Armies.

**10** If only one of you would shut the temple doors, so that you would not light useless fires on my altar! I take no pleasure in you, says Yahweh of Armies, and I will not accept an offering[^13] from your hands.

**11** For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations. In every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering. For my name is great among the nations, says Yahweh of Armies.[^14]

**12** But you profane it when you say that the Lord's[^15] table is defiled, and its fruit, its food, may be despised.[^16]

**13** And you say, "What a weariness this is!" and you sniff at it,[^17] says Yahweh of Armies. You bring what has been stolen,[^18] and the lame and the sick, and you bring these as an offering! Should I accept it from your hands? says Yahweh.

**14** Cursed be the cheat who has a male in his flock and vows to give it, but then sacrifices to the Lord one that is blemished! For I am a great King, says Yahweh of Armies, and my name is feared among the nations.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden". Compare Zechariah 9:1; 12:1.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: *Malachi* means "my messenger" (3:1), and may be a title rather than a name. The Septuagint reads "by the hand of his messenger". The book seems to come from the fifth century BC, after the temple had been rebuilt in 515 BC (Ezra 6:15), when a Persian governor ruled Judah (verse 8). It is set out as a series of disputes, in which Yahweh quotes the people's objections and answers them.
[^4]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement.
[^5]: The language of choosing: Yahweh chose Jacob and passed over Esau (compare Genesis 25:23; 29:30–31; Deuteronomy 21:15). Romans 9:13 quotes verses 2–3.
[^6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. The title is used more than twenty times in Malachi.
[^7]: Literally "defiled bread", the sacrifices offered on the altar as Yahweh's food (Leviticus 21:6).
[^8]: The altar. Also in verse 12.
[^9]: Literally "there is no evil", perhaps quoting the priests: "There is nothing wrong with that!" Also later in the verse. The law forbade the offering of animals with defects (Leviticus 22:18–25; Deuteronomy 15:21).
[^10]: Hebrew *pechah*, the governor of the Persian province of Judah (Haggai 1:1).
[^11]: Literally "lift up your face".
[^12]: Literally "soften the face of God", spoken with irony.
[^13]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, tribute", in Leviticus a grain offering. Also in verses 11 and 13.
[^14]: The Hebrew has no verb in the main clauses, so the verse may also be translated in the future: "my name will be great among the nations".
[^15]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name Yahweh. Also in verse 14.
[^16]: Literally "and its fruit is despised, its food". The meaning of the word for fruit, *niv*, is uncertain.
[^17]: This is one of the *tiqqune sopherim*, the rabbinic "corrections of the scribes": the tradition says that the text once read "you sniff at me" and was changed out of reverence for God.
[^18]: Or "what was torn by wild animals". Hebrew *gazul*, "seized by force".
