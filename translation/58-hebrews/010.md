**1** The law has only a shadow of the good things to come, not the very form of those realities. So it can never,[^1] by the same sacrifices offered continually year after year, make perfect those who draw near.

**2** Otherwise, would they not have stopped being offered? For the worshipers, once cleansed, would no longer have any consciousness of sins.

**3** But in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sins year after year.

**4** For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.

**5** That is why, on coming into the world, he says,[^2] "Sacrifice and offering you did not desire, but a body you prepared for me.[^3]

**6** In burnt offerings and sin offerings[^4] you took no pleasure.[^5]

**7** Then I said, 'Here I am, I have come (in the scroll of the book it is written about me) to do your will, God.'"[^6]

**8** First he says, "Sacrifices and offerings, burnt offerings and sin offerings you did not desire, nor did you take pleasure in them" (these are offered according to the law).

**9** Then he says, "Here I am, I have come to do your will."[^7] He takes away the first in order to establish the second.

**10** And it is by that will that we have been made holy, through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

**11** Every priest stands day after day performing his service and offering again and again the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins.

**12** But this priest offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins and sat down at the right hand of God.[^8]

**13** Since then he has been waiting until his enemies are made a footstool for his feet.[^9]

**14** For by a single offering he has made perfect for all time those who are being made holy.

**15** The Holy Spirit also testifies to us about this. For after saying,

**16** "This is the covenant I will make with them after those days," the Lord says,[^10] "I will put my laws on their hearts, and I will write them on their minds,

**17** and I will remember their sins and their lawless deeds no more."[^11]

**18** Where these have been forgiven, there is no longer any offering for sin.

**19** So, brothers and sisters,[^12] since we have confidence to enter the sanctuary[^13] by the blood of Jesus,

**20** by the new and living way that he opened[^14] for us through the veil, that is, through his flesh,[^15]

**21** and since we have a great priest over the house of God,

**22** let us draw near with a true heart, in the full assurance of faith, with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.[^16]

**23** Let us hold firmly to the hope we confess, without wavering, for the one who promised is faithful.

**24** And let us consider how to stir one another up to love and good works,

**25** not giving up meeting together, as some are in the habit of doing, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day[^17] drawing near.

**26** For if we go on sinning deliberately after we have received the knowledge of the truth, no sacrifice for sins is left,[^18]

**27** but only a terrifying expectation of judgment and a fury of fire that will consume the adversaries.[^19]

**28** Anyone who rejected the law of Moses died without mercy on the testimony of two or three witnesses.[^20]

**29** How much worse punishment do you think will be deserved by the one who has trampled on the Son of God, who has treated as unholy[^21] the blood of the covenant by which he was made holy, and who has insulted the Spirit of grace?

**30** For we know the one who said, "Vengeance is mine; I will repay,"[^22] and again, "The Lord will judge his people."[^23]

**31** It is a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

**32** But remember those earlier days when, after you had been enlightened, you endured a hard struggle[^24] with sufferings.

**33** Sometimes you were publicly exposed to insults and afflictions; at other times you stood alongside those who were treated that way.

**34** For you sympathized with those in prison,[^25] and you accepted with joy the seizure of your property, because you knew that you yourselves had a better possession, one that lasts.[^26]

**35** So do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward.

**36** For you need endurance, so that when you have done the will of God, you may receive what was promised.

**37** For "in a very little while,[^27] the one who is coming will come and will not delay.[^28]

**38** But my righteous one will live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul takes no pleasure in him."[^29]

**39** But we are not among those who shrink back and are destroyed, but among those who have faith and so preserve their lives.[^30]

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "they can never", referring to the priests.
[^2]: That is, Christ. Literally "coming into the world, he says". Psalm 40:7–9 (40:6–8 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 5–7 from the Septuagint.
[^3]: The Hebrew of the psalm reads "but you have opened my ears" (literally "ears you have dug for me").
[^4]: Greek *peri hamartias*, "for sin", the Septuagint's phrase for the sin offering (Hebrew *chatta't*, also translated "purification offering"); also in verses 8 and 18.
[^5]: The Hebrew reads "you did not ask for".
[^6]: The Hebrew reads "in the scroll of the book it is written about me. I delight to do your will, my God; your law is within my heart."
[^7]: Many manuscripts add "God".
[^8]: Or "offered a single sacrifice for sins and sat down forever at the right hand of God".
[^9]: Psalm 110:1 (see 1:13).
[^10]: Or, as part of the quotation, "declares the Lord". Verses 16–17 quote Jeremiah 31:33–34 (see 8:10–12) freely: Jeremiah has "with the house of Israel". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^11]: Some editions add "then he says" at the start of verse 17.
[^12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike.
[^13]: Greek *ton hagion*, "the holy (places)" (see 9:8, 12).
[^14]: Greek *enkainizo*, "inaugurate, dedicate", the verb used of the first covenant in 9:18.
[^15]: The phrase may explain "the veil" or "the way".
[^16]: Compare Ezekiel 36:25, "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you will be clean."
[^17]: That is, the day of the Lord's coming and of judgment.
[^18]: The Greek participle is present, "sinning", which may describe a continuing course. *Deliberately* recalls the law's distinction between sins done unintentionally, for which a sin offering could be made, and sins done "with a high hand", for which it could not (Numbers 15:27–31). As with 6:4–6, interpreters differ on whether verses 26–31 describe believers who fall away, people who reject the truth they have known without ever truly belonging, or a warning of what such rejection would mean.
[^19]: Literally "a zeal of fire about to eat the adversaries". Compare Isaiah 26:11, "let the fire meant for your enemies consume them".
[^20]: Deuteronomy 17:6. *Rejected*: Greek *atheteo*, "set aside, treat as invalid".
[^21]: Literally "common", that is, ordinary or unclean, the opposite of holy.
[^22]: Deuteronomy 32:35, in the form also quoted in Romans 12:19. The Hebrew reads "Vengeance is mine, and repayment". Many manuscripts add "says the Lord".
[^23]: Deuteronomy 32:36 (also Psalm 135:14). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). *Will judge*: or "will vindicate", as this translation renders the Hebrew there.
[^24]: Greek *athlesis*, a contest, as in athletic games.
[^25]: Many manuscripts read "you sympathized with me in my chains".
[^26]: Many manuscripts add "in heaven".
[^27]: Greek *mikron hoson hoson*, words from Isaiah 26:20 in the Septuagint, where the Hebrew reads "for a little while". Verses 37–38 then quote Habakkuk 2:3–4 from the Septuagint.
[^28]: In the Hebrew of Habakkuk it is the vision that "will surely come, it will not delay". Hebrews has "the one who is coming" (Greek *ho erchomenos*), a person.
[^29]: Habakkuk 2:4, with its two lines in the reverse order. The Hebrew reads "Look, his soul is puffed up; it is not upright within him. But the righteous will live by his faithfulness." The Septuagint reads "If he shrinks back, my soul takes no pleasure in him; but the righteous will live by my faithfulness", that is, by God's. *Faith*: Greek *pistis*, which means both "faith" and "faithfulness" (also in verse 39). Many manuscripts read "the righteous one" for "my righteous one".
[^30]: Literally "we are not of shrinking back to destruction, but of faith to the preserving of the soul".
