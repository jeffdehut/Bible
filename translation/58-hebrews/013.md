**1** Let your love for one another as brothers and sisters continue.[^1]

**2** Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing so some have entertained angels without knowing it.[^2]

**3** Remember those in prison as though you were in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you too are in the body.

**4** Let marriage be held in honor by all,[^3] and the marriage bed kept undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.

**5** Keep your lives free from the love of money, and be content with what you have, for he himself has said, "I will never fail you or abandon you."[^4]

**6** So we can say with confidence, "The Lord is my helper; I will not be afraid. What can anyone do to me?"[^5]

**7** Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith.

**8** Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.

**9** Do not be carried away by all kinds of strange teachings. For it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods,[^6] which have not benefited those who live by them.

**10** We have an altar from which those who serve in the tabernacle have no right to eat.

**11** For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the sanctuary[^7] by the high priest as a sin offering[^8] are burned outside the camp.[^9]

**12** So Jesus also suffered outside the city gate,[^10] in order to make the people holy through his own blood.

**13** Let us, then, go out to him outside the camp, bearing the disgrace he bore.

**14** For here we have no lasting city, but we are looking for the city that is to come.

**15** Through him, then, let us continually offer up to God a sacrifice of praise, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name.[^11]

**16** Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for God is pleased with such sacrifices.

**17** Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they keep watch over your souls[^12] as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no benefit to you.

**18** Pray for us. We are sure that we have a clear conscience, and we want to live honorably in every way.

**19** I urge you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you sooner.

**20** Now may the God of peace, who brought back from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep[^13] by the blood of the eternal covenant,[^14] our Lord Jesus,

**21** equip you with everything good[^15] to do his will, working in us[^16] what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever.[^17] Amen.

**22** I urge you, brothers and sisters,[^18] bear with my message of encouragement,[^19] for I have written to you briefly.

**23** You should know[^20] that our brother Timothy has been released. If he comes soon, I will see you with him.

**24** Greet all your leaders and all God's holy people.[^21] Those from Italy send you greetings.[^22]

**25** Grace be with you all.[^23]

[^1]: Literally "Let brotherly love remain". Greek *philadelphia*, "love for brothers (and sisters)".
[^2]: Compare Genesis 18:1–8; 19:1–3.
[^3]: Or "in every way". The Greek has no verb: "Honorable (is) marriage among all".
[^4]: Deuteronomy 31:6, 8; Joshua 1:5, where God speaks in the first person; compare Genesis 28:15. The Greek is emphatic: "I will never, never …".
[^5]: Psalm 118:6, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "Yahweh is for me; I will not be afraid." In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^6]: Probably rules about food, or sacrificial meals.
[^7]: Greek *ta hagia*, here the most holy place (see 9:12).
[^8]: Greek *peri hamartias*, "for sin" (see 10:6).
[^9]: Leviticus 16:27.
[^10]: Literally "outside the gate", the gate of Jerusalem.
[^11]: Compare Hosea 14:3 (14:2 in most English Bibles), where the Septuagint reads "the fruit of our lips" and the Hebrew "we will offer our lips as bulls". *Confess*: or "praise".
[^12]: Or "your lives".
[^13]: Compare Isaiah 63:11, "he who brought them up out of the sea, with the shepherds of his flock".
[^14]: Compare Zechariah 9:11, "the blood of my covenant". *By the blood*: or "with the blood", perhaps going with "the great shepherd".
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "in every good work".
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "in you".
[^17]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions read "forever and ever"; the SBLGNT has the shorter reading, following a minority.
[^18]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike.
[^19]: Literally "the word of exhortation", the phrase used of a sermon in the synagogue in Acts 13:15.
[^20]: Or "You know".
[^21]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^22]: Either believers from Italy who were with the writer, sending greetings home, or believers in Italy.
[^23]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
