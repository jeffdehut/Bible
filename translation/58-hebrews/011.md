**1** Faith is the assurance[^1] of what we hope for, the proof[^2] of what we cannot see.

**2** It was for this that the people of old[^3] received God's approval.

**3** By faith we understand that the universe[^4] was put in order by the word of God, so that what is seen did not come from things that are visible.[^5]

**4** By faith Abel offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did. Through it he was approved as righteous, for God approved his gifts. And through it,[^6] though he died, he still speaks.[^7]

**5** By faith Enoch was taken away so that he did not see death. "He was not found, because God had taken him away."[^8] For before he was taken away, he was approved as one who had pleased God.

**6** And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever comes to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.

**7** By faith Noah, when he was warned about things not yet seen, in reverent fear built an ark to save his household.[^9] By this he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith.

**8** By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place he was going to receive as an inheritance. He went out without knowing where he was going.[^10]

**9** By faith he lived as a foreigner[^11] in the land of the promise, as in a land not his own, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, who were heirs with him of the same promise.

**10** For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose architect and builder is God.

**11** By faith, together with Sarah herself, he received power to father a child,[^12] even though he was past the age for it, because he considered the one who had promised to be faithful.

**12** So from one man, and him as good as dead, there came descendants as many as the stars of the sky and as countless as the sand on the seashore.[^13]

**13** All these died in faith without having received the things that were promised. But they saw them and greeted them from a distance, and they acknowledged that they were strangers and foreigners on the earth.[^14]

**14** People who say such things make it clear that they are looking for a homeland.

**15** If they had been thinking of the land they had left, they would have had the opportunity to return.

**16** But as it is, they long for a better country, that is, a heavenly one. That is why God is not ashamed to be called their God,[^15] for he has prepared a city for them.

**17** By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac.[^16] He who had received the promises was ready to offer up his only son,[^17]

**18** the one of whom it had been said, "It is through Isaac that your offspring will be named."[^18]

**19** He reasoned that God was able even to raise people from the dead, and in a manner of speaking[^19] he did receive Isaac back from the dead.

**20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau with regard to things to come.[^20]

**21** By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and he worshiped, leaning on the top of his staff.[^21]

**22** By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, spoke of the exodus of the Israelites and gave instructions about his bones.[^22]

**23** By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents,[^23] because they saw that the child was beautiful,[^24] and they were not afraid of the king's edict.[^25]

**24** By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,[^26]

**25** choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin.

**26** He considered the reproach of the Messiah[^27] greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking ahead to the reward.

**27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the king's anger,[^28] for he persevered as one who sees him who is invisible.

**28** By faith he kept the Passover and the splashing of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them.[^29]

**29** By faith the people crossed the Red Sea[^30] as if on dry land, but when the Egyptians tried to do so, they were drowned.[^31]

**30** By faith the walls of Jericho fell after they had been circled for seven days.[^32]

**31** By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had welcomed the spies in peace.[^33]

**32** And what more shall I say? Time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson and Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets,

**33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, did what was right,[^34] obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,[^35]

**34** quenched the power of fire,[^36] escaped the edge of the sword,[^37] gained strength out of weakness, became mighty in war, and put foreign armies to flight.

**35** Women received back their dead by resurrection.[^38] Others were tortured,[^39] refusing to accept release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.

**36** Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and imprisonment.[^40]

**37** They were stoned,[^41] they were sawn in two,[^42] they were killed by the sword. They went about in sheepskins[^43] and goatskins, destitute, oppressed, mistreated.

**38** The world was not worthy of them. They wandered in deserts and mountains, in caves and holes in the ground.[^44]

**39** And all of them,[^45] though they were approved for their faith, did not receive what was promised,

**40** since God had foreseen something better for us, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us.

[^1]: Greek *hypostasis*, "what stands under": the reality or foundation of what is hoped for, or confidence in it (compare 1:3; 3:14).
[^2]: Greek *elenchos*, "evidence, proof, conviction".
[^3]: Literally "the elders". *Received God's approval*: literally "were testified to", the verb used again in verses 4, 5 and 39.
[^4]: Literally "the ages", as in 1:2.
[^5]: Or "so that what is seen came into being from what cannot be seen".
[^6]: That is, through his faith, or through his sacrifice. Genesis 4:3–10 tells of the offerings of Cain and Abel; in 4:10 Abel's blood cries out from the ground (compare 12:24).
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "he is still spoken of".
[^8]: Genesis 5:24, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "Enoch walked with God, and then he was no more, because God took him." The Septuagint has "Enoch pleased God", the words echoed later in this verse.
[^9]: Genesis 6:13–22. *When he was warned*: Greek *chrematistheis*, used of a warning from God.
[^10]: Genesis 12:1–5.
[^11]: Greek *paroikeo*, "live as a resident alien".
[^12]: The SBLGNT reads Greek *aute Sarra* as a dative, "together with Sarah herself", which makes Abraham the subject; it here follows a minority reading. Early Greek manuscripts do not mark the difference, so the letters can be read either way. Most editions read "By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive", and NA28 adds "though she was barren"; many manuscripts also add "gave birth" after "the age for it". *To father a child*: literally "for the laying down of seed", an expression used of the father's part.
[^13]: Genesis 22:17 (compare 15:5; 32:13).
[^14]: Compare Genesis 23:4, where Abraham calls himself "a foreigner and a resident alien", and 47:9.
[^15]: Compare Exodus 3:6, 15, "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob".
[^16]: Genesis 22:1–14.
[^17]: Greek *monogenes*, "only, unique" (compare John 1:14). Isaac was the son of the promise, born to Abraham and Sarah (Genesis 21:1–3; 22:2).
[^18]: Genesis 21:12. Literally "in Isaac seed will be called for you".
[^19]: Literally "in a parable", that is, figuratively.
[^20]: Genesis 27:27–40.
[^21]: Genesis 48:1–20, and 47:31 as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew of 47:31 reads "Israel bowed down at the head of his bed": the consonants of *mittah*, "bed", can also be read *matteh*, "staff".
[^22]: Genesis 50:24–25.
[^23]: Literally "fathers".
[^24]: Greek *asteios*, "well formed, fine", the Septuagint's word in Exodus 2:2, where the Hebrew reads "a fine child".
[^25]: Exodus 1:22; 2:2.
[^26]: Exodus 2:10–12.
[^27]: Or "the reproach of the anointed one". Greek *tou christou*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One". Compare Psalm 89:51–52 (89:50–51 in most English Bibles), on the taunts suffered by Yahweh's anointed.
[^28]: Either Moses' flight to Midian (Exodus 2:15) or the exodus (Exodus 12:31–41) may be meant.
[^29]: Exodus 12:21–30. *The destroyer*: Exodus 12:23.
[^30]: Greek *Erythra Thalassa*, the Septuagint's name for the Hebrew *Yam Suph*, "Sea of Reeds" (Exodus 15:4).
[^31]: Literally "they crossed". Exodus 14:21–31.
[^32]: Joshua 6:1–21.
[^33]: Joshua 2:1–21; 6:22–25.
[^34]: Literally "worked righteousness", perhaps "administered justice".
[^35]: Compare Daniel 6:23 (6:22 in most English Bibles).
[^36]: Compare Daniel 3.
[^37]: Literally "the mouths of the sword", a Hebrew way of speaking.
[^38]: Compare 1 Kings 17:17–24; 2 Kings 4:18–37.
[^39]: Greek *tympanizo*, from *tympanon*, "drum": perhaps beaten to death, or stretched on a frame for torture. Compare the martyrs of 2 Maccabees 6–7, who refused to give up God's law and looked for resurrection.
[^40]: Compare Jeremiah 20:2; 37:15.
[^41]: Compare 2 Chronicles 24:20–22.
[^42]: A Jewish tradition says that the prophet Isaiah was killed by being sawn in two. Many manuscripts add "they were tested".
[^43]: Greek *melote*, "sheepskin", the Septuagint's word for Elijah's cloak (1 Kings 19:13, 19).
[^44]: Compare 1 Kings 18:4, 13.
[^45]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions read "And all these"; the SBLGNT leaves out "these", following a minority reading.
