**1** Therefore, since we too are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses,[^1] let us also lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily entangles us,[^2] and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us,

**2** fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer[^3] and perfecter of faith. For the joy that was set before him[^4] he endured the cross, disregarding its shame, and he has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.

**3** Consider him who endured such hostility from sinners against themselves,[^5] so that you do not grow weary and lose heart.

**4** In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood.

**5** And you have forgotten the encouragement that speaks to you as sons:[^6] "My son, do not make light of the Lord's discipline, and do not lose heart when you are corrected by him,[^7]

**6** for the Lord disciplines the one he loves, and he punishes every son he accepts."[^8]

**7** Endure it as discipline.[^9] God is treating you as sons. For what son is there whom his father does not discipline?

**8** If you are left without discipline, which all children share, then you are illegitimate, and not sons.

**9** Besides, we had human fathers[^10] who disciplined us, and we respected them. Should we not much more submit to the Father of spirits[^11] and live?

**10** For they disciplined us for a short time, as seemed best to them, but he disciplines us for our good, so that we may share in his holiness.

**11** No discipline seems joyful at the time; it seems painful. But later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.

**12** So strengthen your drooping hands and your weak knees,[^12]

**13** and make straight paths for your feet,[^13] so that what is lame may not be put out of joint,[^14] but rather be healed.

**14** Pursue peace with everyone, and holiness, without which no one will see the Lord.

**15** See to it that no one falls short of the grace of God, and that no bitter root springs up and causes trouble, and through it many become defiled.[^15]

**16** See that no one is sexually immoral, or godless like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal.[^16]

**17** For you know that afterward, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected. He found no opportunity for repentance, though he sought it with tears.[^17]

**18** For you have not come to something that can be touched,[^18] to a blazing fire, darkness, gloom and a whirlwind,[^19]

**19** to the sound of a trumpet and to a voice speaking words, so that those who heard it begged that no further word be spoken to them.[^20]

**20** For they could not bear the command, "If even an animal touches the mountain, it must be stoned."[^21]

**21** Indeed, so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, "I am terrified and trembling."[^22]

**22** But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to tens of thousands of angels in festal gathering,[^23]

**23** and to the church[^24] of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God the judge of all,[^25] and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect,

**24** and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant,[^26] and to the sprinkled blood that speaks better than the blood of Abel.[^27]

**25** See that you do not refuse the one who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused the one who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we turn away from the one who warns from heaven.

**26** At that time his voice shook the earth, but now he has promised, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heaven."[^28]

**27** The words "Yet once more" show that the things that can be shaken, as created things, will be removed, so that the things that cannot be shaken may remain.

**28** So, since we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, let us be grateful,[^29] and so worship God acceptably, with reverence and awe,

**29** for our God is a consuming fire.[^30]

[^1]: Greek *martyres*: those whose faith God approved and who bore witness to it (chapter 11). The picture may also be of spectators around a stadium.
[^2]: Greek *euperistatos*, a word found only here: perhaps "that clings so closely", or "that so easily distracts".
[^3]: Greek *archegos*, as in 2:10.
[^4]: Or "Instead of the joy that was set before him".
[^5]: The SBLGNT here follows Westcott and Hort; other editions read "against himself".
[^6]: Or "And have you forgotten …?" *Sons*: Greek *huioi*. In verses 5–8 the readers, men and women alike, are addressed as sons in a household.
[^7]: Proverbs 3:11–12, quoted in verses 5–6 from the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "do not reject the discipline of Yahweh, and do not resent his correction". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh, also in verse 6.
[^8]: The Hebrew reads "as a father corrects the son he delights in". *Punishes*: Greek *mastigoo*, "whip, flog".
[^9]: Or "It is for discipline that you endure."
[^10]: Literally "the fathers of our flesh".
[^11]: Compare Numbers 16:22; 27:16, "the God of the spirits of all flesh".
[^12]: Isaiah 35:3. Literally "lift up the drooping hands and the paralyzed knees".
[^13]: Proverbs 4:26, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "Make level the path for your feet".
[^14]: Or "turned aside".
[^15]: Deuteronomy 29:17 (29:18 in most English Bibles), in a form close to the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "no root among you that bears poisonous and bitter fruit".
[^16]: Genesis 25:29–34.
[^17]: Genesis 27:30–40. *An opportunity for repentance*: literally "a place for repentance", either for Esau's own repentance or for a change of mind in his father. *It* may be the repentance or the blessing.
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "to a mountain that can be touched".
[^19]: Exodus 19:16–19; Deuteronomy 4:11; 5:22–23.
[^20]: Exodus 20:18–19; Deuteronomy 5:23–27.
[^21]: Exodus 19:12–13.
[^22]: The words "I am terrified" are from Deuteronomy 9:19, where Moses speaks of his fear of Yahweh's anger after the golden calf.
[^23]: Or "and to tens of thousands of angels, to the festal gathering and church of the firstborn". Greek *panegyris*, a festive assembly.
[^24]: Greek *ekklesia*, "assembly".
[^25]: Or "to a judge who is God of all".
[^26]: Greek *nea*, "new", a different word from *kaine*, "new", in 8:8, 13; 9:15.
[^27]: Literally "than Abel". Compare 11:4; Genesis 4:10.
[^28]: Haggai 2:6, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "Once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth, the sea and the dry land."
[^29]: Literally "let us have grace", which can also mean "let us hold on to grace".
[^30]: Deuteronomy 4:24; 9:3.
