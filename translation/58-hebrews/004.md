**1** So, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us take care[^1] that none of you is found to have fallen short of it.

**2** For we too have had the good news announced to us, just as they did. But the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not joined in faith with those who listened.[^2]

**3** For we who have believed do enter that rest,[^3] just as he has said, "So I swore in my anger, 'They shall never enter my rest'",[^4] even though his works had been finished since the foundation of the world.

**4** For somewhere he has spoken about the seventh day like this: "And on the seventh day God rested from all his works."[^5]

**5** And again in this passage: "They shall never enter my rest."

**6** Since, then, it still remains for some to enter it, and those who first had the good news announced to them did not enter because of their disobedience,[^6]

**7** God again sets a certain day, "Today", saying through David[^7] so long afterward, as has been said already, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts."

**8** For if Joshua[^8] had given them rest, God would not have spoken later about another day.

**9** So then, a Sabbath rest[^9] remains for the people of God.

**10** For whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from their own works, just as God did from his.[^10]

**11** So let us make every effort to enter that rest, so that no one falls by following the same example of disobedience.

**12** For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any double-edged sword.[^11] It penetrates even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of joints and marrow, and it judges the thoughts and intentions of the heart.

**13** No creature is hidden from his sight. Everything is naked and laid bare[^12] before the eyes of the one to whom we must give account.[^13]

**14** Since, then, we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold firmly to the faith we confess.[^14]

**15** For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who has been tested[^15] in every way as we are, yet without sin.

**16** So let us approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us at the time we need it.

[^1]: Literally "let us fear".
[^2]: Literally "not being mixed by faith with those who heard", perhaps those who listened with faith, such as Joshua and Caleb (Numbers 14:6–9). Some editions read "because it was not mixed with faith in those who heard", that is, the message was not met with faith.
[^3]: Literally "we enter into rest". Greek *katapausis*, the word for "rest" in the psalm.
[^4]: Psalm 95:11 (see 3:11).
[^5]: Genesis 2:2. *Rested*: Greek *katepausen*, the verb related to *katapausis*, "rest".
[^6]: Or "unbelief".
[^7]: Literally "in David", that is, in the Psalms. Psalm 95 has no title in the Hebrew; the Septuagint gives it one naming David.
[^8]: Greek *Iesous*, the Greek form of the name Joshua, and also of Jesus. Joshua led Israel into the promised land (Joshua 1:1–6; 21:44).
[^9]: Greek *sabbatismos*, "Sabbath-keeping", a different word from *katapausis*, "rest", in verses 1–8.
[^10]: Literally "For the one who has entered his rest has also himself rested from his works, as God did from his own."
[^11]: Literally "two-mouthed sword".
[^12]: Greek *tetrachelismena*, literally "with the neck bent back", exposed like a sacrificial animal or a defeated wrestler.
[^13]: Literally "to whom our word (is)". Greek *logos* plays on "the word of God" in verse 12; the phrase can also mean "with whom we have to do".
[^14]: Literally "the confession", as in 3:1.
[^15]: Or "tempted", as in 2:18.
