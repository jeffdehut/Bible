**1** Now the first covenant[^1] had regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary.

**2** For a tent was set up, the first one,[^2] in which were the lampstand, the table and the bread of the Presence.[^3] This is called the holy place.

**3** Behind the second veil[^4] was a tent called the most holy place.[^5]

**4** It had the gold altar of incense[^6] and the ark of the covenant, covered with gold on all sides. In the ark were a gold jar holding the manna, Aaron's staff that had budded, and the tablets of the covenant.[^7]

**5** Above it were the cherubim[^8] of glory, overshadowing the atonement cover.[^9] But we cannot speak about these things in detail now.

**6** With these things arranged like this, the priests go continually into the first tent to perform their duties of worship,

**7** but only the high priest goes into the second, once a year, and never without blood, which he offers for himself and for the sins the people have committed in ignorance.[^10]

**8** By this the Holy Spirit shows that the way into the sanctuary[^11] has not yet been disclosed while the first tent is still standing.

**9** This is a symbol[^12] for the present time. According to it, gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot make the worshiper perfect in conscience.

**10** They deal only with food and drink and various washings, regulations for the body[^13] imposed until the time when things are set right.

**11** But when Christ came as high priest of the good things that have now come,[^14] he went through[^15] the greater and more perfect tent, not made with hands, that is, not of this creation.

**12** He entered the sanctuary[^16] once for all, not with the blood of goats and calves[^17] but with his own blood, and obtained eternal redemption.

**13** For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on those who are ceremonially unclean, make them holy so that their bodies are clean,[^18]

**14** how much more will the blood of Christ,[^19] who through the eternal Spirit[^20] offered himself without defect[^21] to God, cleanse our consciences[^22] from dead works so that we may serve the living God!

**15** That is why he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, since a death has taken place that redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant.

**16** For where there is a will,[^23] the death of the one who made it must be established.

**17** For a will takes effect only at death; it is never in force while the one who made it is alive.

**18** That is why not even the first covenant was put into effect without blood.

**19** When Moses had told all the people every commandment of the law, he took the blood of the calves,[^24] with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,

**20** saying, "This is the blood of the covenant that God has commanded for you."[^25]

**21** In the same way he sprinkled with the blood the tabernacle and all the vessels used in worship.

**22** Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood[^26] there is no forgiveness.

**23** So it was necessary for the copies of the heavenly things to be purified by these rites, but the heavenly things themselves need better sacrifices than these.

**24** For Christ did not enter a sanctuary made with hands, a copy of the true one, but heaven itself, now to appear in God's presence on our behalf.

**25** Nor did he enter to offer himself again and again, as the high priest enters the sanctuary year after year[^27] with blood that is not his own.

**26** Otherwise he would have had to suffer again and again since the foundation of the world. But as it is, he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to do away with sin by the sacrifice of himself.

**27** And just as people are destined to die once, and after that comes judgment,

**28** so Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many,[^28] will appear a second time, not to deal with sin,[^29] but to bring salvation to those who are eagerly waiting for him.

[^1]: Literally "the first".
[^2]: The outer room of the tabernacle, which Exodus 26:33 calls "the holy place". Greek *skene*, "tent", the Septuagint's word for the tabernacle and its rooms.
[^3]: Literally "the setting out of the loaves": bread set out before God (Exodus 25:23–30; Leviticus 24:5–9).
[^4]: Greek *katapetasma*, the hanging that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33). The first hanging was the screen at the entrance of the tent (Exodus 26:36).
[^5]: Literally "the holy of holies", as in Exodus 26:33.
[^6]: Greek *thymiaterion*, which can mean an incense altar or a censer (a firepan for burning incense). In Exodus 30:1–6 the gold altar of incense stands in front of the veil; on the Day of Atonement the high priest took burning coals and incense inside the veil (Leviticus 16:12–13).
[^7]: See Exodus 16:33–34 (the manna); Numbers 17:16–26 (17:1–11 in most English Bibles; Aaron's staff); Exodus 25:16 and Deuteronomy 10:1–5 (the tablets). 1 Kings 8:9 says that in Solomon's temple there was nothing in the ark except the tablets.
[^8]: Winged heavenly beings, whose figures of gold stood at the two ends of the atonement cover (Exodus 25:18–20).
[^9]: Greek *hilasterion*, the Septuagint's word for the *kapporet*, the gold lid of the ark, traditionally "mercy seat" (Exodus 25:17–22). On it the high priest sprinkled blood on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:14–15).
[^10]: Greek *agnoemata*, "sins of ignorance", wrongs done unknowingly. See Leviticus 16.
[^11]: Greek *ton hagion*, "of the holy (places)": here the true sanctuary, God's presence. *The first tent*: the outer room (verses 2 and 6), or the earthly tabernacle as a whole.
[^12]: Greek *parabole*, "comparison, illustration".
[^13]: Literally "regulations of the flesh".
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "of the good things to come".
[^15]: Or "by means of".
[^16]: Greek *ta hagia*, "the holy (places)", here the most holy place, which Leviticus 16 calls "the holy place".
[^17]: Greek *moschoi*, "calves, young bulls", the Septuagint's word for the young bull of the sin offering (Leviticus 16:3, 11); also in verse 19.
[^18]: Literally "sanctify for the purity of the flesh". On the ashes of the red heifer, mixed with water for removing impurity, see Numbers 19.
[^19]: Greek *tou christou*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One", referring back to verse 11; also in verse 28.
[^20]: Or "through an eternal spirit".
[^21]: Greek *amomos*, the Septuagint's word for a sacrificial animal "without defect" (Leviticus 1:3).
[^22]: Many manuscripts read "your consciences".
[^23]: Greek *diatheke*, which means both "covenant" and "will, testament". In verses 16–17 Hebrews uses the second sense. Some take the verses to keep the sense "covenant", thinking of the death of the animals by which a covenant was confirmed (Genesis 15:9–18; Jeremiah 34:18).
[^24]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions add "and goats". Exodus 24:3–8 tells of the sprinkling of the blood; it mentions bulls (24:5) but not water, scarlet wool or hyssop, which belong to other rites of cleansing (Leviticus 14:4–7; Numbers 19:6, 18).
[^25]: Exodus 24:8. The Hebrew reads "the blood of the covenant that Yahweh has made with you".
[^26]: Greek *haimatekchysia*, "pouring out of blood". Compare Leviticus 17:11.
[^27]: On the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:34).
[^28]: Compare Isaiah 53:12, "he bore the sin of many".
[^29]: Literally "without sin".
