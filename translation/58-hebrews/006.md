**1** So let us leave behind the elementary teaching about the Messiah[^1] and go on to maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works[^2] and of faith in God,

**2** instruction about washings[^3] and the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment.

**3** And this we will do, if God permits.[^4]

**4** For it is impossible, in the case of those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift and have come to share in the Holy Spirit,

**5** who have tasted the goodness of God's word and the powers of the age to come,

**6** and who have then fallen away,[^5] to renew them again to repentance, since they are crucifying the Son of God all over again for themselves[^6] and holding him up to public contempt.[^7]

**7** For land that drinks in the rain that often falls on it, and produces a crop useful to those for whom it is farmed, receives a blessing from God.

**8** But if it produces thorns and thistles,[^8] it is worthless and close to being cursed, and in the end it will be burned.

**9** Even though we speak like this, dear friends,[^9] we are convinced of better things in your case, things that belong to salvation.

**10** For God is not unjust. He will not forget your work and the love you have shown for his name, in serving God's holy people[^10] and in still serving them.[^11]

**11** We want each of you to show the same eagerness to the end, so that your hope may be fully assured.[^12]

**12** Then you will not become sluggish,[^13] but will imitate those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.

**13** When God made his promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater to swear by, he swore by himself,

**14** saying, "I will richly bless you, and I will greatly multiply you."[^14]

**15** And so, after waiting patiently, Abraham obtained what was promised.

**16** People swear by someone greater than themselves, and for them an oath gives confirmation and puts an end to every dispute.

**17** So when God wanted to show even more clearly to the heirs of the promise that his purpose could not change, he guaranteed it with an oath,[^15]

**18** so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold on to the hope set before us.

**19** We have this hope as an anchor for the soul, sure and firm, and it enters inside the veil,[^16]

**20** where Jesus has entered as a forerunner on our behalf, having become a high priest forever in the order of Melchizedek.[^17]

[^1]: Literally "the word of the beginning of the Messiah". *The Messiah*: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^2]: Probably acts that lead to death, or acts with no life in them (compare 9:14).
[^3]: Greek *baptismoi*, "washings", plural: ritual washings (as in 9:10), perhaps including baptism.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "And let us do this, if God permits."
[^5]: Literally "and having fallen away", the last in a list of descriptions joined by "and". The King James Version's "if they shall fall away" reads it as a condition. Interpreters differ on whom verses 4–6 describe: true believers who can lose what they have received; people who have shared deeply in the life of the community without truly believing; or a case put as a warning, without saying that any of the readers is in it. They also differ on whether "impossible" means impossible for anyone or impossible for human teachers; the Greek does not say who would renew them.
[^6]: Greek *anastauroo*, which can mean "crucify again" or simply "crucify". *Since they are crucifying*: or "while they are crucifying", that is, for as long as they go on doing so. The Greek can give either the reason or the time.
[^7]: Greek *paradeigmatizo*, "make a public example of, expose to disgrace".
[^8]: Compare Genesis 3:17–18, where the ground is cursed and produces "thorns and thistles".
[^9]: Literally "beloved".
[^10]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "your work and the labor of love".
[^12]: Or "so as to have the full assurance of hope". Greek *plerophoria*, "full assurance, certainty".
[^13]: The same word as in 5:11.
[^14]: Genesis 22:17. Literally "blessing I will bless you and multiplying I will multiply you", a Hebrew way of stressing a verb. Genesis has "multiply your offspring" where Hebrews has "multiply you".
[^15]: Literally "he mediated with an oath": he stepped in as a guarantor, by an oath (Genesis 22:16).
[^16]: Greek *katapetasma*, the hanging that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33). *Inside the veil* is the phrase used in Leviticus 16:2, 12, 15 for the high priest's entering the most holy place.
[^17]: Psalm 110:4 (see 5:6).
