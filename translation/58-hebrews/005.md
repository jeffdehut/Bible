**1** Every high priest is chosen from among human beings and appointed to act on their behalf in matters relating to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins.

**2** He is able to deal gently[^1] with those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself is also subject to weakness.[^2]

**3** Because of this he must offer sacrifices for his own sins, just as he does for the people.

**4** And no one takes this honor for himself. He receives it when he is called by God, just as Aaron was.[^3]

**5** In the same way, the Messiah[^4] did not take for himself the glory of becoming high priest. It was given him by the one who said to him, "You are my Son; today I have become your father."[^5]

**6** As he also says in another place, "You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek."[^6]

**7** During his life on earth,[^7] he offered up prayers and pleas, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death,[^8] and he was heard because of his reverence.[^9]

**8** Son though he was, he learned obedience from what he suffered.[^10]

**9** And once he had been made perfect,[^11] he became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey him,

**10** since God designated him a high priest in the order of Melchizedek.

**11** We have much to say about this,[^12] and it is hard to explain, because you have become sluggish in listening.

**12** In fact, though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic elements of God's message.[^13] You have come to need milk, not solid food.

**13** Everyone who lives on milk is inexperienced in the message of righteousness,[^14] because they are still a baby.

**14** But solid food is for the mature, who through practice have trained their senses[^15] to distinguish good from evil.

[^1]: Greek *metriopatheo*, "keep one's feelings in check", and so "be patient, deal gently".
[^2]: Literally "is surrounded by weakness".
[^3]: Exodus 28:1.
[^4]: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^5]: Psalm 2:7 (see 1:5). Literally "today I have begotten you".
[^6]: Psalm 110:4, also quoted in verse 10; 6:20; 7:17, 21. The Hebrew reads "after the manner of Melchizedek". The Septuagint, quoted here, has *kata ten taxin*, "according to the order", that is, the rank or kind of priesthood. Melchizedek was king of Salem and "priest of God Most High" (Genesis 14:18–20); see chapter 7.
[^7]: Literally "in the days of his flesh".
[^8]: Or "out of death".
[^9]: Or "devotion", Greek *eulabeia*. Some take the phrase to mean "he was heard and set free from his fear".
[^10]: Greek *emathen … epathen*, "he learned … he suffered", a play on words.
[^11]: Greek *teleioo*, "make complete, bring to the goal" (see 2:10).
[^12]: Or "about him".
[^13]: Literally "the elements of the beginning of the oracles of God". Other editions accent the Greek differently: "you need to have someone teach you again what the basic elements … are".
[^14]: Or "unskilled in the word of righteousness", perhaps "unskilled in speaking about what is right".
[^15]: Literally "who because of habit have their faculties trained".
