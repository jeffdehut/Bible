**1** That is why we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away.[^1]

**2** For if the message spoken through angels[^2] proved firm, and every violation and every act of disobedience received its just penalty,

**3** how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It was first announced through the Lord, and it was confirmed to us by those who heard him,

**4** while God added his testimony by signs and wonders, by various miracles,[^3] and by gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed[^4] according to his will.

**5** For it was not to angels that he subjected the world to come, which is what we are speaking about.

**6** Instead, someone has testified somewhere: "What is a human being, that you remember him, or a son of man,[^5] that you care for him?

**7** You made him for a little while lower than the angels;[^6] you crowned him with glory and honor.[^7]

**8** You put everything in subjection under his feet." For in putting everything in subjection,[^8] he left nothing that is not subject to him. But at present we do not yet see everything subject to him.

**9** What we do see is Jesus, who was made for a little while lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor because he suffered death,[^9] so that apart from God[^10] he might taste death[^11] for everyone.[^12]

**10** For it was fitting that he, for whom and through whom all things exist, in bringing many sons and daughters[^13] to glory, should make the pioneer[^14] of their salvation perfect[^15] through sufferings.

**11** For the one who makes people holy and those who are made holy all have one origin.[^16] That is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters.[^17]

**12** He says, "I will tell of your name to my brothers and sisters; in the midst of the assembly I will sing your praise."[^18]

**13** And again, "I will put my trust in him."[^19] And again, "Here am I, and the children God has given me."[^20]

**14** Since, then, the children share in flesh and blood,[^21] he himself likewise shared in the same things, so that through his death he might break the power[^22] of the one who holds the power of death, that is, the devil,

**15** and set free all those who were held in slavery all their lives by the fear of death.

**16** For surely it is not angels that he takes hold of to help,[^23] but the descendants[^24] of Abraham.

**17** So he had to be made like his brothers and sisters in every way, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest[^25] in the service of God, to make atonement[^26] for the sins of the people.

**18** Because he himself suffered when he was tested,[^27] he is able to help those who are being tested.

[^1]: Or "drift off course", like a boat carried past its landing place.
[^2]: The law given at Mount Sinai, which Jewish tradition held was given through angels (compare Acts 7:53; Galatians 3:19).
[^3]: Or "powerful deeds".
[^4]: Literally "and by distributions of the Holy Spirit".
[^5]: That is, a human being, a mortal. Psalm 8:5–7 (8:4–6 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 6–8 from the Septuagint. In verse 9 Hebrews applies the words to Jesus.
[^6]: *For a little while*: or "a little", also in verse 9. The Hebrew of the psalm reads "a little lower than God" (or "than the heavenly beings"); the Septuagint has "angels".
[^7]: Some manuscripts add "and you set him over the works of your hands", as in the psalm.
[^8]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions read "in putting everything in subjection to him". The SBLGNT leaves out "to him", following a minority reading.
[^9]: Literally "because of the suffering of death".
[^10]: The SBLGNT reads *choris theou*, "apart from God", here following a minority reading against the other editions. Many manuscripts and most modern editions read *chariti theou*, "by the grace of God". "Apart from God" may mean that Jesus died without God's help, or abandoned by God, or that "everything" in verse 8 does not include God.
[^11]: That is, experience death.
[^12]: Or "for everything".
[^13]: Literally "many sons". Greek *huioi*: the believers, men and women alike, who share the status of the Son (1:2).
[^14]: Greek *archegos*, "leader, founder, pioneer": one who goes first and opens the way for others (also 12:2). Or "to make perfect through sufferings the pioneer who brings many sons and daughters to glory".
[^15]: Greek *teleioo*, "make complete, bring to the goal", a key word in Hebrews (5:9; 7:28; 10:14).
[^16]: Literally "are all from one": from one Father, God, or from one ancestor.
[^17]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike; also in verses 12 and 17.
[^18]: Psalm 22:23 (22:22 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew reads "I will praise you". *Assembly*: Greek *ekklesia*, the word elsewhere translated "church".
[^19]: Isaiah 8:17, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "I will put my hope in him." Compare 2 Samuel 22:3 and Isaiah 12:2.
[^20]: Isaiah 8:18, where the prophet speaks of himself and his children. In the Hebrew, "God" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^21]: Literally "blood and flesh", that is, human nature.
[^22]: Greek *katargeo*, "make powerless, destroy".
[^23]: Literally "takes hold of", as one takes someone by the hand to help (8:9). It has also been understood as "takes on the nature of".
[^24]: Literally "seed".
[^25]: Greek *archiereus*, the chief priest of Israel, who alone entered the most holy place, once a year (Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:7).
[^26]: Greek *hilaskomai*, traditionally "make propitiation" or "make expiation". It is related to *hilasterion*, "atonement cover" (9:5).
[^27]: Or "tempted". The Greek word *peirazo* means both, also later in the verse (and in 4:15).
