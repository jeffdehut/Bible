**1** This Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High,[^1] met Abraham as he was returning from defeating the kings, and blessed him.[^2]

**2** Abraham gave him a tenth[^3] of everything. His name means first "king of righteousness", and then he is also king of Salem, which means "king of peace".[^4]

**3** He is without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life.[^5] Made like the Son of God, he remains a priest for all time.

**4** Consider how great this man was, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth of the best of the plunder.[^6]

**5** Those descendants of Levi who receive the priesthood are commanded by the law to collect a tenth from the people,[^7] that is, from their fellow Israelites,[^8] even though they too are descended from Abraham.[^9]

**6** But this man, who does not trace his descent from them, collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises.

**7** And without any dispute the lesser is blessed by the greater.

**8** In the one case, mortal men receive tithes. In the other, it is one who is declared to be alive.[^10]

**9** One might even say that Levi himself, who receives tithes, paid tithes through Abraham,

**10** for he was still in the body of his ancestor[^11] when Melchizedek met Abraham.

**11** Now if perfection could have been reached through the Levitical priesthood (for on the basis of it the people received the law),[^12] why was there still a need for another priest to arise in the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named in the order of Aaron?

**12** For when the priesthood is changed, there must also be a change of law.

**13** The one these things are said about belonged to another tribe, from which no one has ever served at the altar.

**14** For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah,[^13] and Moses said nothing about priests in connection with that tribe.[^14]

**15** And it is even more evident if another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek,

**16** one who has become a priest not on the basis of a legal requirement about physical descent,[^15] but by the power of an indestructible life.

**17** For it is declared of him, "You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek."[^16]

**18** On the one hand, an earlier commandment is set aside because it was weak and useless

**19** (for the law made nothing perfect). On the other hand, a better hope is brought in, through which we draw near to God.

**20** And it was not without an oath. (Those others became priests without an oath,

**21** but he became a priest with an oath, through the one who said to him, "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind: 'You are a priest forever.'")[^17]

**22** By so much, then, Jesus has become the guarantee[^18] of a better covenant.[^19]

**23** There have been many of those priests, because death prevented them from continuing in office.

**24** But he holds his priesthood permanently,[^20] because he remains forever.

**25** So he is able to save completely[^21] those who come to God through him, since he always lives to intercede for them.

**26** It was fitting that we should have such a high priest: holy, innocent, undefiled, set apart from sinners, and raised higher than the heavens.

**27** He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices day after day, first for his own sins and then for those of the people.[^22] He did this once for all when he offered himself.

**28** For the law appoints as high priests men who are weak, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever.

[^1]: Greek *theos hypsistos*, the Septuagint's rendering of Hebrew *El Elyon*.
[^2]: Genesis 14:17–20.
[^3]: Greek *dekate*, a tithe; also in verses 4–9.
[^4]: In Hebrew *melekh* means "king" and *tsedeq* "righteousness"; *Salem* sounds like *shalom*, "peace".
[^5]: Genesis says nothing of his parents, his ancestry, his birth or his death. Interpreters differ on whether Hebrews argues only from this silence or regards Melchizedek as more than human.
[^6]: Greek *akrothinia*, "the top of the heap", the choicest part of the spoils of war.
[^7]: Numbers 18:21–26.
[^8]: Literally "their brothers".
[^9]: Literally "have come out of the loins of Abraham".
[^10]: Literally "here mortal men receive tithes, but there one of whom it is testified that he lives".
[^11]: Literally "in the loins of his father".
[^12]: Or "in connection with it".
[^13]: Greek *anatello*, "rise" (like the sun) or "spring up" (like a plant).
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "about priesthood".
[^15]: Literally "according to the law of a fleshly commandment".
[^16]: Psalm 110:4 (see 5:6).
[^17]: Psalm 110:4. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). Many manuscripts add "in the order of Melchizedek".
[^18]: Greek *engyos*, "guarantor, surety", one who stands security for another's pledge.
[^19]: Greek *diatheke*, "covenant", the word Hebrews uses for both the covenant made at Sinai and the new covenant (8:6–13). *By so much*: verses 20–22 are one sentence in Greek, "as much as it was not without an oath … by so much Jesus has become the guarantee …".
[^20]: Or "he holds a priesthood that does not pass to anyone else". Greek *aparabatos*, "unchangeable, permanent".
[^21]: Or "for all time".
[^22]: The high priest offered first for his own sins and then for the people's (Leviticus 9:7; 16:6, 15).
