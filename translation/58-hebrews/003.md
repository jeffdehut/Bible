**1** So, holy brothers and sisters,[^1] you who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle[^2] and high priest of the faith we confess.[^3]

**2** He was faithful to the one who appointed him,[^4] just as Moses also was in his house.[^5]

**3** For he has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses, just as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself.

**4** For every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God.

**5** Moses was faithful in all his house as a servant,[^6] to testify to the things that would be spoken later,

**6** but Christ is faithful as a Son over his house. And we are that house, if we hold on to our confidence and the hope we take pride in.[^7]

**7** So, as the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear his voice,[^8]

**8** do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness,[^9]

**9** where your ancestors put me to the test[^10] and saw my works

**10** for forty years.[^11] That is why I was angry with this generation,[^12] and I said, 'Their hearts are always going astray, and they have not known my ways.'[^13]

**11** So I swore in my anger, 'They shall never enter my rest.'"[^14]

**12** Brothers and sisters, see to it that none of you has an evil, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God.

**13** Instead, encourage one another every day, as long as it is called "Today", so that none of you is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

**14** For we have become partners of the Messiah,[^15] if we hold our original confidence[^16] firm to the end.

**15** As it is said,[^17] "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion."

**16** Who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who came out of Egypt, led by Moses?

**17** And with whom was he angry for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?[^18]

**18** And to whom did he swear that they would not enter his rest, if not to those who disobeyed?[^19]

**19** So we see that they could not enter because of unbelief.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of men and women alike; also in verse 12.
[^2]: Greek *apostolos*, "one who is sent", used of Jesus only here in the New Testament.
[^3]: Literally "of our confession". Many manuscripts add "Christ" to "Jesus".
[^4]: Literally "who made him".
[^5]: Numbers 12:7, where God says of Moses, "He is trusted in all my house"; *his house* is God's house. Greek *oikos*, "house", also means "household", the people of God. The word "all" is not in the SBLGNT, which here follows the margin of Westcott and Hort; the other editions read "in all his house", as in verse 5.
[^6]: Greek *therapon*, the word used for Moses in the Septuagint of Numbers 12:7.
[^7]: Literally "the confidence and the boast of the hope". Many manuscripts add "firm to the end", as in verse 14.
[^8]: Psalm 95:7–11, quoted in verses 7–11 from the Septuagint; Hebrews returns to it in verse 15 and in 4:3–7. In the psalm God speaks from verse 8 on.
[^9]: The Hebrew of the psalm reads "as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness". The Septuagint translates the names, *Meribah* ("quarreling") as "the rebellion" and *Massah* as "testing" (Exodus 17:1–7; Numbers 20:1–13).
[^10]: Literally "tested in testing". Many manuscripts read "tested me, tried me".
[^11]: In the Hebrew of the psalm, "for forty years" begins the next sentence, "For forty years I loathed that generation." Hebrews adds "That is why", but in verse 17 it too joins the forty years to God's anger.
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "that generation".
[^13]: The Hebrew reads "They are a people whose hearts go astray"; the Septuagint has "always".
[^14]: Literally "If they will enter my rest", the form of an oath meaning "they will certainly not enter". *My rest*: in the psalm, the land God promised his people (Deuteronomy 12:9).
[^15]: Or "sharers in the Messiah". Greek *metochoi*, as in verse 1 and 1:9. *The Messiah*: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^16]: Literally "the beginning of the confidence". Greek *hypostasis*, here "confidence, assurance", the word translated "being" in 1:3 and "assurance" in 11:1.
[^17]: The words may go with what comes before ("as long as it is said", verses 13–14) or with what follows.
[^18]: Literally "whose limbs fell", their corpses. Compare Numbers 14:29, 32.
[^19]: Or "who refused to believe".
