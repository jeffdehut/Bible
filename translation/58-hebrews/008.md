**1** Now the main point of what we are saying is this: we have such a high priest, one who sat down at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven,

**2** a minister[^1] in the sanctuary[^2] and the true tabernacle,[^3] which the Lord set up, not a human being.

**3** Every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices, so this one too must have something to offer.

**4** If he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, since there are already those who offer the gifts according to the law.[^4]

**5** They serve in a copy and shadow of what is in heaven. This is why Moses, when he was about to finish the tabernacle, was warned, "See that you make everything according to the pattern you were shown on the mountain."[^5]

**6** But as it is, he has obtained a ministry that is more excellent, just as the covenant[^6] he mediates is better, since it has been established on better promises.

**7** For if that first covenant[^7] had been without fault, there would have been no occasion to look for a second.

**8** But God finds fault with them when he says,[^8] "Look, the days are coming, declares the Lord,[^9] when I will establish[^10] a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.

**9** It will not be like the covenant I made with their ancestors on the day I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt. For they did not continue in my covenant, and so I paid no attention to them, declares the Lord.[^11]

**10** For this is the covenant I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the Lord: I will put my laws into their minds, and I will write them on their hearts.[^12] I will be their God, and they will be my people.

**11** No longer will each one teach his fellow citizen,[^13] or each his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for they will all know me, from the least of them to the greatest.

**12** For I will be merciful toward their wrongdoing,[^14] and I will remember their sins no more."[^15]

**13** By speaking of a "new" covenant, he has made the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old will soon disappear.

[^1]: Greek *leitourgos*, one who performs public or priestly service; compare "ministry" (*leitourgia*) in verse 6.
[^2]: Greek *ta hagia*, "the holy (places)".
[^3]: Greek *skene*, "tent", the Septuagint's word for the tabernacle (Exodus 25:9).
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "since there are priests who offer the gifts according to the law".
[^5]: Exodus 25:40. The Hebrew has "make them" (the lampstand and its utensils) where Hebrews has "make everything".
[^6]: Greek *diatheke* (see 7:22).
[^7]: Literally "that first one".
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "For finding fault, he says to them", which leaves open whether the fault is in the people or in the first covenant. Jeremiah 31:31–34 (38:31–34 in the Septuagint) is quoted in verses 8–12, following the Septuagint.
[^9]: In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh, here and in verses 9–11.
[^10]: Greek *synteleso*, "I will accomplish, bring to completion". The Hebrew has "I will make".
[^11]: The Hebrew of Jeremiah reads "my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband (or master)". The Septuagint, quoted here, has "for they did not continue in my covenant, and I paid no attention to them".
[^12]: The Hebrew reads "I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts."
[^13]: The Hebrew reads "his neighbor".
[^14]: Greek *hileos*, "merciful, gracious", related to *hilaskomai*, "make atonement" (2:17). The Hebrew reads "I will forgive their wrongdoing".
[^15]: Many manuscripts add "and their lawless deeds".
