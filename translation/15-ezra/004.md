**1** When the enemies of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles[^1] were building a temple for the LORD,[^2] the God of Israel,

**2** they came to Zerubbabel and to the heads of the ancestral families and said to them, "Let us build with you, for like you we seek your God, and we have been sacrificing to him[^3] since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria, who brought us here."[^4]

**3** But Zerubbabel, Jeshua and the rest of the heads of the ancestral families of Israel said to them, "It is not for you to build a house for our God with us.[^5] We alone will build it for the LORD, the God of Israel, as King Cyrus, the king of Persia, commanded us."

**4** Then the people of the land[^6] discouraged the people of Judah[^7] and made them afraid to build.

**5** They hired advisers against them to frustrate their plans throughout the reign of Cyrus king of Persia and until the reign of Darius king of Persia.[^8]

**6** In the reign of Ahasuerus,[^9] at the beginning of his reign, they wrote an accusation[^10] against the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

**7** And in the days of Artaxerxes,[^11] Bishlam,[^12] Mithredath, Tabeel and the rest of his associates[^13] wrote to Artaxerxes king of Persia. The letter was written in Aramaic script and translated into Aramaic.[^14]

**8** Rehum the commander[^15] and Shimshai the scribe wrote a letter against Jerusalem to King Artaxerxes, as follows:[^16]

**9** Then Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates, the judges, the envoys, the officials, the Persians,[^17] the people of Erech, the Babylonians, the people of Susa, the Dehaites,[^18] the Elamites,

**10** and the rest of the peoples whom the great and noble Osnappar[^19] deported and settled in the city of Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River.[^20] And now:[^21]

**11** This is a copy[^22] of the letter they sent to him: "To King Artaxerxes, from your servants, the men of the province Beyond the River. And now:

**12** Let it be known to the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have gone to Jerusalem. They are rebuilding that rebellious and wicked city. They are finishing the walls and repairing[^23] the foundations.

**13** Now let it be known to the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, they will not pay tribute, tax or toll, and in the end it will bring loss to the kings.[^24]

**14** Now, since we eat the salt of the palace,[^25] and it is not right for us to see the king dishonored, we have sent to inform the king,

**15** so that a search may be made in the record books of your fathers. In the record books you will find and learn that this city is a rebellious city, harmful to kings and provinces, and that revolt has been stirred up in it from long ago. That is why this city was laid waste.

**16** We are informing the king that if this city is rebuilt and its walls are finished, you will then have no share in the province Beyond the River."

**17** The king sent this reply: "To Rehum the commander, Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates who live in Samaria and in the rest of the province Beyond the River: Greetings.[^26] And now:

**18** The letter you sent to us has been read before me in translation.[^27]

**19** I issued a decree,[^28] and a search was made, and it was found that this city has risen up against kings from long ago, and that rebellion and revolt have been carried on in it.

**20** Powerful kings have ruled over Jerusalem and governed the whole province Beyond the River, and tribute, tax and toll were paid to them.

**21** Now issue a decree to make these men stop, so that this city is not rebuilt until a decree is issued by me.

**22** Take care not to be negligent in this matter. Why should the damage grow, to the harm of kings?"

**23** Then, as soon as the copy of King Artaxerxes' letter had been read before Rehum, Shimshai the scribe and their associates, they went in haste to the Jews in Jerusalem and made them stop by force of arms.[^29]

**24** Then the work on the house of God in Jerusalem stopped, and it remained stopped until the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.[^30]

[^1]: Literally "the sons of the exile".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "and we have not been sacrificing" but read "and we have been sacrificing to him".
[^4]: Esarhaddon was king of Assyria from 681 to 669 BC. On the peoples the Assyrians settled in the land, see 2 Kings 17:24–41.
[^5]: Literally "Not for you and for us to build a house for our God".
[^6]: Hebrew *am ha-arets*, here the other people living in the land, as distinct from the returned exiles.
[^7]: Literally "weakened the hands of the people of Judah".
[^8]: Darius I reigned from 522 to 486 BC. Verses 6–23 tell of opposition under later kings, Ahasuerus and Artaxerxes; verse 24 returns to the reign of Darius.
[^9]: Hebrew *Achashverosh*: Xerxes I, who reigned from 486 to 465 BC. He is the king of the book of Esther.
[^10]: Hebrew *sitnah*, from the same root as *satan*, "accuser".
[^11]: Artaxerxes I, who reigned from 465 to 424 BC.
[^12]: Or "in agreement with". Hebrew *bishlam* may be a name or may mean "in peace".
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "his associate" but read "his associates".
[^14]: Or "written in Aramaic and translated". The last word, "Aramaic", may instead be a heading marking the change of language.
[^15]: Aramaic *be'el te'em*, literally "master of decree": a high official, perhaps the commanding officer or chief of staff of the province. Traditionally "chancellor". Also in verses 9 and 17.
[^16]: The text from here to 6:18 is in Aramaic, the common language of government in the Persian Empire, and not in Hebrew.
[^17]: The meaning of these Aramaic words is uncertain. They may be titles borrowed from Persian (*dinaye* "judges", *apharsatkhaye* "envoys" or "investigators", *tarpelaye* "officials", *apharsaye* "officials") or the names of peoples. Verses 9–10 have no main verb: they seem to list the senders named at the head of the letter.
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "that is" but read "the Dehaites", a people not otherwise known. With the written text: "the people of Susa, that is, the Elamites".
[^19]: Probably Ashurbanipal, king of Assyria from 668 to about 627 BC.
[^20]: Aramaic *avar naharah*, "beyond the river": the Persian province west of the Euphrates, which included Syria, Phoenicia and Judah. Also in verses 11, 16–17 and 20.
[^21]: Aramaic *ukhe'enet*, "and now", a formula marking a transition in letters. Also in verses 11 and 17.
[^22]: Aramaic *parshegen*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^23]: The meaning of the Aramaic verb is uncertain: perhaps "repairing", "laying" or "digging out".
[^24]: Or "and the royal revenue will suffer loss". Aramaic *appetom* is a Persian word of uncertain meaning, "in the end" or "revenue".
[^25]: That is, we are in the king's service. Salt was a sign of a binding relationship.
[^26]: Literally "Peace".
[^27]: Or "clearly", "word for word".
[^28]: Aramaic *te'em*, "order, decree". Also in verse 21.
[^29]: Literally "by arm and force".
[^30]: 520 BC.
