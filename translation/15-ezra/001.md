**1** In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia,[^1] to fulfill the word of the LORD[^2] spoken by Jeremiah,[^3] the LORD stirred the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, and he sent a proclamation throughout his whole kingdom and also put it in writing:[^4]

**2** "This is what Cyrus king of Persia says: The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and he has appointed me[^5] to build him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judah.

**3** Whoever among you belongs to any of his people, may his God be with him! Let him go up to Jerusalem, which is in Judah, and build the house of the LORD, the God of Israel. He is the God who is in Jerusalem.[^6]

**4** And every survivor,[^7] in whatever place he is living as a foreigner, is to be helped[^8] by the people of his place with silver and gold, with goods and with livestock, besides freewill offerings for the house of God in Jerusalem."

**5** Then the heads of the ancestral families[^9] of Judah and Benjamin, and the priests and the Levites, everyone whose spirit God had stirred, got ready to go up and build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem.

**6** All their neighbors helped them[^10] with articles of silver, with gold, with goods, with livestock and with valuable gifts, besides everything that was given as a freewill offering.

**7** King Cyrus also brought out the articles of the house of the LORD that Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem and put in the house of his gods.[^11]

**8** Cyrus king of Persia had them brought out by Mithredath the treasurer,[^12] who counted them out to Sheshbazzar, the leader of Judah.[^13]

**9** This was their number: 30 gold basins,[^14] 1,000 silver basins, 29 knives,[^15]

**10** 30 gold bowls, 410 silver bowls of a second kind[^16] and 1,000 other articles.

**11** All the articles of gold and of silver numbered 5,400.[^17] Sheshbazzar brought them all up when the exiles were brought up from Babylon to Jerusalem.

[^1]: Verses 1–3 repeat, with small differences, the last two verses of 2 Chronicles (36:22–23), which break off in the middle of verse 3 at "let him go up". Cyrus II of Persia conquered Babylon in 539 BC, so his first year as its king was 538 BC.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "from the mouth of Jeremiah". See Jeremiah 25:11–12; 29:10.
[^4]: Literally "and he made a voice pass through all his kingdom, and also in writing, saying".
[^5]: Or "charged me". Literally "he has appointed on me".
[^6]: Or "the God of Israel. He is God. It is in Jerusalem." 2 Chronicles 36:23 reads "may the LORD his God be with him, and let him go up" and ends there.
[^7]: Literally "everyone who remains", that is, the people of Judah who survived the exile.
[^8]: Literally "the men of his place are to lift him up".
[^9]: Literally "the heads of the fathers", that is, of the fathers' houses: extended families descended from a common ancestor. The phrase recurs throughout Ezra and Nehemiah.
[^10]: Literally "all those around them strengthened their hands".
[^11]: Or "his god". See 2 Kings 24:13; 25:13–15; Daniel 1:2.
[^12]: Hebrew *gizbar*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^13]: Hebrew *nasi*, "leader, prince". Sheshbazzar appears again in 5:14–16.
[^14]: The meaning of Hebrew *agartelim* is uncertain: some kind of basin or dish. Also later in the verse.
[^15]: The meaning of Hebrew *machalaphim* is uncertain: perhaps knives, or vessels of some other kind.
[^16]: The meaning of Hebrew *mishnim* is uncertain: perhaps "of second quality" or "matching".
[^17]: The items listed in verses 9–10 add up to 2,499.
