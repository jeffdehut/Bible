**1** Then King Darius issued a decree,[^1] and a search was made in the archives[^2] where the treasures were stored in Babylon.

**2** And in Ecbatana,[^3] the fortress in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and this was written in it: "Memorandum:

**3** In the first year of King Cyrus, King Cyrus issued a decree: Concerning the house of God in Jerusalem, let the house be rebuilt as a place where sacrifices are offered, and let its foundations be firmly laid.[^4] Its height is to be sixty cubits[^5] and its width sixty cubits,

**4** with three layers of huge stones[^6] and one layer of new timber.[^7] The cost is to be paid from the royal treasury.[^8]

**5** Also, the articles of gold and silver from the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took out of the temple in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon, are to be returned and brought back to the temple in Jerusalem, each to its place. You are to deposit them in the house of God."

**6** "Now, Tattenai,[^9] governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and your associates, the officials in the province Beyond the River, keep away from there.

**7** Leave the work on this house of God alone. Let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews rebuild this house of God on its site.

**8** I also issue a decree about what you are to do for these elders of the Jews, for the building of this house of God: The cost is to be paid to these men in full from the royal revenue, from the tribute of the province Beyond the River, so that they are not stopped.[^10]

**9** And whatever is needed, young bulls, rams and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, and wheat, salt, wine and oil, as the priests in Jerusalem request, is to be given to them day by day without fail,

**10** so that they may offer sacrifices of pleasing aroma[^11] to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

**11** I also issue a decree that if anyone alters this edict, a beam is to be pulled out of his house, and he is to be lifted up and impaled on it,[^12] and his house is to be made a dunghill[^13] for this.

**12** May the God who has made his name dwell there overthrow any king or people who reaches out a hand to alter this, or to destroy this house of God in Jerusalem. I, Darius, have issued a decree. Let it be carried out with all diligence."

**13** Then Tattenai, the governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their associates did with all diligence just what King Darius had sent word to do.

**14** So the elders of the Jews built and prospered, through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo. They built and finished it by the decree of the God of Israel and by the decree of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia.[^14]

**15** This house was completed on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of King Darius.[^15]

**16** And the Israelites, the priests and the Levites and the rest of the returned exiles,[^16] celebrated the dedication of this house of God with joy.

**17** For the dedication of this house of God they offered a hundred bulls, two hundred rams and four hundred lambs, and as a sin offering[^17] for all Israel, twelve male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

**18** And they installed the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their groups for the service of God in Jerusalem, as it is written in the book of Moses.[^18]

**19** On the fourteenth day of the first month the returned exiles kept the Passover.

**20** The priests and the Levites had purified themselves together, and all of them were clean. They slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their kinsmen the priests and for themselves.

**21** The Israelites who had returned from the exile ate it, together with everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land to join them, to seek Yahweh,[^19] the God of Israel.

**22** For seven days they kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread with joy, because Yahweh had made them joyful and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria[^20] toward them, so that he strengthened their hands in the work on the house of God, the God of Israel.

[^1]: Aramaic *te'em*, "order, decree". The text of 4:8–6:18 is in Aramaic.
[^2]: Literally "in the house of the books".
[^3]: Aramaic *Achmetha*: Ecbatana (modern Hamadan in Iran), a summer residence of the Persian kings.
[^4]: The meaning of the Aramaic is uncertain. Perhaps "let its foundations be retained" or "let burnt offerings be brought".
[^5]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so sixty cubits was about 90 feet (27 m). The length is not given.
[^6]: Literally "stones of rolling"; see 5:8.
[^7]: Or "one layer of timber".
[^8]: Literally "from the house of the king".
[^9]: Darius's reply to Tattenai begins here, after the copy of Cyrus's decree. The text does not mark the change.
[^10]: Or "without delay".
[^11]: Aramaic *nichochin*, related to the Hebrew phrase "pleasing aroma", used of sacrifices (Leviticus 1:9).
[^12]: Or "fastened to it".
[^13]: Or "a heap of rubble".
[^14]: Aramaic *te'em* is used for both the decree of God and the decree of the kings. Artaxerxes reigned after Darius; compare 7:15–24.
[^15]: Adar is the twelfth month, around February–March. The sixth year of Darius was 515 BC.
[^16]: Aramaic *bene galuta*, "the sons of the exile". In verses 19–20 the Hebrew has the same phrase, *bene ha-golah*.
[^17]: Or "purification offering". Aramaic *chatta'ah*, Hebrew *chatta't*: an offering to remove the effects of sin and impurity (Leviticus 4).
[^18]: The Aramaic section that began at 4:8 ends here. From verse 19 the text is in Hebrew again.
[^19]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^20]: Here the Persian king, who now ruled the lands of the former Assyrian Empire.
