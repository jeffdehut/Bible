**1** Then Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, shouldn't I look for a home for you,[^1] where you will be well provided for?

**2** Now, isn't Boaz, whose young women you have been with, a relative of ours? Tonight he will be winnowing barley at the threshing floor.[^2]

**3** So wash, put on perfume, and get dressed,[^3] and go down[^4] to the threshing floor. But don't let the man know you are there until he has finished eating and drinking.

**4** When he lies down, note the place where he lies. Then go in, uncover his feet,[^5] and lie down.[^6] He will tell you what to do."

**5** She said to her, "I will do everything you tell me."[^7]

**6** So she went down to the threshing floor and did everything her mother-in-law had instructed her.

**7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk and was in good spirits,[^8] he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then she came quietly, uncovered his feet, and lay down.

**8** In the middle of the night the man was startled and turned over,[^9] and there was a woman lying at his feet!

**9** He said, "Who are you?" She said, "I am Ruth, your servant. Spread the corner of your garment[^10] over your servant, for you are a guardian-redeemer."[^11]

**10** He said, "May you be blessed by Yahweh,[^12] my daughter. This last act of faithful love[^13] is greater than the first, for you have not gone after the young men, whether poor or rich.

**11** Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I will do for you everything you ask, for everyone in the town[^14] knows that you are a woman of worth.[^15]

**12** It is true that I am a guardian-redeemer,[^16] but there is another guardian-redeemer more closely related than I am.

**13** Stay here tonight. In the morning, if he will act as your guardian-redeemer, good; let him do it. But if he is not willing to act as your guardian-redeemer, then, as surely as Yahweh lives, I will do it myself. Lie down until morning."

**14** So she lay at his feet until morning, but she got up before anyone could recognize anyone else. He said, "It must not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**15** He said, "Bring the shawl you are wearing and hold it out." She held it, and he measured out six measures of barley[^17] and put it on her. Then he went into the town.

**16** When she came to her mother-in-law, Naomi said, "How did it go,[^18] my daughter?" Then she told her everything the man had done for her.

**17** She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said to me,[^19] 'Don't go back to your mother-in-law empty-handed.'"

**18** Naomi said, "Wait here, my daughter, until you find out how the matter turns out. For the man will not rest until he has settled the matter today."

[^1]: Literally "a resting place": security and a settled home. Compare 1:9.
[^2]: Winnowing was done in the evening breeze, by tossing the threshed grain into the air so that the wind blew the chaff away.
[^3]: Literally "put your garments on you". The Hebrew text is written "your garment" but read "your garments".
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "and I will go down" but read "and you will go down". The written form may be an old spelling of "you".
[^5]: Hebrew *margelot*, "the place of the feet". Also in verses 7, 8 and 14.
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "and I will lie down" but read "and you will lie down". As in verse 3, the written form may be an old spelling of "you".
[^7]: The words "to me" are read in the Hebrew tradition but not written in the text.
[^8]: Literally "his heart was good".
[^9]: Or "leaned forward", or "felt about".
[^10]: Literally "your wing". Hebrew *kanaf* means both "wing" and the corner or edge of a garment; compare 2:12. To spread one's garment over a woman was a sign of taking her as wife (Ezekiel 16:8).
[^11]: Hebrew *go'el*. See 2:20.
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^13]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness. See 1:8. The first act was probably her loyalty to Naomi (2:11).
[^14]: Literally "all the gate of my people". The town gate was where its elders met and its business was done.
[^15]: Hebrew *eshet chayil*, "a woman of strength", "a capable woman", as in Proverbs 31:10. Compare 2:1, where Boaz is a man of *chayil*.
[^16]: The Hebrew text has an extra word here, *im*, which is written but not read.
[^17]: Literally "six of barley". The measure is not stated. Also in verse 17.
[^18]: Literally "Who are you, my daughter?"
[^19]: The words "to me" are read in the Hebrew tradition but not written in the text.
