**1** In the days when the judges ruled,[^1] there was a famine in the land. A man from Bethlehem in Judah went to live for a while[^2] in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons.

**2** The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites[^3] from Bethlehem in Judah. They came to the country of Moab and stayed there.

**3** Then Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.

**4** They married Moabite women. One was named Orpah and the other Ruth. They lived there about ten years.

**5** Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died, and the woman was left without her two boys and without her husband.

**6** So she got up with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that Yahweh[^4] had come to the help of his people and given them food.[^5]

**7** She set out from the place where she had been living, and her two daughters-in-law went with her. They went along the road to return to the land of Judah.

**8** Then Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go back, each of you, to your mother's house. May Yahweh show you faithful love,[^6] as you have shown it to the dead and to me.

**9** May Yahweh grant that each of you find rest[^7] in the home of a husband." Then she kissed them, and they wept aloud.[^8]

**10** They said to her, "No, we will go back with you to your people."

**11** But Naomi said, "Go back, my daughters. Why would you go with me? Do I still have sons in my womb who could become your husbands?[^9]

**12** Go back, my daughters, go, for I am too old to have a husband. Even if I said, 'There is hope for me,' even if I had a husband tonight and then gave birth to sons,

**13** would you wait for them until they grew up? Would you keep yourselves from marrying for them? No, my daughters. It is far more bitter for me than for you,[^10] for the hand of Yahweh has gone out against me."

**14** Then they wept aloud again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law goodbye, but Ruth clung[^11] to her.

**15** Naomi said, "Look, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods.[^12] Go back with your sister-in-law."

**16** But Ruth said, "Don't urge me to leave you or to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go, and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God my God.

**17** Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May Yahweh do this to me, and more besides, if anything but death separates me from you."[^13]

**18** When Naomi saw that Ruth was determined to go with her, she said no more to her.

**19** So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. When they came to Bethlehem, the whole town was stirred because of them, and the women said, "Can this be Naomi?"

**20** She said to them, "Don't call me Naomi.[^14] Call me Mara, for the Almighty[^15] has made my life very bitter.

**21** I went away full, but Yahweh has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when Yahweh has testified against me[^16] and the Almighty has brought disaster on me?"

**22** So Naomi came back, and with her Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, who came back[^17] from the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.[^18]

[^1]: Literally "In the days of the judging of the judges". On the judges, see Judges 2:16.
[^2]: Hebrew *gur*: to live as a foreigner in a land not one's own.
[^3]: Ephrath or Ephrathah was another name for Bethlehem or its district (4:11; Genesis 35:19).
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "bread". *Bethlehem* means "house of bread".
[^6]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness, especially within a relationship of commitment. It is a key word in Ruth (2:20; 3:10). The Hebrew text is written "Yahweh will show you faithful love" but read "May Yahweh show you faithful love".
[^7]: That is, security and a settled home. Compare 3:1.
[^8]: Literally "they lifted up their voice and wept"; also in verse 14.
[^9]: Naomi alludes to the custom by which a brother of a man who died childless married his widow (Deuteronomy 25:5–10; compare Genesis 38:8).
[^10]: Or "It is very bitter for me because of you".
[^11]: Hebrew *davaq*, the verb used in Genesis 2:24, "holds fast to his wife".
[^12]: Or "her god".
[^13]: A form of oath. Literally "Thus may Yahweh do to me, and thus may he add: death will separate me and you." Or "… if even death separates me from you."
[^14]: *Naomi* means "pleasant", and *Mara* means "bitter".
[^15]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty". The meaning of the name is uncertain. Also in verse 21.
[^16]: The Hebrew vowel points read "has testified against me". The same consonants can be read "has afflicted me", as the Septuagint understood them.
[^17]: Literally "who returned".
[^18]: In the spring, around April.
