**1** Naomi had a relative[^1] on her husband's side, a man of standing[^2] from the clan of Elimelech, whose name was Boaz.

**2** Ruth the Moabite said to Naomi, "Let me go to the fields and glean[^3] among the ears of grain behind anyone who will be kind to me."[^4] Naomi said to her, "Go, my daughter."

**3** So she went and came and gleaned in the field behind the reapers. As it happened, she came to the part of the field that belonged to Boaz, who was from the clan of Elimelech.

**4** Just then Boaz came from Bethlehem. He said to the reapers, "The LORD[^5] be with you!" They said to him, "The LORD bless you!"

**5** Boaz said to his servant who was in charge of the reapers, "Whose young woman is this?"

**6** The servant in charge of the reapers answered, "She is the young Moabite woman who came back with Naomi from the country of Moab.

**7** She said, 'Please let me glean and gather among the sheaves behind the reapers.' So she came, and she has been on her feet from early morning until now, except for a short rest in the shelter."[^6]

**8** Then Boaz said to Ruth, "Listen to me, my daughter.[^7] Don't go to glean in another field, and don't leave this one. Stay close[^8] to my young women here.

**9** Keep your eyes on the field they are reaping, and follow after them. I have ordered the young men not to touch you. And when you are thirsty, go to the jars and drink from what the young men have drawn."

**10** Then she fell on her face, bowing to the ground, and said to him, "Why have I found favor with you, that you take notice of me, when I am a foreigner?"[^9]

**11** Boaz answered her, "I have been told everything you have done for your mother-in-law since your husband died: how you left your father and your mother and the land of your birth, and came to a people you did not know before.[^10]

**12** May the LORD repay you for what you have done. May you receive a full reward from the LORD, the God of Israel, under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

**13** She said, "May I go on finding favor with you, my lord,[^11] for you have comforted me and spoken kindly[^12] to your servant, though I am not even like one of your servants."

**14** At mealtime Boaz said to her, "Come over here and eat some of the bread, and dip your piece in the vinegar."[^13] So she sat down beside the reapers, and he handed her roasted grain. She ate until she was satisfied, and she had some left over.

**15** When she got up to glean, Boaz ordered his young men, "Let her glean even among the sheaves, and don't humiliate her.

**16** And pull out some stalks for her from the bundles and leave them for her to glean. Don't rebuke her."

**17** So she gleaned in the field until evening. Then she beat out what she had gleaned, and it came to about an ephah[^14] of barley.

**18** She picked it up and went into the town, and her mother-in-law saw what she had gleaned. Then Ruth brought out and gave her what she had left over after she had eaten her fill.

**19** Her mother-in-law said to her, "Where did you glean today? Where did you work? May the man who took notice of you be blessed!" So she told her mother-in-law about the man she had worked with. She said, "The name of the man I worked with today is Boaz."

**20** Naomi said to her daughter-in-law, "May he be blessed by the LORD, who has not stopped showing his faithful love[^15] to the living and the dead!" Naomi also said to her, "The man is a close relative of ours. He is one of our guardian-redeemers."[^16]

**21** Ruth the Moabite said, "He even said to me, 'Stay close to my young men until they have finished all my harvest.'"

**22** Naomi said to Ruth her daughter-in-law, "It is good, my daughter, that you go out with his young women, so that no one will harm you in another field."

**23** So she stayed close to the young women of Boaz to glean until the end of the barley harvest and the wheat harvest.[^17] And she lived with her mother-in-law.

[^1]: The Hebrew text is written "an acquaintance" but read "a relative".
[^2]: Hebrew *gibbor chayil*, "a mighty man of strength", here a man of wealth and good standing. Compare 3:11, where Ruth is called "a woman of *chayil*".
[^3]: To gather the grain the harvesters left behind. The law of Moses gave the poor, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow the right to glean (Leviticus 19:9–10; Deuteronomy 24:19).
[^4]: Literally "after one in whose eyes I find favor".
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: The Hebrew of the end of this verse is uncertain. Literally "this her sitting the house a little".
[^7]: Literally "Have you not heard, my daughter?"
[^8]: Hebrew *davaq*, "cling, hold fast", the verb used in 1:14. Also in verses 21 and 23.
[^9]: In Hebrew, "take notice of" (*hakkir*) and "foreigner" (*nokhriyyah*) sound alike.
[^10]: Literally "yesterday and the day before".
[^11]: Or "You are very kind to me, my lord". Literally "Let me find favor in your eyes, my lord."
[^12]: Literally "spoken to the heart of".
[^13]: Or "sour wine", a drink in which bread was dipped.
[^14]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel).
[^15]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love and kindness. See 1:8. The Hebrew can also mean that Boaz is the one "who has not stopped showing his faithful love".
[^16]: Hebrew *go'el*, traditionally "kinsman-redeemer". A *go'el* was a close relative with the duty to protect the interests of a family member in need: to buy back land a relative had been forced to sell (Leviticus 25:25), to buy back a relative who had sold himself into slavery (Leviticus 25:47–49), and to avenge a relative who had been killed (Numbers 35:19). In Ruth the right to buy back Elimelech's land goes together with marriage to Ruth, to keep the dead man's name alive (4:5, 10; compare Deuteronomy 25:5–10).
[^17]: From about April to June.
