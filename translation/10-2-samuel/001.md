**1** After the death of Saul, when David had come back from defeating the Amalekites, David stayed in Ziklag for two days.

**2** On the third day a man came from Saul's camp, with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**3** David said to him, "Where have you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**4** David said to him, "What happened? Tell me." He said, "The troops fled from the battle. Many of the troops fell and died, and Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too."

**5** David said to the young man who had brought him the news, "How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?"

**6** The young man who had brought him the news said, "I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul, leaning on his spear. The chariots and the horsemen[^1] were closing in on him.

**7** He turned around, saw me, and called to me. I said, 'Here I am.'

**8** He said to me, 'Who are you?' I said to him,[^2] 'I am an Amalekite.'

**9** He said to me, 'Please stand over me and kill me, for agony[^3] has seized me, and yet my life is still in me.'

**10** So I stood over him and killed him, because I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. Then I took the crown that was on his head and the band that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**11** Then David took hold of his clothes[^4] and tore them, and so did all the men who were with him.

**12** They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and for his son Jonathan, and for the people of Yahweh[^5] and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**13** David said to the young man who had brought him the news, "Where are you from?" He said, "I am the son of a foreigner,[^6] an Amalekite."

**14** David said to him, "How is it that you were not afraid to lift your hand to destroy Yahweh's anointed?"[^7]

**15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Go, strike him down!" So he struck him, and he died.

**16** David said to him, "Your blood[^8] is on your own head, because your own mouth testified against you when you said, 'I killed Yahweh's anointed.'"

**17** David sang this lament over Saul and over his son Jonathan,

**18** and he ordered that the people of Judah be taught "The Bow."[^9] It is written in the Book of Jashar:[^10]

**19** "Your glory,[^11] Israel, lies slain on your heights. How the mighty have fallen!

**20** Do not tell it in Gath; do not announce it in the streets of Ashkelon, or the daughters of the Philistines will rejoice; the daughters of the uncircumcised will celebrate.

**21** Mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew and no rain on you, nor fields that yield offerings.[^12] For there the shield of the mighty was defiled,[^13] the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil.[^14]

**22** From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back, and the sword of Saul did not come back empty.

**23** Saul and Jonathan, loved and delightful in their lives, were not parted in their death. They were swifter than eagles; they were stronger than lions.

**24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet and luxury, who put ornaments of gold on your clothing.

**25** How the mighty have fallen in the middle of the battle! Jonathan lies slain on your heights.[^15]

**26** I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan. You were very dear to me. Your love for me was more wonderful than the love of women.

**27** How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"

[^1]: Literally "the masters of the horsemen", that is, the horsemen or their commanders.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "He said to him" but read "I said to him".
[^3]: Hebrew *shavats*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps cramp, dizziness or the agony of death.
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "his garment" but read "his garments".
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^7]: Hebrew *meshiach YHWH*: the king anointed with oil at Yahweh's command (1 Samuel 10:1). *Meshiach*, "anointed one", is the word that gives English "Messiah". Also in verse 16.
[^8]: The Hebrew text is written "your bloods", a plural that can mean "your bloodguilt", but read "your blood".
[^9]: Literally "And he said to teach the sons of Judah 'Bow'." *The Bow* is probably the title of the lament, perhaps taken from verse 22. Some take it to mean that David ordered the people of Judah to be taught archery.
[^10]: Or "the Book of the Upright". A collection of poems that has not survived, also quoted in Joshua 10:13.
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-tsevi*, "the beauty, the glory", probably meaning Saul and Jonathan. The same word means "gazelle", so some translate "The gazelle, Israel, lies slain".
[^12]: Literally "fields of contributions", perhaps meaning fields whose crops supply offerings. The meaning is uncertain.
[^13]: Or "cast aside as worthless".
[^14]: Or "as though not anointed with oil". Leather shields were rubbed with oil before battle. The words may refer to the shield or to Saul, the anointed king.
[^15]: Or "Jonathan, slain on your heights!" As in verse 19, "your" is probably Israel.
