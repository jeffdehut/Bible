**1** Now a worthless man[^1] happened to be there, named Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite. He blew the ram's horn and said, "We have no share in David, no inheritance in the son of Jesse! Every man to his tents,[^2] Israel!"

**2** So all the men of Israel deserted David and followed Sheba son of Bichri. But the men of Judah stayed close to their king, from the Jordan to Jerusalem.

**3** When David came to his house in Jerusalem, the king took the ten women, the concubines he had left to look after the house, and put them in a house under guard. He provided for them, but he did not sleep with them.[^3] They were shut away until the day of their death, living as widows.[^4]

**4** The king said to Amasa, "Call the men of Judah together for me within three days, and be here yourself."

**5** So Amasa went to call Judah together, but he took longer than the time David had set him.

**6** David said to Abishai, "Now Sheba son of Bichri will do us more harm than Absalom did. Take your lord's servants and pursue him, or he will find himself fortified cities and escape from our sight."[^5]

**7** So Joab's men, the Cherethites, the Pelethites and all the warriors went out after him. They left Jerusalem to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.

**8** When they were at the great stone in Gibeon, Amasa came to meet them. Joab was wearing his military tunic,[^6] and over it was a belt with a sword in its sheath fastened at his waist. As he stepped forward, it fell out.[^7]

**9** Joab said to Amasa, "Are you well, my brother?" And Joab's right hand took Amasa by the beard to kiss him.

**10** Amasa was not on guard against the sword in Joab's hand. Joab struck him in the stomach[^8] with it, and his insides spilled out on the ground. He did not strike him a second time, and he died. Then Joab and his brother Abishai pursued Sheba son of Bichri.

**11** One of Joab's young men stood over Amasa and said, "Whoever favors Joab, and whoever is for David, follow Joab!"

**12** Amasa lay wallowing in his blood in the middle of the road. When the man saw that all the troops were stopping, he moved Amasa from the road into the field and threw a garment over him, because he saw that everyone who came by him stopped.

**13** Once he had been removed from the road, everyone went on after Joab to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.

**14** Sheba went through all the tribes of Israel to Abel and Beth-maacah,[^9] and all the Berites.[^10] They assembled and followed him in.

**15** They came and besieged him in Abel of Beth-maacah. They piled up a siege ramp against the city, and it stood against the outer wall. All the troops who were with Joab were battering the wall to bring it down.

**16** Then a wise woman called out from the city, "Listen! Listen! Please tell Joab, 'Come here, so that I may speak with you.'"

**17** He came near to her, and the woman said, "Are you Joab?" He said, "I am." She said to him, "Listen to the words of your servant." He said, "I am listening."

**18** She said, "In the old days they used to say, 'Let them ask at Abel,' and that settled the matter.[^11]

**19** I am one of the peaceful and faithful in Israel.[^12] You are trying to destroy a city that is a mother in Israel.[^13] Why would you swallow up the inheritance of the LORD?"[^14]

**20** Joab answered, "Far be it from me, far be it, that I should swallow up or destroy!

**21** That is not the case. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, named Sheba son of Bichri, has lifted his hand against the king, against David. Hand over just this one man, and I will withdraw from the city." The woman said to Joab, "His head will be thrown to you over the wall."

**22** Then the woman went to all the people with her wisdom, and they cut off the head of Sheba son of Bichri and threw it out to Joab. He blew the ram's horn, and they dispersed from the city, each to his home.[^15] And Joab went back to the king in Jerusalem.

**23** Joab was over the whole army of Israel. Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites[^16] and the Pelethites.

**24** Adoram was over the forced labor.[^17] Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder.[^18]

**25** Sheva[^19] was secretary. Zadok and Abiathar were priests.

**26** And Ira the Jairite was David's priest.[^20]

[^1]: Literally "a man of *beliyya'al*", "a man of worthlessness": a scoundrel.
[^2]: That is, go home.
[^3]: Literally "go in to them", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^4]: Literally "in widowhood of life", perhaps meaning widows while their husband was still alive.
[^5]: Literally "and snatch away our eye". The meaning is uncertain.
[^6]: Literally "girded with his garment, his clothing".
[^7]: Literally "and he went out, and it fell". The Hebrew is difficult. The point seems to be that the sword fell out, perhaps deliberately, so that Joab could pick it up and hold it without arousing suspicion.
[^8]: Hebrew *chomesh*; see 2:23.
[^9]: Probably the same town as Abel of Beth-maacah (verse 15), in the far north of Israel.
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-berim*, a name not otherwise known. Many take it to be a copying error for "the Bichrites", Sheba's clan.
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "Let them ask in Abel and in Dan whether what the faithful of Israel established has come to an end."
[^12]: Literally "I am of the peaceful ones, the faithful ones of Israel".
[^13]: That is, a leading city, on which the surrounding towns depended.
[^14]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The inheritance of the LORD* is his people and their land.
[^15]: Literally "each to his tents".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "the Carites" but read "the Cherethites". The Carites were a royal guard (2 Kings 11:4, 19). On the Cherethites and Pelethites, see 8:18.
[^17]: Hebrew *mas*, gangs of forced laborers. In 1 Kings 4:6 he is called Adoniram.
[^18]: See 8:16.
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "Sheya" but read "Sheva". See 8:17.
[^20]: Hebrew *kohen*, the usual word for priest; compare 8:18.
