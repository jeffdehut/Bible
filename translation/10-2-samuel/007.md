**1** When the king was settled in his house, and the LORD[^1] had given him rest from all his enemies around him,

**2** the king said to Nathan the prophet, "Look, I am living in a house of cedar, while the ark of God stays inside a tent."[^2]

**3** Nathan said to the king, "Go and do all that is in your heart, for the LORD is with you."

**4** But that same night the word of the LORD came to Nathan:

**5** "Go and tell my servant David, 'This is what the LORD says: Are you the one to build me a house to live in?[^3]

**6** For I have not lived in a house from the day I brought the Israelites up out of Egypt to this day. I have been moving about in a tent and a tabernacle.[^4]

**7** Wherever I have moved about among all the Israelites, did I ever say a word to any of the tribes[^5] of Israel whom I commanded to shepherd my people Israel, "Why have you not built me a house of cedar?"'

**8** Now this is what you are to say to my servant David: 'This is what the LORD of Armies[^6] says: I took you from the pasture, from following the sheep, to be ruler[^7] over my people Israel.

**9** I have been with you wherever you have gone, and I have cut off all your enemies from before you. I will make your name great,[^8] like the names of the great ones on the earth.

**10** I will appoint a place for my people Israel, and I will plant them so that they may live in their own place and be disturbed no more. Wicked people[^9] will not oppress them any more, as they did at the beginning,

**11** from the day I appointed judges over my people Israel. I will give you rest from all your enemies. And the LORD declares to you that the LORD will make a house for you.[^10]

**12** When your days are complete and you lie down with your ancestors, I will raise up your offspring[^11] after you, one who will come from your own body, and I will establish his kingdom.

**13** He will build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.

**14** I will be his father, and he will be my son.[^12] When he does wrong, I will discipline him with the rod that men use, with blows inflicted by human beings.[^13]

**15** But my faithful love[^14] will not depart from him, as I took it away from Saul, whom I removed from before you.

**16** Your house and your kingdom will stand firm[^15] forever before you.[^16] Your throne will be established forever.'"

**17** Nathan spoke to David in accordance with all these words and all this vision.

**18** Then King David went in and sat before the LORD and said, "Who am I, Lord GOD,[^17] and what is my house, that you have brought me this far?

**19** And yet this was too small a thing in your eyes, Lord GOD. You have also spoken about your servant's house for a long time to come. And this is instruction for humankind, Lord GOD![^18]

**20** What more can David say to you? You know your servant, Lord GOD.

**21** For the sake of your word,[^19] and as your heart desired, you have done all this great thing, to make it known to your servant.

**22** That is why you are great, Lord GOD. There is no one like you, and there is no God besides you, by all that we have heard with our ears.

**23** And who is like your people Israel, the one nation on earth that God went[^20] to redeem as a people for himself, to make a name for himself, and to do great and awesome things for them and for your land, before your people whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt, from nations and their gods?[^21]

**24** You established your people Israel for yourself as your own people forever, and you, LORD, became their God.

**25** Now, LORD God, confirm forever the word you have spoken concerning your servant and his house, and do as you have said.

**26** Then your name will be great forever, and people will say, 'The LORD of Armies is God over Israel.' And the house of your servant David will be established before you.

**27** For you, LORD of Armies, the God of Israel, have revealed this to your servant,[^22] saying, 'I will build you a house.' That is why your servant has found the courage[^23] to pray this prayer to you.

**28** Now, Lord GOD, you are God, and your words are true, and you have promised this good thing to your servant.

**29** Now be pleased to bless the house of your servant, so that it may continue forever before you. For you, Lord GOD, have spoken, and with your blessing may the house of your servant be blessed forever."

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "inside the curtain", that is, the tent curtains (6:17).
[^3]: The parallel in 1 Chronicles 17:4 reads "You are not the one who will build me a house to live in." The Septuagint of this verse also reads "You will not build".
[^4]: Hebrew *mishkan*, "dwelling", the word used for the tabernacle that Israel carried through the wilderness (Exodus 25:9).
[^5]: Hebrew *shivtei*, "tribes of". 1 Chronicles 17:6 reads *shophtei*, "judges of", which fits "whom I commanded to shepherd my people" (compare verse 11).
[^6]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 26 and 27.
[^7]: Hebrew *nagid*; see 5:2.
[^8]: Or "I have made your name great". The verbs from here to verse 11 can be read as past or future.
[^9]: Literally "sons of injustice".
[^10]: That is, a dynasty, a line of kings from David's descendants. David had wanted to build a "house", a temple, for the LORD (verse 5).
[^11]: Literally "your seed".
[^12]: Literally "I will be to him a father, and he will be to me a son." Quoted in Hebrews 1:5.
[^13]: Literally "with the rod of men and with the blows of the sons of humankind", that is, with the kind of punishment people give.
[^14]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^15]: Hebrew *ne'eman*, "made firm, made sure, faithful".
[^16]: Some Hebrew manuscripts and the Septuagint read "before me".
[^17]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. Also in verses 19, 20, 22, 28 and 29.
[^18]: Hebrew *torat ha-adam*, literally "the law (or instruction) of humankind". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "and this is a teaching for all people", or, as a question, "Is this the way you deal with a man?" The parallel in 1 Chronicles 17:17 is also difficult.
[^19]: 1 Chronicles 17:19 reads "For the sake of your servant".
[^20]: The Hebrew verb is plural, as though "gods went". 1 Chronicles 17:21 has the singular.
[^21]: The Hebrew of the end of this verse is difficult. Literally "and to do for you (plural) the great thing and awesome things for your land, from before your people whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt, nations and its gods." 1 Chronicles 17:21 reads "by driving out nations from before your people whom you redeemed from Egypt".
[^22]: Literally "uncovered the ear of your servant".
[^23]: Literally "has found his heart".
