**1** After this, David defeated the Philistines and subdued them, and David took Metheg-ammah[^1] out of the hand of the Philistines.

**2** He also defeated Moab. He made them lie down on the ground and measured them off with a line. He measured two lengths of line to be put to death, and one full length to be kept alive. So the Moabites became subject[^2] to David and brought him tribute.

**3** David also defeated Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah, when he went to restore his control[^3] at the River Euphrates.[^4]

**4** David captured from him 1,700 horsemen[^5] and twenty thousand foot soldiers. David hamstrung all the chariot horses, but he kept back a hundred of them for chariots.

**5** When the Arameans of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah, David struck down twenty-two thousand of the Arameans.

**6** Then David put garrisons[^6] in Aram of Damascus, and the Arameans became subject to David and brought him tribute. Yahweh[^7] gave David victory wherever he went.

**7** David took the gold shields[^8] that belonged to the officers of Hadadezer and brought them to Jerusalem.

**8** And from Betah and Berothai,[^9] towns of Hadadezer, King David took a very large amount of bronze.

**9** When Toi king of Hamath heard that David had defeated the whole army of Hadadezer,

**10** Toi sent his son Joram[^10] to King David to greet him[^11] and to congratulate him because he had fought against Hadadezer and defeated him, for Hadadezer had often been at war with Toi.[^12] Joram brought with him articles of silver, articles of gold and articles of bronze.

**11** King David dedicated these also to Yahweh, along with the silver and gold that he had dedicated from all the nations he had subdued:

**12** from Aram,[^13] from Moab, from the Ammonites, from the Philistines and from Amalek, and from the plunder of Hadadezer son of Rehob, king of Zobah.

**13** David made a name for himself when he returned from striking down Aram[^14] in the Valley of Salt, eighteen thousand of them.

**14** He put garrisons in Edom. Throughout all Edom he put garrisons, and all the Edomites became subject to David. Yahweh gave David victory wherever he went.

**15** David reigned over all Israel, and David did what was just and right[^15] for all his people.

**16** Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder.[^16]

**17** Zadok son of Ahitub and Ahimelech son of Abiathar[^17] were priests. Seraiah was secretary.[^18]

**18** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over[^19] the Cherethites and the Pelethites.[^20] And David's sons were priests.[^21]

[^1]: Hebrew *meteg ha-ammah*, perhaps "the bridle of the mother city", that is, control of the Philistines' chief city. The meaning is uncertain. 1 Chronicles 18:1 reads "Gath and its villages".
[^2]: Literally "became servants". Also in verses 6 and 14.
[^3]: Literally "his hand", perhaps meaning a monument marking his rule (compare 1 Samuel 15:12, where the same word is used). It is not clear whether "he" is David or Hadadezer.
[^4]: The word "Euphrates" is read but not written in the Hebrew text, which has "the River".
[^5]: 1 Chronicles 18:4 and the Septuagint read "a thousand chariots and seven thousand horsemen".
[^6]: Or "governors". Hebrew *netsivim*. Also in verse 14.
[^7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *shiltei*, a word of uncertain meaning: shields, or perhaps quivers.
[^9]: 1 Chronicles 18:8 has "Tibhath and Cun".
[^10]: 1 Chronicles 18:10 has "Hadoram".
[^11]: Literally "to ask him about his well-being".
[^12]: Literally "for Hadadezer was a man of wars of Toi".
[^13]: Some Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "Edom", as in 1 Chronicles 18:11. In Hebrew the two names differ by one similar letter.
[^14]: Some Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "Edom", as in 1 Chronicles 18:12. The Valley of Salt was near Edom (verse 14; compare the title of Psalm 60).
[^15]: Literally "was doing justice and righteousness".
[^16]: Hebrew *mazkir*, "one who causes to remember": a high official, perhaps the king's herald or keeper of records.
[^17]: Elsewhere Abiathar is the son of Ahimelech (1 Samuel 22:20), and Zadok and Abiathar are the priests (20:25).
[^18]: Hebrew *sopher*, "scribe", the royal secretary. He is called Sheva in 20:25 and Shavsha in 1 Chronicles 18:16.
[^19]: The Hebrew lacks "was over". Compare 20:23 and 1 Chronicles 18:17.
[^20]: Foreign soldiers who served as David's personal guard. The Cherethites were probably from Crete, and the Pelethites may have been Philistines.
[^21]: Hebrew *kohanim*, the usual word for priests. 1 Chronicles 18:17 reads "David's sons were the chief officials at the king's side".
