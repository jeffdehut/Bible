**1** In the days of David there was a famine for three years, year after year. David sought the face of the LORD,[^1] and the LORD said, "It is because of Saul and his house, stained with blood,[^2] because he put the Gibeonites to death."

**2** So the king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. (The Gibeonites were not Israelites but a remnant of the Amorites. The Israelites had sworn an oath to them,[^3] but Saul had tried to strike them down in his zeal for the people of Israel and Judah.)

**3** David said to the Gibeonites, "What shall I do for you? How shall I make atonement,[^4] so that you will bless the inheritance of the LORD?"

**4** The Gibeonites said to him, "It is not a matter of silver or gold between us[^5] and Saul or his house, and it is not for us to put anyone to death in Israel." He said, "Whatever you say, I will do for you."

**5** They said to the king, "The man who consumed us and planned against us, so that we have been wiped out and have no place anywhere in the territory of Israel,

**6** let seven men of his sons be handed over to us, and we will execute them[^6] before the LORD at Gibeah of Saul, the chosen one of the LORD."[^7] The king said, "I will hand them over."

**7** But the king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, son of Saul, because of the oath before the LORD[^8] that was between them, between David and Jonathan son of Saul.

**8** The king took the two sons of Rizpah daughter of Aiah, whom she had borne to Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth,[^9] and the five sons of Michal[^10] daughter of Saul, whom she had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.

**9** He handed them over to the Gibeonites, and they executed them on the mountain before the LORD. The seven of them[^11] fell together. They were put to death in the first days of the harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest.

**10** Then Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it out for herself on the rock, from the beginning of the harvest until rain poured down on them from the sky. She did not let the birds of the sky settle on them by day, or the wild animals by night.

**11** When David was told what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, Saul's concubine, had done,

**12** David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan from the citizens[^12] of Jabesh-gilead. They had stolen them from the public square of Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them on the day the Philistines struck down Saul at Gilboa.

**13** He brought up from there the bones of Saul and the bones of his son Jonathan, and they gathered the bones of those who had been executed.

**14** They buried the bones of Saul and his son Jonathan in the land of Benjamin, at Zela, in the tomb of his father Kish. They did everything the king commanded. After that, God answered prayers for the land.

**15** Once again there was war between the Philistines and Israel. David went down with his servants, and they fought against the Philistines, and David grew exhausted.

**16** Then Ishbi-benob,[^13] one of the descendants of the Raphah,[^14] whose spear weighed three hundred shekels[^15] of bronze, and who was armed with a new weapon,[^16] said he would kill David.

**17** But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his help, struck the Philistine and killed him. Then David's men swore to him, "You must never again go out with us to battle, so that you do not put out the lamp of Israel."

**18** After this there was another battle with the Philistines, at Gob. Then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, who was one of the descendants of the Raphah.

**19** There was another battle with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite struck down Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam.[^17]

**20** There was yet another battle, at Gath, where there was a man of great size[^18] who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number. He too was descended from the Raphah.

**21** When he taunted Israel, Jonathan son of Shimea,[^19] David's brother, struck him down.

**22** These four were descended from the Raphah in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

[^1]: That is, he asked the LORD for guidance. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "and the house of bloods".
[^3]: That is, they had sworn to let them live (Joshua 9:3–27).
[^4]: Hebrew *akhapper*, "make atonement, make amends".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "me" but read "us".
[^6]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain: perhaps "impale", "hang" or "expose" their bodies (compare Numbers 25:4). Also in verses 9 and 13.
[^7]: Hebrew *bechir YHWH*, "the chosen one of the LORD", that is, Saul (1 Samuel 10:24). Many think the text originally read "on the mountain of the LORD" (compare verse 9).
[^8]: Literally "the oath of the LORD" (1 Samuel 20:42).
[^9]: Another son of Saul, not Jonathan's son Mephibosheth (verse 7).
[^10]: Some Hebrew manuscripts and some manuscripts of the Septuagint read "Merab". Merab was given in marriage to Adriel the Meholathite (1 Samuel 18:19), and Michal had no children (6:23).
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "sevenfold" but read "the seven of them".
[^12]: Hebrew *ba'alei*, "the lords of", meaning the citizens or leading men.
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "and they stayed at Nob" but read "Ishbi-benob".
[^14]: Hebrew *ha-rapha*, perhaps the ancestor of the Rephaim, a people remembered as giants (Deuteronomy 2:11, 20). Also in verses 18, 20 and 22.
[^15]: A shekel is about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams), so three hundred shekels is about 7½ pounds (3.5 kg).
[^16]: Literally "girded with a new (one)". The noun is missing: perhaps a new sword or new armor.
[^17]: The heavy rod of a loom. 1 Chronicles 20:5 reads "Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite". In 1 Samuel 17 David kills Goliath. *Oregim* means "weavers", and the word may have been copied into the name from the end of the verse.
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written *midyan* but read *madon*. Both words are of uncertain meaning here; 1 Chronicles 20:6 has "a man of great size".
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "Shimei" but read "Shimea".
