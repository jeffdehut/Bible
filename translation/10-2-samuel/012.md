**1** The LORD[^1] sent Nathan to David. He came to him and said, "There were two men in a certain city, one rich and the other poor.

**2** The rich man had a very large number of sheep and cattle.

**3** But the poor man had nothing except one little ewe lamb that he had bought. He raised it, and it grew up with him and with his children. It would eat from his bit of food, drink from his cup, and sleep in his arms. It was like a daughter to him.

**4** Now a traveler came to the rich man, but he was unwilling to take one of his own sheep or cattle to prepare for the traveler who had come to him. Instead he took the poor man's lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

**5** David's anger burned hot against the man, and he said to Nathan, "As the LORD lives, the man who did this deserves to die![^2]

**6** He must pay for the lamb four times over,[^3] because he did this thing and because he had no pity."

**7** Nathan said to David, "You are the man! This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'I anointed you king over Israel, and I rescued you from the hand of Saul.

**8** I gave you your master's house, and your master's wives into your arms.[^4] I gave you the house of Israel and Judah. And if that had been too little, I would have given you as much again and more.

**9** Why have you despised the word of the LORD by doing what is evil in my sight?[^5] You struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword and took his wife to be your wife. You killed him with the sword of the Ammonites.

**10** Now therefore the sword will never depart from your house, because you despised me and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.'

**11** This is what the LORD says: 'I am going to raise up trouble against you from your own house. Before your eyes I will take your wives and give them to your neighbor, and he will sleep with your wives in broad daylight.[^6]

**12** You did it in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.'"

**13** David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." Nathan said to David, "The LORD, for his part, has taken away your sin. You will not die.

**14** But because by this deed you have treated the enemies of the LORD with utter contempt,[^7] the son who is born to you will surely die."

**15** Then Nathan went home. The LORD struck the child that Uriah's wife had borne to David, and he became very ill.

**16** David pleaded with God for the boy. David fasted, and he went in and spent the nights lying on the ground.

**17** The elders of his household stood over him to raise him up from the ground, but he was not willing, and he would not eat food with them.

**18** On the seventh day the child died. David's servants were afraid to tell him that the child was dead, for they said, "While the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he would not listen to us. How can we tell him the child is dead? He may do something terrible."[^8]

**19** When David saw that his servants were whispering together, David realized that the child was dead. David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" They said, "He is dead."

**20** Then David got up from the ground, washed, anointed himself and changed his clothes.[^9] He went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Then he went to his own house and asked for food, and they set food before him, and he ate.

**21** His servants said to him, "What is this you have done? While the child was alive, you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you got up and ate food."

**22** He said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, because I thought, 'Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me and let the child live.'

**23** But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I will go to him, but he will not come back to me."

**24** Then David comforted his wife Bathsheba. He went to her and slept with her, and she gave birth to a son, and she named him[^10] Solomon. The LORD loved him,

**25** and he sent word through Nathan the prophet, and he named him Jedidiah,[^11] because of the LORD.

**26** Meanwhile Joab fought against Rabbah of the Ammonites and captured the royal city.

**27** Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah, and I have also captured the city of waters.[^12]

**28** Now gather the rest of the troops, camp against the city and capture it. Otherwise I will capture the city, and it will be named after me."[^13]

**29** So David gathered all the troops, went to Rabbah, fought against it and captured it.

**30** He took the crown of their king[^14] from his head. It weighed a talent[^15] of gold, and it held a precious stone. It was placed on David's head. He also brought out a very large amount of plunder from the city.

**31** He brought out the people who were in it and set them to work with saws, iron picks and iron axes, and made them work at the brickworks.[^16] He did the same to all the towns of the Ammonites. Then David and all the troops returned to Jerusalem.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "is a son of death".
[^3]: The Septuagint reads "seven times over". The law required fourfold repayment for a stolen sheep: Exodus 21:37 (22:1 in most English Bibles).
[^4]: Literally "into your bosom".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "in his sight" but read "in my sight".
[^6]: Literally "before the eyes of this sun". This is fulfilled in 16:21–22.
[^7]: Literally "you have greatly scorned the enemies of the LORD". "The enemies of" was probably added by scribes to avoid writing that David scorned the LORD himself.
[^8]: Literally "He may do evil", perhaps meaning that he would harm himself.
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "his garment" but read "his garments".
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "he named him" but read "she named him". *Solomon* (Hebrew *Shelomoh*) is related to *shalom*, "peace".
[^11]: *Jedidiah* means "beloved of the LORD".
[^12]: Probably the part of the city that guarded its water supply.
[^13]: Literally "and my name will be called over it".
[^14]: Hebrew *malkam*, "their king", which may also be read as Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5). The Septuagint reads "Milcom their king".
[^15]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg).
[^16]: Literally "put them to the saw, to the iron picks and to the iron axes, and made them pass through the brick mold". Some understand it as torture (compare 1 Chronicles 20:3). The Hebrew text is written *malkan* but read *malben*, "brick mold" or "brickworks".
