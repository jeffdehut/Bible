**1** When David had gone a little beyond the summit, there was Ziba, Mephibosheth's servant, coming to meet him with a pair of saddled donkeys. On them were two hundred loaves of bread, a hundred clusters of raisins, a hundred summer fruits[^1] and a skin of wine.

**2** The king said to Ziba, "What do you mean by these?" Ziba said, "The donkeys are for the king's household to ride, the bread and summer fruit for the young men to eat, and the wine for those who become exhausted in the wilderness to drink."

**3** The king said, "And where is your master's grandson?"[^2] Ziba said to the king, "He is staying in Jerusalem, for he said, 'Today the house of Israel will give me back my grandfather's[^3] kingdom.'"

**4** The king said to Ziba, "All that belonged to Mephibosheth is now yours." Ziba said, "I bow down. May I find favor in your eyes, my lord the king."

**5** When King David came to Bahurim, a man from the clan of Saul's house came out from there. His name was Shimei son of Gera, and he came out cursing as he went.

**6** He threw stones at David and at all King David's servants, though all the troops and all the warriors were on his right and on his left.

**7** This is what Shimei said as he cursed: "Get out, get out, you man of blood, you scoundrel![^4]

**8** Yahweh[^5] has paid you back for all the blood of the house of Saul, in whose place you have reigned, and Yahweh has handed the kingdom over to your son Absalom. Look at you now, in your own disaster, because you are a man of blood!"

**9** Abishai son of Zeruiah said to the king, "Why should this dead dog curse my lord the king? Let me go over and cut off his head."

**10** But the king said, "What do I have to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah? Let him curse, for Yahweh has told him, 'Curse David.'[^6] Who then can say, 'Why have you done this?'"

**11** David said to Abishai and to all his servants, "Look, my own son, who came from my own body, is seeking my life. How much more now this Benjaminite! Leave him alone, and let him curse, for Yahweh has told him to.

**12** Perhaps Yahweh will look on my tears,[^7] and Yahweh will repay me with good for his cursing today."

**13** So David and his men went on their way, while Shimei went along the hillside opposite him, cursing as he went, throwing stones at him and flinging dust.

**14** The king and all the people who were with him arrived exhausted,[^8] and he refreshed himself there.

**15** Meanwhile Absalom and all the people, the men of Israel, came to Jerusalem, and Ahithophel was with him.

**16** When Hushai the Archite, David's friend,[^9] came to Absalom, Hushai said to Absalom, "Long live the king! Long live the king!"

**17** Absalom said to Hushai, "Is this your loyalty[^10] to your friend? Why didn't you go with your friend?"

**18** Hushai said to Absalom, "No. The one whom Yahweh and this people and all the men of Israel have chosen, his I will be,[^11] and with him I will stay.

**19** Besides, whom should I serve? Should it not be his son? As I served your father, so I will serve you."[^12]

**20** Then Absalom said to Ahithophel, "Give your advice.[^13] What should we do?"

**21** Ahithophel said to Absalom, "Sleep with[^14] your father's concubines, whom he left to look after the house. Then all Israel will hear that you have made yourself a stench to your father, and all who are with you will be encouraged."[^15]

**22** So they pitched the tent for Absalom on the roof, and Absalom slept with his father's concubines in the sight of all Israel.

**23** In those days the advice Ahithophel gave was like that of someone who inquires of the word of God. That is how all the advice of Ahithophel was regarded, both by David and by Absalom.

[^1]: Hebrew *qayits*, "summer fruit", probably figs or cakes of figs. Also in verse 2.
[^2]: Literally "your master's son", that is, Mephibosheth, the grandson of Saul.
[^3]: Literally "my father's".
[^4]: Literally "man of *beliyya'al*", "man of worthlessness".
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "If he curses, and if Yahweh has told him, 'Curse David' …" but read "So let him curse, for Yahweh has told him, 'Curse David.'"
[^7]: Literally "on my eye", as the Hebrew is read, perhaps meaning "my tears". It is written "on my guilt" or "on my affliction". The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "on my affliction".
[^8]: The Hebrew does not name the place, though it goes on to say "there". Some manuscripts of the Septuagint add "at the Jordan" (compare 17:22).
[^9]: See 15:37.
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "not" but read "his". In Hebrew the two words sound the same (*lo*).
[^12]: Literally "so I will be before you".
[^13]: The Hebrew verb is plural, addressed to Ahithophel and the other counselors.
[^14]: Literally "Go in to", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations. Also in verse 22. Taking a king's concubines was a claim to his throne (compare 3:7; 12:11).
[^15]: Literally "the hands of all who are with you will be strong".
