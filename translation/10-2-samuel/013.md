**1** Some time later this happened. Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister named Tamar, and Amnon son of David fell in love with her.

**2** Amnon was so tormented that he made himself ill over his sister Tamar, for she was a virgin, and it seemed impossible to Amnon to do anything to her.

**3** But Amnon had a friend named Jonadab son of Shimeah, David's brother. Jonadab was a very shrewd[^1] man.

**4** He said to him, "Why are you, the king's son, so haggard morning after morning? Won't you tell me?" Amnon said to him, "I love Tamar, my brother Absalom's sister."

**5** Jonadab said to him, "Lie down on your bed and pretend to be ill. When your father comes to see you, say to him, 'Please let my sister Tamar come and give me something to eat. Let her prepare the food in my sight, so that I may watch and eat it from her hand.'"

**6** So Amnon lay down and pretended to be ill. When the king came to see him, Amnon said to the king, "Please let my sister Tamar come and make a couple of cakes[^2] in my sight, so that I may eat from her hand."

**7** David sent word to Tamar at the palace:[^3] "Please go to your brother Amnon's house and prepare food for him."

**8** So Tamar went to her brother Amnon's house, where he was lying down. She took dough, kneaded it, made cakes in his sight and baked the cakes.

**9** Then she took the pan and set them out before him,[^4] but he refused to eat. Amnon said, "Send everyone out from me." So everyone went out from him.

**10** Then Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the food into the bedroom, so that I may eat from your hand." So Tamar took the cakes she had made and brought them to her brother Amnon in the bedroom.

**11** When she brought them near to him to eat, he grabbed her and said to her, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

**12** She said to him, "No, my brother! Do not violate me![^5] Such a thing is not done in Israel. Do not do this disgraceful thing.[^6]

**13** As for me, where could I take my shame? And you would be like one of the disgraceful fools in Israel. Now please speak to the king, for he will not keep me from you."

**14** But he would not listen to her. He overpowered her and raped her.[^7]

**15** Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred. The hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. Amnon said to her, "Get up! Go!"

**16** She said to him, "No! Sending me away would be a greater wrong than the other thing you have done to me."[^8] But he would not listen to her.

**17** He called the young man who served him and said, "Get this woman out of my sight,[^9] put her outside, and bolt the door behind her."

**18** She was wearing a long robe with sleeves,[^10] for this is the kind of robe the king's virgin daughters wore. His servant put her out and bolted the door behind her.

**19** Tamar put ashes on her head and tore the long robe she was wearing. She put her hand on her head and went away, crying out as she went.

**20** Her brother Absalom said to her, "Has your brother Amnon[^11] been with you? Now, my sister, keep quiet. He is your brother. Don't take this thing to heart." So Tamar lived, a desolate woman, in her brother Absalom's house.

**21** When King David heard about all these things, he was very angry.[^12]

**22** Absalom said nothing to Amnon, either bad or good,[^13] for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated his sister Tamar.

**23** Two full years later, Absalom had sheepshearers at Baal-hazor, which is near Ephraim, and Absalom invited all the king's sons.

**24** Absalom came to the king and said, "Your servant has sheepshearers. Please let the king and his servants come with your servant."

**25** The king said to Absalom, "No, my son, we should not all go, or we would be a burden to you." Absalom urged him, but he was not willing to go, though he gave him his blessing.

**26** Then Absalom said, "If not, please let my brother Amnon go with us." The king said to him, "Why should he go with you?"

**27** But Absalom urged him, and he sent Amnon and all the king's sons with him.[^14]

**28** Absalom commanded his young men, "Watch for when Amnon's heart is merry with wine. When I say to you, 'Strike Amnon,' then kill him. Don't be afraid. Haven't I myself commanded you? Be strong and courageous."[^15]

**29** So Absalom's young men did to Amnon as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king's sons got up, each mounted his mule, and they fled.

**30** While they were on the way, the report came to David: "Absalom has struck down all the king's sons, and not one of them is left."

**31** The king got up, tore his clothes and lay down on the ground, and all his servants stood by with their clothes torn.

**32** But Jonadab son of Shimeah, David's brother, spoke up and said, "My lord should not think that they have killed all the young men, the king's sons. Only Amnon is dead. Absalom has been determined to do this[^16] since the day Amnon violated his sister Tamar.

**33** Now my lord the king should not take the matter to heart and think that all the king's sons are dead. Only Amnon is dead."

**34** Meanwhile Absalom fled. The young man who was keeping watch looked up and saw many people coming on the road behind him,[^17] from the side of the hill.

**35** Jonadab said to the king, "Look, the king's sons have come. It has happened just as your servant said."

**36** Just as he finished speaking, the king's sons came in, and they wept aloud. The king and all his servants also wept very bitterly.

**37** Absalom fled and went to Talmai son of Ammihud,[^18] king of Geshur. And David[^19] mourned for his son every day.

**38** Absalom fled and went to Geshur, and he was there for three years.

**39** And King David longed[^20] to go out to Absalom, for he had been consoled over Amnon's death.

[^1]: Literally "wise".
[^2]: Hebrew *levivot*, a kind of cake. The word sounds like *lev*, "heart", and the verb "make" here is formed from the same word. Also in verses 8 and 10.
[^3]: Literally "to the house".
[^4]: Literally "poured out before him".
[^5]: Hebrew *innah*, "humiliate, violate". Used of sexual relations, it means that he forced her. Also in verses 14, 22 and 32.
[^6]: Hebrew *nevalah*, a disgraceful, senseless act, used of serious sexual crimes (Genesis 34:7; Deuteronomy 22:21). The word for "fools" in verse 13 is from the same root.
[^7]: Literally "He was stronger than she, and he violated her and lay with her."
[^8]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "Do not, for this evil is greater than the other that you did with me, to send me away."
[^9]: Literally "Send this one away from me".
[^10]: Hebrew *ketonet passim*, the kind of robe Jacob gave Joseph (Genesis 37:3). The meaning of *passim* is uncertain; it may mean a long robe with sleeves reaching to the wrists, or a robe with decorations. Also in verse 19.
[^11]: Hebrew *Aminon*, perhaps a scornful form of the name.
[^12]: The Septuagint and 4QSam(a), a Dead Sea Scroll of Samuel, add "but he would not hurt the spirit of his son Amnon, because he loved him, for he was his firstborn".
[^13]: Literally "from bad to good", that is, nothing at all.
[^14]: The Septuagint adds "And Absalom made a feast like a king's feast."
[^15]: Literally "be sons of strength".
[^16]: Literally "for it has been set on Absalom's mouth".
[^17]: The Septuagint reads "coming on the Horonaim road, from the side of the hill. And the watchman came and told the king, 'I have seen men coming from the Horonaim road, from the side of the hill.'"
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "Ammihur" but read "Ammihud".
[^19]: Hebrew "he". The Septuagint reads "King David".
[^20]: Literally "And it longed, King David, to go out to Absalom". The verb is feminine, as if its subject were "spirit". 4QSam(a) and the Septuagint read "And the spirit of the king longed". Some understand the verb as "ceased", so that King David's anger against Absalom came to an end.
