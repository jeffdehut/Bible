**1** After this, Absalom got himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men to run ahead of him.

**2** Absalom would get up early and stand beside the road to the gate. Whenever anyone had a dispute to bring before the king for judgment, Absalom would call to him and say, "What town are you from?" And when he said, "Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel,"

**3** Absalom would say to him, "Look, your case is good and right, but there is no one from the king to hear you."

**4** Absalom would say, "If only someone would appoint me judge in the land! Then anyone who had a dispute or a case could come to me, and I would see that he got justice."

**5** And whenever someone came near to bow down to him, he would reach out his hand, take hold of him and kiss him.

**6** Absalom did this to all the Israelites who came to the king for judgment. So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.

**7** At the end of forty years,[^1] Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go to Hebron and fulfill the vow I made to the LORD.[^2]

**8** For your servant made a vow while I was living at Geshur in Aram: 'If the LORD really brings me back to Jerusalem, I will worship[^3] the LORD.'"

**9** The king said to him, "Go in peace." So he got up and went to Hebron.

**10** Then Absalom sent secret messengers throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "As soon as you hear the sound of the ram's horn, say, 'Absalom has become king in Hebron!'"

**11** Two hundred men from Jerusalem went with Absalom. They had been invited and went in all innocence, knowing nothing about the matter.

**12** While he was offering the sacrifices, Absalom sent for[^4] Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counselor, from his town Giloh. The conspiracy grew strong, and the people with Absalom kept increasing.

**13** A messenger came to David and said, "The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom."

**14** David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, "Get up, let us flee, or none of us will escape from Absalom. Leave quickly, or he will soon overtake us, bring disaster down on us, and strike the city with the edge of the sword."

**15** The king's servants said to the king, "Whatever my lord the king decides, your servants are ready."

**16** So the king set out, with all his household following him.[^5] But the king left ten women, concubines, to look after the house.

**17** The king set out, with all the people following him, and they stopped at the last house.[^6]

**18** All his servants passed by beside him, and all the Cherethites, all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites,[^7] six hundred men who had followed him from Gath, passed on before the king.

**19** The king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why should you go with us too? Go back and stay with the king,[^8] for you are a foreigner, and an exile from your own place as well.

**20** You came only yesterday. Should I make you wander about with us today, while I go wherever I may go? Go back, and take your brothers with you. Faithful love and faithfulness be with you."[^9]

**21** But Ittai answered the king, "As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives, wherever my lord the king may be, whether for death or for life, there your servant will be."

**22** David said to Ittai, "Go on, then, and cross over." So Ittai the Gittite crossed over with all his men and all the little ones who were with him.

**23** The whole land was weeping aloud as all the people crossed over. The king crossed the Kidron Valley, and all the people crossed over toward the road to the wilderness.

**24** Zadok was there too, and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of the covenant of God. They set down[^10] the ark of God, and Abiathar went up,[^11] until all the people had finished crossing over from the city.

**25** Then the king said to Zadok, "Take the ark of God back into the city. If I find favor in the eyes of the LORD, he will bring me back and let me see both it and the place where it dwells.

**26** But if he says, 'I take no pleasure in you,' here I am. Let him do to me whatever seems good to him."

**27** The king also said to Zadok the priest, "Are you not a seer?[^12] Go back to the city in peace, and your two sons with you:[^13] your son Ahimaaz and Jonathan son of Abiathar.

**28** See, I will wait at the plains[^14] of the wilderness until word comes from you to inform me."

**29** So Zadok and Abiathar took the ark of God back to Jerusalem, and they stayed there.

**30** David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went. His head was covered, and he walked barefoot. All the people who were with him covered their heads too, and they went up, weeping as they went.

**31** David was told,[^15] "Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom." David said, "LORD, please turn the advice of Ahithophel into foolishness."

**32** When David came to the summit, where people used to worship God, Hushai the Archite was there to meet him, with his robe torn and dirt on his head.

**33** David said to him, "If you go on with me, you will be a burden to me.

**34** But if you go back to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, O king. I was your father's servant in the past, but now I will be your servant,' then you can defeat the advice of Ahithophel for me.

**35** Won't Zadok and Abiathar the priests be there with you? Whatever you hear from the king's house, tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.

**36** Their two sons are there with them, Ahimaaz son of Zadok and Jonathan son of Abiathar. Send me by them everything you hear."

**37** So Hushai, David's friend,[^16] came to the city, just as Absalom was coming into Jerusalem.

[^1]: Some manuscripts of the Septuagint and the Syriac read "four years".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "serve", here probably by offering sacrifice.
[^4]: Literally "sent".
[^5]: Literally "at his feet". Also in verses 17 and 18.
[^6]: Hebrew *beit ha-merchaq*, "the distant house", perhaps the last house at the edge of the city.
[^7]: Men from the Philistine city of Gath, under the command of Ittai (verse 19).
[^8]: That is, with the new king, Absalom.
[^9]: The Septuagint reads "and may the LORD show you faithful love and faithfulness". *Faithful love* is Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^10]: The Hebrew verb usually means "poured". Here it probably means "set down".
[^11]: Or "offered sacrifices". The Hebrew verb "go up" is also used of offering sacrifices.
[^12]: Hebrew *ha-ro'eh attah*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "Are you a seer?", "Do you see?" or "Look!"
[^13]: In this sentence "your" and "you" are plural, meaning Zadok and Abiathar.
[^14]: The Hebrew text is written "at the fords" but read "at the plains".
[^15]: Literally "And David told, saying". The sense is probably "David was told".
[^16]: Perhaps a title: "the King's Friend", a close adviser (16:16; 1 Kings 4:5).
