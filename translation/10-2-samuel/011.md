**1** In the spring of the year,[^1] at the time when kings go out to battle,[^2] David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel. They ravaged the Ammonites and besieged Rabbah. But David stayed in Jerusalem.

**2** One evening David got up from his bed and walked about on the roof of the king's house. From the roof he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very beautiful.

**3** David sent someone to inquire about the woman, and he said,[^3] "Isn't this Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

**4** David sent messengers and took her. She came to him, and he slept with her. She had been purifying herself from her uncleanness.[^4] Then she went back to her house.

**5** The woman became pregnant, and she sent word to David: "I am pregnant."

**6** So David sent word to Joab: "Send me Uriah the Hittite." And Joab sent Uriah to David.

**7** When Uriah came to him, David asked how Joab was doing, how the troops were doing, and how the war was going.[^5]

**8** Then David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet."[^6] Uriah left the king's house, and a gift from the king was sent after him.

**9** But Uriah slept at the entrance of the king's house with all his lord's servants, and he did not go down to his house.

**10** David was told, "Uriah did not go down to his house." So David said to Uriah, "Haven't you just come from a journey? Why didn't you go down to your house?"

**11** Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in tents,[^7] and my lord Joab and my lord's servants are camping in the open field. Should I then go to my house to eat and drink and sleep with my wife? By your life, by your very life,[^8] I will not do this thing!"

**12** David said to Uriah, "Stay here today as well, and tomorrow I will send you back." So Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.[^9]

**13** David invited him, and he ate and drank with him,[^10] and David made him drunk. But in the evening he went out to lie on his bed with his lord's servants, and he did not go down to his house.

**14** In the morning David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it with Uriah.[^11]

**15** In the letter he wrote, "Put Uriah in the front line, where the fighting is fiercest. Then withdraw from him, so that he will be struck down and die."

**16** So while Joab was besieging[^12] the city, he put Uriah at the place where he knew there were strong warriors.

**17** The men of the city came out and fought against Joab, and some of the troops, some of David's servants, fell. Uriah the Hittite also died.

**18** Joab sent and told David all the news of the battle.

**19** He instructed the messenger, "When you have finished telling the king all the news of the battle,

**20** if the king's anger rises and he says to you, 'Why did you go so near the city to fight? Didn't you know that they would shoot from the wall?

**21** Who killed Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth?[^13] Wasn't it a woman who threw an upper millstone down on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why did you go so near the wall?' then you are to say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too.'"

**22** So the messenger went, and he came and told David everything Joab had sent him to say.[^14]

**23** The messenger said to David, "The men were stronger than we were. They came out against us into the open field, but we drove them back to the entrance of the gate.[^15]

**24** Then the archers shot at your servants from the wall, and some of the king's servants died. Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too."

**25** David said to the messenger, "Say this to Joab: 'Don't let this matter upset you,[^16] for the sword devours one as well as another. Press your attack on the city and overthrow it.' And encourage him."

**26** When Uriah's wife heard that her husband Uriah was dead, she mourned for her husband.

**27** When the time of mourning was over, David sent and brought her into his house. She became his wife and bore him a son. But what David had done was evil in the sight of Yahweh.[^17]

[^1]: Literally "at the return of the year".
[^2]: Hebrew *ha-melakhim*, "the kings", as the word is read and as many Hebrew manuscripts write it. The Leningrad Codex writes it with an extra letter, so that it could be read *ha-mal'akhim*, "the messengers".
[^3]: Or "and someone said".
[^4]: That is, after her monthly period (Leviticus 15:19–28). This explains why she was bathing (verse 2).
[^5]: Literally "asked about the *shalom* (well-being) of Joab, the *shalom* of the troops and the *shalom* of the war".
[^6]: That is, go home and rest. Some see a hint that Uriah should sleep with his wife.
[^7]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths, shelters". Or "at Succoth", a town east of the Jordan.
[^8]: Literally "By your life and by the life of your *nephesh* (soul)", a solemn oath.
[^9]: Or "that day. On the next day David invited him …" (verse 13).
[^10]: Literally "he ate before him and drank".
[^11]: Literally "by the hand of Uriah".
[^12]: Literally "watching" or "keeping guard on".
[^13]: That is, Jerubbaal, another name of Gideon (Judges 6:32; 9:50–54), with *bosheth*, "shame", in place of *Baal*; see 2:8.
[^14]: The Septuagint adds that David was angry with Joab and asked the questions Joab had expected in verses 20–21.
[^15]: Literally "but we were upon them as far as the entrance of the gate".
[^16]: Literally "Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes".
[^17]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
