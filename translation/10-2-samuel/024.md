**1** Again the anger of the LORD[^1] burned against Israel, and he incited David against them, saying, "Go, count Israel and Judah."[^2]

**2** So the king said to Joab, the commander of the army, who was with him, "Go through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, and register the people, so that I may know the number of the people."

**3** Joab said to the king, "May the LORD your God multiply the people a hundred times over, while the eyes of my lord the king can still see it! But why does my lord the king want to do this?"

**4** But the king's word prevailed against Joab and the commanders of the army. So Joab and the commanders of the army went out from the king's presence to register the people of Israel.

**5** They crossed the Jordan and camped at Aroer, south of the town that is in the middle of the valley,[^3] toward Gad and on to Jazer.

**6** Then they came to Gilead and to the land of Tahtim-hodshi,[^4] and they came to Dan-jaan[^5] and around toward Sidon.

**7** They came to the fortress of Tyre and to all the towns of the Hivites and the Canaanites, and they went out to the Negev of Judah at Beersheba.

**8** So they went through the whole land, and at the end of nine months and twenty days they came back to Jerusalem.

**9** Joab gave the king the total from the registration of the people: in Israel there were eight hundred thousand valiant men who could draw the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand.[^6]

**10** But David's conscience troubled him[^7] after he had counted the people. David said to the LORD, "I have sinned greatly in what I have done. Now, LORD, please take away the guilt of your servant, for I have acted very foolishly."

**11** When David got up in the morning, the word of the LORD had come to the prophet Gad, David's seer:

**12** "Go and tell David, 'This is what the LORD says: I am setting[^8] three things before you. Choose one of them, and I will do it to you.'"

**13** So Gad came to David and told him. He said to him, "Shall seven years[^9] of famine come upon you in your land? Or shall you flee for three months before your enemies while they pursue you? Or shall there be three days of plague in your land? Now consider and decide what answer I should take back to the one who sent me."

**14** David said to Gad, "I am in great distress. Let us fall into the hand of the LORD, for his mercy is great, but do not let me fall into human hands."

**15** So the LORD sent a plague on Israel from the morning until the appointed time,[^10] and seventy thousand of the people died, from Dan to Beersheba.

**16** When the angel[^11] stretched out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, the LORD relented concerning the disaster[^12] and said to the angel who was destroying the people, "Enough! Now hold back your hand." The angel of the LORD was then by the threshing floor of Araunah[^13] the Jebusite.

**17** When David saw the angel who was striking down the people, he said to the LORD, "I am the one who has sinned; I am the one who has done wrong.[^14] But these sheep, what have they done? Please let your hand be against me and against my father's house."

**18** That day Gad came to David and said to him, "Go up and set up an altar to the LORD on the threshing floor of Araunah[^15] the Jebusite."

**19** So David went up at Gad's word, as the LORD had commanded.

**20** When Araunah looked down and saw the king and his servants coming over toward him, Araunah went out and bowed down to the king with his face to the ground.

**21** Araunah said, "Why has my lord the king come to his servant?" David said, "To buy the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to the LORD, so that the plague may be held back from the people."

**22** Araunah said to David, "Let my lord the king take it and offer up whatever seems good to him. Look, here are the oxen for the burnt offering, and the threshing sledges and the oxen's yokes[^16] for the wood.

**23** All this, O king, Araunah gives to the king."[^17] And Araunah said to the king, "May the LORD your God accept you."

**24** But the king said to Araunah, "No, I will buy it from you for a price. I will not offer burnt offerings to the LORD my God that cost me nothing." So David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels[^18] of silver.

**25** David built an altar to the LORD there and offered burnt offerings and fellowship offerings.[^19] Then the LORD answered prayers for the land, and the plague was held back from Israel.[^20]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: 1 Chronicles 21:1 reads "Satan rose up against Israel and incited David to count Israel."
[^3]: Probably the Arnon valley.
[^4]: Hebrew *tachtim chodshi*, an unexplained name. Some manuscripts of the Septuagint read "the land of the Hittites, to Kadesh".
[^5]: Hebrew *Danah ya'an*. The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "Dan and Ijon", or simply Dan.
[^6]: 1 Chronicles 21:5 gives 1,100,000 for Israel and 470,000 for Judah.
[^7]: Literally "David's heart struck him".
[^8]: Hebrew *notel*, "lay upon", perhaps "impose". 1 Chronicles 21:10 has "offer".
[^9]: 1 Chronicles 21:12 and the Septuagint read "three years".
[^10]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain: perhaps the end of the set time of three days, or the time of the evening sacrifice.
[^11]: Or "the messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. Also in verse 17.
[^12]: Or "changed his mind about the disaster".
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written *ha-ornah* but read *ha-aravnah*, "Araunah". 1 Chronicles 21:15 calls him Ornan.
[^14]: The Septuagint reads "and I, the shepherd, have done wrong".
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "Araniah" but read "Araunah".
[^16]: Literally "the equipment of the oxen".
[^17]: Literally "The whole, Araunah the king gave to the king." Some take it to mean that Araunah had been the Jebusite king of Jerusalem.
[^18]: A shekel is about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so fifty shekels is about 1¼ pounds (575 grams). In 1 Chronicles 21:25 David pays six hundred shekels of gold for the site.
[^19]: Hebrew *shelamim*; see 6:17.
[^20]: The Septuagint adds "And Solomon later added to the altar, for it was small at first."
