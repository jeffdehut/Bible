**1** Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king's heart was set on Absalom.[^1]

**2** So Joab sent to Tekoa and brought a wise woman from there. He said to her, "Pretend to be a mourner. Put on mourning clothes, and do not anoint yourself with oil. Act like a woman who has been mourning for the dead for a long time.

**3** Then go to the king and speak to him in this way." And Joab put the words in her mouth.

**4** When the woman of Tekoa spoke[^2] to the king, she fell with her face to the ground and bowed down, and she said, "Help, O king!"

**5** The king said to her, "What is the matter?" She said, "Alas, I am a widow. My husband is dead.

**6** Your servant had two sons, and the two of them fought with each other in the field. There was no one to separate them, and one struck the other and killed him.

**7** Now the whole clan has risen against your servant, and they say, 'Hand over the one who struck down his brother, so that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed.' So they would destroy the heir as well. They would put out my one remaining coal, and leave my husband neither name nor survivor on the face of the earth."

**8** The king said to the woman, "Go home, and I will give orders concerning you."

**9** The woman of Tekoa said to the king, "My lord the king, let the guilt be on me and on my father's house, and let the king and his throne be innocent."

**10** The king said, "If anyone says anything to you, bring him to me, and he will never touch you again."

**11** She said, "Please let the king remember the LORD[^3] your God, so that the avenger of blood[^4] may not add to the destruction, and they may not destroy my son." He said, "As surely as the LORD lives, not one hair of your son will fall to the ground."

**12** Then the woman said, "Please let your servant speak a word to my lord the king." He said, "Speak."

**13** The woman said, "Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God? For in saying this, the king is like one who is guilty,[^5] since the king has not brought back his own banished son.

**14** We must all die. We are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. But God does not take away life; he devises ways so that a banished person does not remain banished from him.[^6]

**15** Now I have come to say this to my lord the king because the people made me afraid. Your servant thought, 'I will speak to the king. Perhaps the king will do what his servant asks.

**16** For the king will listen and rescue his servant from the hand of the man who would cut off both me and my son from the inheritance of God.'[^7]

**17** And your servant thought, 'May the word of my lord the king bring me rest, for my lord the king is like the angel of God[^8] in discerning good and evil.'[^9] May the LORD your God be with you!"

**18** Then the king answered the woman, "Do not hide from me anything I ask you." The woman said, "Let my lord the king speak."

**19** The king said, "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?" The woman answered, "As surely as you live,[^10] my lord the king, no one can turn to the right or to the left from anything my lord the king has said. Yes, it was your servant Joab who commanded me, and he put all these words in your servant's mouth.

**20** Your servant Joab did this to change the way things stand.[^11] But my lord is wise, with the wisdom of the angel of God, to know everything that happens in the land."

**21** Then the king said to Joab, "Very well, I will do this. Go, bring back the young man Absalom."

**22** Joab fell with his face to the ground, bowed down and blessed the king. Joab said, "Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your sight, my lord the king, because the king has done what your servant asked."[^12]

**23** So Joab got up, went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

**24** But the king said, "He must go to his own house. He is not to see my face." So Absalom went to his own house and did not see the king's face.

**25** Now in all Israel there was no one so much praised for his beauty as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him.

**26** When he cut the hair of his head (he used to cut it at the end of every year,[^13] because it was heavy on him, and so he cut it), he would weigh the hair of his head: two hundred shekels[^14] by the king's weight.

**27** Three sons and one daughter were born to Absalom. Her name was Tamar, and she was a beautiful woman.[^15]

**28** Absalom lived in Jerusalem for two full years without seeing the king's face.

**29** Then Absalom sent for Joab, to send him to the king, but he would not come to him. He sent again, a second time, but he would not come.

**30** So he said to his servants, "Look, Joab's field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire."[^16] So Absalom's servants set the field on fire.

**31** Then Joab got up and came to Absalom at his house and said to him, "Why have your servants set my field on fire?"

**32** Absalom said to Joab, "Look, I sent word to you: 'Come here, so that I may send you to the king to say, "Why did I come from Geshur? It would be better for me to be there still."' Now let me see the king's face, and if there is guilt in me, let him put me to death."

**33** So Joab went to the king and told him. Then he called Absalom, and he came to the king and bowed down to him with his face to the ground before the king. And the king kissed Absalom.

[^1]: Or "was against Absalom". Literally "the heart of the king was on Absalom".
[^2]: Many Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "came".
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *go'el ha-dam*, the nearest relative, whose duty it was to avenge a killing (Numbers 35:19).
[^5]: Or "the king convicts himself".
[^6]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "And God does not lift a *nephesh* (life), and he devises plans so that the banished one is not banished from him."
[^7]: That is, from Israel, the people and the land that belong to God.
[^8]: Or "the messenger of God". Also in verse 20.
[^9]: Literally "in hearing the good and the bad".
[^10]: Literally "By the life of your *nephesh*".
[^11]: Literally "to turn around the face of the matter".
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "his servant" but read "your servant".
[^13]: Literally "at the end of days to days".
[^14]: A shekel is about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so two hundred shekels is about 5 pounds (2.3 kg).
[^15]: The Septuagint adds "and she became the wife of Rehoboam son of Solomon and bore him Abijah". Compare 18:18, where Absalom has no son.
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "and I will set it on fire" but read "set it on fire".
