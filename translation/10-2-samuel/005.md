**1** All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, "Look, we are your own flesh and blood.[^1]

**2** For some time now,[^2] while Saul was king over us, it was you who led Israel out to battle and brought it back.[^3] And Yahweh[^4] said to you, 'You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be ruler[^5] over Israel.'"

**3** All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before Yahweh, and they anointed David king over Israel.

**4** David was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned for forty years.

**5** In Hebron he reigned over Judah for seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned over all Israel and Judah for thirty-three years.

**6** The king and his men went to Jerusalem, to the Jebusites who lived in the land. They said to David, "You will not get in here. Even the blind and the lame will turn you away."[^6] They meant,[^7] "David cannot get in here."

**7** But David captured the stronghold of Zion, that is, the City of David.

**8** On that day David said, "Whoever would strike down the Jebusites must get up through the water shaft[^8] to reach the lame and the blind, who are hated by David."[^9] That is why they say, "The blind and the lame will not come into the house."[^10]

**9** David lived in the stronghold and called it the City of David. David built all around it, from the Millo[^11] inward.

**10** David became greater and greater, for Yahweh, the God of Armies,[^12] was with him.

**11** Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar logs, carpenters and stonemasons,[^13] and they built a house for David.

**12** Then David knew that Yahweh had established him as king over Israel, and that he had exalted his kingdom for the sake of his people Israel.

**13** After he came from Hebron, David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem, and more sons and daughters were born to David.

**14** These are the names of those born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, Solomon,

**15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, Japhia,

**16** Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.

**17** When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, all the Philistines went up to search for David. David heard about it and went down to the stronghold.

**18** The Philistines had come and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**19** David asked Yahweh, "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will you hand them over to me?" Yahweh said to David, "Go up, for I will certainly hand the Philistines over to you."

**20** So David came to Baal-perazim, and David defeated them there. He said, "Yahweh has broken through my enemies ahead of me, as water breaks through." That is why that place was called Baal-perazim.[^14]

**21** The Philistines abandoned their idols there, and David and his men carried them off.[^15]

**22** Once again the Philistines came up and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**23** When David asked Yahweh, he said, "Do not go up. Circle around behind them and come at them opposite the balsam trees.[^16]

**24** When you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, then act quickly, for then Yahweh will have gone out ahead of you to strike down the army of the Philistines."

**25** David did as Yahweh had commanded him, and he struck down the Philistines from Geba[^17] all the way to Gezer.

[^1]: Literally "your bone and your flesh".
[^2]: Literally "both yesterday and the day before".
[^3]: Literally "you were the one who led out and brought in Israel".
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, ruler", someone designated by God to lead (1 Samuel 9:16). Traditionally "prince" or "captain".
[^6]: Or "You will not get in here unless you remove the blind and the lame." The Hebrew can be read either way.
[^7]: Literally "saying".
[^8]: Hebrew *tsinnor*, a rare word of uncertain meaning, perhaps a water channel or shaft leading up into the city. Some take it as a grappling hook, or as the throat ("must strike at the throat"). 1 Chronicles 11:6 reads "Whoever is first to strike down the Jebusites will become chief and commander."
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "who hate David" but read "who are hated by David". Literally "the *nephesh* of David".
[^10]: "The house" may be the house of Yahweh (the temple) or the king's palace. The Septuagint reads "into the house of Yahweh".
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-millo*, "the filling": probably a terraced structure of stone and earth on the slope of the city.
[^12]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD God of hosts". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^13]: Literally "craftsmen of wood and craftsmen of stone for walls".
[^14]: *Baal-perazim* means "lord of breakings through". Hebrew *perets* is "breaking through".
[^15]: 1 Chronicles 14:12 says that David gave orders and they were burned.
[^16]: Hebrew *bekha'im*, a kind of tree or shrub that cannot be identified with certainty. Also in verse 24.
[^17]: 1 Chronicles 14:16 and the Septuagint read "Gibeon".
