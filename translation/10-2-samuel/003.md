**1** The war between the house of Saul and the house of David went on for a long time. David grew stronger and stronger, while the house of Saul grew weaker and weaker.

**2** Sons were born to David in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam of Jezreel.

**3** His second was Chileab,[^1] by Abigail the widow[^2] of Nabal of Carmel. The third was Absalom son of Maacah, the daughter of Talmai king of Geshur.

**4** The fourth was Adonijah son of Haggith, and the fifth was Shephatiah son of Abital.

**5** The sixth was Ithream, by David's wife Eglah. These were born to David in Hebron.

**6** While there was war between the house of Saul and the house of David, Abner was making himself strong in the house of Saul.

**7** Now Saul had had a concubine[^3] named Rizpah daughter of Aiah. And Ish-bosheth[^4] said to Abner, "Why have you slept with[^5] my father's concubine?"

**8** Abner was very angry at Ish-bosheth's words, and he said, "Am I a dog's head who belongs to Judah?[^6] Today I keep showing faithful love[^7] to the house of your father Saul, to his brothers and to his friends, and I have not handed you over to David. Yet today you charge me with a wrong over this woman!

**9** May God deal with Abner severely, and even more,[^8] if I do not do for David what Yahweh[^9] swore to him:

**10** to transfer the kingdom from the house of Saul, and to set up the throne of David over Israel and over Judah, from Dan to Beersheba."

**11** Ish-bosheth could not answer Abner another word, because he was afraid of him.

**12** Abner sent messengers to David on his own behalf,[^10] saying, "Whose is the land?"[^11] and saying, "Make your covenant with me, and my hand will be with you to bring all Israel over to you."

**13** David said, "Good. I will make a covenant with you. But I ask one thing of you: you will not see my face unless you first bring Michal daughter of Saul when you come to see my face."

**14** Then David sent messengers to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, saying, "Give me my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to myself for a hundred Philistine foreskins."

**15** So Ish-bosheth sent and took her from her husband, from Paltiel son of Laish.[^12]

**16** But her husband went with her, weeping as he followed her all the way to Bahurim. Then Abner said to him, "Go back!" And he went back.

**17** Abner conferred with the elders of Israel, saying, "For some time now[^13] you have been wanting David as king over you.

**18** Now do it! For Yahweh has said of David, 'By the hand of my servant David I will save[^14] my people Israel from the hand of the Philistines and from the hand of all their enemies.'"

**19** Abner also spoke directly to the Benjaminites.[^15] Then Abner also went to speak directly to David in Hebron about everything that Israel and the whole house of Benjamin had agreed to.[^16]

**20** When Abner came to David at Hebron, with twenty men, David held a feast for Abner and for the men who were with him.

**21** Abner said to David, "Let me go at once and gather all Israel to my lord the king, so that they may make a covenant with you, and you will reign over all that you desire." So David sent Abner away, and he went in peace.

**22** Just then David's servants and Joab came in from a raid, bringing a great amount of plunder with them. But Abner was no longer with David in Hebron, because David had sent him away, and he had gone in peace.

**23** When Joab and all the army that was with him arrived, Joab was told, "Abner son of Ner came to the king, and he sent him away, and he has gone in peace."

**24** Joab went to the king and said, "What have you done? Abner came to you! Why did you send him away, so that he is now gone?

**25** You know Abner son of Ner. He came to deceive you, to find out your going out and your coming in,[^17] and to find out everything you are doing."

**26** Joab left David and sent messengers after Abner, and they brought him back from the cistern of Sirah. But David did not know.

**27** When Abner returned to Hebron, Joab took him aside into the gateway to speak with him privately, and there he struck him in the stomach.[^18] So he died for the blood of Joab's brother Asahel.[^19]

**28** Afterward, when David heard of it, he said, "I and my kingdom are innocent before Yahweh for ever of the blood of Abner son of Ner.

**29** May it fall[^20] on the head of Joab and on all his father's house. May Joab's house never be without someone who has a discharge or a skin disease,[^21] or who holds a spindle,[^22] or who falls by the sword, or who lacks bread."

**30** So Joab and his brother Abishai killed Abner, because he had killed their brother Asahel at Gibeon in the battle.

**31** Then David said to Joab and to all the people who were with him, "Tear your clothes, put on sackcloth, and mourn before Abner." And King David walked behind the bier.

**32** They buried Abner in Hebron. The king wept aloud at Abner's grave, and all the people wept.

**33** The king sang a lament over Abner and said, "Should Abner have died as a fool dies?[^23]

**34** Your hands were not bound; your feet were not put in bronze chains. As one falls before the wicked,[^24] you fell." And all the people wept over him again.

**35** Then all the people came to urge David to eat some food[^25] while it was still day, but David swore, "May God deal with me severely, and even more, if I taste bread or anything else before the sun goes down."

**36** All the people took note, and it pleased them, just as everything the king did pleased all the people.

**37** So all the people and all Israel knew that day that the king had no part in the killing of Abner son of Ner.

**38** The king said to his servants, "Don't you know that a commander and a great man has fallen in Israel today?

**39** Today I am weak, even though I am anointed king,[^26] and these men, the sons of Zeruiah, are too harsh for me. May Yahweh repay the one who does evil according to his evil!"

[^1]: In 1 Chronicles 3:1 he is called Daniel.
[^2]: Literally "the wife".
[^3]: A wife of lower rank, often a slave. To take a dead king's concubine could be a claim to his throne (16:21–22).
[^4]: Hebrew "he". The name is supplied from verse 8. On the name *Ish-bosheth*, see 2:8.
[^5]: Literally "gone in to", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^6]: A term of contempt. Abner asks whether Ish-bosheth takes him for a worthless traitor who serves Judah.
[^7]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^8]: Literally "Thus may God do to Abner, and thus may he add to him", a solemn oath calling down punishment on oneself if one does not keep one's word. Also in verse 35.
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^10]: Hebrew *tachtav*, literally "in his place". The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "on his own behalf" or "at once".
[^11]: That is, perhaps, "Who controls the land, if not I?" or "To whom should the land belong, if not to you?"
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "Lush" but read "Laish".
[^13]: Literally "both yesterday and the day before".
[^14]: The Hebrew reads "he saved" or "to save" (*hoshia*). Many Hebrew manuscripts and the Septuagint read "I will save" (*oshia*).
[^15]: Literally "spoke in the ears of Benjamin". Also later in the verse, "in the ears of David".
[^16]: Literally "all that was good in the eyes of Israel and in the eyes of the whole house of Benjamin".
[^17]: That is, all your movements and plans.
[^18]: Hebrew *chomesh*; see 2:23.
[^19]: Literally "for the blood of Asahel his brother".
[^20]: Literally "May it whirl" or "May it burst", that is, the guilt for Abner's blood.
[^21]: Hebrew *metsora*, someone with *tsara'at*, traditionally "leprosy": a range of skin conditions that made a person ritually unclean (Leviticus 13–14).
[^22]: Hebrew *pelekh*, a spindle, probably meaning a man fit only for what was then women's work. Some take it to mean a crutch.
[^23]: Hebrew *naval*, a senseless or godless person. The word is the name Nabal (1 Samuel 25:25).
[^24]: Literally "sons of injustice".
[^25]: Literally "bread".
[^26]: Literally "I am tender and anointed king".
