**1** After this, the king of the Ammonites died, and his son Hanun became king in his place.

**2** David said, "I will show faithful love[^1] to Hanun son of Nahash, as his father showed faithful love to me." So David sent his servants to console him about his father. When David's servants came to the land of the Ammonites,

**3** the Ammonite commanders said to their lord Hanun, "Do you think David is honoring your father because he has sent you comforters? Hasn't David sent his servants to you to explore the city, to spy it out and to overthrow it?"

**4** So Hanun took David's servants, shaved off half of each man's beard, cut off their garments in the middle, as far as their buttocks, and sent them away.

**5** When David was told, he sent people to meet them, for the men were deeply humiliated. The king said, "Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown, and then come back."

**6** When the Ammonites saw that they had made themselves a stench to David,[^2] the Ammonites sent and hired the Arameans of Beth-rehob and the Arameans of Zobah, twenty thousand foot soldiers, and the king of Maacah with a thousand men, and the men of Tob,[^3] twelve thousand men.

**7** When David heard of it, he sent Joab with the whole army of warriors.[^4]

**8** The Ammonites came out and drew up in battle formation at the entrance of the city gate, while the Arameans of Zobah and Rehob, and the men of Tob and Maacah, were by themselves in the open country.

**9** When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and behind, he chose some of the best men of Israel and drew them up against the Arameans.

**10** The rest of the troops he put under the command of his brother Abishai, and he drew them up against the Ammonites.

**11** He said, "If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you are to come and rescue me. But if the Ammonites are too strong for you, I will come to rescue you.

**12** Be strong, and let us show ourselves strong for the sake of our people and for the cities of our God. And may Yahweh[^5] do what seems good to him."

**13** So Joab and the troops who were with him advanced to fight against the Arameans, and they fled before him.

**14** When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they fled before Abishai and went into the city. Then Joab returned from fighting the Ammonites and came to Jerusalem.

**15** When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they gathered together.

**16** Hadadezer sent and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River,[^6] and they came to Helam,[^7] with Shobach the commander of Hadadezer's army at their head.

**17** When David was told, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan and came to Helam. The Arameans drew up in battle formation to meet David and fought with him.

**18** But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven hundred of the Aramean charioteers[^8] and forty thousand horsemen. He also struck down Shobach the commander of their army, and he died there.

**19** When all the kings who were subject[^9] to Hadadezer saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with Israel and became subject to them. So the Arameans were afraid to help the Ammonites any more.

[^1]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love, here the loyalty owed between allies.
[^2]: That is, they had made David their bitter enemy.
[^3]: Or "Ish-tob". Hebrew *ish tov*, "the man of Tob", meaning its men. Also in verse 8. According to 1 Chronicles 19:6–7, the Ammonites also hired 32,000 chariots.
[^4]: Literally "all the army, the mighty men".
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: That is, the Euphrates.
[^7]: Or "and their army came". The same Hebrew letters can be read either way. 1 Chronicles 19:16 has "Shophach" for "Shobach".
[^8]: Literally "seven hundred chariots", meaning the men in them. 1 Chronicles 19:18 reads "seven thousand charioteers and forty thousand foot soldiers".
[^9]: Literally "the servants of Hadadezer".
