**1** The king was shaken.[^1] He went up to the room over the gate and wept. As he went, he said, "My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! If only I had died instead of you! Absalom, my son, my son!"

**2** Joab was told, "The king is weeping and mourning for Absalom."

**3** So the victory[^2] that day was turned into mourning for all the troops, because the troops heard that day, "The king is grieving for his son."

**4** The troops stole into the city that day, as troops steal in when they are ashamed because they have fled in battle.

**5** The king covered his face, and the king cried out with a loud voice, "My son Absalom! Absalom, my son, my son!"

**6** Then Joab came into the house to the king and said, "Today you have shamed all your servants, who have saved your life today, and the lives of your sons and your daughters, the lives of your wives and the lives of your concubines,

**7** by loving those who hate you and hating those who love you. For you have made it clear today that commanders and servants mean nothing to you. For I know today that if[^3] Absalom were alive and all of us were dead today, then it would be right in your eyes.

**8** Now get up, go out and speak kindly to your servants.[^4] For I swear by the LORD[^5] that if you do not go out, not one man will stay with you tonight. That will be worse for you than all the trouble that has come upon you from your youth until now."

**9** So the king got up and sat in the gate. When all the troops were told, "Look, the king is sitting in the gate," all the troops came before the king. Meanwhile the Israelites had fled, each to his home.[^6]

**10** All the people were arguing throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "The king rescued us from the hand of our enemies, and he saved us from the hand of the Philistines. But now he has fled from the land because of Absalom.

**11** And Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has died in battle. So now, why are you saying nothing about bringing the king back?"

**12** King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar the priests: "Speak to the elders of Judah and say, 'Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house? What all Israel is saying has reached the king at his house.[^7]

**13** You are my brothers; you are my own flesh and blood.[^8] Why should you be the last to bring the king back?'

**14** And say to Amasa, 'Are you not my own flesh and blood? May God deal with me, and more severely,[^9] if you are not commander of my army from now on,[^10] in place of Joab.'"

**15** So he won over the hearts of all the men of Judah as one man, and they sent word to the king: "Come back, you and all your servants."

**16** So the king came back as far as the Jordan. Judah had come to Gilgal to go and meet the king and to bring the king across the Jordan.

**17** Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah to meet King David.

**18** With him were a thousand men from Benjamin, and Ziba, the steward of the house of Saul, with his fifteen sons and his twenty servants. They rushed down to the Jordan ahead of the king.

**19** They crossed the ford[^11] to bring the king's household over and to do whatever he wished. Shimei son of Gera fell down before the king as he was crossing the Jordan.

**20** He said to the king, "Let not my lord hold me guilty. Do not remember how your servant did wrong on the day my lord the king left[^12] Jerusalem. Let not the king take it to heart.

**21** For your servant knows that I have sinned. But look, today I have come, the first of all the house of Joseph,[^13] to come down to meet my lord the king."

**22** Abishai son of Zeruiah answered, "Shouldn't Shimei be put to death for this, because he cursed the LORD's anointed?"[^14]

**23** But David said, "What do I have to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah, that you should become an adversary[^15] to me today? Should anyone be put to death in Israel today? Don't I know that today I am king over Israel?"

**24** The king said to Shimei, "You will not die." And the king swore an oath to him.

**25** Mephibosheth, the grandson[^16] of Saul, also came down to meet the king. He had not cared for his feet or trimmed his mustache or washed his clothes from the day the king left until the day he came back in peace.

**26** When he came to Jerusalem[^17] to meet the king, the king said to him, "Why didn't you go with me, Mephibosheth?"

**27** He said, "My lord the king, my servant deceived me. Your servant said, 'Let me saddle the donkey for myself,[^18] so that I may ride on it and go with the king,' for your servant is lame.

**28** He has slandered your servant to my lord the king. But my lord the king is like the angel of God.[^19] So do whatever seems good to you.

**29** For all my father's house were nothing but men doomed to death before my lord the king, yet you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What right do I have, then, to cry out to the king any more?"

**30** The king said to him, "Why do you go on talking? I have decided: you and Ziba are to divide the land."

**31** Mephibosheth said to the king, "Let him take it all, now that my lord the king has come home in peace."

**32** Barzillai the Gileadite had come down from Rogelim, and he crossed the Jordan with the king, to send him on his way across the Jordan.[^20]

**33** Barzillai was very old, eighty years old. He had provided for the king while he stayed at Mahanaim, for he was a very wealthy man.

**34** The king said to Barzillai, "Cross over with me, and I will provide for you at my side in Jerusalem."

**35** But Barzillai said to the king, "How many more years do I have to live,[^21] that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

**36** I am now eighty years old. Can I tell the difference between good and bad? Can your servant taste what I eat or what I drink? Can I still listen to the voices of men and women singing? Why then should your servant be an added burden to my lord the king?

**37** Your servant will go only a little way across the Jordan with the king. Why should the king repay me with such a reward?

**38** Please let your servant go back, so that I may die in my own town, near the grave of my father and my mother. But here is your servant Chimham.[^22] Let him cross over with my lord the king, and do for him whatever seems good to you."

**39** The king said, "Chimham will cross over with me, and I will do for him whatever seems good to you. And anything you ask of me I will do for you."

**40** Then all the people crossed the Jordan, and the king crossed over. The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he went back to his own place.

**41** The king went on to Gilgal, and Chimham went on with him. All the troops of Judah brought the king over,[^23] and also half the troops of Israel.

**42** Then all the men of Israel came to the king and said to the king, "Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, and all David's men with him?"

**43** All the men of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is our close relative.[^24] Why are you angry about this? Have we eaten anything at the king's expense? Or has any gift been given to us?"[^25]

**44** The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, "We have ten shares in the king, and we have more claim on David than you do.[^26] Why then did you treat us with contempt? Weren't we the first to speak of bringing back our king?"[^27] But the words of the men of Judah were harsher than the words of the men of Israel.

[^1]: Verse 1 is 18:33 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–44 are 19:1–43.
[^2]: Or "the deliverance". Hebrew *teshu'ah*.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "not" (*lo*) but read "if" (*lu*).
[^4]: Literally "speak to the heart of your servants".
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: Literally "to his tents".
[^7]: The Septuagint has the words "What all Israel is saying has reached the king" at the end of verse 11 (19:10 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: Literally "my bone and my flesh". Also in verse 14.
[^9]: Literally "Thus may God do to me, and thus may he add", a solemn oath calling down punishment on oneself if one does not keep one's word.
[^10]: Literally "before me all the days".
[^11]: Hebrew *avarah*, "crossing", perhaps a ford or a ferry boat. Literally "And the crossing crossed".
[^12]: In the Hebrew text, dots are written over this word, a scribal mark of doubt.
[^13]: Here the northern tribes of Israel, of which Joseph's descendants, Ephraim and Manasseh, were the largest.
[^14]: Hebrew *meshiach YHWH*: the king anointed at the LORD's command (1:14).
[^15]: Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, opponent".
[^16]: Literally "son".
[^17]: Literally "When he came Jerusalem", perhaps "When he came from Jerusalem".
[^18]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "Saddle the donkey for me", an order to Ziba.
[^19]: Or "the messenger of God".
[^20]: The Hebrew text is written "to send him on his way at the Jordan" but read "to send him on his way across the Jordan".
[^21]: Literally "How many are the days of the years of my life".
[^22]: Probably Barzillai's son (compare 1 Kings 2:7). Also in verses 39 and 41.
[^23]: The Hebrew text is written "crossed over with the king" but read "brought the king over".
[^24]: Literally "is near to me".
[^25]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "Or has a carrying been carried to us?"
[^26]: Literally "Ten hands I have in the king, and also in David I am more than you." The Septuagint reads "and I am the firstborn rather than you".
[^27]: Literally "Was not my word first to bring back my king?"
