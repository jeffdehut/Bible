**1** Once more David gathered[^1] all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand.

**2** David and all the people who were with him set out and went from Baale-judah[^2] to bring up from there the ark[^3] of God, which is called by the Name, the name of the LORD of Armies,[^4] who is enthroned on the cherubim.[^5]

**3** They set the ark of God on a new cart and brought it from the house of Abinadab, which was on the hill. Uzzah and Ahio,[^6] the sons of Abinadab, were driving the new cart.

**4** They brought it from the house of Abinadab, which was on the hill, with the ark of God,[^7] and Ahio was walking in front of the ark.

**5** David and the whole house of Israel were celebrating before the LORD with all kinds of instruments made of cypress wood,[^8] with lyres, harps, tambourines, rattles[^9] and cymbals.

**6** When they came to the threshing floor of Nacon,[^10] Uzzah reached out to the ark of God and took hold of it, because the oxen stumbled.

**7** The LORD's anger burned against Uzzah, and God struck him down there for his irreverence,[^11] and he died there beside the ark of God.

**8** David was angry[^12] because the LORD had broken out against Uzzah, and that place is called Perez-uzzah[^13] to this day.

**9** David was afraid of the LORD that day, and he said, "How can the ark of the LORD come to me?"

**10** So David was not willing to move the ark of the LORD to be with him in the City of David. Instead, David took it aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.[^14]

**11** The ark of the LORD stayed in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months, and the LORD blessed Obed-edom and all his household.

**12** King David was told, "The LORD has blessed the household of Obed-edom and everything he has because of the ark of God." So David went and brought up the ark of God from the house of Obed-edom to the City of David with rejoicing.

**13** When those who carried the ark of the LORD had gone six steps, he sacrificed an ox and a fattened calf.

**14** David danced before the LORD with all his might, and David was wearing a linen ephod.[^15]

**15** So David and the whole house of Israel brought up the ark of the LORD with shouting and with the sound of the ram's horn.

**16** As the ark of the LORD was coming into the City of David, Michal daughter of Saul looked down through the window. She saw King David leaping and dancing before the LORD, and she despised him in her heart.

**17** They brought in the ark of the LORD and set it in its place inside the tent that David had pitched for it. Then David offered burnt offerings before the LORD, and fellowship offerings.[^16]

**18** When David had finished offering the burnt offerings and the fellowship offerings, he blessed the people in the name of the LORD of Armies.

**19** He distributed to all the people, to the whole crowd of Israel, both men and women, to each one a ring-shaped loaf of bread, a date cake[^17] and a raisin cake. Then all the people went away, each to his own home.

**20** When David returned to bless his household, Michal daughter of Saul came out to meet David and said, "How the king of Israel has honored himself today, uncovering himself today in the sight of his servants' slave girls, as any worthless fellow would shamelessly uncover himself!"

**21** David said to Michal, "It was before the LORD, who chose me rather than your father or anyone in his house, and appointed me ruler[^18] over the people of the LORD, over Israel. I will celebrate before the LORD.[^19]

**22** I will make myself even more contemptible than this, and I will be humble in my own eyes. But by the slave girls you spoke of, by them I will be held in honor."

**23** And Michal daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death.

[^1]: Literally "David again added". The verb may be read as "gathered".
[^2]: Hebrew *mi-ba'alei Yehudah*, "from Baale-judah" or "from the lords of Judah". 1 Chronicles 13:6 reads "to Baalah, that is, to Kiriath-jearim, which belongs to Judah". The ark had been kept at Kiriath-jearim (1 Samuel 7:1–2).
[^3]: Hebrew *aron*, "chest": the ark of the covenant, the gold-covered chest that held the tablets of the covenant (Exodus 25:10–22). After its return from the Philistines it had stayed at Kiriath-jearim for many years (1 Samuel 7:1–2).
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 18. The Hebrew repeats "name": "called by the name, the name of the LORD of Armies".
[^5]: Or "who sits above the cherubim". Hebrew *keruvim*: winged heavenly beings. Two gold cherubim stood on the cover of the ark (Exodus 25:18–22).
[^6]: The same Hebrew letters can be read *achyo*, "his brother". Also in verse 4.
[^7]: The first part of this verse repeats words from verse 3, perhaps by a copying error.
[^8]: Literally "with all woods of cypresses". 1 Chronicles 13:8, 4QSam(a) (a Dead Sea Scroll of Samuel) and the Septuagint read "with all their might and with songs".
[^9]: Hebrew *mena'an'im*, probably rattles or sistrums, instruments that were shaken.
[^10]: 1 Chronicles 13:9 has "Chidon". The Hebrew says only that Uzzah "reached out"; 1 Chronicles 13:9 reads "reached out his hand".
[^11]: Hebrew *al ha-shal*, a phrase of uncertain meaning, perhaps "for his error" or "for his irreverence". 1 Chronicles 13:10 reads "because he put out his hand to the ark".
[^12]: Or "distressed". Literally "it burned to David".
[^13]: *Perez-uzzah* means "breaking out against Uzzah".
[^14]: That is, a man from Gath. Also in verse 11.
[^15]: Hebrew *ephod*, here a simple linen garment worn by priests serving at the sanctuary (1 Samuel 2:18; 22:18), not the high priest's richly woven ephod (Exodus 28:6–14).
[^16]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". Most of the meat of these sacrifices was eaten by the worshipers (Leviticus 3; 7:11–21). Also in verse 18.
[^17]: Hebrew *eshpar*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps a cake of dates, or a portion of meat.
[^18]: Hebrew *nagid*; see 5:2.
[^19]: The Hebrew is abrupt: "Before the LORD, who chose me … over Israel, and I will celebrate before the LORD."
