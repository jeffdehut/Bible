**1** After this, David asked Yahweh,[^1] "Shall I go up to one of the towns of Judah?" Yahweh said to him, "Go up." David said, "Where shall I go?" He said, "To Hebron."

**2** So David went up there, with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail the widow[^2] of Nabal of Carmel.

**3** David also brought up the men who were with him, each with his household, and they settled in the towns around Hebron.

**4** Then the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. When David was told, "It was the men of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul,"

**5** David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "May Yahweh bless you, because you showed this faithful love[^3] to your lord Saul and buried him.

**6** Now may Yahweh show you faithful love and faithfulness. I too will do good to you, because you have done this.[^4]

**7** Now be strong and courageous,[^5] for your lord Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them."

**8** Now Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul's army, had taken Ish-bosheth[^6] son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim.

**9** He made him king over Gilead, the Ashurites[^7] and Jezreel, and over Ephraim, Benjamin and all Israel.

**10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he reigned for two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

**11** The length of time that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

**12** Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

**13** Joab son of Zeruiah and David's servants also went out, and they met them at the pool of Gibeon. They sat down, one group on one side of the pool and the other on the other side.

**14** Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men get up and hold a contest[^8] in front of us." Joab said, "Let them get up."

**15** So they got up and went over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David's servants.

**16** Each man grabbed his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent's side, and they fell down together. So that place, which is in Gibeon, was called Helkath-hazzurim.[^9]

**17** The battle was very fierce that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were defeated by David's servants.

**18** The three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel was as fast on his feet as a wild gazelle.[^10]

**19** Asahel chased Abner, and he did not turn to the right or to the left from following Abner.

**20** Abner looked behind him and said, "Is that you, Asahel?" He said, "It is."

**21** Abner said to him, "Turn aside to your right or to your left. Grab one of the young men and take his gear[^11] for yourself." But Asahel would not turn aside from following him.

**22** Abner said again to Asahel, "Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground? How could I then look your brother Joab in the face?"

**23** But he refused to turn aside. So Abner struck him in the stomach[^12] with the butt of his spear, and the spear came out of his back. He fell there and died on the spot. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stood still.

**24** But Joab and Abishai chased Abner. As the sun was going down, they came to the hill of Ammah, which is opposite Giah on the way to the wilderness of Gibeon.

**25** The Benjaminites rallied behind Abner and formed a single band, and they took their stand on the top of a hill.

**26** Abner called to Joab, "Must the sword go on devouring for ever? Don't you know that it will be bitter in the end? How long will it be before you tell the troops to turn back from chasing their brothers?"

**27** Joab said, "As God lives, if you had not spoken, the troops would not have given up chasing their brothers until morning."[^13]

**28** Then Joab blew the ram's horn, and all the troops stopped. They did not chase Israel any longer, and they fought no more.

**29** Abner and his men marched through the Arabah[^14] all that night. They crossed the Jordan, went through the whole Bithron,[^15] and came to Mahanaim.

**30** Joab came back from chasing Abner and gathered all the troops. Nineteen of David's servants were missing, besides Asahel.

**31** But David's servants had struck down the men of Benjamin, Abner's men, and 360 of them had died.

**32** They took Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb in Bethlehem. Then Joab and his men marched all night, and day broke for them at Hebron.

[^1]: Literally "asked of Yahweh", that is, sought an answer from Yahweh, probably by the sacred lots of the priest (1 Samuel 23:9–12). *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "the wife".
[^3]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 6.
[^4]: Literally "And I also will do this good with you, because you have done this thing."
[^5]: Literally "let your hands be strong, and be sons of strength".
[^6]: Hebrew *Ish-boshet*, "man of shame". In 1 Chronicles 8:33 and 9:39 he is called Eshbaal, "man of Baal" or "Baal exists". *Boshet*, "shame", seems to have been put in place of the name of the Canaanite god Baal (compare Jeremiah 11:13), though *baal* is also an ordinary word meaning "lord".
[^7]: Hebrew "the Ashurite". This may mean the tribe of Asher, as the Aramaic Targum understands it. The Syriac and the Vulgate read "Geshur".
[^8]: Literally "play", meaning here a contest of arms.
[^9]: *Helkath-hazzurim* probably means "field of flints" or "field of blades". The Septuagint reads "field of the plotters".
[^10]: Literally "like one of the gazelles in the open country".
[^11]: Hebrew *chalitsah*: what is stripped from a fallen soldier, his weapons and armor.
[^12]: Hebrew *chomesh*, probably "the belly". Traditionally "the fifth rib".
[^13]: Literally "then from the morning the troops would have gone up, each from after his brother." Or "if you had not spoken (as in verse 14), the troops would have stopped chasing their brothers this morning".
[^14]: Hebrew *aravah*: the deep valley of the Jordan.
[^15]: Hebrew *ha-bitron*, perhaps "the ravine", the name of a valley or region east of the Jordan. Some understand it as "the whole morning".
