**1** These are the last words of David: "The declaration[^1] of David son of Jesse, the declaration of the man who was raised on high,[^2] the anointed of the God of Jacob, the sweet singer of the songs of Israel:[^3]

**2** The Spirit of the LORD[^4] spoke through me; his word was on my tongue.

**3** The God of Israel spoke; the Rock of Israel said to me: 'One who rules over people in righteousness, who rules in the fear of God,

**4** is like the light of morning when the sun rises, a morning without clouds, when the grass springs from the earth through sunshine after rain.'[^5]

**5** Is not my house like this with God?[^6] For he has made an everlasting covenant with me, ordered in every part and secured. Will he not make all my salvation and all my desire grow?

**6** But the worthless[^7] are all like thorns that are tossed away, for they cannot be picked up by hand.

**7** Whoever touches them must arm himself with iron or the shaft of a spear, and they are utterly burned up with fire where they lie."[^8]

**8** These are the names of David's mighty warriors: Josheb-basshebeth[^9] the Tahchemonite, the chief of the officers.[^10] He was Adino the Eznite, who killed eight hundred men at one time.[^11]

**9** Next to him was Eleazar son of Dodo,[^12] son of Ahohi,[^13] one of the three mighty warriors with David when they taunted the Philistines who were gathered there for battle. The men of Israel withdrew,

**10** but he stood his ground and struck down the Philistines until his hand grew tired and stuck to the sword. The LORD brought about a great victory that day, and the troops came back after him only to strip the dead.

**11** Next to him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. The Philistines had gathered together at Lehi,[^14] where there was a plot of ground full of lentils, and the troops fled from the Philistines.

**12** But he took his stand in the middle of the plot, defended it and struck down the Philistines. And the LORD brought about a great victory.

**13** Three[^15] of the Thirty,[^16] the chiefs, went down at harvest time[^17] and came to David at the cave of Adullam, while a band of Philistines was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.

**14** At that time David was in the stronghold, and a Philistine garrison was then at Bethlehem.

**15** David felt a craving and said, "If only someone would give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate!"

**16** So the three mighty warriors broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate, and carried it back to David. But he would not drink it. He poured it out to the LORD

**17** and said, "Far be it from me, LORD, that I should do this! Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?"[^18] So he would not drink it. These were the deeds of the three mighty warriors.

**18** Abishai, the brother of Joab son of Zeruiah, was chief of the Three.[^19] He wielded his spear against three hundred men and killed them, and he won a name among the Three.

**19** Was he not the most honored of the Three?[^20] He became their commander, but he did not attain to the Three.

**20** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was the son of a valiant man[^21] from Kabzeel, a man of great deeds. He struck down two champions[^22] of Moab. He also went down and killed a lion inside a pit on a snowy day.

**21** He also struck down an Egyptian, a man of impressive appearance. The Egyptian had a spear in his hand, but Benaiah went down to him with a club, snatched the spear out of the Egyptian's hand and killed him with his own spear.

**22** These were the deeds of Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he won a name among the three mighty warriors.

**23** He was more honored than the Thirty, but he did not attain to the Three. And David put him in charge of his bodyguard.[^23]

**24** Among the Thirty were Asahel the brother of Joab; Elhanan son of Dodo, of Bethlehem;

**25** Shammah the Harodite; Elika the Harodite;

**26** Helez the Paltite; Ira son of Ikkesh, the Tekoite;

**27** Abiezer the Anathothite; Mebunnai[^24] the Hushathite;

**28** Zalmon the Ahohite; Maharai the Netophathite;

**29** Heleb[^25] son of Baanah, the Netophathite; Ittai son of Ribai, from Gibeah of the Benjaminites;

**30** Benaiah the Pirathonite; Hiddai of the ravines of Gaash;

**31** Abi-albon the Arbathite; Azmaveth the Barhumite;

**32** Eliahba the Shaalbonite; the sons of Jashen; Jonathan;[^26]

**33** Shammah the Hararite; Ahiam son of Sharar the Hararite;[^27]

**34** Eliphelet son of Ahasbai, the son of the Maacathite; Eliam son of Ahithophel, the Gilonite;

**35** Hezrai[^28] the Carmelite; Paarai the Arbite;

**36** Igal son of Nathan, from Zobah; Bani the Gadite;

**37** Zelek the Ammonite; Naharai the Beerothite, the armor-bearer[^29] of Joab son of Zeruiah;

**38** Ira the Ithrite; Gareb the Ithrite;

**39** and Uriah the Hittite. Thirty-seven in all.

[^1]: Hebrew *ne'um*, a word often used of a prophet's message: "declaration, oracle".
[^2]: Hebrew *huqam al*, "raised up on high". 4QSam(a), a Dead Sea Scroll of Samuel, reads "whom God raised up".
[^3]: Literally "the pleasant one of the songs of Israel". Or "the favorite of the Strong One of Israel", taking *zemirot* in the sense "strength".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "and like the light of morning the sun rises, a morning without clouds; from brightness, from rain, grass from the earth".
[^6]: Or "Truly my house is not so with God."
[^7]: Hebrew *beliyya'al*, "worthlessness": the wicked.
[^8]: Literally "in the sitting", perhaps meaning "on the spot".
[^9]: Hebrew *yoshev ba-shevet*, "one who sits in the seat", probably a damaged form of the name. 1 Chronicles 11:11 has "Jashobeam", and some manuscripts of the Septuagint have "Ishbosheth".
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-shalishi*, perhaps "the officers" (literally "third men", perhaps the third man in a chariot). Many take it as "the Three" (verses 18–23).
[^11]: The Hebrew of this sentence is difficult. Literally "He, Adino the Eznite, over eight hundred slain at one time." The Hebrew text is written *ha-etsno* but read *ha-etsni*, "the Eznite". 1 Chronicles 11:11 reads "He wielded his spear against three hundred, whom he killed at one time" (compare verse 18).
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "Dodai" but read "Dodo".
[^13]: Or "the Ahohite", as in 1 Chronicles 11:12 (compare verse 28).
[^14]: Hebrew *la-chayyah*, "into a troop", or "at Lehi" (Judges 15:9). The same word is translated "band" in verse 13.
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "Thirty" but read "Three".
[^16]: David's elite company of warriors, whose names are listed in verses 24–39.
[^17]: Hebrew *el qatsir*, "to the harvest". 1 Chronicles 11:15 has "to the rock".
[^18]: Literally "The blood of the men who went with their lives?" 1 Chronicles 11:19 reads "Shall I drink the blood of these men, who went at the risk of their lives?"
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written *ha-shelishi*, which may mean "the officers" (as in verse 8), but read "the Three". Also in verse 19.
[^20]: Literally "Of the Three, was he not honored?" Since in the second half of the verse Abishai does not attain to the Three, many think "the Thirty" is meant, as in verse 23.
[^21]: The Hebrew text is written "a living man" but read "a valiant man".
[^22]: Hebrew *ari'el*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "champion". The Septuagint reads "the two sons of Ariel of Moab".
[^23]: Hebrew *mishma'ato*, literally "those who obey him", perhaps his personal guard or council.
[^24]: Called Sibbecai in 21:18 and in 1 Chronicles 11:29.
[^25]: 1 Chronicles 11:30 has "Heled".
[^26]: The Hebrew is probably damaged. 1 Chronicles 11:34 reads "the sons of Hashem the Gizonite, Jonathan son of Shagee the Hararite".
[^27]: Hebrew *ha-arari*, probably another spelling of *ha-harari*, "the Hararite".
[^28]: The Hebrew text is written "Hezro" but read "Hezrai".
[^29]: The Hebrew text is written "the armor-bearers" but read "the armor-bearer".
