**1** Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the River[^1] to the land of the Philistines,[^2] as far as the border of Egypt. They brought tribute and served Solomon all the days of his life.

**2** Solomon's provisions[^3] for one day were thirty kors[^4] of fine flour and sixty kors of meal,

**3** ten fattened oxen, twenty oxen from the pasture and a hundred sheep and goats, besides deer, gazelles, roebucks and fattened fowl.[^5]

**4** For he had dominion over the whole region west of the River,[^6] from Tiphsah to Gaza, over all the kings west of the River, and he had peace on every side around him.

**5** Judah and Israel lived in safety, each man under his own vine and under his own fig tree, from Dan to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon.

**6** Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots,[^7] and twelve thousand horsemen.[^8]

**7** Those governors provided food for King Solomon and for everyone who came to King Solomon's table, each in his month. They let nothing be lacking.

**8** They also brought barley and straw for the horses and the chariot horses[^9] to the place where it was needed,[^10] each according to his assignment.

**9** God gave Solomon wisdom and very great understanding, and breadth of mind[^11] like the sand on the seashore.

**10** Solomon's wisdom was greater than the wisdom of all the people of the east,[^12] and greater than all the wisdom of Egypt.

**11** He was wiser than anyone else, wiser than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, Calcol and Darda, the sons of Mahol.[^13] His fame spread through all the surrounding nations.

**12** He spoke 3,000 proverbs, and his songs numbered 1,005.

**13** He spoke about trees, from the cedar in Lebanon to the hyssop that grows out of the wall. He also spoke about animals, birds, creatures that creep on the ground, and fish.

**14** People came from all nations to hear Solomon's wisdom, sent by all the kings of the earth who had heard of his wisdom.

**15** Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon when he heard that he had been anointed king in place of his father, for Hiram had always been a friend of David.[^14]

**16** Solomon sent this message to Hiram:

**17** "You know that my father David could not build a house for the name of Yahweh[^15] his God because of the wars that surrounded him, until Yahweh put his enemies[^16] under the soles of my feet.[^17]

**18** But now Yahweh my God has given me rest on every side. There is no adversary[^18] and no misfortune.

**19** So I intend to build a house for the name of Yahweh my God, as Yahweh told my father David: 'Your son, whom I will put on your throne in your place, will build the house for my name.'

**20** So now give orders for cedars to be cut for me from Lebanon. My servants will work with your servants, and I will pay you whatever wages you set for your servants. For you know that there is no one among us who knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians."

**21** When Hiram heard Solomon's message, he was very glad and said, "Blessed be Yahweh today, who has given David a wise son over this great people."

**22** Hiram sent word to Solomon: "I have heard the message you sent me. I will do all you want in the matter of cedar timber and cypress timber.[^19]

**23** My servants will bring them down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will make them into rafts on the sea to go to the place you tell me. There I will break them up, and you can take them away. And you will do what I want by providing food for my household."

**24** So Hiram[^20] gave Solomon all the cedar timber and cypress timber he wanted.

**25** Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand kors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty kors of pressed olive oil.[^21] Solomon gave this to Hiram year after year.

**26** Yahweh gave Solomon wisdom, as he had promised him. There was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a treaty.[^22]

**27** King Solomon conscripted forced labor[^23] from all Israel, and the forced labor numbered 30,000 men.

**28** He sent them to Lebanon in shifts of 10,000 a month. They spent a month in Lebanon and two months at home. Adoniram was in charge of the forced labor.

**29** Solomon had 70,000 men who carried loads and 80,000 who cut stone in the hills,

**30** besides Solomon's 3,300 chief officers[^24] who were in charge of the work and directed the people doing the work.

**31** At the king's command they quarried large stones, costly stones, to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stone.[^25]

**32** Solomon's builders and Hiram's builders and the men of Gebal[^26] cut them, and they prepared the timber and the stones to build the house.

[^1]: The Euphrates. Verses 1–14 are 4:21–34 in most English Bibles, and verses 15–32 are 5:1–18.
[^2]: Literally "from the River, the land of the Philistines". 2 Chronicles 9:26 reads "from the River to the land of the Philistines".
[^3]: Literally "Solomon's bread".
[^4]: A kor was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters). Also in verse 25.
[^5]: The identification of some of these animals is uncertain. *Yachmur* (roebuck) may be a fallow deer; *barburim* (fowl) may be geese, cuckoos or guinea fowl.
[^6]: Literally "beyond the River", that is, west of the Euphrates. Also later in the verse.
[^7]: Or "forty thousand teams of chariot horses". 2 Chronicles 9:25 reads "four thousand stalls".
[^8]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*.
[^9]: Hebrew *rekhesh*: swift horses, perhaps those used for chariots.
[^10]: Literally "to the place where it would be". Or "to the place where he (the king) would be".
[^11]: Literally "breadth of heart".
[^12]: Literally "the sons of the east": the peoples east of Israel, in Arabia and Mesopotamia.
[^13]: Ethan the Ezrahite and Heman are named in the titles of Psalms 88 and 89. *Mahol* may be a name, or the phrase may mean "members of the guild of musicians" (Hebrew *machol*, "dance").
[^14]: Literally "a lover of David all the days". The word "love" was used of loyal allies.
[^15]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "A house for the name of Yahweh" is a temple.
[^16]: Literally "them".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "his feet" but read "my feet".
[^18]: Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, opponent".
[^19]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the cypress or the juniper; traditionally "fir". Also in verse 24.
[^20]: Hebrew *Hirom*, another spelling of the name. Also in verse 32.
[^21]: Literally "beaten oil": oil from olives crushed by hand, the finest grade (Exodus 27:20). 2 Chronicles 2:9 (English 2:10) reads "twenty thousand baths of oil".
[^22]: Hebrew *berit*, "covenant".
[^23]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs conscripted to labor for the king (4:6). Also in verse 28.
[^24]: Literally "the officers of the governors". 2 Chronicles 2:17 (English 2:18) gives 3,600.
[^25]: Hebrew *gazit*: stone cut and dressed to shape.
[^26]: Hebrew *ha-givlim*, the people of Gebal (Byblos), a Phoenician city north of Sidon known for its craftsmen.
