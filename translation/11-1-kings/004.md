**1** King Solomon was king over all Israel.

**2** These were his officials:[^1] Azariah son of Zadok was the priest.[^2]

**3** Elihoreph and Ahijah, the sons of Shisha, were secretaries.[^3] Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder.[^4]

**4** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the army. Zadok and Abiathar were priests.

**5** Azariah son of Nathan was over the governors.[^5] Zabud son of Nathan was a priest and the king's friend.[^6]

**6** Ahishar was in charge of the palace.[^7] Adoniram son of Abda was in charge of the forced labor.[^8]

**7** Solomon had twelve governors over all Israel, who provided food for the king and his household. Each one had to provide food for one month in the year.

**8** These are their names: Ben-hur,[^9] in the hill country of Ephraim;

**9** Ben-deker, in Makaz, Shaalbim, Beth-shemesh and Elon-beth-hanan;

**10** Ben-hesed, in Arubboth (Socoh and all the land of Hepher were his);

**11** Ben-abinadab, in all Naphath-dor[^10] (he had Taphath daughter of Solomon as his wife);

**12** Baana son of Ahilud, in Taanach and Megiddo, and all Beth-shean, which is beside Zarethan below Jezreel, from Beth-shean to Abel-meholah, as far as the other side of Jokmeam;

**13** Ben-geber, in Ramoth-gilead (the villages of Jair son of Manasseh, which are in Gilead, were his,[^11] and the region of Argob, which is in Bashan, was his: sixty large cities with walls and bronze bars);[^12]

**14** Ahinadab son of Iddo, in Mahanaim;

**15** Ahimaaz, in Naphtali (he too had married Basemath daughter of Solomon);

**16** Baana son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth;[^13]

**17** Jehoshaphat son of Paruah, in Issachar;

**18** Shimei son of Ela, in Benjamin;

**19** Geber son of Uri, in the land of Gilead, the land of Sihon king of the Amorites and of Og king of Bashan. And there was one governor who was in the land.[^14]

**20** The people of Judah and Israel were as many as the sand on the seashore. They ate and drank and were happy.[^15]

[^1]: Hebrew *sarim*, "princes, officials".
[^2]: Or "Azariah son of Zadok the priest". The Hebrew can be read either way.
[^3]: Hebrew *soferim*, "scribes": royal secretaries who handled the king's correspondence and records.
[^4]: Hebrew *mazkir*, literally "one who brings to remembrance": a high official, perhaps the king's herald or the keeper of the royal records.
[^5]: Hebrew *nitsavim*, "those set over": the officials over the districts listed in verses 7–19.
[^6]: "The king's friend" was probably an official title, for a trusted personal adviser (2 Samuel 15:37; 16:16).
[^7]: Literally "over the house": the official who managed the royal household and estates.
[^8]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs conscripted to labor for the king.
[^9]: Several governors are named only by their father: *Ben-hur* means "son of Hur". So also Ben-deker, Ben-hesed, Ben-abinadab and Ben-geber in verses 9–13.
[^10]: Or "in all the heights of Dor".
[^11]: Hebrew *Havvoth-jair*, "the tent villages of Jair" (Numbers 32:41; Deuteronomy 3:14).
[^12]: That is, bars of bronze for securing the city gates.
[^13]: Or "in Asher and in Aloth".
[^14]: The meaning is uncertain. It may mean that Geber was the only governor over this large region, or that there was one more governor, over the land of Judah.
[^15]: In most English Bibles chapter 4 continues with verses 21–34, which are 5:1–14 in the Hebrew.
