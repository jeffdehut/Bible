**1** Ahab told Jezebel everything Elijah had done, and how he had killed all the prophets with the sword.

**2** Then Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah to say, "May the gods deal with me, and more besides,[^1] if by this time tomorrow I have not made your life like the life of one of them."

**3** He was afraid,[^2] and he got up and ran for his life.[^3] He came to Beersheba, which belongs to Judah, and left his servant there.

**4** He himself went a day's journey into the wilderness. He came and sat down under a broom bush[^4] and asked to die. He said, "It is enough. Now, Yahweh,[^5] take my life, for I am no better than my ancestors."

**5** He lay down and slept under the broom bush. Suddenly an angel[^6] touched him and said to him, "Get up and eat."

**6** He looked, and there by his head was a cake baked on hot stones[^7] and a jug of water. He ate and drank and lay down again.

**7** The angel of Yahweh came back a second time, touched him and said, "Get up and eat, for the journey is too much for you."

**8** He got up and ate and drank, and in the strength of that food he walked forty days and forty nights as far as Horeb,[^8] the mountain of God.

**9** There he came to the cave[^9] and spent the night there. And the word of Yahweh came to him. He said to him, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**10** He said, "I have been very zealous for Yahweh, the God of Armies.[^10] For the Israelites have abandoned your covenant, torn down your altars and killed your prophets with the sword. I alone am left, and they are seeking my life, to take it away."

**11** He said, "Go out and stand on the mountain before Yahweh." And look, Yahweh was passing by. A great and powerful wind was tearing the mountains apart and shattering the rocks before Yahweh, but Yahweh was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but Yahweh was not in the earthquake.

**12** After the earthquake there was a fire, but Yahweh was not in the fire. And after the fire there was the sound of a gentle whisper.[^11]

**13** When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his cloak, went out and stood at the entrance of the cave. And a voice came to him and said, "What are you doing here, Elijah?"

**14** He said, "I have been very zealous for Yahweh, the God of Armies. For the Israelites have abandoned your covenant, torn down your altars and killed your prophets with the sword. I alone am left, and they are seeking my life, to take it away."

**15** Yahweh said to him, "Go back the way you came, to the wilderness of Damascus. When you arrive, anoint Hazael as king over Aram.

**16** Anoint Jehu son of Nimshi as king over Israel, and anoint Elisha son of Shaphat from Abel-meholah as prophet in your place.

**17** Whoever escapes the sword of Hazael, Jehu will kill, and whoever escapes the sword of Jehu, Elisha will kill.

**18** Yet I will leave seven thousand in Israel, all whose knees have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him."

**19** So he went from there and found Elisha son of Shaphat, who was plowing. There were twelve yoke of oxen ahead of him, and he was with the twelfth. Elijah went over to him and threw his cloak on him.

**20** Elisha left the oxen, ran after Elijah and said, "Please let me kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow you." He said to him, "Go back. What have I done to you?"[^12]

**21** So he turned back from following him, took the yoke of oxen and slaughtered them. He boiled their meat with the oxen's gear[^13] and gave it to the people, and they ate. Then he got up and followed Elijah and served him.

[^1]: An oath formula, literally "Thus may the gods do, and thus may they add". The verbs are plural, so "gods" rather than "God".
[^2]: The Hebrew vowel points read "He saw". The same consonants can be read "He was afraid", as the Septuagint reads.
[^3]: Literally "went for his life (*nephesh*)".
[^4]: Hebrew *rotem*, the white broom, a desert shrub; traditionally "juniper". Also in verse 5.
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: Or "a messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. Also in verse 7.
[^7]: Hebrew *uggat retsafim*, a flat loaf baked on hot stones or coals.
[^8]: Another name for Mount Sinai.
[^9]: The Hebrew has "the cave", as if a well-known one. Compare Exodus 33:22.
[^10]: Hebrew *YHWH elohei tseva'ot*. *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Traditionally "the LORD God of hosts". Also in verse 14.
[^11]: Hebrew *qol demamah daqqah*, literally "a sound of thin silence" or "a voice of gentle stillness"; traditionally "a still small voice".
[^12]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "Go back, but remember what I have done to you", or "Go back; I have not stopped you".
[^13]: Literally "with the equipment of the oxen", that is, he burned the wooden yoke and plow for fuel.
