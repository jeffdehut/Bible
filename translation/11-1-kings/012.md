**1** Rehoboam went to Shechem, for all Israel had come to Shechem to make him king.

**2** When Jeroboam son of Nebat heard of it, he was still in Egypt, where he had fled from King Solomon. Jeroboam was living in Egypt.[^1]

**3** They sent and summoned him, and Jeroboam and the whole assembly of Israel came and said to Rehoboam,

**4** "Your father made our yoke heavy. Now lighten the hard service your father demanded and the heavy yoke he put on us, and we will serve you."

**5** He said to them, "Go away for three days, then come back to me." So the people went away.

**6** King Rehoboam consulted the elders who had served[^2] his father Solomon while he was alive. He asked, "How do you advise me to answer this people?"

**7** They said to him,[^3] "If today you will be a servant to this people and serve them, and answer them by speaking kind words to them, they will be your servants forever."

**8** But he rejected the advice the elders gave him, and he consulted the young men[^4] who had grown up with him and who served him.

**9** He said to them, "What do you advise that we answer this people, who have said to me, 'Lighten the yoke your father put on us'?"

**10** The young men who had grown up with him said to him, "This is what you should say to this people who said to you, 'Your father made our yoke heavy, but you must make it lighter for us.' This is what you should tell them: 'My little finger[^5] is thicker than my father's waist.

**11** My father loaded you with a heavy yoke, but I will add to your yoke. My father disciplined you with whips, but I will discipline you with scorpions.'"[^6]

**12** So Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, as the king had told them: "Come back to me on the third day."

**13** The king answered the people harshly. He rejected the advice the elders had given him,

**14** and he spoke to them as the young men advised: "My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add to your yoke. My father disciplined you with whips, but I will discipline you with scorpions."

**15** So the king did not listen to the people, for this turn of events came from the LORD,[^7] to fulfill the word that the LORD had spoken through Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam son of Nebat.

**16** When all Israel saw that the king would not listen to them, the people answered the king, "What share do we have in David? We have no inheritance in the son of Jesse. To your tents, Israel! Now look after your own house, David!" So Israel went to their tents.[^8]

**17** But as for the Israelites who lived in the towns of Judah, Rehoboam reigned over them.

**18** Then King Rehoboam sent Adoram, who was in charge of the forced labor,[^9] but all Israel stoned him to death. King Rehoboam managed to get into his chariot and flee to Jerusalem.

**19** So Israel has been in rebellion against the house of David to this day.

**20** When all Israel heard that Jeroboam had returned, they sent and called him to the assembly and made him king over all Israel. No one followed the house of David except the tribe of Judah alone.

**21** When Rehoboam arrived in Jerusalem, he assembled all the house of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin, 180,000 chosen warriors, to fight against the house of Israel and restore the kingdom to Rehoboam son of Solomon.

**22** But the word of God came to Shemaiah the man of God:[^10]

**23** "Say to Rehoboam son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all the house of Judah and Benjamin, and to the rest of the people,

**24** 'This is what the LORD says: Do not go up or fight against your relatives,[^11] the Israelites. Go home, every one of you, for this thing is from me.'" So they listened to the word of the LORD and went home again, as the LORD had said.[^12]

**25** Then Jeroboam built[^13] Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim and lived there. From there he went out and built Penuel.

**26** Jeroboam said to himself,[^14] "Now the kingdom will go back to the house of David.

**27** If these people go up to offer sacrifices at the house of the LORD in Jerusalem, the heart of these people will turn back to their lord, Rehoboam king of Judah. They will kill me and go back to Rehoboam king of Judah."

**28** So the king took advice and made two calves of gold. He said to the people,[^15] "You have been going up to Jerusalem long enough.[^16] Here are your gods,[^17] Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!"

**29** He set up one in Bethel, and the other he put in Dan.

**30** And this became a sin. The people went before the one as far as Dan.[^18]

**31** He also made shrines on the high places,[^19] and he appointed priests from all sorts of people,[^20] who were not Levites.

**32** Jeroboam instituted a festival on the fifteenth day of the eighth month, like the festival in Judah,[^21] and he went up to the altar. He did this in Bethel, sacrificing to the calves he had made, and he stationed in Bethel the priests of the high places he had made.

**33** On the fifteenth day of the eighth month, the month he had devised on his own,[^22] he went up to the altar he had made in Bethel. He instituted a festival for the Israelites, and he went up to the altar to burn incense.[^23]

[^1]: 2 Chronicles 10:2 reads "Jeroboam returned from Egypt". In Hebrew "lived" and "returned" are written with the same consonants.
[^2]: Literally "stood before". Also in verse 8.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "he said" but read "they said".
[^4]: Literally "the children". Also in verses 10 and 14.
[^5]: Literally "My little one".
[^6]: Probably whips with barbs or spikes. Also in verse 14.
[^7]: Literally "for it was a turn of affairs from the LORD". *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: That is, they went home.
[^9]: Called Adoniram in 4:6 and 5:28 (5:14 in most English Bibles). Hebrew *mas*: work gangs conscripted to labor for the king.
[^10]: "Man of God" is a title for a prophet.
[^11]: Literally "your brothers".
[^12]: After this verse the Septuagint adds a long passage (often numbered 24a–z) that tells the story of Jeroboam's rise in a different form.
[^13]: Or "fortified". Also later in the verse.
[^14]: Literally "in his heart".
[^15]: Literally "to them".
[^16]: Or "It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem."
[^17]: Or "Here is your god". In the Hebrew, "gods" and "brought you up" are plural. See Exodus 32:4, 8.
[^18]: The sentence seems incomplete. It may mean that the people went all the way to Dan to worship before the calf there.
[^19]: Literally "a house of high places". Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops (3:2).
[^20]: Literally "from the ends of the people".
[^21]: The Festival of Shelters, held in Judah on the fifteenth day of the seventh month (8:2; Leviticus 23:34).
[^22]: The Hebrew text is written *millevad*, "apart", but read *millibbo*, "from his own heart".
[^23]: Or "to burn offerings".
