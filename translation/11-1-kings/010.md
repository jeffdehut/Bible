**1** The queen of Sheba[^1] heard of Solomon's fame, connected with the name of Yahweh,[^2] and she came to test him with hard questions.[^3]

**2** She came to Jerusalem with a very great retinue, with camels carrying spices, a great quantity of gold, and precious stones. When she came to Solomon, she talked with him about everything that was on her mind.[^4]

**3** Solomon answered all her questions.[^5] Nothing was hidden from the king that he could not explain to her.

**4** When the queen of Sheba saw all Solomon's wisdom, the house he had built,

**5** the food on his table, the seating of his officials,[^6] the attendance of his servants[^7] and their clothing, his cupbearers, and his burnt offerings that he offered at the house of Yahweh,[^8] it took her breath away.[^9]

**6** She said to the king, "The report I heard in my own country about your words[^10] and your wisdom was true.

**7** But I did not believe the reports until I came and saw it with my own eyes. And look, not even half was told me. Your wisdom and prosperity go beyond the report I heard.

**8** How happy your men must be![^11] How happy these servants of yours, who stand before you continually and hear your wisdom!

**9** Blessed be Yahweh your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel! Because Yahweh loves Israel forever, he has made you king, to do justice and what is right."[^12]

**10** Then she gave the king 120 talents[^13] of gold, a very great quantity of spices, and precious stones. Never again did such an abundance of spices come in as the queen of Sheba gave King Solomon.

**11** Hiram's fleet, which carried gold from Ophir, also brought from Ophir a great quantity of almug wood[^14] and precious stones.

**12** The king used the almug wood to make supports[^15] for the house of Yahweh and for the king's house, and lyres and harps for the singers. No such almug wood has come in or been seen again to this day.

**13** King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba everything she desired, whatever she asked for, besides what he gave her out of his royal generosity.[^16] Then she turned and went back to her own country, she and her servants.

**14** The weight of the gold that came to Solomon in one year was 666 talents of gold,[^17]

**15** besides what came from the traders[^18] and from the business of the merchants, and from all the kings of Arabia[^19] and the governors of the land.

**16** King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold. Six hundred shekels[^20] of gold went into each shield.

**17** He also made three hundred small shields of hammered gold. Three minas[^21] of gold went into each shield. The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.[^22]

**18** The king also made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with fine gold.[^23]

**19** The throne had six steps. The throne had a rounded top at the back,[^24] and there were armrests on each side of the seat, with two lions standing beside the armrests.

**20** Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, one on each side.[^25] Nothing like it had ever been made in any kingdom.

**21** All King Solomon's drinking vessels were gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were pure gold.[^26] There was no silver. Silver was considered of no value in Solomon's days.

**22** For the king had a fleet of Tarshish ships[^27] at sea with Hiram's fleet. Once every three years the fleet of Tarshish ships came, bringing gold and silver, ivory, apes and baboons.[^28]

**23** So King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.

**24** The whole world sought an audience with Solomon[^29] to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.

**25** Each of them brought his gift: articles of silver and articles of gold, garments, weapons[^30] and spices, horses and mules, so much year by year.

**26** Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen.[^31] He had 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen, and he stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.

**27** The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and he made cedar as plentiful as the sycamore-fig trees in the Shephelah.[^32]

**28** Solomon's horses were imported from Egypt and from Kue.[^33] The king's traders bought them from Kue at a price.

**29** A chariot could be imported from Egypt for 600 shekels of silver,[^34] and a horse for 150. In the same way, through their agents,[^35] they exported them to all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

[^1]: A kingdom in southwest Arabia, in what is now Yemen.
[^2]: Literally "the report of Solomon for the name of Yahweh". The meaning of the phrase is uncertain. *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *chidot*, "riddles".
[^4]: Literally "with her heart".
[^5]: Literally "told her all her words".
[^6]: Or "servants". Hebrew *avadim*. Also in verse 8.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "his servant" but read "his servants".
[^8]: Or "and the ascent by which he went up to the house of Yahweh" (compare 2 Chronicles 9:4).
[^9]: Literally "there was no more spirit in her".
[^10]: Or "your affairs". Hebrew *devarim* means both "words" and "matters".
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "your wives".
[^12]: Literally "justice and righteousness".
[^13]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so 120 talents was about 9,000 pounds (4,100 kg).
[^14]: Hebrew *almuggim*, a costly wood that cannot be identified with certainty; perhaps red sandalwood. In 2 Chronicles 2:7 (2:8 in most English Bibles) and 9:10–11 it is called *algummim*. Also in verse 12.
[^15]: Hebrew *mis'ad*, perhaps "railings" or "steps"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^16]: Literally "according to the hand of King Solomon".
[^17]: About 50,000 pounds (22,600 kg).
[^18]: Literally "the men of the travelers". The meaning is uncertain.
[^19]: The Hebrew vowel points read "the mixed peoples" (*ha-erev*). 2 Chronicles 9:14 reads "Arabia".
[^20]: Literally "six hundred of gold". The word "shekels" is understood. A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so six hundred shekels was about 15 pounds (6.9 kg).
[^21]: A mina was fifty shekels, about 1¼ pounds (575 grams), so three minas was about 3¾ pounds (1.7 kg). 2 Chronicles 9:16 reads "three hundred (shekels)".
[^22]: See 7:2.
[^23]: Hebrew *zahav mufaz*, perhaps "refined gold".
[^24]: Literally "and a round head was to the throne from behind it". The Septuagint reads "calves' heads", from a Hebrew word with the same consonants.
[^25]: Literally "on this side and on that". Also in verse 19.
[^26]: Hebrew *zahav sagur*, perhaps "refined gold" or "solid gold" (6:20).
[^27]: Large seagoing ships, of the kind that sailed to Tarshish, a distant port in the west, perhaps in Spain. Also later in the verse.
[^28]: Or "peacocks". Hebrew *tukkiyyim*, of uncertain meaning.
[^29]: Literally "sought the face of Solomon".
[^30]: Hebrew *neshek*, "weapons". Some take it as a word meaning "myrrh".
[^31]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*. Also later in the verse.
[^32]: The low hills between the hill country of Judah and the coastal plain.
[^33]: Hebrew *miqveh*, probably Kue, that is, Cilicia, in what is now southern Turkey. Also later in the verse. Some take the word as "in droves".
[^34]: Literally "six hundred of silver", that is, shekels: about 15 pounds (6.9 kg). The horse cost 150 shekels, about 3¾ pounds (1.7 kg).
[^35]: Literally "by their hand".
