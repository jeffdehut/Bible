**1** King David was old, well on in years.[^1] They covered him with blankets,[^2] but he could not get warm.

**2** So his servants said to him, "Let a young woman, a virgin, be found for my lord the king. Let her wait on the king and take care of him.[^3] She can lie in your arms,[^4] and then my lord the king will get warm."

**3** So they searched throughout the territory of Israel for a beautiful young woman. They found Abishag the Shunammite[^5] and brought her to the king.

**4** The young woman was very beautiful. She took care of the king and served him, but the king did not have sexual relations with her.[^6]

**5** Now Adonijah son of Haggith was putting himself forward, saying, "I will be king." He got himself chariots and horsemen,[^7] with fifty men to run ahead of him.

**6** His father had never once crossed him[^8] by asking, "Why have you done this?" He was also very handsome, and he was born next after Absalom.[^9]

**7** He conferred with Joab son of Zeruiah and with Abiathar the priest,[^10] and they followed Adonijah and supported him.

**8** But Zadok the priest, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, Nathan the prophet, Shimei, Rei and David's warriors[^11] did not side with Adonijah.

**9** Adonijah sacrificed sheep, cattle and fattened calves by the Stone of Zoheleth,[^12] which is beside En-rogel.[^13] He invited all his brothers, the king's sons, and all the men of Judah who were the king's servants.[^14]

**10** But he did not invite Nathan the prophet, Benaiah, the warriors, or his brother Solomon.

**11** Then Nathan said to Bathsheba, Solomon's mother, "Haven't you heard that Adonijah son of Haggith has become king, and our lord David does not know it?

**12** So now come, let me give you some advice, so that you can save your own life and the life of your son Solomon.

**13** Go in to King David and say to him, 'My lord the king, did you not swear to your servant,[^15] "Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne"? Why then has Adonijah become king?'

**14** Then, while you are still there speaking with the king, I will come in after you and confirm what you say."[^16]

**15** So Bathsheba went in to the king in his room. The king was very old, and Abishag the Shunammite was serving the king.

**16** Bathsheba bowed low and knelt before the king. The king said, "What do you want?"[^17]

**17** She said to him, "My lord, you yourself swore to your servant by the LORD[^18] your God, 'Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne.'

**18** But now, look, Adonijah has become king, and now, my lord the king, you do not know it.

**19** He has sacrificed oxen, fattened calves and sheep in great numbers, and he has invited all the king's sons, Abiathar the priest and Joab the commander of the army. But he has not invited your servant Solomon.

**20** As for you, my lord the king, the eyes of all Israel are on you, for you to tell them who will sit on the throne of my lord the king after him.

**21** Otherwise, when my lord the king sleeps with his ancestors,[^19] my son Solomon and I will be treated as criminals."[^20]

**22** While she was still speaking with the king, Nathan the prophet arrived.

**23** The king was told, "Nathan the prophet is here." He came before the king and bowed down to the king with his face to the ground.

**24** Nathan said, "My lord the king, have you said, 'Adonijah will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne'?[^21]

**25** For today he has gone down and sacrificed oxen, fattened calves and sheep in great numbers. He has invited all the king's sons, the commanders of the army and Abiathar the priest. They are there now, eating and drinking in his presence, and they are saying, 'Long live King Adonijah!'

**26** But he has not invited me, your servant, or Zadok the priest, or Benaiah son of Jehoiada, or your servant Solomon.

**27** Has my lord the king done this without letting your servant[^22] know who will sit on the throne of my lord the king after him?"

**28** Then King David answered, "Call Bathsheba to me." She came into the king's presence and stood before the king.

**29** The king swore an oath and said, "As surely as the LORD lives, who has rescued my life from every trouble,

**30** just as I swore to you by the LORD, the God of Israel, 'Your son Solomon will be king after me, and he will sit on my throne in my place,' so I will do this very day."

**31** Bathsheba bowed with her face to the ground and knelt before the king. She said, "May my lord King David live forever!"

**32** King David said, "Call Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet and Benaiah son of Jehoiada to me." They came before the king,

**33** and the king said to them, "Take your lord's servants with you. Have my son Solomon ride on my own mule, and take him down to Gihon.[^23]

**34** There Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet are to anoint him king over Israel. Blow the ram's horn[^24] and say, 'Long live King Solomon!'

**35** Then come up after him, and he is to come and sit on my throne. He will be king in my place. I have appointed him to be ruler[^25] over Israel and over Judah."

**36** Benaiah son of Jehoiada answered the king, "Amen! May the LORD, the God of my lord the king, say so too.

**37** As the LORD has been with my lord the king, so may he be with Solomon, and may he make his throne even greater than the throne of my lord King David."

**38** So Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites[^26] went down. They had Solomon ride on King David's mule, and they took him to Gihon.

**39** Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the tent[^27] and anointed Solomon. They blew the ram's horn, and all the people said, "Long live King Solomon!"

**40** All the people went up after him, playing flutes and rejoicing greatly, so that the earth split with the noise.[^28]

**41** Adonijah and all the guests who were with him heard it as they finished eating. When Joab heard the sound of the ram's horn, he said, "Why is the city in such an uproar?"[^29]

**42** While he was still speaking, Jonathan son of Abiathar the priest arrived. Adonijah said, "Come in, for you are a worthy man, and you must be bringing good news."

**43** Jonathan answered Adonijah, "Not at all! Our lord King David has made Solomon king.

**44** The king has sent with him Zadok the priest, Nathan the prophet, Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites, and they have had him ride on the king's mule.

**45** Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet have anointed him king at Gihon. They have gone up from there rejoicing, and the city is in an uproar. That is the noise you have heard.

**46** Solomon has even taken his seat on the royal throne.

**47** And the king's servants have come to congratulate[^30] our lord King David, saying, 'May God[^31] make the name of Solomon more famous than your name, and make his throne greater than your throne!' And the king bowed down on his bed.

**48** The king also said this: 'Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who has today provided someone to sit on my throne,[^32] while my own eyes see it.'"

**49** Then all Adonijah's guests trembled. They got up and went their own ways.

**50** Adonijah was afraid of Solomon. He got up, went and took hold of the horns of the altar.[^33]

**51** Solomon was told, "Adonijah is afraid of King Solomon. He has taken hold of the horns of the altar and is saying, 'Let King Solomon swear to me today that he will not put his servant to death with the sword.'"

**52** Solomon said, "If he proves to be a worthy man, not one hair of his head will fall to the ground. But if wrongdoing is found in him, he will die."

**53** Then King Solomon sent men, and they brought him down from the altar. He came and bowed down to King Solomon, and Solomon said to him, "Go home."[^34]

[^1]: Literally "had come into the days".
[^2]: Literally "with garments".
[^3]: Literally "Let her stand before the king and be a *sokhenet* to him." The Hebrew word, used only here and in verse 4, means a caretaker or attendant.
[^4]: Literally "in your bosom". The servants turn from speaking of "my lord the king" to speaking to him directly.
[^5]: From Shunem, a town in the territory of Issachar (Joshua 19:18).
[^6]: Literally "did not know her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^7]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim* can mean either.
[^8]: Literally "had not grieved him from his days", that is, he had never in his life corrected him.
[^9]: Literally "and she bore him after Absalom". The subject is his mother, Haggith.
[^10]: Literally "His words were with Joab … and with Abiathar the priest".
[^11]: Hebrew *gibborim*, "mighty men": David's elite troops (2 Samuel 23:8–39). Also in verse 10.
[^12]: The name may mean "Serpent's Stone" or "Sliding Stone".
[^13]: A spring just south of Jerusalem, where the Kidron Valley meets the Valley of Hinnom (Joshua 15:7; 18:16).
[^14]: Or "officials". Hebrew *avadim* means servants of any rank, including the king's courtiers and officers.
[^15]: Hebrew *amah*, "female servant", used in polite speech of oneself. Also in verse 17.
[^16]: Literally "fill up your words".
[^17]: Literally "What is to you?"
[^18]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^19]: Literally "lies down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^20]: Literally "will be sinners", that is, treated as offenders.
[^21]: Or "You must have said". The Hebrew has no question marker, but the sense is a question.
[^22]: The Hebrew text is written "your servants" but read "your servant".
[^23]: A spring on the east side of Jerusalem, in the Kidron Valley. Also in verses 38 and 45.
[^24]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn. Also in verses 39 and 41.
[^25]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, prince".
[^26]: David's bodyguard (2 Samuel 8:18; 20:23). The names probably mean "Cretans" and "Philistines", foreign soldiers in the king's service. Also in verse 44.
[^27]: The tent David had set up for the ark in Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6:17).
[^28]: Literally "the earth split with their sound". The Septuagint reads "the earth resounded".
[^29]: Literally "Why is the sound of the town in an uproar?"
[^30]: Literally "to bless".
[^31]: The Hebrew text is written "your God" but read "God".
[^32]: Literally "who has given today one sitting on my throne". The Septuagint reads "one of my offspring sitting on my throne".
[^33]: The projections at the four corners of the altar (Exodus 27:2). Taking hold of them was a plea for protection (Exodus 21:13–14). Also in verse 51.
[^34]: Literally "Go to your house".
