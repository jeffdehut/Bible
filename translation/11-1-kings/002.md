**1** When the time drew near for David to die,[^1] he gave his son Solomon these instructions:

**2** "I am about to go the way of all the earth. So be strong, and show yourself a man.

**3** Keep the charge of the LORD[^2] your God:[^3] walk in his ways, and keep his decrees, his commands, his rulings and his statutes,[^4] as it is written in the Law of Moses, so that you may succeed[^5] in everything you do and wherever you turn,

**4** and so that the LORD may keep the promise he made about me: 'If your sons take care how they live,[^6] walking before me faithfully with all their heart and with all their soul, you will never lack a man on the throne of Israel.'[^7]

**5** "You also know what Joab son of Zeruiah did to me, what he did to the two commanders of the armies of Israel, Abner son of Ner and Amasa son of Jether. He killed them, shedding the blood of war in peacetime,[^8] and he put the blood of war on the belt around his waist and on the sandals on his feet.[^9]

**6** Act according to your wisdom, and do not let his gray head go down to Sheol[^10] in peace.

**7** But show faithful love[^11] to the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite, and let them be among those who eat at your table, for they stood by me[^12] when I fled from your brother Absalom.

**8** "Look, you have with you Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim. He cursed me with a bitter curse on the day I went to Mahanaim. When he came down to meet me at the Jordan, I swore to him by the LORD, 'I will not put you to death with the sword.'[^13]

**9** But now, do not let him go unpunished. You are a wise man, and you will know what to do to him. Bring his gray head down to Sheol in blood."

**10** Then David slept with his ancestors[^14] and was buried in the City of David.[^15]

**11** David reigned over Israel for forty years.[^16] He reigned seven years in Hebron and thirty-three years in Jerusalem.

**12** So Solomon sat on the throne of his father David, and his kingdom was firmly established.

**13** Then Adonijah son of Haggith came to Bathsheba, Solomon's mother. She said, "Do you come in peace?" He said, "In peace."

**14** Then he said, "I have something to say to you." She said, "Speak."

**15** He said, "You know that the kingship was mine, and all Israel looked to me to be their king.[^17] But the kingship has turned around and gone to my brother, for it came to him from the LORD.

**16** Now I have one request to ask of you. Do not refuse me."[^18] She said to him, "Speak."

**17** He said, "Please speak to King Solomon, for he will not refuse you, and ask him to give me Abishag the Shunammite as my wife."

**18** Bathsheba said, "Very well. I will speak to the king for you."

**19** So Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak to him for Adonijah. The king rose to meet her and bowed down to her. Then he sat down on his throne and had a throne set for the king's mother, and she sat at his right hand.

**20** She said, "I have one small request to ask of you. Do not refuse me." The king said to her, "Ask, my mother, for I will not refuse you."

**21** She said, "Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to your brother Adonijah as his wife."

**22** King Solomon answered his mother, "Why are you asking for Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? Ask for the kingdom for him too, since he is my older brother, for him and for Abiathar the priest and for Joab son of Zeruiah!"[^19]

**23** Then King Solomon swore by the LORD, "May God deal with me, and more besides,[^20] if Adonijah has not made this request at the cost of his life!

**24** Now, as the LORD lives, who has established me and set me on the throne of my father David, and who has made me a house[^21] as he promised, Adonijah will be put to death today."

**25** So King Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada,[^22] and he struck Adonijah down, and he died.

**26** To Abiathar the priest the king said, "Go to Anathoth, to your fields. You deserve to die,[^23] but I will not put you to death today, because you carried the ark of the Lord GOD[^24] before my father David, and because you shared in all the hardships my father suffered."

**27** So Solomon banished Abiathar from being a priest of the LORD, fulfilling the word that the LORD had spoken at Shiloh about the house of Eli.[^25]

**28** When the news reached Joab, for Joab had sided with Adonijah, though he had not sided with Absalom, Joab fled to the tent of the LORD and took hold of the horns of the altar.[^26]

**29** King Solomon was told, "Joab has fled to the tent of the LORD, and he is there beside the altar." So Solomon sent Benaiah son of Jehoiada, saying, "Go, strike him down."

**30** Benaiah went to the tent of the LORD and said to him, "This is what the king says: 'Come out!'" But he said, "No, I will die here." Benaiah brought word back to the king: "This is what Joab said, and this is how he answered me."

**31** The king said to him, "Do as he has said. Strike him down and bury him, and so take away from me and from my father's house the blood that Joab shed without cause.

**32** The LORD will bring his blood back on his own head,[^27] because he struck down two men more righteous and better than he was, and killed them with the sword without my father David knowing it: Abner son of Ner, commander of the army of Israel, and Amasa son of Jether, commander of the army of Judah.

**33** Their blood will come back on the head of Joab and on the head of his descendants forever. But for David and his descendants, his house and his throne, there will be peace from the LORD forever."

**34** So Benaiah son of Jehoiada went up, struck Joab down and killed him. He was buried at his own home[^28] in the wilderness.

**35** The king put Benaiah son of Jehoiada over the army in his place, and the king put Zadok the priest in place of Abiathar.

**36** Then the king sent for Shimei and said to him, "Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and live there. Do not go out from there anywhere at all.[^29]

**37** On the day you go out and cross the Kidron Valley,[^30] know for certain that you will die. Your blood will be on your own head."

**38** Shimei said to the king, "What you say is good. Your servant will do just as my lord the king has said." So Shimei lived in Jerusalem for a long time.

**39** But at the end of three years, two of Shimei's slaves[^31] ran away to Achish son of Maacah, king of Gath. Shimei was told, "Your slaves are in Gath."

**40** So Shimei got up, saddled his donkey and went to Achish at Gath to look for his slaves. Shimei went and brought his slaves back from Gath.

**41** Solomon was told that Shimei had gone from Jerusalem to Gath and had come back.

**42** The king sent for Shimei and said to him, "Did I not make you swear by the LORD, and warn you, 'On the day you go out and go anywhere at all, know for certain that you will die'? And you said to me, 'What you say is good. I have heard it.'[^32]

**43** Why then have you not kept your oath to the LORD[^33] and the command I gave you?"

**44** The king also said to Shimei, "You know in your heart all the wrong you did to my father David.[^34] The LORD will bring your wrongdoing back on your own head.

**45** But King Solomon will be blessed, and the throne of David will be established before the LORD forever."

**46** Then the king gave orders to Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he went out and struck Shimei down, and he died. So the kingdom was firmly established in Solomon's hands.

[^1]: Literally "The days of David drew near to die".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "Keep the keeping of the LORD your God", that is, what he requires.
[^4]: Hebrew *edot*, traditionally "testimonies": the solemn terms of the covenant.
[^5]: Or "act wisely".
[^6]: Literally "keep their way".
[^7]: Literally "a man will not be cut off for you from upon the throne of Israel". See 2 Samuel 7:12–16.
[^8]: Literally "he set the blood of war in peace". Joab killed Abner and Amasa when there was no war (2 Samuel 3:27; 20:9–10).
[^9]: That is, he made himself guilty of their blood.
[^10]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. Also in verse 9.
[^11]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^12]: Literally "they came near to me", that is, with help (2 Samuel 17:27–29; 19:32–40; 19:31–39 in most English Bibles).
[^13]: See 2 Samuel 16:5–13; 19:17–24 (19:16–23 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^15]: The fortress of Zion, the oldest part of Jerusalem, which David captured (2 Samuel 5:7, 9).
[^16]: Literally "And the days that David reigned over Israel were forty years."
[^17]: Literally "all Israel set their faces on me to reign".
[^18]: Literally "Do not turn back my face." Also in verses 17 and 20.
[^19]: Literally "and for him and for Abiathar the priest and for Joab son of Zeruiah". Abiathar and Joab had supported Adonijah (1:7).
[^20]: An oath formula, literally "Thus may God do to me, and thus may he add."
[^21]: That is, a dynasty (2 Samuel 7:11–16).
[^22]: Literally "sent by the hand of Benaiah son of Jehoiada".
[^23]: Literally "you are a man of death".
[^24]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. The ark is the gold-covered chest that held the stone tablets of the covenant (Exodus 25:10–22). David had brought it to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6).
[^25]: See 1 Samuel 2:27–36. Abiathar was a descendant of Eli (1 Samuel 14:3; 22:20).
[^26]: See 1:50.
[^27]: That is, the LORD will hold Joab responsible for the blood he shed. Also in verses 33, 37 and 44.
[^28]: Literally "in his house".
[^29]: Literally "here and there". Also in verse 42.
[^30]: Hebrew *nachal Qidron*, the valley on the east side of Jerusalem, with a stream that flows in the rainy season.
[^31]: Or "servants". Hebrew *avadim*. Also in verse 40.
[^32]: Or "The word I have heard is good."
[^33]: Literally "the oath of the LORD".
[^34]: Literally "You know all the evil that your heart knows, which you did to David my father."
