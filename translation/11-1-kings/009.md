**1** When Solomon had finished building the house of Yahweh[^1] and the king's house, and everything else Solomon desired to do,[^2]

**2** Yahweh appeared to Solomon a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon.

**3** Yahweh said to him, "I have heard your prayer and your plea that you made before me. I have consecrated this house that you have built, by putting my name there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there for all time.

**4** As for you, if you walk before me as your father David walked, with integrity of heart and uprightness, doing all that I have commanded you, and if you keep my decrees and my rulings,

**5** then I will establish the throne of your kingdom over Israel forever, as I promised your father David when I said, 'You will never lack a man on the throne of Israel.'[^3]

**6** But if you and your sons turn away from following me, and do not keep my commands and my decrees that I have set before you, but go and serve other gods and bow down to them,

**7** then I will cut Israel off from the land I have given them, and I will reject[^4] this house that I have consecrated for my name. Israel will become a byword and an object of ridicule[^5] among all the peoples.

**8** And as for this house, which is so exalted,[^6] everyone who passes by it will be appalled and will hiss.[^7] They will say, 'Why has Yahweh done this to this land and to this house?'

**9** And people will answer, 'Because they abandoned Yahweh their God, who brought their ancestors out of the land of Egypt, and they embraced other gods and bowed down to them and served them. That is why Yahweh has brought all this disaster on them.'"

**10** At the end of twenty years, in which Solomon had built the two houses, the house of Yahweh and the king's house,

**11** and Hiram king of Tyre had supplied Solomon with cedar timber, cypress timber[^8] and gold, as much as he wanted, King Solomon gave Hiram twenty towns in the land of Galilee.

**12** But when Hiram came from Tyre to see the towns Solomon had given him, he was not pleased with them.[^9]

**13** He said, "What are these towns you have given me, my brother?" And he called them the land of Cabul,[^10] as they are called to this day.

**14** Now Hiram had sent the king 120 talents[^11] of gold.

**15** This is the account of the forced labor[^12] that King Solomon conscripted to build the house of Yahweh, his own house, the Millo,[^13] the wall of Jerusalem, Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer.

**16** Pharaoh king of Egypt had come up and captured Gezer and burned it down. He had killed the Canaanites who lived in the city, and he had given it as a wedding gift[^14] to his daughter, Solomon's wife.

**17** So Solomon rebuilt Gezer. He also built Lower Beth-horon,

**18** Baalath, and Tadmor[^15] in the wilderness, in the land,[^16]

**19** and all the storage cities that Solomon had, the cities for his chariots and the cities for his horsemen,[^17] and whatever Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon and in all the land under his rule.

**20** As for all the people who were left of the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, who were not Israelites,

**21** their descendants who were left after them in the land, whom the Israelites had not been able to destroy completely,[^18] Solomon conscripted them as slave labor,[^19] as they are to this day.

**22** But Solomon did not make slaves of any of the Israelites. They were his soldiers, his servants,[^20] his commanders, his officers,[^21] and the commanders of his chariots and his horsemen.

**23** These were the chief officers who were over Solomon's work: 550 of them, who directed the people doing the work.[^22]

**24** But only after Pharaoh's daughter had gone up from the City of David to the house Solomon had built for her did he build the Millo.

**25** Three times a year Solomon offered burnt offerings and fellowship offerings on the altar he had built for Yahweh, burning incense with them before Yahweh.[^23] So he completed the house.[^24]

**26** King Solomon also built a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, which is near Eloth on the shore of the Red Sea,[^25] in the land of Edom.

**27** Hiram sent his servants with the fleet, sailors who knew the sea, together with Solomon's servants.

**28** They went to Ophir[^26] and brought back from there 420 talents of gold,[^27] which they delivered to King Solomon.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "and all the desire of Solomon that he wished to do".
[^3]: Literally "A man will not be cut off for you from upon the throne of Israel." See 2:4.
[^4]: Literally "send away from before my face".
[^5]: Literally "a proverb and a sharp saying".
[^6]: Literally "And this house will be high". The sense is uncertain. Compare 2 Chronicles 7:21.
[^7]: Or "whistle", a sound of shock or scorn.
[^8]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the cypress or the juniper (5:22).
[^9]: Literally "they were not right in his eyes".
[^10]: *Cabul* sounds like a Hebrew phrase meaning "like nothing". A town named Cabul lay in the territory of Asher (Joshua 19:27).
[^11]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so 120 talents was about 9,000 pounds (4,100 kg).
[^12]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs conscripted to labor for the king (4:6; 5:27).
[^13]: Hebrew *millo*, "filling": probably a terrace or earthwork supporting buildings in the City of David. Also in verse 24.
[^14]: Hebrew *shilluchim*, a parting gift given to a bride by her father.
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "Tamar" but read "Tadmor". Tamar was a town in the south of Judah (Ezekiel 47:19); Tadmor was the oasis city later called Palmyra, in the Syrian desert (2 Chronicles 8:4).
[^16]: The Hebrew seems to leave out the name of the land.
[^17]: Or "horses". Hebrew *parashim*. Also in verse 22.
[^18]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^19]: Literally "forced labor of a slave".
[^20]: Or "officials". Hebrew *avadim*.
[^21]: Hebrew *shalishim*, an officer's title of uncertain meaning, perhaps from "third", the third man in a chariot.
[^22]: 2 Chronicles 8:10 gives 250.
[^23]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and burning incense with it, which was before Yahweh".
[^24]: Or "So he fulfilled the obligations of the house".
[^25]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it means the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^26]: A land famous for its gold, perhaps in southern Arabia or on the coast of East Africa.
[^27]: About 31,500 pounds (14,300 kg). 2 Chronicles 8:18 gives 450 talents.
