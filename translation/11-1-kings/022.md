**1** For three years there was no war between Aram and Israel.

**2** In the third year Jehoshaphat king of Judah went down to the king of Israel.

**3** The king of Israel said to his servants, "Do you know that Ramoth-gilead belongs to us, yet we are doing nothing to take it out of the hand of the king of Aram?"

**4** He said to Jehoshaphat, "Will you go with me to battle at Ramoth-gilead?" Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, "I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses."

**5** But Jehoshaphat also said to the king of Israel, "Please inquire first for the word of the LORD."[^1]

**6** So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, about four hundred men, and said to them, "Shall I go to battle against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I hold back?" They said, "Go up, for the Lord[^2] will hand it over to the king."

**7** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no other prophet of the LORD here, so that we can inquire of him?"

**8** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is still one man through whom we can inquire of the LORD, but I hate him, because he never prophesies anything good about me, only disaster. He is Micaiah son of Imlah." Jehoshaphat said, "The king should not say that."

**9** So the king of Israel called an officer[^3] and said, "Bring Micaiah son of Imlah quickly."

**10** The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were sitting each on his throne, dressed in their robes, at the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets were prophesying before them.

**11** Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had made himself horns of iron, and he said, "This is what the LORD says: With these you will gore the Arameans until they are destroyed."

**12** All the prophets were prophesying the same, saying, "Go up to Ramoth-gilead and succeed. The LORD will hand it over to the king."

**13** The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him, "Look, the prophets with one voice are speaking favorably to the king.[^4] Please let your word[^5] be like the word of one of them, and speak favorably."

**14** But Micaiah said, "As surely as the LORD lives, whatever the LORD says to me, that is what I will speak."

**15** When he came to the king, the king said to him, "Micaiah, shall we go to battle against Ramoth-gilead, or shall we hold back?" He said to him, "Go up and succeed. The LORD will hand it over to the king."

**16** But the king said to him, "How many times must I make you swear to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of the LORD?"

**17** Then he said, "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains, like sheep that have no shepherd. And the LORD said, 'These have no master. Let each of them go home in peace.'"

**18** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "Didn't I tell you he would not prophesy anything good about me, only disaster?"

**19** Micaiah said,[^6] "Therefore hear the word of the LORD. I saw the LORD sitting on his throne, with the whole host of heaven standing beside him, on his right and on his left.

**20** And the LORD said, 'Who will entice Ahab to go up and fall at Ramoth-gilead?' One said one thing, and another said another.

**21** Then a spirit[^7] came forward and stood before the LORD and said, 'I will entice him.' The LORD said to him, 'How?'

**22** He said, 'I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.' The LORD said, 'You will entice him, and you will succeed. Go and do it.'

**23** So now, the LORD has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours. The LORD has pronounced disaster against you."[^8]

**24** Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came up and struck Micaiah on the cheek and said, "Which way did the Spirit of the LORD go when he left me to speak to you?"

**25** Micaiah said, "You will see on the day when you go into an inner room[^9] to hide."

**26** The king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah and bring him back to Amon the governor of the city and to Joash the king's son,[^10]

**27** and say, 'This is what the king says: Put this fellow in prison and feed him on scant bread and water[^11] until I come back safely.'"

**28** Micaiah said, "If you ever come back safely, the LORD has not spoken through me." And he said, "Listen, all you peoples!"[^12]

**29** So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-gilead.

**30** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will disguise myself and go into battle, but you wear your robes." So the king of Israel disguised himself and went into battle.

**31** Now the king of Aram had commanded his thirty-two chariot commanders, "Do not fight with anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel alone."

**32** When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, "Surely that is the king of Israel," and they turned to fight against him. But Jehoshaphat cried out.

**33** When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.

**34** But a man drew his bow at random[^13] and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armor.[^14] He said to his chariot driver, "Turn around[^15] and get me out of the fighting,[^16] for I am wounded."

**35** The battle grew fierce that day, and the king was propped up in his chariot facing the Arameans. In the evening he died, and the blood from his wound ran into the bottom of the chariot.

**36** As the sun went down, a cry went through the army: "Every man to his city! Every man to his land!"

**37** So the king died and was brought[^17] to Samaria, and they buried the king in Samaria.

**38** They washed the chariot by the pool of Samaria, and the dogs licked up his blood, and the prostitutes bathed there,[^18] according to the word of the LORD that he had spoken.

**39** As for the rest of the acts of Ahab, and all that he did, the ivory house[^19] he built and all the cities he built, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?[^20]

**40** Then Ahab slept with his ancestors,[^21] and his son Ahaziah became king in his place.

**41** Jehoshaphat son of Asa became king over Judah in the fourth year of Ahab king of Israel.

**42** Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.

**43** He walked in all the way of his father Asa and did not turn aside from it, doing what was right in the eyes of the LORD.

**44** But the high places were not taken away. The people still sacrificed and burned incense on the high places.[^22]

**45** Jehoshaphat also made peace with the king of Israel.

**46** As for the rest of the acts of Jehoshaphat, the might he showed and how he fought, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Judah?

**47** He also removed from the land the rest of the male shrine prostitutes[^23] who were left from the days of his father Asa.

**48** There was no king in Edom. A deputy[^24] was king.

**49** Jehoshaphat made[^25] ships of Tarshish[^26] to go to Ophir for gold, but they did not go, for the ships were wrecked at Ezion-geber.

**50** Then Ahaziah son of Ahab said to Jehoshaphat, "Let my servants go with your servants in the ships." But Jehoshaphat was not willing.

**51** Then Jehoshaphat slept with his ancestors and was buried with his ancestors in the city of his ancestor David.[^27] And his son Jehoram became king in his place.

**52** Ahaziah son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the seventeenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned over Israel two years.

**53** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD. He walked in the way of his father, in the way of his mother, and in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.

**54** He served Baal and bowed down to him, and he provoked the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger, just as his father had done.

[^1]: Literally "Please seek, as the day, the word of the LORD", that is, today, before going. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", here instead of God's personal name.
[^3]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official; the word can mean "eunuch".
[^4]: Literally "the words of the prophets are one mouth good to the king".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "your words" but read "your word".
[^6]: Literally "He said".
[^7]: Literally "the spirit". Hebrew *ruach* can also mean "wind" or "breath".
[^8]: Literally "The LORD has spoken evil concerning you."
[^9]: Literally "room within room".
[^10]: "The king's son" may be a title for a royal official rather than a son of Ahab.
[^11]: Literally "bread of oppression and water of oppression".
[^12]: The same words begin the book of Micah (Micah 1:2).
[^13]: Literally "in his innocence", that is, without aiming at anyone in particular.
[^14]: Literally "between the attachments and the coat of mail": a gap in the armor.
[^15]: Literally "Turn your hand".
[^16]: Literally "out of the camp", here the army in battle.
[^17]: Literally "and he came".
[^18]: The Hebrew is unclear. Some take it to mean that the prostitutes bathed in the pool, others that the chariot or the armor was washed.
[^19]: A house decorated or inlaid with ivory.
[^20]: Literally "the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel": the royal records, which have not survived (14:19). So also "the annals of the kings of Judah" (verse 46).
[^21]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death. Also in verse 51.
[^22]: Verses 44–54 are 22:43b–53 in most English Bibles: this verse is the second half of verse 43.
[^23]: Hebrew *qadesh*, "consecrated one": probably a man who served at a shrine and took part in sexual acts as part of its worship (14:24).
[^24]: Hebrew *nitsav*, the word used for Solomon's governors (4:5, 7).
[^25]: The Hebrew text is written with a word that would mean "ten" but read "made".
[^26]: Large seagoing ships, of the kind that sailed to Tarshish (10:22).
[^27]: Literally "his father", as often of an ancestor.
