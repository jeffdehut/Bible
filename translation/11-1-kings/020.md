**1** Ben-hadad king of Aram gathered his whole army. Thirty-two kings were with him, with horses and chariots. He went up and besieged Samaria and attacked it.[^1]

**2** He sent messengers into the city to Ahab king of Israel,

**3** and he said to him,[^2] "This is what Ben-hadad says: 'Your silver and your gold are mine, and your wives and the best of your sons are mine.'"

**4** The king of Israel answered, "As you say, my lord the king, I am yours, with all that I have."

**5** The messengers came back and said, "This is what Ben-hadad says: 'I sent to you, saying, "You are to give me your silver and your gold, your wives and your sons."

**6** But at this time tomorrow I will send my servants to you, and they will search your house and the houses of your servants. They will lay their hands on everything you value[^3] and take it away.'"

**7** Then the king of Israel called all the elders of the land and said, "Mark this, and see how this man is looking for trouble. For he sent to me for my wives and my sons, my silver and my gold, and I did not refuse him."

**8** All the elders and all the people said to him, "Do not listen to him or agree."

**9** So he said to Ben-hadad's messengers, "Tell my lord the king, 'All that you first demanded of your servant I will do, but this thing I cannot do.'" The messengers went and brought him the answer.

**10** Then Ben-hadad sent to him and said, "May the gods deal with me, and more besides,[^4] if the dust of Samaria will be enough for a handful for each of the people who follow me."[^5]

**11** The king of Israel answered, "Tell him, 'The one who puts on his armor should not boast like the one who takes it off.'"

**12** When Ben-hadad heard this message, he was drinking with the kings in the shelters.[^6] He said to his servants, "Take your positions!"[^7] So they took their positions against the city.

**13** Just then a prophet came up to Ahab king of Israel and said, "This is what Yahweh[^8] says: 'Have you seen this whole great army? Look, I will hand it over to you today, and you will know that I am Yahweh.'"

**14** Ahab said, "By whom?" He said, "This is what Yahweh says: 'By the young men who serve the district governors.'" Ahab said, "Who will start the battle?" He said, "You."

**15** So Ahab mustered the young men who served the district governors, and they were 232. After them he mustered all the people, all the Israelites, 7,000.

**16** They went out at noon, while Ben-hadad was drinking himself drunk in the shelters, he and the thirty-two kings who were helping him.

**17** The young men who served the district governors went out first. Ben-hadad sent out scouts,[^9] and they told him, "Men have come out from Samaria."

**18** He said, "If they have come out for peace, take them alive. If they have come out for war, take them alive."

**19** So these went out from the city, the young men who served the district governors, with the army behind them.

**20** Each one struck down his man, and the Arameans fled, with Israel pursuing them. But Ben-hadad king of Aram escaped on a horse with some horsemen.

**21** The king of Israel went out and struck down the horses and the chariots, and he defeated the Arameans with a great slaughter.

**22** Then the prophet came to the king of Israel and said to him, "Go, strengthen your forces, and consider carefully what you must do, for in the spring[^10] the king of Aram will come up against you."

**23** Meanwhile the servants of the king of Aram said to him, "Their gods are gods of the hills.[^11] That is why they were stronger than we were. But let us fight them on the plain, and surely we will be stronger than they are.

**24** Do this: remove each of the kings from his post, and put governors[^12] in their place.

**25** Then raise an army like the army you lost, horse for horse and chariot for chariot. Let us fight them on the plain, and surely we will be stronger than they are." He listened to their advice and did so.

**26** In the spring Ben-hadad mustered the Arameans and went up to Aphek to fight against Israel.

**27** The Israelites were mustered and given provisions, and they went out to meet them. The Israelites camped opposite them like two little flocks of goats, while the Arameans filled the countryside.

**28** Then the man of God came near and said to the king of Israel, "This is what Yahweh says: 'Because the Arameans have said, "Yahweh is a god of the hills, but he is not a god of the valleys," I will hand over all this great army to you, and you will know that I am Yahweh.'"

**29** They camped opposite each other for seven days. On the seventh day the battle was joined, and the Israelites struck down a hundred thousand Aramean foot soldiers in one day.

**30** The rest fled into the city of Aphek, and the wall fell on 27,000 of the men who were left. Ben-hadad also fled and came into the city, and he hid in an inner room.[^13]

**31** His servants said to him, "Look, we have heard that the kings of the house of Israel are merciful kings.[^14] Let us put sackcloth around our waists and ropes on our heads and go out to the king of Israel. Perhaps he will spare your life."

**32** So they tied sackcloth around their waists and put ropes on their heads, and they came to the king of Israel and said, "Your servant Ben-hadad says, 'Please let me live.'" He said, "Is he still alive? He is my brother."

**33** The men took this as a sign and quickly caught up his word,[^15] saying, "Yes, your brother Ben-hadad!" He said, "Go and bring him." So Ben-hadad came out to him, and he had him come up into his chariot.

**34** Ben-hadad said to him, "I will give back the cities my father took from your father, and you may set up markets[^16] for yourself in Damascus, as my father did in Samaria." Ahab said, "On the terms of this treaty I will let you go."[^17] So he made a treaty with him and let him go.

**35** Then a man from the company of the prophets[^18] said to his companion by the word of Yahweh, "Strike me, please." But the man refused to strike him.

**36** So he said to him, "Because you have not obeyed the voice of Yahweh, as soon as you leave me, a lion will kill you." When he left him, a lion met him and killed him.

**37** Then he found another man and said, "Strike me, please." So the man struck him and wounded him.

**38** Then the prophet went and waited for the king by the road. He had disguised himself with a bandage[^19] over his eyes.

**39** As the king passed by, he cried out to the king, "Your servant went out into the thick of the battle, and a man turned aside and brought a man to me and said, 'Guard this man. If he goes missing, your life will be for his life, or else you will pay a talent[^20] of silver.'

**40** But while your servant was busy here and there, he was gone." The king of Israel said to him, "That is your sentence. You yourself have decided it."

**41** Then he quickly removed the bandage from his eyes, and the king of Israel recognized him as one of the prophets.

**42** He said to the king, "This is what Yahweh says: 'Because you have let go out of your hand the man I had devoted to destruction,[^21] your life will be for his life, and your people for his people.'"

**43** So the king of Israel went home sullen and angry, and he came to Samaria.

[^1]: In the Septuagint, chapters 20 and 21 come in the reverse order.
[^2]: In most English Bibles, the words "and he said to him, 'This is what Ben-hadad says'" end verse 2.
[^3]: Literally "every delight of your eyes".
[^4]: An oath formula, literally "Thus may the gods do to me, and thus may they add". The verbs are plural.
[^5]: Literally "for handfuls for all the people who are at my feet".
[^6]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths": temporary huts in the camp. Some take it as the town of Succoth. Also in verse 16.
[^7]: Literally "Set!", probably meaning "Get into position" or "Set up the siege works".
[^8]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^9]: Literally "Ben-hadad sent".
[^10]: Literally "at the return of the year". Also in verse 26.
[^11]: Or "Their God is a god of the hills". Hebrew *elohim* can mean either.
[^12]: Hebrew *pachot*, "governors", here probably officers appointed by the king.
[^13]: Literally "room within room".
[^14]: Literally "kings of *chesed*": kings who show loyalty and mercy.
[^15]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Perhaps "quickly took him at his word".
[^16]: Literally "streets": probably quarters in the city for merchants.
[^17]: The Hebrew does not say who speaks these words. They must be Ahab's.
[^18]: Literally "the sons of the prophets": a group or guild of prophets.
[^19]: Hebrew *afer*, perhaps a bandage or headband. Also in verse 41.
[^20]: About 75 pounds (34 kg).
[^21]: Literally "the man of my *cherem*": someone given over completely to God, which in war meant that he was to be destroyed.
