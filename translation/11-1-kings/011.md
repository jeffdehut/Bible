**1** King Solomon loved many foreign women besides Pharaoh's daughter: Moabite, Ammonite, Edomite, Sidonian and Hittite women,

**2** from the nations of which the LORD[^1] had said to the Israelites, "You must not intermarry with them, and they must not intermarry with you,[^2] for they will surely turn your hearts after their gods." Yet Solomon clung to them in love.

**3** He had seven hundred wives of royal birth[^3] and three hundred concubines, and his wives turned his heart away.

**4** When Solomon was old, his wives turned his heart after other gods, and his heart was not fully devoted to the LORD his God, as the heart of his father David had been.

**5** Solomon followed Ashtoreth,[^4] the goddess of the Sidonians, and Milcom,[^5] the detestable idol[^6] of the Ammonites.

**6** So Solomon did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and he did not follow the LORD fully, as his father David had done.

**7** Then Solomon built a high place[^7] for Chemosh, the detestable idol of Moab, on the hill east of Jerusalem,[^8] and for Molech, the detestable idol of the Ammonites.

**8** He did the same for all his foreign wives, who burned incense[^9] and sacrificed to their gods.

**9** So the LORD was angry with Solomon, because his heart had turned away from the LORD, the God of Israel, who had appeared to him twice

**10** and had commanded him about this very thing, that he must not follow other gods. But he did not keep what the LORD commanded.

**11** So the LORD said to Solomon, "Since this is what you have done,[^10] and you have not kept my covenant and my decrees, which I commanded you, I will certainly tear the kingdom away from you and give it to your servant.

**12** Yet for the sake of your father David I will not do it in your lifetime. I will tear it out of the hand of your son.

**13** But I will not tear away the whole kingdom. I will give your son one tribe, for the sake of my servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, which I have chosen."

**14** Then the LORD raised up an adversary[^11] against Solomon, Hadad the Edomite. He was of the royal line in Edom.

**15** Earlier, when David was at war with Edom,[^12] Joab the commander of the army went up to bury the dead, and he struck down every male in Edom.

**16** For Joab and all Israel stayed there six months, until he had cut off every male in Edom.

**17** But Hadad[^13] fled to Egypt with some Edomites who were servants of his father. Hadad was still a young boy.

**18** They set out from Midian and came to Paran. They took men with them from Paran and came to Egypt, to Pharaoh king of Egypt, who gave him a house, provided him with food[^14] and gave him land.

**19** Hadad found great favor with Pharaoh,[^15] so that he gave him as his wife the sister of his own wife, the sister of Queen[^16] Tahpenes.

**20** The sister of Tahpenes bore him his son Genubath, and Tahpenes brought him up[^17] in Pharaoh's house. Genubath lived in Pharaoh's house among Pharaoh's sons.

**21** When Hadad heard in Egypt that David slept with his ancestors[^18] and that Joab the commander of the army was dead, Hadad said to Pharaoh, "Let me go, so that I can return to my own country."

**22** Pharaoh said to him, "What have you lacked with me, that you now want to go to your own country?" He said, "Nothing. But please let me go."[^19]

**23** God also raised up against Solomon[^20] another adversary, Rezon son of Eliada, who had fled from his master Hadadezer king of Zobah.

**24** He gathered men around him and became the leader of a raiding band, when David was killing them.[^21] They went to Damascus and settled there, and they ruled in Damascus.[^22]

**25** He was an adversary of Israel all the days of Solomon, adding to the harm that Hadad did.[^23] He loathed Israel, and he reigned over Aram.[^24]

**26** Jeroboam son of Nebat also rebelled against the king.[^25] He was an Ephraimite[^26] from Zeredah, one of Solomon's servants, and his mother's name was Zeruah, a widow.

**27** This is how he came to rebel against the king. Solomon was building the Millo[^27] and closing up the gap in the wall of the city of his father David.

**28** Jeroboam was a man of great ability.[^28] When Solomon saw how the young man did his work, he put him in charge of all the forced labor[^29] of the house of Joseph.[^30]

**29** At that time, when Jeroboam went out of Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah the Shilonite[^31] met him on the road. Ahijah was wearing a new cloak,[^32] and the two of them were alone in the open country.

**30** Ahijah took hold of the new cloak he was wearing and tore it into twelve pieces.

**31** He said to Jeroboam, "Take ten pieces for yourself, for this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'Look, I am about to tear the kingdom out of Solomon's hand, and I will give you ten tribes.

**32** But he will have one tribe, for the sake of my servant David and for the sake of Jerusalem, the city I have chosen out of all the tribes of Israel.

**33** For they have abandoned me and bowed down to Ashtoreth the goddess of the Sidonians, Chemosh the god of Moab, and Milcom the god of the Ammonites. They have not walked in my ways, doing what is right in my eyes and keeping my decrees and my rulings, as his father David did.[^33]

**34** But I will not take the whole kingdom out of his hand. I will keep him as ruler[^34] all the days of his life, for the sake of my servant David, whom I chose, who kept my commands and my decrees.

**35** I will take the kingdom out of his son's hand and give it to you, ten tribes.

**36** I will give one tribe to his son, so that my servant David may always have a lamp[^35] before me in Jerusalem, the city I have chosen for myself, to put my name there.

**37** As for you, I will take you, and you will reign over all that you desire.[^36] You will be king over Israel.

**38** If you listen to all that I command you, and walk in my ways, and do what is right in my eyes by keeping my decrees and my commands, as my servant David did, I will be with you. I will build you a lasting dynasty,[^37] as I built for David, and I will give Israel to you.

**39** I will humble the descendants of David because of this, but not forever.'"

**40** Solomon tried to kill Jeroboam, but Jeroboam fled to Egypt, to Shishak king of Egypt, and he stayed in Egypt until Solomon died.

**41** As for the rest of the acts of Solomon, and all that he did, and his wisdom, are they not written in the book of the acts of Solomon?[^38]

**42** The time that Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel was forty years.

**43** Then Solomon slept with his ancestors and was buried in the city of his father David. And his son Rehoboam became king in his place.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "You shall not go in among them, and they shall not go in among you." See Deuteronomy 7:3–4.
[^3]: Literally "princesses".
[^4]: Hebrew *Ashtoret*, the Canaanite goddess Astarte. Also in verse 33.
[^5]: The god of the Ammonites, also called Molech (verse 7). Also in verse 33.
[^6]: Hebrew *shiqquts*, "detestable thing", a contemptuous word for an idol. Also in verse 7.
[^7]: Hebrew *bamah*: a place of worship, often on a hilltop (3:2).
[^8]: Literally "on the mountain that is in front of Jerusalem", the Mount of Olives (2 Kings 23:13).
[^9]: Or "burned offerings".
[^10]: Literally "Because this has been with you".
[^11]: Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, opponent". Also in verses 23 and 25.
[^12]: Literally "when David was with Edom". See 2 Samuel 8:13–14.
[^13]: Hebrew *Adad*, another spelling of the name.
[^14]: Literally "said bread for him".
[^15]: Literally "in the eyes of Pharaoh".
[^16]: Hebrew *gevirah*, "great lady": the title of the queen or of the king's mother.
[^17]: Literally "weaned him".
[^18]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^19]: Literally "No, but you must surely send me away."
[^20]: Literally "against him".
[^21]: That is, when David defeated Hadadezer's forces (2 Samuel 8:3–8; 10:18).
[^22]: Literally "and they reigned in Damascus".
[^23]: Literally "and with the evil that Hadad". The Hebrew is abrupt.
[^24]: The kingdom of the Arameans, in what is now Syria, with its capital at Damascus.
[^25]: Literally "lifted up his hand against the king". Also in verse 27.
[^26]: Hebrew *Ephrati*, here meaning someone from the tribe of Ephraim.
[^27]: Hebrew *millo*, "filling": probably a terrace or earthwork supporting buildings in the City of David (9:15).
[^28]: Hebrew *gibbor chayil*, "a mighty man of valor": a man of strength, standing and ability.
[^29]: Hebrew *sevel*, "burden": the work gangs that carried loads.
[^30]: The tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh.
[^31]: From Shiloh, in Ephraim, where the ark had once been kept (1 Samuel 1–4).
[^32]: Literally "he". The new cloak is Ahijah's (verse 30).
[^33]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "he has abandoned me … he has not walked".
[^34]: Hebrew *nasi*, "leader, prince".
[^35]: Hebrew *nir*, probably "lamp", a picture of a family line that continues. Some take it to mean "dominion".
[^36]: Literally "all that your soul desires".
[^37]: Literally "a sure house".
[^38]: This book, like "the book of the annals of the kings of Israel" and "of the kings of Judah" (14:19, 29), has not survived.
