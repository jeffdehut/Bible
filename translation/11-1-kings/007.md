**1** Solomon spent thirteen years building his own house, and he finished his whole house.

**2** He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon,[^1] a hundred cubits[^2] long, fifty cubits wide and thirty cubits high, on four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams on the pillars.

**3** It was roofed with cedar above the beams[^3] that were on the pillars, forty-five of them, fifteen in each row.

**4** There were three rows of window frames,[^4] with window facing window three times.[^5]

**5** All the doorways and doorposts had square frames, with window facing window three times.[^6]

**6** He made the Hall of Pillars fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide. There was a porch in front of the pillars, with pillars and a canopy[^7] in front of them.

**7** He made the Hall of the Throne where he was to judge, the Hall of Judgment. It was lined with cedar from floor to floor.[^8]

**8** His own house where he was to live, in the other courtyard set back from the hall,[^9] was of the same workmanship. Solomon also made a house like this hall for Pharaoh's daughter, whom he had married.

**9** All these were made of costly stones, dressed stone[^10] cut to measure and trimmed with saws on the inside and the outside, from the foundation to the coping,[^11] and on the outside as far as the great courtyard.

**10** The foundation was laid with costly stones, large stones, stones of ten cubits and stones of eight cubits.

**11** Above them were costly stones, dressed stone cut to measure, and cedar.

**12** The great courtyard had three courses of dressed stone all around and one course of cedar beams, as did the inner courtyard of the house of Yahweh[^12] and the porch of the house.

**13** King Solomon sent and brought Hiram[^13] from Tyre.

**14** He was the son of a widow from the tribe of Naphtali,[^14] and his father was a man of Tyre, a craftsman in bronze.[^15] He was filled with wisdom, understanding and skill for doing every kind of work in bronze. He came to King Solomon and did all his work.

**15** He cast[^16] the two pillars of bronze. The one pillar was eighteen cubits high, and a cord of twelve cubits went around the other pillar.[^17]

**16** He also made two capitals[^18] of cast bronze to set on the tops of the pillars. The one capital was five cubits high, and the other capital was five cubits high.

**17** There were lattices of network and wreaths of chainwork[^19] for the capitals on top of the pillars, seven for the one capital and seven for the other capital.

**18** He made the pillars, and there were two rows all around on the one network, to cover the capitals that were on top of the pomegranates.[^20] He did the same for the other capital.

**19** The capitals that were on top of the pillars in the porch were of lily work, four cubits.[^21]

**20** The capitals on the two pillars also had pomegranates above, next to the rounded part[^22] beside the network. There were two hundred pomegranates in rows all around, on the other capital as well.[^23]

**21** He set up the pillars at the porch of the main hall.[^24] He set up the pillar on the right and named it Jakin,[^25] and he set up the pillar on the left and named it Boaz.[^26]

**22** On top of the pillars was lily work. So the work on the pillars was finished.

**23** He made the Sea[^27] of cast metal, ten cubits from rim to rim, round all around and five cubits high. A line of thirty cubits measured around it.[^28]

**24** Under its rim, gourds[^29] encircled it all around, ten to a cubit,[^30] going all around the Sea. The gourds were in two rows, cast in one piece with it.

**25** It stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west,[^31] three facing south and three facing east. The Sea rested on top of them, and all their hindquarters were toward the center.

**26** It was a handbreadth[^32] thick, and its rim was made like the rim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It held two thousand baths.[^33]

**27** He also made the ten stands[^34] of bronze. Each stand was four cubits long, four cubits wide and three cubits high.

**28** This is how the stands were made: they had panels, and the panels were set between the frames.[^35]

**29** On the panels between the frames were lions, oxen and cherubim. On the frames there was a pedestal[^36] above, and below the lions and the oxen were wreaths of hanging work.[^37]

**30** Each stand had four bronze wheels with bronze axles, and its four feet had supports.[^38] The supports were cast underneath the basin,[^39] with wreaths at the side of each.[^40]

**31** Its opening inside the crown[^41] and upward was a cubit. Its opening was round, made like a pedestal, a cubit and a half. On its opening there were also engravings. Their panels were square, not round.

**32** The four wheels were underneath the panels, and the axles[^42] of the wheels were in the stand. Each wheel was a cubit and a half high.

**33** The wheels were made like chariot wheels. Their axles, rims, spokes and hubs were all of cast metal.

**34** There were four supports at the four corners of each stand. The supports were of one piece with the stand.

**35** At the top of the stand there was a round band half a cubit high. On top of the stand, its stays[^43] and its panels were of one piece with it.

**36** On the surfaces of its stays and on its panels he engraved cherubim, lions and palm trees, wherever there was space,[^44] with wreaths all around.

**37** This is how he made the ten stands. All of them were cast alike, with the same measurements and the same shape.

**38** He made ten basins of bronze. Each basin held forty baths, and each basin measured four cubits.[^45] There was one basin on each of the ten stands.

**39** He put five of the stands on the south side of the house and five on the north side.[^46] He put the Sea on the south side of the house, toward the southeast.[^47]

**40** Hiram[^48] also made the basins,[^49] the shovels and the bowls. So Hiram finished all the work that he did for King Solomon on the house of Yahweh:

**41** the two pillars; the two bowls[^50] of the capitals that were on top of the pillars; the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on top of the pillars;

**42** the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the pillars;[^51]

**43** the ten stands, and the ten basins on the stands;

**44** the one Sea, and the twelve oxen under the Sea;

**45** and the pots, the shovels and the bowls. All these utensils[^52] that Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of Yahweh were of polished bronze.

**46** The king cast them in the plain of the Jordan, in the clay ground[^53] between Succoth and Zarethan.

**47** Solomon left all the utensils unweighed, because there were so very many. The weight of the bronze was never determined.

**48** Solomon also made all the utensils that were in the house of Yahweh: the gold altar;[^54] the gold table on which the bread of the Presence[^55] was placed;

**49** the lampstands of pure gold, five on the south side and five on the north,[^56] in front of the inner sanctuary;[^57] the flowers,[^58] the lamps and the tongs, of gold;

**50** the cups,[^59] the wick trimmers, the bowls, the dishes[^60] and the firepans, of pure gold; and the sockets[^61] for the doors of the inner house, the most holy place,[^62] and for the doors of the house, the main hall, of gold.

**51** So all the work that King Solomon did for the house of Yahweh was finished. Then Solomon brought in the things his father David had dedicated:[^63] the silver, the gold and the utensils. He put them in the treasuries of the house of Yahweh.

[^1]: Probably named for its many cedar pillars, standing like a forest. See 10:17, 21.
[^2]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the building was about 150 feet long, 75 feet wide and 45 feet high (45 by 22.5 by 13.5 m).
[^3]: Hebrew *tsela'ot*, literally "ribs" or "sides", the word used for the side rooms of the temple (6:5). It may mean side rooms here too.
[^4]: Hebrew *shequfim*, perhaps "window frames" (6:4) or "beams".
[^5]: Literally "and sight to sight three times". The meaning of this verse and the next is uncertain.
[^6]: Literally "and opposite sight to sight three times".
[^7]: Hebrew *av*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps a canopy, an overhang, or a threshold.
[^8]: Literally "from the floor to the floor": perhaps over the whole floor, or from the floor to the ceiling.
[^9]: Literally "the other courtyard, from the house to the hall".
[^10]: Hebrew *gazit*: stone cut and dressed to shape. Also in verses 11 and 12.
[^11]: Hebrew *tefachot*, perhaps the top course of the wall, or the eaves.
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple.
[^13]: Not King Hiram of Tyre (5:15, English 5:1), but a craftsman with the same name. In 2 Chronicles 2:12 (2:13 in most English Bibles) he is called Huram-abi.
[^14]: 2 Chronicles 2:13 (2:14 in most English Bibles) says his mother was from the tribe of Dan.
[^15]: Or "copper". Hebrew *nechoshet* means both.
[^16]: Literally "formed".
[^17]: That is, each pillar was about 27 feet (8.1 m) high and 18 feet (5.4 m) around.
[^18]: Hebrew *koterot*: the decorated tops of the pillars.
[^19]: Hebrew *gedilim*, "twisted threads", like the tassels of Deuteronomy 22:12; here twisted chains forming wreaths.
[^20]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Many interpreters think "pillars" and "pomegranates" have changed places: "He made the pomegranates in two rows all around on the one network, to cover the capitals that were on top of the pillars."
[^21]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps the lily-shaped part of each capital was four cubits high.
[^22]: Literally "the belly".
[^23]: Literally "and the pomegranates were two hundred, rows all around, on the second capital". Each capital seems to have had two hundred pomegranates (verse 42).
[^24]: Hebrew *heikhal*, the main room of the temple (6:3).
[^25]: *Jakin* probably means "he establishes".
[^26]: *Boaz* probably means "in him is strength".
[^27]: A huge basin of bronze, called "the Sea" for its size. According to 2 Chronicles 4:6 the priests washed in it.
[^28]: The Sea was about 15 feet (4.5 m) across, 7½ feet (2.3 m) high and 45 feet (13.5 m) around.
[^29]: Ornaments shaped like gourds (6:18). Also later in the verse.
[^30]: Or "for ten cubits".
[^31]: Literally "toward the sea", that is, the Mediterranean.
[^32]: About 3 inches (7.5 cm).
[^33]: A bath was about 6 gallons (22 liters), so the Sea held about 12,000 gallons (44,000 liters). 2 Chronicles 4:5 reads "three thousand baths".
[^34]: Hebrew *mekhonot*: bronze stands on wheels, each carrying a basin (verse 38).
[^35]: Hebrew *shelabbim*, perhaps the uprights or crosspieces of the frame. Also in verse 29.
[^36]: Hebrew *ken*, "pedestal" or "stand". Or "On the frames above it was the same". Also in verse 31.
[^37]: Literally "wreaths, work of descent": perhaps hanging garlands, or hammered work.
[^38]: Literally "shoulders". Also in verse 34.
[^39]: Hebrew *kiyyor*, the word used for the bronze basin of the tabernacle (Exodus 30:18). Also in verses 38, 40 and 43.
[^40]: Literally "across from each, wreaths". The meaning is uncertain.
[^41]: Hebrew *koteret*, the word translated "capital" in verse 16; here perhaps a round frame at the top of the stand. The meaning of this verse is uncertain.
[^42]: Literally "hands". Also in verse 33.
[^43]: Literally "hands", here perhaps braces or handles. Also in verse 36.
[^44]: Literally "according to the bare space of each". The meaning is uncertain.
[^45]: Probably its width. Forty baths was about 240 gallons (880 liters).
[^46]: Literally "on the shoulder of the house on the right … on the shoulder of the house on its left". Facing east, the right side is the south.
[^47]: Literally "from the right shoulder of the house, eastward, opposite the south".
[^48]: Hebrew *Hirom*, another spelling of the name.
[^49]: Hebrew *kiyyorot*. Verse 45 and 2 Chronicles 4:11 have a similar word, *sirot*, "pots".
[^50]: Hebrew *gullot*, "bowls": the rounded parts of the capitals. Also in verse 42.
[^51]: Literally "on the face of the pillars".
[^52]: The Hebrew text is written *ha'ohel*, "the tent", but read *ha'elleh*, "these".
[^53]: Literally "in the thickness of the ground", probably clay ground suitable for molds.
[^54]: The altar of incense (6:20–22), called the "gold altar" in Exodus 39:38.
[^55]: Literally "bread of the face": bread set out in Yahweh's presence (Exodus 25:30; Leviticus 24:5–9).
[^56]: Literally "five on the right and five on the left".
[^57]: Hebrew *devir*, the most holy place (6:5, 16).
[^58]: Hebrew *perach*, "blossom": flower-shaped ornaments on the lampstands (Exodus 25:31).
[^59]: Hebrew *sippot*: cups or basins.
[^60]: Hebrew *kappot*, "palms": small dishes, perhaps for incense (Exodus 25:29).
[^61]: Hebrew *potot*, perhaps the sockets for the door pivots.
[^62]: Literally "the holy of holies".
[^63]: Literally "the holy things of David his father".
