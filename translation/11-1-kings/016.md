**1** The word of Yahweh[^1] came to Jehu son of Hanani against Baasha:

**2** "I raised you up from the dust and made you ruler[^2] over my people Israel, but you have walked in the way of Jeroboam and have caused my people Israel to sin, provoking me to anger with their sins.

**3** So I am about to sweep away[^3] Baasha and his house, and I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat.

**4** Anyone belonging to Baasha who dies in the city, the dogs will eat, and anyone of his who dies in the open country, the birds of the sky will eat."

**5** As for the rest of the acts of Baasha, what he did and his might, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?[^4]

**6** Then Baasha slept with his ancestors[^5] and was buried in Tirzah. And his son Elah became king in his place.

**7** Moreover, the word of Yahweh came through the prophet Jehu son of Hanani against Baasha and his house, because of all the evil he did in the eyes of Yahweh, provoking him to anger with the work of his hands and becoming like the house of Jeroboam, and also because he struck it down.[^6]

**8** In the twenty-sixth year of Asa king of Judah, Elah son of Baasha became king over Israel in Tirzah, and he reigned two years.

**9** His servant Zimri, commander of half his chariots, conspired against him. Elah was in Tirzah, drinking himself drunk in the house of Arza, who was in charge of the palace[^7] in Tirzah.

**10** Zimri came in, struck him down and killed him in the twenty-seventh year of Asa king of Judah, and he became king in his place.

**11** As soon as he became king and sat on his throne, he struck down the whole house of Baasha. He did not leave him a single male,[^8] whether relative[^9] or friend.

**12** So Zimri destroyed the whole house of Baasha, according to the word of Yahweh that he had spoken against Baasha through Jehu the prophet,

**13** because of all the sins of Baasha and the sins of his son Elah, which they committed and which they caused Israel to commit, provoking Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger with their worthless idols.[^10]

**14** As for the rest of the acts of Elah, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?

**15** In the twenty-seventh year of Asa king of Judah, Zimri reigned seven days in Tirzah. The army[^11] was encamped against Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines.

**16** When the army in the camp heard it said, "Zimri has conspired and has even struck down the king," all Israel made Omri, the commander of the army, king over Israel that very day in the camp.

**17** Omri and all Israel with him went up from Gibbethon and besieged Tirzah.

**18** When Zimri saw that the city was taken, he went into the citadel[^12] of the king's house and burned the king's house over himself, and he died,

**19** because of the sins[^13] he had committed, doing what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, walking in the way of Jeroboam and in the sin he committed, causing Israel to sin.

**20** As for the rest of the acts of Zimri, and the conspiracy he carried out, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?

**21** Then the people of Israel were divided in two. Half the people followed Tibni son of Ginath, to make him king, and the other half followed Omri.

**22** But the people who followed Omri overpowered the people who followed Tibni son of Ginath. Tibni died,[^14] and Omri became king.

**23** In the thirty-first year of Asa king of Judah, Omri became king over Israel, and he reigned twelve years. He reigned six years in Tirzah.

**24** He bought the hill of Samaria from Shemer for two talents[^15] of silver. He built on the hill and named the city he built Samaria,[^16] after the name of Shemer, the owner of the hill.

**25** Omri did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh. He did more evil than all who were before him.

**26** He walked in all the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat and in his sin,[^17] which he caused Israel to commit, provoking Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger with their worthless idols.

**27** As for the rest of the acts of Omri, what he did and the might he showed, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?

**28** Then Omri slept with his ancestors and was buried in Samaria. And his son Ahab became king in his place.

**29** Ahab son of Omri became king over Israel in the thirty-eighth year of Asa king of Judah, and Ahab son of Omri reigned over Israel in Samaria twenty-two years.

**30** Ahab son of Omri did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, more than all who were before him.

**31** As if it were a trivial thing for him to walk in the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, he married Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians, and he went and served Baal[^18] and bowed down to him.

**32** He set up an altar for Baal in the house of Baal that he built in Samaria.

**33** Ahab also made the Asherah pole.[^19] Ahab did more to provoke Yahweh, the God of Israel, to anger than all the kings of Israel who were before him.

**34** In his days Hiel of Bethel rebuilt Jericho. He laid its foundation at the cost of Abiram his firstborn, and he set up its gates at the cost of Segub his youngest son,[^20] according to the word of Yahweh that he had spoken through Joshua son of Nun.[^21]

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, prince".
[^3]: Or "burn up" (14:10).
[^4]: Literally "the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel": the royal records, which have not survived (14:19).
[^5]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death. Also in verse 28.
[^6]: That is, Baasha was condemned also for destroying the house of Jeroboam (15:27–29).
[^7]: Literally "over the house" (4:6).
[^8]: Literally "one who urinates against the wall", a coarse expression for a male (14:10).
[^9]: Hebrew *go'el*, a relative with the duty to protect the family and avenge its dead.
[^10]: Hebrew *havalim*, "vapors, empty things": a contemptuous word for idols. Also in verse 26.
[^11]: Literally "the people". Also in verse 16.
[^12]: Hebrew *armon*, the fortified tower or keep of the palace.
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "his sin" but read "his sins".
[^14]: The Septuagint adds "and his brother Joram at that time".
[^15]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so two talents were about 150 pounds (68 kg).
[^16]: Hebrew *Shomron*, a name that sounds like *Shemer*.
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "his sins" but read "his sin".
[^18]: *Baal*, "lord, master": the Canaanite storm god, worshiped as the giver of rain and fertility.
[^19]: Hebrew *ha-asherah*: a wooden pole or tree that was a symbol of the goddess Asherah (14:15).
[^20]: That is, these sons died, the first when the foundation was laid and the last when the gates were set up.
[^21]: See Joshua 6:26.
