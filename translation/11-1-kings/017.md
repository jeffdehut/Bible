**1** Now Elijah the Tishbite, one of the settlers in Gilead,[^1] said to Ahab, "As the LORD,[^2] the God of Israel, lives, whom I serve,[^3] there will be no dew or rain in these years except at my word."

**2** Then the word of the LORD came to him:

**3** "Leave here, turn east and hide in the Kerith Ravine,[^4] east of the Jordan.[^5]

**4** You will drink from the stream, and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

**5** So he went and did as the LORD had said. He went and stayed in the Kerith Ravine, east of the Jordan.

**6** The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning, and bread and meat in the evening, and he drank from the stream.

**7** After a while the stream dried up, because there had been no rain in the land.

**8** Then the word of the LORD came to him:

**9** "Get up, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and stay there. I have commanded a widow there to feed you."

**10** So he got up and went to Zarephath. When he came to the gate of the town, a widow was there gathering sticks. He called to her and said, "Please bring me a little water in a jar, so that I can drink."

**11** As she was going to get it, he called to her and said, "Please bring me a piece of bread in your hand."

**12** She said, "As surely as the LORD your God lives, I have nothing baked,[^6] only a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug. Look, I am gathering a couple of sticks, so that I can go in and prepare it for myself and my son, and we will eat it and die."

**13** Elijah said to her, "Do not be afraid. Go and do as you have said. But first make me a small cake from it and bring it out to me. After that make something for yourself and your son.

**14** For this is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: The jar of flour will not be used up, and the jug of oil will not run dry, until the day the LORD sends rain on the face of the earth."

**15** She went and did as Elijah had said, and she and he and her household[^7] ate for many days.

**16** The jar of flour was not used up, and the jug of oil did not run dry, according to the word of the LORD that he had spoken through Elijah.

**17** Some time later the son of the woman who owned the house became sick. His illness grew so severe that there was no breath left in him.

**18** She said to Elijah, "What have you against me, man of God?[^8] Have you come to me to bring my sin to remembrance and to kill my son?"

**19** He said to her, "Give me your son." He took him from her arms,[^9] carried him up to the upper room where he was staying, and laid him on his own bed.

**20** Then he cried out to the LORD, "LORD my God, have you brought disaster even on the widow I am staying with, by killing her son?"

**21** Then he stretched himself out on the child three times and cried out to the LORD, "LORD my God, please let this child's life[^10] come back into him!"

**22** The LORD listened to Elijah's voice, and the child's life came back into him, and he lived.

**23** Elijah took the child and brought him down from the upper room into the house and gave him to his mother. Elijah said, "Look, your son is alive."

**24** The woman said to Elijah, "Now I know that you are a man of God, and that the word of the LORD in your mouth is truth."

[^1]: Hebrew *mittoshavei Gil'ad*, "from the settlers of Gilead". The Septuagint reads the same consonants as "from Tishbe in Gilead".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "before whom I stand".
[^4]: Hebrew *nachal Kerit*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. Also in verse 5.
[^5]: Literally "which is in front of the Jordan". Also in verse 5.
[^6]: Hebrew *ma'og*, "cake", a round loaf baked on hot stones.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "he and she" but read "she and he".
[^8]: Literally "What is there between me and you?"
[^9]: Literally "from her bosom".
[^10]: Hebrew *nephesh*: life, soul, self. Also in verse 22.
