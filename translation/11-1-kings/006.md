**1** In the four hundred and eightieth year[^1] after the Israelites came out of the land of Egypt, in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel, in the month of Ziv,[^2] which is the second month, he began to build[^3] the house for Yahweh.[^4]

**2** The house that King Solomon built for Yahweh was sixty cubits[^5] long, twenty wide and thirty cubits high.

**3** The porch[^6] in front of the main hall[^7] of the house was twenty cubits long, across the width of the house, and it extended ten cubits in front of the house.[^8]

**4** He made windows for the house with recessed frames.[^9]

**5** Against the wall of the house he built a side structure[^10] all around, against the walls of the house all around the main hall and the inner sanctuary,[^11] and he made side rooms[^12] all around.

**6** The lowest story was five cubits wide, the middle one six cubits wide and the third seven cubits wide. For he made offsets[^13] all around the outside of the house, so that the beams would not be set into the walls of the house.

**7** When the house was built, it was built with stone finished at the quarry.[^14] No hammer or axe or any iron tool was heard in the house while it was being built.

**8** The entrance to the middle story of side rooms[^15] was on the south side of the house.[^16] Stairs[^17] went up to the middle story, and from the middle story to the third.

**9** So he built the house and finished it. He roofed the house with beams and planks of cedar.[^18]

**10** He built the side structure against the whole house, each story five cubits high, and it was joined to the house with cedar beams.

**11** Then the word of Yahweh came to Solomon:

**12** "As for this house you are building, if you walk in my statutes, carry out my rulings and keep all my commandments by walking in them, I will fulfill through you the promise I made to your father David.[^19]

**13** I will live[^20] among the Israelites, and I will not abandon my people Israel."

**14** So Solomon built the house and finished it.

**15** He lined the walls of the house on the inside with boards of cedar. From the floor of the house up to the ceiling[^21] he covered them with wood on the inside, and he covered the floor of the house with boards of cypress.[^22]

**16** He built off twenty cubits at the rear of the house with boards of cedar from the floor up to the ceiling.[^23] He built this inside as an inner sanctuary, as the most holy place.[^24]

**17** The house, that is, the main hall in front of it,[^25] was forty cubits long.

**18** The cedar inside the house was carved with gourds and open flowers.[^26] All was cedar. No stone was seen.

**19** He prepared the inner sanctuary inside the house, to put the ark of the covenant of Yahweh there.

**20** The inside of the inner sanctuary[^27] was twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and twenty cubits high, and he overlaid it with pure gold.[^28] He also overlaid the altar with cedar.[^29]

**21** Solomon overlaid the inside of the house with pure gold. He stretched chains of gold across the front of the inner sanctuary, and he overlaid it with gold.

**22** He overlaid the whole house with gold, until the whole house was finished. He also overlaid with gold the whole altar that belonged to the inner sanctuary.[^30]

**23** In the inner sanctuary he made two cherubim[^31] of olive wood,[^32] each ten cubits high.

**24** One wing of the cherub was five cubits long, and the other wing of the cherub was five cubits long: ten cubits from the tip of one wing to the tip of the other.

**25** The second cherub also measured ten cubits. Both cherubim had the same measurements and the same shape.

**26** The height of the one cherub was ten cubits, and so was that of the other cherub.

**27** He put the cherubim in the middle of the inner room of the house.[^33] The wings of the cherubim were spread out, so that the wing of one touched the one wall and the wing of the other cherub touched the other wall, while their wings touched each other in the middle of the room, wing to wing.

**28** He overlaid the cherubim with gold.

**29** On all the walls of the house all around he carved engravings of cherubim, palm trees and open flowers, in both the inner and the outer rooms.[^34]

**30** He overlaid the floor of the house with gold, in both the inner and the outer rooms.

**31** For the entrance of the inner sanctuary he made doors of olive wood. The lintel and the doorposts formed a fifth.[^35]

**32** On the two doors of olive wood he carved cherubim, palm trees and open flowers, and he overlaid them with gold. He spread the gold over the cherubim and the palm trees.[^36]

**33** In the same way, for the entrance of the main hall he made doorposts of olive wood, a fourth.[^37]

**34** There were two doors of cypress wood. The two leaves of the one door turned on pivots, and the two leaves[^38] of the other door turned on pivots.

**35** He carved cherubim, palm trees and open flowers on them, and he overlaid them with gold fitted evenly over the carving.

**36** He built the inner courtyard with three courses of dressed stone[^39] and one course of cedar beams.

**37** In the fourth year the foundation of the house of Yahweh was laid, in the month of Ziv.

**38** In the eleventh year, in the month of Bul,[^40] which is the eighth month, the house was finished in all its details and according to all its specifications.[^41] He spent seven years building it.

[^1]: The Septuagint reads "the four hundred and fortieth year".
[^2]: An old name for the second month, around April–May. Also in verse 37.
[^3]: Literally "he built".
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. The Hebrew calls the temple simply "the house".
[^5]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the house was about 90 feet long, 30 feet wide and 45 feet high (27 by 9 by 13.5 m).
[^6]: Hebrew *ulam*: an entrance hall in front of the main hall; traditionally "porch" or "vestibule".
[^7]: Hebrew *heikhal*, "palace, temple": here the large main room of the house, in front of the inner sanctuary. Traditionally "the temple" or "the nave".
[^8]: Literally "ten cubits was its width in front of the house".
[^9]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Perhaps "windows with closed lattices" or "narrow windows set in frames".
[^10]: Hebrew *yatsia*: a structure of three stories built against the outside walls of the house and divided into side rooms. Also in verse 10.
[^11]: Hebrew *devir*: the innermost room, the most holy place (verse 16). Traditionally "the oracle".
[^12]: Literally "ribs" or "sides". Also in verse 8.
[^13]: Hebrew *migra'ot*, "reductions": ledges where the wall became thinner at each story, on which the beams of the side structure rested.
[^14]: Literally "whole stone of the quarry".
[^15]: The Septuagint reads "the lowest story".
[^16]: Literally "on the right shoulder of the house". Directions were given facing east, so the right side is the south.
[^17]: Hebrew *lulim*, a rare word: perhaps winding stairs, or trapdoors or shafts.
[^18]: The meaning of the Hebrew words *gevim* and *sederot* is uncertain.
[^19]: Literally "I will establish my word with you, which I spoke to David your father." See 2 Samuel 7:12–16.
[^20]: Hebrew *shakhan*, "dwell", the verb from which *mishkan*, "tabernacle", comes.
[^21]: Literally "up to the walls of the ceiling".
[^22]: Hebrew *berosh*, a tall conifer: perhaps the cypress or the juniper (5:22). Also in verse 34.
[^23]: Literally "up to the walls".
[^24]: Literally "the holy of holies".
[^25]: The Hebrew word translated "in front of it" (*lifnai*) is unusual, and the meaning is uncertain.
[^26]: Carved ornaments shaped like gourds and like flowers opening. Also in verses 29, 32 and 35.
[^27]: Literally "And before the inner sanctuary". The meaning is uncertain.
[^28]: Hebrew *zahav sagur*, perhaps "refined gold" or "solid gold". Also in verse 21.
[^29]: Or "He also overlaid an altar of cedar." This is probably the altar of incense, which stood in front of the inner sanctuary (verse 22; 7:48; Exodus 30:1–6).
[^30]: Literally "the whole altar that was to the inner sanctuary", that is, the altar in front of it.
[^31]: Hebrew *keruvim* (singular *keruv*, "cherub"): winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (Genesis 3:24; Exodus 25:18–22).
[^32]: Literally "trees of oil": the olive tree, or another tree rich in oil or resin. Also in verses 31–33.
[^33]: Literally "the inner house", that is, the inner sanctuary.
[^34]: Literally "from the inside and to the outside": the inner sanctuary and the main hall. Also in verse 30.
[^35]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps the frame of the doorway had five sides, or the doorway was a fifth of the width of the wall.
[^36]: Or "hammered the gold onto".
[^37]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps the frame had four sides, or the doorway was a fourth of the width of the wall.
[^38]: The Hebrew here has *qela'im*, a word usually meaning "curtains" or "carvings", where the first half of the verse has *tsela'im*, "leaves". The sense is probably the same.
[^39]: Hebrew *gazit*: stone cut and dressed to shape.
[^40]: An old name for the eighth month, around October–November.
[^41]: The Hebrew text is written "its specification" but read "its specifications".
