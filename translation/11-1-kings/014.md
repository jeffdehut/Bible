**1** At that time Abijah son of Jeroboam became sick.

**2** Jeroboam said to his wife, "Get up and disguise yourself, so that no one will know you are Jeroboam's wife. Then go to Shiloh. Ahijah the prophet is there, the one who told me I would be king over this people.

**3** Take with you ten loaves of bread, some cakes[^1] and a jar of honey, and go to him. He will tell you what will happen to the boy."

**4** Jeroboam's wife did so. She got up and went to Shiloh and came to Ahijah's house. Now Ahijah could not see, for his eyes had grown dim[^2] because of his age.

**5** But Yahweh[^3] had said to Ahijah, "Jeroboam's wife is coming to ask you about her son, for he is sick. This is what you are to say to her.[^4] When she comes, she will be pretending to be someone else."

**6** When Ahijah heard the sound of her feet as she came in at the door, he said, "Come in, wife of Jeroboam. Why are you pretending to be someone else? I have been sent to you with hard news.

**7** Go and tell Jeroboam, 'This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: I raised you up from among the people and made you ruler[^5] over my people Israel.

**8** I tore the kingdom away from the house of David and gave it to you. But you have not been like my servant David, who kept my commands and followed me with all his heart, doing only what was right in my eyes.

**9** You have done more evil than all who were before you. You have gone and made yourself other gods and images of cast metal,[^6] provoking me to anger, and you have thrown me behind your back.

**10** Therefore I am going to bring disaster on the house of Jeroboam. I will cut off from Jeroboam every male,[^7] whether slave or free,[^8] in Israel. I will sweep away[^9] the house of Jeroboam, as one sweeps away dung, until it is all gone.

**11** Anyone belonging to Jeroboam who dies in the city, the dogs will eat, and anyone who dies in the open country, the birds of the sky will eat. For Yahweh has spoken.'

**12** As for you, get up and go home. When your feet enter the city, the child will die.

**13** All Israel will mourn for him and bury him. He is the only one of Jeroboam's family who will come to a grave, because in him alone, in the house of Jeroboam, something good has been found toward Yahweh, the God of Israel.

**14** Yahweh will raise up for himself a king over Israel who will cut off the house of Jeroboam. This is the day! What more? Even now![^10]

**15** Yahweh will strike Israel, until it shakes like a reed in the water. He will uproot Israel from this good land that he gave to their ancestors and scatter them beyond the River,[^11] because they have made their Asherah poles,[^12] provoking Yahweh to anger.

**16** He will give Israel up because of the sins of Jeroboam, which he committed and which he caused Israel to commit."

**17** Then Jeroboam's wife got up and left and came to Tirzah. As she came over the threshold of the house, the boy died.

**18** They buried him, and all Israel mourned for him, according to the word of Yahweh that he had spoken through his servant Ahijah the prophet.

**19** As for the rest of the acts of Jeroboam, how he fought and how he reigned, they are written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel.[^13]

**20** The time that Jeroboam reigned was twenty-two years. Then he slept with his ancestors,[^14] and his son Nadab became king in his place.

**21** Rehoboam son of Solomon reigned in Judah. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city Yahweh had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite.

**22** Judah did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh. With the sins they committed they provoked him to jealousy more than all their ancestors had done.

**23** They too built themselves high places,[^15] standing stones[^16] and Asherah poles on every high hill and under every green tree.[^17]

**24** There were even male shrine prostitutes[^18] in the land. They did all the detestable practices of the nations that Yahweh had driven out before the Israelites.

**25** In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem.[^19]

**26** He took away the treasures of the house of Yahweh and the treasures of the king's house. He took everything. He also took all the gold shields that Solomon had made.

**27** So King Rehoboam made bronze shields to replace them, and he entrusted them to the commanders of the guard[^20] who kept watch at the entrance of the king's house.

**28** Whenever the king went into the house of Yahweh, the guards carried them, and then they brought them back to the guardroom.

**29** As for the rest of the acts of Rehoboam, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Judah?

**30** There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam continually.[^21]

**31** Then Rehoboam slept with his ancestors and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. His mother's name was Naamah the Ammonite. And his son Abijam[^22] became king in his place.

[^1]: Hebrew *niqqudim*, perhaps crumbly cakes or biscuits.
[^2]: Literally "his eyes had stood still".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "Thus and thus you shall speak to her."
[^5]: Hebrew *nagid*, "leader, prince".
[^6]: Hebrew *massekhot*, images made of molten metal (Exodus 32:4).
[^7]: Literally "one who urinates against the wall", a coarse expression for a male.
[^8]: Hebrew *atsur ve'azuv*, "shut up and left": a phrase of uncertain meaning that covers everyone. Perhaps "slave and free", or "under restraint and at large".
[^9]: Or "burn up". Also later in the verse.
[^10]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "This the day, and what? Even now."
[^11]: The Euphrates.
[^12]: Hebrew *asherim*: wooden poles or trees that were symbols of the goddess Asherah. Also in verse 23.
[^13]: Literally "the book of the words of the days of the kings of Israel": the royal records, which have not survived. So also "the annals of the kings of Judah" (verse 29).
[^14]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death. Also in verse 31.
[^15]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops (3:2).
[^16]: Hebrew *matsevot*: standing stones set up as objects of worship.
[^17]: Or "every leafy tree".
[^18]: Hebrew *qadesh*, "consecrated one": probably a man who served at a shrine and took part in sexual acts as part of its worship (Deuteronomy 23:18). The exact practice is uncertain.
[^19]: Pharaoh Sheshonq I, who led a campaign into Judah and Israel about 925 BC.
[^20]: Literally "the runners": the king's bodyguard, who ran ahead of his chariot (1:5). Also in verse 28.
[^21]: Literally "all the days".
[^22]: Called Abijah in 2 Chronicles 12:16–14:1.
