**1** Then the Israelites set out and camped in the plains of Moab, on the other side of the Jordan from Jericho.[^1]

**2** Balak son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.

**3** Moab was terrified of the people, because there were so many of them. Moab was filled with dread because of the Israelites.

**4** Moab said to the elders of Midian, "This horde[^2] will now lick up everything around us, as an ox licks up the grass of the field." Balak son of Zippor was king of Moab at that time.

**5** He sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, which is by the River,[^3] in the land of his own people,[^4] to summon him. He said, "Look, a people has come out of Egypt. They cover the face of the land,[^5] and they have settled opposite me.

**6** Now please come and curse this people for me, for they are too strong for me. Perhaps I will be able to defeat them and drive them out of the land. For I know that whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed."

**7** So the elders of Moab and the elders of Midian set out, with the fee for divination[^6] in their hands. They came to Balaam and told him what Balak had said.

**8** Balaam said to them, "Stay here tonight, and I will bring you back whatever answer the LORD[^7] gives me." So the officials of Moab stayed with Balaam.

**9** Then God came to Balaam and said, "Who are these men with you?"

**10** Balaam said to God, "Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, has sent them to me with this message:

**11** 'Look, the people that has come out of Egypt covers the face of the land. Now come and put a curse on them for me. Perhaps I will be able to fight against them and drive them out.'"

**12** But God said to Balaam, "Do not go with them. You must not curse the people, for they are blessed."

**13** Balaam got up in the morning and said to Balak's officials, "Go back to your own country, for the LORD has refused to let me go with you."

**14** So the officials of Moab got up and went back to Balak and said, "Balaam refused to come with us."

**15** Balak sent officials once again, more of them and more distinguished than the first.

**16** They came to Balaam and said to him, "This is what Balak son of Zippor says: Please do not let anything stop you from coming to me.

**17** For I will reward you very richly,[^8] and I will do whatever you tell me. Please come and put a curse on this people for me."

**18** But Balaam answered Balak's servants, "Even if Balak gave me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the command[^9] of the LORD my God to do anything, small or great.

**19** Now please stay here tonight as well, so that I may find out what more the LORD will say to me."

**20** God came to Balaam at night and said to him, "If the men have come to summon you, get up and go with them. But you must do only what I tell you."

**21** So Balaam got up in the morning, saddled his donkey, and went with the officials of Moab.

**22** But God's anger burned because he was going, and the angel of the LORD[^10] took his stand in the road to oppose him.[^11] Balaam was riding on his donkey, and his two servants were with him.

**23** The donkey saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his sword drawn in his hand, so the donkey turned off the road and went into the field. Balaam beat the donkey to turn her back onto the road.

**24** Then the angel of the LORD stood in a narrow path between the vineyards, with a wall on one side and a wall on the other.

**25** When the donkey saw the angel of the LORD, she pressed herself against the wall and crushed Balaam's foot against the wall. So he beat her again.

**26** Then the angel of the LORD went on ahead and stood in a narrow place where there was no room to turn aside, either to the right or to the left.

**27** When the donkey saw the angel of the LORD, she lay down under Balaam. Balaam's anger burned, and he beat the donkey with his stick.

**28** Then the LORD opened the donkey's mouth, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you, that you have beaten me these three times?"

**29** Balaam said to the donkey, "You have made a fool of me! If I had a sword in my hand, I would kill you right now."

**30** The donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not your donkey, which you have ridden all your life until this day? Have I ever been in the habit of doing this to you?" "No," he said.

**31** Then the LORD opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his sword drawn in his hand. He bowed down and fell face down to the ground.

**32** The angel of the LORD said to him, "Why have you beaten your donkey these three times? I have come out to oppose you,[^12] because your way is reckless before me.[^13]

**33** The donkey saw me and turned aside from me these three times. If she had not turned aside from me,[^14] I would certainly have killed you by now and let her live."

**34** Balaam said to the angel of the LORD, "I have sinned. I did not know that you were standing in the road against me. Now if this displeases you, I will go back."

**35** The angel of the LORD said to Balaam, "Go with the men, but you must say only what I tell you." So Balaam went with Balak's officials.

**36** When Balak heard that Balaam was coming, he went out to meet him at Ir-moab,[^15] which is on the Arnon border, at the farthest edge of the territory.

**37** Balak said to Balaam, "Did I not send for you urgently to summon you? Why did you not come to me? Am I really not able to reward you?"

**38** Balaam said to Balak, "Well, I have come to you now. But do I have the power to say anything at all? I can speak only the word that God puts in my mouth."

**39** Then Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath-huzoth.[^16]

**40** Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep, and sent some to Balaam and to the officials who were with him.

**41** In the morning Balak took Balaam and brought him up to Bamoth-baal.[^17] From there he could see the edge of the people.[^18]

[^1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan of Jericho": on the east side of the Jordan, opposite Jericho.
[^2]: Literally "the assembly".
[^3]: The Euphrates.
[^4]: Literally "the land of the sons of his people". The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "the land of the Ammonites".
[^5]: Literally "the eye of the land". Also in verse 11.
[^6]: Hebrew *qesamim*, "divinations": payment for divining, or perhaps the objects used in divination.
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: Literally "I will greatly honor you". Also in verse 37.
[^9]: Literally "the mouth".
[^10]: Or "the messenger of the LORD". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both.
[^11]: Hebrew *le-satan*, "as an adversary".
[^12]: Literally "as an adversary (*satan*)".
[^13]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Or "because the journey is against my will".
[^14]: Hebrew *ulay*, usually "perhaps", here taken in the sense "if not, unless". Literally "Perhaps she turned aside from me."
[^15]: Or "the city of Moab".
[^16]: The name means "town of streets".
[^17]: The name means "the high places of Baal".
[^18]: Or "a part of the people".
