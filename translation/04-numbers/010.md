**1** The LORD[^1] said to Moses,

**2** "Make two silver trumpets.[^2] Make them of hammered work.[^3] You are to use them for calling the community together and for setting the camps on the move.

**3** When both are blown,[^4] the whole community is to gather before you at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**4** If only one is blown, the leaders, the heads of the clans[^5] of Israel, are to gather before you.

**5** When you sound an alarm,[^6] the camps on the east side are to set out.

**6** When you sound a second alarm, the camps on the south side are to set out. An alarm is to be sounded whenever they are to set out.[^7]

**7** But when the assembly is to be gathered, you are to blow the trumpets without sounding an alarm.

**8** The sons of Aaron, the priests, are to blow the trumpets. This is to be a permanent decree for you throughout your generations.

**9** When you go to war in your land against an enemy who is oppressing you, sound an alarm on the trumpets. Then you will be remembered before the LORD your God, and you will be saved from your enemies.

**10** Also on your days of rejoicing, at your appointed festivals and at the beginning of your months, blow the trumpets over your burnt offerings and over your fellowship offerings.[^8] They will be a reminder of you before your God. I am the LORD your God."

**11** In the second year, on the twentieth day of the second month, the cloud lifted from above the tabernacle of the testimony.

**12** The Israelites set out from the wilderness of Sinai, journeying in stages, and the cloud settled in the wilderness of Paran.

**13** They set out for the first time at the LORD's command through Moses.

**14** The banner of the camp of Judah set out first, by their divisions. Nahshon son of Amminadab was over its division.

**15** Nethanel son of Zuar was over the division of the tribe of Issachar,

**16** and Eliab son of Helon was over the division of the tribe of Zebulun.

**17** Then the tabernacle was taken down, and the Gershonites and the Merarites, who carried the tabernacle, set out.

**18** Next the banner of the camp of Reuben set out, by their divisions. Elizur son of Shedeur was over its division.

**19** Shelumiel son of Zurishaddai was over the division of the tribe of Simeon,

**20** and Eliasaph son of Deuel was over the division of the tribe of Gad.

**21** Then the Kohathites set out, carrying the holy things.[^9] The tabernacle was to be set up before they arrived.

**22** Next the banner of the camp of Ephraim set out, by their divisions. Elishama son of Ammihud was over its division.

**23** Gamaliel son of Pedahzur was over the division of the tribe of Manasseh,

**24** and Abidan son of Gideoni was over the division of the tribe of Benjamin.

**25** Then the banner of the camp of Dan set out, by their divisions, as the rear guard for all the camps. Ahiezer son of Ammishaddai was over its division.

**26** Pagiel son of Ochran was over the division of the tribe of Asher,

**27** and Ahira son of Enan was over the division of the tribe of Naphtali.

**28** This was the order of march of the Israelites by their divisions when they set out.

**29** Moses said to Hobab son of Reuel the Midianite, Moses' father-in-law,[^10] "We are setting out for the place about which the LORD said, 'I will give it to you.' Come with us, and we will treat you well, for the LORD has promised good things to Israel."

**30** But he said to him, "I will not go. I will go back to my own land and my own relatives."

**31** Moses said, "Please do not leave us. You know where we should camp in the wilderness, and you can be our eyes.

**32** If you come with us, whatever good the LORD does for us, we will do the same for you."

**33** So they set out from the mountain of the LORD and traveled for three days. The ark of the covenant of the LORD went ahead of them during those three days to look for a place for them to rest.

**34** The cloud of the LORD was over them by day when they set out from the camp.

**35** Whenever the ark set out, Moses would say,[^11] "Rise up, LORD! May your enemies be scattered, and may those who hate you flee before you."[^12]

**36** And whenever it came to rest, he would say, "Return, LORD, to the countless thousands of Israel."[^13]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *chatsotsrot*: straight metal trumpets, not the ram's horn (*shofar*).
[^3]: Hebrew *miqshah*: hammered out of a single piece of metal.
[^4]: Literally "When they blow them".
[^5]: Literally "the thousands"; see the note on 1:16.
[^6]: Hebrew *teru'ah*: a loud blast or shout, perhaps a series of short notes, as distinct from the plain blast of verse 7. Also in verses 6 and 9.
[^7]: The Septuagint adds instructions for a third and a fourth alarm, for the camps on the west and on the north.
[^8]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers.
[^9]: Literally "the sanctuary", here the holy objects of the sanctuary (4:4–15).
[^10]: The Hebrew may mean that either Reuel or Hobab was Moses' father-in-law. Reuel is Moses' father-in-law in Exodus 2:18–21, where he is a priest of Midian, but in Judges 4:11 Hobab is called Moses' father-in-law. Elsewhere Moses' father-in-law is called Jethro (Exodus 3:1).
[^11]: In the Hebrew text verses 35–36 are set off by two marks shaped like the letter *nun* written upside down, one before verse 35 and one after verse 36. Similar marks appear in Psalm 107. The rabbis understood the marks to mean that these two verses are not in their original place, or that they form a book of their own. The Septuagint places verse 34 after verse 36.
[^12]: Compare Psalm 68:2 (68:1 in most English Bibles).
[^13]: Literally "the ten thousands of the thousands of Israel". Or "Bring back, LORD, the countless thousands of Israel."
