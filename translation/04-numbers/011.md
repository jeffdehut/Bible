**1** The people began complaining about their hardships in the hearing of the LORD.[^1] When the LORD heard it, his anger burned. Fire from the LORD burned among them and consumed the outskirts of the camp.

**2** The people cried out to Moses, Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire died down.

**3** So that place was named Taberah,[^2] because fire from the LORD had burned among them.

**4** The mixed crowd[^3] among them had a strong craving, and the Israelites also began weeping again. They said, "If only we had meat to eat!

**5** We remember the fish we used to eat in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions and the garlic.

**6** But now our appetite is gone.[^4] There is nothing at all, nothing for us to look at but this manna!"

**7** The manna was like coriander seed, and it looked like bdellium.[^5]

**8** The people went around gathering it. They ground it in hand mills or crushed it in a mortar, boiled it in a pot, and made it into cakes. It tasted like something made with olive oil.[^6]

**9** When the dew fell on the camp at night, the manna fell with it.

**10** Moses heard the people weeping, family by family, each at the entrance of his tent. The LORD became very angry, and Moses was troubled.[^7]

**11** Moses said to the LORD, "Why have you treated your servant so badly? Why have I not found favor in your eyes, that you put the burden of all this people on me?

**12** Did I conceive all this people? Did I give birth to them, that you should say to me, 'Carry them in your arms,[^8] as a nurse[^9] carries a nursing baby,' to the land you swore to give their ancestors?

**13** Where am I to get meat to give to all this people? They keep weeping to me and saying, 'Give us meat to eat!'

**14** I cannot carry all this people by myself. It is too heavy for me.

**15** If this is how you are going to treat me, please kill me at once, if I have found favor in your eyes, and do not let me see my own misery."

**16** The LORD said to Moses, "Gather for me seventy men from the elders of Israel, men you know to be elders of the people and officials[^10] over them. Bring them to the tent of meeting, and have them stand there with you.

**17** I will come down and speak with you there. I will take some of the spirit[^11] that is on you and put it on them. They will carry the burden of the people with you, so that you will not have to carry it alone.

**18** "And say to the people: 'Consecrate yourselves for tomorrow, and you will eat meat. You wept in the hearing of the LORD and said, "If only we had meat to eat! We were better off in Egypt!" So the LORD will give you meat, and you will eat it.

**19** You will eat it not just for one day, or two days, or five days, or ten days, or twenty days,

**20** but for a whole month, until it comes out of your nostrils and you are sick of it, because you have rejected the LORD who is among you, and you have wept before him, saying, "Why did we ever leave Egypt?"'"

**21** But Moses said, "The people I am among number six hundred thousand men on foot, and you say, 'I will give them meat, and they will eat it for a whole month'!

**22** Could enough flocks and herds be slaughtered for them to have enough? Could all the fish of the sea be caught for them to have enough?"

**23** The LORD said to Moses, "Is the LORD's arm too short?[^12] Now you will see whether or not what I say will happen to you."

**24** So Moses went out and told the people the LORD's words. He gathered seventy men from the elders of the people and had them stand around the tent.

**25** Then the LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him. He took some of the spirit that was on him and put it on the seventy elders. When the spirit rested on them, they prophesied, but they did not do so again.[^13]

**26** Two men had stayed behind in the camp. One was named Eldad and the other Medad. The spirit rested on them too. They were among those whose names had been written down, but they had not gone out to the tent, and they prophesied in the camp.

**27** A young man ran and told Moses, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp!"

**28** Joshua son of Nun, who had been Moses' assistant since his youth,[^14] spoke up and said, "Moses, my lord, stop them!"

**29** But Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for my sake? I wish that all the LORD's people were prophets, and that the LORD would put his spirit on them!"

**30** Then Moses went back to the camp, he and the elders of Israel.

**31** A wind[^15] went out from the LORD and drove quail in from the sea. It brought them down all around the camp, about a day's journey on one side and a day's journey on the other, about two cubits[^16] deep on the ground.

**32** All that day and all night and all the next day the people went out and gathered the quail. No one gathered less than ten homers.[^17] They spread them out for themselves all around the camp.

**33** But while the meat was still between their teeth, before it was chewed,[^18] the LORD's anger burned against the people, and the LORD struck the people with a very severe plague.

**34** So that place was named Kibroth-hattaavah,[^19] because there they buried the people who had had the craving.

**35** From Kibroth-hattaavah the people set out for Hazeroth, and they stayed at Hazeroth.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. The first part of the verse is literally "The people were like those complaining of evil in the ears of the LORD."
[^2]: *Taberah* means "burning".
[^3]: Hebrew *asafsuf*, "rabble", a rare word: probably the people of other nations who had left Egypt with the Israelites (Exodus 12:38).
[^4]: Literally "our *nephesh* is dried up". *Nephesh* can mean "throat", "appetite" or "life".
[^5]: An aromatic, yellowish resin (Genesis 2:12). Literally "its eye was like the eye of bdellium".
[^6]: Hebrew *leshad ha-shemen*, of uncertain meaning; perhaps "cream of oil" or "cakes baked with oil".
[^7]: Literally "and in the eyes of Moses it was evil".
[^8]: Literally "in your bosom".
[^9]: Hebrew *omen*, a guardian or foster parent who looks after a child. The word is masculine.
[^10]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers, perhaps record-keepers (Exodus 5:6).
[^11]: Or "Spirit". Hebrew *ruach*: here the spirit given by God, which enabled Moses to lead. Also in verses 25, 26 and 29.
[^12]: Literally "Is the LORD's hand short?", that is, is his power limited?
[^13]: Or "and they did not stop".
[^14]: Or "one of his chosen men".
[^15]: Hebrew *ruach*, the word translated "spirit" in verses 17–29.
[^16]: About 3 feet (90 cm). Or "flying about two cubits above the ground".
[^17]: A homer was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters), so ten homers would be about 60 bushels (2,200 liters).
[^18]: Literally "before it was cut off"; or "before it was used up".
[^19]: *Kibroth-hattaavah* means "graves of craving". Also in verse 35.
