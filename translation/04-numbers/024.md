**1** When Balaam saw that it pleased the LORD[^1] to bless Israel, he did not go as at the other times to look for omens.[^2] Instead he turned his face toward the wilderness.

**2** Balaam looked up and saw Israel camped tribe by tribe, and the Spirit of God came upon him.

**3** Then he spoke his oracle:[^3] "The utterance[^4] of Balaam son of Beor, the utterance of the man whose eye is opened,[^5]

**4** the utterance of one who hears the words of God, who sees the vision of the Almighty,[^6] who falls down, yet with his eyes uncovered:

**5** How beautiful are your tents, Jacob, your dwelling places, Israel!

**6** Like valleys they stretch out, like gardens beside a river, like aloes[^7] that the LORD has planted, like cedars beside the waters.

**7** Water will flow from his buckets, and his seed will be by abundant waters.[^8] His king will be greater than Agag,[^9] and his kingdom will be exalted.

**8** God is bringing him out of Egypt; he has the strength of a wild ox.[^10] He will devour the nations that are his enemies; he will crush their bones and pierce them with his arrows.[^11]

**9** He crouches, he lies down like a lion, like a lioness: who dares to rouse him? Blessed is everyone who blesses you, and cursed is everyone who curses you!"[^12]

**10** Balak's anger burned against Balaam, and he struck his hands together. Balak said to Balaam, "I summoned you to curse my enemies, and instead you have blessed them these three times!

**11** Now go back home at once![^13] I said I would reward you richly, but the LORD has kept you from being rewarded."[^14]

**12** Balaam said to Balak, "Did I not also tell the messengers you sent to me,

**13** 'Even if Balak gave me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the command[^15] of the LORD to do anything good or bad of my own will.[^16] Whatever the LORD says, that is what I will say'?

**14** Now I am going back to my people. Come, let me tell you what this people will do to your people in days to come."[^17]

**15** Then he spoke his oracle: "The utterance of Balaam son of Beor, the utterance of the man whose eye is opened,

**16** the utterance of one who hears the words of God, who has knowledge from the Most High,[^18] who sees the vision of the Almighty, who falls down, yet with his eyes uncovered:

**17** I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near. A star will march out of Jacob; a scepter[^19] will rise out of Israel. He will crush the foreheads[^20] of Moab, and tear down all the sons of Sheth.[^21]

**18** Edom will become a conquered land; Seir, his enemy, will become a conquered land,[^22] while Israel acts with strength.

**19** One from Jacob will rule, and destroy the survivors of the city."[^23]

**20** Then Balaam saw Amalek and spoke his oracle: "Amalek was the first of the nations, but its end will be destruction."[^24]

**21** Then he saw the Kenites and spoke his oracle: "Your dwelling place is secure, and your nest[^25] is set in the rock.

**22** Yet Kain[^26] will be burned. How long until Asshur[^27] takes you captive?"

**23** Then he spoke his oracle: "Alas, who can survive when God does this?[^28]

**24** Ships will come from the coast of Kittim.[^29] They will humble Asshur and humble Eber, but he too will come to destruction."

**25** Then Balaam got up and went back to his place, and Balak also went his way.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *nechashim*: signs read in events or in nature to learn the future.
[^3]: Hebrew *mashal*; see 23:7. Also in verses 15, 20, 21 and 23.
[^4]: Hebrew *ne'um*, a word used to introduce the solemn words of a prophet. Also in verses 4, 15 and 16.
[^5]: The meaning of the Hebrew word *shetum* is uncertain: "opened", or possibly "closed". Also in verse 15.
[^6]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. Also in verse 16.
[^7]: Hebrew *ahalim*, a fragrant tree. The word sounds like *ohalim*, "tents", in verse 5.
[^8]: *Seed* may mean crops or descendants. Balaam's poems speak of Israel as one person. The Septuagint reads "A man will come out of his seed, and he will rule over many nations".
[^9]: The Septuagint reads "Gog". Agag was a later king of the Amalekites (1 Samuel 15:8).
[^10]: Or "he is for him like the horns of a wild ox". See 23:22.
[^11]: Literally "and his arrows he will smash".
[^12]: Compare Genesis 12:3; 27:29.
[^13]: Literally "Flee to your place."
[^14]: Literally "from honor".
[^15]: Literally "the mouth".
[^16]: Literally "from my heart".
[^17]: Literally "in the latter part of the days".
[^18]: Literally "who knows the knowledge of *Elyon*". *Elyon*, "Most High", is a title of God.
[^19]: Hebrew *shevet*, "rod, staff, scepter", also the word for "tribe".
[^20]: Literally "the corners", that is, of the head, or "the borders".
[^21]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain. Jeremiah 48:45, which echoes this verse, has "the skulls of the sons of tumult". *Sheth* may be the name of a people, or a word meaning "tumult".
[^22]: Hebrew *yereshah*, "a possession": a land taken over by others.
[^23]: Or "the survivors from Ir", perhaps Ar of Moab (21:28).
[^24]: Literally "Amalek was the beginning of the nations, but its end is to perish". *First* may mean first in rank or in time.
[^25]: Hebrew *qen*, "nest", a play on *Qeni*, "Kenite".
[^26]: The ancestor of the Kenites, or another name for them.
[^27]: Assyria, or possibly a tribe of northern Arabia (compare Genesis 25:3). Also in verse 24.
[^28]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "Who will live from his setting it, God?"
[^29]: Cyprus. In later writings the name is used of peoples from the west across the sea.
