**1** Miriam and Aaron spoke against Moses[^1] because of the Cushite woman he had married, for he had married a Cushite woman.[^2]

**2** They said, "Has the LORD[^3] spoken only through Moses? Has he not spoken through us as well?" And the LORD heard it.

**3** Now the man Moses was very humble,[^4] more humble than anyone else on the face of the earth.

**4** Suddenly the LORD said to Moses, Aaron and Miriam, "Come out to the tent of meeting, all three of you." So the three of them went out.

**5** The LORD came down in a pillar of cloud and stood at the entrance of the tent. He called Aaron and Miriam, and they both came forward.

**6** He said, "Listen to my words. When there is a prophet among you, I, the LORD, make myself known to him in a vision; I speak to him in a dream.[^5]

**7** Not so with my servant Moses. He is trusted[^6] in all my house.

**8** With him I speak face to face,[^7] plainly and not in riddles, and he sees the form of the LORD. So why were you not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?"

**9** The LORD's anger burned against them, and he left.

**10** When the cloud moved away from above the tent, there was Miriam, stricken with a skin disease, as white as snow.[^8] Aaron turned toward Miriam and saw that she had the disease.

**11** Aaron said to Moses, "Please, my lord, do not hold against us the sin we have so foolishly committed.[^9]

**12** Do not let her be like a stillborn child, whose flesh is half eaten away when it comes out of its mother's womb."

**13** So Moses cried out to the LORD, "O God, please heal her!"[^10]

**14** The LORD said to Moses, "If her father had spat in her face, would she not be in disgrace for seven days? Let her be shut outside the camp for seven days. After that she may be brought back."

**15** So Miriam was shut outside the camp for seven days, and the people did not set out until Miriam was brought back.

**16** After that the people set out from Hazeroth and camped in the wilderness of Paran.

[^1]: The verb is feminine singular, which suggests that Miriam took the lead.
[^2]: *Cush* is usually the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia). Some identify this woman with Zipporah the Midianite (Exodus 2:21), comparing Habakkuk 3:7, where Cushan is parallel to Midian.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Or "meek". Hebrew *anav*.
[^5]: The Hebrew is unusual. Literally "If your prophet is, the LORD, in a vision to him I make myself known; in a dream I speak with him."
[^6]: Or "faithful".
[^7]: Literally "mouth to mouth".
[^8]: Hebrew *metsora'at*, traditionally "leprous": stricken with one of the skin diseases described in Leviticus 13. "As white as snow" may refer to the color of the skin or to its flaking.
[^9]: Literally "do not put sin on us, in that we have acted foolishly and in that we have sinned".
[^10]: In Hebrew this prayer is five short words: *El na, refa na lah*.
