**1** Balaam said to Balak, "Build me seven altars here, and prepare seven bulls and seven rams for me here."

**2** Balak did as Balaam had said, and Balak and Balaam offered a bull and a ram on each altar.[^1]

**3** Then Balaam said to Balak, "Stand beside your burnt offering while I go. Perhaps Yahweh[^2] will come to meet me. Whatever he shows me, I will tell you." And he went off to a bare height.[^3]

**4** God met Balaam, and Balaam said to him, "I have prepared the seven altars, and I have offered a bull and a ram on each altar."

**5** Yahweh put a word in Balaam's mouth and said, "Go back to Balak, and this is what you are to say."

**6** So he went back to him, and there was Balak, standing beside his burnt offering, he and all the officials of Moab.

**7** Then Balaam spoke his oracle:[^4] "Balak has brought me from Aram, the king of Moab from the mountains of the east. 'Come, curse Jacob for me! Come, denounce Israel!'

**8** How can I curse those whom God has not cursed? How can I denounce those whom Yahweh has not denounced?

**9** From the top of the rocks I see them; from the hills I watch them.[^5] Here is a people that lives apart, and does not count itself among the nations.

**10** Who can count the dust of Jacob, or number a fourth part of Israel?[^6] Let me die the death of the upright,[^7] and let my end be like theirs!"

**11** Balak said to Balaam, "What have you done to me? I brought you to curse my enemies, and instead you have done nothing but bless them!"

**12** He answered, "Must I not take care to say what Yahweh puts in my mouth?"

**13** Then Balak said to him, "Please come with me to another place, where you can see them. You will see only the edge of them; you will not see all of them. Curse them for me from there."

**14** So he took him to the Field of Zophim,[^8] on the top of Pisgah. He built seven altars and offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

**15** Balaam said to Balak, "Stand here beside your burnt offering while I meet with him over there."

**16** Yahweh met Balaam and put a word in his mouth, and said, "Go back to Balak, and this is what you are to say."

**17** So he came to him, and there was Balak, standing beside his burnt offering, with the officials of Moab. Balak asked him, "What did Yahweh say?"

**18** Then Balaam spoke his oracle: "Get up, Balak, and listen! Hear me, son of Zippor!

**19** God is not a human being, that he should lie, or a mortal,[^9] that he should change his mind. Has he said it, and will he not do it? Has he spoken, and will he not carry it out?

**20** Look, I have received a command to bless. He has blessed, and I cannot turn it back.

**21** He sees no wrongdoing in Jacob; he finds no trouble in Israel.[^10] Yahweh their God is with them, and the shout of a king is among them.[^11]

**22** God is bringing them out of Egypt; they have the strength of a wild ox.[^12]

**23** For there is no omen against Jacob, no divination against Israel.[^13] Now it will be said of Jacob and of Israel, 'See what God has done!'

**24** Look, a people rises up like a lioness, and raises itself like a lion. It does not lie down until it has eaten its prey and drunk the blood of the slain."

**25** Then Balak said to Balaam, "Do not curse them at all, and do not bless them at all!"

**26** Balaam answered Balak, "Did I not tell you, 'Whatever Yahweh says, that is what I must do'?"

**27** Balak said to Balaam, "Please come, and I will take you to another place. Perhaps it will be right in God's eyes for you to curse them for me from there."

**28** So Balak took Balaam to the top of Peor, which looks down over the wasteland.[^14]

**29** Balaam said to Balak, "Build me seven altars here, and prepare seven bulls and seven rams for me here."

**30** Balak did as Balaam had said, and offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

[^1]: Literally "on the altar", that is, on each one. Also in verses 4, 14 and 30.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *shefi*, of uncertain meaning: perhaps "a bare hill". Some understand it as "alone" or "quietly".
[^4]: Hebrew *mashal*: a poem or saying, often a proverb or a taunt; here a prophetic poem. Literally "he lifted up his *mashal*". Also in verse 18.
[^5]: Literally "him". Balaam's poems speak of Jacob, or Israel, as one person. Here they are translated with "they" and "them".
[^6]: Hebrew *rova*, "a fourth part". Some take it to mean "dust cloud", parallel to "dust" in the first line.
[^7]: Hebrew *yesharim*. Some see here a play on *Yeshurun*, a poetic name for Israel (Deuteronomy 32:15).
[^8]: Or "the field of the lookouts".
[^9]: Literally "a son of man".
[^10]: Or "No misfortune is seen in Jacob; no trouble is seen in Israel."
[^11]: Literally "in him". The shout is the acclaim for a king, perhaps Yahweh as Israel's king.
[^12]: Or "he is for them like the horns of a wild ox". Hebrew *to'afot*, "horns" or "strength", and *re'em*, the wild ox (aurochs), traditionally "unicorn". The same words are in 24:8.
[^13]: Or "there is no sorcery in Jacob, no divination in Israel".
[^14]: Or "over Jeshimon".
