**1** The LORD[^1] spoke to Moses and Aaron:

**2** "This is the rule of the law[^2] that the LORD has commanded: Tell the Israelites to bring you a red heifer[^3] without defect, which has no blemish and has never been under a yoke.

**3** Give it to Eleazar the priest. It is to be taken outside the camp and slaughtered in his presence.

**4** Eleazar the priest is to take some of its blood on his finger and sprinkle some of its blood seven times toward the front of the tent of meeting.

**5** Then the heifer is to be burned while he watches. Its hide, its flesh and its blood are to be burned, together with its dung.

**6** The priest is to take cedar wood, hyssop[^4] and scarlet yarn,[^5] and throw them into the fire where the heifer is burning.

**7** Then the priest must wash his clothes and bathe his body in water. After that he may come into the camp, but the priest will be unclean until evening.

**8** The one who burns the heifer must also wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water, and he will be unclean until evening.

**9** "A man who is clean is to gather up the ashes of the heifer and put them in a clean place outside the camp. They are to be kept for the community of the Israelites for the water for impurity.[^6] It is a sin offering.[^7]

**10** The one who gathers up the ashes of the heifer must wash his clothes, and he will be unclean until evening. This is to be a permanent decree for the Israelites and for the foreigner who lives among them.

**11** "Whoever touches the dead body of any human being[^8] will be unclean for seven days.

**12** He must purify himself with the water[^9] on the third day and on the seventh day, and then he will be clean. But if he does not purify himself on the third day and on the seventh day, he will not be clean.

**13** Whoever touches a dead body, the body of any human being who has died, and does not purify himself, defiles the tabernacle of the LORD. That person is to be cut off[^10] from Israel. Because the water for impurity has not been splashed on him, he is unclean. His uncleanness is still on him.

**14** "This is the law when someone dies in a tent: everyone who comes into the tent and everyone who is in the tent will be unclean for seven days.

**15** Every open container that has no lid fastened on it will be unclean.

**16** And anyone out in the open[^11] who touches someone killed by the sword, or someone who has died, or a human bone, or a grave, will be unclean for seven days.

**17** "For the unclean person, they are to take some of the ashes[^12] from the burning of the sin offering and pour fresh water[^13] over them in a container.

**18** Then a man who is clean is to take hyssop, dip it in the water, and sprinkle it on the tent, on all the furnishings, and on the people who were there. He is also to sprinkle it on anyone who touched the bone, or the one who was killed, or the one who died, or the grave.

**19** The clean person is to sprinkle the unclean person on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day he is to purify him. Then the person being purified must wash his clothes and bathe in water, and in the evening he will be clean.

**20** But anyone who becomes unclean and does not purify himself is to be cut off from among the assembly, because he has defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water for impurity has not been splashed on him. He is unclean.

**21** This is to be a permanent decree for them. The one who sprinkles the water for impurity must wash his clothes, and whoever touches the water for impurity will be unclean until evening.

**22** Anything that the unclean person touches will be unclean, and anyone who touches it will be unclean until evening."

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "the decree of the instruction (*torah*)".
[^3]: Hebrew *parah*, a young cow. Or "a heifer that is entirely red, without defect".
[^4]: A small bushy plant used for sprinkling (Exodus 12:22; Leviticus 14:4).
[^5]: Literally "crimson of the worm": wool dyed with a crimson dye made from an insect.
[^6]: Hebrew *me niddah*, literally "water of impurity": water for removing impurity. Traditionally "water of separation". Also in verses 13, 20 and 21.
[^7]: Or "a purification offering" (see 6:11). Also in verse 17.
[^8]: Literally "touches a dead one, any *nephesh* of a human being". Here *nephesh* means a person, or a dead body.
[^9]: Literally "with it".
[^10]: See 9:13. Also in verse 20.
[^11]: Literally "on the face of the field".
[^12]: Literally "dust".
[^13]: Literally "living water": running water from a spring or stream.
