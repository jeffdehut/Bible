**1** The Canaanite king of Arad, who lived in the Negev,[^1] heard that Israel was coming by the road to Atharim.[^2] He fought against Israel and took some of them captive.

**2** Then Israel made a vow to the LORD:[^3] "If you will hand this people over to me, I will completely destroy their towns."[^4]

**3** The LORD listened to Israel's voice and handed the Canaanites over. They completely destroyed them and their towns, and the place was named Hormah.[^5]

**4** They set out from Mount Hor by the road to the Red Sea,[^6] to go around the land of Edom. But the people grew impatient[^7] on the way.

**5** The people spoke against God and against Moses: "Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread and there is no water, and we are sick of this miserable food!"[^8]

**6** Then the LORD sent fiery snakes[^9] among the people. They bit the people, and many of the people of Israel died.

**7** The people came to Moses and said, "We have sinned, because we have spoken against the LORD and against you. Pray to the LORD to take the snakes away from us." So Moses prayed for the people.

**8** The LORD said to Moses, "Make a fiery snake[^10] and put it on a pole. Anyone who has been bitten can look at it and live."

**9** So Moses made a bronze snake[^11] and put it on the pole. Whenever a snake bit someone, and he looked at the bronze snake, he lived.

**10** The Israelites set out and camped at Oboth.

**11** They set out from Oboth and camped at Iye-abarim,[^12] in the wilderness that faces Moab, toward the sunrise.

**12** From there they set out and camped in the Zered Valley.[^13]

**13** From there they set out and camped on the other side of the Arnon, in the wilderness that extends from the territory of the Amorites. For the Arnon is the border of Moab, between Moab and the Amorites.

**14** That is why it is said in the Book of the Wars of the LORD: "… Waheb in Suphah,[^14] and the valleys of the Arnon,

**15** and the slope of the valleys that stretches toward the settlement of Ar and lies along the border of Moab."

**16** From there they went on to Beer.[^15] That is the well where the LORD said to Moses, "Gather the people together, and I will give them water."

**17** Then Israel sang this song: "Spring up, O well! Sing to it!

**18** The well that the princes dug, that the nobles of the people hollowed out with the scepter, with their staffs." From the wilderness they went on to Mattanah,[^16]

**19** from Mattanah to Nahaliel, from Nahaliel to Bamoth,

**20** and from Bamoth to the valley in the country of Moab, by the top of Pisgah, which looks down over the wasteland.[^17]

**21** Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, saying,

**22** "Let me pass through your land. We will not turn aside into any field or vineyard, and we will not drink water from any well. We will keep to the King's Highway until we have passed through your territory."

**23** But Sihon would not let Israel pass through his territory. Sihon gathered all his people and went out into the wilderness against Israel. He came to Jahaz and fought against Israel.

**24** Israel struck him down with the sword[^18] and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, as far as the Ammonites, for the border of the Ammonites was strongly held.[^19]

**25** Israel took all these towns, and Israel settled in all the towns of the Amorites, in Heshbon and all its villages.[^20]

**26** For Heshbon was the city of Sihon king of the Amorites. He had fought against the former king of Moab and taken all his land from him as far as the Arnon.

**27** That is why the poets[^21] say: "Come to Heshbon! Let it be rebuilt! Let the city of Sihon be restored!

**28** For fire went out from Heshbon, a flame from the town of Sihon. It consumed Ar of Moab, the lords of the heights of the Arnon.

**29** Woe to you, Moab! You are destroyed, people of Chemosh![^22] He has made his sons fugitives and given his daughters into captivity to Sihon king of the Amorites.

**30** But we shot them down.[^23] Heshbon is destroyed as far as Dibon. We laid waste as far as Nophah, which reaches to Medeba."[^24]

**31** So Israel settled in the land of the Amorites.

**32** Moses sent men to spy out Jazer, and they captured its villages and drove out[^25] the Amorites who were there.

**33** Then they turned and went up by the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan came out against them, he and all his people, to do battle at Edrei.

**34** The LORD said to Moses, "Do not be afraid of him, for I have handed him over to you, with all his people and his land. Do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon."

**35** So they struck him down, with his sons and all his people, until no survivor was left to him. And they took possession of his land.

[^1]: The dry region in the south of Canaan.
[^2]: Hebrew *derekh ha-atarim*, of uncertain meaning. It may be the name of a route or place; it has traditionally been understood as "the way of the spies" (compare 13:21).
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *hacharamti*: "I will devote to destruction", that is, give over completely to the LORD by destroying them. Also in verse 3.
[^5]: *Hormah* sounds like *cherem*, "something devoted to destruction".
[^6]: Hebrew *Yam Suf* (see 14:25). Here it probably means the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^7]: Literally "the soul of the people became short".
[^8]: Literally "our *nephesh* loathes this worthless bread", that is, the manna.
[^9]: Hebrew *nechashim seraphim*, "burning snakes", perhaps named for the burning pain of their bite, or for their color. *Saraph* is also the word for the winged beings in Isaiah 6:2.
[^10]: Literally "Make for yourself a *saraph*".
[^11]: Hebrew *nechash nechoshet*, a play on words: "snake" and "bronze" (or "copper") sound alike.
[^12]: The name means "ruins of Abarim" or "ruins of the regions beyond".
[^13]: Hebrew *nachal*, a stream bed that is dry for much of the year (see 13:23). Also in verses 14 and 15.
[^14]: The quotation begins in the middle of a sentence; the Hebrew has the sign of an object with no verb before it. *Waheb* and *Suphah* are probably place names. Its meaning is uncertain.
[^15]: *Beer* means "well".
[^16]: *Mattanah* means "gift". Some read "from the wilderness, a gift" as the last line of the song.
[^17]: Or "over Jeshimon".
[^18]: Literally "by the mouth of the sword".
[^19]: The Septuagint reads "for Jazer is the border of the Ammonites".
[^20]: Literally "daughters": the smaller towns that depended on it.
[^21]: Hebrew *moshelim*, "those who speak in sayings (*meshalim*)": the composers or reciters of popular poems and taunts.
[^22]: Chemosh was the god of Moab.
[^23]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Or "Their dominion has perished".
[^24]: In the Hebrew text a dot is written over the last letter of the word translated "which", a scribal mark of doubt. Without that letter the word means "fire" ("fire as far as Medeba"), and the Septuagint has "fire" here.
[^25]: The Hebrew text is written "took possession of" but read "drove out".
