**1** The LORD[^1] spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai in the first month of the second year after they came out of the land of Egypt. He said,

**2** "Let the Israelites keep[^2] the Passover at its appointed time.

**3** Keep it at its appointed time, at twilight[^3] on the fourteenth day of this month. Keep it in keeping with all its statutes and all its regulations."

**4** So Moses told the Israelites to keep the Passover,

**5** and they kept the Passover in the first month, at twilight on the fourteenth day of the month, in the wilderness of Sinai. The Israelites did everything just as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**6** But there were some men who were unclean because of a dead body,[^4] so they could not keep the Passover on that day. They came before Moses and Aaron that same day,

**7** and those men said to him, "We are unclean because of a dead body. Why should we be kept from presenting the LORD's offering at its appointed time with the other Israelites?"

**8** Moses said to them, "Wait, and I will hear what the LORD commands concerning you."

**9** The LORD said to Moses,

**10** "Tell the Israelites: If any of you or your descendants is unclean because of a dead body, or is away on a distant journey,[^5] he may still keep the Passover to the LORD.

**11** They are to keep it in the second month, at twilight on the fourteenth day. They are to eat it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

**12** They must not leave any of it until morning, and they must not break any of its bones. They are to keep it in keeping with every statute of the Passover.

**13** But if anyone who is clean and is not on a journey fails to keep the Passover, that person is to be cut off from his people,[^6] because he did not present the LORD's offering at its appointed time. That man will bear the consequences of his sin.

**14** "If a foreigner[^7] living among you wants to keep the Passover to the LORD, he is to keep it in keeping with the statute and the regulation for the Passover. You are to have one statute for the foreigner and for the native-born."

**15** On the day the tabernacle was set up, the cloud covered the tabernacle, the tent of the testimony. From evening until morning it would be over the tabernacle, looking like fire.

**16** That is how it always was. The cloud covered it,[^8] and at night it looked like fire.

**17** Whenever the cloud lifted from above the tent, the Israelites would set out after it, and in the place where the cloud settled,[^9] there the Israelites would camp.

**18** At the LORD's command[^10] the Israelites set out, and at the LORD's command they camped. As long as the cloud stayed over the tabernacle, they remained in camp.

**19** When the cloud remained over the tabernacle for many days, the Israelites kept the LORD's charge[^11] and did not set out.

**20** Sometimes the cloud was over the tabernacle only for a few days. At the LORD's command they camped, and at the LORD's command they set out.

**21** Sometimes the cloud stayed only from evening until morning, and when the cloud lifted in the morning, they set out. Or if it stayed for a day and a night, when the cloud lifted, they set out.

**22** Whether it was two days or a month or a longer time,[^12] as long as the cloud remained over the tabernacle, resting on it, the Israelites remained in camp and did not set out. But when it lifted, they set out.

**23** At the LORD's command they camped, and at the LORD's command they set out. They kept the LORD's charge, at the LORD's command through Moses.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "do", "make"; also in verses 3–6 and 10–14.
[^3]: Literally "between the two evenings": probably the time between sunset and dark. Also in verses 5 and 11.
[^4]: Literally "unclean for the *nephesh* of a human being", that is, a corpse. Also in verses 7 and 10.
[^5]: In the Hebrew text the last letter of the word "distant" has a dot written above it, one of the fifteen places in the Hebrew Bible marked in this way. Traditionally the dot marks the letter or word as doubtful.
[^6]: That is, excluded from the community of Israel, or put to death by God. The exact meaning of the expression is debated.
[^7]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^8]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "The cloud covered it by day".
[^9]: Hebrew *shakhan*, "dwell, settle", the verb from which *mishkan*, "tabernacle", comes. Also in verses 18 and 22.
[^10]: Literally "At the mouth of the LORD"; also in verses 20 and 23.
[^11]: Literally "kept the charge of the LORD", that is, did what he required of them. Also in verse 23.
[^12]: Literally "or days". The word sometimes means "a year".
