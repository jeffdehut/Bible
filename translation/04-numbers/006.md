**1** The LORD[^1] said to Moses,

**2** "Speak to the Israelites and tell them: When a man or a woman makes a special vow, the vow of a Nazirite,[^2] to dedicate themselves to the LORD,

**3** he[^3] is to keep away from wine and beer.[^4] He must not drink vinegar made from wine or vinegar made from beer. He must not drink any grape juice, or eat grapes, fresh or dried.

**4** As long as he is a Nazirite,[^5] he must not eat anything that comes from the grapevine, not even the seeds or the skins.[^6]

**5** "As long as his vow of separation lasts, no razor is to touch his head. Until the time for which he has dedicated himself to the LORD is completed, he is to be holy. He is to let the hair of his head grow long.

**6** "As long as he is dedicated to the LORD, he must not go near a dead body.[^7]

**7** Even if his father or his mother, his brother or his sister dies, he must not make himself unclean for them, because the sign of his dedication to his God[^8] is on his head.

**8** As long as he is a Nazirite, he is holy to the LORD.

**9** "If someone dies suddenly beside him, and so makes his consecrated head unclean, he must shave his head on the day of his cleansing. He is to shave it on the seventh day.

**10** On the eighth day he is to bring two doves[^9] or two young pigeons to the priest at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**11** The priest is to offer one as a sin offering[^10] and the other as a burnt offering,[^11] and make atonement for him, because he incurred sin through the dead body.[^12] That same day he is to consecrate his head again.

**12** He must dedicate to the LORD his days as a Nazirite again and bring a year-old male lamb as a guilt offering.[^13] The earlier days do not count, because his consecration was made unclean.

**13** "This is the law for the Nazirite when the time of his separation is completed. He is to be brought[^14] to the entrance of the tent of meeting,

**14** and he is to present his offering to the LORD: one year-old male lamb without defect as a burnt offering, one year-old female lamb without defect as a sin offering, one ram without defect as a fellowship offering,[^15]

**15** and a basket of bread made without yeast, loaves made of fine flour mixed with oil, and wafers made without yeast spread with oil, together with their grain offerings and their drink offerings.

**16** The priest is to present them before the LORD and offer his sin offering and his burnt offering.

**17** He is to offer the ram as a fellowship offering to the LORD, together with the basket of bread made without yeast. The priest is also to offer its grain offering and its drink offering.

**18** "Then at the entrance of the tent of meeting the Nazirite is to shave his consecrated head. He is to take the hair from his consecrated head and put it on the fire that is under the fellowship offering.

**19** The priest is to take the boiled shoulder of the ram, one loaf made without yeast from the basket and one wafer made without yeast, and put them in the hands of the Nazirite after he has shaved off his consecrated hair.

**20** The priest is to wave them as a wave offering[^16] before the LORD. They are holy and belong to the priest, together with the breast that is waved and the thigh that is presented.[^17] After that the Nazirite may drink wine.

**21** "This is the law for the Nazirite who makes a vow, and for his offering to the LORD for his separation, in addition to whatever else he can afford.[^18] He must carry out the vow he has made, in keeping with the law of his separation."

**22** The LORD said to Moses,

**23** "Speak to Aaron and his sons and tell them: This is how you are to bless the Israelites. Say to them:[^19]

**24** 'May the LORD bless you and keep you.[^20]

**25** May the LORD make his face shine on you[^21] and be gracious to you.

**26** May the LORD turn his face toward you[^22] and give you peace.'[^23]

**27** So they will put my name on the Israelites, and I myself will bless them."

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *nazir*, from *nazar*, "separate, dedicate": someone set apart for the LORD. The related noun *nezer*, "separation, dedication", is translated "vow of separation", "separation" or "consecration" in this chapter. Traditionally "Nazarite".
[^3]: The Hebrew uses masculine forms from here on, but verse 2 makes clear that the law applies to women as well.
[^4]: Or "fermented drink"; brewed from grain.
[^5]: Literally "All the days of his separation"; also in verse 8.
[^6]: Two rare words of uncertain meaning. They may refer to the seeds and skins of grapes, or to unripe grapes and their skins.
[^7]: Literally "a dead *nephesh*" (person).
[^8]: Literally "the *nezer* of his God": his consecration, here his uncut hair. *Nezer* can also mean "crown".
[^9]: Or "turtledoves".
[^10]: Hebrew *chatta't*, traditionally "sin offering": an offering that removed the uncleanness caused by sin or impurity (Leviticus 4). Some translate it "purification offering". Also in verses 14 and 16.
[^11]: Hebrew *olah*, "what goes up": an offering burned whole on the altar (Leviticus 1).
[^12]: Literally "because he sinned on account of the *nephesh*".
[^13]: Hebrew *asham*: an offering to make amends for a wrong (Leviticus 5:14–19).
[^14]: Or "He is to bring it", that is, his offering.
[^15]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offering". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers. Also in verses 17 and 18.
[^16]: Hebrew *tenufah*, from *henif*, traditionally "wave": to lift or move an offering before the LORD as a sign that it is presented to him.
[^17]: Literally "the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the contribution". Hebrew *terumah*, "contribution", is something set apart for a sacred purpose (Exodus 25:2).
[^18]: Literally "besides what his hand can reach".
[^19]: The "you" in the blessing (verses 24–26) is singular: each Israelite, and Israel as a whole. In Hebrew its three lines grow from three words to five to seven, and each line names the LORD. A form of this blessing is inscribed on two small silver scrolls found at Ketef Hinnom in Jerusalem, dating from about 600 BC.
[^20]: Or "protect you", "watch over you". Hebrew *shamar*, "guard, keep".
[^21]: To make one's face shine on someone is to look on them with favor and kindness.
[^22]: Literally "lift up his face to you", that is, look on you with favor.
[^23]: Hebrew *shalom*: peace, well-being, wholeness.
