**1** Now the snake[^1] was more crafty[^2] than any wild animal that Yahweh God[^3] had made. It said to the woman, "Did God really say, 'You must not eat from any tree in the garden'?"

**2** The woman said to the snake, "We may eat fruit from the trees in the garden,

**3** but about the fruit of the tree that is in the middle of the garden, God said, 'You must not eat from it or touch it, or you will die.'"

**4** But the snake said to the woman, "You will certainly not die!

**5** For God knows that on the day you eat from it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God,[^4] knowing good and evil."

**6** The woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was desirable for gaining wisdom.[^5] So she took some of its fruit and ate it. She also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.

**7** Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked. They sewed fig leaves together and made themselves loincloths.

**8** They heard the sound[^6] of Yahweh God walking in the garden in the breeze of the day,[^7] and the man and his wife hid from Yahweh God among the trees of the garden.

**9** But Yahweh God called to the man and said to him, "Where are you?"

**10** He said, "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid."

**11** He said, "Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?"

**12** The man said, "The woman you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate."

**13** Then Yahweh God said to the woman, "What is this you have done?" The woman said, "The snake deceived me, and I ate."

**14** Yahweh God said to the snake, "Because you have done this, you are cursed more than all the livestock and all the wild animals.[^8] You will crawl on your belly, and you will eat dust all the days of your life.

**15** I will put hostility between you and the woman, and between your offspring[^9] and her offspring. He[^10] will strike[^11] your head, and you will strike his heel."

**16** To the woman he said, "I will make your pain in pregnancy very great.[^12] In pain you will give birth to children. Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you."

**17** To the man[^13] he said, "Because you listened to your wife and ate from the tree I commanded you about, saying, 'You must not eat from it,' the ground is cursed because of you. With painful toil you will eat from it all the days of your life.

**18** It will produce thorns and thistles for you, and you will eat the plants of the field.

**19** By the sweat of your brow[^14] you will eat bread until you return to the ground, for from it you were taken. You are dust, and to dust you will return."

**20** The man named his wife Eve,[^15] because she became the mother of everyone living.

**21** Yahweh God made garments of skin for the man[^16] and his wife, and he clothed them.

**22** Then Yahweh God said, "Look, the man has become like one of us, knowing good and evil. And now, he might reach out his hand and take from the tree of life too, and eat, and live forever!"[^17]

**23** So Yahweh God sent him out of the garden of Eden to work the ground from which he had been taken.

**24** He drove the man out. And to the east of the garden of Eden[^18] he stationed the cherubim[^19] and a flaming sword that turned every way,[^20] to guard the way to the tree of life.

[^1]: Hebrew *nachash*, traditionally "serpent".
[^2]: Hebrew *arum*, "shrewd, crafty", sounds like *arummim*, "naked" (2:25; 3:7).
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Or "like gods", "like divine beings". Hebrew *elohim* can mean God or gods.
[^5]: Or "desirable to make one wise".
[^6]: Or "voice"; also in verse 10.
[^7]: That is, at the cool, breezy time of day, toward evening. Hebrew *ruach* means wind or breath.
[^8]: Or "you are cursed and set apart from all the livestock and all the wild animals".
[^9]: Literally "seed", here and in the next phrase. The Hebrew word is singular and can mean one descendant or many.
[^10]: Or "It", referring to the offspring.
[^11]: Hebrew *shuph*, used for both actions in this verse. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "crush", "bruise" or "strike at".
[^12]: Literally "I will greatly multiply your pain and your pregnancy".
[^13]: The Hebrew vowel points read "To Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "To the man", as elsewhere in chapters 2–3.
[^14]: Literally "face".
[^15]: Hebrew *Chavvah*, which sounds like the Hebrew word for "living".
[^16]: As in verse 17, the vowel points read "for Adam".
[^17]: The Hebrew sentence breaks off: "And now, so that he does not reach out his hand …".
[^18]: Or "in front of the garden of Eden".
[^19]: Winged heavenly beings who guard holy places (compare Exodus 25:18–22; Ezekiel 10).
[^20]: Literally "the flame of the sword that turned itself".
