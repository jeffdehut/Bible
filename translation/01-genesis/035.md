**1** God said to Jacob, "Get up, go up to Bethel, and live there. Make an altar there to the God who appeared to you when you were running away from your brother Esau."

**2** So Jacob said to his household and to everyone who was with him, "Get rid of the foreign gods that are among you. Purify yourselves, and change your clothes.

**3** Then let us get up and go up to Bethel. I will make an altar there to the God who answered me when I was in distress and who has been with me wherever I have gone."

**4** So they gave Jacob all the foreign gods they had, and the rings that were in their ears, and Jacob buried them under the oak[^1] near Shechem.

**5** As they set out, a terror from God[^2] fell on the towns all around them, so that no one pursued Jacob's sons.

**6** Jacob and all the people who were with him came to Luz (that is, Bethel) in the land of Canaan.

**7** There he built an altar, and he called the place El-bethel,[^3] because it was there that God had revealed himself[^4] to him when he was running away from his brother.

**8** Rebekah's nurse Deborah died, and she was buried under the oak below Bethel. So it was named Allon-bacuth.[^5]

**9** God appeared to Jacob again after he came from Paddan-aram, and he blessed him.

**10** God said to him, "Your name is Jacob. You will no longer be called Jacob; your name will be Israel." So he named him Israel.

**11** God also said to him, "I am God Almighty.[^6] Be fruitful and multiply. A nation, and a whole company of nations, will come from you, and kings will be among your descendants.[^7]

**12** The land I gave to Abraham and to Isaac I will give to you, and I will give the land to your descendants after you."

**13** Then God went up from him at the place where he had spoken with him.

**14** Jacob set up a standing stone[^8] at the place where God had spoken with him, a marker of stone. He poured out a drink offering on it and poured oil on it.

**15** Jacob named the place where God had spoken with him Bethel.

**16** Then they set out from Bethel. While they were still some distance from Ephrath, Rachel went into labor, and her labor was hard.

**17** While she was in the hardest part of her labor, the midwife said to her, "Don't be afraid, for you have another son."

**18** As her life[^9] was slipping away, for she was dying, she named him Ben-oni.[^10] But his father called him Benjamin.[^11]

**19** So Rachel died, and she was buried on the way to Ephrath (that is, Bethlehem).

**20** Jacob set up a standing stone over her grave. It is the marker of Rachel's grave to this day.

**21** Israel set out again and pitched his tent beyond Migdal-eder.[^12]

**22** While Israel was living in that region, Reuben went and slept with Bilhah, his father's concubine,[^13] and Israel heard about it.[^14] Jacob had twelve sons.

**23** The sons of Leah: Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, then Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar and Zebulun.

**24** The sons of Rachel: Joseph and Benjamin.

**25** The sons of Rachel's servant Bilhah: Dan and Naphtali.

**26** The sons of Leah's servant Zilpah: Gad and Asher. These were the sons of Jacob who were born to him in Paddan-aram.

**27** Jacob came to his father Isaac at Mamre, at Kiriath-arba (that is, Hebron), where Abraham and Isaac had lived as foreigners.

**28** Isaac lived 180 years.

**29** Then Isaac breathed his last and died, and he was gathered to his people, old and full of years.[^15] His sons Esau and Jacob buried him.

[^1]: Hebrew *elah*, a large tree, perhaps a terebinth. Traditionally "oak".
[^2]: Or "a great terror". Hebrew can use "God" to describe something as very great.
[^3]: *El-bethel* means "the God of Bethel". *Bethel* means "house of God".
[^4]: The Hebrew verb is plural, which is unusual with *Elohim* (God). It may be read "the heavenly beings had appeared to him", recalling the angels of 28:12.
[^5]: *Allon-bacuth* means "oak of weeping".
[^6]: Hebrew *El Shaddai*. The meaning of *Shaddai* is uncertain.
[^7]: Literally "kings will come out from your loins".
[^8]: Hebrew *matsevah*, an upright stone set up as a marker or memorial. Traditionally "pillar". Also in verse 20.
[^9]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "life, breath".
[^10]: *Ben-oni* means "son of my sorrow", or perhaps "son of my strength".
[^11]: *Benjamin* means "son of the right hand", that is, a son of good fortune, or "son of the south".
[^12]: *Migdal-eder* means "tower of the flock".
[^13]: Hebrew *pilegesh*, a wife of lower status than a principal wife.
[^14]: The Hebrew text has a paragraph break in the middle of this verse, at this point. The Septuagint adds "and it was evil in his sight".
[^15]: Literally "satisfied with days".
