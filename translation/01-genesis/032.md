**1** Early in the morning Laban got up, kissed his grandchildren[^1] and his daughters, and blessed them. Then Laban left and went back home.[^2]

**2** Jacob also went on his way, and angels of God met him.

**3** When Jacob saw them, he said, "This is God's camp!" So he named that place Mahanaim.[^3]

**4** Jacob sent messengers[^4] ahead of him to his brother Esau in the land of Seir, the country of Edom.

**5** He instructed them, "This is what you are to say to my lord Esau: 'Your servant Jacob says this: I have been living with Laban as a foreigner, and I have stayed there until now.

**6** I have oxen and donkeys, flocks, and male and female servants. I am sending this message to my lord in the hope of finding favor with you.'"

**7** The messengers came back to Jacob and said, "We went to your brother Esau. He is coming to meet you, and four hundred men are with him."

**8** Jacob was very afraid and distressed. He divided the people who were with him, and the flocks, the cattle and the camels, into two camps.

**9** He thought,[^5] "If Esau comes to one camp and attacks it, the camp that is left can escape."

**10** Then Jacob said, "God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, LORD,[^6] you who said to me, 'Go back to your country and your relatives, and I will do you good,'

**11** I am not worthy of all the faithful love[^7] and all the faithfulness you have shown your servant. For I crossed this Jordan with only my staff, and now I have become two camps.

**12** Please rescue me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, for I am afraid of him. He may come and attack me, and the mothers with the children.[^8]

**13** But you said, 'I will certainly do you good and make your descendants like the sand of the sea, which is too much to be counted.'"

**14** He spent that night there. Then from what he had with him he chose a gift for his brother Esau:

**15** two hundred female goats and twenty male goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams,

**16** thirty nursing camels with their young, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty female donkeys and ten male donkeys.

**17** He put each herd by itself in the care of his servants, and he said to his servants, "Go on ahead of me, and keep some space between one herd and the next."

**18** He instructed the first one, "When my brother Esau meets you and asks you, 'Whose servant are you? Where are you going? And whose animals are these in front of you?'

**19** then say, 'They belong to your servant Jacob. They are a gift sent to my lord Esau. And look, he himself is right behind us.'"

**20** He gave the same instructions to the second, to the third, and to all who were following the herds: "Say this same thing to Esau when you find him.

**21** And say, 'Look, your servant Jacob himself is right behind us.'" For he thought, "I will win him over[^9] with the gift that is going ahead of me. Afterward I will see his face; perhaps he will accept me."[^10]

**22** So the gift went on ahead of him, while he himself spent that night in the camp.

**23** That night he got up and took his two wives, his two servants and his eleven children, and he crossed the ford of the Jabbok.

**24** He took them and sent them across the stream, and he sent across everything he had.

**25** So Jacob was left alone, and a man wrestled[^11] with him until daybreak.

**26** When the man saw that he could not overpower Jacob, he touched the socket of his hip, and Jacob's hip was wrenched as he wrestled with him.

**27** Then the man said, "Let me go, for dawn is breaking." But Jacob said, "I will not let you go unless you bless me."

**28** He said to him, "What is your name?" He said, "Jacob."

**29** He said, "Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel,[^12] for you have struggled with God and with human beings, and you have won."

**30** Jacob asked him, "Please tell me your name." But he said, "Why do you ask my name?" And he blessed him there.

**31** So Jacob named the place Peniel,[^13] "because I have seen God face to face, and yet my life[^14] has been spared."

**32** The sun rose on him as he passed Penuel,[^15] and he was limping because of his hip.

**33** That is why to this day the Israelites do not eat the sinew of the thigh[^16] that is on the hip socket, because he touched the socket of Jacob's hip at the sinew of the thigh.

[^1]: Verse 1 is 31:55 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–33 are 32:1–32. *Grandchildren*: literally "sons". In Hebrew "son" can mean any descendant.
[^2]: Literally "to his place".
[^3]: *Mahanaim* means "two camps". Compare verses 8 and 11.
[^4]: Hebrew *mal'akhim*, the same word as "angels" in verse 2.
[^5]: Literally "He said".
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7]: Hebrew *chasadim*, the plural of *chesed*: loyal, faithful love, here in its many acts.
[^8]: Literally "mother upon sons".
[^9]: Literally "I will cover his face", meaning "appease him". This verse and the next play on the word *panim*, "face": literally "the gift going before my face … I will see his face … perhaps he will lift up my face", and "the gift passed on before his face". The word returns in the name Peniel (verse 31).
[^10]: Literally "lift up my face".
[^11]: Hebrew *ye'aveq*, which sounds like *Yabboq* (Jabbok) and *Ya'aqov* (Jacob).
[^12]: *Israel* (Hebrew *Yisra'el*) sounds like *sarita*, "you have struggled", together with *El*, "God". The name can mean "he struggles with God" or "God struggles".
[^13]: *Peniel* means "face of God".
[^14]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "life, self".
[^15]: Another form of the name Peniel.
[^16]: Hebrew *gid ha-nasheh*. It is often identified as the sciatic nerve, which runs down the back of the thigh.
