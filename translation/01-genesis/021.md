**1** The LORD[^1] visited[^2] Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did for Sarah what he had promised.

**2** Sarah became pregnant and bore Abraham a son in his old age, at the appointed time God had told him.

**3** Abraham named his son who was born to him, whom Sarah bore him, Isaac.

**4** Abraham circumcised his son Isaac when he was eight days old, as God had commanded him.

**5** Abraham was 100 years old when his son Isaac was born to him.

**6** Sarah said, "God has brought me laughter. Everyone who hears will laugh with me."[^3]

**7** And she said, "Who would have said to Abraham that Sarah would nurse children? Yet I have borne him a son in his old age."

**8** The child grew and was weaned, and Abraham held a great feast on the day Isaac was weaned.

**9** But Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom she had borne to Abraham, laughing.[^4]

**10** So she said to Abraham, "Drive out this slave woman[^5] and her son, for the son of this slave woman will not share the inheritance with my son, with Isaac."

**11** This was very displeasing to Abraham because of his son.

**12** But God said to Abraham, "Do not be distressed about the boy and your slave woman. Listen to whatever Sarah tells you, for it is through Isaac that your offspring will be named.[^6]

**13** I will also make the son of the slave woman into a nation, because he is your offspring."

**14** Abraham got up early in the morning, took bread and a skin of water, and gave them to Hagar. He put them on her shoulder, along with the child,[^7] and sent her away. She went and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba.

**15** When the water in the skin was gone, she left the child under one of the bushes.

**16** Then she went and sat down by herself opposite him, about a bowshot away, for she said, "I cannot watch the child die." As she sat opposite him, she raised her voice and wept.[^8]

**17** God heard the boy's voice, and the angel of God called to Hagar from heaven and said to her, "What is wrong, Hagar? Do not be afraid, for God has heard the boy's voice there where he is.

**18** Get up, lift up the boy and hold him by the hand, for I will make him into a great nation."

**19** Then God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water. She went and filled the skin with water and gave the boy a drink.

**20** God was with the boy as he grew up. He lived in the wilderness and became an archer.

**21** He lived in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother got a wife for him from the land of Egypt.

**22** At that time Abimelech, with Phicol the commander of his army, said to Abraham, "God is with you in everything you do.

**23** Now swear to me here by God that you will not deal falsely with me, or with my children or my descendants. Show me and the land where you have been living as a foreigner the same faithful love[^9] that I have shown you."

**24** Abraham said, "I swear it."

**25** But Abraham complained to Abimelech about a well of water that Abimelech's servants had seized.

**26** Abimelech said, "I don't know who did this. You did not tell me, and I did not hear of it until today."

**27** So Abraham took flocks and herds and gave them to Abimelech, and the two of them made a covenant.

**28** Abraham set seven ewe lambs from the flock apart by themselves.

**29** Abimelech said to Abraham, "What is the meaning of these seven ewe lambs that you have set apart by themselves?"

**30** He said, "You are to take these seven ewe lambs from my hand, as a witness for me that I dug this well."

**31** That is why that place was called Beersheba,[^10] because there the two of them swore an oath.

**32** So they made a covenant at Beersheba. Then Abimelech and Phicol the commander of his army got up and returned to the land of the Philistines.

**33** Abraham planted a tamarisk tree in Beersheba, and there he called on the name of the LORD, the Everlasting God.[^11]

**34** And Abraham stayed as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines for a long time.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *paqad*, "attend to, take note of, come to help".
[^3]: Or "will laugh at me". The Hebrew words for "laughter" and "laugh" sound like the name Isaac (17:17–19).
[^4]: Hebrew *metsacheq*, from the same root as the name Isaac. It can mean laughing, playing, joking or mocking. The Septuagint and the Vulgate add "with her son Isaac".
[^5]: Hebrew *amah*, a female slave; also in verses 12–13. In 16:1 Hagar is called *shiphchah*, "servant".
[^6]: Literally "for in Isaac seed will be called for you". Hebrew *zera*, "seed", is translated "offspring" here and in verse 13.
[^7]: Literally "putting them on her shoulder, and the child". The Hebrew can also be read to mean that he put the child on her shoulder.
[^8]: The Septuagint reads "and the child cried out and wept".
[^9]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^10]: *Beersheba* can mean "well of seven" or "well of the oath".
[^11]: Hebrew *El Olam*.
