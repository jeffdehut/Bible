**1** Jacob settled in the land where his father had lived as a foreigner, the land of Canaan.

**2** This is the family history[^1] of Jacob. Joseph, a young man of seventeen, was tending the flock with his brothers. He was a helper[^2] to the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives. And Joseph brought their father a bad report about them.

**3** Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his other sons, because he was the son of his old age. And he made him a special robe.[^3]

**4** When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him and could not speak a kind word to him.

**5** Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers, they hated him even more.

**6** He said to them, "Please listen to this dream I had.

**7** We were binding sheaves of grain out in the field. Then my sheaf got up and stood upright, and your sheaves gathered around it and bowed down to my sheaf."

**8** His brothers said to him, "Are you really going to be king over us? Are you really going to rule over us?" And they hated him even more because of his dreams and what he said.

**9** Then he had another dream and told it to his brothers. He said, "Listen, I have had another dream. The sun, the moon and eleven stars were bowing down to me."

**10** When he told it to his father and his brothers, his father rebuked him and said to him, "What is this dream you have had? Are your mother and I and your brothers really going to come and bow down to the ground before you?"

**11** His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind.

**12** His brothers went to pasture their father's flock[^4] near Shechem.

**13** Israel said to Joseph, "Your brothers are pasturing the flock at Shechem, aren't they? Come, I am going to send you to them." He said to him, "Here I am."

**14** So he said to him, "Go and see how your brothers are doing and how the flock is doing, and bring word back to me." So he sent him from the Valley of Hebron, and Joseph came to Shechem.

**15** A man found him wandering in the countryside, and the man asked him, "What are you looking for?"

**16** He said, "I am looking for my brothers. Please tell me where they are pasturing the flock."

**17** The man said, "They have moved on from here, for I heard them say, 'Let's go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers and found them at Dothan.

**18** They saw him in the distance, and before he reached them, they plotted to kill him.

**19** They said to one another, "Look, here comes that dreamer![^5]

**20** Come on now, let's kill him and throw him into one of the pits.[^6] We will say that a wild animal ate him. Then we will see what becomes of his dreams."

**21** But when Reuben heard this, he rescued him from their hands. He said, "Let's not take his life."[^7]

**22** Reuben said to them, "Don't shed blood. Throw him into this pit here in the wilderness, but don't lay a hand on him." He said this so that he could rescue him from their hands and bring him back to his father.

**23** So when Joseph came to his brothers, they stripped him of his robe, the special robe he was wearing.

**24** They took him and threw him into the pit. The pit was empty; there was no water in it.

**25** Then they sat down to eat a meal.[^8] When they looked up, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead. Their camels were carrying spices, balm and myrrh,[^9] and they were on their way down to Egypt.

**26** Judah said to his brothers, "What will we gain if we kill our brother and cover up his blood?

**27** Come, let's sell him to the Ishmaelites and not lay our hands on him. After all, he is our brother, our own flesh." His brothers agreed.

**28** Some Midianite traders passed by, and they pulled Joseph up out of the pit and sold him to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver.[^10] And they took Joseph to Egypt.[^11]

**29** When Reuben returned to the pit, Joseph was not in the pit! He tore his clothes.

**30** He went back to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! And I, where can I go?"

**31** Then they took Joseph's robe, slaughtered a male goat, and dipped the robe in the blood.

**32** They sent the special robe and had it brought to their father, and they said, "We found this. Please look at it closely. Is it your son's robe or not?"

**33** He recognized it and said, "It is my son's robe! A wild animal has eaten him. Joseph has surely been torn to pieces!"

**34** Then Jacob tore his clothes, put sackcloth around his waist, and mourned for his son for many days.

**35** All his sons and all his daughters came to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted. He said, "No, I will go down to my son in Sheol,[^12] mourning." And his father wept for him.

**36** Meanwhile the Midianites[^13] sold Joseph in Egypt to Potiphar, one of Pharaoh's officials,[^14] the captain of the guard.[^15]

[^1]: Hebrew *toledot*, "generations", a heading used throughout Genesis to begin the account of a person's family and descendants. What follows is the story of Jacob's sons.
[^2]: Hebrew *na'ar*, "boy, young man", also used of a young assistant. Or "He was a boy with the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah".
[^3]: Hebrew *ketonet passim*. The meaning of *passim* is uncertain; it may mean a long robe with sleeves reaching to the wrists, or a robe with decorations. The Septuagint has "a many-colored robe", the source of the traditional "coat of many colors". Also in verses 23 and 32.
[^4]: In the Hebrew text the small word that marks the object of the verb has dots written above it, one of a few places where the scribes marked a word in this way.
[^5]: Literally "this master of dreams".
[^6]: Hebrew *bor*, a pit dug to collect rainwater, a cistern. Also in verses 22–24 and 28–29.
[^7]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "life".
[^8]: Literally "to eat bread".
[^9]: The exact substances are uncertain. They were valuable plant resins, used as medicines and perfumes.
[^10]: Literally "twenty of silver", probably twenty shekels, about 8 ounces (230 grams) of silver.
[^11]: The Hebrew does not make clear who pulled Joseph out of the pit and sold him: the brothers or the Midianites. Compare 39:1, where the Ishmaelites bring Joseph down to Egypt, and 45:4, where Joseph tells his brothers "you sold me".
[^12]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^13]: Hebrew *Medanim*, "Medanites", probably another spelling of *Midyanim*, "Midianites" (verse 28). The Medanites were a related people (25:2).
[^14]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official. The word can also mean "eunuch".
[^15]: Literally "chief of the slaughterers": the head of Pharaoh's bodyguard, which carried out executions.
