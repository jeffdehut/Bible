**1** The man had sexual relations with[^1] his wife Eve, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Cain.[^2] She said, "I have gotten a man with the help of the LORD."[^3]

**2** Then she gave birth again, to his brother Abel.[^4] Abel kept flocks, and Cain worked the ground.

**3** In the course of time[^5] Cain brought some of the produce of the ground as an offering[^6] to the LORD.

**4** And Abel, for his part, brought some of the firstborn of his flock and some of their fat portions. The LORD looked with favor on Abel and his offering,

**5** but on Cain and his offering he did not look with favor. So Cain was very angry, and his face fell.

**6** The LORD said to Cain, "Why are you angry? Why has your face fallen?

**7** If you do what is right, will you not be accepted?[^7] But if you do not do what is right, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, but you must rule over it."[^8]

**8** Cain spoke to his brother Abel.[^9] And while they were in the field, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.

**9** Then the LORD said to Cain, "Where is your brother Abel?" He said, "I don't know. Am I my brother's keeper?"

**10** Then he said, "What have you done? Your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground![^10]

**11** Now you are cursed from the ground,[^11] which opened its mouth to receive your brother's blood from your hand.

**12** When you work the ground, it will no longer give you its strength. You will be a restless wanderer[^12] on the earth."

**13** Cain said to the LORD, "My punishment is too great to bear.[^13]

**14** Look, today you are driving me away from the face of the ground, and I will be hidden from your presence. I will be a restless wanderer on the earth, and whoever finds me will kill me."

**15** But the LORD said to him, "Therefore,[^14] whoever kills Cain will suffer vengeance seven times over." And the LORD put a mark on Cain,[^15] so that no one who found him would strike him down.

**16** So Cain went out from the LORD's presence and lived in the land of Nod,[^16] east of Eden.

**17** Cain had sexual relations with his wife, and she became pregnant and gave birth to Enoch. He was building a city, and he named the city after his son Enoch.

**18** Irad was born to Enoch. Irad fathered Mehujael, Mehujael[^17] fathered Methushael, and Methushael fathered Lamech.

**19** Lamech married two wives. The name of one was Adah, and the name of the second was Zillah.

**20** Adah gave birth to Jabal. He was the father of those who live in tents and keep livestock.[^18]

**21** His brother's name was Jubal. He was the father of all who play the lyre and the pipe.[^19]

**22** Zillah also gave birth, to Tubal-cain, who forged every kind of tool of bronze and iron.[^20] Tubal-cain's sister was Naamah.

**23** Lamech said to his wives, "Adah and Zillah, listen to my voice; wives of Lamech, hear what I say: I have killed a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me.

**24** If Cain is avenged seven times, then Lamech seventy-seven times."

**25** Adam[^21] had sexual relations with his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth.[^22] "For God has granted me another child[^23] in place of Abel," she said, "since Cain killed him."

**26** A son was born to Seth too, and he named him Enosh.[^24] At that time people began to call on the name of the LORD.[^25]

[^1]: Literally "knew"; also in verses 17 and 25.
[^2]: *Cain* sounds like the Hebrew *qaniti*, "I have gotten".
[^3]: Literally "with the LORD". *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *Hevel*, which means "breath" or "vapor".
[^5]: Literally "At the end of days".
[^6]: Hebrew *minchah*, a gift or tribute; also in verses 4 and 5.
[^7]: Literally "is there not a lifting up?", that is, of your face (compare verses 5–6), or of your offering.
[^8]: Or "and you will rule over it". The words are the same as those spoken to the woman in 3:16.
[^9]: Literally "Cain said to his brother Abel", with no words following. The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate add "Let us go out into the field."
[^10]: Literally "The voice of your brother's blood is crying out". The word "blood" is plural in the Hebrew here and in verse 11.
[^11]: Or "banished from the ground".
[^12]: Hebrew *na va-nad*; also in verse 14.
[^13]: Or "My guilt is too great to be forgiven". Hebrew *avon* means both guilt and its punishment.
[^14]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "Not so!"
[^15]: Literally "set a sign for Cain".
[^16]: *Nod* means "wandering" (compare verses 12 and 14).
[^17]: Hebrew *Mehijael*, a different spelling of the same name.
[^18]: *Father* here means the ancestor or founder of a way of life.
[^19]: Hebrew *kinnor*, a stringed instrument (traditionally "harp"), and *ugav*, a wind instrument (traditionally "flute").
[^20]: Literally "a hammerer of every cutting tool of bronze and iron". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^21]: Here, without the article, *adam* is used as a personal name.
[^22]: Hebrew *Shet*, which sounds like *shat*, "granted".
[^23]: Literally "another seed".
[^24]: *Enosh* means "human being".
[^25]: That is, to worship the LORD by calling on his name. Or "to proclaim the name of the LORD".
