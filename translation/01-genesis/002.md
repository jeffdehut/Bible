**1** So the heavens and the earth were completed, with everything in them.[^1]

**2** On the seventh day God finished the work he had been doing,[^2] and on the seventh day he rested[^3] from all the work he had done.

**3** God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work he had done in creating.[^4]

**4** These are the generations[^5] of the heavens and the earth when they were created. When[^6] the LORD God[^7] made the earth and the heavens,

**5** no shrub of the field was yet on the earth, and no plant of the field had yet sprung up, for the LORD God had not sent rain on the earth, and there was no human being to work the ground.[^8]

**6** But a stream[^9] would rise from the earth and water the whole surface of the ground.

**7** Then the LORD God formed the man[^10] from the dust of the ground. He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living being.[^11]

**8** The LORD God planted a garden in Eden,[^12] in the east,[^13] and there he put the man he had formed.

**9** Out of the ground the LORD God made every tree grow that is pleasing to look at and good for food, with the tree of life in the middle of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.[^14]

**10** A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden. From there it divided and became four branches.[^15]

**11** The name of the first is Pishon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold.

**12** The gold of that land is good. Bdellium[^16] and onyx stone[^17] are there too.

**13** The name of the second river is Gihon. It is the one that winds through the whole land of Cush.[^18]

**14** The name of the third river is the Tigris.[^19] It is the one that flows east of Assyria.[^20] And the fourth river is the Euphrates.[^21]

**15** The LORD God took the man and settled him in the garden of Eden to work it and take care of it.[^22]

**16** The LORD God commanded the man, "You may freely eat from every tree in the garden,

**17** but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. For on the day you eat from it, you will certainly die."[^23]

**18** Then the LORD God said, "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make a helper who is right for him."[^24]

**19** So out of the ground the LORD God formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky,[^25] and he brought them to the man to see what he would call them. Whatever the man called each living creature,[^26] that was its name.

**20** The man gave names to all the livestock, to the birds of the sky, and to every wild animal. But for the man[^27] no helper who was right for him was found.

**21** So the LORD God made a deep sleep fall on the man, and he slept. Then he took one of his ribs[^28] and closed up the flesh in its place.

**22** The LORD God built the rib he had taken from the man into a woman, and he brought her to the man.

**23** The man said, "This one at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh! She will be called 'woman,'[^29] because she was taken out of man."

**24** That is why a man leaves his father and his mother and holds fast to his wife, and they become one flesh.

**25** The man and his wife were both naked,[^30] and they felt no shame.

[^1]: Literally "and all their host", that is, everything that fills them.
[^2]: Or "By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing". The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "On the sixth day".
[^3]: Hebrew *shavat*, "stopped, ceased", the root of the word *Sabbath*. Also in verse 3.
[^4]: Literally "from all his work which God created to do".
[^5]: Hebrew *toledot*, from the verb "to father, give birth": what came from someone, their descendants and their story. The formula "These are the generations of …" heads each main section of Genesis.
[^6]: Literally "On the day".
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. In chapters 2–3 it is usually joined with "God" (Elohim): "the LORD God".
[^8]: Hebrew *adam*, "human being", sounds like *adamah*, "ground".
[^9]: Hebrew *ed*, a rare word of uncertain meaning: perhaps a spring or flow of water. Traditionally "mist".
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-adam*, "the human", the same word as in verse 5. In chapters 2–3 it is not yet a personal name.
[^11]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase used for the animals in 1:20, 24 and 30.
[^12]: *Eden* sounds like a Hebrew word meaning "delight" or "abundance".
[^13]: Or "long ago".
[^14]: Or "the tree of knowing good and evil".
[^15]: Literally "four heads".
[^16]: An aromatic resin. Some think it is a pearl.
[^17]: Hebrew *shoham*, a precious stone that cannot be identified with certainty.
[^18]: Usually the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia); here perhaps a region in Mesopotamia (compare 10:8).
[^19]: Hebrew *Hiddeqel*.
[^20]: Hebrew *Ashur*. Or "in front of Assyria".
[^21]: Hebrew *Perat*.
[^22]: Or "to serve it and guard it".
[^23]: Literally "dying you will die", an emphatic form.
[^24]: Hebrew *ezer kenegdo*, literally "a helper corresponding to him" or "opposite him". *Ezer*, "helper", is often used of God (Psalm 33:20). Also in verse 20.
[^25]: Or "Now out of the ground the LORD God had formed every wild animal and every bird of the sky".
[^26]: Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*, the phrase translated "living being" in verse 7.
[^27]: The Hebrew vowel points read "for Adam", as a name. The same consonants can be read "for the man", as elsewhere in the chapter.
[^28]: Or "one of his sides". Hebrew *tsela* usually means a side, as of a building or the ark.
[^29]: Hebrew *ishah*, "woman", sounds like *ish*, "man".
[^30]: Hebrew *arummim*. In 3:1 the snake is *arum*, "crafty", a play on words.
