**1** When Rachel saw that she had not borne Jacob any children, she became jealous of her sister. She said to Jacob, "Give me children, or I will die!"

**2** Jacob became angry with Rachel and said, "Am I in the place of God, who has kept you from having children?"[^1]

**3** She said, "Here is my servant[^2] Bilhah. Sleep with her,[^3] so that she can give birth on my knees,[^4] and I too can have children through her."[^5]

**4** So she gave him her servant Bilhah as a wife, and Jacob slept with her.

**5** Bilhah became pregnant and bore Jacob a son.

**6** Then Rachel said, "God has judged in my favor. He has also heard my voice and given me a son." That is why she named him Dan.[^6]

**7** Rachel's servant Bilhah became pregnant again and bore Jacob a second son.

**8** Then Rachel said, "I have wrestled mighty wrestlings[^7] with my sister, and I have won." So she named him Naphtali.[^8]

**9** When Leah saw that she had stopped having children, she took her servant Zilpah and gave her to Jacob as a wife.

**10** Leah's servant Zilpah bore Jacob a son.

**11** Leah said, "Good fortune has come!"[^9] So she named him Gad.

**12** Leah's servant Zilpah bore Jacob a second son.

**13** Leah said, "How happy I am! For women[^10] will call me happy." So she named him Asher.[^11]

**14** During the wheat harvest Reuben went out and found some mandrakes[^12] in the field, and he brought them to his mother Leah. Rachel said to Leah, "Please give me some of your son's mandrakes."

**15** But Leah said to her, "Isn't it enough that you have taken my husband? Will you take my son's mandrakes too?" Rachel said, "All right, he can sleep with you tonight in exchange for your son's mandrakes."

**16** When Jacob came in from the field in the evening, Leah went out to meet him and said, "You must sleep with me,[^13] for I have paid for you with my son's mandrakes." So he slept with her that night.

**17** God listened to Leah, and she became pregnant and bore Jacob a fifth son.

**18** Leah said, "God has given me my wages, because I gave my servant to my husband." So she named him Issachar.[^14]

**19** Leah became pregnant again and bore Jacob a sixth son.

**20** Leah said, "God has given me a good gift. This time my husband will honor me,[^15] for I have borne him six sons." So she named him Zebulun.[^16]

**21** Afterward she gave birth to a daughter and named her Dinah.

**22** Then God remembered Rachel. God listened to her and opened her womb.

**23** She became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and she said, "God has taken away my disgrace."

**24** She named him Joseph, saying, "May the LORD[^17] add another son for me."[^18]

**25** After Rachel had given birth to Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, "Send me away, so that I can go to my own place and my own country.

**26** Give me my wives and my children, for whom I have worked for you, and let me go. You know how I have worked for you."

**27** But Laban said to him, "Please, if I have found favor with you …[^19] I have learned by divination[^20] that the LORD has blessed me because of you."

**28** He went on, "Name your wages, and I will pay them."

**29** Jacob said to him, "You know how I have worked for you, and how your livestock have done in my care.

**30** The little you had before I came has grown into a great amount, and the LORD has blessed you wherever I went.[^21] But now, when will I provide for my own household too?"

**31** Laban said, "What shall I give you?" Jacob said, "Don't give me anything. If you will do this one thing for me, I will go back to pasturing and taking care of your flock.

**32** Let me go through your whole flock today and remove from it every speckled or spotted animal,[^22] every dark-colored lamb, and every spotted or speckled goat. They will be my wages.

**33** My honesty[^23] will answer for me in the future, when you come to check the wages I have received. Any goat in my possession that is not speckled or spotted, or any lamb that is not dark-colored, will count as stolen."

**34** Laban said, "Good! Let it be as you have said."

**35** But that same day Laban removed the male goats that were streaked or spotted, and all the female goats that were speckled or spotted, every one that had white on it, and every dark-colored lamb, and he put them in the care of his sons.

**36** He put a distance of three days' journey between himself and Jacob, while Jacob was pasturing the rest of Laban's flock.

**37** Then Jacob took fresh branches of poplar,[^24] almond and plane trees, and he peeled white stripes in them, exposing the white wood of the branches.

**38** He set the branches he had peeled in the troughs, the watering places where the flocks came to drink, right in front of the flocks. And the animals mated when they came to drink.

**39** The flocks mated in front of the branches, and they gave birth to young that were streaked, speckled and spotted.

**40** Jacob separated out the lambs, and he made the flock face the streaked and all the dark-colored animals in Laban's flock. So he made separate flocks for himself and did not put them with Laban's flock.

**41** Whenever the stronger animals were mating, Jacob would put the branches in the troughs in front of the animals, so that they would mate beside the branches.

**42** But when the animals were weak, he would not put them there. So the weak ones went to Laban, and the strong ones to Jacob.

**43** So the man became extremely rich. He had large flocks, female and male servants, camels and donkeys.

[^1]: Literally "who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb".
[^2]: Hebrew *amah*, a female servant or slave. In verse 4 and elsewhere in this chapter the word is *shiphchah*, with the same meaning.
[^3]: Literally "go in to her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations. Also in verses 4 and 16.
[^4]: That is, so that her child would be counted as Rachel's own.
[^5]: Literally "and I too will be built up from her". The Hebrew for "be built up" (*ibbaneh*) sounds like *ben*, "son".
[^6]: *Dan* sounds like *dananni*, "he has judged me" or "he has vindicated me".
[^7]: Literally "wrestlings of God". The phrase may mean "great wrestlings" or "a struggle over God's favor".
[^8]: *Naphtali* sounds like *niphtalti*, "I have wrestled".
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "With good fortune!" (*begad*) but read "Good fortune has come!" (*ba gad*). *Gad* means "good fortune".
[^10]: Literally "daughters".
[^11]: *Asher* sounds like *ashar*, "call happy", and *osher*, "happiness".
[^12]: Hebrew *duda'im*, a plant whose root and fruit were believed to arouse love and help a woman become pregnant.
[^13]: Literally "You will come in to me".
[^14]: *Issachar* sounds like *sakar*, "wages", the word used in this verse and in verse 16 ("I have paid for you", literally "I have hired you").
[^15]: The Hebrew word *yizbeleni* is rare. It probably means "he will honor me", or perhaps "he will live with me".
[^16]: *Zebulun* sounds like *zeved*, "gift", and *yizbeleni*, "he will honor me".
[^17]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^18]: *Joseph* (Hebrew *Yoseph*) sounds like *yoseph*, "may he add", and also like *asaph*, "he has taken away", in verse 23.
[^19]: Laban does not finish his sentence. He means "please stay".
[^20]: Hebrew *nichashti*. Some scholars take it from a similar word meaning "I have prospered".
[^21]: Literally "at my foot".
[^22]: Hebrew *seh*, a single animal of the flock, either a sheep or a goat. Sheep in that region were usually white and goats dark, so Jacob asks only for the unusual ones.
[^23]: Hebrew *tsedaqah*, "righteousness".
[^24]: Hebrew *livneh*, which sounds like *lavan*, "white", and like the name Laban (Hebrew *Lavan*).
