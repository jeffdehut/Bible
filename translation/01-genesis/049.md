**1** Then Jacob called his sons and said, "Gather around, so that I can tell you what will happen to you in the days to come.[^1]

**2** Come together and listen, you sons of Jacob; listen to Israel your father.

**3** Reuben, you are my firstborn, my strength and the first fruit of my vigor, first in honor and first in power.[^2]

**4** Reckless[^3] as water, you will not be first, for you went up onto your father's bed. Then you defiled it: he went up onto my couch![^4]

**5** Simeon and Levi are brothers; their swords[^5] are weapons of violence.

**6** May I never enter their council; may my heart[^6] never join their assembly. For in their anger they killed men, and at their whim they crippled oxen.[^7]

**7** Cursed be their anger, for it is fierce, and their fury, for it is cruel! I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel.

**8** Judah, your brothers will praise you.[^8] Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies; your father's sons will bow down to you.

**9** Judah is a lion's cub. You have come up from the prey, my son. He crouches and lies down like a lion, like a lioness; who dares to rouse him?

**10** The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff[^9] from between his feet, until Shiloh comes,[^10] and the obedience of the peoples is his.

**11** He ties his donkey to the vine, his donkey's colt to the choicest vine. He washes his clothes in wine, his robes in the blood of grapes.

**12** His eyes are darker than wine, and his teeth whiter than milk.[^11]

**13** Zebulun will live by the seashore. He will be a harbor for ships, and his border will reach to Sidon.

**14** Issachar is a strong-boned donkey, lying down between the sheepfolds.[^12]

**15** He sees that his resting place is good and that the land is pleasant, so he bends his shoulder to the load and becomes a slave at forced labor.

**16** Dan will judge[^13] his people as one of the tribes of Israel.

**17** May Dan be a snake by the road, a viper by the path, that bites the horse's heels so that its rider falls backward.

**18** I wait for your salvation, Yahweh![^14]

**19** Gad will be attacked by a band of raiders, but he will attack them at their heels.[^15]

**20** Asher's food will be rich,[^16] and he will provide delicacies fit for a king.

**21** Naphtali is a doe let loose; he gives beautiful words.[^17]

**22** Joseph is a fruitful vine, a fruitful vine by a spring, whose branches climb over the wall.[^18]

**23** Archers attacked him fiercely; they shot at him and harassed him.[^19]

**24** But his bow stayed steady, and his arms[^20] were kept strong by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob, by the name of the Shepherd,[^21] the Stone of Israel,

**25** by the God of your father, who helps you, by the Almighty,[^22] who blesses you with blessings of the heavens above, blessings of the deep that lies below, blessings of the breasts and of the womb.[^23]

**26** The blessings of your father are greater than the blessings of the ancient mountains, the bounty of the everlasting hills.[^24] May they rest on the head of Joseph, on the brow of the one set apart[^25] from his brothers.

**27** Benjamin is a ravenous wolf. In the morning he devours the prey, and in the evening he divides the plunder."

**28** All these are the twelve tribes of Israel, and this is what their father said to them when he blessed them. He blessed each one with the blessing suited to him.

**29** Then he gave them his instructions. He said to them, "I am about to be gathered to my people. Bury me with my fathers in the cave in the field of Ephron the Hittite,

**30** the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre in the land of Canaan. Abraham bought it, along with the field, from Ephron the Hittite as a burial site of his own.

**31** There they buried Abraham and his wife Sarah; there they buried Isaac and his wife Rebekah; and there I buried Leah.

**32** The field and the cave in it were bought from the Hittites."[^26]

**33** When Jacob finished giving instructions to his sons, he drew his feet up into the bed, breathed his last, and was gathered to his people.

[^1]: Literally "in the end of the days", meaning the future, not necessarily the end of time.
[^2]: Literally "excess of dignity and excess of strength". In verse 4, "you will not be first" uses a verb from the same root (*yeter*, "excess").
[^3]: Hebrew *pachaz*, "boiling over, unrestrained".
[^4]: The Hebrew switches from "you" to "he", perhaps as Jacob turns to the other brothers. See 35:22.
[^5]: The meaning of the Hebrew word *mekherotehem* is uncertain. It may mean "swords", "plans" or "origins".
[^6]: Hebrew *kevodi*, "my glory, my honor", meaning my inner self. The parallel line has *nephesh* (soul, self): literally "May my *nephesh* not enter their council". See chapter 34.
[^7]: Literally "hamstrung an ox": they cut the tendons of the animals' legs so that they could not walk.
[^8]: Hebrew *yoducha*, "they will praise you", which sounds like *Yehudah* (Judah). See 29:35.
[^9]: Or "the lawgiver". Hebrew *mechoqeq* means one who decrees, or the staff that is the sign of his authority.
[^10]: Hebrew *shiloh*, whose meaning is much debated. It may be read as a title, "until Shiloh comes"; as the town of Shiloh, "until he comes to Shiloh"; with different vowels (*shello*), "until he comes to whom it belongs", as the Septuagint and the Syriac understood it; or, divided differently (*shai lo*), "until tribute comes to him". The word is written *Shiloh* with a final *h* but read with a final *o*, two spellings of the same word.
[^11]: Or "His eyes are dark from wine, and his teeth white from milk".
[^12]: Or "between the saddlebags". The meaning of the Hebrew word *mishpetayim* is uncertain.
[^13]: *Dan* sounds like *yadin*, "he will judge" or "he will provide justice". See 30:6.
[^14]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^15]: The verse plays on the name Gad: *Gad gedud yegudennu, ve-hu yagud aqev*, "Gad, raiders will raid him, but he will raid at their heel."
[^16]: Literally "From Asher, his bread will be fat". Many scholars join the first letter of this verse to the last word of verse 19, reading "at their heels" there and "Asher's bread" here.
[^17]: Or "that bears lovely fawns". The Hebrew for "words" (*imrei*) may also be read as a word for young animals.
[^18]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. *Porat*, "fruitful", sounds like Ephraim (41:52). The verse may also be read "Joseph is a wild colt, a wild colt by a spring, wild donkeys on a hillside."
[^19]: Literally "The masters of arrows made it bitter for him, and they shot, and they bore a grudge against him."
[^20]: Literally "the arms of his hands".
[^21]: The Hebrew vowel points read *mi-sham*, "from there, the Shepherd". The same consonants can be read *mi-shem*, "by the name of the Shepherd".
[^22]: Hebrew *Shaddai*. The meaning of the name is uncertain. The Samaritan Pentateuch, the Septuagint and the Syriac read "God Almighty" (*El Shaddai*).
[^23]: "Breasts" (*shadayim*) sounds like *Shaddai*, the Almighty.
[^24]: The Hebrew vowel points read "are greater than the blessings of my parents, to the limit of the everlasting hills". The same consonants can be divided to read "the ancient mountains", which matches "the everlasting hills", as the Septuagint understood them (compare Deuteronomy 33:15).
[^25]: Hebrew *nazir*, "one consecrated, set apart", or "prince".
[^26]: Literally "the sons of Heth".
