**1** Jacob set out[^1] and went to the land of the people of the east.[^2]

**2** He looked, and there in the open country was a well, with three flocks of sheep[^3] lying beside it, because the flocks were watered from that well. The stone over the mouth of the well was large.

**3** When all the flocks had gathered there, they would roll the stone off the mouth of the well and water the sheep. Then they would put the stone back in its place over the mouth of the well.

**4** Jacob said to them, "My brothers,[^4] where are you from?" They said, "We are from Haran."

**5** He said to them, "Do you know Laban son of Nahor?"[^5] They said, "We know him."

**6** He said to them, "Is he well?"[^6] They said, "He is well. And look, here comes his daughter Rachel with the sheep."

**7** He said, "Look, it is still broad daylight.[^7] It is not time for the livestock to be gathered in. Water the sheep, and go and let them graze."

**8** They said, "We can't, until all the flocks are gathered[^8] and the stone is rolled off the mouth of the well. Then we water the sheep."

**9** While he was still speaking with them, Rachel came with her father's sheep, for she was a shepherd.

**10** When Jacob saw Rachel, the daughter of his mother's brother Laban, and the sheep of his mother's brother Laban, he went up, rolled the stone off the mouth of the well, and watered the sheep of his mother's brother Laban.

**11** Then Jacob kissed Rachel, and he wept aloud.[^9]

**12** Jacob told Rachel that he was her father's relative[^10] and that he was Rebekah's son. She ran and told her father.

**13** As soon as Laban heard the news about Jacob, his sister's son, he ran to meet him. He hugged him and kissed him and brought him to his house, and Jacob told Laban all these things.

**14** Laban said to him, "You really are my own flesh and blood."[^11] And Jacob stayed with him for a whole month.

**15** Then Laban said to Jacob, "Should you work for me for nothing just because you are my relative? Tell me what your wages should be."

**16** Now Laban had two daughters. The older one was named Leah, and the younger one was named Rachel.

**17** Leah's eyes were weak,[^12] but Rachel had a lovely figure and was beautiful to look at.

**18** Jacob loved Rachel, so he said, "I will work for you seven years for your younger daughter Rachel."

**19** Laban said, "It is better for me to give her to you than to give her to another man. Stay with me."

**20** So Jacob worked seven years for Rachel, and they seemed to him like only a few days, because he loved her.

**21** Then Jacob said to Laban, "Give me my wife, for my time is up, so that I may sleep with her."[^13]

**22** So Laban gathered all the people of the place and held a feast.

**23** But in the evening he took his daughter Leah and brought her to Jacob, and Jacob slept with her.

**24** Laban gave his servant[^14] Zilpah to his daughter Leah as her servant.

**25** In the morning, there was Leah! Jacob said to Laban, "What have you done to me? Didn't I work for you for Rachel? Why have you deceived me?"

**26** Laban said, "It is not done in our place to give the younger daughter in marriage before the firstborn.

**27** Finish this one's week,[^15] and we will give you the other one too, in return for working for me another seven years."

**28** Jacob did so. He finished Leah's week, and Laban gave him his daughter Rachel as his wife.

**29** Laban gave his servant Bilhah to his daughter Rachel as her servant.

**30** Jacob slept with Rachel too, and he loved Rachel more than Leah. And he worked for Laban another seven years.

**31** When the LORD[^16] saw that Leah was unloved,[^17] he opened her womb. But Rachel was unable to have children.

**32** Leah became pregnant and gave birth to a son, and she named him Reuben, for she said, "The LORD has seen my misery. Surely now my husband will love me."[^18]

**33** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "The LORD heard that I was unloved, so he has given me this one too." So she named him Simeon.[^19]

**34** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "Now this time my husband will become attached to me, for I have borne him three sons." That is why he was named Levi.[^20]

**35** She became pregnant again and gave birth to a son. She said, "This time I will praise the LORD." That is why she named him Judah.[^21] Then she stopped having children.

[^1]: Literally "lifted his feet".
[^2]: Literally "the sons of the east", the peoples living east of Canaan. Here it means the region of Haran in Aram.
[^3]: Hebrew *tson*, small livestock: sheep and goats together.
[^4]: A friendly way of addressing strangers.
[^5]: Laban was Nahor's grandson (24:24, 29). In Hebrew "son" can mean any descendant.
[^6]: Literally "Is there peace to him?" Hebrew *shalom*, here meaning well-being.
[^7]: Literally "the day is still great".
[^8]: The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint read "until all the shepherds are gathered".
[^9]: Literally "lifted up his voice and wept".
[^10]: Literally "brother", here meaning a close relative. Jacob was the son of Laban's sister. Also in verse 15.
[^11]: Literally "my bone and my flesh".
[^12]: Hebrew *rakkot*, "soft, tender". It may mean that Leah's eyes were dull or weak, or that they were delicate and lovely. Either way, the contrast is with Rachel's beauty.
[^13]: Literally "go in to her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations. Also in verses 23 and 30.
[^14]: Hebrew *shiphchah*, a female servant or slave who belonged to her mistress. Also in verse 29.
[^15]: The seven days of wedding celebrations for Leah.
[^16]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^17]: Literally "hated", meaning loved less than Rachel. Also in verse 33.
[^18]: *Reuben* sounds like Hebrew *re'u ben*, "Look, a son!", and like *ra'ah be-onyi*, "he has seen my misery".
[^19]: *Simeon* (Hebrew *Shim'on*) sounds like *shama*, "he heard".
[^20]: *Levi* sounds like *yillaveh*, "he will become attached". Literally "he called his name Levi". The subject is not named; it may be Jacob, or the verb may be impersonal.
[^21]: *Judah* (Hebrew *Yehudah*) sounds like *odeh*, "I will praise".
