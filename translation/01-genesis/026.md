**1** Now there was a famine in the land, besides the earlier famine that had occurred in the days of Abraham, and Isaac went to Abimelech king of the Philistines, in Gerar.

**2** The LORD[^1] appeared to him and said, "Do not go down to Egypt. Live in the land that I tell you about.

**3** Stay as a foreigner in this land, and I will be with you and bless you. For to you and to your offspring[^2] I will give all these lands, and I will fulfill the oath that I swore to your father Abraham.

**4** I will multiply your offspring like the stars of the sky and give your offspring all these lands, and in your offspring all the nations of the earth will be blessed,[^3]

**5** because Abraham obeyed my voice and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes and my laws."[^4]

**6** So Isaac lived in Gerar.

**7** When the men of the place asked about his wife, he said, "She is my sister," for he was afraid to say, "my wife," thinking, "The men of the place might kill me because of Rebekah, for she is beautiful."

**8** When he had been there a long time, Abimelech king of the Philistines looked down through the window and saw Isaac caressing[^5] his wife Rebekah.

**9** So Abimelech summoned Isaac and said, "So she is your wife! How could you say, 'She is my sister'?" Isaac said to him, "Because I thought I might die because of her."

**10** Abimelech said, "What is this you have done to us? One of the people might easily have slept with your wife, and you would have brought guilt on us."

**11** So Abimelech commanded all the people, "Whoever touches this man or his wife will surely be put to death."

**12** Isaac sowed seed in that land, and that year he reaped a hundred times what he had sown,[^6] for the LORD blessed him.

**13** The man became rich, and he kept growing richer until he was very rich.

**14** He had flocks and herds and many servants, so that the Philistines envied him.

**15** All the wells that his father's servants had dug in the days of his father Abraham, the Philistines had stopped up and filled with dirt.

**16** Abimelech said to Isaac, "Go away from us, for you have become much too powerful for us."

**17** So Isaac went away from there, camped in the valley[^7] of Gerar and settled there.

**18** Isaac dug again the wells of water that had been dug in the days of his father Abraham and that the Philistines had stopped up after Abraham's death, and he gave them the same names that his father had given them.

**19** Isaac's servants dug in the valley and found a well of fresh water[^8] there.

**20** But the herders of Gerar quarreled with Isaac's herders, saying, "The water is ours." So he named the well Esek,[^9] because they had disputed with him.

**21** Then they dug another well, and they quarreled over that one too, so he named it Sitnah.[^10]

**22** He moved on from there and dug another well, and they did not quarrel over it. So he named it Rehoboth,[^11] saying, "Now the LORD has made room for us, and we will be fruitful in the land."

**23** From there he went up to Beersheba.

**24** That night the LORD appeared to him and said, "I am the God of your father Abraham. Do not be afraid, for I am with you. I will bless you and multiply your offspring for the sake of my servant Abraham."

**25** So he built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD. He pitched his tent there, and Isaac's servants dug a well there.

**26** Then Abimelech came to him from Gerar, with Ahuzzath his adviser[^12] and Phicol the commander of his army.

**27** Isaac said to them, "Why have you come to me, since you hate me and sent me away from you?"

**28** They said, "We have clearly seen that the LORD has been with you. So we said, 'Let there be an oath[^13] between us, between us and you, and let us make a covenant with you,

**29** that you will do us no harm, just as we have not touched you, and have done you nothing but good, and have sent you away in peace.' You are now blessed by the LORD."

**30** So he made them a feast, and they ate and drank.

**31** They got up early in the morning and swore an oath to one another. Then Isaac sent them on their way, and they left him in peace.

**32** That same day Isaac's servants came and told him about the well they had dug. They said to him, "We have found water!"

**33** He named it Shibah.[^14] That is why the name of the city is Beersheba to this day.

**34** When Esau was forty years old, he married Judith daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite.

**35** They made life bitter[^15] for Isaac and Rebekah.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "seed"; also in verses 4 and 24.
[^3]: Or "will bless themselves by your offspring"; see 22:18.
[^4]: Hebrew *torot*, the plural of *torah*, "instruction".
[^5]: Hebrew *metsacheq*, from the same root as the name Isaac; see 21:9.
[^6]: Literally "he found in that year a hundred measures". The Septuagint reads "a hundredfold of barley".
[^7]: Hebrew *nachal*, a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season; also in verse 19.
[^8]: Literally "living water", that is, water from a spring.
[^9]: *Esek* means "dispute".
[^10]: *Sitnah* means "hostility" or "accusation".
[^11]: *Rehoboth* means "wide spaces".
[^12]: Literally "his friend", perhaps a title for a royal adviser.
[^13]: Hebrew *alah*, an oath sworn under a curse.
[^14]: *Shibah* sounds like the Hebrew words for "oath" and "seven"; compare 21:31.
[^15]: Literally "They were bitterness of spirit". Hebrew *ruach*.
