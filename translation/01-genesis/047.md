**1** Joseph went in and told Pharaoh, "My father and my brothers have come from the land of Canaan with their flocks and their cattle and everything they have. They are now in the land of Goshen."

**2** He had taken five men from among his brothers,[^1] and he presented them to Pharaoh.

**3** Pharaoh asked his brothers, "What is your occupation?" They said to Pharaoh, "Your servants are shepherds, both we and our ancestors."

**4** They also said to Pharaoh, "We have come to live in the land as foreigners, because there is no pasture for your servants' flocks; the famine is severe in the land of Canaan. So now, please let your servants settle in the land of Goshen."

**5** Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Your father and your brothers have come to you.

**6** The land of Egypt is open to you. Settle your father and your brothers in the best part of the land. Let them live in the land of Goshen. And if you know of any capable men among them, put them in charge of my own livestock."

**7** Then Joseph brought his father Jacob in and presented him to Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed[^2] Pharaoh.

**8** Pharaoh asked Jacob, "How old are you?"[^3]

**9** Jacob said to Pharaoh, "The years of my wanderings[^4] are 130. The years of my life have been few and hard, and they have not reached the years of the life of my fathers in their wanderings."

**10** Then Jacob blessed Pharaoh and went out from Pharaoh's presence.

**11** So Joseph settled his father and his brothers and gave them property in the land of Egypt, in the best part of the land, the region of Rameses, as Pharaoh had ordered.

**12** Joseph provided food for his father, his brothers and all his father's household, according to the number of their little children.

**13** There was no food anywhere in the land, for the famine was very severe. The land of Egypt and the land of Canaan were wasting away because of the famine.

**14** Joseph collected all the money to be found in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan in payment for the grain they were buying, and Joseph brought the money into Pharaoh's palace.

**15** When the money was used up in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan, all the Egyptians came to Joseph and said, "Give us food! Why should we die before your eyes? For our money is gone."

**16** Joseph said, "Give me your livestock. If your money is gone, I will give you food in exchange for your livestock."

**17** So they brought their livestock to Joseph, and Joseph gave them food in exchange for their horses, their flocks, their cattle and their donkeys. He got them through that year with food in exchange for all their livestock.

**18** When that year was over, they came to him the next year and said to him, "We cannot hide from my lord that our money is gone, and our herds of animals belong to my lord. Nothing is left for my lord except our bodies and our land.

**19** Why should we die before your eyes, both we and our land? Buy us and our land in exchange for food, and we with our land will be slaves[^5] to Pharaoh. Give us seed, so that we may live and not die, and so that the land will not become desolate."

**20** So Joseph bought all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh. The Egyptians all sold their fields, because the famine was too severe for them. So the land became Pharaoh's.

**21** As for the people, he moved them into the cities[^6] from one end of Egypt's territory to the other.

**22** Only the land of the priests he did not buy, because the priests had a fixed allowance from Pharaoh, and they lived on the allowance Pharaoh gave them. That is why they did not sell their land.

**23** Joseph said to the people, "Now that I have bought you and your land today for Pharaoh, here is seed for you. Sow the land.

**24** At harvest time give a fifth to Pharaoh. The other four-fifths will be yours, as seed for the fields and as food for yourselves, for your households and for your little children."

**25** They said, "You have saved our lives. May we find favor with my lord, and we will be slaves to Pharaoh."

**26** So Joseph made it a law concerning the land of Egypt, still in force today, that a fifth belongs to Pharaoh. Only the land of the priests did not become Pharaoh's.

**27** So Israel settled in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen. They acquired property there, and they were fruitful and multiplied greatly.

**28** Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years, and the length of Jacob's life was 147 years.

**29** When the time came near for Israel to die, he called his son Joseph and said to him, "If I have found favor with you, put your hand under my thigh[^7] and show me faithful love and faithfulness:[^8] please do not bury me in Egypt.

**30** When I lie down with my fathers, carry me out of Egypt and bury me in their burial place." Joseph said, "I will do as you say."

**31** Israel said, "Swear to me." So Joseph swore to him. Then Israel bowed down at the head of his bed.[^9]

[^1]: Or "from the whole number of his brothers". The Hebrew phrase is unusual.
[^2]: Or "greeted". Also in verse 10.
[^3]: Literally "How many are the days of the years of your life?"
[^4]: Hebrew *megurim*, a life lived as a foreigner, away from one's own land.
[^5]: Hebrew *avadim*, "slaves" or "servants". Also in verse 25.
[^6]: The Samaritan Pentateuch and the Septuagint read "he made them slaves".
[^7]: A way of making a solemn promise (compare 24:2, 9).
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed ve-emet*, "loyal love and truth", a pair that means steadfast, reliable kindness.
[^9]: The Septuagint, reading the same Hebrew consonants with different vowels (*matteh*, "staff", instead of *mittah*, "bed"), has "on the top of his staff", the wording quoted in Hebrews 11:21.
