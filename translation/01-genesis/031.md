**1** Jacob heard what Laban's sons were saying: "Jacob has taken everything that belonged to our father. He has made all this wealth[^1] from what belonged to our father."

**2** And Jacob saw from Laban's face that he was not as friendly toward him as before.[^2]

**3** Then the LORD[^3] said to Jacob, "Go back to the land of your fathers and to your relatives, and I will be with you."

**4** So Jacob sent for Rachel and Leah and called them out to the field where his flock was.

**5** He said to them, "I can see from your father's face that he is not as friendly toward me as before. But the God of my father has been with me.

**6** You know that I have worked for your father with all my strength.

**7** Yet your father has cheated me and changed my wages ten times.[^4] But God did not let him harm me.

**8** If he said, 'The speckled ones will be your wages,' then the whole flock gave birth to speckled young. And if he said, 'The streaked ones will be your wages,' then the whole flock gave birth to streaked young.

**9** So God has taken away your father's livestock and given them to me.

**10** Once, in the season when the flock was mating, I looked up and saw in a dream that the male goats mating with the flock were streaked, speckled and mottled.

**11** The angel of God said to me in the dream, 'Jacob!' And I said, 'Here I am.'

**12** He said, 'Look up and see: all the male goats mating with the flock are streaked, speckled and mottled. For I have seen everything that Laban has been doing to you.

**13** I am the God of Bethel,[^5] where you anointed a standing stone[^6] and made a vow to me. Now get up, leave this land, and go back to the land of your birth.'"

**14** Rachel and Leah answered him, "Do we still have any share or inheritance in our father's house?

**15** Doesn't he regard us as foreigners? For he has sold us, and he has completely used up the money paid for us.[^7]

**16** All the wealth that God has taken away from our father belongs to us and to our children. So now do whatever God has told you."

**17** Then Jacob got up and put his children and his wives on the camels.

**18** He drove all his livestock ahead of him, with all the property he had acquired, the livestock he owned, which he had acquired in Paddan-aram, to go to his father Isaac in the land of Canaan.

**19** Now Laban had gone to shear his sheep, and Rachel stole the household gods[^8] that belonged to her father.

**20** And Jacob deceived[^9] Laban the Aramean by not telling him that he was running away.

**21** So he ran away with everything he had. He set out, crossed the River,[^10] and headed for the hill country of Gilead.

**22** On the third day Laban was told that Jacob had run away.

**23** He took his relatives[^11] with him and pursued Jacob for seven days, and he caught up with him in the hill country of Gilead.

**24** But God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream at night and said to him, "Be careful not to say anything to Jacob, either good or bad."[^12]

**25** Laban caught up with Jacob. Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban and his relatives camped[^13] in the hill country of Gilead.

**26** Laban said to Jacob, "What have you done? You have deceived me[^14] and carried off my daughters like prisoners of war!

**27** Why did you run away secretly and deceive me,[^15] and not tell me? I would have sent you off with celebration and songs, with tambourines and harps.

**28** You did not even let me kiss my grandchildren[^16] and my daughters goodbye. You have acted foolishly.

**29** I have the power to do you harm. But last night the God of your father said to me, 'Be careful not to say anything to Jacob, either good or bad.'

**30** Now you have gone away because you longed so much for your father's house. But why did you steal my gods?"

**31** Jacob answered Laban, "I was afraid, because I thought you would take your daughters away from me by force.

**32** But anyone you find with your gods will not live. In front of our relatives, point out anything of yours that is with me, and take it." Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them.

**33** So Laban went into Jacob's tent, into Leah's tent, and into the tent of the two servants,[^17] but he did not find them. He came out of Leah's tent and went into Rachel's tent.

**34** Now Rachel had taken the household gods, put them in the camel's saddle, and sat on them. Laban felt through the whole tent but did not find them.

**35** She said to her father, "Don't be angry, my lord, that I cannot get up in front of you, for I am having my period."[^18] He searched, but he did not find the household gods.

**36** Then Jacob became angry and quarreled with Laban. Jacob said to Laban, "What is my crime? What is my sin, that you have chased after me so hotly?

**37** You have felt through all my things. What have you found of all your household things? Put it here in front of my relatives and your relatives, and let them decide between the two of us.

**38** I have been with you these twenty years. Your ewes and your female goats have not miscarried, and I have not eaten the rams of your flock.

**39** I did not bring you any animal torn by wild beasts; I bore the loss myself. You demanded payment from me for anything stolen, whether by day or by night.

**40** This is how I was: by day the heat consumed me, and by night the cold, and sleep fled from my eyes.

**41** These twenty years I have been in your household. I worked for you fourteen years for your two daughters and six years for your flock, and you changed my wages ten times.

**42** If the God of my father, the God of Abraham and the Fear of Isaac,[^19] had not been on my side, you would surely have sent me away empty-handed by now. But God has seen my misery and the hard work of my hands, and last night he gave his verdict."[^20]

**43** Laban answered Jacob, "The daughters are my daughters, the children are my children, the flocks are my flocks, and everything you see is mine. But what can I do today about these daughters of mine, or about the children they have borne?

**44** So come, let us make a covenant, you and I, and let it be a witness between me and you."

**45** So Jacob took a stone and set it up as a standing stone.

**46** Jacob said to his relatives, "Gather stones." So they took stones and made a heap, and they ate there beside the heap.

**47** Laban called it Jegar-sahadutha, but Jacob called it Galeed.[^21]

**48** Laban said, "This heap is a witness between me and you today." That is why it was named Galeed.

**49** It was also called Mizpah,[^22] because he said, "May the LORD keep watch between me and you when we are out of each other's sight.

**50** If you mistreat my daughters, or if you take other wives besides my daughters, even though no one is with us, see: God is a witness between me and you."

**51** Laban also said to Jacob, "Here is this heap, and here is the standing stone that I have set up[^23] between me and you.

**52** This heap is a witness, and the standing stone is a witness, that I will not go past this heap to you, and you will not go past this heap and this standing stone to me, to do harm.

**53** May the God of Abraham and the God of Nahor, the God of their father, judge between us."[^24] So Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac.

**54** Jacob offered a sacrifice on the mountain and invited his relatives to eat a meal.[^25] They ate the meal and spent the night on the mountain.

[^1]: Hebrew *kavod*, "weight, honor, glory", here meaning wealth.
[^2]: Literally "and look, he was not with him as yesterday and the day before". Also in verse 5.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "ten counts", probably meaning "again and again". Also in verse 41.
[^5]: Literally "I am the God Bethel" (Hebrew *ha-El Beth-El*). The Septuagint reads "I am the God who appeared to you in the place of God". *Bethel* means "house of God"; see 28:10–22.
[^6]: Hebrew *matsevah*, an upright stone set up as a marker or memorial. Traditionally "pillar". Also in verses 45, 51 and 52.
[^7]: Literally "and he has completely eaten up our money (silver)". The bride-price Jacob paid in work should have benefited Laban's daughters.
[^8]: Hebrew *teraphim*, small images of gods kept in the home. Also in verses 34 and 35.
[^9]: Literally "stole the heart of". The Hebrew for "heart" (*lev*) sounds like Laban (*Lavan*), and "stole" repeats the word used of Rachel in verse 19.
[^10]: The Euphrates.
[^11]: Literally "brothers", here meaning his kinsmen. Also in verses 25, 32, 37, 46 and 54.
[^12]: Literally "from good to bad", meaning that Laban must not try to make Jacob do anything. Also in verse 29.
[^13]: Literally "Laban pitched his brothers", meaning that he set up camp with them.
[^14]: Literally "stolen my heart", as in verse 20.
[^15]: Literally "steal me".
[^16]: Literally "my sons". In Hebrew "son" can mean any descendant.
[^17]: Hebrew *amah*, a female servant or slave. The two servants are Bilhah and Zilpah.
[^18]: Literally "the way of women is upon me".
[^19]: Hebrew *pachad Yitschaq*, a title for God: the one whom Isaac feared and revered. Some scholars take *pachad* here to mean "Kinsman". Also in verse 53.
[^20]: Or "and last night he rebuked you".
[^21]: Both names mean "heap of witness". *Jegar-sahadutha* is Aramaic, Laban's language; *Galeed* is Hebrew. These two words are the only Aramaic in Genesis.
[^22]: *Mizpah* means "watchtower" and sounds like *yitseph*, "may he keep watch". Literally "and the Mizpah, because he said".
[^23]: In verse 45 it was Jacob who set up the stone.
[^24]: The Hebrew verb "judge" is plural, so Laban may be speaking of more than one god: "the gods of Abraham and the gods of Nahor".
[^25]: Literally "to eat bread". Also "the meal" later in the verse.
