**1** But God remembered Noah and all the wild animals and all the livestock that were with him in the ark. God made a wind[^1] blow over the earth, and the waters went down.

**2** The springs of the deep and the windows of the sky were closed, and the rain from the sky was held back.

**3** The waters steadily receded from the earth. At the end of one hundred and fifty days the waters had gone down,

**4** and in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat.[^2]

**5** The waters went on decreasing until the tenth month. In the tenth month, on the first day of the month, the tops of the mountains appeared.

**6** At the end of forty days Noah opened the window of the ark that he had made

**7** and sent out the raven. It kept flying back and forth until the waters had dried up from the earth.

**8** Then he sent out the dove from him, to see whether the waters had gone down from the face of the ground.

**9** But the dove found no place to rest its feet,[^3] and it returned to him in the ark, because there was water over the whole surface of the earth. He reached out his hand, took it, and brought it back in to himself in the ark.

**10** He waited another seven days and again sent out the dove from the ark.

**11** The dove came back to him in the evening, and there in its beak was a freshly plucked olive leaf! So Noah knew that the waters had gone down from the earth.

**12** He waited yet another seven days and sent out the dove, and it did not return to him again.

**13** In the six hundred and first year,[^4] in the first month, on the first day of the month, the waters had dried up from the earth. Noah removed the covering of the ark and looked, and the surface of the ground was dry.

**14** In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth was completely dry.

**15** Then God said to Noah,

**16** "Go out of the ark, you and your wife, your sons and your sons' wives with you.

**17** Bring out with you every living creature that is with you, of all living creatures:[^5] birds, livestock, and every creature that moves along the ground. Let them swarm on the earth and be fruitful and multiply on the earth."

**18** So Noah went out, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives.

**19** Every animal, every creature that moves along the ground, and every bird, everything that moves on the earth, went out of the ark by their families.

**20** Then Noah built an altar to Yahweh.[^6] He took some of every clean animal and some of every clean bird and offered burnt offerings[^7] on the altar.

**21** Yahweh smelled the pleasing aroma,[^8] and Yahweh said in his heart,[^9] "I will never again curse the ground because of human beings, even though[^10] the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth. And I will never again strike down every living thing, as I have done.

**22** As long as the earth lasts, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will not cease."

[^1]: Hebrew *ruach*, the word used in 1:2.
[^2]: A region in what is now eastern Turkey and Armenia, called Urartu in ancient records.
[^3]: Literally "no resting place for the sole of its foot".
[^4]: That is, of Noah's life (see 7:11).
[^5]: Literally "all flesh".
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^7]: Offerings that were burned whole on the altar.
[^8]: Hebrew *reach ha-nichoach*, "the soothing smell". *Nichoach* sounds like the name Noah.
[^9]: Or "said to himself".
[^10]: Or "for".
