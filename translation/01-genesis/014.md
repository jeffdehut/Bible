**1** In the days of Amraphel king of Shinar, Arioch king of Ellasar, Chedorlaomer king of Elam, and Tidal king of Goiim,[^1]

**2** these kings made war against Bera king of Sodom, Birsha king of Gomorrah, Shinab king of Admah, Shemeber king of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela (that is, Zoar).

**3** All these joined forces in the Valley of Siddim (that is, the Salt Sea).[^2]

**4** For twelve years they had served Chedorlaomer, but in the thirteenth year they rebelled.

**5** In the fourteenth year Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him came and defeated the Rephaim in Ashteroth-karnaim, the Zuzim in Ham, the Emim in Shaveh-kiriathaim,

**6** and the Horites in their hill country of Seir, as far as El-paran, which is by the wilderness.

**7** Then they turned back and came to En-mishpat (that is, Kadesh), and they conquered all the territory of the Amalekites, and also the Amorites who were living in Hazazon-tamar.

**8** Then the king of Sodom, the king of Gomorrah, the king of Admah, the king of Zeboiim and the king of Bela (that is, Zoar) marched out and lined up for battle against them in the Valley of Siddim,

**9** against Chedorlaomer king of Elam, Tidal king of Goiim, Amraphel king of Shinar and Arioch king of Ellasar: four kings against the five.

**10** Now the Valley of Siddim was full of tar pits.[^3] As the kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled, they fell into them,[^4] and the rest fled to the hill country.

**11** The victors took all the goods of Sodom and Gomorrah and all their food, and went away.

**12** They also took Lot, Abram's nephew, and his goods, and went away, for he was living in Sodom.

**13** Then a man who had escaped came and told Abram the Hebrew.[^5] Abram was living by the oaks[^6] of Mamre the Amorite, the brother of Eshcol and of Aner. These men were Abram's allies.[^7]

**14** When Abram heard that his relative[^8] had been taken captive, he called out[^9] his trained men, born in his household, 318 of them, and he pursued them as far as Dan.

**15** At night he divided his forces against them, he and his servants, and he defeated them and pursued them as far as Hobah, north[^10] of Damascus.

**16** He brought back all the goods. He also brought back his relative Lot and his goods, together with the women and the other people.

**17** After Abram returned from defeating Chedorlaomer and the kings who were with him, the king of Sodom came out to meet him in the Valley of Shaveh (that is, the King's Valley).

**18** And Melchizedek[^11] king of Salem[^12] brought out bread and wine. He was priest of God Most High.[^13]

**19** He blessed him and said, "Blessed be Abram by God Most High, Creator[^14] of heaven and earth.

**20** And blessed be God Most High, who has handed your enemies over to you." Then Abram gave him a tenth of everything.[^15]

**21** The king of Sodom said to Abram, "Give me the people,[^16] and take the goods for yourself."

**22** But Abram said to the king of Sodom, "I have raised my hand[^17] to the LORD,[^18] God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth,

**23** that I will not take a thread or a sandal strap, or anything that is yours, so that you will never say, 'I made Abram rich.'

**24** I will take nothing except what the young men have eaten, and the share of the men who went with me: Aner, Eshcol and Mamre. Let them take their share."

[^1]: Or "king of nations"; also in verse 9.
[^2]: That is, the Dead Sea.
[^3]: Literally "pits, pits of tar", that is, bitumen.
[^4]: Or "they fell there". The king of Sodom appears again in verse 17.
[^5]: The first use of the word in the Bible. It may be connected with the name Eber (10:21; 11:14–17).
[^6]: Or "terebinths".
[^7]: Literally "masters of a covenant with Abram".
[^8]: Literally "brother"; also in verse 16. Lot was Abram's nephew.
[^9]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb is uncertain; literally "emptied out".
[^10]: Literally "on the left of", that is, for someone facing east.
[^11]: *Melchizedek* means "king of righteousness".
[^12]: Probably Jerusalem (compare Psalm 76:3, 76:2 in English).
[^13]: Hebrew *El Elyon*; also in verses 19, 20 and 22.
[^14]: Or "Owner", "Maker"; also in verse 22.
[^15]: Literally "he gave him a tenth of everything".
[^16]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here meaning the persons who had been captured.
[^17]: That is, I have sworn an oath.
[^18]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
