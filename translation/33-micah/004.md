**1** In the days to come,[^1] the mountain of the LORD's[^2] house will be established as the highest of the mountains;[^3] it will be raised above the hills, and peoples will stream to it.[^4]

**2** Many nations will come and say, "Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob, so that he may teach us his ways and we may walk in his paths." For instruction[^5] will go out from Zion, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

**3** He will judge between many peoples and settle disputes for strong nations far away. They will beat their swords into plowshares[^6] and their spears into pruning knives. Nation will not lift up sword against nation, and they will not learn war anymore.

**4** Each of them will sit under his own vine and under his own fig tree, and no one will make them afraid,[^7] for the mouth of the LORD of Armies[^8] has spoken.

**5** For all the peoples walk, each in the name of its god, but we will walk in the name of the LORD our God forever and ever.

**6** On that day, declares the LORD,[^9] I will gather the lame[^10] and bring together those who have been driven away, those whom I have afflicted.

**7** I will make the lame a remnant, and those driven far away[^11] a strong nation. The LORD will reign over them on Mount Zion from now on and forever.

**8** And you, watchtower of the flock,[^12] stronghold of Daughter Zion,[^13] to you it will come: the former dominion will come, the kingdom of Daughter Jerusalem.

**9** Now why do you cry aloud? Is there no king in you? Has your counselor perished, that pain has seized you like a woman in labor?

**10** Writhe and groan,[^14] Daughter Zion, like a woman in labor, for now you will go out from the city and camp in the open country. You will go to Babylon.[^15] There you will be rescued; there the LORD will redeem you from the hand of your enemies.

**11** But now many nations have gathered against you. They say, "Let her be defiled,[^16] and let our eyes gloat over Zion."

**12** But they do not know the thoughts of the LORD, and they do not understand his plan: he has gathered them like sheaves to the threshing floor.

**13** Rise and thresh, Daughter Zion, for I will make your horn iron and your hooves bronze, and you will crush many peoples. You will devote[^17] their plunder to the LORD, their wealth to the Lord of the whole earth.

**14** Now gather your troops, daughter of troops![^18] A siege has been laid against us. With a rod they will strike the judge of Israel on the cheek.[^19]

[^1]: Literally "at the end of the days". Verses 1–3 are found also in Isaiah 2:2–4, with small differences. Verse 4 is found only in Micah.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "at the head of the mountains".
[^4]: Or "will flow to it", like a river.
[^5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^6]: Hebrew *ittim*, probably iron plow points or hoes. Joel 4:10 (3:10 in most English Bibles) turns the image around: "Beat your plowshares into swords."
[^7]: A picture of peace and safety, as in the days of Solomon (1 Kings 5:5; 4:25 in most English Bibles; compare Zechariah 3:10).
[^8]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^9]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^10]: The Hebrew is feminine singular, "the limping one", picturing the people as a lame ewe of the flock. Also in verse 7. Compare Zephaniah 3:19.
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-nahala'ah*, found only here. Perhaps "those cast far off".
[^12]: Hebrew *Migdal-eder*, a shepherd's tower for watching over the flock. Genesis 35:21 names a place near Bethlehem by it. Here it is Jerusalem.
[^13]: Hebrew *ophel*, "mound, stronghold", also the name of the hill of the City of David, south of the temple (2 Chronicles 27:3). *Daughter Zion* is a poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman.
[^14]: The meaning of the second verb is uncertain. Perhaps "push" or "burst out", as in giving birth.
[^15]: In Micah's time Assyria was the great power. Babylon became the leading power about a century later, and took the people of Judah into exile there (compare 2 Kings 20:12–18).
[^16]: Or "profaned".
[^17]: Hebrew *ve-hacharamti*, which usually means "I will devote", but is probably here an old form of "you will devote". To devote something was to give it wholly to God, often by destroying it (Joshua 6:17–19).
[^18]: The meaning is uncertain. The verb can mean "gash yourself", a mourning rite (Jeremiah 16:6), or "gather in bands". Here it plays on *gedud*, "troop, raiding band". Verse 14 is 5:1 in most English Bibles.
[^19]: The *judge of Israel* is its ruler. *Judge* (*shofet*) sounds like *shevet*, "rod".
