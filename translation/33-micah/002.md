**1** Woe to[^1] those who plan wickedness and work out evil on their beds! When morning dawns they carry it out, because it is in their power.[^2]

**2** They covet fields and seize them, and houses, and take them. They oppress a man and his household, a person and his inheritance.[^3]

**3** Therefore this is what Yahweh[^4] says: Look, I am planning disaster against this clan, from which you cannot free your necks.[^5] You will no longer walk proudly, for it will be an evil time.[^6]

**4** On that day they will take up a taunt song[^7] against you and lament with a bitter lament,[^8] saying, "We are utterly ruined! He changes the portion of my people. How he takes it away from me! He divides our fields among traitors."[^9]

**5** Therefore you will have no one to cast the measuring line by lot[^10] in the assembly of Yahweh.

**6** "Do not preach," they preach.[^11] "No one should preach about such things. Disgrace will not overtake us."[^12]

**7** Should this be said, house of Jacob?[^13] Is the Spirit of Yahweh impatient?[^14] Are these the things he does? Do not my words do good to the one who walks uprightly?

**8** But lately my people have risen up as an enemy.[^15] You strip the robe from off the garment of those who pass by trustingly, like men returning from war.[^16]

**9** You drive the women of my people out of the homes they delight in. From their young children you take away my splendor[^17] forever.

**10** Get up and go, for this is no place of rest,[^18] because of the uncleanness that destroys with a painful destruction.[^19]

**11** If a man went about with wind and lies[^20] and deceived, saying, "I will preach to you about wine and beer," he would be the preacher for this people!

**12** I will surely gather all of you, Jacob; I will surely bring together the remnant of Israel. I will put them together like sheep in a fold,[^21] like a flock in the middle of its pasture. The place will hum with people.

**13** The one who breaks open the way[^22] will go up before them. They will break through, pass through the gate and go out by it. Their king will pass on before them, Yahweh at their head.

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^2]: Literally "because their hand is to God", an idiom for "it is within their power" (Genesis 31:29).
[^3]: Hebrew *nachalah*, the family land handed down from one generation to the next (1 Kings 21:3).
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: The disaster is pictured as a yoke.
[^6]: The same words are found in Amos 5:13.
[^7]: Hebrew *mashal*, a saying or proverb, here a mocking song.
[^8]: Literally "and lament a lament, 'It has happened,'", a play on similar-sounding words: *nahah nehi nihyah*.
[^9]: Or "to the one who takes us captive". Hebrew *shovev*, "rebel, apostate". The Hebrew of the verse is difficult.
[^10]: The land of Israel was first divided among its clans by lot (Joshua 14:2; 18:8–10). When the land is shared out again, the oppressors will have no share.
[^11]: Literally "drip", as in Amos 7:16. Also in verse 11. The speakers are the people, or their prophets, who want to silence Micah.
[^12]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "They shall not drip to these; disgraces will not move away".
[^13]: Some scholars read "Is the house of Jacob cursed?", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^14]: Literally "Is the Spirit of Yahweh short?", that is, impatient or lacking in power (compare Numbers 11:23). Hebrew *ruach* can mean spirit, wind or breath.
[^15]: The Hebrew is difficult. Some scholars read "But you rise up against my people as an enemy", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^16]: Or "like men who are against war", peaceful people who expect no harm.
[^17]: Perhaps their share in the land, or the freedom they had as Yahweh's people.
[^18]: The promised land was to be Israel's place of rest (Deuteronomy 12:9).
[^19]: Or "because it is unclean, it will be destroyed with a painful destruction". The Hebrew verb may also mean "take in pledge".
[^20]: Hebrew *ruach*, "wind" or "spirit", the same word as in verse 7: a false prophet who claims the spirit but has only wind.
[^21]: Hebrew *botsrah*, which is also the name Bozrah, a city of Edom, famous for its sheep (Isaiah 34:6).
[^22]: Hebrew *ha-porets*, "the breaker", like a shepherd breaking open the gap in a sheepfold to lead the flock out.
