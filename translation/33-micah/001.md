**1** The word of the LORD[^1] that came to Micah of Moresheth[^2] in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah,[^3] which he saw concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.

**2** Hear, you peoples, all of you;[^4] listen, earth, and all that is in it! Let the Lord GOD[^5] be a witness against you, the Lord from his holy temple.

**3** For look, the LORD is coming out of his place. He will come down and tread on the heights of the earth.

**4** The mountains will melt under him, and the valleys will split open, like wax before the fire, like water poured down a slope.

**5** All this is because of the rebellion[^6] of Jacob, because of the sins of the house of Israel. What is the rebellion of Jacob? Is it not Samaria? And what are the high places of Judah?[^7] Are they not Jerusalem?

**6** So I will make Samaria a heap of rubble in the open country, a place for planting vineyards. I will pour her stones down into the valley and lay bare her foundations.

**7** All her carved images will be smashed, all her prostitute's wages[^8] will be burned with fire, and I will make all her idols a desolation. For she gathered them from a prostitute's wages, and to a prostitute's wages they will return.

**8** Because of this I will mourn and wail; I will go barefoot[^9] and naked. I will mourn like the jackals, and lament like the ostriches.

**9** For her wound is incurable;[^10] it has come to Judah. It has reached the gate of my people, Jerusalem itself.

**10** Tell it not in Gath;[^11] do not weep at all.[^12] In Beth-leaphrah, roll yourself in the dust.[^13]

**11** Pass on your way, you who live in Shaphir, in shameful nakedness.[^14] Those who live in Zaanan do not come out.[^15] Beth-ezel is wailing; it will take its support away from you.[^16]

**12** For those who live in Maroth writhe in anguish, waiting for good,[^17] because disaster has come down from the LORD to the gate of Jerusalem.

**13** Harness the horses to the chariot, you who live in Lachish.[^18] She was the beginning of sin for Daughter Zion,[^19] for in you were found the rebellions of Israel.

**14** Therefore you will give parting gifts to Moresheth-gath.[^20] The houses of Achzib will be a deception to the kings of Israel.[^21]

**15** I will yet bring a conqueror against you, you who live in Mareshah.[^22] The glory of Israel will come to Adullam.[^23]

**16** Shave your head bald and cut off your hair for the children you delight in. Make yourself as bald as the vulture,[^24] for they have gone from you into exile.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Micah* is a short form of *Mikhayahu*, "Who is like the LORD?" (compare 7:18). Moresheth, also called Moresheth-gath (verse 14), was a town in the low hills of Judah, about 25 miles (40 km) southwest of Jerusalem.
[^3]: These kings reigned from about 750 to 686 BC. Jeremiah 26:18 quotes Micah 3:12 as spoken in the days of Hezekiah.
[^4]: Literally "Hear, peoples, all of them". The same words end the story of the prophet Micaiah son of Imlah in 1 Kings 22:28.
[^5]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. The second *Lord* in the verse is *Adonai* alone.
[^6]: Hebrew *pesha*, "rebellion, transgression", a deliberate breaking of faith. Also in verse 13.
[^7]: The high places were hill shrines where sacrifices were offered. The Septuagint reads "the sin of the house of Judah".
[^8]: Hebrew *etnan*, the fee paid to a prostitute. The gifts given at Samaria's shrines are pictured as the earnings of an unfaithful wife (Hosea 2:14; 2:12 in most English Bibles; 9:1). When the city falls, its treasures will go to adorn the temples of the conquerors.
[^9]: Or "stripped". The prophet acts out the fate of captives, as Isaiah did (Isaiah 20:2–4).
[^10]: Literally "her wounds are incurable". *Her* is Samaria.
[^11]: Verses 10–15 are a series of plays on the names of towns, most of them in the low hills of Judah near Micah's home, on the path of an invading army, probably that of Sennacherib king of Assyria in 701 BC. The Hebrew is difficult, and several of the towns are unknown. This line repeats David's lament for Saul (2 Samuel 1:20), and *Gath* (*gat*) sounds like *taggidu*, "tell".
[^12]: Literally "weeping, do not weep". Some scholars read "In Acco do not weep", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^13]: *Beth-leaphrah* means "house of dust", and *afar* is "dust". The Hebrew text is written "I have rolled myself" but read "roll yourself".
[^14]: *Shaphir* means "beautiful", but its people will go into exile naked and ashamed. Literally "in nakedness of shame".
[^15]: *Zaanan* sounds like *yatse'ah*, "come out".
[^16]: Literally "The wailing of Beth-ezel will take from you its standing place". The meaning is uncertain. *Beth-ezel* may mean "house nearby" or "house of removal".
[^17]: *Maroth* means "bitterness". Its people hoped for good, but disaster came. Or "wait anxiously for good".
[^18]: *Lachish*, the chief fortress of the low hills, sounds like *rekhesh*, "team of horses". Sennacherib besieged and took it in 701 BC (2 Kings 18:13–14).
[^19]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. Lachish may have been the first city in Judah to trust in chariots and horses, or to bring in the sins of the north.
[^20]: Hebrew *shilluchim*, the gifts given to a daughter when she leaves home to be married. *Moresheth* sounds like *me'orasah*, "betrothed". Judah will have to give up Micah's home town as a father gives away his daughter.
[^21]: *Achzib* sounds like *akhzav*, "deceptive", a word used of a stream that dries up (Jeremiah 15:18). *The kings of Israel* here may be the kings of Judah.
[^22]: Hebrew *ha-yoresh*, "the one who takes possession", sounds like *Mareshah*.
[^23]: The cave of Adullam was where David hid from Saul (1 Samuel 22:1). Israel's nobles, its *glory*, will have to hide there too.
[^24]: Hebrew *nesher*, which can mean "eagle" or "vulture", here the griffon vulture, whose head and neck are almost bare. Shaving the head was a sign of mourning (Amos 8:10).
