**1** Hear what Yahweh[^1] is saying: Rise, plead your case[^2] before the mountains, and let the hills hear your voice.

**2** Hear, you mountains, Yahweh's case, you enduring foundations of the earth! For Yahweh has a case against his people, and he will argue it with Israel.

**3** My people, what have I done to you? How have I wearied you?[^3] Answer me!

**4** For I brought you up from the land of Egypt and redeemed you from the house of slavery. I sent Moses, Aaron and Miriam ahead of you.

**5** My people, remember what Balak king of Moab planned and what Balaam son of Beor answered him,[^4] and what happened from Shittim to Gilgal,[^5] so that you may know the saving acts[^6] of Yahweh.

**6** "With what shall I come before Yahweh[^7] and bow down before God on high? Shall I come before him with burnt offerings, with calves a year old?

**7** Will Yahweh be pleased with thousands of rams, with ten thousand rivers of oil? Shall I give my firstborn for my rebellion, the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?"[^8]

**8** He has told you, mortal,[^9] what is good. And what does Yahweh require of you but to do justice, to love faithful love,[^10] and to walk humbly[^11] with your God?

**9** Listen! Yahweh is calling to the city, and it is wisdom to fear your name:[^12] Hear of the rod, and of the one who appointed it.[^13]

**10** Can I forget[^14] the treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked, and the short ephah,[^15] which is cursed?

**11** Can I acquit[^16] someone with wicked scales, with a bag of false weights?

**12** Her rich men are full of violence; her inhabitants speak lies, and their tongues in their mouths are deceitful.

**13** So I have struck you with a painful wound,[^17] to make you desolate because of your sins.

**14** You will eat but not be satisfied; your hunger[^18] will remain inside you. You will carry things away but not save them,[^19] and what you do save I will give to the sword.

**15** You will sow but not reap; you will tread olives but not anoint yourself with oil, and tread grapes[^20] but not drink wine.

**16** For the decrees of Omri are kept, and all the practices of the house of Ahab,[^21] and you have followed their advice. So I will make you a desolation, and the city's inhabitants an object of hissing;[^22] you will bear the scorn of my people.[^23]

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *riv*, a lawsuit. Yahweh brings a case against his people, with the mountains and the foundations of the earth as witnesses (compare Deuteronomy 32:1; Isaiah 1:2). Also in verse 2.
[^3]: A play on words: *he'le'tikha*, "I have wearied you", and *he'elitikha*, "I brought you up", in verse 4.
[^4]: Balak hired Balaam to curse Israel, but Balaam blessed them instead (Numbers 22–24).
[^5]: Shittim was Israel's last camp east of the Jordan (Numbers 25:1; Joshua 3:1), and Gilgal the first camp west of it, after the crossing (Joshua 4:19). *What happened* is added: literally "from Shittim to Gilgal".
[^6]: Hebrew *tsidqot*, "righteous acts", here Yahweh's acts of rescue (Judges 5:11).
[^7]: In verses 6–7 a worshiper asks what Yahweh wants, and in verse 8 the prophet answers.
[^8]: Hebrew *nephesh*, "soul, self, life". The sacrifice of children was forbidden and condemned (Leviticus 18:21; 2 Kings 16:3; 21:6).
[^9]: Hebrew *adam*, "human being".
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love, shown to God and to others. Traditionally "to love mercy" or "to love kindness".
[^11]: Or "carefully", "wisely". The meaning of the Hebrew word, *hatsnea*, is not certain. This verse is often taken as a summary of the prophets' teaching (compare Isaiah 1:17; Hosea 6:6; Amos 5:24).
[^12]: The Hebrew of verses 9–10 is difficult. Literally "and wisdom sees your name". The same consonants can be read "fear your name". The Septuagint reads "and he will save those who fear his name".
[^13]: The rod of punishment (Isaiah 10:5). Or "Hear, you tribe, and the one who assembled it". Hebrew *matteh* can mean "rod" or "tribe".
[^14]: Literally "Is there yet a man of the house of the wicked …?" Many scholars read "Can I forget …?", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^15]: Literally "the ephah of leanness": a measure made smaller than it should be, to cheat the buyer (Amos 8:5). An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel).
[^16]: Literally "Shall I be pure with …?" The same consonants can be read "Shall I acquit …?"
[^17]: Literally "I have made sick, striking you". The Septuagint reads "I will begin to strike you".
[^18]: Hebrew *yeshach*, found only here. The meaning is uncertain: perhaps "emptiness".
[^19]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^20]: Hebrew *tirosh*, new wine, the fresh juice of the grapes.
[^21]: Omri and his son Ahab, kings of Israel in the ninth century BC, built Samaria and promoted the worship of Baal. Ahab took Naboth's vineyard by fraud and murder (1 Kings 16:23–33; 21).
[^22]: People whistled in horror at the sight of a ruin (Jeremiah 19:8). *The city's inhabitants* is literally "her inhabitants".
[^23]: The Septuagint reads "the scorn of peoples".
