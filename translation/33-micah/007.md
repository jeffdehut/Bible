**1** Woe is me![^1] For I have become like one who gathers summer fruit, like one who gleans after the grape harvest. There is no cluster to eat, no early fig that I[^2] long for.

**2** The faithful[^3] have vanished from the land, and there is no one upright among human beings. All of them lie in wait to shed blood; each hunts his brother with a net.

**3** Their hands are skilled at doing evil.[^4] The official asks for gifts, and the judge judges for a bribe. The powerful man speaks out what he craves, and so they weave it together.[^5]

**4** The best of them is like a briar, the most upright worse than a thorn hedge. The day of your watchmen,[^6] the day of your punishment, has come; now their confusion will come.

**5** Do not trust a neighbor; do not rely on a friend. Guard the doors of your mouth from the woman who lies in your arms.

**6** For the son treats his father with contempt, the daughter rises up against her mother, and the daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law. A man's enemies are the people of his own household.[^7]

**7** But as for me, I will watch for the LORD;[^8] I will wait for the God of my salvation. My God will hear me.

**8** Do not gloat over me, my enemy![^9] Though I have fallen, I will rise. Though I sit in darkness, the LORD is my light.

**9** I will bear the anger of the LORD, because I have sinned against him, until he pleads my case and gives me justice. He will bring me out into the light; I will see his righteousness.[^10]

**10** Then my enemy will see it, and shame will cover her, she who said to me, "Where is the LORD your God?" My eyes will look on her; now she will be trampled down like mud in the streets.

**11** A day for building your walls! On that day the boundary will be extended far.[^11]

**12** On that day they will come to you from Assyria and the cities of Egypt,[^12] from Egypt to the River,[^13] from sea to sea and from mountain to mountain.

**13** But the earth will become a desolation because of those who live in it, as the fruit of their deeds.

**14** Shepherd your people with your staff, the flock that is your inheritance, which lives alone in a forest in the middle of a fruitful land.[^14] Let them graze in Bashan and Gilead[^15] as in the days of old.

**15** As in the days when you came out of the land of Egypt, I will show them wonders.[^16]

**16** The nations will see and be ashamed of all their might. They will put their hands over their mouths; their ears will be deaf.

**17** They will lick the dust like a snake, like creatures that crawl on the ground. They will come trembling out of their strongholds. They will turn in fear to the LORD our God, and they will be afraid of you.

**18** Who is a God like you,[^17] who forgives wrongdoing and passes over the rebellion of the remnant of his inheritance? He does not hold on to his anger forever, because he delights in faithful love.[^18]

**19** He will again have compassion on us; he will tread our wrongdoings underfoot. You will hurl all their sins[^19] into the depths of the sea.

**20** You will show faithfulness to Jacob and faithful love to Abraham,[^20] as you swore to our ancestors in the days of old.

[^1]: Hebrew *alelai li*, a cry of grief, found also in Job 10:15.
[^2]: Literally "my *nephesh*", "my soul".
[^3]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful, loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^4]: Literally "On evil, both hands, to do it well".
[^5]: The meaning of the last words is uncertain. Literally "he, and they weave it".
[^6]: The prophets, who watched and warned of the coming day (Jeremiah 6:17; Ezekiel 3:17).
[^7]: Jesus echoes this verse in Matthew 10:35–36 and Luke 12:53.
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: The Hebrew is feminine. In verses 8–10 Zion speaks to an enemy city or nation, and in verses 11–13 she is addressed.
[^10]: Or "his saving justice". Hebrew *tsedaqah*, "righteousness", here the LORD's faithfulness in putting things right.
[^11]: Hebrew *choq*, "boundary" or "decree". Or "the decree will be far off".
[^12]: Hebrew *matsor*, a rare poetic name for Egypt (2 Kings 19:24; Isaiah 19:6), which also means "siege" or "fortress". Also later in the verse.
[^13]: The Euphrates.
[^14]: Hebrew *karmel*, "garden land, orchard", also the name of Mount Carmel (Amos 1:2).
[^15]: Rich pasture lands east of the Jordan (Jeremiah 50:19).
[^16]: The LORD answers the prayer of verse 14. *Them* is literally "him", that is, Israel. Some scholars read "Show us wonders", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^17]: The question echoes Micah's own name, "Who is like the LORD?" (1:1), and the song of Moses (Exodus 15:11). *Forgives wrongdoing* is literally "lifts guilt" (compare Exodus 34:7).
[^18]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^19]: The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "our sins".
[^20]: Literally "You will give truth to Jacob, faithful love to Abraham". Hebrew *emet*, "faithfulness, truth", and *chesed*, "faithful love", are the pair in the LORD's description of himself in Exodus 34:6. Compare Genesis 22:16–18; 28:13–15.
