**1** For look, in those days and at that time, when I restore the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem,[^1]

**2** I will gather all the nations and bring them down to the Valley of Jehoshaphat.[^2] There I will enter into judgment with them on behalf of my people, my inheritance Israel, whom they scattered among the nations, and because they divided up my land.

**3** They cast lots for my people. They traded a boy for a prostitute, and sold a girl for wine and drank it.

**4** What are you to me, Tyre and Sidon and all the regions of Philistia? Are you paying me back for something? If you are paying me back, I will swiftly and speedily return your deeds on your own heads.

**5** For you took my silver and my gold, and carried my rich treasures into your temples.[^3]

**6** You sold the people of Judah and Jerusalem to the Greeks,[^4] to remove them far from their own territory.

**7** Look, I am going to rouse them from the place where you sold them, and I will return your deeds on your own heads.

**8** I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the people of Judah, and they will sell them to the Sabeans,[^5] to a nation far away, for Yahweh[^6] has spoken.

**9** Proclaim this among the nations: Prepare for war![^7] Rouse the warriors! Let all the soldiers draw near and come up!

**10** Beat your plowshares into swords and your pruning knives into spears.[^8] Let the weak say, "I am a warrior!"

**11** Hurry[^9] and come, all you nations around, and gather there. Bring down your warriors, Yahweh!

**12** Let the nations rouse themselves and come up to the Valley of Jehoshaphat, for there I will sit to judge all the nations around.

**13** Swing the sickle, for the harvest is ripe. Come, tread the grapes, for the winepress is full. The vats overflow, for their wickedness is great.[^10]

**14** Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision![^11] For the day of Yahweh is near in the valley of decision.

**15** Sun and moon grow dark, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**16** Yahweh roars from Zion and raises his voice from Jerusalem,[^12] and the heavens and the earth shake. But Yahweh is a refuge for his people, a stronghold for the Israelites.

**17** Then you will know that I am Yahweh your God, who lives in Zion, my holy mountain. Jerusalem will be holy, and foreigners will never again pass through it.

**18** On that day the mountains will drip sweet wine, and the hills will flow with milk. All the streambeds of Judah will flow with water, and a spring will come out from the house of Yahweh and water the Valley of Shittim.[^13]

**19** Egypt will become a desolation, and Edom a desolate wilderness, because of the violence done to the people of Judah, in whose land they shed innocent blood.

**20** But Judah will be inhabited forever, and Jerusalem from generation to generation.

**21** I will avenge their blood, which I have not yet avenged.[^14] Yahweh lives in Zion.

[^1]: Verses 1–21 are 3:1–21 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Jehoshaphat means "Yahweh judges". The valley is not otherwise known; the name fits the judgment that takes place there (verse 12). Since the 4th century AD it has been identified with the Kidron Valley east of Jerusalem.
[^3]: Or "your palaces". Hebrew *heikhalot*.
[^4]: Hebrew *Yevanim*, the Ionians: the Greeks.
[^5]: The people of Sheba in southern Arabia, traders who traveled great distances (1 Kings 10:1–2; Job 1:15).
[^6]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^7]: Literally "Sanctify war", that is, prepare for battle with the rites that went before it.
[^8]: The words reverse Isaiah 2:4 and Micah 4:3.
[^9]: The meaning of the Hebrew *ushu* is uncertain. It may mean "hurry" or "come to help".
[^10]: Compare Revelation 14:14–20.
[^11]: Hebrew *charuts*, "decision" or "verdict". The word can also mean a threshing sledge (Isaiah 41:15).
[^12]: The same words begin the book of Amos (Amos 1:2).
[^13]: Or "the Valley of Acacias". Compare Ezekiel 47:1–12; Zechariah 14:8.
[^14]: Literally "I will declare innocent their blood that I have not declared innocent". The meaning is uncertain. It may be a question: "Shall I leave their blood unpunished? I will not." The Septuagint reads "I will avenge their blood, and I will not leave it unpunished".
