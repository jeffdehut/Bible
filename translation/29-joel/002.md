**1** Blow the horn in Zion; sound the alarm on my holy mountain! Let all who live in the land tremble, for the day of the LORD[^1] is coming; it is near:

**2** a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness. Like dawn spreading over the mountains[^2] a great and mighty people comes. There has never been anything like it, and there will never be again after it, through the years of all generations.

**3** Before them fire devours, and behind them a flame blazes. Before them the land is like the garden of Eden, and behind them a desolate wilderness. Nothing escapes them.

**4** Their appearance is like the appearance of horses, and they run like war horses.

**5** With a noise like chariots they leap on the tops of the mountains, like the crackling of a flame of fire devouring stubble, like a mighty people drawn up for battle.

**6** Before them peoples are in anguish; every face turns pale.[^3]

**7** They charge like warriors; they climb the wall like soldiers. Each goes on his way, and they do not swerve from their paths.

**8** They do not jostle one another; each goes in his own track. They burst through the weapons and are not stopped.[^4]

**9** They rush on the city; they run along the wall; they climb into the houses; they enter through the windows like a thief.

**10** Before them the earth quakes, the heavens tremble. Sun and moon grow dark, and the stars withdraw their shining.

**11** The LORD raises his voice before his army. His camp is very great; mighty is the one who carries out his word. For the day of the LORD is great and very terrible. Who can endure it?

**12** Yet even now, declares the LORD, return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping and with mourning.

**13** Tear your hearts and not your garments. Return to the LORD your God, for he is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in faithful love,[^5] and he relents from sending disaster.[^6]

**14** Who knows? He may turn and relent, and leave behind him a blessing: a grain offering and a drink offering for the LORD your God.

**15** Blow the horn in Zion! Set apart a fast; call a solemn assembly.

**16** Gather the people; consecrate the congregation. Assemble the elders; gather the children, even nursing infants. Let the bridegroom leave his room and the bride her bridal chamber.[^7]

**17** Between the porch and the altar let the priests, who serve the LORD, weep and say, "Spare your people, LORD. Do not make your inheritance a disgrace, a byword among the nations.[^8] Why should they say among the peoples, 'Where is their God?'"

**18** Then the LORD became jealous for his land and took pity on his people.[^9]

**19** The LORD answered his people, "I am sending you grain, new wine and olive oil, and you will be satisfied with them. I will never again make you a disgrace among the nations.

**20** I will drive the northerner[^10] far from you, and banish him into a parched and desolate land, his front into the eastern sea and his rear into the western sea.[^11] His stench will rise; his foul smell will rise. For he has done great things!"[^12]

**21** Do not be afraid, land; rejoice and be glad, for the LORD has done great things!

**22** Do not be afraid, you wild animals, for the pastures of the wilderness are turning green. The tree bears its fruit; the fig tree and the vine give their full yield.

**23** Children of Zion, rejoice and be glad in the LORD your God, for he has given you the autumn rain in righteousness.[^13] He has poured down rain for you, the autumn rain and the spring rain, as before.[^14]

**24** The threshing floors will be full of grain; the vats will overflow with new wine and olive oil.

**25** I will repay you for the years that the swarming locust has eaten, the hopping locust, the destroying locust and the cutting locust,[^15] my great army that I sent against you.

**26** You will eat your fill and be satisfied, and you will praise the name of the LORD your God, who has dealt wonderfully with you. My people will never again be put to shame.

**27** Then you will know that I am in the middle of Israel, and that I am the LORD your God, and there is no other. My people will never again be put to shame.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "Like blackness spread over the mountains", reading the same consonants as *shechor*, "blackness", instead of *shachar*, "dawn".
[^3]: Literally "all faces gather a glow". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain (compare Nahum 2:11, 2:10 in most English Bibles).
[^4]: Or "and do not break ranks". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^5]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^6]: Compare Exodus 34:6–7; Jonah 4:2.
[^7]: Hebrew *chuppah*, a covering or canopy (see Isaiah 4:5).
[^8]: Or "for the nations to rule over them".
[^9]: Or "Then the LORD will be jealous for his land and take pity on his people." The Hebrew verbs are in the form usually used to narrate past events.
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-tsefoni*, "the northern one": the locust army, pictured as an invader from the north (compare Jeremiah 1:14; Ezekiel 38:6, 15).
[^11]: The Dead Sea and the Mediterranean.
[^12]: Here perhaps "for he has acted proudly", of the northern army. In verse 21 the same words are used of the LORD.
[^13]: Or "he has given you the teacher of righteousness". Hebrew *ha-moreh* can mean "the autumn rain" or "the teacher" (compare Hosea 10:12). *In righteousness* may mean "in due measure" or "as a sign of his faithfulness".
[^14]: Or "in the first month". Hebrew *malqosh*, the spring rain of March and April, and *moreh*, the autumn rain that begins the growing season.
[^15]: See 1:4.
