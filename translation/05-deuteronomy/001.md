**1** These are the words that Moses spoke to all Israel east of the Jordan,[^1] in the wilderness, in the Arabah[^2] opposite Suph,[^3] between Paran and Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth and Di-zahab.

**2** It is eleven days' journey from Horeb[^4] to Kadesh-barnea by the Mount Seir road.

**3** In the fortieth year, on the first day of the eleventh month, Moses spoke to the Israelites, telling them everything the LORD[^5] had commanded him to tell them.

**4** This was after he had defeated Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon, and Og king of Bashan, who lived in Ashtaroth, at Edrei.[^6]

**5** East of the Jordan, in the land of Moab, Moses began to explain this law.[^7] He said:[^8]

**6** "The LORD our God spoke to us at Horeb. He said, 'You have stayed at this mountain long enough.

**7** Turn and set out. Go to the hill country of the Amorites and to all their neighbors in the Arabah, in the hill country, in the Shephelah,[^9] in the Negev[^10] and along the seacoast: the land of the Canaanites, and Lebanon, as far as the great river, the river Euphrates.

**8** See, I have set the land before you. Go in and take possession of the land that the LORD swore to give to your ancestors,[^11] to Abraham, to Isaac and to Jacob, and to their descendants after them.'

**9** At that time I said to you, 'I cannot carry you by myself.

**10** The LORD your God has multiplied you, and here you are today, as many as the stars of the sky.

**11** May the LORD, the God of your ancestors, make you a thousand times as many as you are, and bless you as he has promised you.

**12** How can I bear by myself the weight of you, the burden of you and your quarrels?

**13** Choose[^12] wise, discerning and respected[^13] men from each of your tribes, and I will make them your leaders.'

**14** You answered me, 'What you have told us to do is good.'

**15** So I took the leaders of your tribes, wise and respected men, and made them leaders over you: commanders of thousands, commanders of hundreds, commanders of fifties and commanders of tens, and officials[^14] for your tribes.

**16** At that time I gave your judges this command: 'Hear the disputes between your fellow Israelites,[^15] and judge fairly between one person and another, whether a fellow Israelite or a foreigner who lives among you.[^16]

**17** Do not show partiality in judging.[^17] Hear the small and the great alike. Do not be afraid of anyone, for judgment belongs to God. Bring to me any case that is too hard for you, and I will hear it.'

**18** At that time I commanded you all the things you were to do.

**19** Then we set out from Horeb and went through all that great and terrible wilderness that you saw, on the way to the hill country of the Amorites, as the LORD our God had commanded us. And we came to Kadesh-barnea.

**20** I said to you, 'You have come to the hill country of the Amorites, which the LORD our God is giving us.

**21** See, the LORD your God has set the land before you.[^18] Go up and take possession of it, as the LORD, the God of your ancestors, has told you. Do not be afraid or discouraged.'

**22** Then all of you came to me and said, 'Let us send men ahead of us to explore the land for us. Let them bring us back a report about the route we should take up and the cities we will come to.'

**23** The idea seemed good to me, so I took twelve men from among you, one man from each tribe.

**24** They turned and went up into the hill country and came to the Valley of Eshcol,[^19] and they spied out the land.

**25** They took some of the fruit of the land in their hands and brought it down to us. They brought us back a report and said, 'The land that the LORD our God is giving us is good.'

**26** But you were not willing to go up. You rebelled against the command[^20] of the LORD your God.

**27** You grumbled in your tents and said, 'The LORD hates us. That is why he brought us out of the land of Egypt, to hand us over to the Amorites to destroy us.

**28** Where can we go up to? Our brothers have made our hearts melt.[^21] They say, "The people are bigger and taller than we are. The cities are large, with walls up to the sky.[^22] And we even saw the Anakim[^23] there."'

**29** Then I said to you, 'Do not be terrified or afraid of them.

**30** The LORD your God, who is going ahead of you, will himself fight for you, just as he did for you in Egypt before your eyes,

**31** and in the wilderness. There you saw how the LORD your God carried you, as a man carries his son, all the way you went until you came to this place.'

**32** Yet in spite of this,[^24] you did not trust the LORD your God,

**33** who went ahead of you on the way to look for a place for you to camp, in fire by night to show you the way you should go, and in the cloud by day.

**34** When the LORD heard what you said,[^25] he was angry, and he swore,

**35** 'Not one of these men, this evil generation, will see the good land that I swore to give to your ancestors,

**36** except Caleb son of Jephunneh. He will see it, and I will give him and his children the land he has walked on, because he followed the LORD completely.'[^26]

**37** Because of you, the LORD was angry with me too. He said, 'You will not enter it either.

**38** Joshua son of Nun, who serves you,[^27] will enter it. Encourage him, for he will lead Israel to take possession of it.[^28]

**39** And your little ones, who you said would be taken as plunder, and your children, who today do not yet know good from bad, they will enter it. I will give it to them, and they will take possession of it.

**40** But as for you, turn around and set out for the wilderness, by the road to the Red Sea.'[^29]

**41** Then you answered me, 'We have sinned against the LORD. We will go up and fight, just as the LORD our God commanded us.' Each of you put on his weapons, and you thought it easy[^30] to go up into the hill country.

**42** But the LORD said to me, 'Tell them, "Do not go up and do not fight, for I am not with you.[^31] You will be defeated by your enemies."'

**43** So I told you, but you would not listen. You rebelled against the LORD's command, and in your arrogance you went up into the hill country.

**44** The Amorites who lived in that hill country came out against you and chased you like a swarm of bees. They struck you down in Seir as far as Hormah.

**45** You came back and wept before the LORD, but the LORD did not listen to your voice or pay any attention to you.

**46** So you stayed in Kadesh for a long time, all the time that you stayed there.[^32]

[^1]: Literally "beyond the Jordan", seen from the land west of the river. Also in verse 5.
[^2]: Hebrew *aravah*: the deep rift valley of the Jordan and the Dead Sea, and its continuation south to the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^3]: *Suph* is otherwise unknown. It may be short for *Yam Suph*, the Sea of Reeds (verse 40).
[^4]: Deuteronomy calls the mountain of God Horeb, the name used for Sinai in Exodus 3:1 and 17:6.
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: Edrei is where Og was defeated (3:1). Compare Joshua 12:4, "who lived in Ashtaroth and in Edrei".
[^7]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, law". The verb *be'er*, "explain", means to make something plain (compare 27:8).
[^8]: Moses' first speech runs from verse 6 to 4:40.
[^9]: The low hills between the coastal plain and the hill country of Judah.
[^10]: The dry country in the south of the land.
[^11]: Literally "fathers"; also in verses 11, 21 and 35.
[^12]: Literally "Give".
[^13]: Literally "known"; also in verse 15.
[^14]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions.
[^15]: Literally "your brothers".
[^16]: Literally "between a man and his brother and his foreigner". Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^17]: Literally "Do not recognize faces in judgment".
[^18]: Here the Hebrew changes from "you" plural to "you" singular, addressing Israel as one person. Deuteronomy often moves between the two.
[^19]: Hebrew *nachal*: a valley with a stream that flows in the rainy season. *Eshcol* means "cluster" of grapes (Numbers 13:23–24).
[^20]: Literally "mouth"; also in verse 43.
[^21]: That is, they have made us lose courage.
[^22]: Literally "fortified up to the heavens".
[^23]: Literally "the sons of the Anakim", a people known for their great height (Numbers 13:28, 33).
[^24]: Or "in this matter". Literally "And in this word".
[^25]: Literally "the sound of your words".
[^26]: Literally "he has filled after the LORD".
[^27]: Literally "who stands before you".
[^28]: Or "he will give it to Israel as their inheritance". Literally "he will cause Israel to inherit it".
[^29]: Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds", traditionally the Red Sea. Here it is probably the Gulf of Aqaba.
[^30]: The Hebrew verb occurs only here. It may mean "you thought it easy" or "you were eager".
[^31]: Literally "for I am not in your midst".
[^32]: Literally "according to the days that you stayed". The phrase probably means a long stay; it may also be read "as long as you did".
