**1** Suppose a man marries a woman and becomes her husband, and she does not please him because he has found something indecent[^1] about her. He writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand and sends her away from his house.[^2]

**2** She leaves his house and goes and becomes another man's wife.

**3** Then the second man turns against her,[^3] writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand and sends her away from his house, or the second man who married her dies.

**4** In that case her first husband, who sent her away, may not take her back to be his wife, since she has been defiled, for that would be detestable to Yahweh.[^4] You must not bring sin on the land that Yahweh your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**5** When a man has newly married,[^5] he is not to go out with the army or be given any other duty.[^6] He is to be free to stay at home for one year and bring joy to the wife he has married.

**6** No one is to take a pair of millstones, or even the upper millstone, as security for a debt,[^7] for that would be taking a life as security.

**7** If someone is found kidnapping a fellow Israelite[^8] and treating him as a slave[^9] or selling him, that kidnapper must die. So you must purge[^10] the evil from among you.

**8** In cases of skin disease,[^11] be very careful to do everything the Levitical priests[^12] instruct you. Be careful to do as I have commanded them.

**9** Remember what Yahweh your God did to Miriam on the way when you came out of Egypt.[^13]

**10** When you make your neighbor a loan of any kind, do not go into his house to take what he offers as security.

**11** Stay outside, and let the man to whom you are making the loan bring the security out to you.

**12** If he is poor, do not go to sleep with his security in your keeping.

**13** Be sure to give it back to him at sunset, so that he may sleep in his cloak and bless you. And it will be counted as righteousness for you before Yahweh your God.[^14]

**14** Do not take advantage of a hired worker who is poor and needy, whether he is a fellow Israelite or a foreigner[^15] who lives in your land, in one of your towns.[^16]

**15** Pay him his wages each day before sunset,[^17] because he is poor and is counting on it.[^18] Otherwise he may cry out to Yahweh against you, and you will be guilty of sin.

**16** Parents are not to be put to death for their children, and children are not to be put to death for their parents.[^19] Each is to be put to death for his own sin.

**17** Do not deny justice to the foreigner or the fatherless,[^20] and do not take a widow's cloak as security.

**18** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and Yahweh your God redeemed you from there. That is why I command you to do this.

**19** When you reap the harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, do not go back to get it. It is to be for the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow, so that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.

**20** When you beat your olive trees, do not go over the branches a second time.[^21] What is left is to be for the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow.

**21** When you gather the grapes of your vineyard, do not go over it a second time.[^22] What is left is to be for the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow.

**22** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt. That is why I command you to do this.

[^1]: Literally "nakedness of a thing". The phrase was much debated in later times.
[^2]: Verses 1–4 are discussed in Mark 10:2–9.
[^3]: Literally "hates her".
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "takes a new wife".
[^6]: Literally "and nothing shall pass over him for any matter".
[^7]: Millstones were needed every day to grind grain for bread.
[^8]: Literally "stealing a *nephesh* (person) from his brothers, the sons of Israel".
[^9]: Hebrew *hit'ammer*, a rare verb, perhaps "treat as merchandise" or "deal harshly with" (21:14).
[^10]: Literally "burn out".
[^11]: Literally "Guard yourself in the stroke of *tsara'at*". Hebrew *tsara'at*, traditionally "leprosy", covers a range of skin diseases (Leviticus 13–14).
[^12]: Literally "the priests, the Levites".
[^13]: Numbers 12:10–15.
[^14]: Compare Exodus 22:25–26.
[^15]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verses 17, 19, 20 and 21.
[^16]: Literally "in your land, in your gates".
[^17]: Literally "On his day you shall give him his wage, and the sun shall not go down on it".
[^18]: Literally "and to it he lifts up his *nephesh* (self)".
[^19]: Literally "Fathers … sons". This verse is quoted in 2 Kings 14:6.
[^20]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan". Also in verses 19, 20 and 21.
[^21]: Literally "do not go over the boughs after you".
[^22]: Literally "do not glean after you".
