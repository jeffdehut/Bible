**1** Then Moses and the elders of Israel commanded the people, "Keep the whole command[^1] that I am giving you today.

**2** On the day you cross the Jordan into the land that the LORD[^2] your God is giving you, set up some large stones and coat them with plaster.

**3** Write on them all the words of this law when you have crossed over, so that you may enter the land that the LORD your God is giving you, a land flowing with milk and honey, as the LORD, the God of your ancestors,[^3] has promised you.

**4** When you have crossed the Jordan, set up these stones on Mount Ebal,[^4] as I am commanding you today, and coat them with plaster.

**5** Build an altar there to the LORD your God, an altar of stones. Do not use any iron tool on them.[^5]

**6** Build the altar of the LORD your God with uncut stones,[^6] and offer burnt offerings on it to the LORD your God.

**7** Sacrifice fellowship offerings[^7] and eat them there, and rejoice before the LORD your God.

**8** And write very clearly[^8] on the stones all the words of this law."

**9** Then Moses and the Levitical priests[^9] spoke to all Israel, "Be silent and listen, Israel. This day you have become the people of the LORD your God.

**10** So listen to the voice of the LORD your God, and follow his commands and his decrees that I am giving you today."

**11** That day Moses gave the people this command:

**12** "When you have crossed the Jordan, these tribes are to stand on Mount Gerizim to bless the people: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph and Benjamin.

**13** And these tribes are to stand on Mount Ebal for the curse: Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan and Naphtali.

**14** The Levites are to proclaim in a loud voice to all the people of Israel:[^10]

**15** 'Cursed is anyone who makes a carved or cast idol,[^11] something detestable to the LORD, the work of a craftsman's hands, and sets it up in secret.' And all the people are to respond, 'Amen.'[^12]

**16** 'Cursed is anyone who treats his father or his mother with contempt.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**17** 'Cursed is anyone who moves his neighbor's boundary marker.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**18** 'Cursed is anyone who leads a blind person astray on the road.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**19** 'Cursed is anyone who denies justice to the foreigner,[^13] the fatherless[^14] or the widow.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**20** 'Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his father's wife, for he uncovers his father's garment.'[^15] And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**21** 'Cursed is anyone who has sexual relations with any animal.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**22** 'Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his sister, the daughter of his father or the daughter of his mother.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**23** 'Cursed is anyone who sleeps with his mother-in-law.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**24** 'Cursed is anyone who strikes down his neighbor in secret.' And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**25** 'Cursed is anyone who accepts a bribe to kill an innocent person.'[^16] And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

**26** 'Cursed is anyone who does not uphold the words of this law by doing them.'[^17] And all the people are to say, 'Amen.'

[^1]: The singular means the law as a whole.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "fathers".
[^4]: The Samaritan Pentateuch reads "Mount Gerizim".
[^5]: Literally "Do not wield iron over them". Compare Exodus 20:25.
[^6]: Literally "whole stones".
[^7]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings". In these sacrifices most of the meat was eaten by the worshipers.
[^8]: Literally "making it plain, well". The verb *be'er* is the one used in 1:5.
[^9]: Literally "the priests, the Levites".
[^10]: Literally "The Levites shall answer and say to every man of Israel with a loud voice".
[^11]: Hebrew *pesel* and *massekhah*, an image carved from wood or stone and an image cast in metal.
[^12]: Hebrew *amen*, "surely, so be it".
[^13]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner.
[^14]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan".
[^15]: Literally "uncovers the corner of his father's garment", that is, takes what belongs to his father (23:1).
[^16]: Literally "to strike a life, innocent blood".
[^17]: Paul quotes this verse in Galatians 3:10, in a form close to the Greek Septuagint, which reads "all the words of this law".
