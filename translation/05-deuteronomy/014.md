**1** You are the children[^1] of Yahweh[^2] your God. Do not cut yourselves or shave the front of your heads[^3] for the dead.

**2** For you are a people holy to Yahweh your God. Yahweh has chosen you out of all the peoples on the face of the earth to be his people, his treasured possession.[^4]

**3** Do not eat anything detestable.[^5]

**4** These are the animals you may eat: the ox, the sheep, the goat,

**5** the deer, the gazelle, the roe deer, the wild goat, the ibex, the antelope and the mountain sheep.[^6]

**6** You may eat any animal that has a divided hoof, split in two, and that chews the cud.

**7** But of those that chew the cud or that have a divided, split hoof, you must not eat these: the camel, the hare and the hyrax.[^7] Although they chew the cud, they do not have a divided hoof. They are unclean for you.

**8** And the pig is unclean for you, because although it has a divided hoof, it does not chew the cud. You must not eat their meat or touch their carcasses.

**9** Of all the creatures that live in the water, you may eat these: you may eat anything that has fins and scales.

**10** But you must not eat anything that does not have fins and scales. It is unclean for you.

**11** You may eat any clean bird.

**12** But these are the ones you must not eat:[^8] the eagle, the bearded vulture, the black vulture,

**13** the red kite, the black kite, any kind of falcon,[^9]

**14** any kind of raven,

**15** the ostrich,[^10] the nighthawk, the seagull, any kind of hawk,

**16** the little owl, the great owl, the white owl,

**17** the desert owl, the carrion vulture, the cormorant,

**18** the stork, any kind of heron, the hoopoe and the bat.

**19** All winged swarming creatures[^11] are unclean for you. They must not be eaten.

**20** You may eat any clean winged creature.

**21** Do not eat anything that has died by itself. You may give it to the foreigner[^12] who lives in your towns,[^13] and he may eat it, or you may sell it to an outsider.[^14] For you are a people holy to Yahweh your God. Do not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**22** Each year you must set aside a tenth of all the produce of your seed that comes from the field.

**23** In the presence of Yahweh your God, at the place he will choose as a dwelling for his name,[^15] you are to eat the tithe of your grain, your new wine and your olive oil, and the firstborn of your herds and your flocks, so that you may learn to fear Yahweh your God always.

**24** But Yahweh your God may bless you so much that the journey is too long for you to carry the tithe, because the place where Yahweh your God will choose to put his name is too far from you.

**25** Then exchange it for money. Wrap up the money[^16] and take it with you, and go to the place that Yahweh your God will choose.

**26** Spend the money on whatever you want: cattle or sheep, wine or beer,[^17] or anything you wish.[^18] Eat it there in the presence of Yahweh your God, and rejoice, you and your household.

**27** And do not neglect the Levite who lives in your towns, for he has no share or inheritance among you.

**28** At the end of every three years, bring out the whole tithe of your produce for that year and store it in your towns.

**29** Then the Levite, who has no share or inheritance among you, and the foreigner, the fatherless[^19] and the widow who live in your towns, may come and eat and be satisfied, so that Yahweh your God may bless you in all the work you do with your hands.

[^1]: Literally "sons".
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "make a bald place between your eyes".
[^4]: Hebrew *segullah*, a king's or an owner's personal treasure.
[^5]: Hebrew *to'evah*: something abhorrent, especially in worship.
[^6]: The exact identification of several of these animals is uncertain.
[^7]: Hebrew *shaphan*, a small animal that lives among rocks, traditionally "rock badger" or "coney". The hare and the hyrax move their jaws as if chewing the cud.
[^8]: The identification of many of the birds in verses 12–18 is uncertain. Compare the list in Leviticus 11:13–19.
[^9]: The Hebrew has three similar names here, *ra'ah*, *ayyah* and *dayyah*. Leviticus 11:14 has two, *da'ah* and *ayyah*.
[^10]: Literally "the daughter of the *ya'anah*". Or "the eagle owl".
[^11]: That is, winged insects.
[^12]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 29.
[^13]: Literally "within your gates"; also in verses 27, 28 and 29.
[^14]: Hebrew *nokhri*: a foreigner from another land who has not settled among the Israelites.
[^15]: Literally "to make his name dwell there".
[^16]: Literally "bind up the silver in your hand".
[^17]: Hebrew *shekhar*, an intoxicating drink, probably beer made from grain.
[^18]: Literally "anything your *nephesh* (self) asks of you".
[^19]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan".
