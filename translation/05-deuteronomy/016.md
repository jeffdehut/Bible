**1** Observe the month of Abib[^1] and keep the Passover[^2] for Yahweh[^3] your God, for in the month of Abib Yahweh your God brought you out of Egypt by night.

**2** Sacrifice the Passover animal for Yahweh your God from your flock or your herd, at the place that Yahweh will choose as a dwelling for his name.[^4]

**3** Do not eat anything made with yeast with it. For seven days eat bread made without yeast with it, the bread of hardship, because you left the land of Egypt in haste. Do this so that all the days of your life you may remember the day you left the land of Egypt.

**4** No yeast is to be found among you anywhere in your territory for seven days, and none of the meat you sacrifice on the evening of the first day is to be left overnight until morning.

**5** You may not sacrifice the Passover in just any of the towns[^5] that Yahweh your God is giving you,

**6** but only at the place that Yahweh your God will choose as a dwelling for his name. There you are to sacrifice the Passover in the evening, at sunset, at the time[^6] when you came out of Egypt.

**7** Cook[^7] it and eat it at the place that Yahweh your God will choose. Then in the morning turn and go back to your tents.

**8** For six days eat bread made without yeast, and on the seventh day hold a solemn assembly[^8] for Yahweh your God. Do no work on that day.

**9** Count seven weeks. Begin to count the seven weeks from the time you first put the sickle to the standing grain.

**10** Then keep the Festival of Weeks for Yahweh your God with a freewill offering from your hand, given in proportion to[^9] how Yahweh your God has blessed you.

**11** Rejoice before Yahweh your God at the place that Yahweh your God will choose as a dwelling for his name, you and your son and your daughter, your male and female slaves, the Levite who lives in your towns, and the foreigner,[^10] the fatherless[^11] and the widow who live among you.

**12** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and be careful to follow these decrees.

**13** Keep the Festival of Shelters[^12] for seven days, when you have gathered in the produce of your threshing floor and your winepress.

**14** Rejoice at your festival, you and your son and your daughter, your male and female slaves, and the Levite, the foreigner, the fatherless and the widow who live in your towns.

**15** For seven days keep the festival for Yahweh your God at the place that Yahweh will choose, for Yahweh your God will bless you in all your harvest and in all the work of your hands, and you will be full of joy.[^13]

**16** Three times a year all your males are to appear before Yahweh your God[^14] at the place he will choose: at the Festival of Unleavened Bread, at the Festival of Weeks and at the Festival of Shelters. No one is to appear before Yahweh empty-handed.

**17** Each of you is to give as he is able,[^15] according to the blessing that Yahweh your God has given you.

**18** Appoint judges and officials[^16] for your tribes in all the towns that Yahweh your God is giving you, and they are to judge the people with righteous judgment.

**19** Do not twist justice. Do not show partiality.[^17] Do not accept a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and twists the words of those who are in the right.[^18]

**20** Pursue justice, justice alone,[^19] so that you may live and possess the land that Yahweh your God is giving you.

**21** Do not plant any kind of tree as an Asherah pole[^20] beside the altar you build for Yahweh your God.

**22** And do not set up a standing stone,[^21] which Yahweh your God hates.

[^1]: *Abib* means "new ears of grain": the first month of the year, around March–April.
[^2]: Hebrew *pesach*: both the festival and the lamb or young goat sacrificed at it (Exodus 12). Also in verses 2, 5 and 6.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "to make his name dwell there"; also in verses 6 and 11.
[^5]: Literally "gates"; also in verses 11, 14 and 18.
[^6]: Hebrew *mo'ed*, "appointed time": either the time of day or the season of the year.
[^7]: Hebrew *bashal*, usually "boil". In Exodus 12:8–9 the Passover lamb is to be roasted and not boiled.
[^8]: Hebrew *atseret*, a closing assembly.
[^9]: Hebrew *missat*, a word found only here, probably meaning "the amount of".
[^10]: Hebrew *ger*: someone from elsewhere who lives among the Israelites, a resident foreigner. Also in verse 14.
[^11]: Hebrew *yatom*, a child who has lost his father, traditionally "orphan". Also in verse 14.
[^12]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths, shelters": huts made of branches (Leviticus 23:42–43). Traditionally "Tabernacles". Also in verse 16. Exodus 23:16 calls it the Festival of Ingathering.
[^13]: Literally "and you will be only joyful".
[^14]: Literally "be seen at the face of Yahweh your God"; also later in the verse. The same consonants could be read "see the face of Yahweh your God".
[^15]: Literally "according to the gift of his hand".
[^16]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions.
[^17]: Literally "Do not recognize faces".
[^18]: Compare Exodus 23:8.
[^19]: Literally "Righteousness, righteousness you shall pursue". Hebrew *tsedeq*.
[^20]: Hebrew *asherah*: a wooden pole or tree that was a symbol of the goddess Asherah.
[^21]: Hebrew *matsevah*: a standing stone set up as an object of worship.
