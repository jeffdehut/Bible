**1** When you go out to war against your enemies and see horses and chariots and an army larger than yours,[^1] do not be afraid of them, for the LORD[^2] your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt, is with you.

**2** When you are about to go into battle, the priest is to come forward and speak to the army.[^3]

**3** He is to say to them, "Hear, Israel. Today you are going into battle against your enemies. Do not lose heart.[^4] Do not be afraid, or alarmed, or terrified of them.

**4** For the LORD your God is the one who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies and to give you victory."[^5]

**5** Then the officials[^6] are to say to the army, "Has anyone built a new house and not yet dedicated it? Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else dedicate it.

**6** Has anyone planted a vineyard and not yet begun to enjoy its fruit?[^7] Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else enjoy it.

**7** Has anyone become engaged to a woman and not yet married her? Let him go back to his house, or he may die in battle and someone else marry her."

**8** Then the officials are to speak further to the army and say, "Is anyone afraid or fainthearted? Let him go back to his house, so that he does not make his brothers lose heart as he has."[^8]

**9** When the officials have finished speaking to the army, commanders are to be appointed at the head of the troops.[^9]

**10** When you approach a town to attack it, first offer it terms of peace.

**11** If it accepts your terms[^10] and opens its gates to you, all the people found in it are to become forced laborers for you and serve you.

**12** But if it does not make peace with you and goes to war with you, then lay siege to it.

**13** When the LORD your God hands it over to you, put all its males to the sword.[^11]

**14** But the women, the children, the livestock and everything else in the town, all its plunder, you may take as spoil for yourselves. And you may enjoy[^12] the spoil of your enemies that the LORD your God has given you.

**15** This is what you are to do to all the towns that are very far away from you, which are not among the towns of these nations.

**16** But in the towns of these peoples that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, you must not leave alive anything that breathes.

**17** You must devote them completely to destruction:[^13] the Hittites, the Amorites, the Canaanites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, as the LORD your God has commanded you,

**18** so that they will not teach you to do all the detestable things they do for their gods, and so cause you to sin against the LORD your God.

**19** When you besiege a town for a long time, fighting against it to capture it, do not destroy its trees by swinging an axe against them. You may eat from them, but you must not cut them down. Are the trees of the field human beings, that you should besiege them?[^14]

**20** Only the trees that you know are not fruit trees may you destroy and cut down, to build siege works against the town that is at war with you, until it falls.

[^1]: Literally "a people more numerous than you".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "the people"; also in verses 5, 8 and 9.
[^4]: Literally "Do not let your heart be faint".
[^5]: Literally "to save you".
[^6]: Hebrew *shoterim*: officers who kept records and helped the leaders carry out their decisions. Also in verses 8 and 9.
[^7]: Hebrew *chillel*, "make common": begin to use the fruit for ordinary purposes. A vineyard's fruit could be eaten freely only from the fifth year (Leviticus 19:23–25).
[^8]: Literally "so that the heart of his brothers may not melt like his heart".
[^9]: Literally "they shall appoint commanders of armies at the head of the people".
[^10]: Literally "If it answers you peace".
[^11]: Literally "strike all its males with the mouth of the sword".
[^12]: Literally "eat".
[^13]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^14]: The Hebrew vowel points read "for the human being is the tree of the field", which may mean that human life depends on the trees. The same consonants can be read as a question, as here.
