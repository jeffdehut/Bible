**1** But there were also false prophets among the people,[^1] just as there will be false teachers among you. They will secretly bring in destructive heresies,[^2] even denying the Master[^3] who bought them, and so bring swift destruction on themselves.

**2** Many will follow their debauchery,[^4] and because of them the way of truth will be slandered.

**3** In their greed they will exploit you with made-up stories. Their condemnation, pronounced long ago, is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep.

**4** For if God did not spare the angels when they sinned, but cast them into Tartarus[^5] and committed them to chains of deep darkness[^6] to be kept until the judgment;

**5** if he did not spare the ancient world, but protected Noah, a herald of righteousness, with seven others,[^7] when he brought the flood on a world of ungodly people;

**6** if he condemned the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to destruction,[^8] reducing them to ashes, and made them an example of what is coming for the ungodly;[^9]

**7** and if he rescued righteous Lot, who was worn down by the debauched conduct of lawless people

**8** (for that righteous man, living among them day after day, was tormenting his righteous soul over the lawless deeds he saw and heard),

**9** then the Lord knows[^10] how to rescue the godly from trial, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment until the day of judgment,[^11]

**10** especially those who follow the flesh in its polluting desire[^12] and despise authority.[^13] Bold and arrogant, they are not afraid to slander the glorious ones,[^14]

**11** whereas angels, though greater in strength and power, do not bring a slanderous judgment against them.[^15]

**12** But these people, like unreasoning animals, creatures of instinct born to be caught and destroyed, slander what they do not understand,[^16] and in their destruction they too will be destroyed.[^17]

**13** They will suffer wrong as the wage for their wrongdoing.[^18] They count it a pleasure to revel in broad daylight. They are blots and blemishes,[^19] reveling in their deceptions[^20] as they feast with you.

**14** They have eyes full of adultery[^21] that never stop sinning. They entice unstable souls. They have hearts trained in greed. They are accursed children![^22]

**15** They have left the straight road and gone astray, following the way of Balaam son of Bosor,[^23] who loved the wages of wrongdoing,

**16** but was rebuked for his own wrongdoing: a donkey that cannot speak spoke with a human voice and restrained the prophet's madness.[^24]

**17** These people are springs without water and mists[^25] driven by a storm. The gloom of darkness has been kept for them.[^26]

**18** For they speak arrogant and empty words, and by the desires of the flesh, by debauchery, they entice people who are just escaping[^27] from those who live in error.

**19** They promise them freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption. For people are slaves of whatever has mastered them.

**20** For if, after they have escaped the defilements of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they[^28] are again entangled in them and overcome, their final condition has become worse than the first.[^29]

**21** It would have been better for them never to have known the way of righteousness than, after knowing it, to turn back from the holy commandment that was handed on to them.

**22** What the true proverb says has happened to them: "A dog returns to its own vomit,"[^30] and, "A sow that has been washed goes back to wallowing in the mud."[^31]

[^1]: That is, Israel (compare Deuteronomy 13:2–6, 13:1–5 in most English Bibles). Verses 1–18 closely parallel Jude 4–16, and 3:1–3 parallels Jude 17–18; many of the same Greek words and images are used.
[^2]: Greek *haireseis*, "opinions, sects, factions"; literally "heresies of destruction".
[^3]: Greek *despotes*, "master, owner" (compare Jude 4).
[^4]: Greek *aselgeia*, "debauchery, unrestrained behavior" (compare Jude 4); also in verses 7 and 18.
[^5]: Greek *tartaroo*, "cast into Tartarus". In Greek myth Tartarus was the deepest part of the underworld, where the Titans were imprisoned; Jewish writers used the name for the place where rebel angels were held. Compare Jude 6 and the note there; Genesis 6:1–4.
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "pits of deep darkness".
[^7]: Literally "Noah, the eighth", that is, Noah and seven others (compare 1 Peter 3:20; Genesis 7:13). Genesis does not record Noah's preaching; later Jewish tradition told of it.
[^8]: Genesis 19:24–29. Some manuscripts leave out "to destruction".
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "an example for those who would live ungodly lives". Compare Jude 7.
[^10]: Verses 4–10 are one long sentence in Greek. Its conditions ("if God did not spare …", verses 4–7) are completed here; verse 8 is an aside.
[^11]: Or "to keep the unrighteous for punishment on the day of judgment".
[^12]: Literally "who go after flesh in desire of defilement" (compare Jude 7).
[^13]: Or "lordship". Greek *kyriotes* (compare Jude 8).
[^14]: Literally "glories", probably angelic beings (compare Jude 8).
[^15]: Many manuscripts and most modern editions add "before the Lord"; the SBLGNT leaves these words out, following a minority reading. Compare Jude 9.
[^16]: Literally "slandering in what they do not know". Compare Jude 10.
[^17]: Or "they will be destroyed like them", the animals.
[^18]: Literally "being wronged (as) the wage of wrong", a play on words. Many manuscripts read "receiving the wage of wrongdoing".
[^19]: Greek *spiloi kai momoi*, "spots and blemishes". Compare Jude 12, "hidden reefs" (*spilades*).
[^20]: Some manuscripts read "in their love feasts" (Greek *agapais* for *apatais*), as in Jude 12.
[^21]: Literally "full of an adulteress".
[^22]: Literally "children of a curse", a Hebrew way of speaking.
[^23]: Numbers 22–24, where Balaam's father is *Beor* (22:5). Some manuscripts read "Beor" here. Compare Jude 11.
[^24]: Numbers 22:21–35.
[^25]: Many manuscripts read "clouds", as in Jude 12.
[^26]: Many manuscripts add "forever", as in Jude 13.
[^27]: Many manuscripts read "who have truly escaped". Literally "who are barely escaping".
[^28]: *They* may be the false teachers or the people they entice.
[^29]: The same words as in Matthew 12:45.
[^30]: Proverbs 26:11, "Like a dog that returns to its vomit is a fool who repeats his folly."
[^31]: This saying is not from the Old Testament. A similar saying about a pig is found in the ancient story of Ahiqar.
