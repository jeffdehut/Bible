**1** The words of Amos,[^1] who was one of the sheep breeders[^2] of Tekoa,[^3] which he saw[^4] concerning Israel in the days of Uzziah king of Judah and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash king of Israel, two years before the earthquake.[^5]

**2** He said: The LORD[^6] roars from Zion and raises his voice[^7] from Jerusalem. The pastures of the shepherds mourn,[^8] and the top of Carmel[^9] withers.

**3** This is what the LORD says:[^10] For three crimes[^11] of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn it back,[^12] because they threshed Gilead with threshing sledges of iron.[^13]

**4** So I will send fire on the house of Hazael, and it will devour the fortresses[^14] of Ben-hadad.[^15]

**5** I will break the gate bar of Damascus. I will cut off the one who sits enthroned[^16] in the Valley of Aven, and the one who holds the scepter in Beth-eden.[^17] The people of Aram will go into exile to Kir,[^18] says the LORD.

**6** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Gaza, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they carried away whole communities[^19] into exile, to hand them over to Edom.

**7** So I will send fire on the wall of Gaza, and it will devour its fortresses.

**8** I will cut off the one who sits enthroned in Ashdod, and the one who holds the scepter in Ashkelon. I will turn my hand against Ekron, and the remnant of the Philistines will perish,[^20] says the Lord GOD.[^21]

**9** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Tyre, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they handed over whole communities to Edom and did not remember the covenant of brothers.[^22]

**10** So I will send fire on the wall of Tyre, and it will devour its fortresses.

**11** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of Edom, and for four, I will not turn it back, because he pursued his brother[^23] with the sword and stifled all pity.[^24] His anger tore without end, and he kept his fury forever.[^25]

**12** So I will send fire on Teman, and it will devour the fortresses of Bozrah.[^26]

**13** This is what the LORD says: For three crimes of the Ammonites, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they ripped open the pregnant women of Gilead to enlarge their territory.

**14** So I will kindle a fire on the wall of Rabbah,[^27] and it will devour its fortresses, with a battle cry on the day of battle, with a storm on the day of the whirlwind.

**15** Their king[^28] will go into exile, he and his officials together, says the LORD.

[^1]: A name from the verb "to carry a load".
[^2]: Hebrew *noqedim*, owners or keepers of sheep. The king of Moab is called a *noqed* in 2 Kings 3:4. Amos also calls himself a herdsman and a tender of sycamore figs (7:14).
[^3]: A town in the hills of Judah, about 10 miles (16 km) south of Jerusalem (2 Samuel 14:2).
[^4]: Hebrew *chazah*, "see", used of what a prophet receives in a vision.
[^5]: Uzziah reigned in Judah about 792–740 BC, and Jeroboam II in Israel about 793–753 BC. Amos probably spoke about 760 BC. The earthquake was remembered long afterward (Zechariah 14:5).
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7]: Literally "gives his voice". The same line is found in Joel 4:16 (3:16 in most English Bibles). The LORD roars like a lion (3:4, 8).
[^8]: Or "dry up". The Hebrew verb *aval* can mean either.
[^9]: The wooded ridge above the coast of northern Israel, one of the most fertile places in the land.
[^10]: In Amos's poetry, as in Isaiah, the words of the prophet and the words of the LORD alternate. The LORD's words in the poems are not put in quotation marks.
[^11]: Hebrew *pesha*, "rebellion, transgression", a deliberate breaking of what is right. Traditionally "transgressions". *For three … and for four*: a number saying (Proverbs 30:15–31). The crimes are many, and the last fills the measure. Each oracle in 1:3–2:16 then names one or more of them.
[^12]: Literally "I will not turn it back", that is, the punishment, or the word the LORD has spoken. Also in verses 6, 9, 11 and 13 and in 2:1, 4 and 6.
[^13]: Threshing sledges were heavy boards studded underneath with stones or iron spikes, dragged over grain. Gilead, east of the Jordan, belonged to Israel. Compare 2 Kings 10:32–33; 13:7.
[^14]: Hebrew *armenot*, the fortified palaces of a city. Also in verses 7, 10, 12 and 14.
[^15]: Hazael and his son Ben-hadad were kings of Aram, whose capital was Damascus (2 Kings 8:7–15; 13:3, 24).
[^16]: Or "the inhabitant". Also in verse 8.
[^17]: *Valley of Aven* means "valley of wickedness", perhaps the valley between the Lebanon ranges. *Beth-eden* means "house of delight", probably the Aramean state of Bit-adini on the Euphrates.
[^18]: The land the Arameans had come from (9:7). Compare 2 Kings 16:9.
[^19]: Literally "a complete exile". Also in verse 9. The people were sold as slaves.
[^20]: Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon and Ekron were four of the five chief cities of the Philistines. The fifth, Gath, is not named (compare 6:2).
[^21]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^22]: A treaty that bound the two sides as "brothers", perhaps with Israel (1 Kings 5:26, 5:12 in most English Bibles; 9:13).
[^23]: Israel. Edom was descended from Esau, the brother of Jacob (Genesis 25:24–26; 36:1).
[^24]: Literally "destroyed his compassion".
[^25]: Or "his fury kept watch forever". Many scholars read "he kept his anger forever" in the first line too, with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^26]: Teman was a region of Edom, and Bozrah one of its chief cities.
[^27]: The capital of the Ammonites, modern Amman in Jordan.
[^28]: Hebrew *malkam*, "their king". The same consonants can be read *Milkom*, Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5). A similar line in Jeremiah 49:3 adds "his priests".
