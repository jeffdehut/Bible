**1** Hear this word, which I take up over you as a lament,[^1] house of Israel:

**2** Fallen, never to rise again, is the virgin Israel.[^2] She lies abandoned on her land, with no one to raise her up.

**3** For this is what the Lord Yahweh[^3] says: The city that marches out a thousand strong will have a hundred left, and the one that marches out a hundred strong will have ten left, for the house of Israel.

**4** For this is what Yahweh says to the house of Israel: Seek me and live!

**5** But do not seek Bethel, do not go to Gilgal, and do not cross over to Beersheba.[^4] For Gilgal will surely go into exile, and Bethel will come to nothing.[^5]

**6** Seek Yahweh and live, or he will break out like fire on the house of Joseph,[^6] and it will devour, with no one to put it out for Bethel.

**7** You who turn justice into wormwood[^7] and throw righteousness to the ground!

**8** He who made the Pleiades and Orion,[^8] who turns deep darkness[^9] into morning and darkens day into night, who calls for the waters of the sea and pours them out on the face of the earth: Yahweh is his name.

**9** He makes destruction flash out[^10] against the strong, and destruction comes on the fortress.

**10** They hate the one who gives correction in the gate,[^11] and they detest the one who speaks with integrity.

**11** Therefore, because you trample on the poor[^12] and take from them a levy of grain, you have built houses of cut stone, but you will not live in them; you have planted pleasant vineyards, but you will not drink their wine.[^13]

**12** For I know how many your crimes are and how great your sins: you who harass the innocent, who take bribes,[^14] and who turn aside the needy in the gate.

**13** Therefore the prudent will keep silent at such a time, for it is an evil time.

**14** Seek good and not evil, so that you may live. Then Yahweh, the God of Armies,[^15] will be with you, as you say he is.

**15** Hate evil, love good, and establish justice in the gate. Perhaps Yahweh, the God of Armies, will be gracious to the remnant of Joseph.

**16** Therefore this is what Yahweh, the God of Armies, the Lord,[^16] says: There will be wailing in all the squares, and in all the streets they will say, "Alas! Alas!"[^17] They will call the farmer to mourning, and those skilled in lament[^18] to wailing.

**17** In all the vineyards there will be wailing, for I will pass through your midst,[^19] says Yahweh.

**18** Woe to[^20] you who long for the day of Yahweh![^21] Why would you want the day of Yahweh? It is darkness, and not light.

**19** It will be as when someone flees from a lion and a bear meets him, or goes into the house and leans his hand against the wall, and a snake bites him.

**20** Is not the day of Yahweh darkness, and not light, gloom with no brightness in it?

**21** I hate, I reject your festivals; I take no delight in your solemn assemblies.[^22]

**22** Even though you offer me burnt offerings and your grain offerings, I will not accept them; I will not look at the fellowship offerings[^23] of your fattened animals.

**23** Take away from me the noise of your songs; I will not listen to the music of your harps.

**24** But let justice roll on like waters, and righteousness like a stream that never runs dry.[^24]

**25** Did you bring me sacrifices and grain offerings in the wilderness for forty years, house of Israel?

**26** You will carry Sikkuth your king and Kiyyun your images, the star of your god,[^25] which you made for yourselves,

**27** and I will send you into exile beyond Damascus,[^26] says Yahweh, whose name is the God of Armies.

[^1]: Hebrew *qinah*, a funeral song. Verse 2 is in the rhythm of such a song, with a long line followed by a short one.
[^2]: Literally "the virgin of Israel", Israel pictured as a young woman who has died before her time.
[^3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD". *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: An ancient shrine in the far south of Judah (Genesis 21:33; 26:23–25). On Bethel and Gilgal, see 4:4.
[^5]: Two plays on words. *Ha-gilgal galoh yigleh*, "Gilgal will surely go into exile". *Bethel*, "house of God", will become *aven*, "nothing" or "wickedness". Hosea uses Beth-aven, "house of wickedness", as a mocking name for Bethel (Hosea 4:15).
[^6]: The northern kingdom, whose chief tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh, were descended from Joseph. Also in verse 15.
[^7]: A bitter plant. Compare 6:12.
[^8]: Hebrew *kimah* and *kesil*, two constellations, probably the Pleiades and Orion (Job 9:9; 38:31). This is the second of the three hymns in Amos (see 4:13).
[^9]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", sometimes understood as "the shadow of death" (Psalm 23:4).
[^10]: The Hebrew verb, found only here and in a few other places, is uncertain. Literally perhaps "makes destruction smile". Or "brings destruction suddenly".
[^11]: The open space by the city gate, where the elders heard cases and settled disputes. Also in verses 12 and 15.
[^12]: Hebrew *boshaskhem*, found only here. Perhaps "you levy taxes on the poor", which fits the next words.
[^13]: Compare Deuteronomy 28:30, 39; Zephaniah 1:13.
[^14]: Hebrew *kofer*, a payment that buys someone's release from the penalty they deserve (Numbers 35:31).
[^15]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, "Yahweh, the God of Armies". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Compare "Yahweh of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". Also in verses 15, 16 and 27.
[^16]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name Yahweh.
[^17]: Hebrew *ho ho*, a cry of grief, related to *hoy* (verse 18).
[^18]: Professional mourners, often women (Jeremiah 9:16, 9:17 in most English Bibles).
[^19]: As Yahweh passed through Egypt on the night of the Passover (Exodus 12:12).
[^20]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^21]: The day when Yahweh would act in power. The people expected it to bring victory and light for Israel.
[^22]: Hebrew *atsarah*, a festive gathering for worship. Literally "I will not smell your solemn assemblies", the pleasing smell of their offerings (Leviticus 26:31).
[^23]: Hebrew *shelem*, the sacrifice shared in a meal by the worshipers (Leviticus 3; 7:11–21).
[^24]: Hebrew *nachal eitan*, "a lasting stream". Most streams in the land flow only in the rainy season.
[^25]: *Sikkuth* and *Kiyyun* are probably names of Mesopotamian gods connected with the planet Saturn, known from Assyrian texts as Sakkut and Kaiwan. The Hebrew vowels make them sound like *shiqquts*, "detestable thing". Or "the shrine of your king and the stand of your images". The Septuagint reads "You took up the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Rephan, their images", and Acts 7:42–43 quotes verses 25–27 in that form. *You will carry* may also be translated "You carried".
[^26]: Acts 7:43 reads "beyond Babylon".
