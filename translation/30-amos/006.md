**1** Woe to[^1] those who are at ease in Zion, and to those who feel secure on the mountain of Samaria, the notable men of the first of the nations, to whom the house of Israel comes!

**2** Cross over to Calneh and look; go from there to Hamath the great; then go down to Gath of the Philistines.[^2] Are they better than these kingdoms? Is their territory larger than yours?

**3** You push away the day of disaster, yet you bring near the reign of violence.[^3]

**4** They lie on beds of ivory and sprawl on their couches. They eat lambs from the flock and calves from the stall.

**5** They improvise[^4] to the sound of the harp; like David, they invent musical instruments for themselves.

**6** They drink wine by the bowlful[^5] and anoint themselves with the finest oils, but they do not grieve over the ruin of Joseph.

**7** Therefore they will now go into exile at the head of the exiles, and the feasting[^6] of those who sprawl will come to an end.

**8** The Lord Yahweh[^7] has sworn by himself,[^8] declares Yahweh,[^9] the God of Armies:[^10] I loathe the pride of Jacob and hate his fortresses. I will hand over the city and everything in it.

**9** If ten men are left in one house, they will die.

**10** And when a relative, the one who burns the dead,[^11] picks up a body to carry the bones out of the house, he will say to someone in the back of the house, "Is anyone else with you?" He will say, "No one." Then he will say, "Hush! We must not mention the name of Yahweh."[^12]

**11** For look, Yahweh is giving the command, and he will strike the great house into fragments and the small house into splinters.

**12** Do horses run on rock? Does anyone plow there with oxen?[^13] Yet you have turned justice into poison[^14] and the fruit of righteousness into wormwood.

**13** You who rejoice over Lo-debar,[^15] who say, "Did we not take Karnaim for ourselves by our own strength?"

**14** For look, I am raising up a nation against you, house of Israel, declares Yahweh, the God of Armies, and they will oppress you from Lebo-hamath to the Wadi of the Arabah.[^16]

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*. See 5:18.
[^2]: Calneh and Hamath were cities in northern Syria, and Gath one of the five Philistine cities. All three had been conquered (Isaiah 10:9; 2 Kings 12:18, 12:17 in most English Bibles). The point may be that Israel is no safer than they were.
[^3]: Literally "the seat of violence", perhaps a court of injustice.
[^4]: Hebrew *paratim*, found only here. Perhaps "strum" or "sing idle songs".
[^5]: Literally "from bowls". The word is used for the large bowls in which the blood of sacrifices was collected (Exodus 27:3).
[^6]: Hebrew *marzeach*, a feast held by a group of wealthy men, sometimes in connection with mourning (Jeremiah 16:5). *Sprawl* is the word used in verse 4.
[^7]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD".
[^8]: Literally "by his *nephesh*", "by his life".
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Declares Yahweh* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verse 14.
[^10]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*. See 4:13. Also in verse 14.
[^11]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "his uncle and the one who burns him". Burning the dead was unusual in Israel and may point to a plague. Or "the one who burns spices for him" in honor of the dead (Jeremiah 34:5).
[^12]: To call on Yahweh, even in a blessing or a vow, might draw his attention and bring more death.
[^13]: Literally "Does one plow with the oxen?" Some scholars read "Does one plow the sea with oxen?", dividing the Hebrew letters differently.
[^14]: Hebrew *rosh*, a poisonous plant. On wormwood, see 5:7.
[^15]: Hebrew *lo davar*, which means "nothing". Lo-debar and Karnaim, "horns", a picture of strength, were towns in Gilead, east of the Jordan, which Israel had recently taken back from Aram (2 Samuel 9:4; 2 Kings 14:25).
[^16]: Lebo-hamath, "the entrance to Hamath", was on the northern border of Israel, and the Wadi of the Arabah near the Dead Sea, in the south. These were the borders that Jeroboam II had restored (2 Kings 14:25).
