**1** This is what the Lord GOD[^1] showed me: he was forming a swarm of locusts[^2] when the late crop[^3] was beginning to come up. It was the late crop after the king's mowings.[^4]

**2** When they had finished eating the plants of the land, I said, "Lord GOD, forgive, please! How can Jacob survive?[^5] He is so small."

**3** The LORD relented[^6] concerning this. "It will not happen," said the LORD.

**4** This is what the Lord GOD showed me: the Lord GOD was calling for judgment by fire.[^7] It devoured the great deep[^8] and was devouring the land.[^9]

**5** Then I said, "Lord GOD, stop, please! How can Jacob survive? He is so small."

**6** The LORD relented concerning this. "This too will not happen," said the Lord GOD.

**7** This is what he showed me: the Lord was standing beside a wall built with a plumb line, with a plumb line in his hand.[^10]

**8** The LORD said to me, "What do you see, Amos?" I said, "A plumb line." Then the Lord said, "Look, I am setting a plumb line among my people Israel. I will not pass them by again.[^11]

**9** The high places[^12] of Isaac[^13] will be made desolate, and the sanctuaries of Israel will be laid waste. I will rise against the house of Jeroboam with the sword."

**10** Then Amaziah the priest of Bethel sent a message to Jeroboam king of Israel: "Amos has conspired against you in the very heart of the house of Israel. The land cannot bear all his words.

**11** For this is what Amos has said: 'Jeroboam will die by the sword, and Israel will surely go into exile away from its land.'"

**12** Amaziah said to Amos, "Seer,[^14] go! Run away to the land of Judah. Earn your living[^15] there, and prophesy there.

**13** But never prophesy at Bethel again, for it is the king's sanctuary and a royal temple."[^16]

**14** Amos answered Amaziah, "I am not a prophet, and I am not a prophet's son.[^17] I am a herdsman and a tender of sycamore figs.[^18]

**15** But the LORD took me from following the flock, and the LORD said to me, 'Go, prophesy to my people Israel.'

**16** So now hear the word of the LORD. You say, 'Do not prophesy against Israel, and do not preach[^19] against the house of Isaac.'

**17** Therefore this is what the LORD says: Your wife will become a prostitute in the city, and your sons and your daughters will fall by the sword. Your land will be divided up with a measuring line, and you yourself will die in an unclean land.[^20] And Israel will surely go into exile away from its land."

[^1]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Also in verses 2, 4, 5 and 6.
[^2]: Hebrew *govai*, a word for locusts not among the four in Joel 1:4 (compare Nahum 3:17).
[^3]: Hebrew *leqesh*, the later growth of grass and grain after the spring rains.
[^4]: Perhaps the first cutting of the grass, taken as a tax for the king's animals (1 Kings 18:5). After it, the late crop was all the people had.
[^5]: Literally "Who is Jacob, that he could stand?" Also in verse 5.
[^6]: Or "changed his mind". Also in verse 6.
[^7]: Literally "calling to contend by fire", as in a lawsuit. Some scholars read "calling for a shower of fire", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^8]: Hebrew *tehom rabbah*, the waters under the earth that fed the springs and rivers (Genesis 7:11).
[^9]: Literally "the portion", perhaps the land allotted to Israel.
[^10]: Hebrew *anakh*, found only in verses 7–8. The traditional meaning is "plumb line", a cord with a weight of lead, used to test whether a wall stands straight. The word may instead mean "tin", a metal used in making weapons: "standing on a wall of tin, with tin in his hand".
[^11]: That is, I will no longer pass over their sins and spare them. Also in 8:2.
[^12]: Hill shrines where sacrifices were offered.
[^13]: A poetic name for Israel, found only in Amos (also verse 16). Isaac was the father of Jacob.
[^14]: Hebrew *chozeh*, "one who sees visions", from the verb in 1:1.
[^15]: Literally "eat bread".
[^16]: Literally "a house of the kingdom".
[^17]: The Hebrew has no verb. Or "I was not a prophet …; I was a herdsman". A *prophet's son* is a member of a group of prophets (2 Kings 2:3, 5).
[^18]: The fruit of the sycamore-fig tree had to be slit or pinched to make it ripen. The tree does not grow at the height of Tekoa (1:1), so Amos may have worked in the lowlands.
[^19]: Literally "drip", as words flow from the mouth (Micah 2:6, 11).
[^20]: A foreign land, unclean because other gods are worshiped there.
