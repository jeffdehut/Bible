**1** I saw the Lord[^1] standing beside the altar,[^2] and he said: Strike the tops of the pillars so that the thresholds shake, and shatter them on the heads of all the people.[^3] Those who are left of them I will kill with the sword. Not one of them will get away; not one will escape.

**2** If they dig down into Sheol,[^4] from there my hand will take them. If they climb up to the heavens, from there I will bring them down.

**3** If they hide on the top of Carmel, from there I will search them out and take them. If they hide from my sight on the floor of the sea, from there I will command the serpent,[^5] and it will bite them.

**4** If they go into captivity before their enemies, from there I will command the sword, and it will kill them. I will set my eyes on them for harm and not for good.

**5** The Lord Yahweh of Armies[^6] touches the earth, and it melts, and all who live in it mourn. All of it rises like the Nile and sinks again like the river of Egypt.

**6** He builds his upper rooms[^7] in the heavens and sets the foundations of his vault[^8] on the earth. He calls for the waters of the sea and pours them out on the face of the earth: Yahweh is his name.

**7** Are you not like the Cushites[^9] to me, people of Israel? declares Yahweh.[^10] Did I not bring Israel up from the land of Egypt, and the Philistines from Caphtor,[^11] and the Arameans from Kir?

**8** Look, the eyes of the Lord Yahweh are on the sinful kingdom, and I will destroy it from the face of the earth. Yet I will not completely destroy the house of Jacob, declares Yahweh.

**9** For look, I am giving the command, and I will shake the house of Israel among all the nations, as grain is shaken in a sieve, and not a pebble[^12] falls to the ground.

**10** All the sinners among my people will die by the sword, those who say, "Disaster will not come near us or overtake us."

**11** On that day I will raise up the fallen shelter[^13] of David. I will repair its broken walls and raise up its ruins, and I will rebuild it as it was in days of old,

**12** so that they may take possession of what is left of Edom and of all the nations that are called by my name,[^14] declares Yahweh, who will do this.[^15]

**13** Look, the days are coming, declares Yahweh, when the one who plows will overtake the one who reaps, and the one who treads grapes the one who sows the seed.[^16] The mountains will drip sweet wine, and all the hills will flow with it.[^17]

**14** I will restore the fortunes[^18] of my people Israel. They will rebuild the ruined cities and live in them; they will plant vineyards and drink their wine; they will make gardens and eat their fruit.

**15** I will plant them on their land, and they will never again be uprooted from the land that I have given them, says Yahweh your God.

[^1]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name Yahweh.
[^2]: Probably the altar at Bethel (3:14).
[^3]: Literally "shatter them on the head of all of them". The meaning is uncertain.
[^4]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead. Compare Psalm 139:7–10.
[^5]: The monster of the sea, as in Isaiah 27:1.
[^6]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH ha-tseva'ot*, "the Lord Yahweh of the Armies". *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Verses 5–6 are the third of the three hymns in Amos (see 4:13).
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "his upper room" but read "his upper rooms". Or "his stairs".
[^8]: Hebrew *aguddah*, "a band, a bundle", here probably the dome of the sky, pictured as resting on the earth.
[^9]: The people of Cush, the land south of Egypt on the upper Nile, at the far edge of the world Israel knew.
[^10]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verses 8, 12 and 13.
[^11]: Probably Crete, from where the Philistines had come (Deuteronomy 2:23; Jeremiah 47:4). On Kir, see 1:5.
[^12]: Hebrew *tseror*, "pebble", or perhaps "kernel". A sieve lets the grain fall through and keeps back the stones: no sinner will escape. If the word means "kernel", the point is that not one grain will be lost.
[^13]: Hebrew *sukkah*, a hut or booth. The royal house of David is pictured as a shelter that has fallen down. *Its broken walls* is literally "their breaches", perhaps of the two kingdoms, Israel and Judah.
[^14]: Literally "over whom my name is called", that is, which belong to me.
[^15]: Acts 15:16–17 quotes verses 11–12 from the Greek Septuagint, which reads in verse 12 "so that the rest of humankind, and all the nations over whom my name is called, may seek", where Acts adds "the Lord". The Septuagint follows a Hebrew text that read *adam*, "humankind", for *edom*, "Edom", and *yidreshu*, "they will seek", for *yirshu*, "they will take possession". The quotation in Acts also begins "After this I will return".
[^16]: The harvest will be so large that it lasts until the time comes to plow and sow again (Leviticus 26:5).
[^17]: Literally "will melt". Compare Joel 4:18 (3:18 in most English Bibles).
[^18]: Literally "turn the turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back the captives".
