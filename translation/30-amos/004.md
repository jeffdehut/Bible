**1** Hear this word, you cows of Bashan[^1] on the mountain of Samaria, who oppress the poor, who crush the needy, who say to your husbands,[^2] "Bring us something to drink!"

**2** The Lord Yahweh[^3] has sworn by his holiness: Look, the days are coming on you when you will be carried away with hooks, and the last of you with fishhooks.[^4]

**3** You will go out through the breaches in the wall, each woman straight ahead, and you will be thrown out toward Harmon,[^5] declares Yahweh.[^6]

**4** Come to Bethel and rebel;[^7] go to Gilgal[^8] and rebel all the more! Bring your sacrifices every morning, your tithes every third day.[^9]

**5** Burn a thanksgiving offering of bread made with yeast,[^10] and proclaim your freewill offerings; announce them! For that is what you love to do, people of Israel, declares the Lord Yahweh.

**6** I gave you clean teeth[^11] in all your cities and a lack of bread in all your places, yet you did not return to me, declares Yahweh.

**7** I also held back the rain from you when there were still three months to the harvest.[^12] I sent rain on one city and sent no rain on another city. One field had rain, and the field that had no rain dried up.

**8** Two or three cities staggered to one city to drink water, and could not drink their fill, yet you did not return to me, declares Yahweh.

**9** I struck you with blight and mildew.[^13] Your many gardens and vineyards,[^14] your fig trees and your olive trees, the cutting locust[^15] devoured, yet you did not return to me, declares Yahweh.

**10** I sent a plague among you as I did in Egypt.[^16] I killed your young men with the sword, and your horses were captured.[^17] I made the stench of your camps rise into your nostrils, yet you did not return to me, declares Yahweh.

**11** I overthrew some of you, as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah,[^18] and you were like a burning stick snatched from the fire, yet you did not return to me, declares Yahweh.

**12** Therefore this is what I will do to you, Israel. Because I will do this to you,[^19] prepare to meet your God, Israel!

**13** For look, he who forms the mountains and creates the wind,[^20] who tells human beings what his thoughts are,[^21] who turns the dawn into darkness,[^22] and treads on the heights of the earth: Yahweh, the God of Armies,[^23] is his name.

[^1]: A fertile highland east of the Sea of Galilee, famous for its well-fed cattle (Deuteronomy 32:14; Psalm 22:13, 22:12 in most English Bibles). Here the phrase describes the wealthy women of Samaria.
[^2]: Literally "their lords".
[^3]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD". *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *The Lord Yahweh* is also in verse 5.
[^4]: The meaning of both words for hooks is uncertain. Or "in baskets … in fishing pots". *The last of you* may also mean "your descendants".
[^5]: The place is unknown. Perhaps Mount Hermon, in the north, is meant. The verb is active, literally "you will throw", perhaps "you will throw yourselves".
[^6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verses 5–11.
[^7]: The verb is related to *pesha*, "crime, rebellion" (1:3). The invitation is ironic: their worship at the shrines is itself rebellion.
[^8]: An ancient shrine near Jericho (Joshua 4:19–20; 1 Samuel 11:14–15; Hosea 4:15). Bethel, also a shrine, is about 11 miles (18 km) north of Jerusalem.
[^9]: Literally "for three days". Or "on the third day", perhaps of a pilgrimage. The law required tithes once a year, and a special tithe every third year (Deuteronomy 14:22–29).
[^10]: Bread made with yeast was eaten with a thanksgiving offering, but no yeast was to be burned on the altar (Leviticus 2:11; 7:13).
[^11]: That is, nothing to eat.
[^12]: The late rains of winter and early spring, which the grain needed before the harvest.
[^13]: Blight, scorching by the hot east wind, and mildew, a disease that turns grain yellow, are named together in Deuteronomy 28:22 and 1 Kings 8:37.
[^14]: Literally "the multitude of your gardens and your vineyards". Some scholars read "I laid waste your gardens and your vineyards", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^15]: Hebrew *gazam*, one of the four words for locusts in Joel 1:4. They may be different kinds of locust or stages in the insect's growth.
[^16]: Literally "in the way of Egypt", that is, like the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 9:3; Deuteronomy 28:27, 60). Or "on the road to Egypt".
[^17]: Literally "with the captivity of your horses".
[^18]: Genesis 19:24–25. The verb *overthrew* is the word used of their destruction.
[^19]: What Yahweh will do is not stated.
[^20]: Or "spirit". Hebrew *ruach* can mean wind, breath or spirit.
[^21]: Or "their thoughts": the Hebrew may mean God's thoughts or human thoughts. The meaning of the word, *seach*, found only here, is uncertain.
[^22]: Or "who makes the dawn and the darkness". This verse is the first of three hymns in Amos that praise Yahweh as Creator and end with his name (5:8–9; 9:5–6).
[^23]: Hebrew *YHWH Elohei tseva'ot*, "Yahweh, the God of Armies". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Compare "Yahweh of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty".
