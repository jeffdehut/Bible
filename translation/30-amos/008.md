**1** This is what the Lord Yahweh[^1] showed me: a basket of summer fruit.[^2]

**2** He said, "What do you see, Amos?" I said, "A basket of summer fruit." Then Yahweh said to me, "The end[^3] has come for my people Israel. I will not pass them by again.[^4]

**3** On that day the songs of the temple[^5] will become wailing, declares the Lord Yahweh.[^6] So many corpses, thrown out everywhere! Hush!"[^7]

**4** Hear this, you who trample[^8] the needy and bring the poor of the land[^9] to an end,

**5** who say, "When will the new moon be over, so that we can sell grain, and the Sabbath, so that we can open the stores of wheat?[^10] We will make the ephah small and the shekel heavy[^11] and cheat with dishonest scales,

**6** buying the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of sandals,[^12] and selling even the sweepings of the wheat."

**7** Yahweh has sworn by the Pride of Jacob:[^13] I will never forget anything they have done.

**8** Will not the land tremble because of this, and everyone who lives in it mourn? All of it will rise like the Nile,[^14] be tossed about, and sink again like the river of Egypt.

**9** On that day, declares the Lord Yahweh, I will make the sun go down at noon and darken the earth in broad daylight.[^15]

**10** I will turn your festivals into mourning and all your songs into a lament. I will put sackcloth on every waist and shave every head bald.[^16] I will make it like the mourning for an only son, and its end like a bitter day.

**11** Look, the days are coming, declares the Lord Yahweh, when I will send a famine on the land: not a famine of bread or a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of Yahweh.

**12** People will stagger from sea to sea[^17] and wander from north to east, running back and forth to seek the word of Yahweh, but they will not find it.

**13** On that day the beautiful young women and the young men will faint from thirst.

**14** Those who swear by the guilt of Samaria,[^18] who say, "As your god lives, Dan," and "As the way to Beersheba lives,"[^19] will fall and never rise again.

[^1]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD". Also in verses 3, 9 and 11.
[^2]: Hebrew *kelub qayits*. *Qayits* is the fruit of late summer, chiefly figs, gathered at the end of the farming year.
[^3]: Hebrew *qets*, "end". The vision rests on a play on words: *qayits*, "summer fruit", sounds like *qets*, "end". The ripe fruit at the end of the year points to Israel's end.
[^4]: See 7:8.
[^5]: Or "palace". Some scholars read "the singing women of the palace", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^6]: Hebrew *ne'um Adonai YHWH*, "the declaration of the Lord Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verses 9 and 11.
[^7]: The Hebrew is abrupt. Literally "Many the corpse; in every place he has thrown; hush!" Compare 6:10.
[^8]: See 2:7.
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "the humble of the land" but read "the poor of the land".
[^10]: Trading stopped on the new moon and the Sabbath (2 Kings 4:23; Nehemiah 10:32, 10:31 in most English Bibles). *Open the stores of wheat*: literally "open wheat".
[^11]: The ephah was the measure in which grain was sold, probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel). The shekel was the weight, about 2/5 ounce (11.5 grams), used to weigh the buyer's silver. A small measure and a heavy weight cheated the buyer twice.
[^12]: Compare 2:6.
[^13]: Perhaps a title of God, like "the Glory of Israel" (1 Samuel 15:29). Or Yahweh swears by the arrogance of Jacob (6:8), which is as sure not to change as his own oath.
[^14]: Hebrew *ka'or*, "like the light", probably a short spelling of *kay'or*, "like the Nile", as in 9:5. The Nile rose in flood every year and fell again.
[^15]: Perhaps an eclipse of the sun. A total eclipse was visible in the region on 15 June 763 BC.
[^16]: Literally "I will bring up sackcloth on all waists and baldness on every head". Wearing sackcloth and shaving the head were signs of mourning (Isaiah 15:2–3; Micah 1:16).
[^17]: From the Dead Sea in the east to the Mediterranean in the west, that is, the length and breadth of the land.
[^18]: Perhaps a mocking name for a goddess worshiped in Samaria, Ashima (2 Kings 17:30), or for the calf of Bethel (Hosea 8:5–6).
[^19]: Dan, in the far north, and Beersheba, in the far south, had shrines (1 Kings 12:29; Amos 5:5). *The way to Beersheba* may be the pilgrimage to its shrine. Some scholars read "As your god lives, Beersheba", with a small change to the Hebrew.
