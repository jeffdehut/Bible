**1** This is what Yahweh[^1] says: For three crimes of Moab, and for four, I will not turn it back,[^2] because he burned the bones of the king of Edom to lime.[^3]

**2** So I will send fire on Moab, and it will devour the fortresses of Kerioth.[^4] Moab will die amid the uproar, with a battle cry and the sound of the ram's horn.[^5]

**3** I will cut off the ruler[^6] from within her and kill all her officials with him, says Yahweh.

**4** This is what Yahweh says: For three crimes of Judah, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they rejected the instruction[^7] of Yahweh and did not keep his decrees. Their lies,[^8] which their ancestors followed, have led them astray.

**5** So I will send fire on Judah, and it will devour the fortresses of Jerusalem.

**6** This is what Yahweh says: For three crimes of Israel, and for four, I will not turn it back, because they sell the innocent[^9] for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals.[^10]

**7** They trample the heads of the poor into the dust of the ground[^11] and push the humble out of the way.[^12] A man and his father go to the same young woman,[^13] and so they profane my holy name.

**8** They stretch out beside every altar on garments taken in pledge,[^14] and in the house of their God[^15] they drink wine taken from those they have fined.

**9** Yet it was I who destroyed the Amorite[^16] before them, though he was as tall as the cedars and as strong as the oaks. I destroyed his fruit above and his roots below.

**10** It was I who brought you up from the land of Egypt and led you forty years in the wilderness, to take possession of the land of the Amorite.

**11** I raised up some of your sons to be prophets, and some of your young men to be Nazirites.[^17] Is it not so, people of Israel? declares Yahweh.[^18]

**12** But you made the Nazirites drink wine, and you commanded the prophets, "Do not prophesy."

**13** Look, I am going to crush you down where you stand,[^19] as a cart crushes when it is full of sheaves.

**14** The swift will have nowhere to flee;[^20] the strong will not hold on to his strength, and the warrior will not save his life.

**15** The one who handles the bow will not stand his ground, the swift of foot will not escape, and the one who rides the horse will not save his life.

**16** Even the bravest of the warriors[^21] will flee naked on that day, declares Yahweh.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: See 1:3. Also in verses 4 and 6.
[^3]: Burning the bones of the dead was an outrage against them (compare 2 Kings 23:16).
[^4]: A city of Moab (Jeremiah 48:24). Or "the fortresses of its cities".
[^5]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^6]: Hebrew *shofet*, "judge", here the one who governs.
[^7]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law".
[^8]: Probably false gods.
[^9]: Hebrew *tsaddiq*, "righteous", here one who is in the right, perhaps in a lawsuit.
[^10]: Perhaps for a debt as small as the price of sandals, or for a bribe. Compare 8:6.
[^11]: Literally "who pant for the dust of the earth on the head of the poor". The verb *sha'af* means "pant, long for", but here it may be a form of *shuf*, "trample". If it means "long for", they covet even the dust that the poor throw on their heads in grief.
[^12]: Literally "turn aside the way of the humble", that is, deny them justice.
[^13]: Hebrew *na'arah*, a girl or young woman, perhaps a servant in the household. Compare Leviticus 18:8, 15.
[^14]: The law required a cloak taken as a pledge for a debt to be given back before night (Exodus 22:25–26, 22:26–27 in most English Bibles; Deuteronomy 24:12–13).
[^15]: Or "their gods".
[^16]: Here the peoples who lived in Canaan before Israel (Genesis 15:16; Numbers 13:32–33).
[^17]: People dedicated to God by a vow, who drank no wine (Numbers 6:1–21).
[^18]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verse 16.
[^19]: Literally "I am pressing beneath you". The Hebrew verb *'uq* is found only here, and its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "press down", or "split open", as the ground splits under a heavy cart.
[^20]: Literally "flight will perish from the swift".
[^21]: Literally "the stout of heart among the warriors".
