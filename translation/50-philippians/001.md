**1** Paul and Timothy, servants[^1] of Christ Jesus,[^2] to all God's holy people[^3] in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, together with the overseers[^4] and deacons:[^5]

**2** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3** I thank my God every time I remember you,[^6]

**4** always in every prayer of mine for all of you, praying with joy,

**5** because of your partnership[^7] in the good news from the first day until now.

**6** I am confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.

**7** It is right for me to think this way about all of you, because I have you in my heart,[^8] since both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the good news you all share with me in grace.

**8** For God is my witness how I long for all of you with the affection[^9] of Christ Jesus.

**9** And this is my prayer: that your love may overflow more and more in knowledge and all insight,

**10** so that you may approve what is excellent,[^10] and may be pure and blameless for the day of Christ,

**11** filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

**12** I want you to know, brothers and sisters,[^11] that what has happened to me has actually served to advance the good news,

**13** so that it has become clear throughout the whole praetorian guard[^12] and to everyone else that I am in chains for Christ.[^13]

**14** And most of the brothers and sisters, made confident in the Lord by my chains,[^14] are much more bold to speak the word[^15] without fear.

**15** Some, it is true, proclaim Christ even from envy and rivalry, but others from goodwill.

**16** These do it out of love, knowing that I am put here for the defense of the good news.[^16]

**17** The others proclaim Christ out of selfish ambition,[^17] not sincerely, thinking they will stir up trouble for me in my chains.

**18** What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in this I rejoice. Yes, and I will rejoice,

**19** for I know that through your prayers and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance,[^18]

**20** according to my eager expectation and hope that I will not be put to shame in anything, but that with all boldness, now as always, Christ will be exalted in my body, whether by life or by death.

**21** For to me, to live is Christ and to die is gain.

**22** But if I am to live in the flesh,[^19] that means fruitful work for me. Yet which I will choose I cannot say.[^20]

**23** I am torn between the two. My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better,

**24** but to remain in the flesh is more necessary for your sake.

**25** Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with all of you for your progress and joy in the faith,

**26** so that your boasting in Christ Jesus may overflow because of me, through my coming[^21] to you again.

**27** Only, live as citizens[^22] worthy of the good news of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear about you that you are standing firm in one spirit, with one mind[^23] striving side by side for the faith of the good news,

**28** and that you are not frightened in any way by your opponents. (This is a sign to them of destruction, but of your salvation, and that from God.

**29** For it has been granted to you, for the sake of Christ, not only to believe in him but also to suffer for him.)

**30** You are engaged in the same struggle that you saw I had and now hear that I have.

[^1]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^2]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^3]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^4]: Greek *episkopoi*, "overseers", traditionally "bishops".
[^5]: Greek *diakonoi*, "servants", here used of people holding a recognized role in the church; traditionally "deacons".
[^6]: Or "for all your remembrance of me". Literally "at all the remembrance of you".
[^7]: Greek *koinonia*, "sharing, partnership, fellowship".
[^8]: Or "because you have me in your heart".
[^9]: Literally "with the inward parts", the seat of the deepest feelings.
[^10]: Or "discern what matters". Literally "test the things that differ".
[^11]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 14.
[^12]: Greek *praitorion*, the emperor's guard, or the headquarters of a governor.
[^13]: Literally "that my chains have become evident in Christ".
[^14]: Or "most of the brothers and sisters in the Lord, made confident by my chains".
[^15]: Some manuscripts read "the word of God".
[^16]: Many manuscripts have verses 16 and 17 in the reverse order.
[^17]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^18]: Or "salvation". The words "this will turn out for my deliverance" are the same as Job 13:16 in the Septuagint.
[^19]: That is, in the body; also in verse 24.
[^20]: The Greek is abrupt; literally "But if to live in the flesh, this for me is fruit of work; and what I will choose I do not make known."
[^21]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival".
[^22]: Greek *politeuomai*, "to live as a citizen". Philippi was a Roman colony, and many of its people were Roman citizens.
[^23]: Literally "with one soul". *In one spirit*: or "in one Spirit".
