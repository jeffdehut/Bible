**1** So if there is any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love, any sharing in the Spirit,[^1] any affection and compassion,

**2** make my joy complete by being of the same mind, having the same love, being one in spirit[^2] and of one mind.

**3** Do nothing out of selfish ambition[^3] or conceit,[^4] but in humility regard others as more important than yourselves,

**4** each of you looking not only to your own interests but also to the interests of others.[^5]

**5** Have this attitude among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus:[^6]

**6** who, being in the form[^7] of God,[^8] did not regard equality with God as something to be grasped,[^9]

**7** but emptied himself,[^10] taking the form of a servant,[^11] being made in human likeness. And being found in appearance as a human being,

**8** he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross.

**9** Therefore God also highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name,

**10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven and on earth and under the earth,

**11** and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord,[^12] to the glory of God the Father.

**12** So then, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed, not only as in my presence[^13] but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,

**13** for it is God who is at work in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.[^14]

**14** Do everything without grumbling or arguing,

**15** so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without fault in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation,[^15] among whom you shine like lights[^16] in the world,

**16** holding fast[^17] the word of life, so that I may have reason to boast on the day of Christ that I did not run in vain or labor in vain.

**17** But even if I am being poured out like a drink offering on the sacrifice and service[^18] of your faith, I rejoice, and I rejoice with all of you.

**18** In the same way you too should rejoice, and rejoice with me.

**19** I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be cheered when I learn how you are.

**20** For I have no one else like him,[^19] who will genuinely care about your concerns.

**21** For they all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.

**22** But you know his proven worth, how as a child with a father he has served[^20] with me in the good news.

**23** So I hope to send him as soon as I see how things go with me,

**24** and I am confident in the Lord that I myself will also come soon.

**25** But I have thought it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier, and your messenger[^21] and minister[^22] to my need,

**26** for he has been longing for all of you,[^23] and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill.

**27** Indeed he was ill, close to death. But God had mercy on him, and not only on him but on me also, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.

**28** So I am sending[^24] him all the more eagerly, so that when you see him again you may rejoice, and I may be less anxious.

**29** So welcome him in the Lord with all joy, and hold people like him in honor,

**30** because he came close to death for the work of Christ,[^25] risking his life to make up for what was lacking in your service to me.[^26]

[^1]: Greek *koinonia*, "sharing, fellowship". Or "fellowship with the Spirit", or "any common spirit".
[^2]: Greek *sympsychoi*, "one in soul".
[^3]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^4]: Literally "empty glory".
[^5]: Literally "not looking each to your own things, but also each to the things of others".
[^6]: The second half of the verse has no verb. Or "which you also have in Christ Jesus", that is, the attitude that belongs to those who are in Christ. Many manuscripts read "For let this attitude be in you which was also in Christ Jesus".
[^7]: Greek *morphe*, "form", the outward shape in which something appears; also in verse 7 ("the form of a servant"). How far the word also implies the nature of what appears is debated.
[^8]: Or "though he was in the form of God", or "because he was in the form of God". The Greek participle allows either.
[^9]: Greek *harpagmos*, a rare word, from *harpazo*, "to seize". It may mean something to be seized, which he did not yet have, or something already his, to be held on to or used for his own advantage.
[^10]: Greek *ekenosen*, from *kenoo*, "to empty", also "to make of no account". The verse does not say of what he emptied himself; the phrases that follow describe what it involved.
[^11]: Or "a slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^12]: Verses 10–11 echo Isaiah 45:23, where Yahweh, God's personal name, says, "To me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear allegiance." The Septuagint has "every tongue will confess to God". *Lord*: Greek *kyrios*, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the name Yahweh.
[^13]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", as in 1:26.
[^14]: Literally "for the good pleasure".
[^15]: Compare Deuteronomy 32:5.
[^16]: Or "stars". Compare Daniel 12:3.
[^17]: Or "holding out", that is, offering.
[^18]: Greek *leitourgia*, a public or priestly service; compare *leitourgos*, "minister", in verse 25, and verse 30.
[^19]: Greek *isopsychos*, "of equal soul, like-minded".
[^20]: Greek *douleuo*, "serve as a slave".
[^21]: Greek *apostolos*, "one who is sent", the word translated "apostle" elsewhere.
[^22]: Greek *leitourgos*, one who performs a public or priestly service.
[^23]: Some manuscripts read "longing to see all of you".
[^24]: Literally "I sent", written from the point of view of the readers, who will receive the letter when Epaphroditus arrives.
[^25]: Some manuscripts read "the work of the Lord".
[^26]: Literally "to fill up your lack of service toward me". Greek *leitourgia*, as in verse 17.
