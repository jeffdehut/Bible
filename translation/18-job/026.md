**1** Then Job answered:

**2** "How you have helped the powerless![^1] How you have saved the arm that has no strength!

**3** How you have counseled one who has no wisdom, and what a wealth of sound wisdom[^2] you have made known!

**4** With whose help have you uttered these words?[^3] Whose breath[^4] has come out of you?

**5** The shades of the dead[^5] writhe beneath the waters, and so do those who live in them.

**6** Sheol[^6] is naked before him, and Abaddon[^7] has no covering.

**7** He stretches out the north over empty space[^8] and hangs the earth on nothing.

**8** He wraps up the waters in his clouds, and the cloud does not burst under them.

**9** He covers the face of his throne,[^9] spreading his cloud over it.

**10** He has marked out a circle on the face of the waters,[^10] at the boundary between light and darkness.

**11** The pillars of heaven tremble and are astounded at his rebuke.

**12** By his power he stilled[^11] the sea; by his understanding he shattered Rahab.[^12]

**13** By his breath[^13] the heavens became clear; his hand pierced the fleeing serpent.[^14]

**14** Look, these are only the edges of his ways;[^15] how faint is the whisper we hear of him![^16] But the thunder of his mighty deeds,[^17] who can understand?"

[^1]: Job is being sarcastic. In verses 2–4, *you* is singular: Job speaks to Bildad.
[^2]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, "sound wisdom", as in 5:12.
[^3]: Literally "Whom have you told words?": either "To whom" or "With whose help".
[^4]: Or "Whose spirit". Hebrew *neshamah*.
[^5]: Hebrew *repha'im*, the dead in the world below (Psalm 88:11 (88:10 in most English Bibles); Isaiah 14:9). Some interpreters think verses 5–14 belong to Bildad's speech in chapter 25 (see the note at 25:1).
[^6]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^7]: Hebrew *Abaddon*, "Destruction", a name for the realm of the dead (28:22; 31:12; Proverbs 15:11).
[^8]: Hebrew *tohu*, "emptiness, formlessness", the word in Genesis 1:2. *The north* (Hebrew *tsafon*) may be the northern sky, or the mountain of the gods in the far north (compare Isaiah 14:13).
[^9]: Hebrew *kisseh*, a spelling of *kisse*, "throne". The same consonants can be read *keseh*, "full moon": "He covers the face of the full moon".
[^10]: Literally "He has circled a limit on the face of the waters". The horizon is pictured as a circle drawn on the waters at the edge of the world (compare Proverbs 8:27).
[^11]: Or "he stirred up". The Hebrew verb *raga* can mean either.
[^12]: A sea monster, a figure of chaos (see 9:13).
[^13]: Or "By his wind". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^14]: Or "the gliding serpent". Isaiah 27:1 uses the same words of Leviathan.
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "his way" but read "his ways".
[^16]: Literally "how small a whisper of a word is heard of him".
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "his might" but read "his mighty deeds".
