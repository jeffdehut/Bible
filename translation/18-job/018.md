**1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered:

**2** "How long will you hunt for words?[^1] Show some sense, and then we can talk.

**3** Why are we counted as cattle, considered stupid[^2] in your sight?

**4** You who tear yourself apart in your anger, is the earth to be abandoned for your sake, or the rock moved from its place?

**5** Yes, the light of the wicked is put out, and the flame of his fire does not shine.

**6** The light grows dark in his tent, and his lamp above him is put out.

**7** His vigorous stride is shortened, and his own schemes bring him down.

**8** For his own feet throw him into a net, and he walks onto its mesh.

**9** A trap seizes him by the heel; a snare holds him fast.

**10** A noose is hidden for him on the ground, a trap for him on the path.

**11** Terrors frighten him on every side and chase him at every step.

**12** His strength is starved,[^3] and disaster is ready at his side.

**13** It eats away the parts of his skin; the firstborn of Death[^4] devours his limbs.

**14** He is torn from the tent he trusted in and marched off to the king of terrors.[^5]

**15** What is not his lives in his tent;[^6] sulfur is scattered over his home.

**16** His roots dry up beneath, and his branches wither above.

**17** The memory of him perishes from the earth, and he has no name in the land.[^7]

**18** He is driven out of light into darkness and chased out of the world.

**19** He has no offspring or descendant among his people, and no survivor where he used to live.

**20** Those in the west are appalled at his fate, and those in the east are seized with horror.[^8]

**21** Surely such are the dwellings of the unjust, and this is the place of one who does not know God."

[^1]: Hebrew *kintse*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "snares": literally "How long will you set snares for words?" Or "How long before you put an end to words?" The Hebrew "you" is plural here and in verse 3.
[^2]: Or "unclean".
[^3]: Or "Calamity is hungry for him". Hebrew *on*, "strength", may be read *awen*, "trouble".
[^4]: Probably a deadly disease. Death (Hebrew *mavet*) may be pictured as a person, as in Canaanite myth, where Mot (Death) is a god.
[^5]: Death, pictured as a king.
[^6]: Literally "from without to him". The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Or "Nothing of his remains in his tent".
[^7]: Literally "on the face of the outside", perhaps "in the streets" or "in the open country".
[^8]: Or "Later generations are appalled at his day, and earlier ones are seized with horror". Literally "those after" and "those before".
