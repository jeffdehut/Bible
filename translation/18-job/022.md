**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered:

**2** "Can a man be of use to God? Even a wise man is of use only to himself.

**3** Is it any pleasure to the Almighty[^1] if you are righteous? Is it any gain to him if you make your ways blameless?

**4** Is it because of your fear of God[^2] that he rebukes you and enters into judgment with you?

**5** Is not your wickedness great? Are not your iniquities endless?

**6** For you took pledges from your brothers for no reason[^3] and stripped the naked of their clothing.

**7** You gave no water to the weary to drink, and you withheld bread from the hungry,

**8** while the man with power owned the land, and the man of rank lived in it.[^4]

**9** You sent widows away empty-handed, and the arms of orphans were crushed.

**10** That is why snares are all around you, and sudden dread terrifies you,

**11** or darkness, so that you cannot see, and a flood of water covers you.

**12** Is not God[^5] in the height of heaven? See the highest stars, how lofty they are!

**13** Yet you say, 'What does God know? Can he judge through thick darkness?

**14** Thick clouds veil him, so that he does not see, as he walks on the vault of heaven.'

**15** Will you keep to the ancient path that wicked men have walked,

**16** who were snatched away before their time, whose foundations were washed away by a river?[^6]

**17** They said to God, 'Leave us alone!' and 'What can the Almighty do to us?'[^7]

**18** Yet it was he who filled their houses with good things. But the counsel of the wicked is far from me.[^8]

**19** The righteous see it and are glad; the innocent mock them:

**20** 'Surely our adversaries[^9] are cut off, and fire has consumed what they left.'

**21** Come to terms with him,[^10] and be at peace; in this way good will come to you.

**22** Accept instruction from his mouth, and lay up his words in your heart.

**23** If you return to the Almighty, you will be restored.[^11] If you remove injustice far from your tents,

**24** if you lay your gold[^12] in the dust, the gold of Ophir[^13] among the stones of the wadis,

**25** then the Almighty will be your gold and your choicest silver.[^14]

**26** For then you will delight in the Almighty and lift up your face to God.

**27** You will pray to him, and he will hear you, and you will fulfill your vows.

**28** You will decide on a matter, and it will be done for you, and light will shine on your ways.

**29** When people are brought low, and you say, 'Lift them up!' he will save the downcast.[^15]

**30** He will rescue even someone who is not innocent;[^16] he will be rescued through the cleanness of your hands."

[^1]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. Also in verses 17, 23, 25 and 26.
[^2]: Literally "your fear".
[^3]: Hebrew *chinnam*, the word in 1:9 and 2:3.
[^4]: Literally "the man of arm, the land is his, and the lifted of face lives in it". Eliphaz may mean Job himself.
[^5]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 26.
[^6]: Literally "a river was poured out on their foundation". Some see here an allusion to the flood (Genesis 6–8).
[^7]: Literally "to them".
[^8]: Eliphaz repeats Job's words in 21:16.
[^9]: Hebrew *qimanu*, a word found only here, probably "those who rise against us".
[^10]: Hebrew *hasken*, the verb translated "be of use" in verse 2.
[^11]: Literally "you will be built up".
[^12]: Hebrew *betser*, "precious ore". Also in verse 25.
[^13]: A land famous for its fine gold, probably in southern Arabia or East Africa.
[^14]: Literally "silver of heaps (or peaks)". Hebrew *to'afot*, a word of uncertain meaning.
[^15]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "When they make low, and you say 'Lifting up!', and the lowly of eyes he will save".
[^16]: Hebrew *i-naqi*, "not innocent", a rare form. Some take it as "the innocent".
