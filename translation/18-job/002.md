**1** One day the sons of God[^1] came to present themselves before the LORD,[^2] and the Accuser[^3] also came among them to present himself before the LORD.

**2** The LORD said to the Accuser, "Where have you come from?" The Accuser answered the LORD, "From roaming the earth and walking about on it."

**3** The LORD said to the Accuser, "Have you considered my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth, a blameless and upright man who fears God and turns away from evil. He still holds firmly to his integrity, though you incited me against him to ruin him for no reason."[^4]

**4** The Accuser answered the LORD, "Skin for skin![^5] A man will give everything he has for his life.

**5** But stretch out your hand now and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will surely curse you to your face."[^6]

**6** The LORD said to the Accuser, "Very well, he is in your power. Only spare his life."

**7** So the Accuser went out from the presence of the LORD, and he struck Job with painful sores from the sole of his foot to the crown of his head.

**8** Job took a piece of broken pottery to scrape himself with as he sat among the ashes.

**9** His wife said to him, "Are you still holding firmly to your integrity? Curse God[^7] and die!"

**10** But he said to her, "You are talking like a foolish woman.[^8] Shall we accept the good from God, and not accept the bad?" In all this Job did not sin with his lips.

**11** When Job's three friends heard about all this trouble that had come on him, they came, each from his own place: Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite.[^9] They arranged to meet and come to show him sympathy[^10] and comfort him.

**12** When they looked up from a distance, they did not recognize him. They wept aloud, and each one tore his robe, and they threw dust into the air over their heads.[^11]

**13** Then they sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights. No one said a word to him, for they saw that his pain was very great.

[^1]: Literally "the sons of God": heavenly beings who serve in God's court (see 1:6). The Greek Septuagint reads "the angels of God".
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan".
[^4]: Literally "to swallow him up". *For no reason* is Hebrew *chinnam*, the word the Accuser used in 1:9, "for nothing".
[^5]: A proverb whose exact sense is uncertain, perhaps "one skin for another": a person will give up anything to save his own skin.
[^6]: Literally "if he will not bless you to your face". The Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse" (see 1:5 and 1:11).
[^7]: Literally "Bless God". The Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse" (see 1:5).
[^8]: Hebrew *nevalot*, "foolish women", in the sense of senseless and godless.
[^9]: *Teman* was a region of Edom (Genesis 36:11, 34). *Shuah* was a son of Abraham and Keturah (Genesis 25:2). Where *Naamah* was is not known.
[^10]: Literally "to shake the head for him", a gesture of grief and sympathy.
[^11]: Literally "they threw dust on their heads toward the sky", a sign of mourning.
