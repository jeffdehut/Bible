**1** "But now they laugh at me, those younger than I, whose fathers I would have refused to put with the dogs of my flock.

**2** What use to me was the strength of their hands? Their vigor[^1] had perished.

**3** Gaunt from want and hunger, they gnaw the dry ground,[^2] in the gloom of desolation and waste.[^3]

**4** They pluck saltwort[^4] among the bushes, and the root of the broom bush is their food.[^5]

**5** They are driven out from the community;[^6] people shout after them as after a thief.

**6** They have to live in the gullies of the wadis, in holes in the ground and among the rocks.

**7** Among the bushes they bray;[^7] under the nettles they huddle together.

**8** A senseless brood, a nameless brood,[^8] they were whipped out of the land.

**9** And now I have become their song;[^9] I have become a byword to them.

**10** They detest me; they keep their distance from me; they do not hesitate to spit in my face.

**11** Because he has loosened my cord[^10] and humbled me, they have thrown off all restraint before me.[^11]

**12** On my right the rabble[^12] rise up; they push my feet away[^13] and build their roads of ruin against me.[^14]

**13** They tear up my path; they profit from my disaster;[^15] no one holds them back.[^16]

**14** As through a wide breach they come; amid the crash[^17] they roll in.

**15** Terrors are turned against me; they chase away my dignity[^18] like the wind, and my safety has passed away like a cloud.

**16** And now my life is poured out within me;[^19] days of affliction have taken hold of me.

**17** At night my bones are pierced within me,[^20] and the pains that gnaw me take no rest.

**18** With great force my garment is disfigured; it binds me like the collar of my tunic.[^21]

**19** He has thrown me into the mud,[^22] and I have become like dust and ashes.[^23]

**20** I cry to you for help, but you do not answer me; I stand up, and you just look at me.[^24]

**21** You have turned cruel to me; with the might of your hand you attack me.[^25]

**22** You lift me up on the wind and make me ride it; you melt me away, and all my success.[^26]

**23** For I know you will bring me back to death,[^27] to the house appointed for all the living.

**24** Yet does not one in ruins stretch out a hand, and in his disaster cry for help?[^28]

**25** Did I not weep for the one whose life was hard?[^29] Was my soul not grieved for the needy?

**26** Yet when I hoped for good, evil came; when I waited for light, darkness came.

**27** My insides are in turmoil and never still;[^30] days of affliction confront me.

**28** I go about darkened, but not by the sun;[^31] I stand up in the assembly and cry for help.

**29** I have become a brother to jackals and a companion to ostriches.[^32]

**30** My skin has turned black and peels from me;[^33] my bones burn with fever.[^34]

**31** My lyre is turned to mourning, and my pipe to the sound of weeping.

[^1]: Hebrew *kelach*, a rare word, perhaps "full vigor" or "ripe old age", as in 5:26.
[^2]: Or "they flee to the dry ground". Hebrew *orqim*, a rare word.
[^3]: Literally "the yesterday (or "night") of ruin and devastation". The meaning of this line is uncertain.
[^4]: Hebrew *malluach*, a salty plant that grows in the desert.
[^5]: Literally "their bread". Broom roots are bitter and were mostly used as fuel (Psalm 120:4). The same consonants can be read "to warm themselves".
[^6]: Literally "from the midst". Hebrew *gev*, a rare word.
[^7]: Like hungry wild donkeys (compare 6:5).
[^8]: Literally "sons of a fool, also sons of no name".
[^9]: That is, a mocking song.
[^10]: *He* is God, though the Hebrew does not name him. *My cord* is perhaps my bowstring (compare 29:20) or my tent cord (compare 4:21). The Hebrew text is written "his cord" but read "my cord". With "his cord", the line may mean "each of them has loosened his cord (his restraint) and humbled me".
[^11]: Literally "they have let loose the bridle before me".
[^12]: Hebrew *pirchach*, a word found only here, perhaps "brood" or "rabble".
[^13]: Literally "they send away my feet". The meaning is uncertain.
[^14]: Like an army building siege ramps against a city (19:12).
[^15]: Literally "they help toward my disaster".
[^16]: Literally "there is no helper for them": perhaps "they need no one's help". The meaning is uncertain.
[^17]: Hebrew *sho'ah*, "ruin, storm", the word translated "desolation" in verse 3.
[^18]: Hebrew *nedivah*, "nobility, honor".
[^19]: Literally "my soul pours itself out upon me".
[^20]: Literally "The night pierces my bones from upon me".
[^21]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. *Is disfigured* is literally "disguises itself". Some scholars read "seizes my garment", with a small change to the Hebrew, with God as the subject: "With great force he seizes my garment; he grips me by the collar of my tunic".
[^22]: *He* is God.
[^23]: The same pair of words, "dust and ashes", is used in 42:6 (compare Genesis 18:27).
[^24]: Literally "and you consider me".
[^25]: Hebrew *satam*, "bear a grudge against, persecute", a verb close to *satan*, "accuser".
[^26]: The Hebrew text is written *teshuwwah*, "(in) the roar of the storm", but read *tushiyyah*, "success" or "sound wisdom", as in 5:12. With the written form: "you toss me about in the roar of the storm".
[^27]: Literally "you will return me to death".
[^28]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "Surely not into a heap of ruins does he stretch out a hand; if in his disaster, to them a cry for help". Or "Surely no one strikes a ruined man when he cries for help in his disaster".
[^29]: Literally "the one hard of day".
[^30]: Literally "My bowels boil and are not silent".
[^31]: Literally "I go about dark, without the sun" (Hebrew *chammah*, "heat, sun"): darkened, perhaps in mourning, but not by the sun. Some scholars read *nechamah*, "comfort", for *chammah*, with a small change to the Hebrew: "I go about mourning, without comfort".
[^32]: Hebrew *benot ya'anah*, perhaps "ostriches" or "eagle owls". Both jackals and these birds make mournful cries in desolate places (Micah 1:8).
[^33]: Literally "My skin is black from upon me".
[^34]: Literally "my bone burns from heat".
