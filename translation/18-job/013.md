**1** "Look, my eye has seen all this; my ear has heard and understood it.

**2** What you know, I also know; I am not inferior to you.

**3** But I would speak to the Almighty;[^1] I want to argue my case with God.

**4** But you smear over the truth with lies;[^2] you are all worthless physicians.

**5** If only you would keep completely silent! That would be wisdom on your part.

**6** Hear now my argument; listen to the pleadings of my lips.

**7** Will you speak what is false on God's behalf? Will you speak deceitfully for him?

**8** Will you take his side?[^3] Will you argue the case for God?

**9** Would it go well with you if he examined you? Can you deceive him as one deceives a mortal?

**10** He will surely rebuke you if you secretly take sides.

**11** Will not his majesty terrify you, and dread of him fall on you?

**12** Your memorable sayings are proverbs of ashes; your defenses are defenses of clay.

**13** Be silent before me, and let me speak, and let come on me what may.

**14** Why should I take my flesh in my teeth and put my life in my hands?[^4]

**15** Even if he kills me, I will hope in him;[^5] yet I will argue my ways to his face.

**16** This too will be my salvation, for no godless person would come before him.

**17** Listen closely to my words; let my declaration be in your ears.

**18** Look now, I have prepared my case; I know that I will be proved right.

**19** Who is there who will contend with me? For then I would be silent and die.[^6]

**20** Only do not do two things to me, and then I will not hide from your face:[^7]

**21** take your hand far away from me, and do not let dread of you terrify me.

**22** Then call, and I will answer; or let me speak, and you reply to me.

**23** How many are my iniquities and sins? Make me know my offense and my sin.

**24** Why do you hide your face and count me as your enemy?[^8]

**25** Will you frighten a windblown leaf? Will you chase dry straw?

**26** For you write bitter charges against me and make me inherit the sins of my youth.

**27** You put my feet in the stocks and watch all my paths; you mark a line around the soles of my feet.[^9]

**28** And so he[^10] wastes away like something rotten, like a garment eaten by moths.

[^1]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^2]: Literally "you are plasterers of lies".
[^3]: Literally "lift up his face", a phrase for showing partiality in court. Also in verse 10.
[^4]: Both lines mean "risk my life". Or "Whatever happens, I will take my flesh in my teeth".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "Look, he will kill me; I have no hope" (*lo*, "not") but read "I will hope in him" (*lo*, "for him"). The verb *yachal* means "hope" or "wait". Traditionally "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him".
[^6]: That is, if anyone could prove him wrong, he would fall silent and die.
[^7]: Job speaks to God from here to the end of chapter 14.
[^8]: In Hebrew, *oyev*, "enemy", sounds like *Iyyov*, "Job".
[^9]: Literally "you engrave yourself on the roots of my feet": perhaps a mark branded on a slave's feet, or a boundary drawn around them.
[^10]: The human being of 14:1. This verse may belong with chapter 14.
