**1** Then Bildad the Shuhite answered:[^1]

**2** "Dominion and dread are his; he makes peace in his high places.[^2]

**3** Can his troops be counted?[^3] On whom does his light not rise?

**4** How then can a mortal be in the right before God? How can one born of a woman be pure?[^4]

**5** Look, even the moon is not bright,[^5] and the stars are not pure in his eyes;

**6** how much less a mortal, who is a maggot, and a human being,[^6] who is a worm!"

[^1]: Bildad's third speech is very short, and Zophar, who spoke after Bildad in the first two rounds of speeches (chapters 11 and 20), has no third speech. Many interpreters think that parts of chapters 24–27 have been moved from their original speakers: some, for example, give 26:5–14 to Bildad and 27:13–23 to Zophar. The text is translated here as it stands.
[^2]: Literally "Ruling and dread are with him". *His high places* are the heavens.
[^3]: Literally "Is there a number to his troops?": probably the stars, or the heavenly beings who serve him.
[^4]: Compare Eliphaz's words in 4:17–19 and 15:14–16.
[^5]: Literally "Look, as far as the moon, and it does not shine". Hebrew *ya'ahil*, a verb found only here, probably "shine".
[^6]: Literally "a son of *adam*", that is, of humankind.
