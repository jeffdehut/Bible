**1** Then Elihu answered:[^1]

**2** "Do you think this is just? You say, 'I am more in the right than God.'[^2]

**3** For you ask, 'What use is it to you?[^3] What do I gain by not sinning?'[^4]

**4** I will answer you, and your friends with you.

**5** Look up at the heavens and see; gaze at the clouds, high above you.

**6** If you have sinned, what do you do to him? If your transgressions are many, what do you do to him?

**7** If you are righteous, what do you give him? Or what does he receive from your hand?

**8** Your wickedness affects only someone like yourself, and your righteousness only a human being.[^5]

**9** Because of the many oppressions,[^6] people cry out; they cry for help because of the arm of the mighty.

**10** But no one says, 'Where is God[^7] my Maker, who gives songs in the night,

**11** who teaches us more than the animals of the earth, and makes us wiser than the birds of the sky?'[^8]

**12** There they cry out, but he does not answer, because of the pride of evildoers.

**13** Surely God does not listen to an empty cry;[^9] the Almighty[^10] pays no attention to it.

**14** How much less when you say that you do not see him, that your case is before him, and you are waiting for him!

**15** And now, because his anger does not punish, and he does not take much notice of transgression,[^11]

**16** Job opens his mouth in empty talk;[^12] he multiplies words without knowledge."

[^1]: Literally "And Elihu answered and said" (see 34:1).
[^2]: Literally "my righteousness from God": either "my righteousness is more than God's" or "I am in the right before God".
[^3]: Either Job speaking to God, or Elihu repeating Job's question in his own words: "What use is it to you, Job?"
[^4]: Literally "What do I gain more than from my sin?"
[^5]: Literally "a son of *adam*", that is, of humankind.
[^6]: Or "the many oppressors".
[^7]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^8]: Or "who teaches us by the animals of the earth, and makes us wise by the birds of the sky" (compare 12:7).
[^9]: Literally "Surely emptiness God does not hear".
[^10]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^11]: Hebrew *pash*, a word found only here, perhaps "arrogance" or "folly". It may be a form of *pesha*, "transgression".
[^12]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": what has no substance.
