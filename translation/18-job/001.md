**1** There was a man in the land of Uz[^1] whose name was Job. That man was blameless[^2] and upright; he feared God and turned away from evil.

**2** Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**3** His livestock numbered seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred female donkeys, and he had a great many servants. That man was the greatest of all the people of the East.[^3]

**4** His sons used to go and hold a feast in the house of each one in turn,[^4] and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

**5** When the days of feasting had run their course, Job would send for them and purify them.[^5] He would get up early in the morning and offer burnt offerings for each one of them, for Job thought, "Perhaps my children[^6] have sinned and cursed[^7] God in their hearts." This is what Job always did.

**6** One day the sons of God[^8] came to present themselves before Yahweh,[^9] and the Accuser[^10] also came among them.

**7** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Where have you come from?" The Accuser answered Yahweh, "From roaming the earth and walking about on it."

**8** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Have you considered[^11] my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth, a blameless and upright man who fears God and turns away from evil."

**9** The Accuser answered Yahweh, "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?

**10** Have you not put a hedge around him and his household and everything he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock have spread out over the land.

**11** But stretch out your hand now and touch everything he has, and he will surely curse you to your face."[^12]

**12** Yahweh said to the Accuser, "Very well, everything he has is in your power.[^13] Only do not stretch out your hand against him." Then the Accuser went out from the presence of Yahweh.

**13** One day, when Job's sons and daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their oldest brother,

**14** a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys were grazing beside them,

**15** and the Sabeans[^14] swooped down and took them. They struck down the servants[^15] with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**16** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The fire of God[^16] fell from the sky and burned up the sheep and the servants and consumed them, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**17** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three raiding parties[^17] and made a raid on the camels and took them. They struck down the servants with the sword, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**18** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their oldest brother,

**19** and suddenly a great wind came from across the desert and struck the four corners of the house. It fell on the young people,[^18] and they died, and I alone have escaped to tell you."

**20** Then Job got up, tore his robe and shaved his head. He fell to the ground and worshiped.[^19]

**21** He said, "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked I will return there.[^20] Yahweh gave, and Yahweh has taken away. May the name of Yahweh be blessed."

**22** In all this Job did not sin, and he did not accuse God of wrongdoing.[^21]

[^1]: A region east or southeast of Israel, perhaps in Edom (Lamentations 4:21) or northern Arabia.
[^2]: Hebrew *tam*: whole, complete, a person of integrity. Also in verse 8.
[^3]: Literally "the sons of the east", the peoples east of Israel.
[^4]: Literally "in the house of each one, his day": perhaps each son's birthday (compare 3:1), or each one in turn.
[^5]: Literally "make them holy". He had them prepare to come before God.
[^6]: Literally "my sons".
[^7]: Literally "blessed". Here and in verse 11, 2:5 and 2:9, the Hebrew says "bless" in place of "curse", to avoid writing the words "curse God".
[^8]: Literally "the sons of God" (Hebrew *bene ha-elohim*): heavenly beings who serve in God's court, as in 2:1 and 38:7 (compare Genesis 6:2; Psalm 29:1). The Greek Septuagint reads "the angels of God".
[^9]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan".
[^11]: Literally "set your heart on".
[^12]: Literally "if he will not bless you to your face". On "bless" for "curse", see verse 5. The form "if not" is that of an oath: "I swear he will".
[^13]: Literally "in your hand".
[^14]: Hebrew *Sheva*, Sheba, a people of Arabia.
[^15]: Or "the young men". Also in verses 16 and 17.
[^16]: Probably lightning. Or "a tremendous fire".
[^17]: Literally "set three heads".
[^18]: Hebrew *ne'arim*, the word translated "servants" in verses 15–17.
[^19]: Or "bowed down", as in worship.
[^20]: Perhaps to the earth, pictured as the mother of all (compare Psalm 139:15), or to the grave.
[^21]: Literally "he did not give unseemliness to God". Hebrew *tiflah*: what is unfitting or offensive.
