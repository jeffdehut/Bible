**1** Then Eliphaz the Temanite answered:

**2** "Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge and fill his belly with the east wind?

**3** Should he argue with useless talk, with words that do no good?

**4** But you are doing away with the fear of God[^1] and hindering devotion before God.

**5** For your guilt teaches your mouth, and you choose the tongue of the crafty.

**6** Your own mouth condemns you, not I; your own lips testify against you.

**7** Were you the first human being ever born? Were you brought forth before the hills?

**8** Do you listen in on the council of God?[^2] Do you keep wisdom to yourself?

**9** What do you know that we do not know? What do you understand that is not with us as well?

**10** Both the gray-haired and the aged are among us, older than your father.

**11** Are the consolations of God too small for you, or a word spoken gently to you?

**12** Why does your heart carry you away, and why do your eyes flash,[^3]

**13** that you turn your spirit against God and let such words come out of your mouth?

**14** What is a mortal, that he could be pure, or one born of woman, that he could be righteous?

**15** If God puts no trust in his holy ones,[^4] and even the heavens are not pure in his sight,

**16** how much less one who is detestable and corrupt, a man who drinks wickedness like water!

**17** I will explain to you; listen to me, and let me tell what I have seen,

**18** what wise men have declared, and have not hidden, what came from their fathers,

**19** to whom alone the land was given, when no stranger passed among them.

**20** The wicked man writhes in pain all his days, through all the years that are stored up for the ruthless.

**21** The sound of terrors is in his ears; in time of peace the destroyer comes upon him.

**22** He does not believe he will come back out of darkness; he is marked out for the sword.

**23** He wanders about for bread: 'Where is it?'[^5] He knows that a day of darkness is ready at hand.

**24** Distress and anguish terrify him; they overpower him like a king ready for the attack.[^6]

**25** For he has stretched out his hand against God and defied the Almighty,[^7]

**26** charging at him headlong,[^8] with the thick bosses of his shields.

**27** For he has covered his face with his fat and put rolls of fat on his loins,

**28** and he has lived in ruined cities, in houses no one should live in, which are destined to become heaps of rubble.

**29** He will not grow rich, and his wealth will not last; his possessions will not spread over the land.[^9]

**30** He will not escape the darkness; a flame will wither his shoots, and he will be swept away by the breath of his mouth.[^10]

**31** Let him not trust in emptiness, deceiving himself, for emptiness will be his reward.

**32** It will be paid in full before his time, and his branch will not be green.

**33** He will be like a vine that drops its unripe grapes, like an olive tree that sheds its blossoms.

**34** For the company of the godless is barren, and fire consumes the tents of bribery.

**35** They conceive trouble and give birth to wickedness; their womb prepares deceit."

[^1]: Literally "fear".
[^2]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. *Council* is Hebrew *sod*, the inner circle of God's advisers, or its secret counsel.
[^3]: Hebrew *razam*, a verb found only here, perhaps "flash" or "wink".
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "his holy one" but read "his holy ones": heavenly beings (compare 5:1).
[^5]: The Greek Septuagint reads "he is appointed as food for vultures", reading *ayyah*, "kite, bird of prey", for *ayyeh*, "where?".
[^6]: Hebrew *kidor*, a word found only here, perhaps "attack" or "battle".
[^7]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^8]: Literally "with neck", that is, with head down or neck stiffened.
[^9]: Hebrew *minlam*, a word found only here and of uncertain meaning: perhaps "their possessions" or "their ears of grain". Literally "and their *minlam* will not bend to the earth".
[^10]: Probably the breath of God's mouth (compare 4:9). The Hebrew vowel points read "he will turn away". With a small change, the line reads "his blossom will be swept away by the wind".
