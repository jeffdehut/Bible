**1** "Why are times not stored up by the Almighty?[^1] Why do those who know him[^2] never see his days?[^3]

**2** Some move boundary stones;[^4] they seize flocks and pasture them.

**3** They drive away the orphans' donkey; they take the widow's ox as a pledge.[^5]

**4** They push the needy off the road; the poor of the land all hide themselves together.

**5** Look, like wild donkeys in the wilderness, they go out to their work, searching for food;[^6] the desert gives them bread for their children.[^7]

**6** They harvest fodder[^8] in the field, and they glean the vineyard of the wicked.

**7** They spend the night naked, without clothing; they have no covering in the cold.

**8** They are drenched by the mountain downpours; with no shelter, they hug the rock.

**9** Some snatch the orphan from the breast,[^9] and they take pledges from the poor.[^10]

**10** They go about naked, without clothing; hungry, they carry the sheaves.

**11** Between their terraces[^11] they press out oil; they tread the winepresses, yet they are thirsty.

**12** From the city men groan,[^12] and the wounded[^13] cry for help, yet God[^14] does not count it as wrong.[^15]

**13** These are among those who rebel against the light; they do not know its ways, and they do not stay in its paths.

**14** The murderer rises at daybreak;[^16] he kills the poor and the needy, and in the night he is like a thief.

**15** The eye of the adulterer watches for the twilight. He says, 'No eye will see me,' and he puts a covering over his face.

**16** In the dark they break into houses;[^17] by day they shut themselves in; they do not know the light.

**17** For to all of them the morning is deep darkness,[^18] for they are well acquainted with the terrors of deep darkness.[^19]

**18** He is swift on the surface of the waters;[^20] their portion in the land is cursed; he does not turn toward the vineyards.[^21]

**19** Drought and heat snatch away the snow waters; so does Sheol[^22] those who have sinned.

**20** The womb forgets him; the worm feeds on him with relish;[^23] he is remembered no more, and wickedness is broken like a tree.

**21** He feeds on[^24] the barren woman who bears no child, and does no good to the widow.

**22** Yet by his power he prolongs the life of the mighty;[^25] they rise up when they had no hope of living.[^26]

**23** He lets them rest secure,[^27] and they are supported, and his eyes are on their ways.

**24** They are exalted for a little while, and then they are gone. They are brought low and gathered up like everyone else;[^28] they are cut off like the heads of grain.

**25** If it is not so, who can prove me a liar and reduce my words to nothing?"

[^1]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. The *times* are probably times of judgment, when God would call the wicked to account.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "and they know" but read "and those who know him".
[^3]: That is, the days when he judges.
[^4]: Literally "They move boundaries". The subject is not stated; the wicked are meant. Boundary stones marked a family's land (Deuteronomy 19:14).
[^5]: A pledge was something taken as security for a debt (compare Deuteronomy 24:17).
[^6]: Literally "seeking early for prey". From here to verse 8, *they* are the poor of verse 4.
[^7]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "the desert, to him, bread for the young ones".
[^8]: Hebrew *belilo*, "his fodder", perhaps the wicked man's. Divided as *beli lo*, "not his": "they reap in a field that is not theirs".
[^9]: Literally "They snatch the orphan from the breast": the wicked, as in verses 2–4.
[^10]: Or "they take the poor man's infant as a pledge", reading *ul*, "infant", for *al*, "on".
[^11]: Hebrew *shurot*, "walls" or "rows": perhaps the terrace walls of olive groves, or rows of olive trees.
[^12]: Or "the dying groan", reading the same Hebrew consonants with different vowels.
[^13]: Literally "the soul of the wounded".
[^14]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^15]: Hebrew *tiflah*, "what is unfitting", the word in 1:22. Or, with different vowels, *tefillah*, "prayer": "yet God does not heed their prayer".
[^16]: Literally "at the light". Some take it to mean "before the light" or "at dusk", or read *lo or*, "when there is no light".
[^17]: Literally "he digs through houses", through their walls of mud brick.
[^18]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death". Also later in the verse.
[^19]: Literally "for he recognizes the terrors of deep darkness".
[^20]: Verses 18–24 describe the swift end of the wicked, as the friends do (compare 18:5–21 and 20:4–29), though Job has just said that God does not call them to account (verses 1 and 12; 21:7–34). Interpreters explain this in different ways: Job is quoting the friends' view in order to reject it (verse 25), or wishing it were true ("Let him be swift …"), or these verses belong to a speech of Bildad or Zophar in this third round of speeches (see the note at 25:1). The Hebrew has no marker, and they are translated here as they stand, as part of Job's speech. The picture in this line is of the wicked man swept away like foam on the water.
[^21]: Literally "he does not turn the way of the vineyards": he will not go out to enjoy them.
[^22]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^23]: Literally "the worm finds him sweet".
[^24]: Literally "He grazes on", that is, he devours what she has.
[^25]: Literally "he draws out the mighty by his power". *He* is probably God, as in verse 23, though the Hebrew does not say. Or "he drags away the mighty by his power", of the wicked man.
[^26]: Literally "he rises and does not believe in life".
[^27]: Literally "He gives him security".
[^28]: Literally "like all". The Greek Septuagint reads "like mallow (in the heat)", a plant that wilts quickly.
