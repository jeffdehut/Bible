**1** After this Job opened his mouth and cursed the day he was born.[^1]

**2** Job said:[^2]

**3** "Let the day perish on which I was born, and the night that said, 'A boy has been conceived!'

**4** That day, let it be darkness! Let God[^3] above not seek it out, and let no light shine on it.

**5** Let darkness and deep darkness[^4] claim it;[^5] let a cloud settle over it; let the blackness of the day[^6] terrify it.

**6** That night, let thick darkness seize it! Let it not rejoice[^7] among the days of the year; let it not come into the count of the months.

**7** Yes, let that night be barren; let no shout of joy come into it.

**8** Let those who curse days curse it, those who are ready to rouse Leviathan.[^8]

**9** Let the stars of its dawn[^9] be dark; let it wait for light that does not come, and let it not see the eyelids of the morning,[^10]

**10** because it did not shut the doors of my mother's womb[^11] and hide trouble from my eyes.

**11** Why did I not die at birth,[^12] come out of the womb and breathe my last?

**12** Why were there knees to receive me, or breasts for me to nurse?

**13** For now I would be lying down in quiet; I would be asleep, and then I would be at rest

**14** with kings and counselors of the earth, who rebuilt ruins for themselves,[^13]

**15** or with princes who had gold, who filled their houses with silver.

**16** Or why was I not hidden away like a stillborn child,[^14] like infants who never see the light?

**17** There the wicked stop their raging, and there the weary find rest.[^15]

**18** Prisoners are at ease together; they no longer hear the voice of the slave driver.

**19** The small and the great are there alike, and the slave is free from his master.

**20** Why does he[^16] give light to the one who suffers, and life to the bitter in soul,

**21** who long for death, but it does not come, who dig for it more than for hidden treasure,

**22** who would rejoice and shout for joy,[^17] who would be glad to find the grave?

**23** Why give light to a man whose way is hidden, whom God has hedged in?[^18]

**24** For sighing comes to me in place of my food,[^19] and my groans pour out like water.

**25** For what I feared has come upon me, and what I dreaded has happened to me.

**26** I have no ease, I have no quiet, I have no rest, and turmoil has come."

[^1]: Literally "cursed his day". Here the Hebrew uses the ordinary word for "curse" (*qillel*).
[^2]: Literally "Job answered and said", a common way of beginning a speech.
[^3]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 23.
[^4]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death".
[^5]: Literally "redeem it", that is, take it back as their own. Hebrew *ga'al*. Some take the verb as a different *ga'al*, "defile it".
[^6]: Hebrew *kimrire yom*, a phrase of uncertain meaning: perhaps "the darkenings of the day", such as an eclipse, or "the bitterness of the day".
[^7]: The Hebrew vowel points read "let it not rejoice". The same consonants can be read "let it not be joined", which fits the parallel line.
[^8]: A sea monster, a figure of chaos in the poetry of the ancient Near East (40:25 (English 41:1); Psalm 74:14; Isaiah 27:1). Those who "curse days" may be sorcerers thought able to bring disaster. Some interpreters read *yam*, "the Sea", for *yom*, "day", in the first line, as a parallel to Leviathan.
[^9]: Hebrew *neshef*, "twilight", here the twilight before dawn.
[^10]: The first rays of the sun, pictured as the opening eyelids of the dawn.
[^11]: Literally "the doors of my belly".
[^12]: Literally "from the womb".
[^13]: Or "who built for themselves what are now ruins". Hebrew *choravot*, "ruins, desolate places". Some think of monuments or tombs built in desolate places.
[^14]: Literally "Or like a hidden miscarriage I would not be".
[^15]: Literally "those weary of strength".
[^16]: God is meant, though the Hebrew does not name him.
[^17]: Literally "who rejoice to rejoicing". Some read *gal*, "a heap (of stones)", for *gil*, "rejoicing": "who would rejoice over a burial mound".
[^18]: The verb echoes 1:10, where the Accuser says God has "put a hedge around" Job to protect him. Here the hedge hems him in.
[^19]: Literally "before my bread": either "instead of my food" or "at my meals".
