**1** "Do you know when the mountain goats give birth? Do you watch over the does when they calve?[^1]

**2** Can you count the months they must fulfill, and do you know the time when they give birth?

**3** They crouch and bring forth their young; they are delivered of their labor pains.[^2]

**4** Their young grow strong and grow up in the open field; they go out and do not return to them.

**5** Who let the wild donkey go free? Who untied the ropes of the wild ass,[^3]

**6** to which I gave the desert[^4] as its home, and the salt land as its dwelling?

**7** It laughs at the commotion of the town; it does not hear the shouts of a driver.

**8** It ranges the mountains as its pasture and searches for every green thing.

**9** Will the wild ox[^5] be willing to serve you? Will it spend the night by your feeding trough?

**10** Can you bind the wild ox in the furrow with ropes? Will it harrow the valleys behind you?

**11** Will you rely on it because its strength is great? Will you leave your heavy work to it?

**12** Will you trust it to bring in[^6] your grain and gather it to your threshing floor?

**13** The wings of the ostrich flap joyfully, but are they the pinions and plumage of the stork?[^7]

**14** For she leaves her eggs on the ground and lets them warm in the dust,

**15** forgetting that a foot may crush them, and a wild animal may trample them.

**16** She treats her young harshly, as if they were not hers; she does not fear that her labor may be in vain,[^8]

**17** for God[^9] has made her forget wisdom and has given her no share of understanding.

**18** But when she rises up to run,[^10] she laughs at the horse and its rider.

**19** Do you give the horse its might? Do you clothe its neck with a mane?[^11]

**20** Do you make it leap like a locust? Its majestic snorting is terrifying.

**21** It paws[^12] in the valley and rejoices in its strength; it goes out to meet the weapons.

**22** It laughs at fear and is not dismayed; it does not turn back from the sword.

**23** The quiver rattles against it, the flashing spear and the javelin.[^13]

**24** With trembling and excitement it swallows the ground; it cannot stand still[^14] when the trumpet[^15] sounds.

**25** Whenever the trumpet sounds, it says, 'Aha!' From far away it smells the battle, the thunder of the commanders and the war cry.

**26** Is it by your understanding that the hawk soars and spreads its wings[^16] toward the south?

**27** Is it at your command that the eagle[^17] mounts up and makes its nest on high?

**28** It lives on the cliff and spends the night there, on the crag of the rock, its stronghold.

**29** From there it spies out its food;[^18] its eyes see it from far away.

**30** Its young ones gulp down blood, and where the slain are, there it is."[^19]

[^1]: Literally "the writhing (in labor) of the does".
[^2]: Literally "they send away their pangs".
[^3]: Hebrew *pere* and *arod*, two words for the wild donkey.
[^4]: Hebrew *aravah*, the dry steppe.
[^5]: Hebrew *re'em*, the wild ox or aurochs, a very large and powerful wild bull, now extinct. Traditionally "unicorn".
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "that it will return" but read "that it will bring back".
[^7]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. *Ostrich* is Hebrew *renanim*, "the shrill-voiced ones". *Stork* is Hebrew *chasidah*, a word that sounds like *chasid*, "loyal, loving": unlike the stork, the ostrich is not a caring parent (verses 14–16).
[^8]: Literally "in vain is her labor, without fear".
[^9]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^10]: Hebrew *tamri*, a verb found only here, perhaps "rise up" or "flap her wings". Literally "When she lifts herself on high".
[^11]: Hebrew *ra'mah*, a word found only here, perhaps "mane". It sounds like *ra'am*, "thunder", and older translations read "thunder".
[^12]: Literally "They paw".
[^13]: Hebrew *kidon*, perhaps a javelin or a curved sword.
[^14]: Literally "it does not believe", or "it does not stand firm".
[^15]: Hebrew *shofar*, a ram's-horn trumpet. Also in verse 25.
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "its wing" but read "its wings".
[^17]: Or "the vulture". Hebrew *nesher*.
[^18]: Literally "it digs for food".
[^19]: Compare Matthew 24:28; Luke 17:37.
