**1** Then Job answered:

**2** "Truly, I know that it is so. But how can a mortal be in the right with God?

**3** If he wanted to argue his case with him, he could not answer him once in a thousand times.[^1]

**4** He is wise in heart and mighty in strength. Who has ever defied him and come out unharmed?

**5** He moves mountains, and they do not know it, when he overturns them in his anger.

**6** He shakes the earth out of its place, and its pillars tremble.

**7** He speaks to the sun,[^2] and it does not rise; he seals up the stars.

**8** He alone stretches out the heavens and treads on the waves of the sea.[^3]

**9** He made the Bear, Orion and the Pleiades,[^4] and the chambers of the south.[^5]

**10** He does great things beyond searching out, wonders beyond counting.[^6]

**11** Look, he passes by me, and I do not see him; he moves on, and I do not perceive him.

**12** Look, he snatches away; who can stop him? Who can say to him, 'What are you doing?'

**13** God[^7] does not hold back his anger; the helpers of Rahab[^8] bowed down beneath him.

**14** How then could I answer him, or choose my words to argue with him?

**15** Even if I were in the right, I could not answer him; I would have to plead for mercy with my judge.[^9]

**16** If I called and he answered me, I would not believe that he was listening to my voice.

**17** For he crushes me with a storm[^10] and multiplies my wounds for no reason.[^11]

**18** He will not let me catch my breath, but fills me with bitterness.

**19** If it is a matter of strength, look, he is mighty! If it is a matter of justice, who can summon him?[^12]

**20** Even if I were in the right, my own mouth would condemn me; if I were blameless, it would declare me crooked.[^13]

**21** I am blameless. I do not care about myself;[^14] I loathe my life.

**22** It is all one; that is why I say, 'He destroys the blameless and the wicked alike.'

**23** When a scourge brings sudden death, he mocks at the despair[^15] of the innocent.

**24** The earth is handed over to the wicked; he covers the faces of its judges. If it is not he, then who is it?

**25** My days are swifter than a runner; they flee away without seeing any good.

**26** They glide by like boats of reed,[^16] like an eagle swooping on its prey.

**27** If I say, 'I will forget my complaint, I will change my expression[^17] and be cheerful,'

**28** I still dread all my sufferings, for I know you will not hold me innocent.

**29** I will be counted guilty anyway. Why then should I struggle in vain?[^18]

**30** If I washed myself with snow water[^19] and cleansed my hands with lye,

**31** even then you would plunge me into a pit, and my own clothes would detest me.

**32** For he is not a man like me, that I could answer him, that we could go to court together.

**33** There is no arbiter[^20] between us, who might lay his hand on us both.

**34** Let him take his rod away from me, and let not dread of him terrify me.

**35** Then I would speak without fear of him, for I am not like that in myself.[^21]

[^1]: Or "God would not answer him once in a thousand times". The Hebrew does not make clear who would not answer whom.
[^2]: Hebrew *cheres*, a rare word for the sun.
[^3]: Literally "on the backs (or heights) of the sea". The Sea (*Yam*) may be pictured as a monster that God tramples.
[^4]: Hebrew *Ash*, *Kesil* and *Kimah*, constellations that cannot be identified with certainty. *Kesil* means "fool" and *Kimah* "cluster". Also in 38:31–32.
[^5]: Perhaps the constellations of the southern sky.
[^6]: Job repeats Eliphaz's words in 5:9.
[^7]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^8]: A sea monster, a figure of chaos (26:12; Psalm 89:11 (English 89:10); Isaiah 51:9). This is a different Hebrew word from the name of Rahab in Joshua 2.
[^9]: Or "my opponent at law". Hebrew *meshofti*.
[^10]: Or "for a trifle", literally "for a hair". The Hebrew word can be read either way.
[^11]: Hebrew *chinnam*, the word in 1:9 and 2:3.
[^12]: Literally "who will summon me?", as if Job were quoting God.
[^13]: Or "he would declare me crooked".
[^14]: Literally "I do not know my soul".
[^15]: Hebrew *massat*, a word of uncertain meaning: perhaps "despair", or "trial".
[^16]: Hebrew *eveh*, probably papyrus. Light papyrus boats were fast on the Nile.
[^17]: Literally "abandon my face".
[^18]: Literally "with a breath": uselessly. Hebrew *hevel*.
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written "with snow" but read "with snow water".
[^20]: Hebrew *mokhiach*, one who decides between two parties in a dispute. The Greek Septuagint reads "If only there were a mediator between us", reading *lu*, "if only", for *lo*, "not".
[^21]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "for not so am I with myself": perhaps "for I know I am not guilty", or "but as things are, I cannot".
