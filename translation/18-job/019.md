**1** Then Job answered:

**2** "How long will you torment me and crush me with words?

**3** Ten times now you have insulted me; are you not ashamed to wrong me?[^1]

**4** Even if it is true that I have gone astray, my error remains my own concern.[^2]

**5** If you really want to exalt yourselves over me and use my disgrace as an argument against me,

**6** then know that God[^3] has put me in the wrong[^4] and closed his net around me.

**7** Look, I cry out, 'Violence!' but I get no answer; I call for help, but there is no justice.

**8** He has walled up my way so that I cannot pass, and he has set darkness on my paths.

**9** He has stripped me of my honor and taken the crown from my head.

**10** He tears me down on every side, and I am gone; he has uprooted my hope like a tree.

**11** He has kindled his anger against me and counts me as one of his enemies.

**12** His troops advance together; they build up a siege ramp against me[^5] and camp around my tent.

**13** He has put my brothers far from me; those who know me are wholly estranged from me.

**14** My relatives have failed me; my close friends have forgotten me.

**15** The guests in my house and my female servants count me as a stranger; I have become a foreigner in their eyes.

**16** I call my servant, but he does not answer, though I plead with him with my own mouth.

**17** My breath is repulsive to my wife; I am loathsome to my own brothers.[^6]

**18** Even young children despise me; when I get up, they speak against me.

**19** All my intimate friends[^7] detest me; those I love have turned against me.

**20** My bones cling to my skin and my flesh; I have escaped by the skin of my teeth.[^8]

**21** Have pity on me, have pity on me, you my friends, for the hand of God has struck me!

**22** Why do you pursue me as God does? Will you never have enough of my flesh?[^9]

**23** Oh, that my words were written down! Oh, that they were inscribed in a scroll,[^10]

**24** with an iron pen and lead, carved in the rock forever!

**25** But I know that my redeemer[^11] lives, and that at the last he will rise up upon the earth.[^12]

**26** And after my skin has been stripped off like this,[^13] yet in my flesh I will see God.[^14]

**27** I myself will see him, with my own eyes, I and not a stranger.[^15] My heart faints within me![^16]

**28** If you say, 'How we will hound him!' and that the root of the trouble is found in me,[^17]

**29** be afraid of the sword yourselves, for wrath brings the punishment of the sword,[^18] so that you may know there is a judgment."[^19]

[^1]: Hebrew *tahkeru*, a verb found only here. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "wrong", "attack" or "be hard on".
[^2]: Literally "my error lodges with me".
[^3]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verses 21 and 26.
[^4]: The verb Bildad used in 8:3: "Does God pervert justice?"
[^5]: Literally "they have cast up their way against me".
[^6]: Literally "the sons of my womb", probably the sons of the same mother, his brothers. Job's own children had died (1:19).
[^7]: Literally "the men of my council".
[^8]: The meaning of this phrase is uncertain. It may mean that he has barely escaped with his life, or that nothing is left of him but his gums.
[^9]: To "eat someone's flesh" is an idiom for slandering them (compare Daniel 3:8, in Aramaic).
[^10]: Or "on an inscription". Verse 24 may picture letters cut in the rock and filled with lead.
[^11]: Hebrew *go'el*: the nearest relative, whose duty was to stand up for a family member in need, buying back property or a relative sold into slavery (Leviticus 25:25, 47–49), avenging a killing (Numbers 35:19), and taking up a relative's cause (Proverbs 23:10–11). Here the word may also be rendered "defender" or "vindicator": one who will take up Job's case and clear his name. Job does not say who his *go'el* is. Interpreters have understood God himself, or a heavenly witness or advocate like the ones Job longs for in 9:33 and 16:19–21. Traditionally "my Redeemer".
[^12]: Literally "and last upon the dust he will stand". *At the last* (Hebrew *acharon*) may mean "in the end", "afterward", or "as the last one", the one who has the final word. *Rise up* may be the legal term for a witness standing to testify (Deuteronomy 19:15–16). *The earth* is literally "the dust", which may mean the ground, or the dust of the grave (17:16).
[^13]: The Hebrew of verses 26–27 is very difficult. The first line of verse 26 is literally "and after my skin they have struck off (or "torn") this".
[^14]: Literally "and from my flesh I will see God". *From my flesh* may mean "from within my flesh", still in the body, or "away from my flesh", without the body. So the verse may speak of seeing God in this life, after his disease has done its work, or after death.
[^15]: Or "and not as a stranger", "and not another". *I myself will see him* may also be read "I will see him on my side".
[^16]: Literally "my kidneys fail within my bosom". The kidneys were thought of as the seat of the deepest feelings.
[^17]: Literally "and the root of the matter is found in me". Many interpreters read "in him", as the friends' own words.
[^18]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "for wrath is iniquities of the sword".
[^19]: The Hebrew text is written *shedin*, "that there is judgment", but read *shaddun*, a form of uncertain meaning: perhaps also "that there is a judgment", or a play on *Shaddai*, "the Almighty".
