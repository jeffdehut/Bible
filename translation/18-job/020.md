**1** Then Zophar the Naamathite answered:

**2** "So my troubled thoughts compel me to answer, because of the turmoil within me.[^1]

**3** I hear a rebuke that insults me, and a spirit from my understanding answers me.[^2]

**4** Do you not know this from of old, ever since humankind was placed on the earth,

**5** that the shout of joy of the wicked is short, and the gladness of the godless lasts only a moment?

**6** Though his pride reaches up to the heavens and his head touches the clouds,

**7** he will perish forever like his own dung; those who saw him will say, 'Where is he?'

**8** He flies away like a dream and cannot be found; he is chased away like a vision of the night.

**9** The eye that saw him will see him no more, and his place will no longer behold him.

**10** His children will seek the favor of the poor,[^3] and his own hands will give back his wealth.

**11** His bones are full of youthful vigor, but it will lie down with him in the dust.

**12** Though evil is sweet in his mouth, and he hides it under his tongue,

**13** though he savors it and will not let it go, but holds it in his mouth,

**14** his food turns sour in his stomach; it becomes the venom of cobras within him.[^4]

**15** He swallows riches and vomits them up; God makes his belly throw them out.

**16** He will suck the poison of cobras; the tongue of the viper will kill him.

**17** He will not look on the streams, the rivers, the torrents of honey and curds.

**18** He will give back what he toiled for and not swallow it; he will not enjoy the profit of his trading.[^5]

**19** For he has crushed and abandoned the poor; he has seized a house that he did not build.

**20** Because his greed knew no rest,[^6] he will not escape with anything he desired.

**21** Nothing was left that he did not devour; that is why his prosperity will not last.

**22** In the fullness of his plenty he will be in distress; the full force of misery will come upon him.[^7]

**23** When he has filled his belly, God[^8] will send his burning anger against him and rain it down on him as his food.[^9]

**24** He may flee from an iron weapon, but a bronze arrow[^10] will pierce him.

**25** He pulls it out, and it comes out of his back, the glittering point[^11] out of his gall; terrors come upon him.

**26** Total darkness lies in wait for his treasures. A fire not fanned by anyone will consume him; it will devour what is left in his tent.

**27** The heavens will reveal his guilt, and the earth will rise up against him.

**28** The produce of his house will be carried off, flowing away on the day of God's anger.[^12]

**29** This is the portion of the wicked from God, the inheritance appointed for him by God."[^13]

[^1]: Literally "because of my haste within me".
[^2]: Or "and my understanding inspires me to answer".
[^3]: Or "The poor will crush his children". The verb *yeratsu* may come from *ratsah*, "seek favor", or *ratsats*, "crush".
[^4]: Literally "the gall of cobras is within him".
[^5]: Literally "according to the wealth of his exchange, and he will not rejoice". The Hebrew of this line is uncertain.
[^6]: Literally "Because he did not know quiet in his belly".
[^7]: Literally "every hand of the wretched will come upon him". The same consonants can be read *amal*, "misery", for *amel*, "the wretched".
[^8]: Literally "he". God is meant.
[^9]: Hebrew *bilchumo*, a word of uncertain meaning: perhaps "as his food" or "into his flesh".
[^10]: Literally "a bow of bronze".
[^11]: Literally "the lightning", that is, the flashing arrowhead.
[^12]: Literally "his anger". Or "A flood will carry off his house, torrents on the day of his anger".
[^13]: Literally "the inheritance of his word (or decree) from God". The first line has *Elohim*, the second *El*.
