**1** So these three men stopped answering Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes.[^1]

**2** Then Elihu[^2] son of Barachel the Buzite, of the family of Ram, became angry. He was angry with Job because he had justified himself rather than God.[^3]

**3** He was also angry with Job's three friends, because they had found no answer and yet had condemned Job.[^4]

**4** Elihu had waited to speak to Job,[^5] because they were older than he was.

**5** But when Elihu saw that there was no answer in the mouths of the three men, he became angry.

**6** So Elihu son of Barachel the Buzite answered:[^6] "I am young in years, and you are old. That is why I held back[^7] and was afraid to tell you what I know.

**7** I thought, 'Age should speak, and many years should teach wisdom.'[^8]

**8** But it is the spirit in a person,[^9] the breath of the Almighty,[^10] that gives them understanding.

**9** It is not the old[^11] who are wise, nor the aged who understand what is right.

**10** So I say, listen to me; let me also tell you what I know.

**11** Look, I waited for your words; I listened to your reasoning, while you searched for what to say.

**12** I paid close attention to you, but not one of you refuted Job[^12] or answered his words.

**13** Do not say, 'We have found wisdom; let God drive him back,[^13] not a man.'

**14** He has not directed his words against me, and I will not answer him with your arguments.

**15** They are dismayed; they answer no more; words have deserted them.[^14]

**16** Must I wait because they do not speak, because they stand there and answer no more?

**17** I too will give my answer;[^15] I too will tell what I know.

**18** For I am full of words; the spirit within me presses me.[^16]

**19** Look, my belly is like wine that has no vent; like new wineskins, it is ready to burst.

**20** Let me speak and find relief;[^17] let me open my lips and answer.

**21** I will not show partiality to anyone or flatter any person.[^18]

**22** For I do not know how to flatter; if I did, my Maker would soon carry me away.[^19]

[^1]: The Greek Septuagint reads "in their eyes".
[^2]: *Elihu* means "He is my God". *Buz* was a son of Nahor, Abraham's brother (Genesis 22:21).
[^3]: Literally "because he declared his soul righteous rather than God", or "before God".
[^4]: An ancient Jewish scribal tradition counts this among places where the scribes changed the text out of reverence for God, from "and so had condemned God".
[^5]: Literally "Elihu had waited for Job with words".
[^6]: Elihu speaks from here to the end of chapter 37.
[^7]: Hebrew *zachalti*, a rare verb, related to the word for creatures that crawl in the dust (Deuteronomy 32:24).
[^8]: Literally "Days should speak, and many years make wisdom known".
[^9]: Or "But truly it is a spirit in a mortal". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, breath".
[^10]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^11]: Literally "the many", perhaps "the great" or "those many in years".
[^12]: Literally "there was no one among you to rebuke (or convince) Job".
[^13]: Literally "God will drive him away", as the wind drives away chaff.
[^14]: Literally "they have moved words away from themselves". In verses 15–16, Elihu speaks about the three friends rather than to them.
[^15]: Literally "my portion".
[^16]: Literally "the spirit of my belly presses me". Or "the wind in my belly". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^17]: Literally "that it may be roomy for me".
[^18]: Literally "I will not lift up anyone's face, and to a human being (*adam*) I will not give titles".
[^19]: Literally "in a little while my Maker would lift me up".
