**1** "At this my heart also trembles and leaps from its place.

**2** Listen, listen to the roar of his voice,[^1] the rumbling that comes from his mouth.

**3** He lets it loose under the whole heaven, and his lightning[^2] to the ends of the earth.

**4** After it his voice roars; he thunders with his majestic voice, and he does not hold back the lightning[^3] when his voice is heard.

**5** God thunders wonderfully with his voice; he does great things that we cannot understand.

**6** For he says to the snow, 'Fall on the earth,' and to the rain shower, his mighty downpours.[^4]

**7** He seals up the hand of every person,[^5] so that all the people he has made may know it.[^6]

**8** The animals go into their dens and stay in their lairs.

**9** The storm comes out from its chamber,[^7] and the cold from the scattering winds.[^8]

**10** By the breath of God ice is given, and the broad waters are frozen solid.[^9]

**11** He loads the thick cloud with moisture;[^10] the clouds scatter his lightning.[^11]

**12** They turn round and round by his guidance,[^12] to do all that he commands them over the face of the inhabited earth.

**13** Whether for punishment,[^13] or for his land, or for faithful love,[^14] he makes it happen.

**14** Listen to this, Job; stop and consider the wonders of God.

**15** Do you know how God[^15] directs them[^16] and makes the lightning of his cloud shine?

**16** Do you know how the clouds are balanced,[^17] the wonders of one who is perfect in knowledge?[^18]

**17** You, whose clothes are hot when the land lies still under the south wind,

**18** can you, as he does, spread out the skies, hard as a mirror of cast metal?[^19]

**19** Teach us what we should say to him; we cannot set out our case because of the darkness.

**20** Should he be told that I want to speak? Would anyone ask to be swallowed up?[^20]

**21** Now no one can look at the light when it is bright in the skies, when the wind has passed and swept them clean.[^21]

**22** Out of the north comes golden splendor;[^22] around God is awesome majesty.

**23** The Almighty:[^23] we cannot find him. He is great in power, and in justice and abundant righteousness he does not oppress.

**24** Therefore people fear him; he does not regard any who are wise in their own heart."[^24]

[^1]: Or "his thunder". Hebrew *qol*, "voice", is also the word for thunder. Also in verses 4 and 5.
[^2]: Literally "his light". Also in verses 11 and 15.
[^3]: Literally "them", probably the lightning bolts of verse 3.
[^4]: Literally "and the shower of rain, and the shower of the rains of his strength".
[^5]: That is, he stops all human work during the storms of winter. Literally "In the hand of every human being (*adam*) he seals".
[^6]: Literally "to know, all the men of his work".
[^7]: Literally "from the chamber", perhaps "the chambers of the south" (9:9).
[^8]: Hebrew *mezarim*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps the north winds that scatter the clouds.
[^9]: Literally "and the breadth of the waters is in constraint".
[^10]: Hebrew *ri*, a word found only here, probably "moisture".
[^11]: Literally "the cloud of his light scatters".
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "his guidance" but read "his directions". *They* are the clouds.
[^13]: Literally "for a rod".
[^14]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^15]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 22.
[^16]: Literally "sets upon them".
[^17]: Literally "the balancings of the clouds". Hebrew *miflas*, a word found only here.
[^18]: Elihu used the same phrase of himself in 36:4.
[^19]: Ancient mirrors were made of polished bronze. The verb *raqa*, "spread out, beat out (like metal)", is related to *raqia*, the "dome" of the sky in Genesis 1:6.
[^20]: Literally "If a man speaks, surely he will be swallowed up". The meaning is uncertain.
[^21]: Literally "And now they have not seen the light; it is bright in the skies, and a wind has passed and cleansed them".
[^22]: Literally "From the north comes gold". The far north was thought of as the place where God (or the gods) lived (compare Isaiah 14:13; Psalm 48:3 (English 48:2)).
[^23]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^24]: Literally "he does not see all the wise of heart", that is, those who think themselves wise.
