**1** "But now, Job, hear what I have to say, and listen to all my words.

**2** Look, I open my mouth; my tongue speaks in my mouth.[^1]

**3** My words come from an upright heart, and my lips speak sincerely what they know.[^2]

**4** The spirit of God[^3] has made me; the breath of the Almighty[^4] gives me life.

**5** Answer me if you can; set out your case before me; take your stand.

**6** Look, before God I am just as you are;[^5] I too was pinched off from clay.

**7** So no fear of me need terrify you, and my pressure[^6] will not be heavy on you.

**8** But you have said in my hearing, and I heard the sound of your words:

**9** 'I am pure, without transgression; I am clean, and there is no guilt in me.

**10** Look, he finds pretexts[^7] against me; he counts me as his enemy.

**11** He puts my feet in the stocks; he watches all my paths.'[^8]

**12** Look, in this you are not right, I answer you, for God[^9] is greater than any mortal.

**13** Why do you contend with him, saying that he answers none of a person's words?[^10]

**14** For God speaks in one way, and in two, though people do not notice it.

**15** In a dream, a vision of the night, when deep sleep falls on people as they slumber on their beds,

**16** then he opens people's ears and seals their warning,[^11]

**17** to turn a person away from what he is doing,[^12] and to keep a man from pride.[^13]

**18** He keeps his soul back from the pit,[^14] his life from crossing the channel of death.[^15]

**19** A person is also disciplined with pain on his bed, while all his bones are in constant torment,[^16]

**20** so that his life loathes bread, and his soul the food it once craved.[^17]

**21** His flesh wastes away until it cannot be seen, and his bones, once hidden, stick out.[^18]

**22** His soul draws near to the pit, and his life to those who bring death.[^19]

**23** If there is an angel[^20] for him, a mediator,[^21] one among a thousand, to tell a person what is right for him,[^22]

**24** and he is gracious to him and says, 'Spare him[^23] from going down to the pit; I have found a ransom,'

**25** then his flesh becomes fresher than a child's;[^24] he returns to the days of his youth.

**26** He prays to God, and God accepts him; he sees his face with a shout of joy, and he restores to the mortal his righteousness.[^25]

**27** He sings before people and says, 'I sinned and perverted what was right, but I was not repaid as I deserved.'[^26]

**28** He has redeemed his soul from going down to the pit, and his life will see the light.[^27]

**29** Look, God does all these things, twice, three times, with a man,

**30** to bring back his soul from the pit, so that he may be lit with the light of life.[^28]

**31** Pay attention, Job, listen to me; be silent, and I will speak.

**32** If you have anything to say, answer me; speak, for I want to prove you right.

**33** If not, listen to me; be silent, and I will teach you wisdom."

[^1]: Literally "in my palate".
[^2]: Literally "The uprightness of my heart is my sayings, and the knowledge of my lips they speak purely".
[^3]: Or "The Spirit of God", "The breath of God". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^4]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^5]: Literally "Look, I am like your mouth to God".
[^6]: Hebrew *akhpi*, a word found only here, perhaps "pressure" or "hand" (compare 13:21).
[^7]: Hebrew *tenu'ot*, "pretexts, occasions for hostility".
[^8]: Job's own words in 13:27; compare 13:24.
[^9]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verse 26.
[^10]: Literally "for all his words he does not answer". Or "for he gives no account of any of his doings".
[^11]: Literally "and he seals their discipline". The same consonants can be read "and he terrifies them with warnings".
[^12]: Literally "to turn aside a human being (*adam*) from a deed".
[^13]: Literally "and pride from a man he covers".
[^14]: Hebrew *shachat*, the grave, the world of the dead. Also in verses 22, 24, 28 and 30.
[^15]: Hebrew *shelach*, perhaps a channel or river of the underworld, or a weapon: "from perishing by the sword".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "and the strife in his bones is constant" but read "and the multitude of his bones is constant", that is, all his bones are in constant pain.
[^17]: Literally "food of desire".
[^18]: The Hebrew text is written "and the bareness of his bones" but read "and his bones are laid bare". Literally "his bones that were not seen are laid bare".
[^19]: Hebrew *memitim*, "those who put to death": perhaps angels of death, or the dead.
[^20]: Or "a messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh*.
[^21]: Hebrew *melits*, "interpreter, spokesman": one who speaks for another (compare 16:20; Genesis 42:23).
[^22]: Literally "to declare to a human being (*adam*) his uprightness": either what is right for him to do, or that he is upright.
[^23]: Hebrew *pede'ehu*, a form found only here. Some scholars read *pedehu*, "Ransom him", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^24]: Hebrew *rutafash*, a word found only here, probably "grow fresh". Literally "his flesh is fresher than youth".
[^25]: That is, God restores him to a right standing.
[^26]: Literally "and it was not made equal to me".
[^27]: The Hebrew text is written "my soul … my life" but read "his soul … his life". With the written form, the man's words continue to the end of this verse.
[^28]: Literally "to be lit with the light of the living".
