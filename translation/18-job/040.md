**1** Then Yahweh[^1] answered Job:

**2** "Will the one who contends with the Almighty[^2] correct him? Let the one who argues with God answer it!"[^3]

**3** Then Job answered Yahweh:

**4** "Look, I am of small account;[^4] what can I answer you? I put my hand over my mouth.

**5** I have spoken once, and I will not answer; twice, but I will say no more."

**6** Then Yahweh answered Job out of the storm:

**7** "Brace yourself like a man; I will question you, and you will inform me.[^5]

**8** Would you even annul my justice?[^6] Would you condemn me so that you may be in the right?

**9** Do you have an arm like God's? Can you thunder with a voice like his?

**10** Adorn yourself, then, with pride and dignity; clothe yourself with splendor and majesty.

**11** Pour out the floods of your anger; look at everyone who is proud and bring him low.

**12** Look at everyone who is proud and humble him; tread down the wicked where they stand.

**13** Hide them all in the dust together; bind their faces in the hidden place.[^7]

**14** Then I too will praise you,[^8] for your own right hand can save you.

**15** Look now at Behemoth,[^9] which I made, as I made you;[^10] it eats grass like an ox.

**16** Look at the strength in its loins, and the power in the muscles of its belly.

**17** It stiffens its tail like a cedar;[^11] the sinews of its thighs[^12] are knit together.

**18** Its bones are tubes of bronze, its limbs like bars of iron.

**19** It is the first of the ways of God;[^13] only its Maker can bring his sword near it.[^14]

**20** For the mountains bring it their produce,[^15] where all the wild animals play.

**21** Under the lotus plants[^16] it lies, in the shelter of the reeds and the marsh.

**22** The lotus plants cover it with their shade; the willows of the wadi surround it.

**23** Look, if the river rages,[^17] it is not alarmed; it is confident, though the Jordan bursts against its mouth.

**24** Can anyone take it by its eyes, or pierce its nose with snares?[^18]

**25** Can you draw out Leviathan[^19] with a fishhook, or press down its tongue with a cord?

**26** Can you put a rope of rushes[^20] through its nose, or pierce its jaw with a hook?

**27** Will it plead with you again and again? Will it speak gentle words to you?

**28** Will it make a covenant with you, for you to take it as your servant for life?[^21]

**29** Will you play with it as with a bird, or put it on a leash for your young girls?

**30** Will traders[^22] bargain over it? Will they divide it up among the merchants?[^23]

**31** Can you fill its skin with harpoons, or its head with fishing spears?

**32** Lay your hand on it; remember the battle, and you will not do it again!

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^3]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "Is contending with the Almighty correcting? One who reproves God, let him answer it". Or "Will a faultfinder contend with the Almighty?"
[^4]: Literally "I am light", the opposite of "heavy", that is, weighty and honored.
[^5]: Yahweh repeats his words in 38:3.
[^6]: Hebrew *mishpat*: justice, judgment, the right in a case.
[^7]: The world of the dead.
[^8]: Or "acknowledge you".
[^9]: Hebrew *behemot*, the plural of *behemah*, "beast": "the beast" above all others, a huge land animal. Many identify it with the hippopotamus, which lives among the reeds and in the river (verses 21–23); others with the elephant, or with a monster of chaos, paired with Leviathan (40:25–41:26 (English 41:1–34)). Verses 15–24 describe it.
[^10]: Literally "which I made with you".
[^11]: Or "It bends its tail like a cedar". Hebrew *yachpots*, a word of uncertain meaning here. Some take *tail* as a euphemism.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "its thigh" but read "its thighs".
[^13]: Literally "the beginning of the ways of God": the chief of his works, or the first he made (compare Proverbs 8:22, said of wisdom).
[^14]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "the one who made it brings near his sword". Or "its Maker gave it its sword", that is, its tusks or teeth.
[^15]: Hebrew *bul*, "produce", perhaps "tribute".
[^16]: Hebrew *tse'elim*, a word found only here and in verse 22, probably the thorny lotus, a shrub of hot, wet places.
[^17]: Literally "oppresses". The Jordan in the next line may stand for any great river.
[^18]: Literally "In its eyes he will take it; with snares he will pierce the nose". The Hebrew has no word marking a question, but the verse is usually read as one.
[^19]: Verses 25–32 are 41:1–8 in most English Bibles. *Leviathan* is a great sea creature, pictured elsewhere as a monster of chaos that God defeated (3:8; Psalm 74:14; Isaiah 27:1; compare Psalm 104:26). Here it is a creature God made, described with features of the crocodile; some think of a whale. The description runs to the end of chapter 41.
[^20]: Hebrew *agmon*, a rope made of rushes.
[^21]: Or "slave". Literally "a servant forever", the term for a slave who chose to stay with his master for life (Deuteronomy 15:17).
[^22]: Hebrew *chabbarim*, "partners", perhaps a guild of fishermen or traders.
[^23]: Literally "among the Canaanites", who were known as traders.
