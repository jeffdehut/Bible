**1** Job took up his discourse again:[^1]

**2** "As surely as God lives, who has denied me justice,[^2] and the Almighty,[^3] who has made my soul bitter,

**3** as long as my breath is in me, and the breath of God[^4] is in my nostrils,

**4** my lips will not speak wickedness, and my tongue will not utter deceit.[^5]

**5** Far be it from me to declare that you are right![^6] Until I breathe my last, I will not give up my integrity.

**6** I hold fast to my righteousness and will not let it go; my heart does not reproach me for any of my days.

**7** May my enemy be like the wicked, and the one who rises against me like the unjust.

**8** For what hope does the godless have when he is cut off,[^7] when God takes away his life?[^8]

**9** Will God hear his cry when trouble comes upon him?

**10** Will he take delight in the Almighty? Will he call on God at all times?

**11** I will teach you about the power[^9] of God; I will not hide what is with the Almighty.

**12** Look, all of you have seen it yourselves. Why then do you talk such empty nonsense?[^10]

**13** This is the portion of a wicked person from God, the inheritance that the ruthless receive from the Almighty:[^11]

**14** If his children are many, it is for the sword, and his offspring will not have enough bread.

**15** Those who survive him[^12] will be buried by the plague,[^13] and his widows will not weep.[^14]

**16** Though he heaps up silver like dust and piles up clothing like clay,

**17** he may pile it up, but the righteous will wear it, and the innocent will divide the silver.

**18** He builds his house like a moth,[^15] like a hut that a watchman makes.

**19** He lies down rich, but will not do so again;[^16] he opens his eyes, and it is gone.[^17]

**20** Terrors overtake him like a flood;[^18] in the night a storm wind carries him off.

**21** The east wind lifts him up, and he is gone; it sweeps him out of his place.

**22** It hurls itself at him without pity;[^19] he flees headlong from its power.

**23** It claps its hands at him and hisses at him from its place.[^20]

[^1]: Literally "And Job again lifted up his *mashal* and said". Hebrew *mashal* usually means a proverb or a poem; here, a solemn speech. Also in 29:1. This new heading, at the point where Zophar would be expected to speak, is one reason some think the order of this third round of speeches has been disturbed (see the note at 25:1).
[^2]: Literally "who has taken away my justice". Hebrew *mishpat*: justice, the right owed to me in my case. *As surely as God lives* is the form of an oath (literally "God lives").
[^3]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. Also in verses 10, 11 and 13.
[^4]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verses 8 and 10. *Breath* is Hebrew *ruach*, "breath, spirit".
[^5]: Literally "if my lips speak wickedness, if my tongue utters deceit …", the form of a solemn oath: "may God punish me if I do".
[^6]: Literally "to declare you righteous". Here *you* is plural: Job speaks to the three friends.
[^7]: Hebrew *yivtsa*, "he cuts off" or "he makes gain". Or "however much he has gained".
[^8]: Literally "when God draws out his soul". Hebrew *yeshel*, a rare verb, perhaps "draws out" or "takes away".
[^9]: Literally "the hand".
[^10]: Literally "Why then do you become empty with emptiness?" Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor": what has no substance.
[^11]: Compare Zophar's words in 20:29. Some interpreters think verses 13–23 are Zophar's third speech, which is otherwise missing (see the note at 25:1). Others take them as Job turning his friends' teaching back on them, or as Job's own view of what the wicked deserve. In the Hebrew text they stand within Job's speech.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "his survivor" but read "his survivors".
[^13]: Literally "will be buried in death". *Death* here probably means plague (compare Jeremiah 15:2).
[^14]: Perhaps because no proper mourning rites can be held (compare Psalm 78:64).
[^15]: Hebrew *ash*, "moth", perhaps meaning its cocoon.
[^16]: Literally "he will not be gathered". The Greek Septuagint reads "he will not add (to it)", that is, he lies down rich for the last time.
[^17]: Literally "and he is not": either his wealth is gone, or he himself.
[^18]: Literally "like waters".
[^19]: Or "He (God) hurls at him without pity". The subject is not stated.
[^20]: Clapping the hands and hissing were gestures of scorn (Lamentations 2:15). The subject may be the wind, or people who see his fall: "People clap their hands at him and hiss at him from his place".
