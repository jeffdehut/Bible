**1** Then Job answered Yahweh:[^1]

**2** "I know[^2] that you can do all things, and that no purpose of yours can be thwarted.

**3** 'Who is this who hides counsel without knowledge?'[^3] So I have spoken of what I did not understand, things too wonderful for me, which I did not know.

**4** 'Listen, and I will speak; I will question you, and you will inform me.'[^4]

**5** I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you.

**6** Therefore I retract and repent in dust and ashes."[^5]

**7** After Yahweh had spoken these words to Job, Yahweh said to Eliphaz the Temanite, "My anger burns against you and against your two friends, for you have not spoken of me[^6] what is right, as my servant Job has.

**8** So now take seven bulls and seven rams, go to my servant Job, and offer a burnt offering for yourselves. My servant Job will pray for you, for I will accept his prayer[^7] and not deal with you as your folly deserves.[^8] For you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has."

**9** So Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Naamathite went and did as Yahweh had told them, and Yahweh accepted Job's prayer.[^9]

**10** When Job had prayed for his friends, Yahweh restored his fortunes,[^10] and Yahweh gave Job twice as much as he had before.

**11** Then all his brothers and sisters and all who had known him before came to him and ate a meal[^11] with him in his house. They showed him sympathy[^12] and comforted him for all the trouble that Yahweh had brought on him, and each of them gave him a *qesitah*[^13] and a gold ring.

**12** Yahweh blessed the latter part of Job's life more than the first. He had fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen and a thousand female donkeys.[^14]

**13** He also had seven sons[^15] and three daughters.

**14** He named the first Jemimah, the second Keziah, and the third Keren-happuch.[^16]

**15** In all the land no women were found as beautiful as Job's daughters, and their father gave them an inheritance along with their brothers.

**16** After this Job lived 140 years, and he saw his children and their children to the fourth generation.[^17]

**17** And Job died, old and full of days.[^18]

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "You know" but read "I know".
[^3]: Job quotes Yahweh's words in 38:2.
[^4]: Job takes up Yahweh's challenge in 38:3 and 40:7. Or these are Job's own words to Yahweh, not a quotation.
[^5]: The Hebrew of this verse is notoriously difficult, and each part can be read more than one way. *Retract* is Hebrew *em'as*, "I reject, I despise", with no object stated: "I despise myself" (the traditional rendering, "I abhor myself"), "I reject what I said", or, taking it from a similar verb, "I melt away". *Repent* is Hebrew *nichamti*, which can mean "I am sorry, I change my mind" or "I am comforted, I take comfort". *In dust and ashes* is literally "on" or "concerning dust and ashes": either Job sits in them as a mourner (2:8), or they stand for human frailty (30:19; Genesis 18:27). So the verse can also be read "I despise myself, and I repent in dust and ashes", "I retract, and I give up dust and ashes", that is, my mourning, or "I retract, and I am comforted, though I am dust and ashes".
[^6]: Or "to me". *What is right* is Hebrew *nekhonah*, "what is right, true, firm". Also in verse 8.
[^7]: Literally "I will lift up his face".
[^8]: Literally "so as not to do folly with you". Hebrew *nevalah*, "folly, disgrace", from the same root as the word Job used of his wife's words in 2:10.
[^9]: Literally "lifted up the face of Job".
[^10]: Literally "turned the turning of Job", an idiom for restoring someone's fortunes.
[^11]: Literally "ate bread".
[^12]: Literally "they shook the head for him" (see 2:11).
[^13]: A *qesitah* was a unit of money; its value is not known (Genesis 33:19).
[^14]: Exactly double the numbers in 1:3.
[^15]: Hebrew *shiv'anah*, an unusual form of "seven", which an ancient Jewish tradition takes to mean "twice seven", fourteen.
[^16]: *Jemimah* probably means "dove"; *Keziah*, "cassia", a fragrant spice (Psalm 45:9 (English 45:8)); and *Keren-happuch*, "horn of eye paint", a container for a cosmetic.
[^17]: Literally "his sons and the sons of his sons, four generations".
[^18]: That is, after a long and full life (compare Genesis 25:8; 35:29). The Greek Septuagint adds a longer ending: it says that Job will rise again with those whom the Lord raises up, and, drawing on a Syriac book, it identifies him with Jobab, a king of Edom (Genesis 36:33).
