**1** "I am sick of my life. I will give free rein to my complaint; I will speak in the bitterness of my soul.

**2** I will say to God:[^1] Do not condemn me; let me know why you contend with me.

**3** Does it seem good to you to oppress, to reject the work of your hands and smile on the plans of the wicked?[^2]

**4** Do you have eyes of flesh? Do you see as a mortal sees?

**5** Are your days like the days of a mortal, or your years like the years of a man,

**6** that you search for my guilt and seek out my sin,

**7** though you know that I am not guilty, and there is no one to rescue me from your hand?

**8** Your hands shaped me and made me, all of me, and now you destroy me.[^3]

**9** Remember that you made me like clay; will you turn me back into dust?

**10** Did you not pour me out like milk and curdle me like cheese?

**11** You clothed me with skin and flesh and wove me together with bones and sinews.

**12** You gave me life and faithful love,[^4] and your care has watched over my spirit.

**13** Yet these things you hid in your heart; I know that this was your purpose.[^5]

**14** If I sinned, you would watch me and would not acquit me of my guilt.

**15** If I am guilty, woe to me! And if I am in the right, I cannot lift my head, so filled with shame and looking at my misery.[^6]

**16** And if it is lifted up,[^7] you hunt me like a lion, and again you show your wondrous power against me.

**17** You bring new witnesses against me and increase your anger toward me; fresh troops come against me in waves.[^8]

**18** Why then did you bring me out of the womb? I should have perished before any eye saw me.

**19** I would have been as though I had never been, carried from the womb to the grave.

**20** Are not my days few? Stop! Leave me alone,[^9] so that I may have a little cheer

**21** before I go, never to return, to the land of darkness and deep darkness,[^10]

**22** a land of gloom like the blackest night, of deep darkness and disorder, where even the light is like the blackest night."

[^1]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Job speaks to God from here to the end of the chapter.
[^2]: Literally "shine on the counsel of the wicked".
[^3]: Literally "made me together all around, and you swallow me up".
[^4]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^5]: Literally "this is with you".
[^6]: Or "Look at my misery!" The Hebrew vowel points read "see" as a command. Some scholars read *reveh*, "soaked", for *re'eh*, "seeing", with a small change to the Hebrew: "soaked in my misery".
[^7]: That is, my head. Literally "it rises up proudly".
[^8]: Literally "changes and an army are with me". Hebrew *chalifot ve-tsava*: perhaps troops coming on in relays.
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "Let him stop! Let him leave me alone" but read "Stop! Leave me alone".
[^10]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death". Also in verse 22.
