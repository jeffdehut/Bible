**1** "Does not a human being have hard service[^1] on earth? Are not his days like the days of a hired worker?

**2** Like a slave who longs for the shade, like a hired worker who waits for his wages,

**3** so I have been given months of futility[^2] as my portion, and nights of misery have been assigned to me.

**4** When I lie down I say, 'When will I get up?' But the night drags on,[^3] and I toss and turn until dawn.

**5** My flesh is clothed with worms and crusts of dirt; my skin hardens, then breaks open again.[^4]

**6** My days are swifter than a weaver's shuttle, and they come to an end without hope.[^5]

**7** Remember that my life is a breath; my eye will never again see good.

**8** The eye that sees me will see me no more; your eyes will look for me, but I will be gone.

**9** As a cloud fades away and is gone, so one who goes down to Sheol[^6] does not come up.

**10** He will not return to his house again, and his place will not know him anymore.

**11** So I will not restrain my mouth. I will speak in the anguish of my spirit; I will complain in the bitterness of my soul.

**12** Am I the sea, or the sea monster,[^7] that you set a guard over me?

**13** When I say, 'My bed will comfort me, my couch will ease my complaint,'

**14** then you terrify me with dreams and frighten me with visions,

**15** so that I would choose strangling, death rather than these bones of mine.[^8]

**16** I have had enough;[^9] I will not live forever. Leave me alone, for my days are a breath.

**17** What is a human being, that you make so much of him, that you pay attention to him,[^10]

**18** that you examine him every morning and test him every moment?

**19** How long will you not look away from me, or leave me alone long enough to swallow my spit?

**20** If I have sinned, what have I done to you, you watcher of humankind? Why have you made me your target? Why have I become a burden to myself?[^11]

**21** Why do you not pardon my offense and take away my guilt? For now I will lie down in the dust; you will search for me, but I will be gone."

[^1]: Hebrew *tsava*, military service or forced labor.
[^2]: Or "emptiness", "worthlessness".
[^3]: The Hebrew of this phrase is uncertain. Literally "and the measuring of evening".
[^4]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Or "my skin cracks and oozes".
[^5]: The word for "hope" (*tiqvah*) can also mean "thread", continuing the picture of the weaver.
[^6]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^7]: Hebrew *yam*, "sea", and *tannin*, "sea monster": powers of chaos that God holds in check (Psalm 74:13; Isaiah 51:9).
[^8]: Literally "rather than my bones". Or "rather than my pains".
[^9]: Literally "I refuse" or "I reject", with no object stated: perhaps "I loathe my life".
[^10]: Literally "set your heart on him". Compare Psalm 8:5 (English 8:4), which asks this question in praise.
[^11]: An ancient Jewish scribal tradition counts this among places where the scribes changed the text out of reverence for God, from "a burden to you". The Greek Septuagint reads "a burden to you".
