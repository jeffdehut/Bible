**1** "A human being, born of woman, is short of days and full of turmoil.

**2** He comes up like a flower and withers; he flees like a shadow and does not last.

**3** Do you really fix your eyes on such a one? Do you bring me into judgment with you?

**4** Who can bring something clean out of something unclean? No one!

**5** Since his days are determined, and the number of his months is with you, and you have set him limits that he cannot pass,[^1]

**6** look away from him, and let him rest, so that he can enjoy his day like a hired worker.

**7** For there is hope for a tree: if it is cut down, it will sprout again, and its shoots will not fail.

**8** Though its root grows old in the earth and its stump dies in the dust,

**9** at the scent of water it will bud and put out branches like a young plant.

**10** But a man dies and lies powerless; a human being breathes his last, and where is he?

**11** As water vanishes from a lake[^2] and a river dries up and is parched,

**12** so a man lies down and does not rise. Until the heavens are no more, people will not wake, or be roused from their sleep.

**13** If only you would hide me in Sheol,[^3] conceal me until your anger is past, set me a fixed time, and then remember me!

**14** If a man dies, will he live again? All the days of my hard service I would wait, until my relief[^4] should come.

**15** You would call, and I would answer you; you would long for the work of your hands.

**16** For then you would count my steps, but not keep watch over my sin.[^5]

**17** My offense would be sealed up in a bag, and you would cover over my guilt.[^6]

**18** But as a mountain falls and crumbles away,[^7] and a rock is moved from its place,

**19** as water wears away stones and its torrents wash away the soil of the earth,[^8] so you destroy the hope of a mortal.

**20** You overpower him forever, and he passes away; you change his appearance and send him away.

**21** His children are honored, but he does not know it; they are brought low, but he does not notice them.

**22** He feels only the pain of his own body, and mourns only for himself."[^9]

[^1]: The Hebrew text is written "his limit" but read "his limits".
[^2]: Hebrew *yam*, "sea", used here of a lake or body of water.
[^3]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^4]: Hebrew *chalifah*, "change", as when soldiers on duty are relieved. *Hard service* is the word in 7:1.
[^5]: Or "For now you count my steps; do you not keep watch over my sin?"
[^6]: Literally "you would plaster over my guilt", the verb used of the friends in 13:4.
[^7]: Literally "a falling mountain withers". The Hebrew of this phrase is uncertain.
[^8]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "its outpourings wash away the dust of the earth".
[^9]: Literally "His flesh on him feels pain, and his soul on him mourns".
