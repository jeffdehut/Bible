**1** "Surely there is a mine for silver,[^1] and a place for the gold that people refine.

**2** Iron is taken from the dust, and copper[^2] is smelted from stone.

**3** People put an end to darkness;[^3] they search out the farthest limits, for the stone that lies in gloom and deep darkness.[^4]

**4** They open a shaft far from where anyone lives, in places forgotten by any foot; far from other people they dangle and sway.[^5]

**5** The earth, from which bread comes, is turned upside down beneath, as if by fire.[^6]

**6** Its stones are the place of sapphires,[^7] and it holds dust of gold.[^8]

**7** No bird of prey knows that path; the falcon's eye has not seen it.

**8** Proud beasts[^9] have not trodden it; no lion has passed over it.

**9** People put their hand to the flint and overturn mountains from their roots.

**10** They cut channels[^10] through the rocks, and their eyes see every precious thing.

**11** They dam up the sources of the rivers[^11] and bring hidden things out into the light.

**12** But wisdom, where can it be found? Where is the place of understanding?

**13** No mortal knows its worth;[^12] it is not found in the land of the living.

**14** The deep[^13] says, 'It is not in me,' and the sea says, 'It is not with me.'

**15** It cannot be bought with solid gold,[^14] and its price cannot be weighed out in silver.

**16** It cannot be valued in the gold of Ophir,[^15] in precious onyx[^16] or sapphire.

**17** Gold and glass[^17] cannot compare with it, nor can it be exchanged for vessels of fine gold.

**18** Coral and crystal are not worth mentioning; the price of wisdom is above pearls.[^18]

**19** The topaz of Cush[^19] cannot compare with it; it cannot be valued in pure gold.

**20** Where then does wisdom come from? Where is the place of understanding?

**21** It is hidden from the eyes of every living thing, concealed even from the birds of the sky.

**22** Abaddon[^20] and Death say, 'With our ears we have heard a rumor of it.'

**23** God understands the way to it; he knows its place.

**24** For he looks to the ends of the earth; he sees everything under the heavens.

**25** When he gave the wind its weight and measured out the waters,

**26** when he made a decree for the rain and a path for the thunderbolt,[^21]

**27** then he saw it and declared it;[^22] he established it and searched it out.

**28** And he said to humankind, 'Look, the fear of the Lord,[^23] that is wisdom, and to turn away from evil is understanding.'"[^24]

[^1]: Chapter 28 is a poem on wisdom. It has no heading of its own, so in the text as it stands it continues Job's speech from chapter 27 (compare 29:1). Its calm, reflective tone has led many interpreters to think of it as an independent poem, perhaps spoken by the narrator. *Mine* is literally "a place where it comes out" (Hebrew *motsa*).
[^2]: Or "bronze". Hebrew *nechushah*.
[^3]: Literally "He sets an end to darkness": the miner, who brings light into the dark.
[^4]: Hebrew *tsalmavet*, "deep darkness", traditionally read as "the shadow of death".
[^5]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "He breaks open a wadi away from the sojourner; the ones forgotten by the foot, they hang low; away from mortals they sway". It probably describes miners lowered on ropes into the shafts.
[^6]: Perhaps the fire used to crack rock in mining.
[^7]: Hebrew *sappir*, probably lapis lazuli, a deep blue stone flecked with gold. Also in verse 16.
[^8]: Or "and it (the sapphire) has flecks of gold".
[^9]: Literally "the sons of pride", probably the great wild animals (compare 41:26 (English 41:34)).
[^10]: Hebrew *ye'orim*, a word used of the channels of the Nile; here tunnels or water channels in a mine.
[^11]: The Hebrew vowel points read "From the weeping of the rivers he binds", that is, he stops the water from seeping into the mine. The same consonants can be read "the sources of the rivers" (compare "the springs of the sea", 38:16).
[^12]: Or "its place in order". The Greek Septuagint reads "its way".
[^13]: Hebrew *tehom*, the deep waters under the earth (Genesis 1:2).
[^14]: Hebrew *segor*, a rare word for gold, probably "pure" or "solid" gold. Verses 15–19 use several words for gold and precious stones, some of which cannot be identified with certainty.
[^15]: A region famous for its fine gold, perhaps in southern Arabia or East Africa (1 Kings 9:28).
[^16]: Hebrew *shoham*, a precious stone that cannot be identified with certainty.
[^17]: Hebrew *zekhukhit*, a word found only here. Glass was costly in the ancient world.
[^18]: Hebrew *ramot*, *gavish* and *peninim*, precious things that cannot be identified with certainty. *Peninim* may be red coral (Lamentations 4:7) or pearls. *Price* is literally "drawing out", perhaps the acquiring of it.
[^19]: Hebrew *pitdah*, a precious stone, perhaps topaz or chrysolite. *Cush* is usually the region south of Egypt (ancient Nubia).
[^20]: Hebrew *Abaddon*, "Destruction", a name for the realm of the dead (see 26:6).
[^21]: Literally "for the flash of the thunders".
[^22]: Or "appraised it", "counted it".
[^23]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "Lord", used of God only here in Job.
[^24]: The words echo the description of Job in 1:1: he "feared God and turned away from evil". Compare Proverbs 1:7; 9:10.
