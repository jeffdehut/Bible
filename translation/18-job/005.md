**1** "Call out now! Is there anyone who will answer you? To which of the holy ones[^1] will you turn?

**2** For resentment kills the fool, and jealousy slays the simple.

**3** I myself have seen a fool taking root, but suddenly I cursed his home.[^2]

**4** His children are far from safety; they are crushed in the gate,[^3] with no one to rescue them.

**5** The hungry eat his harvest and take it even from among the thorns, and the thirsty pant after their wealth.[^4]

**6** For trouble does not come out of the dust, and misery does not sprout from the ground.

**7** Rather, human beings are born to misery, as surely as sparks fly upward.[^5]

**8** But as for me, I would appeal to God, and to God I would commit my cause.[^6]

**9** He does great things beyond searching out, wonders beyond counting.

**10** He gives rain on the earth and sends water on the fields.

**11** He sets the lowly on high, and those who mourn are lifted to safety.

**12** He frustrates the plans of the crafty, so that their hands achieve no success.[^7]

**13** He catches the wise in their own craftiness,[^8] and the schemes of the devious are swept away.

**14** By day they run into darkness, and at noon they grope as if it were night.

**15** But he saves the needy from the sword of their mouth,[^9] and from the hand of the mighty.

**16** So the poor have hope, and injustice shuts its mouth.

**17** How happy is the one whom God[^10] corrects! So do not reject the discipline of the Almighty.[^11]

**18** For he wounds, but he binds up; he strikes, but his hands[^12] heal.

**19** He will rescue you from six troubles, and in seven no harm will touch you.[^13]

**20** In famine he will ransom you from death, and in war from the power of the sword.

**21** You will be hidden from the lash of the tongue, and you will not fear destruction when it comes.

**22** You will laugh at destruction and famine, and you will not fear the wild animals of the earth.

**23** For you will have a covenant with the stones of the field,[^14] and the wild animals of the field will be at peace with you.

**24** You will know that your tent is secure; you will inspect your home[^15] and find nothing missing.[^16]

**25** You will know that your descendants will be many, and your offspring like the grass of the earth.

**26** You will come to the grave in ripe old age,[^17] as sheaves of grain are brought up in their season.

**27** Look, we have searched this out; it is true. Hear it, and know it for yourself."

[^1]: Heavenly beings (compare 15:15; Psalm 89:6–8 (English 89:5–7)).
[^2]: Or "I declared his home cursed". The Hebrew word for "home" can also mean "pasture".
[^3]: The city gate, where legal cases were heard.
[^4]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. The middle line is literally "and to (or from) thorns he takes it". *The thirsty* reads Hebrew *tsammim* as a short spelling of *tsme'im*. Elsewhere *tsammim* means "snare" (18:9): "and a snare gapes for their wealth".
[^5]: Literally "and the sons of Resheph fly high". *Resheph* means "flame", and was also the name of a Canaanite god of plague. Or "as birds fly high". With a slight change of vowels, the first line can be read "human beings give birth to misery".
[^6]: The first line has *El*, the second *Elohim*, both "God".
[^7]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, "success" or "sound wisdom".
[^8]: 1 Corinthians 3:19 quotes this line.
[^9]: Literally "from the sword, from their mouth". Their words are pictured as a sword.
[^10]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job.
[^11]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "his hand" but read "his hands".
[^13]: A numerical saying: "six, and even seven" means every one of them.
[^14]: That is, stones will not spoil your fields.
[^15]: Or "your pasture", "your fold".
[^16]: Literally "and not miss", a verb that usually means "sin".
[^17]: Hebrew *kelach*, a rare word, perhaps "full vigor" or "ripe old age".
