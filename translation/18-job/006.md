**1** Then Job answered:

**2** "If only my anguish could be weighed, and my calamity laid on the scales with it!

**3** For then it would be heavier than the sand of the seas. That is why my words have been wild.[^1]

**4** For the arrows of the Almighty[^2] are in me; my spirit drinks their poison. The terrors of God[^3] are lined up against me.

**5** Does a wild donkey bray when it has grass, or an ox bellow over its fodder?

**6** Can tasteless food be eaten without salt? Is there any flavor in the white of an egg?[^4]

**7** I refuse to touch them; they are like loathsome food to me.[^5]

**8** If only my request would be granted, and God would give me what I hope for:

**9** that God would be willing to crush me, that he would let loose his hand and cut me off!

**10** Then I would still have this comfort, and I would leap for joy[^6] in pain that does not spare me: that I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

**11** What strength do I have, that I should wait? What end is ahead of me, that I should be patient?[^7]

**12** Is my strength the strength of stones? Is my flesh bronze?

**13** Is it not that I have no help in myself, and any resource[^8] has been driven from me?

**14** One who is wasting away should have faithful love[^9] from his friend, even if he forsakes the fear of the Almighty.[^10]

**15** My brothers have been as treacherous as a wadi,[^11] like the channels of wadis that run away,

**16** which are dark with ice, where the snow piles up and hides itself,

**17** but when they are scorched[^12] they cease; when it is hot, they vanish from their place.

**18** Caravans turn aside from their route;[^13] they go up into the wasteland and perish.

**19** The caravans of Tema look for water; the travelers of Sheba hope for it.[^14]

**20** They are put to shame because they trusted; they arrive there and are dismayed.

**21** For now you have become like that;[^15] you see something terrible and are afraid.

**22** Have I said, 'Give me something,' or 'Pay a bribe for me from your wealth,'

**23** or 'Rescue me from the hand of the enemy,' or 'Ransom me from the hand of the ruthless'?

**24** Teach me, and I will be silent; make me understand where I have gone wrong.

**25** How painful are honest words![^16] But what does your arguing prove?[^17]

**26** Do you mean to correct my words, and treat the words of a desperate man as wind?

**27** You would even cast lots over an orphan,[^18] and bargain over your friend.

**28** But now, please, look at me. Would I lie to your face?

**29** Turn back, please; let there be no injustice. Turn back again; my righteousness is still at stake.[^19]

**30** Is there injustice on my tongue? Can my palate not taste what is ruinous?[^20]

[^1]: Hebrew *la'u*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "wild", "rash" or "stammering".
[^2]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain. Also in verse 14.
[^3]: Hebrew *Eloah*, a poetic form of *Elohim*, "God", used often in Job. Also in verses 8 and 9.
[^4]: Hebrew *rir challamut*, a phrase of uncertain meaning: perhaps "the white of an egg" or "the slime of the mallow plant".
[^5]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "They are like the sickness of my bread".
[^6]: Hebrew *salad*, a verb found only here. Its meaning is uncertain: perhaps "leap for joy", or "recoil", "shudder".
[^7]: Literally "that I should lengthen my soul".
[^8]: Hebrew *tushiyyah*, "success, sound wisdom", as in 5:12.
[^9]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^10]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. Literally "To the one melting from his friend faithful love, and the fear of the Almighty he forsakes". Or "One who withholds faithful love from his friend forsakes the fear of the Almighty".
[^11]: A stream bed that runs with water in the rainy season and is dry in summer.
[^12]: Hebrew *zarav*, a verb found only here, perhaps "dry up", "flow away" or "be scorched".
[^13]: Or "The paths of their course twist and turn", of the streams themselves.
[^14]: *Tema* and *Sheba* were trading peoples of Arabia.
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "you have become nothing" (*lo*, "not") but read "you have become (like) it" (*lo*, "to it"), that is, like the dry wadi. The meaning of the read form is uncertain.
[^16]: Or "How forceful are honest words!"
[^17]: Literally "What does reproving from you reprove?"
[^18]: Literally "cause to fall over an orphan", probably lots for a child sold for debt.
[^19]: Literally "my righteousness is still in it".
[^20]: Hebrew *havvot*, "calamities, ruin", the word translated "calamity" in verse 2.
