**1** Gather yourselves together, gather together,[^1] you shameless nation,[^2]

**2** before the decree takes effect[^3] and the day blows past like chaff, before the burning anger of Yahweh[^4] comes on you, before the day of Yahweh's anger comes on you.

**3** Seek Yahweh, all you humble of the land, who do what he commands.[^5] Seek righteousness; seek humility. Perhaps you will be sheltered on the day of Yahweh's anger.

**4** For Gaza will be abandoned, and Ashkelon will become a desolation. Ashdod will be driven out at noon, and Ekron will be uprooted.[^6]

**5** Woe to[^7] you who live by the seacoast, you nation of the Cherethites![^8] The word of Yahweh is against you, Canaan, land of the Philistines: I will destroy you until no one is left living there.

**6** The seacoast will become pastures, with meadows[^9] for shepherds and pens for flocks.

**7** The coast will belong to the remnant of the house of Judah; they will graze their flocks there.[^10] In the houses of Ashkelon they will lie down in the evening, for Yahweh their God will care for them and restore their fortunes.[^11]

**8** I have heard the taunts of Moab and the insults of the Ammonites, how they taunted my people and made boasts against their territory.[^12]

**9** Therefore, as I live, declares Yahweh of Armies,[^13] the God of Israel, Moab will become like Sodom and the Ammonites like Gomorrah, a place overgrown with weeds,[^14] a salt pit and a desolation forever. The remnant of my people will plunder them, and the survivors of my nation[^15] will possess them.

**10** This is what they will get for their pride, because they taunted and made boasts against the people of Yahweh of Armies.

**11** Yahweh will be terrible against them, for he will starve[^16] all the gods of the earth. And all the coastlands of the nations[^17] will bow down to him, each in its own place.

**12** You too, Cushites,[^18] will be slain by my sword.[^19]

**13** And he will stretch out his hand against the north and destroy Assyria. He will make Nineveh a desolation, as dry as the desert.

**14** Flocks will lie down in her midst, all kinds of wild animals.[^20] Both the desert owl and the screech owl[^21] will roost on the tops of her pillars. A voice will hoot in the window; desolation[^22] will be on the threshold, for her cedar paneling has been stripped bare.

**15** This is the exultant city that lived in security, that said in her heart, "I am, and there is no one else."[^23] How she has become a desolation, a lair for wild animals! Everyone who passes by her hisses and shakes his fist.

[^1]: The meaning of the Hebrew verbs is uncertain. They may come from *qash*, "stubble": "gather yourselves like stubble".
[^2]: Literally "nation that is not ashamed". Or "nation that is not desired".
[^3]: Literally "before a decree gives birth". The Hebrew of the first half of the verse is difficult.
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "who have done his justice".
[^6]: Two plays on words: *Azzah azuvah*, "Gaza will be abandoned", and *Eqron te'aqer*, "Ekron will be uprooted". *At noon* may mean "in broad daylight" or "in half a day". Gath, the fifth Philistine city, is not named (compare Amos 1:6–8).
[^7]: Hebrew *hoy*, a cry of mourning for the dead ("Alas!", 1 Kings 13:30). The prophets use it to announce disaster, and in that sense it is translated "Woe to" throughout this book.
[^8]: A people closely linked with the Philistines, perhaps from Crete (1 Samuel 30:14; Ezekiel 25:16).
[^9]: Hebrew *kerot*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "meadows" or "caves". It sounds like *keretim*, "Cherethites" (verse 5).
[^10]: Literally "on them they will graze".
[^11]: Literally "turn their turning", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "bring back their captives".
[^12]: Or "and enlarged themselves at the expense of their territory".
[^13]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares Yahweh* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. *Yahweh of Armies* is also in verse 10.
[^14]: Literally "a possession of nettles". Moab and Ammon lay east of the Dead Sea, near the place where Sodom and Gomorrah once stood (Genesis 19:24–38).
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "the survivors of the nation" but read "the survivors of my nation".
[^16]: Or "make lean, waste away", perhaps by depriving the gods of the offerings of their worshipers.
[^17]: Hebrew *iyyim*, "coasts, islands", the far-off lands across the sea.
[^18]: The people of Cush, on the upper Nile, whose kings ruled Egypt until 663 BC. Cush may stand for Egypt here.
[^19]: Literally "they are the slain of my sword".
[^20]: Literally "all the animals of a nation". The meaning is uncertain.
[^21]: Hebrew *qa'at* and *qippod*, birds of uncertain kind (Isaiah 34:11).
[^22]: Hebrew *chorev*. The Septuagint reads "ravens".
[^23]: The words of a city that thinks it is like God. Babylon says the same in Isaiah 47:8, 10.
