**1** The word of the LORD[^1] that came to Zephaniah[^2] son of Cushi, son of Gedaliah, son of Amariah, son of Hezekiah,[^3] in the days of Josiah son of Amon king of Judah.[^4]

**2** I will sweep away everything, completely,[^5] from the face of the earth, declares the LORD.[^6]

**3** I will sweep away human beings and animals; I will sweep away the birds of the sky and the fish of the sea, and the things that make the wicked stumble.[^7] I will cut off humankind from the face of the earth,[^8] declares the LORD.

**4** I will stretch out my hand against Judah and against all who live in Jerusalem. I will cut off from this place what is left of Baal, and the name of the idol priests[^9] along with the priests,

**5** those who bow down on the rooftops to the host of heaven,[^10] and those who bow down and swear allegiance to the LORD but also swear by Milcom,[^11]

**6** and those who turn back from following the LORD, who do not seek the LORD or inquire of him.

**7** Be silent before the Lord GOD,[^12] for the day of the LORD is near. The LORD has prepared a sacrifice; he has consecrated his guests.[^13]

**8** On the day of the LORD's sacrifice I will punish the officials and the king's sons, and all who dress in foreign clothing.[^14]

**9** On that day I will punish everyone who leaps over the threshold,[^15] who fill their master's house[^16] with violence and deceit.

**10** On that day, declares the LORD, a cry will be heard from the Fish Gate, wailing from the Second Quarter,[^17] and a great crash from the hills.

**11** Wail, you who live in the Mortar,[^18] for all the traders[^19] are destroyed; all who weigh out silver are cut off.

**12** At that time I will search Jerusalem with lamps, and I will punish the people who sit complacent, like wine left on its dregs,[^20] who say in their hearts, "The LORD will do nothing, good or bad."

**13** Their wealth will be plundered and their houses made a desolation. They will build houses but not live in them; they will plant vineyards but not drink their wine.[^21]

**14** The great day of the LORD is near, near and coming very quickly. Listen! The day of the LORD! There the warrior cries out bitterly.[^22]

**15** That day is a day of wrath, a day of distress and anguish, a day of ruin and devastation,[^23] a day of darkness and gloom, a day of clouds and thick darkness,[^24]

**16** a day of the ram's horn[^25] and the battle cry against the fortified cities and against the high corner towers.

**17** I will bring such distress on humankind that they will walk like the blind, because they have sinned against the LORD. Their blood will be poured out like dust, and their flesh[^26] like dung.

**18** Neither their silver nor their gold will be able to save them on the day of the LORD's wrath. In the fire of his jealousy the whole earth[^27] will be consumed, for he will make an end, a sudden and terrible end, of all who live on the earth.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: The name means "The LORD has hidden" or "The LORD protects".
[^3]: Perhaps King Hezekiah of Judah (about 715–686 BC), though the text does not call him king.
[^4]: Josiah reigned about 640–609 BC. In 622 BC he began a reform that removed the worship of other gods from Judah (2 Kings 22–23), and verses 4–6 may come from the years before it.
[^5]: Hebrew *asof asef*, "I will surely gather", here in the sense of sweeping away. The words may also play on *suf*, "come to an end". Also in verse 3.
[^6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 3 and 10.
[^7]: Hebrew *ha-makhshelot*, "the stumbling blocks", perhaps idols. The meaning of the phrase is uncertain. The list of living things reverses the order of their creation in Genesis 1.
[^8]: A play on words: *adam*, "humankind", and *adamah*, "ground, earth", as in Genesis 2:7.
[^9]: Hebrew *kemarim*, a word used only of priests of other gods (2 Kings 23:5; Hosea 10:5).
[^10]: The sun, the moon and the stars, worshiped on flat roofs (2 Kings 23:5, 12; Jeremiah 19:13).
[^11]: The Hebrew is pointed *malkam*, "their king". The same consonants can be read *Milkom*, Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5; 2 Kings 23:13).
[^12]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. *Be silent* is Hebrew *has*, "Hush!", as in Habakkuk 2:20.
[^13]: Judah itself is the sacrifice, and the guests are the nations invited to share in it (Isaiah 34:6; Jeremiah 46:10).
[^14]: Clothing that marked those who followed foreign customs and the worship of other gods (compare 2 Kings 10:22).
[^15]: Perhaps a foreign custom of worship (1 Samuel 5:5), or rushing into houses to rob them.
[^16]: Or "the house of their lord", perhaps the king's palace or the temple of their god.
[^17]: The Fish Gate was in the north wall of Jerusalem (Nehemiah 3:3), and the Second Quarter, Hebrew *Mishneh*, a newer part of the city (2 Kings 22:14).
[^18]: Hebrew *makhtesh*, "mortar", a hollow, probably a market district in a valley of Jerusalem.
[^19]: Literally "the people of Canaan". *Canaanite* came to mean "trader" (Hosea 12:8, 12:7 in most English Bibles).
[^20]: Literally "who thicken on their dregs", like wine left too long on its sediment, which becomes thick and syrupy (Jeremiah 48:11).
[^21]: Compare Deuteronomy 28:30, 39; Amos 5:11.
[^22]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "The sound of the day of the LORD: bitter, crying out there is a warrior."
[^23]: Hebrew *sho'ah u-mesho'ah*, two words of similar sound.
[^24]: The same words are found in Joel 2:2. The Latin translation of verse 15, *Dies irae, dies illa*, "That day is a day of wrath", gave its name to a medieval hymn.
[^25]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^26]: Hebrew *lechum*, found elsewhere only in Job 20:23, probably "flesh" or "entrails".
[^27]: Or "the whole land". Also later in the verse.
