**1** Woe to[^1] the rebellious and defiled one, the oppressing city![^2]

**2** She does not listen to the voice; she does not accept correction. She does not trust in the LORD;[^3] she does not draw near to her God.

**3** Her officials within her are roaring lions; her judges are wolves at evening, who leave nothing until the morning.[^4]

**4** Her prophets are reckless, treacherous men. Her priests profane what is holy; they do violence to the law.[^5]

**5** The LORD within her is righteous; he does no wrong. Morning by morning he brings his justice to light; he never fails. But the unjust know no shame.

**6** I have cut off nations; their corner towers are desolate. I have laid waste their streets, so that no one walks in them. Their cities are ruined, with no one in them, no one living there.

**7** I said, "Surely you will fear me; you will accept correction."[^6] Then her dwelling would not be cut off, in spite of all the punishment I have brought on her.[^7] But they rose early to make all their deeds corrupt.

**8** Therefore wait for me, declares the LORD,[^8] for the day when I rise up as a witness.[^9] For my decision is to gather nations, to assemble kingdoms, to pour out on them my indignation, all my burning anger. In the fire of my jealousy the whole earth will be consumed.

**9** For then I will change the speech of the peoples to a pure speech,[^10] so that all of them may call on the name of the LORD and serve him shoulder to shoulder.[^11]

**10** From beyond the rivers of Cush,[^12] my worshipers, my scattered people,[^13] will bring me offerings.

**11** On that day you will not be put to shame for all the deeds by which you rebelled against me, for then I will remove from your midst those who exult in their pride, and you will never again be haughty on my holy mountain.

**12** But I will leave in your midst a people humble and poor, and they will take refuge in the name of the LORD.

**13** The remnant of Israel will do no wrong and tell no lies, and no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouths. They will graze and lie down, with no one to make them afraid.[^14]

**14** Sing aloud, Daughter Zion![^15] Shout, Israel! Rejoice and exult with all your heart, Daughter Jerusalem!

**15** The LORD has taken away the judgments against you;[^16] he has turned back your enemy. The King of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst; you will never again fear disaster.

**16** On that day it will be said to Jerusalem: "Do not be afraid, Zion; do not let your hands hang limp.

**17** The LORD your God is in your midst, a warrior who saves. He will rejoice over you with gladness; he will be quiet in his love;[^17] he will delight in you with singing."

**18** I will gather those who grieve for the appointed festivals, who were away from you, a burden on her and a disgrace.[^18]

**19** Look, at that time I will deal with all who oppress you. I will save the lame and gather the outcast,[^19] and I will give them praise and renown in every land where they were put to shame.

**20** At that time I will bring you home, at the time when I gather you. For I will give you renown and praise among all the peoples of the earth, when I restore your fortunes[^20] before your eyes, says the LORD.

[^1]: Hebrew *hoy*. See 2:5.
[^2]: Jerusalem (verses 2–4). *Rebellious* may also mean "filthy". *Oppressing* (*ha-yonah*) is the same in sound as "the dove", perhaps a play on words (compare Hosea 7:11).
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "they do not gnaw until the morning". The meaning is uncertain. Compare Habakkuk 1:8.
[^5]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching, law". Compare Ezekiel 22:26.
[^6]: *You* is Jerusalem. The Hebrew is feminine.
[^7]: Literally "all that I have appointed against her".
[^8]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement.
[^9]: The Hebrew is pointed *la'ad*, "for plunder". The same consonants can be read *le'ed*, "as a witness", as the Septuagint and the Syriac read.
[^10]: Literally "a purified lip". The Hebrew word *safah*, "lip", is the word for "language" in the story of Babel, where the one speech of humankind was confused (Genesis 11:1–9).
[^11]: Literally "with one shoulder".
[^12]: The land on the upper Nile, south of Egypt (2:12), at the edge of the known world.
[^13]: Literally "my suppliants, the daughter of my scattered ones". The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^14]: Like sheep in a safe pasture (Micah 4:4; 7:14). Revelation 14:5 echoes the words "no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouths".
[^15]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. So also *Daughter Jerusalem*.
[^16]: Literally "your judgments", the punishments decreed against Jerusalem.
[^17]: Or "he will quiet you by his love". The Septuagint reads "he will renew you in his love".
[^18]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "Those grieving from the appointed time I have gathered; from you they were; a burden on her, a disgrace."
[^19]: The Hebrew is feminine singular, picturing the people as a lame ewe of the flock, as in Micah 4:6–7.
[^20]: Literally "when I turn your turnings", an idiom for restoring what was lost. Or "when I bring back your captives".
