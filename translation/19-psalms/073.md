**1** A psalm of Asaph.[^1] Truly God is good to Israel, to those who are pure in heart.

**2** But as for me, my feet had almost stumbled; my steps had nearly slipped.

**3** For I envied the boastful, when I saw the prosperity[^2] of the wicked.

**4** For they have no pangs until their death;[^3] their bodies are healthy and well fed.

**5** They are not in trouble as others are;[^4] they are not struck as other people are.

**6** So pride is their necklace; violence covers them like a garment.

**7** Their eyes bulge from fatness;[^5] the imaginations of their hearts overflow.

**8** They mock and speak with malice; from on high they threaten oppression.

**9** They set their mouths against the heavens, and their tongues strut through the earth.

**10** So his people turn here,[^6] and drink up waters in abundance.[^7]

**11** And they say, "How does God know? Is there knowledge in the Most High?"[^8]

**12** See, these are the wicked; always at ease, they increase in wealth.

**13** Surely in vain I have kept my heart clean and washed my hands in innocence.

**14** For I have been struck all day long, and punished every morning.

**15** If I had said, "I will speak like this," I would have betrayed the generation of your children.

**16** But when I tried to understand this, it was troublesome in my eyes,

**17** until I went into the sanctuary of God;[^9] then I understood their end.

**18** Surely you set them on slippery ground; you make them fall into ruin.

**19** How suddenly they are destroyed, swept away completely by terrors!

**20** Like a dream when one awakes, Lord, when you rouse yourself,[^10] you will despise their image.[^11]

**21** When my heart was bitter, and I was pierced within,[^12]

**22** I was senseless and did not understand; I was like a beast[^13] before you.

**23** Yet I am always with you; you hold me by my right hand.

**24** You guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will take me into glory.[^14]

**25** Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth I desire besides you.[^15]

**26** My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the rock[^16] of my heart and my portion forever.

**27** For see, those who are far from you will perish; you destroy all who are unfaithful to you.[^17]

**28** But as for me, it is good to be near God;[^18] I have made the Lord GOD[^19] my refuge, so that I may tell of all your works.

[^1]: *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). Psalms 73–83 are all "of Asaph". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. Psalm 73 begins Book 3 of the Psalms (Psalms 73–89).
[^2]: Hebrew *shalom*: peace, well-being, prosperity.
[^3]: Literally "there are no pangs to their death". Some scholars divide the letters differently to read *lamo tam*, "they have no pangs; their bodies are sound", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^4]: Literally "In the trouble of a mortal they are not".
[^5]: Literally "Their eye goes out from fat". The Septuagint reads "Their iniquity goes out from fat".
[^6]: That is, perhaps, to the wicked and their way of thinking. The Hebrew text is written "So he brings back his people here" but read "So his people turn here".
[^7]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "and waters of fullness are drained by them".
[^8]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^9]: Literally "the sanctuaries of God".
[^10]: Or "in the city".
[^11]: Or "their phantom", "their shadow".
[^12]: Literally "in my kidneys". The kidneys were thought of as the seat of the emotions and the inner life.
[^13]: Hebrew *behemot*, "beasts", perhaps meaning "a great beast", "a brute".
[^14]: Or "and afterward you will receive me with honor", or "and lead me after glory". Compare 49:16 (49:15 in most English Bibles).
[^15]: Literally "and with you I desire nothing on earth".
[^16]: Literally "the rock of my heart".
[^17]: Literally "everyone who goes away from you as a prostitute", a common picture of unfaithfulness to God.
[^18]: Literally "the nearness of God is good for me".
[^19]: *The Lord GOD*: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". God's personal name, YHWH, is written GOD in capitals after "Lord", and "the LORD" elsewhere, as in most English Bibles.
