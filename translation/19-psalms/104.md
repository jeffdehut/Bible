**1** Bless the LORD,[^1] my soul![^2] LORD my God, you are very great; you are clothed with splendor and majesty.[^3]

**2** He wraps himself in light as in a robe; he stretches out the heavens like a tent curtain.[^4]

**3** He lays the beams of his upper rooms on the waters;[^5] he makes the clouds his chariot; he rides on the wings of the wind.

**4** He makes the winds his messengers, flames of fire his servants.[^6]

**5** He set the earth on its foundations, so that it will never be moved.

**6** You covered it with the deep[^7] as with a garment; the waters stood above the mountains.

**7** At your rebuke they fled; at the sound of your thunder they hurried away.

**8** They went up the mountains; they went down into the valleys,[^8] to the place you appointed for them.

**9** You set a boundary they may not pass, so that they will not return to cover the earth.[^9]

**10** He makes springs gush out into the valleys; they flow between the hills.

**11** They give drink to every wild animal; the wild donkeys quench their thirst.

**12** Beside them the birds of the sky have their homes; they sing among the branches.

**13** He waters the mountains from his upper rooms; the earth is satisfied with the fruit of your works.

**14** He makes grass grow for the cattle, and plants for people to cultivate,[^10] to bring forth food[^11] from the earth,

**15** and wine to gladden the human heart, oil to make faces shine, and bread to strengthen the human heart.

**16** The trees of the LORD drink their fill, the cedars of Lebanon that he planted,

**17** where the birds build their nests; the stork has her home in the junipers.

**18** The high mountains are for the wild goats; the crags are a refuge for the rock badgers.[^12]

**19** He made the moon to mark the seasons;[^13] the sun knows when to set.

**20** You bring darkness, and it is night, when all the animals of the forest creep about.

**21** The young lions roar for their prey, seeking their food from God.

**22** When the sun rises, they withdraw and lie down in their dens.

**23** People go out to their work, and to their labor until evening.

**24** How many are your works, LORD! In wisdom you have made them all; the earth is full of your creatures.

**25** There is the sea, great and wide,[^14] teeming with creatures beyond number, living things both small and great.

**26** There the ships go to and fro, and Leviathan,[^15] which you formed to play in it.[^16]

**27** All of them look to you to give them their food at the right time.

**28** When you give it to them, they gather it up; when you open your hand, they are filled with good things.

**29** When you hide your face, they are terrified; when you take away their breath, they die and return to their dust.[^17]

**30** When you send out your Spirit,[^18] they are created, and you renew the face of the ground.

**31** May the glory of the LORD endure forever; may the LORD rejoice in his works,

**32** he who looks at the earth, and it trembles, who touches the mountains, and they smoke.

**33** I will sing to the LORD as long as I live; I will sing praise to my God while I have being.

**34** May my meditation be pleasing to him; I rejoice in the LORD.

**35** Let sinners vanish from the earth, and let the wicked be no more. Bless the LORD, my soul! Praise the LORD![^19]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Also in verse 35. Psalm 104 has no title in the Hebrew. The Septuagint gives it the title "Of David". Like Psalm 103, it begins and ends with "Bless the LORD, my soul".
[^3]: Psalm 104 praises God as the maker and keeper of the world. It follows roughly the order of the creation account in Genesis 1: light and the heavens (verses 2–4), the earth and the waters (5–9), springs, plants and animals (10–18), the moon and the sun (19–23), and the sea and its creatures (25–26). Many scholars compare it with the Egyptian *Great Hymn to the Aten*, which has some similar images (compare especially verses 20–30). The psalm moves between speaking to God ("you") and speaking about him ("he").
[^4]: Hebrew *yeri'ah*, the word used for the curtains of the tabernacle (Exodus 26:1).
[^5]: The waters above the sky (Genesis 1:7).
[^6]: Or "He makes his messengers winds, and his servants flaming fire." Hebrew *mal'akh* means "messenger" or "angel", and *ruach* "wind" or "spirit". Hebrews 1:7 quotes this verse from the Septuagint in the second sense: "He makes his angels winds, and his servants flames of fire."
[^7]: Hebrew *tehom*, the deep waters of Genesis 1:2.
[^8]: Or "The mountains rose; the valleys sank down".
[^9]: Compare Job 38:8–11; Jeremiah 5:22.
[^10]: Literally "for the work of humankind", or "for humankind to use".
[^11]: Hebrew *lechem*, "bread", also food in general, as in verse 15.
[^12]: Hebrew *shephannim*, the rock hyrax, a small animal that lives among rocks (Leviticus 11:5; Proverbs 30:26).
[^13]: Hebrew *mo'adim*, "appointed times", including the festivals (compare Genesis 1:14).
[^14]: Literally "wide of hands", stretching out on every side.
[^15]: A great sea creature, pictured elsewhere as a monster of chaos (74:14; Job 3:8; 40:25, English 41:1; Isaiah 27:1). Here it is one of the creatures God has made.
[^16]: Or "to play with".
[^17]: Compare Genesis 3:19.
[^18]: Or "your breath". Hebrew *ruach* means spirit, wind or breath; in verse 29 it is "their breath" (*rucham*).
[^19]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH. This is the first time the call appears in the Psalms. The Septuagint places it at the start of Psalm 105 instead.
