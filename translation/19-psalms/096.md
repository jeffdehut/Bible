**1** Sing to the LORD[^1] a new song; sing to the LORD, all the earth.[^2]

**2** Sing to the LORD, bless his name; proclaim his salvation day after day.

**3** Declare his glory among the nations, his wonders among all the peoples.

**4** For the LORD is great and greatly to be praised; he is to be feared above all gods.

**5** For all the gods of the peoples are idols,[^3] but the LORD made the heavens.

**6** Splendor and majesty are before him; strength and beauty are in his sanctuary.[^4]

**7** Ascribe to the LORD, you families of the peoples, ascribe to the LORD glory and strength.

**8** Ascribe to the LORD the glory due his name;[^5] bring an offering and come into his courts.[^6]

**9** Worship the LORD in the splendor of holiness;[^7] tremble before him, all the earth.

**10** Say among the nations, "The LORD reigns![^8] The world is firmly established; it cannot be moved. He will judge the peoples with fairness."

**11** Let the heavens rejoice and the earth be glad; let the sea roar, and all that fills it;

**12** let the fields exult, and everything in them. Then all the trees of the forest will sing for joy

**13** before the LORD, for he comes, for he comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world with righteousness, and the peoples with his faithfulness.[^9]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Psalm 96 has no title in the Hebrew. The Septuagint gives it one: "When the house was being built after the captivity. A song of David."
[^2]: Most of this psalm appears also in 1 Chronicles 16:23–33, in the song of thanksgiving sung when David brought the ark to Jerusalem, with some differences in wording and order. The notes give the main differences. For *a new song*, compare 33:3 and 98:1. 1 Chronicles 16:23 has only "Sing to the LORD, all the earth", and then the second half of verse 2.
[^3]: Hebrew *elilim*, "worthless things", which sounds like *elohim*, "gods".
[^4]: 1 Chronicles 16:27 has "strength and joy are in his place".
[^5]: Literally "the glory of his name". Verses 7–9 are close to 29:1–2.
[^6]: 1 Chronicles 16:29 has "come before him".
[^7]: Or "in holy attire".
[^8]: Or "The LORD has become king!" (see the note on 93:1). In 1 Chronicles 16:31 these words come after the first half of verse 11, and the rest of this verse is not there, except for the line "The world is firmly established; it cannot be moved" (1 Chronicles 16:30).
[^9]: Hebrew *emunah*: firmness, reliability, faithfulness. 1 Chronicles 16:33 ends at "for he comes to judge the earth". Verse 13 is almost the same as 98:9.
