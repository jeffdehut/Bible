**1** For the director. Of David. A psalm. A song.[^1]

**2** Let God rise up, let his enemies be scattered; let those who hate him flee before him.[^2]

**3** As smoke is blown away, so blow them away; as wax melts before the fire, so let the wicked perish before God.

**4** But let the righteous be glad; let them rejoice before God; let them celebrate with joy.

**5** Sing to God, sing praises to his name; build up a road for the one who rides through the deserts.[^3] His name is Yah;[^4] rejoice before him.

**6** A father of the fatherless and a defender of widows is God in his holy dwelling.

**7** God gives the lonely a home to live in; he leads prisoners out into prosperity,[^5] but the rebellious live in a parched land.

**8** God, when you went out before your people, when you marched through the wasteland, Selah,[^6]

**9** the earth shook, the heavens poured down rain before God, the One of Sinai,[^7] before God, the God of Israel.

**10** You poured out abundant rain, God; when your inheritance was weary, you restored it.

**11** Your people[^8] settled in it; in your goodness, God, you provided for the afflicted.

**12** The Lord gives the word; the women who bring the good news are a great army:[^9]

**13** "The kings of armies flee, they flee!" And the woman who stays at home[^10] divides the plunder.

**14** Though you lie down among the sheepfolds,[^11] the wings of a dove are covered with silver, its feathers with shining gold.

**15** When the Almighty[^12] scattered kings there, it snowed on Zalmon.[^13]

**16** A mountain of God[^14] is the mountain of Bashan; a mountain of many peaks is the mountain of Bashan.

**17** Why do you look with envy,[^15] you mountains of many peaks, at the mountain God desired for his dwelling? Yes, Yahweh[^16] will dwell there forever.

**18** The chariots of God are twice ten thousand, thousands upon thousands; the Lord is among them, Sinai is in the holy place.[^17]

**19** You went up to the heights; you led captives away; you received gifts from people,[^18] even from the rebellious, so that Yah God might dwell there.

**20** Blessed be the Lord, who day after day bears our burden,[^19] the God who is our salvation. Selah.

**21** Our God is a God who saves,[^20] and to Yahweh the Lord belong the ways of escape from death.[^21]

**22** Surely God will crush the heads of his enemies, the hairy crown of the one who walks in his guilt.

**23** The Lord said, "I will bring them back from Bashan; I will bring them back from the depths of the sea,

**24** so that your foot may crush them in blood,[^22] and the tongues of your dogs may have their share of the enemies."[^23]

**25** Your processions have been seen, God, the processions of my God, my King, into the sanctuary.[^24]

**26** The singers go in front, the musicians last, and between them young women playing tambourines.

**27** In the assemblies bless God, Yahweh, you who come from the spring of Israel.[^25]

**28** There is little Benjamin, leading them,[^26] the princes of Judah in their throng,[^27] the princes of Zebulun, the princes of Naphtali.

**29** Your God has commanded your strength.[^28] Show your strength, God, you who have acted for us.

**30** Because of your temple at Jerusalem, kings will bring you gifts.

**31** Rebuke the beast among the reeds,[^29] the herd of bulls with the calves of the peoples, until it bows down with pieces of silver. He has scattered the peoples who delight in war.[^30]

**32** Envoys[^31] will come from Egypt; Cush[^32] will quickly stretch out its hands to God.

**33** Kingdoms of the earth, sing to God; sing praises to the Lord. Selah.

**34** to the one who rides in the heavens, the ancient heavens. Listen, he utters his voice, a mighty voice.

**35** Ascribe power to God, whose majesty is over Israel, and whose power is in the skies.

**36** You are awesome, God, from your sanctuary.[^33] The God of Israel is the one who gives strength and power to his people. Blessed be God!

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–36 are 68:1–35 in most English Bibles. This psalm is one of the most difficult in the Psalter. Many of its words are rare, some lines seem to be quotations from older poems, and the connection between its sections is often unclear. The notes mark the most uncertain lines.
[^2]: Compare Numbers 10:35, the words Moses spoke whenever the ark set out.
[^3]: Literally "cast up (a highway) for the one who rides in the *aravot*", the desert plains. Some take *aravot* as "clouds", comparing verse 34 and a title of the god Baal in Canaanite poetry, "rider of the clouds". The verb *sollu* may also mean "lift up (a song)".
[^4]: Hebrew *Yah*, a short form of the name Yahweh, as in *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah". Also in verse 19.
[^5]: Hebrew *kosharot*, found only here, perhaps "prosperity" or "singing".
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verses 20 and 33. Verses 8–9 are close to Judges 5:4–5.
[^7]: Literally "this Sinai", perhaps an old way of saying "the One of Sinai" (compare Judges 5:5).
[^8]: Literally "Your living things", perhaps "your flock" or "your community".
[^9]: Or "The Lord gives the word; great is the army of women who bring the news".
[^10]: Or "the beauty of the house". The Hebrew of verses 13–15 is very difficult, and their connection with the context is uncertain.
[^11]: Or "among the campfires", "between the saddlebags". The word is close to the one in Judges 5:16.
[^12]: Hebrew *Shaddai*. The meaning of the name is uncertain.
[^13]: A mountain, perhaps the one near Shechem (Judges 9:48), or one in Bashan. *Zalmon* is related to *tselem*, "shadow, darkness".
[^14]: Or "A mighty mountain", as "mountains of God" can mean mighty mountains (compare 36:7, English 36:6).
[^15]: Hebrew *teratsedun*, found only here; the meaning is uncertain.
[^16]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^17]: Literally "the Lord among them, Sinai, in holiness". Some scholars read "the Lord has come from Sinai into the holy place", with a small change to the Hebrew (compare Deuteronomy 33:2).
[^18]: Literally "among *adam* (humankind)". Ephesians 4:8 quotes this verse in a form that reads "he gave gifts to people".
[^19]: Or "who carries us".
[^20]: Literally "God is for us a God of acts of salvation".
[^21]: Literally "to death the goings out".
[^22]: Literally "so that you may crush your foot in blood". The Septuagint reads "so that your foot may be dipped in blood" (compare 58:11).
[^23]: Literally "the tongue of your dogs, from the enemies its portion".
[^24]: Or "in holiness".
[^25]: That is, you descendants of Israel.
[^26]: Or "ruling them".
[^27]: Hebrew *rigmatam*, found only here, perhaps "their crowd".
[^28]: The Septuagint reads "Command your strength, God".
[^29]: Probably Egypt, pictured as a crocodile or hippopotamus among the reeds of the Nile.
[^30]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "...with calves of peoples, trampling himself with pieces of silver; he scattered peoples who delight in wars".
[^31]: Hebrew *chashmannim*, found only here; the meaning is uncertain, perhaps "envoys", "nobles" or "bronze".
[^32]: The lands on the upper Nile, south of Egypt.
[^33]: Literally "from your sanctuaries".
