**1** Give thanks to the LORD;[^1] call on his name. Make known his deeds among the peoples.[^2]

**2** Sing to him, sing praises to him; tell of all his wonders.

**3** Glory in his holy name; let the hearts of those who seek the LORD rejoice.

**4** Seek the LORD and his strength; seek his face[^3] always.

**5** Remember the wonders he has done, his miracles and the judgments he has spoken,[^4]

**6** you offspring of Abraham his servant,[^5] children of Jacob, his chosen ones.

**7** He is the LORD our God; his judgments are in all the earth.

**8** He remembers his covenant forever,[^6] the word he commanded for a thousand generations,

**9** the covenant he made with Abraham, his oath to Isaac.

**10** He confirmed it to Jacob as a decree, to Israel as an everlasting covenant,

**11** saying, "To you I will give the land of Canaan as the portion of your inheritance."[^7]

**12** When they were few in number,[^8] very few, and foreigners in the land,

**13** they wandered from nation to nation, from one kingdom to another people.

**14** He allowed no one to oppress them; he rebuked kings on their account:

**15** "Do not touch my anointed ones;[^9] do not harm my prophets."

**16** He called down a famine on the land; he broke every staff of bread.[^10]

**17** He sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave.

**18** They hurt his foot[^11] with shackles; his neck[^12] was put in iron,

**19** until the time that his word came true; the word of the LORD tested him.[^13]

**20** The king sent and released him; the ruler of peoples set him free.

**21** He made him lord of his household, and ruler over all his possessions,

**22** to bind his princes as he pleased,[^14] and to teach his elders wisdom.

**23** Then Israel came to Egypt; Jacob lived as a foreigner in the land of Ham.[^15]

**24** The LORD made his people very fruitful, and made them stronger than their adversaries.

**25** He turned their hearts to hate his people, to deal craftily with his servants.

**26** He sent Moses his servant, and Aaron, whom he had chosen.

**27** They performed his signs among them,[^16] and wonders in the land of Ham.

**28** He sent darkness and made it dark, and they did not rebel against his word.[^17]

**29** He turned their waters into blood, and killed their fish.

**30** Their land swarmed with frogs, even in the chambers of their kings.

**31** He spoke, and there came swarms of flies,[^18] and gnats throughout their country.

**32** He gave them hail for rain, and flaming fire throughout their land.

**33** He struck down their vines and their fig trees, and shattered the trees of their country.

**34** He spoke, and the locusts came, and young locusts without number.

**35** They devoured all the plants in their land, and devoured the fruit of their ground.

**36** He struck down all the firstborn in their land, the first fruit of all their strength.[^19]

**37** He brought them out with silver and gold, and among his tribes no one stumbled.

**38** Egypt was glad when they left, for dread of them had fallen on the Egyptians.

**39** He spread out a cloud as a covering, and fire to give light by night.

**40** They asked,[^20] and he brought quail, and satisfied them with the bread of heaven.[^21]

**41** He opened the rock, and water gushed out; it flowed through the desert like a river.

**42** For he remembered his holy word to Abraham his servant.[^22]

**43** He brought out his people with joy, his chosen ones with shouts of joy.

**44** He gave them the lands of the nations, and they took possession of what the peoples had toiled for,

**45** so that they would keep his decrees and observe his laws. Praise the LORD![^23]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Psalm 105 has no title in the Hebrew.
[^2]: Verses 1–15 appear also in 1 Chronicles 16:8–22, in the song of thanksgiving sung when David brought the ark to Jerusalem, with small differences. The psalm then retells the story of Israel from Abraham to the entry into the land, as the keeping of God's promise to Abraham (verses 8–11, 42).
[^3]: Or "his presence".
[^4]: Literally "the judgments of his mouth".
[^5]: 1 Chronicles 16:13 has "Israel his servant".
[^6]: 1 Chronicles 16:15 has "Remember his covenant forever".
[^7]: Literally "the cord of your inheritance", the measuring line used to mark out a share of land.
[^8]: 1 Chronicles 16:19 has "When you were few in number".
[^9]: Hebrew *meshichai*, "my anointed ones", from *mashiach*. Here it refers to the patriarchs, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
[^10]: That is, the supply of food that people depend on, as one leans on a staff. The story of Joseph that follows is told in Genesis 37–50.
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "his feet" but read "his foot".
[^12]: Hebrew *nephesh*, here perhaps "neck" or "throat" (compare 69:2, 69:1 in most English Bibles), or "he himself".
[^13]: *His word* may be Joseph's word, his reading of the dreams (Genesis 40–41), or God's word. *Tested*: or "refined", as metal is refined.
[^14]: Literally "by his *nephesh*". The Septuagint reads "to instruct his princes", from a verb that differs by one letter.
[^15]: That is, Egypt (compare Genesis 10:6, where Ham is the ancestor of Egypt).
[^16]: Literally "They set among them the words of his signs". The plagues in verses 28–36 follow the account of Exodus 7–12, but in a different order, and without the plagues on livestock and of boils.
[^17]: The Hebrew text is written "his words" but read "his word". *They* are probably Moses and Aaron. The Septuagint reads "and they rebelled against his words", without "not".
[^18]: Hebrew *arov*, "swarm" or "mixture" (see Exodus 8:17, 8:21 in most English Bibles). *Gnats*: Hebrew *kinnim* (Exodus 8:12–14, 8:16–18 in most English Bibles).
[^19]: Compare Genesis 49:3.
[^20]: Literally "He asked", that is, the people. The events are told in Exodus 16 and Numbers 11:31–34.
[^21]: The manna (Exodus 16:4; compare 78:24).
[^22]: Compare Genesis 15:13–16.
[^23]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah" (see the note on 104:35).
