**1** For the director. According to Jeduthun.[^1] Of Asaph. A psalm.[^2]

**2** My voice goes up to God, and I cry out; my voice goes up to God, and he will listen to me.[^3]

**3** In the day of my trouble I seek the Lord; at night my hand is stretched out without growing weary;[^4] my soul[^5] refuses to be comforted.

**4** I remember God, and I groan; I meditate, and my spirit grows faint. Selah.[^6]

**5** You hold my eyelids open;[^7] I am so troubled that I cannot speak.

**6** I think about the days of old, the years long past.

**7** I remember my song in the night; I meditate in my heart, and my spirit searches:

**8** "Will the Lord reject forever? Will he never again show favor?

**9** Has his faithful love[^8] ceased forever? Has his promise[^9] come to an end for all generations?

**10** Has God forgotten to be gracious? Has he in anger shut up his compassion?" Selah.

**11** Then I said, "This is my grief: that the right hand of the Most High has changed."[^10]

**12** I will remember[^11] the deeds of Yah;[^12] yes, I will remember your wonders of old.

**13** I will meditate on all your work, and ponder your mighty deeds.

**14** God, your way is in holiness.[^13] What god is great like God?

**15** You are the God who works wonders; you have made known your strength among the peoples.

**16** With your arm you redeemed your people, the descendants of Jacob and Joseph. Selah.

**17** The waters saw you, God, the waters saw you and writhed; the depths too trembled.

**18** The clouds poured down water; the skies thundered;[^14] your arrows flashed back and forth.

**19** The sound of your thunder was in the whirlwind;[^15] lightning lit up the world; the earth trembled and shook.

**20** Your way was through the sea, your path[^16] through the mighty waters, yet your footprints were not seen.

**21** You led your people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron.

[^1]: Hebrew *al-yedutun*. The Hebrew text is written "Jedithun" but read "Jeduthun". Jeduthun was one of David's chief musicians (see the note on the title of Psalm 62).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–21 are 77:1–20 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "My voice to God, and I cry out; my voice to God, and listen to me".
[^4]: Literally "my hand at night was poured out and did not grow numb", that is, stretched out in prayer.
[^5]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verses 10 and 16.
[^7]: Literally "You have held the guards of my eyes".
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^9]: Literally "word".
[^10]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "My sickness, it is the changing of the right hand of the Most High". *Shenot*, "changing", can also mean "years": "the years of the right hand of the Most High". *Most High*: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "I will make mention of" but read "I will remember".
[^12]: Hebrew *Yah*, a short form of the name Yahweh, as in *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah".
[^13]: Or "in the holy place".
[^14]: Literally "gave a voice".
[^15]: Hebrew *galgal*, "wheel" or "whirlwind".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "your paths" but read "your path".
