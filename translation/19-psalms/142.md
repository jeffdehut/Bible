**1** A maskil of David, when he was in the cave. A prayer.[^1]

**2** With my voice I cry out to Yahweh;[^2] with my voice I plead with Yahweh for mercy.

**3** I pour out my complaint before him; I tell him of my trouble.

**4** When my spirit grows faint within me, you know my way. In the path where I walk they have hidden a trap for me.

**5** Look to my right and see: there is no one who takes notice of me.[^3] I have no refuge;[^4] no one cares for my life.[^5]

**6** I cry out to you, Yahweh; I say, "You are my refuge, my portion in the land of the living."

**7** Listen to my cry, for I am brought very low. Rescue me from my persecutors, for they are too strong for me.

**8** Bring me out of prison,[^6] so that I may give thanks to your name. The righteous will gather around me,[^7] for you will deal generously with me.

[^1]: *Maskil*: a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "a skillful psalm" or "a psalm of instruction". *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". *In the cave*: David hid from Saul in the cave of Adullam (1 Samuel 22:1) and in a cave near En-gedi (1 Samuel 24:4, 24:3 in most English Bibles); compare the title of Psalm 57. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–8 are 142:1–7 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: An advocate or protector stood at a person's right hand (compare 16:8; 109:31; 110:5).
[^4]: Literally "Refuge has perished from me".
[^5]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Also in verse 8.
[^6]: Literally "Bring my soul (*nephesh*) out of prison".
[^7]: Or "will put a crown on me". Hebrew *yakhtiru*, from the word for "crown".
