**1** Praise the LORD![^1] Praise God in his sanctuary;[^2] praise him in the mighty dome of the sky![^3]

**2** Praise him for his mighty deeds; praise him for his surpassing greatness![^4]

**3** Praise him with the blast of the ram's horn;[^5] praise him with harp and lyre!

**4** Praise him with tambourine and dancing; praise him with strings and pipe!

**5** Praise him with clashing cymbals; praise him with resounding cymbals![^6]

**6** Let everything that has breath praise the LORD! Praise the LORD![^7]

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH. Also at the end of verse 6.
[^2]: Or "in his holiness". Hebrew *qodesh*, "holiness, holy place": the temple on earth, or God's dwelling in heaven.
[^3]: Literally "in the dome of his strength". Hebrew *raqia*, the sky pictured as a solid vault (Genesis 1:6–8; see 19:2).
[^4]: Literally "according to the abundance of his greatness".
[^5]: Hebrew *shofar*.
[^6]: Literally "cymbals of sound" and "cymbals of loud shouting": perhaps two kinds of cymbals, or two ways of sounding them.
[^7]: Psalm 150 closes Book 5 of the Psalms (Psalms 107–150) and the whole book. Each of the other four books ends with a blessing (41:14; 72:18–19; 89:53; 106:48; 41:13 and 89:52 in most English Bibles); here the whole psalm is the closing call to praise, and the last five psalms (146–150) each begin and end with "Praise Yah". The Septuagint adds a short Psalm 151, spoken by David about his victory over Goliath, and a longer Hebrew form of it is found in a Dead Sea Scroll (11QPs-a).
