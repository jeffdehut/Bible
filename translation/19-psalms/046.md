**1** For the director. Of the sons of Korah. According to Alamoth.[^1] A song.[^2]

**2** God is our refuge and strength, a help in trouble, always ready to be found.[^3]

**3** So we will not fear, though the earth gives way, though the mountains topple into the heart of the seas,

**4** though its waters roar and foam, and the mountains quake at its surging. Selah.[^4]

**5** There is a river whose streams make glad the city of God, the holy dwelling place of the Most High.[^5]

**6** God is in her midst; she will not be shaken. God will help her at the break of day.[^6]

**7** Nations roar, kingdoms totter; he raises his voice, and the earth melts.

**8** The LORD of Armies[^7] is with us; the God of Jacob is our fortress. Selah.

**9** Come, see the works of the LORD, the desolations[^8] he has brought on the earth.

**10** He makes wars cease to the end of the earth; he breaks the bow and snaps the spear; he burns the chariots[^9] with fire.

**11** Be still,[^10] and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations; I will be exalted in the earth.

**12** The LORD of Armies is with us; the God of Jacob is our fortress. Selah.

[^1]: Hebrew *al-alamot*, a musical term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "for soprano voices" (compare 1 Chronicles 15:20).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of the sons of Korah*: or "for the sons of Korah", a family of Levites who served as temple singers. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–12 are 46:1–11 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "a help in troubles, found very much".
[^4]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verses 8 and 12.
[^5]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God. Literally "the holy place of the dwellings of the Most High".
[^6]: Literally "at the turning of the morning".
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verse 12.
[^8]: Or "the astonishing deeds".
[^9]: Hebrew *agalot*, "carts, wagons". The Septuagint reads "shields".
[^10]: Or "Stop!", "Let go!"
