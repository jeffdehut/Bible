**1** A song of ascents.[^1] "Often they have attacked me from my youth," let Israel now say,

**2** "often they have attacked me from my youth, yet they have not overcome me.

**3** The plowers plowed over my back; they made their furrows long."[^2]

**4** Yahweh[^3] is righteous; he has cut the cords of the wicked.[^4]

**5** May all who hate Zion be put to shame and turned back.

**6** May they be like grass on the rooftops, which withers before it grows up,[^5]

**7** with which the reaper does not fill his hand, nor the binder of sheaves his arms,[^6]

**8** and those who pass by do not say, "The blessing of Yahweh be on you! We bless you in the name of Yahweh."[^7]

[^1]: *A song of ascents*: Hebrew *shir ha-ma'alot* (see the note on 120:1). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: That is, they cut deep wounds in me, like a plow cutting furrows, perhaps by flogging.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Perhaps the ropes that harnessed the plow to the ox, or the cords that bound Israel.
[^5]: Or "before it is pulled up". Grass that sprouted in the thin soil of a flat roof soon dried up in the sun.
[^6]: Literally "his bosom", the fold of the garment where an armful of grain was carried.
[^7]: Passersby greeted reapers with a blessing (compare Ruth 2:4). The last words may be the psalm's own closing blessing.
