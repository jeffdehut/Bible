**1** For the director. Of David. A psalm.[^1] God of my praise,[^2] do not be silent!

**2** For the mouth of the wicked and the mouth of the deceitful are opened against me; they speak against me with a lying tongue.

**3** They surround me with words of hatred, and attack me without cause.

**4** In return for my love they accuse me,[^3] but I am in prayer.[^4]

**5** They repay me evil for good, and hatred for my love.

**6** Appoint a wicked man against him; let an accuser[^5] stand at his right hand.[^6]

**7** When he is judged, let him come out guilty,[^7] and let his prayer be counted as sin.

**8** May his days be few; may another take his office.[^8]

**9** May his children be fatherless, and his wife a widow.

**10** May his children wander about and beg, searching for food far from their ruined homes.[^9]

**11** May the creditor seize all that he has; may strangers plunder the fruit of his labor.

**12** May there be no one to show him faithful love,[^10] and no one to have pity on his fatherless children.

**13** May his descendants be cut off; may their name be blotted out in the next generation.

**14** May the iniquity of his ancestors be remembered before the LORD,[^11] and do not let the sin of his mother be blotted out.

**15** Let them be before the LORD continually, and may he cut off their memory from the earth,

**16** because he did not remember to show faithful love, but hounded the afflicted and needy man, and the brokenhearted, to put them to death.

**17** He loved cursing, and it has come on him; he took no pleasure in blessing, and it has stayed far from him.[^12]

**18** He clothed himself with cursing as with his robe; it has soaked into his body[^13] like water, and into his bones like oil.

**19** May it be to him like a garment he wraps around himself, like a belt he always wears.

**20** May this be the reward of my accusers from the LORD, of those who speak evil against my life.[^14]

**21** But you, GOD my Lord,[^15] deal with me for the sake of your name; because your faithful love is good, rescue me.

**22** For I am afflicted and needy, and my heart is pierced within me.

**23** I fade away like a lengthening shadow; I am shaken off like a locust.

**24** My knees give way from fasting; my body has grown thin and gaunt.[^16]

**25** I have become a disgrace to them; when they see me, they shake their heads.[^17]

**26** Help me, LORD my God; save me according to your faithful love.

**27** Let them know that this is your hand, that you, LORD, have done it.

**28** Let them curse, but you will bless. When they rise up, let them be put to shame, and let your servant rejoice.

**29** May my accusers be clothed with disgrace, and wrapped in their own shame as in a cloak.

**30** With my mouth I will give great thanks to the LORD; I will praise him in the midst of the crowd.

**31** For he stands at the right hand of the needy, to save him from those who would condemn him to death.[^18]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: That is, the God whom I praise.
[^3]: Hebrew *yistenuni*, from the same root as *satan*, "accuser" (verse 6). Also in verses 20 and 29, "my accusers".
[^4]: Literally "but I, prayer". Or "but I pray for them".
[^5]: Hebrew *satan*, "adversary, accuser", here without the article, as in a court of law. The accuser stood at the right hand of the accused (compare Zechariah 3:1). The Septuagint reads "the devil".
[^6]: Verses 6–19 contain some of the harshest curses in the Bible. They speak of one man ("him"), while the enemies in verses 2–5 and 20–29 are many. Many interpreters read verses 6–19 as the psalmist's own prayer against the leader of his enemies. Others read them as the curses his enemies speak against him (compare "they speak against me", verse 2, and "those who speak evil against my life", verse 20), which he quotes and then hands over to God. On that reading, verse 20 means "This is what my accusers seek from the LORD". The Hebrew has no quotation marks, and either reading is possible. This translation does not put them in quotation marks.
[^7]: Literally "let him go out wicked", that is, condemned.
[^8]: Hebrew *pequddah*, "charge, office". Acts 1:20 quotes this line from the Septuagint, with 69:26 (69:25 in most English Bibles), and applies it to Judas.
[^9]: Literally "and search from their ruins".
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verses 16, 21 and 26.
[^11]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^12]: Or "so let it come on him… so let it be far from him". The Hebrew verbs in verses 17–18 are in the past tense, but the context is a curse.
[^13]: Literally "into his inner parts".
[^14]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Also in verse 31. On the meaning of this verse, see the note on verse 6.
[^15]: Hebrew *YHWH Adonai*, the reverse of the more common *Adonai YHWH*, "the Lord GOD". Next to "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^16]: Literally "my flesh has grown lean, without fat".
[^17]: Compare 22:8 (22:7 in most English Bibles) and Mark 15:29.
[^18]: Literally "from those who judge his soul (*nephesh*)".
