**1** For the director. A psalm of David. A song.[^1]

**2** Praise waits for you in silence, God, in Zion,[^2] and to you vows will be fulfilled.

**3** You who hear prayer, to you all flesh will come.

**4** When iniquities[^3] overwhelm me, you atone for our transgressions.[^4]

**5** Happy[^5] is the one you choose and bring near to live in your courts! We will be satisfied with the good things of your house, your holy temple.

**6** With awesome deeds you answer us in righteousness, God of our salvation, the hope of all the ends of the earth and of the farthest seas,

**7** who sets the mountains firm by his strength, clothed with might,

**8** who stills the roaring of the seas, the roaring of their waves, and the tumult of the peoples.

**9** Those who live at the ends of the earth are in awe at your signs; you make the lands of the morning and the evening shout for joy.[^6]

**10** You care for the land and water it; you make it very rich. The stream of God is full of water; you provide their grain, for so you have prepared the land.[^7]

**11** You drench its furrows, you level its ridges; you soften it with showers and bless its growth.

**12** You crown the year with your goodness, and your tracks[^8] overflow with abundance.

**13** The pastures of the wilderness overflow; the hills are clothed with joy.[^9]

**14** The meadows are clothed with flocks, and the valleys are covered with grain; they shout for joy, yes, they sing.

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–14 are 65:1–13 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "To you silence (is) praise, God, in Zion". The Septuagint reads "Praise is fitting for you", taking the word *dumiyyah*, "silence", from a similar root.
[^3]: Literally "matters of iniquities".
[^4]: Or "you forgive", "you cover". Hebrew *kipper*, the verb used for making atonement.
[^5]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^6]: Literally "you make the goings out of the morning and the evening shout for joy", that is, the far east and the far west.
[^7]: Literally "you have prepared it".
[^8]: Literally "your wagon tracks", as God's chariot passes over the land.
[^9]: Literally "gird themselves with joy".
