**1** For the director. Of the sons of Korah. A psalm.[^1]

**2** Yahweh,[^2] you have shown favor to your land; you have restored the fortunes of Jacob.[^3]

**3** You have forgiven[^4] the iniquity of your people; you have covered all their sin. Selah.[^5]

**4** You have withdrawn all your fury; you have turned from your burning anger.

**5** Restore us,[^6] God of our salvation, and put an end to your displeasure toward us.

**6** Will you be angry with us forever? Will you drag out your anger to all generations?

**7** Will you not give us life again, so that your people may rejoice in you?

**8** Show us your faithful love,[^7] Yahweh, and grant us your salvation.

**9** Let me hear what God, Yahweh, will say, for he will speak peace to his people, to his faithful ones;[^8] but let them not turn back to folly.[^9]

**10** Surely his salvation is near to those who fear him, so that glory may dwell in our land.

**11** Faithful love and faithfulness[^10] have met; righteousness and peace have kissed.

**12** Faithfulness springs up from the earth, and righteousness looks down from heaven.

**13** Yahweh will indeed give what is good, and our land will yield its harvest.

**14** Righteousness will go before him, and make a path for his steps.[^11]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of the sons of Korah*: or "for the sons of Korah", a family of Levites who served as temple singers. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–14 are 85:1–13 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "you have brought back the captives of Jacob".
[^4]: Literally "lifted", that is, taken away.
[^5]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^6]: Or "Return to us". Literally "Turn us back".
[^7]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 11.
[^8]: Hebrew *chasidim*, "the faithful, the loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^9]: The Septuagint reads "and to those who turn their hearts to him".
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed* and *emet*, the pair in God's description of himself in Exodus 34:6. *Emet* means firmness, reliability, truth. Also in verse 12.
[^11]: Literally "and will set his steps for a way". Or "and will set us on the way of his steps".
