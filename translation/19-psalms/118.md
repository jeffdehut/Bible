**1** Give thanks to Yahweh,[^1] for he is good; his faithful love[^2] endures forever.[^3]

**2** Let Israel say, "His faithful love endures forever."

**3** Let the house of Aaron say, "His faithful love endures forever."

**4** Let those who fear Yahweh say, "His faithful love endures forever."[^4]

**5** In my distress I called on Yah;[^5] Yah answered me and set me in a broad place.[^6]

**6** Yahweh is for me; I will not be afraid. What can anyone[^7] do to me?[^8]

**7** Yahweh is for me as my helper;[^9] I will look in triumph on those who hate me.

**8** It is better to take refuge in Yahweh than to trust in human beings.

**9** It is better to take refuge in Yahweh than to trust in nobles.

**10** All the nations surrounded me; in the name of Yahweh I cut them off.[^10]

**11** They surrounded me, yes, they surrounded me; in the name of Yahweh I cut them off.

**12** They surrounded me like bees; they died out like a fire of thorns.[^11] In the name of Yahweh I cut them off.

**13** You pushed me hard to make me fall,[^12] but Yahweh helped me.

**14** Yah is my strength and my song;[^13] he has become my salvation.

**15** The sound of joyful shouting and salvation is in the tents of the righteous: "The right hand of Yahweh does mighty deeds!

**16** The right hand of Yahweh is lifted high; the right hand of Yahweh does mighty deeds!"

**17** I will not die, but live, and I will tell of the works of Yah.

**18** Yah has disciplined me severely, but he has not given me over to death.

**19** Open the gates of righteousness for me; I will enter through them and give thanks to Yah.

**20** This is the gate of Yahweh; the righteous will enter through it.

**21** I will give thanks to you, for you have answered me, and have become my salvation.

**22** The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.[^14]

**23** This was Yahweh's doing, and it is marvelous in our eyes.[^15]

**24** This is the day that Yahweh has made;[^16] let us rejoice and be glad in it.

**25** Please, Yahweh, save us![^17] Please, Yahweh, give us success!

**26** Blessed is the one who comes in the name of Yahweh![^18] We bless you[^19] from the house of Yahweh.

**27** Yahweh is God, and he has given us light. Bind the festival sacrifice with cords to the horns of the altar.[^20]

**28** You are my God, and I will give thanks to you; my God, I will exalt you.

**29** Give thanks to Yahweh, for he is good; his faithful love endures forever.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^3]: Literally "for his faithful love is forever". Also in verses 2–4 and 29. The same refrain runs through Psalm 136.
[^4]: Verses 2–4 call on Israel, the priests (the house of Aaron) and all who fear Yahweh, as in 115:9–11.
[^5]: Hebrew *Yah*, a short form of the name Yahweh. Also in verses 14 and 17–19.
[^6]: Literally "Yah answered me in the broad place". *Distress* is literally "the narrow place".
[^7]: Hebrew *adam*, "a human being".
[^8]: Hebrews 13:6 quotes this verse from the Septuagint, which reads "The Lord is my helper; I will not be afraid."
[^9]: Literally "Yahweh is for me among my helpers".
[^10]: Hebrew *amilam*, a rare form, perhaps "I cut them off" or "I fended them off". Also in verses 11 and 12. The verb may be the one used for circumcision.
[^11]: That is, as quickly as a fire of dry thorns. The Septuagint reads "they blazed up like a fire among thorns".
[^12]: Literally "You pushed, pushed me to fall", speaking to the enemy. The Septuagint reads "I was pushed hard, so that I fell".
[^13]: Or "my might". Hebrew *zimrah*. This line repeats Exodus 15:2 (compare Isaiah 12:2).
[^14]: Literally "the head of the corner": either a cornerstone in the foundation or the capstone at the top of a wall. Jesus quotes verses 22–23 in Mark 12:10–11 (also Matthew 21:42; Luke 20:17), and Acts 4:11 and 1 Peter 2:7 apply verse 22 to him.
[^15]: Literally "From Yahweh this has come".
[^16]: Or "This is the day on which Yahweh has acted". *In it*: or "in him".
[^17]: Hebrew *hoshi'ah na*, "save, please", the phrase that became "Hosanna" (Mark 11:9–10). The Hebrew does not say whom.
[^18]: The crowds greet Jesus with this line in Mark 11:9 (also Matthew 21:9; Luke 19:38; John 12:13), and Jesus quotes it in Matthew 23:39 and Luke 13:35.
[^19]: "You" is plural: the priests bless those who come up to the temple.
[^20]: The Hebrew of this line is uncertain. Literally "Bind the festival with cords (or branches) up to the horns of the altar", perhaps the animal for the festival sacrifice, or a festal procession with branches as far as the altar. The horns of the altar were the projections at its four corners (Exodus 27:2).
