**1** Whoever lives in the shelter of the Most High[^1] will rest in the shadow of the Almighty.[^2]

**2** I will say to the LORD,[^3] "My refuge and my fortress, my God, in whom I trust."

**3** For he will rescue you from the snare of the fowler, and from the deadly plague.[^4]

**4** He will cover you with his feathers, and under his wings you will find refuge; his faithfulness is a shield and a wall.[^5]

**5** You will not fear the terror of the night, or the arrow that flies by day,

**6** or the plague that stalks in the darkness, or the destruction that lays waste at noon.[^6]

**7** A thousand may fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand, but it will not come near you.

**8** You will only look with your eyes and see the punishment of the wicked.

**9** For you, LORD, are my refuge! You have made the Most High your dwelling place;[^7]

**10** no harm will befall you, and no plague will come near your tent.

**11** For he will command his angels concerning you, to guard you in all your ways.

**12** They will lift you up in their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.[^8]

**13** You will tread on the lion and the cobra; you will trample the young lion and the serpent.[^9]

**14** Because he holds fast to me in love,[^10] I will rescue him; I will set him safe on high, because he knows my name.

**15** He will call on me, and I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble; I will deliver him and honor him.

**16** With long life I will satisfy him, and show him my salvation.

[^1]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God. Also in verse 9. Psalm 91 has no title in the Hebrew. The Septuagint gives it the title "A song of praise, of David".
[^2]: Hebrew *Shaddai*, traditionally "the Almighty"; the meaning is uncertain.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "from the plague of destruction". The same consonants can be read *davar*, "word", for *dever*, "plague", as the Septuagint did: "from a troubling word".
[^5]: Hebrew *socherah*, found only here, perhaps a wall around a city, or a kind of shield. *Faithfulness*: Hebrew *emet*, firmness, reliability, truth.
[^6]: Hebrew *qetev*, "destruction" or "pestilence". The Septuagint reads "the demon of noonday".
[^7]: Literally "For you, LORD, my refuge; the Most High you have made your dwelling." The speaker changes suddenly: the first line repeats the words of the one who trusts (verse 2), and the second speaks to him again. Or "Because you have made the LORD, my refuge, the Most High, your dwelling place".
[^8]: In Matthew 4:6 and Luke 4:10–11 the devil quotes verses 11–12 to Jesus.
[^9]: Hebrew *tannin*, a serpent or sea monster.
[^10]: Hebrew *chashaq*, "be attached to, set one's love on". God speaks in verses 14–16.
