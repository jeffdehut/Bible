**1** For the director. A psalm of David.[^1]

**2** May Yahweh[^2] answer you in the day of trouble; may the name of the God of Jacob keep you safe.[^3]

**3** May he send you help from the sanctuary, and support you from Zion.

**4** May he remember all your offerings,[^4] and accept your burnt offering.[^5] Selah.[^6]

**5** May he give you what your heart desires, and fulfill all your plans.

**6** We will shout for joy at your victory,[^7] and raise our banners in the name of our God. May Yahweh fulfill all your requests.

**7** Now I know that Yahweh saves his anointed;[^8] he will answer him from his holy heaven with the saving might of his right hand.

**8** Some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we call on the name of Yahweh our God.[^9]

**9** They collapse and fall, but we rise and stand firm.

**10** Yahweh, save! May the king answer us on the day we call.[^10]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–10 are 20:1–9 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "set you on high", out of reach of danger.
[^4]: Hebrew *minchah*, often the grain offering (Leviticus 2), but also any gift or offering.
[^5]: Literally "find your burnt offering fat", that is, rich and acceptable.
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^7]: Or "your salvation". Hebrew *yeshu'ah*.
[^8]: Hebrew *meshicho*, "his anointed one", the king anointed at Yahweh's command.
[^9]: Literally "we cause to remember the name", that is, we invoke it.
[^10]: The Hebrew accents divide the verse this way, and "the king" may then be God. The Septuagint reads "Lord, save the king, and answer us on the day we call."
