**1** Of David.[^1] I will give thanks to you with all my heart; before the gods[^2] I will sing your praise.

**2** I will bow down toward your holy temple and give thanks to your name for your faithful love[^3] and your faithfulness, for you have made your promise greater than all your name.[^4]

**3** On the day I called, you answered me; you made me bold, with strength in my soul.[^5]

**4** All the kings of the earth will give thanks to you, LORD,[^6] when they hear the words of your mouth.[^7]

**5** They will sing of the ways of the LORD, for great is the glory of the LORD.

**6** For though the LORD is high, he looks on the lowly; but the proud he knows from far away.

**7** Though I walk in the midst of trouble, you preserve my life; you stretch out your hand against the anger of my enemies, and your right hand saves me.

**8** The LORD will fulfill his purpose for me.[^8] LORD, your faithful love endures forever; do not abandon the works of your hands.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. Psalms 138–145 all have David's name in their titles.
[^2]: Hebrew *elohim*, which can mean "God", "gods" or "heavenly beings". Here probably the gods of the nations, or the heavenly beings around God's throne. The Septuagint reads "before the angels".
[^3]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 8.
[^4]: Literally "for you have made great your promise (*imrah*) above all your name". The Hebrew is difficult. Perhaps "you have done even more than you promised", or "you have exalted your name and your promise above everything".
[^5]: Literally "you made me bold in my soul (*nephesh*), strength".
[^6]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^7]: Or "for they have heard".
[^8]: Literally "The LORD will complete (it) on my behalf".
