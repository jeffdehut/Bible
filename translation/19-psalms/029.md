**1** A psalm of David.[^1] Ascribe to the LORD,[^2] you heavenly beings,[^3] ascribe to the LORD glory and strength.

**2** Ascribe to the LORD the glory due his name;[^4] worship the LORD in the splendor of holiness.[^5]

**3** The voice of the LORD is over the waters; the God of glory thunders, the LORD, over the mighty waters.

**4** The voice of the LORD is powerful; the voice of the LORD is majestic.

**5** The voice of the LORD breaks the cedars; the LORD breaks the cedars of Lebanon.

**6** He makes Lebanon skip like a calf, and Sirion[^6] like a young wild ox.

**7** The voice of the LORD strikes with flames of fire.[^7]

**8** The voice of the LORD shakes the wilderness; the LORD shakes the wilderness of Kadesh.

**9** The voice of the LORD makes the deer give birth,[^8] and strips the forests bare, and in his temple everyone says, "Glory!"

**10** The LORD sat enthroned over the flood;[^9] the LORD sits enthroned as King forever.

**11** May the LORD give strength to his people; may the LORD bless his people with peace.[^10]

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "sons of gods" (Hebrew *bene elim*): heavenly beings who serve in God's court (compare Job 1:6; Psalm 89:7; 89:6 in most English Bibles).
[^4]: Literally "the glory of his name". Verses 1–2 are close to 1 Chronicles 16:28–29 and Psalm 96:7–9.
[^5]: Or "in holy attire".
[^6]: Another name for Mount Hermon (Deuteronomy 3:9).
[^7]: Literally "hews out flames of fire", perhaps of lightning.
[^8]: Or, with different vowels, "makes the oaks whirl".
[^9]: Hebrew *mabbul*, the word used for the great flood in Genesis 6–11.
[^10]: Or "The LORD gives strength to his people; the LORD blesses his people with peace."
