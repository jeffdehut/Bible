**1** Of David.[^1] Blessed be Yahweh,[^2] my rock, who trains my hands for war, my fingers for battle,

**2** my faithful love[^3] and my fortress, my stronghold and my deliverer, my shield, in whom I take refuge, who subdues my people under me.[^4]

**3** Yahweh, what is a human being, that you take notice of him, a son of man,[^5] that you think of him?

**4** A human being is like a breath;[^6] his days are like a passing shadow.

**5** Yahweh, bend your heavens and come down; touch the mountains, so that they smoke.

**6** Flash out lightning and scatter them; send out your arrows and throw them into confusion.

**7** Stretch out your hands from on high; set me free and rescue me from the mighty waters, from the hand of foreigners,

**8** whose mouths speak lies, and whose right hand is a right hand of falsehood.[^7]

**9** God, I will sing a new song to you; on a ten-stringed harp I will make music to you,

**10** the one who gives victory to kings, who sets David his servant free from the cruel sword.[^8]

**11** Set me free and rescue me from the hand of foreigners, whose mouths speak lies, and whose right hand is a right hand of falsehood.

**12** May our sons in their youth be like plants grown tall,[^9] our daughters like corner pillars carved for a palace.[^10]

**13** May our barns be full, supplying every kind of produce; may our flocks increase by thousands, by tens of thousands in our fields.

**14** May our cattle be heavy with young;[^11] may there be no breach in the walls, no going into exile,[^12] and no cry of distress in our streets.

**15** Happy[^13] are the people for whom it is so; happy are the people whose God is Yahweh!

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. Verses 1–11 draw on many lines of Psalm 18 (compare 18:3, 10, 15, 17, 35, 48; 18:2, 9, 14, 16, 34, 47 in most English Bibles).
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *chasdi*, "my *chesed*" (loyal, faithful love): the God who shows me faithful love.
[^4]: Some Hebrew manuscripts read "who subdues peoples under me", as in 18:48 (18:47 in most English Bibles).
[^5]: Literally "a son of *enosh* (mortal)". Compare 8:5 (8:4 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor", the word of Ecclesiastes 1:2.
[^7]: The right hand was raised to swear an oath: their oaths are false.
[^8]: Literally "the evil sword". Verse 10 continues verse 9: the song is sung to the one who gives victory to kings.
[^9]: Literally "That our sons...". The Hebrew links verses 12–14 to what goes before with the word *asher*, "which, that", in an unclear way. Or "Then our sons will be like plants".
[^10]: Or "for a temple". Hebrew *heikhal*.
[^11]: Or "May our oxen be well laden". Hebrew *alluf*, "ox, cow".
[^12]: Literally "no breach and no going out". Or, continuing the picture of the cattle, "no miscarriage and no untimely birth".
[^13]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed". Also in the next line.
