**1** A psalm of Asaph.[^1] God takes his stand in the divine council;[^2] in the midst of the gods[^3] he gives judgment:

**2** "How long will you judge unjustly, and show favor to the wicked?[^4] Selah.[^5]

**3** Give justice to the poor and the fatherless; uphold the rights of the afflicted and the destitute.

**4** Rescue the poor and the needy; deliver them from the hand of the wicked."

**5** They do not know, and they do not understand; they walk about in darkness. All the foundations of the earth are shaken.[^6]

**6** "I said, 'You are gods;[^7] you are all sons of the Most High.'[^8]

**7** But you will die like human beings,[^9] and fall like any of the princes."

**8** Rise up, God, judge the earth, for all the nations are your inheritance.[^10]

[^1]: *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "in the assembly of El", or "in the assembly of God". *El* is a word for God, and in the myths of Canaan it was also the name of the chief god, who presided over an assembly of gods. Here God (*Elohim*) stands in judgment in his own heavenly council (compare 89:6–8, English 89:5–7; 1 Kings 22:19; Job 1:6).
[^3]: Hebrew *elohim*, the same word as "God" at the start of the verse. Here it means the members of God's council, heavenly beings who were given rule over the nations (compare Deuteronomy 32:8, as read in the Septuagint and a Dead Sea Scroll). Many Jewish and Christian interpreters have understood these "gods" as human rulers or judges, who judge in God's name (compare Exodus 21:6; 22:7–8, where *ha-elohim*, "God", may mean the judges).
[^4]: Literally "lift up the faces of the wicked", that is, show them partiality.
[^5]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^6]: Verse 5 may be God's words about the gods of the council, or the psalmist's own comment. Their injustice shakes the order of the world.
[^7]: Hebrew *elohim*, as in verse 1. Jesus quotes this line in John 10:34, arguing from it that Scripture calls "gods" those "to whom the word of God came" (John 10:35).
[^8]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^9]: Literally "like *adam*": like humankind, or "like Adam".
[^10]: Or "for you will take possession of all the nations".
