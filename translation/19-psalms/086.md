**1** A prayer of David.[^1] Turn your ear, LORD,[^2] and answer me, for I am afflicted and needy.

**2** Guard my life,[^3] for I am faithful;[^4] you are my God; save your servant, who trusts in you.

**3** Be gracious to me, Lord, for I call to you all day long.

**4** Make your servant's soul glad, for to you, Lord, I lift up my soul.

**5** For you, Lord, are good and forgiving, abounding in faithful love[^5] to all who call on you.

**6** Listen, LORD, to my prayer, and pay attention to the sound of my pleas for mercy.

**7** In the day of my trouble I call on you, for you will answer me.

**8** There is no one like you among the gods, Lord, and there are no works like yours.

**9** All the nations you have made will come and bow down before you, Lord, and they will glorify your name.

**10** For you are great, and you do wonders; you alone are God.

**11** Teach me your way, LORD, so that I may walk in your truth; give me an undivided heart[^6] to fear your name.

**12** I will give thanks to you, Lord my God, with all my heart, and I will glorify your name forever.

**13** For your faithful love toward me is great; you have rescued my life from the depths of Sheol.[^7]

**14** God, the arrogant have risen against me, and a band of ruthless men seeks my life; they do not set you before them.[^8]

**15** But you, Lord, are a God compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in faithful love and faithfulness.[^9]

**16** Turn to me and be gracious to me; give your strength to your servant, and save the son of your female servant.[^10]

**17** Show me a sign of your goodness,[^11] so that those who hate me may see it and be put to shame, for you, LORD, have helped me and comforted me.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". It is the only psalm in Book 3 (Psalms 73–89) with David's name in its title. In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. Many of its lines echo other psalms and Exodus 34:6.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Also in verses 4, 13 and 14.
[^4]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful one", related to *chesed*, "faithful love". Or "devoted to you".
[^5]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verses 13 and 15.
[^6]: Literally "unite my heart". The Septuagint reads "let my heart rejoice", taking the same consonants from another verb.
[^7]: Literally "from lowest Sheol". Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^8]: This verse is almost the same as 54:5 (54:3 in most English Bibles), which has "strangers" where this verse has "the arrogant".
[^9]: These words repeat God's description of himself in Exodus 34:6 (compare 103:8; 145:8).
[^10]: That is, a servant born in the household, and so bound to it for life (compare 116:16).
[^11]: Literally "Do with me a sign for good".
