**1** For the director. According to "Do Not Destroy".[^1] A psalm of Asaph. A song.[^2]

**2** We give thanks to you, God, we give thanks, and your name is near; people tell of your wonderful deeds.

**3** "When I choose the appointed time,[^3] I myself will judge with fairness.

**4** When the earth and all who live in it melt away, it is I who hold its pillars firm. Selah.[^4]

**5** I said to the boastful, 'Do not boast,' and to the wicked, 'Do not lift up your horn.[^5]

**6** Do not lift up your horn on high, or speak with an arrogant neck.'"[^6]

**7** For not from the east or from the west, and not from the wilderness, comes lifting up.[^7]

**8** For God is the judge; he puts down one and lifts up another.

**9** For in the hand of Yahweh[^8] there is a cup, with foaming wine, richly mixed; he pours from it, and all the wicked of the earth must drain it to the dregs and drink.

**10** But as for me, I will declare it forever; I will sing praises to the God of Jacob.

**11** All the horns of the wicked I will cut off, but the horns of the righteous will be lifted up.[^9]

[^1]: Hebrew *al-tashchet*, "do not destroy", probably the name of a tune (see the title of Psalm 57).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–11 are 75:1–10 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: God speaks in verses 3–6, or perhaps only in verses 3–4.
[^4]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^5]: The horn of an animal is a picture of strength and pride. Also in verses 6 and 11.
[^6]: Literally "with a neck of arrogance". The Septuagint reads "speak injustice against God".
[^7]: Hebrew *harim*, "lifting up", as in verses 5–6 and 8. Or "and not from the wilderness of the mountains".
[^8]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^9]: The speaker may be God, as in verses 3–6, or the psalmist.
