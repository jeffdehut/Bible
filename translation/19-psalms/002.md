**1** Why do the nations rage,[^1] and the peoples plot in vain?[^2]

**2** The kings of the earth take their stand, and the rulers conspire together, against the LORD[^3] and against his anointed one:[^4]

**3** "Let us break their chains, and throw off their ropes from us!"

**4** The one who sits in heaven laughs; the Lord scoffs at them.

**5** Then he speaks to them in his anger, and terrifies them in his fury:

**6** "I myself have installed[^5] my king on Zion, my holy mountain."

**7** I will proclaim the decree of the LORD.[^6] He said to me, "You are my son; today I have become your father.[^7]

**8** Ask me, and I will make the nations your inheritance, the ends of the earth your possession.

**9** You will break them with a rod of iron;[^8] you will smash them like a potter's jar."

**10** So now, you kings, be wise; take warning, you who rule the earth.[^9]

**11** Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

**12** Kiss the son,[^10] or he will be angry, and you will perish on your way, for his anger flares up quickly. Happy[^11] are all who take refuge in him.

[^1]: Or "conspire", "gather in uproar". Acts 4:25–26 quotes verses 1–2.
[^2]: Literally "murmur emptiness".
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *meshicho*, "his anointed one", the king anointed at the LORD's command, from *mashiach*, the word behind "Messiah". Later Jewish and Christian readers heard the promised Messiah in this psalm, and Acts 4:25–27 applies verses 1–2 to Jesus.
[^5]: Hebrew *nasakhti*. The verb usually means "pour out" (as a drink offering), so perhaps "consecrated"; here it probably means "set up, installed".
[^6]: The king now speaks. Or "I will proclaim the decree: The LORD said to me".
[^7]: Literally "today I have begotten you". At his enthronement the king becomes the LORD's son (compare 2 Samuel 7:14, "I will be his father, and he will be my son"). Acts 13:33 and Hebrews 1:5; 5:5 quote this line, and it is echoed in the voice from heaven at Jesus' baptism (Mark 1:11).
[^8]: Or "with an iron scepter". The Septuagint, reading the same consonants with different vowels, has "You will shepherd them with a rod of iron", the wording found in Revelation 2:27; 12:5; 19:15.
[^9]: Literally "you judges of the earth".
[^10]: Hebrew *nashqu-bar*, a disputed phrase. *Bar* is the Aramaic word for "son" (verse 7 has the Hebrew *ben*), and a kiss was a sign of homage (compare 1 Kings 19:18). But *bar* can also mean "pure", giving "Kiss sincerely" or "Do homage in purity". The Septuagint reads "Take hold of instruction" (or "discipline"). Some scholars rearrange the words at the end of verse 11 and the start of verse 12 to read "with trembling kiss his feet". It is not clear whether "he" and "his" in the rest of the verse refer to the LORD or to the son.
[^11]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
