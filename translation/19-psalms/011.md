**1** For the director. Of David.[^1] In the LORD[^2] I take refuge. How can you say to me,[^3] "Flee like a bird to your mountain"?[^4]

**2** For look, the wicked bend the bow; they have fitted their arrow to the string, to shoot from the darkness at the upright in heart.

**3** When the foundations are torn down, what can the righteous do?

**4** The LORD is in his holy temple; the LORD's throne is in heaven. His eyes watch; his gaze[^5] tests human beings.

**5** The LORD tests the righteous, but the wicked and those who love violence he hates with all his being.[^6]

**6** He will rain snares[^7] on the wicked; fire and sulfur and a scorching wind will be the portion of their cup.

**7** For the LORD is righteous; he loves righteous deeds. The upright will see his face.[^8]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "to my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^4]: Literally "Flee to your mountain, bird".
[^5]: Literally "his eyelids".
[^6]: Literally "his soul (*nephesh*) hates". Or "The LORD tests the righteous and the wicked, and he hates those who love violence."
[^7]: Hebrew *pachim*. Some scholars read *pachamei*, "coals of", with a small change to the Hebrew: "He will rain coals of fire and sulfur on the wicked".
[^8]: Or "His face looks on the upright."
