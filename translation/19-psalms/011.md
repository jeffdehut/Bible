**1** For the director. Of David.[^1] In Yahweh[^2] I take refuge. How can you say to me,[^3] "Flee like a bird to your mountain"?[^4]

**2** For look, the wicked bend the bow; they have fitted their arrow to the string, to shoot from the darkness at the upright in heart.

**3** When the foundations are torn down, what can the righteous do?

**4** Yahweh is in his holy temple; Yahweh's throne is in heaven. His eyes watch; his gaze[^5] tests human beings.

**5** Yahweh tests the righteous, but the wicked and those who love violence he hates with all his being.[^6]

**6** He will rain snares[^7] on the wicked; fire and sulfur and a scorching wind will be the portion of their cup.

**7** For Yahweh is righteous; he loves righteous deeds. The upright will see his face.[^8]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "to my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^4]: Literally "Flee to your mountain, bird".
[^5]: Literally "his eyelids".
[^6]: Literally "his soul (*nephesh*) hates". Or "Yahweh tests the righteous and the wicked, and he hates those who love violence."
[^7]: Many interpreters read *pachamei*, "coals of", for *pachim*, "snares", a change of one letter: "He will rain coals of fire and sulfur on the wicked".
[^8]: Or "His face looks on the upright."
