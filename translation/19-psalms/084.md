**1** For the director. According to the Gittith.[^1] Of the sons of Korah. A psalm.[^2]

**2** How lovely is your dwelling place,[^3] LORD of Armies!

**3** My soul[^4] longs, it even faints for the courts of the LORD; my heart and my flesh sing for joy to the living God.

**4** Even the sparrow has found a home, and the swallow a nest for herself, where she may lay her young, at your altars, LORD of Armies, my King and my God.

**5** Happy[^5] are those who live in your house; they will praise you continually.[^6] Selah.[^7]

**6** Happy are those whose strength is in you, whose hearts are set on the highways.[^8]

**7** As they pass through the Valley of Baca,[^9] they make it a place of springs; the early rain also covers it with blessings.[^10]

**8** They go from strength to strength; each one appears before God in Zion.[^11]

**9** LORD God of Armies, hear my prayer; listen, God of Jacob. Selah.

**10** Look on our shield, God;[^12] look on the face of your anointed.[^13]

**11** For a day in your courts is better than a thousand elsewhere.[^14] I would rather stand at the threshold[^15] of the house of my God than live in the tents of wickedness.

**12** For the LORD God is a sun and a shield; the LORD gives grace and glory. He does not hold back any good thing from those who walk blamelessly.

**13** LORD of Armies, happy is the one who trusts in you.

[^1]: Hebrew *gittit*, a term of uncertain meaning (see the note on the title of Psalm 8).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of the sons of Korah*: or "for the sons of Korah", a family of Levites who served as temple singers. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–13 are 84:1–12 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Literally "your dwellings". *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 4 and 13, and in verse 9, where the Hebrew is *YHWH Elohim tseva'ot*, "the LORD God of Armies".
[^4]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^5]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed" (see the note on 1:1). Also in verses 6 and 13.
[^6]: Literally "they will still praise you".
[^7]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 9.
[^8]: Literally "highways are in their heart", that is, the roads of pilgrimage to Zion.
[^9]: Hebrew *ha-bakha*, perhaps a kind of balsam tree that grows in dry places (compare 2 Samuel 5:23–24), or "weeping" (from *bakhah*, "weep"). The valley is unknown.
[^10]: Hebrew *berakhot*. The same consonants can be read *berekhot*, "pools".
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "the God of gods will be seen in Zion", taking *el*, "to", as *El*, "God". For "appear before God", compare 42:3.
[^12]: Or "God, our shield, look". *Our shield* may be the king, who is also "your anointed".
[^13]: Hebrew *meshichekha*, "your anointed one", from *mashiach*: the king, anointed with oil.
[^14]: Literally "than a thousand".
[^15]: Hebrew *histofef*, from *saf*, "threshold": to wait at the threshold, or to serve as a doorkeeper.
