**1** For the director. According to the Sheminith.[^1] A psalm of David.[^2]

**2** Save, Yahweh,[^3] for the faithful[^4] are no more; the loyal have vanished from among human beings.

**3** They speak lies to one another; with flattering lips and a double heart[^5] they speak.

**4** May Yahweh cut off all flattering lips, the tongue that speaks boastfully,

**5** those who say, "With our tongue we will prevail; our lips are our own. Who is master over us?"

**6** "Because the afflicted are plundered, because the needy groan, now I will arise," says Yahweh. "I will place them in the safety they long for."[^6]

**7** The words of Yahweh are pure words, like silver refined in a furnace in the ground,[^7] purified seven times.

**8** You, Yahweh, will keep them; you will guard each one[^8] from this generation forever.

**9** The wicked prowl on every side, when what is vile is honored among human beings.[^9]

[^1]: Hebrew *sheminit*, "the eighth", a term of uncertain meaning: perhaps an eight-stringed instrument, a lower octave, or a tune. Also in the title of Psalm 6.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–9 are 12:1–8 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful, loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love". Literally "the faithful one has come to an end".
[^5]: Literally "with a heart and a heart".
[^6]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "I will set in safety; he pants for it". Or "I will set him in safety from the one who sneers at him".
[^7]: The meaning of the Hebrew phrase *ba'alil la'arets* is uncertain, perhaps "in a crucible of earth".
[^8]: Literally "him". The Septuagint reads "us" in both lines.
[^9]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "as vileness is exalted for the sons of *adam* (humankind)".
