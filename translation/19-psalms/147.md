**1** Praise the LORD![^1] For it is good to sing praises to our God; for it is pleasant, and praise is fitting.

**2** The LORD[^2] builds up Jerusalem; he gathers the outcasts of Israel.[^3]

**3** He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds.

**4** He counts the number of the stars; he gives names to all of them.[^4]

**5** Great is our Lord, and abundant in power; his understanding is beyond measure.[^5]

**6** The LORD lifts up the humble; he casts the wicked down to the ground.

**7** Sing to the LORD with thanksgiving; make music to our God on the lyre.

**8** He covers the heavens with clouds; he prepares rain for the earth; he makes grass grow on the mountains.[^6]

**9** He gives the animals their food, and the young ravens when they call.

**10** He takes no delight in the strength of the horse, no pleasure in the legs of a warrior.[^7]

**11** The LORD takes pleasure in those who fear him, in those who hope in his faithful love.[^8]

**12** Extol the LORD, Jerusalem! Praise your God, Zion![^9]

**13** For he has strengthened the bars of your gates; he has blessed your children within you.

**14** He grants peace within your borders; he satisfies you with the finest wheat.[^10]

**15** He sends out his command[^11] to the earth; his word runs swiftly.

**16** He gives snow like wool; he scatters frost like ashes.

**17** He hurls down his hail like crumbs. Who can stand before his cold?

**18** He sends out his word and melts them; he makes his wind blow, and the waters flow.

**19** He declares his words[^12] to Jacob, his decrees and his rulings to Israel.

**20** He has not done this for any other nation; they do not know his rulings. Praise the LORD!

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH. Also at the end of verse 20.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: That is, the exiles who had been scattered (compare Isaiah 11:12; 56:8).
[^4]: Literally "he calls names to all of them". Compare Isaiah 40:26.
[^5]: Literally "there is no number to his understanding".
[^6]: The Septuagint adds "and plants for the service of human beings", as in 104:14.
[^7]: Literally "the legs of the man": the strength of a runner or a soldier.
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^9]: The Septuagint begins a new psalm here, so that verses 1–11 and 12–20 are two psalms (see the note on 114:1).
[^10]: Literally "the fat of wheat".
[^11]: Hebrew *imrah*, "saying, word", translated "promise" in Psalm 119.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "his word" but read "his words".
