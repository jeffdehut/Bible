**1** Praise Yah![^1] Praise, you servants of Yahweh;[^2] praise the name of Yahweh.[^3]

**2** May the name of Yahweh be blessed, from now on and forever.

**3** From the rising of the sun to its setting, the name of Yahweh is to be praised.

**4** Yahweh is high above all nations; his glory is above the heavens.

**5** Who is like Yahweh our God, who is enthroned on high,

**6** who stoops down to look on the heavens and the earth?[^4]

**7** He raises the poor from the dust, and lifts the needy from the ash heap,[^5]

**8** to seat them with nobles, with the nobles of his people.[^6]

**9** He gives the childless woman a home, as a happy mother of children.[^7] Praise Yah!

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", traditionally "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name Yahweh. Also at the end of verse 9.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Psalms 113–118 are known in Jewish tradition as the Hallel ("praise"), sung at Passover and the other festivals. Psalms 113–114 are sung before the Passover meal and 115–118 after it; the hymn sung at the end of the Last Supper (Mark 14:26) may have been part of it.
[^4]: Literally "who makes high to sit, who makes low to see, in the heavens and on the earth". Or "who sits enthroned on high and looks far down on the heavens and the earth".
[^5]: Or "the garbage heap", the refuse dump outside a town, where beggars sat.
[^6]: Verses 7–8 are nearly the same as Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2:8.
[^7]: Literally "who seats the barren woman of the house, a joyful mother of sons". Compare 1 Samuel 2:5.
