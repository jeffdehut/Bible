**1** For the director. Of David.[^1] The fool[^2] says in his heart, "There is no God." They are corrupt; they do detestable deeds; there is no one who does good.

**2** The LORD[^3] looks down from heaven on human beings, to see if there is anyone who understands, anyone who seeks God.

**3** They have all turned aside; together they have become corrupt. There is no one who does good, not even one.[^4]

**4** Do they know nothing, all the evildoers, who eat up my people as they eat bread, and do not call on the LORD?

**5** There they are, in great terror, for God is with the generation of the righteous.

**6** You would frustrate the plans of the afflicted, but the LORD is their refuge.[^5]

**7** Oh, that salvation for Israel would come from Zion! When the LORD restores the fortunes of his people,[^6] let Jacob rejoice, let Israel be glad.

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. Psalm 14 is almost the same as Psalm 53:2–7 (53:1–6 in most English Bibles). Psalm 53 has *Elohim*, "God", where this psalm has "the LORD", and its verse 6 differs from verses 5–6 here.
[^2]: Hebrew *naval*, someone who is morally and spiritually senseless, not merely unintelligent.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Romans 3:10–12 quotes verses 1–3 freely.
[^5]: Literally "You would put to shame the counsel of the afflicted, for the LORD is his refuge." "You" are the evildoers.
[^6]: Or "brings back his captive people".
