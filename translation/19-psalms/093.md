**1** The LORD[^1] reigns;[^2] he is clothed with majesty. The LORD is clothed; he has wrapped himself in strength. The world is firmly established; it cannot be moved.[^3]

**2** Your throne was established long ago; you are from everlasting.

**3** The floods have lifted up, LORD, the floods have lifted up their voice; the floods lift up their pounding waves.[^4]

**4** Mightier than the sound of many waters, mightier than the breakers of the sea, is the LORD, mighty on high.

**5** Your statutes[^5] are very trustworthy; holiness befits your house, LORD, for all time to come.[^6]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Psalm 93 has no title in the Hebrew. The Septuagint gives it one: "For the day before the Sabbath, when the earth was settled. A song of praise, of David."
[^2]: Or "The LORD has become king", "The LORD is king". Hebrew *YHWH malakh*. Psalms 93 and 95–99 are often called the "The LORD is king" psalms, or the enthronement psalms. They celebrate the LORD's rule as king over the whole world and its peoples (93:1; 95:3; 96:10; 97:1; 98:6; 99:1; compare 47:9).
[^3]: The same words are in 96:10 and 1 Chronicles 16:30.
[^4]: Hebrew *dokhyam*, found only here, "their pounding" or "their crashing". *Floods*: Hebrew *neharot*, "rivers", here the great waters that stand for the forces of chaos which God rules (compare 89:10, English 89:9).
[^5]: Hebrew *edot*, traditionally "testimonies": the solemn terms of the covenant.
[^6]: Literally "for length of days".
