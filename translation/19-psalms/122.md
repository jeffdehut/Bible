**1** A song of ascents. Of David.[^1] I was glad when they said to me, "Let us go to the house of Yahweh."[^2]

**2** Our feet are standing within your gates, Jerusalem.

**3** Jerusalem, built as a city that is bound firmly together,[^3]

**4** where the tribes go up, the tribes of Yah,[^4] according to the statute[^5] given to Israel, to give thanks to the name of Yahweh.

**5** For there the thrones for judgment stand,[^6] the thrones of the house of David.

**6** Pray for the peace of Jerusalem:[^7] "May those who love you be secure.

**7** May there be peace within your walls, security within your fortresses."

**8** For the sake of my brothers and my friends, I will say, "Peace be within you."

**9** For the sake of the house of Yahweh our God, I will seek your good.

[^1]: *A song of ascents*: Hebrew *shir ha-ma'alot* (see the note on 120:1). *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "compact", its houses joined closely together.
[^4]: Hebrew *Yah*, a short form of the name Yahweh.
[^5]: Hebrew *edut*, traditionally "testimony": the solemn terms of the covenant. The law required every Israelite man to appear before Yahweh three times a year (Exodus 23:17; Deuteronomy 16:16).
[^6]: Literally "sit", or "sat".
[^7]: Literally "Ask the peace (*shalom*) of Jerusalem". Verses 6–7 play on the sound of the city's name, *Yerushalayim*: *sha'alu shelom* "ask the peace", *yishlayu* "be secure", *shalom* "peace", *shalvah* "security".
