**1** A maskil of Asaph.[^1] Listen, my people, to my instruction;[^2] turn your ears to the words of my mouth.

**2** I will open my mouth in a parable;[^3] I will pour out riddles from of old,[^4]

**3** things that we have heard and known, that our ancestors have told us.

**4** We will not hide them from their children; we will tell the next generation the praiseworthy deeds of the LORD,[^5] his might, and the wonders he has done.

**5** He established a statute[^6] in Jacob, and set a law in Israel, which he commanded our ancestors to make known to their children,

**6** so that the next generation might know them, the children yet to be born, and they in turn would rise up and tell their children,

**7** so that they would put their confidence in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commands,

**8** and so that they would not be like their ancestors, a stubborn and rebellious generation, a generation whose heart was not steadfast, whose spirit was not faithful to God.

**9** The Ephraimites, armed with bows,[^7] turned back on the day of battle.

**10** They did not keep God's covenant, and they refused to walk in his law.

**11** They forgot what he had done, the wonders he had shown them.

**12** In the sight of their ancestors he did wonders in the land of Egypt, in the region of Zoan.[^8]

**13** He split the sea and led them through; he made the waters stand up like a heap.[^9]

**14** He led them with the cloud by day, and all night long with the light of fire.

**15** He split rocks in the wilderness, and gave them drink as abundant as the great deep.[^10]

**16** He brought streams out of the rock, and made water flow down like rivers.

**17** But they went on sinning against him, rebelling against the Most High[^11] in the desert.

**18** They put God to the test in their hearts, demanding food for their craving.[^12]

**19** They spoke against God; they said, "Can God spread a table in the wilderness?

**20** True, he struck the rock, and water gushed out, and streams overflowed. But can he also give bread, or provide meat for his people?"

**21** So when the LORD heard, he was furious; a fire was kindled against Jacob, and his anger rose against Israel,[^13]

**22** because they did not believe in God, or trust in his salvation.

**23** Yet he commanded the skies above, and opened the doors of heaven;

**24** he rained down manna on them to eat, and gave them the grain of heaven.[^14]

**25** Each of them ate the bread of the mighty;[^15] he sent them food in abundance.

**26** He let loose the east wind in the heavens, and by his power he drove out the south wind.

**27** He rained meat on them like dust, winged birds like the sand of the seas.

**28** He let them fall in the middle of their camp, all around their dwellings.[^16]

**29** So they ate and were well filled, for he gave them what they craved.

**30** But before they had turned from their craving, while the food was still in their mouths,

**31** the anger of God rose against them; he killed the strongest of them,[^17] and struck down the young men of Israel.[^18]

**32** In spite of all this, they went on sinning; they did not believe in his wonders.

**33** So he made their days vanish like a breath,[^19] and their years in sudden terror.

**34** Whenever he killed some of them, the rest would seek him; they would turn and look eagerly for God.

**35** They remembered that God was their rock, God Most High their redeemer.

**36** But they flattered him with their mouths, and lied to him with their tongues.

**37** Their hearts were not steadfast toward him; they were not faithful to his covenant.

**38** Yet he, being compassionate, forgave[^20] their iniquity and did not destroy them; again and again he held back his anger, and did not stir up all his wrath.

**39** He remembered that they were only flesh, a breath of wind[^21] that passes and does not return.

**40** How often they rebelled against him in the wilderness, and grieved him in the wasteland!

**41** Again and again they put God to the test, and provoked[^22] the Holy One of Israel.

**42** They did not remember his power,[^23] the day when he redeemed them from the adversary,

**43** when he displayed his signs in Egypt, and his wonders in the region of Zoan.[^24]

**44** He turned their rivers[^25] into blood, so that they could not drink from their streams.

**45** He sent swarms of flies among them, which devoured them, and frogs, which destroyed them.

**46** He gave their crops to the grasshopper, and the fruit of their labor to the locust.

**47** He killed their vines with hail, and their sycamore-fig trees with frost.[^26]

**48** He gave over their cattle to the hail, and their livestock to bolts of lightning.

**49** He let loose on them his burning anger, fury, indignation and distress, a band of destroying angels.[^27]

**50** He cleared a path for his anger; he did not spare them from death, but gave their lives[^28] over to the plague.

**51** He struck down every firstborn in Egypt, the firstfruits of their strength in the tents of Ham.[^29]

**52** Then he led out his people like sheep, and guided them like a flock in the wilderness.

**53** He led them in safety, so that they were not afraid; but the sea covered their enemies.

**54** He brought them to his holy land,[^30] to this mountain that his right hand had won.

**55** He drove out nations before them, and allotted their land as an inheritance by measuring line;[^31] he settled the tribes of Israel in their tents.

**56** Yet they tested God Most High and rebelled against him, and did not keep his statutes.

**57** They turned away and were treacherous like their ancestors; they twisted like a faulty bow.[^32]

**58** They provoked him to anger with their high places, and made him jealous with their carved images.

**59** When God heard, he was furious, and he utterly rejected Israel.

**60** He abandoned the tabernacle at Shiloh, the tent where he had dwelt among human beings.[^33]

**61** He gave up his strength to captivity, his splendor into the hand of the adversary.[^34]

**62** He handed his people over to the sword, and he was furious with his inheritance.

**63** Fire consumed their young men, and their young women had no wedding songs.[^35]

**64** Their priests fell by the sword, and their widows could not weep.

**65** Then the Lord awoke as from sleep, like a warrior shouting because of wine.[^36]

**66** He beat back his adversaries;[^37] he put them to everlasting shame.

**67** He rejected the tent of Joseph, and did not choose the tribe of Ephraim;

**68** but he chose the tribe of Judah, Mount Zion, which he loves.

**69** He built his sanctuary like the heights,[^38] like the earth, which he founded forever.

**70** He chose David his servant, and took him from the sheepfolds;

**71** from following the nursing ewes he brought him to shepherd Jacob his people, Israel his inheritance.

**72** He shepherded them with integrity of heart,[^39] and guided them with skillful hands.

[^1]: *Maskil*: a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "a skillful psalm" or "a psalm of instruction". *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. The psalm retells Israel's history from the exodus to the choice of David and Zion, as a warning to each new generation.
[^2]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching".
[^3]: Hebrew *mashal*, a saying, parable or poem.
[^4]: Matthew 13:35 quotes this verse.
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: Hebrew *edut*, traditionally "testimony": the solemn terms of the covenant. Also in verse 56, in the plural.
[^7]: Literally "equipped shooters of the bow". The event is not known.
[^8]: Literally "the field of Zoan". Zoan was a city in the eastern Nile delta (Numbers 13:22).
[^9]: Compare Exodus 14:21–22; 15:8.
[^10]: Literally "like the great deeps". Compare Exodus 17:6; Numbers 20:11.
[^11]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God. Also in verses 35 and 56.
[^12]: Literally "for their soul (*nephesh*)", that is, their appetite.
[^13]: Verses 18–31 recall Exodus 16 and Numbers 11.
[^14]: John 6:31 quotes this verse.
[^15]: Hebrew *abbirim*, "mighty ones". The Septuagint reads "the bread of angels".
[^16]: Literally "his camp... his dwellings": Israel's.
[^17]: Literally "among their fat ones".
[^18]: Compare Numbers 11:33–34.
[^19]: Hebrew *hevel*, "vapor, breath".
[^20]: Or "atoned for", "covered". Hebrew *kipper*, the verb used for making atonement.
[^21]: Hebrew *ruach*, "wind, breath, spirit".
[^22]: Or "pained". The meaning of the Hebrew *hitvu* is uncertain.
[^23]: Literally "his hand".
[^24]: Verses 44–51 recall the plagues of Egypt (Exodus 7–12), not all of them, and not in the same order.
[^25]: Hebrew *ye'orim*, the Nile and its channels.
[^26]: Hebrew *chanamal*, found only here, perhaps "frost" or "hailstones".
[^27]: Literally "a sending of messengers of evils".
[^28]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. The second line can also be read "but gave their animals over to the plague".
[^29]: Ham was the ancestor of the Egyptians (Genesis 10:6).
[^30]: Literally "to the territory of his holiness".
[^31]: Literally "and made them fall by a line as an inheritance".
[^32]: Literally "a bow of deceit", one that does not shoot straight.
[^33]: See 1 Samuel 4 and Jeremiah 7:12–14.
[^34]: That is, the ark of the covenant, captured by the Philistines (1 Samuel 4:21–22).
[^35]: Literally "were not praised".
[^36]: Or "like a warrior overcome by wine". The meaning of *mitronen* is uncertain.
[^37]: Or "He struck his adversaries from behind".
[^38]: Literally "like high places", perhaps the heavens.
[^39]: Literally "according to the blamelessness of his heart".
