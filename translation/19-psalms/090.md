**1** A prayer of Moses, the man of God.[^1] Lord, you have been our dwelling place[^2] in every generation.

**2** Before the mountains were born, before you brought forth the earth and the world,[^3] from everlasting to everlasting you are God.

**3** You turn human beings back to dust,[^4] and say, "Return, children of humankind."[^5]

**4** For a thousand years in your sight are like yesterday when it has passed, like a watch in the night.[^6]

**5** You sweep them away; they are like a sleep.[^7] In the morning they are like grass that springs up;

**6** in the morning it flourishes and springs up; by evening it withers and dries up.

**7** For we are consumed by your anger, and by your wrath we are terrified.

**8** You have set our iniquities before you, our secret sins in the light of your face.

**9** For all our days pass away under your fury; we finish our years like a sigh.[^8]

**10** The days of our years are seventy years, or eighty if we are strong;[^9] yet the best of them[^10] is toil and trouble, for they are soon gone, and we fly away.

**11** Who knows the power of your anger? Your wrath is as great as the fear that is due to you.[^11]

**12** So teach us to count our days, so that we may gain a heart of wisdom.

**13** Turn back, Yahweh![^12] How long? Have compassion on your servants.[^13]

**14** Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love,[^14] so that we may sing for joy and be glad all our days.

**15** Make us glad for as many days as you have afflicted us, for as many years as we have seen trouble.

**16** Let your work be seen by your servants, and your splendor by their children.

**17** May the favor[^15] of the Lord our God be on us. Establish the work of our hands for us; yes, establish the work of our hands.

[^1]: *Of Moses*: or "for Moses". Moses is called "the man of God" also in Deuteronomy 33:1. This is the only psalm with Moses' name in its title. Psalm 90 begins Book 4 of the Psalms (Psalms 90–106). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ma'on*, "dwelling, refuge". The Septuagint reads "refuge".
[^3]: Literally "before you writhed in labor with the earth and the world", an image of giving birth. Or "before the earth and the world were brought forth".
[^4]: Hebrew *dakka*, "what is crushed", that is, dust (compare Genesis 3:19).
[^5]: Literally "sons of *adam*". *Return* may mean "return to dust", or "turn back" to God.
[^6]: One of the three parts into which the night was divided. 2 Peter 3:8 echoes this verse.
[^7]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "You flood them away; they become sleep".
[^8]: Or "a murmur", "a breath".
[^9]: Literally "or if with strength, eighty years".
[^10]: Literally "their pride".
[^11]: Literally "and according to your fear, your wrath".
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^13]: Or "Relent concerning your servants". Moses uses the same words, "turn" and "relent", when he prays for the people in Exodus 32:12.
[^14]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^15]: Hebrew *no'am*, "pleasantness, kindness, beauty".
