**1** A song. A psalm of David.[^1]

**2** My heart is steadfast, God; I will sing and make music, with my glory too.[^2]

**3** Wake up, harp and lyre! I will wake the dawn.

**4** I will give thanks to you among the peoples, Yahweh;[^3] I will sing praise to you among the nations.

**5** For your faithful love[^4] is great, above the heavens,[^5] and your faithfulness to the clouds.

**6** Be exalted above the heavens, God; let your glory be over all the earth.

**7** So that those you love may be rescued, save by your right hand, and answer me.[^6]

**8** God has spoken in his holiness:[^7] "I will exult; I will divide up Shechem, and measure out the Valley of Succoth.

**9** Gilead is mine, Manasseh is mine; Ephraim is the helmet on my head;[^8] Judah is my scepter.[^9]

**10** Moab is my washbasin; on Edom I throw my sandal;[^10] over Philistia I shout in triumph."[^11]

**11** Who will bring me to the fortified city?[^12] Who will lead me to Edom?[^13]

**12** Have you not rejected us, God? You no longer go out, God, with our armies.

**13** Give us help against the adversary, for human help is worthless.[^14]

**14** With God we will do valiantly; he will trample our adversaries.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–14 are 108:1–13 in most English Bibles. The psalm joins two passages found elsewhere, with small differences: verses 2–6 are 57:8–12 (57:7–11 in most English Bibles), and verses 7–14 are 60:7–14 (60:5–12 in most English Bibles). The notes give the main differences.
[^2]: Hebrew *af-kevodi*, "also my glory", perhaps meaning "with my whole being" (compare 16:9; 57:9, English 57:8). Psalm 57:8–9 (57:7–8 in most English Bibles) repeats "my heart is steadfast" and has "Wake up, my glory!"
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Psalm 57:10 (57:9 in most English Bibles) has "Lord".
[^4]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^5]: Psalm 57:11 (57:10 in most English Bibles) has "reaching to the heavens".
[^6]: Psalm 60:7 (60:5 in most English Bibles) is written "answer us" but read "answer me".
[^7]: Or "in his holy place".
[^8]: Literally "the stronghold of my head".
[^9]: Or "my ruler's staff" (compare Genesis 49:10).
[^10]: Throwing a sandal on land was perhaps a sign of taking possession of it (compare Ruth 4:7).
[^11]: Psalm 60:10 (60:8 in most English Bibles) has "Philistia, shout aloud because of me!"
[^12]: Hebrew *ir mivtsar*. Psalm 60:11 (60:9 in most English Bibles) has *ir matsor*, with the same meaning.
[^13]: Literally "Who has led me to Edom?", as in 60:11 (60:9 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Literally "the salvation of *adam* (humankind) is worthless".
