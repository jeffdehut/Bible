**1** Why, Yahweh,[^1] do you stand far off? Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble?[^2]

**2** In his arrogance the wicked hotly pursues the afflicted. Let them be caught in the schemes they have devised.[^3]

**3** For the wicked boasts of what he desires,[^4] and the greedy one curses[^5] and despises Yahweh.

**4** The wicked, in his pride,[^6] does not seek him.[^7] All his thoughts are, "There is no God."

**5** His ways[^8] prosper at all times. Your judgments are high above him, out of his sight; he sneers[^9] at all his adversaries.

**6** He says in his heart, "I will not be shaken. Through all generations I will never meet trouble."

**7** His mouth is full of curses, deceit and oppression;[^10] under his tongue are trouble and wickedness.

**8** He sits in ambush in the villages; in hiding places he murders the innocent. His eyes secretly watch for the helpless.[^11]

**9** He lies in wait in secret like a lion in its thicket; he lies in wait to seize the afflicted. He seizes the afflicted and drags him off in his net.

**10** He crouches, he bows down, and a crowd of the wretched[^12] fall by his might.[^13]

**11** He says in his heart, "God has forgotten; he has hidden his face; he will never see."

**12** Rise up, Yahweh! God, lift up your hand. Do not forget the humble.[^14]

**13** Why does the wicked despise God? He says in his heart, "You will not call me to account."

**14** But you do see! For you take note of trouble and grief, to take the matter into your hand. The helpless commit themselves to you; you have been the helper of the fatherless.

**15** Break the arm of the wicked and the evil one; call his wickedness to account until you find no more.

**16** Yahweh is King forever and ever; the nations have perished from his land.

**17** You have heard the desire of the humble, Yahweh; you strengthen their heart; you listen closely,[^15]

**18** to do justice for the fatherless and the oppressed, so that mortals who are of the earth may strike terror no more.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Psalm 10 has no title. It continues the acrostic pattern of Psalm 9 (see the note at 9:2), and the Septuagint treats the two as one psalm.
[^3]: Or "The afflicted are caught in the schemes that the wicked have devised."
[^4]: Literally "of the desire of his soul (*nephesh*)".
[^5]: Literally "blesses", a way of avoiding the words "curses God". Or "he blesses the greedy and despises Yahweh".
[^6]: Literally "according to the height of his nose".
[^7]: Or "says, 'He will not call to account.'"
[^8]: The Hebrew text is written "his way" but read "his ways".
[^9]: Literally "he puffs".
[^10]: Romans 3:14 quotes the first part of this verse.
[^11]: Hebrew *chelekah*, a rare word found only in this psalm (also verses 10 and 14), probably "the helpless" or "the unfortunate".
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written as one word, *chelka'im*, "the helpless" (compare verse 8), but read as two, *chel ka'im*, "a host of the dejected".
[^13]: Or "by his claws", "by his strong ones". The verse can also be read with the victim as the subject: "He is crushed, he sinks down".
[^14]: The Hebrew text is written "the afflicted" (*aniyyim*) but read "the humble" (*anavim*).
[^15]: Literally "you incline your ear".
