**1** For the director. With stringed instruments. A psalm of David.[^1]

**2** When I call, answer me, God of my righteousness![^2] When I was in distress, you gave me room.[^3] Be gracious to me and hear my prayer.

**3** You people,[^4] how long will you turn my honor into shame? How long will you love what is worthless and seek after lies?[^5] Selah.[^6]

**4** Know that Yahweh[^7] has set apart the faithful one[^8] for himself; Yahweh hears when I call to him.

**5** Tremble,[^9] and do not sin. Search your hearts[^10] on your beds, and be still. Selah.

**6** Offer right sacrifices,[^11] and trust in Yahweh.

**7** Many are saying, "Who will show us good?" Let the light of your face shine on us, Yahweh.[^12]

**8** You have put more joy in my heart than they have when their grain and new wine abound.

**9** In peace I will lie down and sleep at once,[^13] for you alone, Yahweh, make me live in safety.[^14]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–9 are 4:1–8 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "my righteous God", "God who upholds my right".
[^3]: Literally "in the narrow place you made it wide for me".
[^4]: Literally "Sons of a man", perhaps "men of rank".
[^5]: Or "love worthless things and seek false gods".
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 5.
[^7]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful, loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^9]: Or "Be angry". The Septuagint reads "Be angry, and do not sin", the wording quoted in Ephesians 4:26.
[^10]: Literally "Speak in your hearts", that is, think it over.
[^11]: Literally "sacrifices of righteousness".
[^12]: Literally "Lift up over us the light of your face", that is, look on us with favor (compare Numbers 6:25–26).
[^13]: Literally "together", that is, both lie down and sleep.
[^14]: Or "for you, Yahweh, make me live alone, in safety", that is, untroubled.
