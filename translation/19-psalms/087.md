**1** Of the sons of Korah. A psalm. A song.[^1] The city he founded stands on the holy mountains.[^2]

**2** The LORD[^3] loves the gates of Zion more than all the dwellings of Jacob.

**3** Glorious things are spoken of you, city of God. Selah.[^4]

**4** "I will record Rahab[^5] and Babylon among those who know me; look, Philistia and Tyre, with Cush:[^6] 'This one was born there.'"[^7]

**5** And of Zion it will be said, "Each one and every one[^8] was born in her," and the Most High[^9] himself will establish her.

**6** The LORD will record, as he registers the peoples,[^10] "This one was born there." Selah.

**7** Singers and dancers alike say,[^11] "All my springs are in you."

[^1]: *Of the sons of Korah*: or "for the sons of Korah", a family of Levites who served as temple singers. In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "His foundation is on the holy mountains", that is, Zion, the city God founded.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 6.
[^5]: Hebrew *Rahav*, a poetic name for Egypt (Isaiah 30:7), taken from the name of a sea monster (89:11, 89:10 in most English Bibles; Job 26:12). It is not the Rahab of Joshua 2, whose name is spelled differently in Hebrew.
[^6]: The land on the upper Nile, south of Egypt.
[^7]: That is, people of these nations will be counted as born in Zion, as its own citizens.
[^8]: Literally "a man and a man".
[^9]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^10]: Literally "in the writing of the peoples", that is, in the register of citizens.
[^11]: Literally "And singers like dancers: all my springs are in you." The Hebrew is very brief, and "say" is supplied. *Dancers*: or "flute players". *Springs*: sources of life and blessing.
