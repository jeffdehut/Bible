**1** A song. A psalm of the sons of Korah.[^1]

**2** Great is Yahweh,[^2] and most worthy of praise, in the city of our God, his holy mountain.

**3** Beautiful in its height, the joy of all the earth, is Mount Zion, the heights of Zaphon,[^3] the city of the great King.

**4** God, in her citadels, has shown himself a fortress.

**5** For see, the kings assembled; they advanced together.

**6** They saw, and they were astounded; they were terrified and fled in haste.

**7** Trembling seized them there, pain like a woman in labor.

**8** With an east wind you shatter the ships of Tarshish.[^4]

**9** As we have heard, so we have seen, in the city of Yahweh of Armies,[^5] in the city of our God: God will establish it forever. Selah.[^6]

**10** We ponder your faithful love,[^7] God, within your temple.

**11** Like your name, God, your praise reaches to the ends of the earth; your right hand is full of righteousness.

**12** Let Mount Zion rejoice; let the daughters of Judah[^8] be glad because of your judgments.

**13** Walk around Zion, go all around her; count her towers.

**14** Consider well her ramparts; pass through her citadels,[^9] so that you may tell the next generation

**15** that this is God, our God, forever and ever. He will guide us until death.[^10]

[^1]: *Of the sons of Korah*: or "for the sons of Korah", a family of Levites who served as temple singers. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–15 are 48:1–14 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "in the far north". *Tsaphon*, "north", was also the name of a mountain in Syria where Canaanite myth placed the home of the gods (compare Isaiah 14:13).
[^4]: Large seagoing ships.
[^5]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^7]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^8]: That is, the towns of Judah.
[^9]: Hebrew *passegu*, found only here; the meaning is uncertain, perhaps "view".
[^10]: Hebrew *al-mut*, "until death" or "over death". The Septuagint reads "forever". Some scholars take the words as a musical direction, like *almut labben* in the title of Psalm 9.
