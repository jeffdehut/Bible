**1** A psalm. A song for the Sabbath day.[^1]

**2** It is good to give thanks to Yahweh,[^2] and to sing praise to your name, Most High,[^3]

**3** to declare your faithful love[^4] in the morning, and your faithfulness by night,

**4** on the ten-stringed instrument and on the harp, to the melody[^5] of the lyre.

**5** For you have made me glad, Yahweh, by what you have done; I sing for joy at the works of your hands.

**6** How great are your works, Yahweh! How very deep are your thoughts!

**7** The senseless man does not know, and the fool does not understand this:

**8** though the wicked sprout like grass, and all evildoers flourish, they will be destroyed forever.

**9** But you, Yahweh, are on high forever.

**10** For look, your enemies, Yahweh, for look, your enemies will perish; all evildoers will be scattered.

**11** But you have raised my horn like that of the wild ox;[^6] I am anointed with fresh oil.[^7]

**12** My eyes have looked in triumph on those who lie in wait for me; my ears have heard the downfall of the evildoers who rise against me.[^8]

**13** The righteous will flourish like the palm tree, and grow tall like a cedar in Lebanon.

**14** Planted in the house of Yahweh, they will flourish in the courts of our God.

**15** They will still bear fruit in old age; they will stay full of sap and green,

**16** to declare that Yahweh is upright; he is my rock, and there is no injustice in him.

[^1]: This is the only psalm whose title names the Sabbath. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–16 are 92:1–15 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^4]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^5]: Hebrew *higgayon*, "a murmuring sound", perhaps the resounding music of the lyre. Compare 9:17, where it is a musical term.
[^6]: A symbol of strength. Hebrew *re'em*, the wild ox.
[^7]: Hebrew *balloti*, of uncertain meaning, probably "I am anointed" or "you have poured over me". The Septuagint reads "my old age with rich oil", taking the same consonants from another verb.
[^8]: Literally "My eye has looked on those who watch me; of those who rise against me, evildoers, my ears hear."
