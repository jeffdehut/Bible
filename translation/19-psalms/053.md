**1** For the director. According to Mahalath.[^1] A maskil of David.[^2]

**2** The fool[^3] says in his heart, "There is no God." They are corrupt; they do detestable wrong; there is no one who does good.

**3** God looks down from heaven on human beings, to see if there is anyone who understands, anyone who seeks God.

**4** They have all turned back;[^4] together they have become corrupt. There is no one who does good, not even one.[^5]

**5** Do they know nothing, the evildoers, who eat up my people as they eat bread, and do not call on God?

**6** There they are, in great terror, where there was nothing to fear! For God has scattered the bones of those who besieged you;[^6] you have put them to shame, for God has rejected them.[^7]

**7** Oh, that salvation for Israel would come from Zion! When God restores the fortunes of his people,[^8] let Jacob rejoice, let Israel be glad.

[^1]: Hebrew *al-machalat*, a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps a tune, or "for sickness" (from *chalah*, "be sick"). Also in the title of Psalm 88.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Maskil*: a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "a skillful psalm" or "a psalm of instruction". *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–7 are 53:1–6 in most English Bibles. Psalm 53 is almost the same as Psalm 14. It has *Elohim*, "God", where Psalm 14 has "Yahweh" (see the note on 42:2), and its verse 6 differs from 14:5–6.
[^3]: Hebrew *naval*, someone who is morally and spiritually senseless, not merely unintelligent.
[^4]: Hebrew *sag*. Psalm 14:3 has *sar*, "turned aside".
[^5]: Romans 3:10–12 quotes verses 2–4 freely (compare 14:1–3).
[^6]: Literally "the bones of the one who camps against you".
[^7]: Psalm 14:5–6 reads instead "There they are, in great terror, for God is with the generation of the righteous. You would frustrate the plans of the afflicted, but Yahweh is their refuge."
[^8]: Or "brings back his captive people".
