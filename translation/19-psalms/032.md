**1** Of David. A maskil.[^1] Happy[^2] is the one whose offense is forgiven, whose sin is covered.[^3]

**2** Happy is the person to whom the LORD[^4] does not count guilt, and in whose spirit there is no deceit.

**3** When I kept silent, my bones wasted away through my groaning[^5] all day long.

**4** For day and night your hand was heavy on me; my strength was dried up[^6] as in the heat of summer. Selah.[^7]

**5** I made my sin known to you, and I did not cover up my guilt. I said, "I will confess my offenses to the LORD," and you forgave the guilt of my sin. Selah.

**6** So let everyone who is faithful[^8] pray to you while you may be found.[^9] Surely when the great waters overflow, they will not reach him.

**7** You are my hiding place; you protect me from trouble; you surround me with shouts of deliverance.[^10] Selah.

**8** I will instruct you and teach you in the way you should go; I will counsel you with my eye on you.[^11]

**9** Do not be like a horse or a mule, without understanding, which must be held in with bit and bridle, or it will not come near you.[^12]

**10** Many are the pains of the wicked, but the one who trusts in the LORD is surrounded by faithful love.[^13]

**11** Rejoice in the LORD and be glad, you righteous; shout for joy, all you upright in heart.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". *Maskil*: a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "a skillful psalm" or "a psalm of instruction". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed". Also in verse 2.
[^3]: Romans 4:7–8 quotes verses 1–2.
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "my roaring".
[^6]: Literally "my sap was turned". Hebrew *leshad*, a rare word, perhaps "vitality, moisture".
[^7]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verses 5 and 7.
[^8]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful, loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^9]: Literally "at a time of finding".
[^10]: The Hebrew of this phrase is unusual. Literally "shouts of escape".
[^11]: Here the LORD seems to speak, though the speaker may be the psalmist teaching others.
[^12]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "with bit and bridle, its ornament, to curb it; not coming near to you".
[^13]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
