**1** A miktam[^1] of David.[^2] Keep me safe, God, for in you I take refuge.

**2** I said[^3] to the LORD,[^4] "You are my Lord; I have nothing good apart from you."[^5]

**3** As for the holy ones who are in the land, they are the noble ones in whom is all my delight.[^6]

**4** The sorrows[^7] of those who hurry after another god will multiply. I will not pour out their drink offerings of blood, or take their names on my lips.

**5** LORD, you are my allotted portion and my cup; you hold my lot.

**6** The boundary lines have fallen for me in pleasant places; yes, I have a beautiful inheritance.

**7** I will bless the LORD, who counsels me; even at night my heart[^8] instructs me.

**8** I have set the LORD always before me; because he is at my right hand, I will not be shaken.[^9]

**9** So my heart is glad and my whole being rejoices;[^10] my body too will rest secure.[^11]

**10** For you will not abandon my life[^12] to Sheol;[^13] you will not let your faithful one[^14] see the pit.[^15]

**11** You make known to me the path of life. In your presence there is fullness of joy; at your right hand there are pleasures forever.

[^1]: Hebrew *miktam*, a term of uncertain meaning. The Septuagint understood it as "an inscription" on a stone. Also in the titles of Psalms 56–60.
[^2]: Or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^3]: The Hebrew reads "you said", addressed to a woman, perhaps to the speaker's own soul. The Septuagint reads "I said".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "my good is not beyond you".
[^6]: The Hebrew of verses 3–4 is difficult. "The holy ones" may be God's faithful people in the land, or the gods worshiped there, which verse 4 rejects.
[^7]: The word for "sorrows" (*atsvot*) sounds like a word for "idols" (*atsabbim*).
[^8]: Literally "my kidneys", thought of as the seat of the emotions and the inner life.
[^9]: Acts 2:25–28 quotes verses 8–11 from the Septuagint.
[^10]: Literally "my glory (*kavod*) rejoices". The Septuagint reads "my tongue rejoices", the wording quoted in Acts 2:26.
[^11]: Literally "my flesh will dwell in safety". The Septuagint reads "my flesh will dwell in hope" (Acts 2:26).
[^12]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^13]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^14]: Hebrew *chasid*, "faithful, loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^15]: Hebrew *shachat*, "pit", a name for the grave and the realm of the dead (as in 30:10, 30:9 in most English Bibles). The Septuagint renders it "corruption" or "decay", and Acts 2:27, 31 and 13:35 quote this verse in that form.
