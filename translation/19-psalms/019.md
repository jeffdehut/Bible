**1** For the director. A psalm of David.[^1]

**2** The heavens declare the glory of God; the dome of the sky[^2] proclaims the work of his hands.

**3** Day after day they pour out speech; night after night they make knowledge known.

**4** There is no speech, there are no words; their voice is not heard.[^3]

**5** Yet their line[^4] goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. In the heavens he has set a tent for the sun,[^5]

**6** which comes out like a bridegroom from his chamber, and rejoices like a champion to run the course.

**7** It rises at one end of the heavens and makes its circuit to the other end; nothing is hidden from its heat.

**8** The law[^6] of the LORD[^7] is perfect, restoring the soul;[^8] the statutes[^9] of the LORD are trustworthy, making the simple wise.

**9** The precepts of the LORD are right, giving joy to the heart; the command of the LORD is pure, giving light to the eyes.

**10** The fear of the LORD is clean, enduring forever; the rulings of the LORD are true, and righteous altogether.

**11** They are more desirable than gold, than much fine gold; they are sweeter than honey, than honey dripping from the comb.

**12** By them your servant is warned; in keeping them there is great reward.

**13** Who can discern his own errors? Clear me from hidden faults.

**14** Keep your servant also from willful sins;[^10] do not let them rule over me. Then I will be blameless, and innocent of great wrongdoing.

**15** May the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be pleasing to you, LORD, my rock and my redeemer.[^11]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–15 are 19:1–14 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *raqia*, the sky pictured as a solid vault (Genesis 1:6–8). Traditionally "firmament".
[^3]: Or "There is no speech or language where their voice is not heard."
[^4]: Hebrew *qav*, "line", perhaps a measuring line that marks out their domain. The Septuagint reads "their sound", the wording quoted in Romans 10:18.
[^5]: Literally "in them", that is, in the heavens.
[^6]: Hebrew *torah*, "instruction, teaching". Verses 8–10 use six words for God's teaching. This translation keeps one English word for each: *law*, *statutes*, *precepts*, *command*, *fear* and *rulings*.
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Or "renewing life".
[^9]: Hebrew *edut*, traditionally "testimony": the solemn terms of the covenant.
[^10]: Or "from the arrogant".
[^11]: Hebrew *go'el*, a close relative with the duty to protect and rescue a family member in need (see Ruth 2:20).
