**1** A song of ascents.[^1] I lift up my eyes to the mountains. Where does my help come from?

**2** My help comes from Yahweh,[^2] the maker of heaven and earth.

**3** He will not let your foot slip;[^3] the one who guards you[^4] will not slumber.

**4** Look, the one who guards Israel will neither slumber nor sleep.

**5** Yahweh is the one who guards you; Yahweh is your shade at your right hand.

**6** The sun will not strike you by day, nor the moon by night.[^5]

**7** Yahweh will guard you from all harm; he will guard your life.[^6]

**8** Yahweh will guard your going out and your coming in,[^7] from now on and forever.

[^1]: Here the Hebrew has *shir lamma'alot*, "a song for the ascents", a slightly different form of the title of Psalms 120–134 (see the note on 120:1). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "May he not let your foot slip; may the one who guards you not slumber."
[^4]: Hebrew *shomer*, "guard, keeper", from *shamar*, "guard, keep", the word that runs through verses 3–8.
[^5]: The sun's heat could cause sunstroke, and the moon was thought to cause harm by night.
[^6]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^7]: That is, all that you do, wherever you go.
