**1** For the director. Of David. To bring to remembrance.[^1]

**2** God, come to rescue me;[^2] Yahweh,[^3] come quickly to help me!

**3** Let those who seek my life[^4] be put to shame and confounded; let those who delight in my harm be turned back and disgraced.

**4** Let those who say, "Aha! Aha!" turn back because of their shame.[^5]

**5** But let all who seek you rejoice and be glad in you; let those who love your salvation say always, "Great is God!"[^6]

**6** As for me, I am afflicted and needy; God, come quickly to me! You are my help and my deliverer; Yahweh, do not delay.[^7]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". *To bring to remembrance*: Hebrew *lehazkir* (see the note on the title of Psalm 38). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–6 are 70:1–5 in most English Bibles. Psalm 70 is almost the same as 40:14–18 (40:13–17 in most English Bibles). In several places it has *Elohim*, "God", where Psalm 40 has "Yahweh" (see the note on 42:2), and the notes below give the other differences.
[^2]: Literally "God, to rescue me". Psalm 40:14 (40:13 in most English Bibles) has "Be pleased, Yahweh, to rescue me".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Psalm 40:15 (40:14 in most English Bibles) adds "together" and "to sweep it away".
[^5]: Literally "turn back on the heel of their shame". Psalm 40:16 (40:15 in most English Bibles) has "Let them be appalled because of their shame", and "who say to me".
[^6]: Psalm 40:17 (40:16 in most English Bibles) has "Great is Yahweh!"
[^7]: Psalm 40:18 (40:17 in most English Bibles) has "may the Lord think of me" for "God, come quickly to me", and "my God, do not delay" for "Yahweh, do not delay".
