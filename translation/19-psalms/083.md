**1** A song. A psalm of Asaph.[^1]

**2** God, do not keep silent; do not be quiet, and do not be still, God.

**3** For look, your enemies are in an uproar; those who hate you have raised their heads.

**4** Against your people they make crafty plans; they conspire against those you shelter.[^2]

**5** They say, "Come, let us wipe them out as a nation, so that the name of Israel will be remembered no more."

**6** For they conspire with one mind;[^3] against you they make a covenant:

**7** the tents of Edom and the Ishmaelites, Moab and the Hagrites,[^4]

**8** Gebal[^5] and Ammon and Amalek, Philistia with the people of Tyre.

**9** Assyria too has joined them; they have become the strong arm of the descendants of Lot.[^6] Selah.[^7]

**10** Do to them as you did to Midian,[^8] as to Sisera and Jabin at the Kishon River,[^9]

**11** who were destroyed at En-dor,[^10] who became dung for the ground.

**12** Make their nobles like Oreb and Zeeb, and all their princes like Zebah and Zalmunna,[^11]

**13** who said, "Let us take possession for ourselves of the pastures of God."[^12]

**14** My God, make them like whirling dust,[^13] like chaff before the wind.

**15** As fire burns a forest, and as a flame sets the mountains ablaze,

**16** so pursue them with your tempest, and terrify them with your storm.

**17** Fill their faces with shame, so that they will seek your name, Yahweh.[^14]

**18** Let them be put to shame and terrified forever; let them be disgraced and perish.

**19** Let them know that you alone, whose name is Yahweh, are the Most High[^15] over all the earth.

[^1]: *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–19 are 83:1–18 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "against your hidden ones", those whom God keeps hidden and safe (compare 27:5).
[^3]: Literally "with heart together".
[^4]: A people living east of Gilead (1 Chronicles 5:10, 19–20).
[^5]: Probably the Phoenician city of Gebal, later called Byblos (compare Joshua 13:5). Some think instead of a region south of the Dead Sea, near Edom.
[^6]: Literally "they have become an arm to the sons of Lot", that is, Moab and Ammon (Genesis 19:36–38).
[^7]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^8]: The victory of Gideon over Midian is told in Judges 7–8.
[^9]: The victory over Sisera and Jabin is told in Judges 4–5. Hebrew *nachal*, a stream or wadi.
[^10]: En-dor is not named in Judges 4–5. It lay near Mount Tabor and the plain where the battle was fought (compare Joshua 17:11).
[^11]: The leaders of Midian (Judges 7:25; 8:21).
[^12]: That is, the land that belongs to God.
[^13]: Hebrew *galgal*, "wheel", perhaps the dry heads of wild plants that the wind rolls along like wheels (compare Isaiah 17:13).
[^14]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^15]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
