**1** A Shiggaion[^1] of David, which he sang to Yahweh[^2] concerning Cush, a Benjaminite.[^3]

**2** Yahweh my God, in you I take refuge. Save me from all who pursue me, and rescue me,

**3** or like a lion he will tear me[^4] to pieces, dragging me away with no one to rescue me.

**4** Yahweh my God, if I have done this, if there is wrong on my hands,

**5** if I have repaid my friend[^5] with evil, or plundered my adversary without cause,[^6]

**6** then let the enemy pursue me[^7] and overtake me; let him trample my life to the ground, and lay my honor[^8] in the dust. Selah.[^9]

**7** Rise up, Yahweh, in your anger; lift yourself up against the fury of my adversaries. Awake for me; you have decreed justice.[^10]

**8** Let the assembly of the peoples gather around you, and over it return on high.

**9** Yahweh judges the peoples. Judge me, Yahweh, according to my righteousness, and according to the integrity that is in me.

**10** Let the evil of the wicked come to an end, but establish the righteous, you who test hearts and minds,[^11] righteous God!

**11** My shield is in God's hands,[^12] who saves the upright in heart.

**12** God is a righteous judge, a God who is angry every day.

**13** If someone does not turn back, he will sharpen his sword; he has bent his bow and made it ready.[^13]

**14** He has prepared his deadly weapons; he makes his arrows into flaming shafts.

**15** See, he is in labor with evil; he conceives trouble and gives birth to lies.

**16** He digs a pit and hollows it out, and falls into the hole he has made.

**17** His trouble comes back on his own head; his violence comes down on his own skull.[^14]

**18** I will give thanks to Yahweh for his righteousness, and sing praise to the name of Yahweh Most High.[^15]

[^1]: Hebrew *shiggayon*, a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps a lament, or a song with a changing rhythm (from *shagah*, "wander, stray"). Compare Habakkuk 3:1.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". Literally "concerning the words of Cush, a Benjaminite"; Cush is not mentioned elsewhere. In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–18 are 7:1–17 in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^5]: Literally "the one at peace with me".
[^6]: The verb usually means "rescue". Or "when I have spared my adversary without cause".
[^7]: Literally "my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^8]: Hebrew *kavod*, "glory, honor". Here it may stand for the self, the whole person.
[^9]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^10]: Or "Awake for me to the judgment you have commanded".
[^11]: Literally "hearts and kidneys". The kidneys were thought of as the seat of the emotions and the inner life.
[^12]: Literally "My shield is upon God", that is, God is responsible for my protection.
[^13]: The Hebrew of verses 13–14 can be read in two ways: God sharpens his sword against anyone who does not repent, or the wicked person sharpens his sword (and so prepares his own death, verses 15–17). Verse 13 may also be read "Surely he will sharpen his sword again".
[^14]: Literally "on the crown of his head".
[^15]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
