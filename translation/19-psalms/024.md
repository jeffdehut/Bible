**1** Of David. A psalm.[^1] The earth is the LORD's,[^2] and everything in it, the world and those who live in it.[^3]

**2** For he founded it on the seas, and established it on the rivers.

**3** Who may go up the mountain of the LORD? Who may stand in his holy place?

**4** The one who has clean hands and a pure heart, who has not lifted up my soul to what is false,[^4] and has not sworn deceitfully.

**5** He will receive blessing from the LORD, and righteousness[^5] from the God of his salvation.

**6** Such is the generation of those who seek him, who seek your face, Jacob.[^6] Selah.[^7]

**7** Lift up your heads, you gates! Be lifted up, you ancient doors,[^8] so that the King of glory may come in.

**8** Who is this King of glory? The LORD, strong and mighty, the LORD, mighty in battle!

**9** Lift up your heads, you gates! Lift them up, you ancient doors, so that the King of glory may come in.

**10** Who is he, this King of glory? The LORD of Armies,[^9] he is the King of glory! Selah.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: 1 Corinthians 10:26 quotes the first line of this verse.
[^4]: "My" is probably the LORD's own self: the line echoes the command not to misuse the LORD's name, literally "lift up" his name "to what is false" (Exodus 20:7), that is, not to swear falsely by him. Some Hebrew manuscripts read "his soul (*nephesh*)": "who has not set his heart on what is false".
[^5]: Or "vindication". Hebrew *tsedaqah*.
[^6]: The Septuagint reads "who seek the face of the God of Jacob".
[^7]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 10.
[^8]: Literally "doors of long ago", or "everlasting doors".
[^9]: *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
