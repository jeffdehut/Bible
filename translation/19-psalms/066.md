**1** For the director. A song. A psalm.[^1] Shout for joy to God, all the earth!

**2** Sing praises to the glory of his name; make his praise glorious.

**3** Say to God, "How awesome are your deeds! Because of your great power your enemies cringe before you.

**4** All the earth bows down to you and sings praises to you; they sing praises to your name." Selah.[^2]

**5** Come and see the works of God, awesome in his deeds toward human beings.

**6** He turned the sea into dry land; they passed through the river on foot. There we rejoiced in him.[^3]

**7** He rules by his might forever; his eyes keep watch on the nations. Let the rebellious not exalt themselves. Selah.

**8** Bless our God, you peoples; let the sound of his praise be heard,

**9** who has kept us among the living,[^4] and has not let our feet slip.

**10** For you have tested us, God; you have refined us as silver is refined.

**11** You brought us into the net;[^5] you laid a heavy burden on our backs.[^6]

**12** You let people ride over our heads; we went through fire and through water, but you brought us out to a place of abundance.[^7]

**13** I will come into your house with burnt offerings; I will fulfill my vows to you,

**14** the vows my lips uttered and my mouth spoke when I was in trouble.

**15** I will offer you burnt offerings of fat animals, with the smoke of rams; I will offer bulls with goats. Selah.

**16** Come and hear, all you who fear God, and I will tell what he has done for me.[^8]

**17** I cried aloud to him with my mouth, and his praise[^9] was on my tongue.

**18** If I had cherished wickedness in my heart, the Lord would not have listened.

**19** But truly God has listened; he has paid attention to the voice of my prayer.

**20** Blessed be God, who has not turned away my prayer, or his faithful love[^10] from me.

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verses 7 and 15.
[^3]: Or "There let us rejoice in him". Compare the crossing of the sea (Exodus 14) and of the Jordan (Joshua 3).
[^4]: Literally "who set our soul (*nephesh*) in life".
[^5]: Or "into prison", "into a stronghold".
[^6]: Literally "on our loins".
[^7]: Literally "to saturation". The Septuagint reads "to refreshment".
[^8]: Literally "for my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^9]: Literally "exaltation".
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
