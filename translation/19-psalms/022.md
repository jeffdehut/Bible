**1** For the director. According to "The Doe of the Dawn".[^1] A psalm of David.[^2]

**2** My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?[^3] Why are you so far from saving me, so far from the words of my groaning?[^4]

**3** My God, I cry out by day, but you do not answer, and by night, but I find no rest.

**4** Yet you are holy, enthroned on the praises of Israel.[^5]

**5** In you our ancestors trusted; they trusted, and you rescued them.

**6** To you they cried out and were saved; in you they trusted and were not put to shame.

**7** But I am a worm and not a man, scorned by everyone,[^6] despised by the people.

**8** All who see me mock me; they sneer at me[^7] and shake their heads:

**9** "Commit yourself to the LORD![^8] Let him rescue him; let him deliver him, since he delights in him!"[^9]

**10** Yet you are the one who brought me out of the womb, who made me feel safe at my mother's breasts.

**11** On you I was cast from birth;[^10] from my mother's womb you have been my God.

**12** Do not be far from me, for trouble is near, and there is no one to help.

**13** Many bulls surround me; strong bulls of Bashan[^11] encircle me.

**14** They open their mouths wide against me, like a ravening and roaring lion.

**15** I am poured out like water, and all my bones are out of joint. My heart has become like wax; it melts within me.[^12]

**16** My strength is dried up like a piece of broken pottery, and my tongue sticks to the roof of my mouth;[^13] you lay me in the dust of death.

**17** For dogs surround me; a band of evildoers encircles me. Like a lion they attack my hands and my feet.[^14]

**18** I can count all my bones; they stare and gloat over me.

**19** They divide my clothes among them, and for my garment they cast lots.[^15]

**20** But you, LORD, do not be far away; my strength, come quickly to help me.

**21** Rescue my life[^16] from the sword, my only life[^17] from the power of the dog.

**22** Save me from the mouth of the lion, from the horns of the wild oxen. You have answered me![^18]

**23** I will tell of your name to my brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will praise you.[^19]

**24** You who fear the LORD, praise him! All you descendants of Jacob, honor him! Stand in awe of him, all you descendants of Israel!

**25** For he has not despised or scorned the suffering of the afflicted; he has not hidden his face from him, but when he cried to him for help, he heard.

**26** From you comes my praise in the great assembly; I will fulfill my vows before those who fear him.

**27** The humble will eat and be satisfied; those who seek the LORD will praise him. May your hearts live forever!

**28** All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the LORD, and all the families of the nations will bow down before you.

**29** For kingship belongs to the LORD, and he rules over the nations.

**30** All the prosperous of the earth will eat and bow down; all who go down to the dust will kneel before him, those who cannot keep themselves alive.[^20]

**31** Descendants will serve him; people will tell of the Lord to the next generation.[^21]

**32** They will come and proclaim his righteousness to a people yet to be born: he has done it!

[^1]: Hebrew *ayyelet hashachar*, probably the name of a tune.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–32 are 22:1–31 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *eli eli lamah azavtani*. Jesus cries out these words from the cross, in Aramaic, in Mark 15:34 and Matthew 27:46.
[^4]: Literally "my roaring", like a lion's.
[^5]: Or "Yet you are the Holy One, enthroned; you are the praise of Israel."
[^6]: Literally "the scorn of humankind (*adam*)".
[^7]: Literally "they open wide with the lip". Mark 15:29 recalls this verse.
[^8]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *Commit yourself*: literally "Roll (it) on the LORD". The Septuagint reads "He hoped in the Lord".
[^9]: Matthew 27:43 echoes this verse.
[^10]: Literally "from the womb".
[^11]: A fertile region east of the Jordan, famous for its cattle.
[^12]: Literally "in the middle of my inner parts".
[^13]: Literally "my jaws".
[^14]: Hebrew *ka'ari yadai veraglai*, literally "like a lion my hands and my feet". The line has no verb; "attack" is supplied from the context. The Septuagint reads "they dug (or pierced) my hands and my feet", and some Hebrew manuscripts have a verb, *ka'aru*, in place of *ka'ari*, "like a lion".
[^15]: Mark 15:24 recalls this verse, and John 19:24 quotes it.
[^16]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^17]: Literally "my only one", that is, my precious life.
[^18]: Literally "and from the horns of the wild oxen you have answered me". The Septuagint reads "and my lowliness from the horns of the wild oxen". At this point the psalm turns from lament to praise.
[^19]: Hebrews 2:12 quotes this verse.
[^20]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "All the fat ones of the earth have eaten and bowed down; before him will kneel all who go down to the dust, and his soul (*nephesh*) he did not keep alive." Or "even the one who could not keep himself alive".
[^21]: Literally "Offspring will serve him; it will be told of the Lord to the generation". Or "Offspring will serve him, a generation that will be told of the Lord".
