**1** A psalm of David.[^1] LORD,[^2] I call to you; come quickly to me. Listen to my voice when I call to you.

**2** May my prayer be set before you like incense, the lifting up of my hands like the evening offering.[^3]

**3** Set a guard, LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips.

**4** Do not let my heart turn to anything evil, to do wicked deeds with those who are evildoers; do not let me eat of their delicacies.

**5** Let a righteous person strike me: it is faithful love;[^4] let him rebuke me: it is oil for my head; let my head not refuse it. For my prayer is still against their evil deeds.[^5]

**6** When their rulers are thrown down from the sides of the cliff,[^6] they will hear my words, for they are pleasant.

**7** As when one plows and breaks up the ground, our bones are scattered at the mouth of Sheol.[^7]

**8** But my eyes are on you, GOD the Lord; in you I take refuge; do not pour out my life.[^8]

**9** Keep me from the trap they have laid for me,[^9] and from the snares of the evildoers.

**10** Let the wicked fall into their own nets,[^10] while I pass by safely.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *minchah*, often the grain offering (Leviticus 2), but also any gift or offering. Incense was offered morning and evening (Exodus 30:7–8), and an offering was made each evening (Exodus 29:39–41; 2 Kings 16:15).
[^4]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love; here, a kindness. Or "Let a righteous person strike me in faithful love and rebuke me", or "If a righteous person strikes me, it is a kindness".
[^5]: Literally "for still, and my prayer in their evils". The Hebrew of verses 5–7 is very difficult, and every rendering of them is uncertain. Or "my prayer continues even in their troubles".
[^6]: Literally "into the hands of the rock". Or "When their judges are handed over to the rock".
[^7]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^8]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Or "do not leave me defenseless". Compare Isaiah 53:12, "he poured out his life to death". *GOD the Lord*: Hebrew *YHWH Adonai*. Next to "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.
[^9]: Literally "from the hands of the trap they have set for me".
[^10]: Literally "into his nets", perhaps God's nets, or each into his own.
