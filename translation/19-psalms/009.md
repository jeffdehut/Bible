**1** For the director. According to Muth-labben.[^1] A psalm of David.[^2]

**2** I will give thanks to Yahweh[^3] with all my heart; I will tell of all your wonderful deeds.[^4]

**3** I will rejoice and exult in you; I will sing praise to your name, Most High.[^5]

**4** When my enemies turn back, they stumble and perish before you.

**5** For you have upheld my right and my cause; you have sat on your throne, judging rightly.

**6** You have rebuked the nations; you have destroyed the wicked; you have wiped out their name forever and ever.

**7** The enemy is finished, in ruins forever;[^6] you have uprooted their cities; the very memory of them has perished.

**8** But Yahweh sits enthroned forever; he has set up his throne for judgment.

**9** He will judge the world in righteousness; he will judge the peoples with fairness.

**10** Yahweh is a stronghold for the oppressed, a stronghold in times of trouble.

**11** Those who know your name will trust in you, for you, Yahweh, have not abandoned those who seek you.

**12** Sing praises to Yahweh, who dwells in Zion; proclaim his deeds among the peoples.

**13** For the one who avenges bloodshed remembers them; he does not forget the cry of the humble.[^7]

**14** Be gracious to me, Yahweh! See how I suffer from those who hate me, you who lift me up from the gates of death,

**15** so that I may tell of all your praises in the gates of Daughter Zion,[^8] and rejoice in your salvation.

**16** The nations have sunk into the pit they made; their foot is caught in the net they hid.

**17** Yahweh has made himself known; he has carried out justice. The wicked are snared by the work of their own hands.[^9] Higgaion.[^10] Selah.[^11]

**18** The wicked will return to Sheol,[^12] all the nations that forget God.

**19** For the needy will not always be forgotten, nor will the hope of the afflicted perish forever.[^13]

**20** Rise up, Yahweh! Do not let mortals prevail; let the nations be judged before you.

**21** Strike them with terror,[^14] Yahweh; let the nations know that they are only mortals. Selah.

[^1]: Hebrew *almut labben*, a term of uncertain meaning. It may be read "on the death of the son", perhaps the name of a tune, or it may be connected with *alamot* (the title of Psalm 46; 1 Chronicles 15:20).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–21 are 9:1–20 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Psalms 9 and 10 together form one incomplete acrostic poem. In the Hebrew, the verses begin with the letters of the alphabet in order, about one letter for every two verses. Psalm 9 runs from *aleph* (verses 2–3) to *kaph* (verse 19), with *dalet* missing. Psalm 10 begins with *lamed* (verse 1) and ends with *qof*, *resh*, *shin* and *tav* (verses 12–17), but in 10:2–11 the pattern is broken. The Septuagint treats Psalms 9 and 10 as a single psalm.
[^5]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^6]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "The enemy, they are finished, ruins forever".
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "the afflicted" (*aniyyim*) but read "the humble" (*anavim*). The two words are closely related.
[^8]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman.
[^9]: The Hebrew vowel points read "he snares the wicked by the work of his hands". The same consonants can be read "the wicked are snared", as here.
[^10]: Hebrew *higgayon*, a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps a musical direction. Elsewhere the word means "meditation" or "murmuring" (Psalms 19:15 and 92:4; 19:14 and 92:3 in English).
[^11]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 21.
[^12]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "the humble" (*anavim*) but read "the afflicted" (*aniyyim*).
[^14]: Literally "Set fear for them". Hebrew *morah*, here probably a spelling of *mora*, "fear"; elsewhere *morah* means "razor".
