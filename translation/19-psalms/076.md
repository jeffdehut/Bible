**1** For the director. With stringed instruments. A psalm of Asaph. A song.[^1]

**2** God is known in Judah; his name is great in Israel.

**3** His tent[^2] is in Salem,[^3] his dwelling place in Zion.

**4** There he broke the flaming arrows of the bow,[^4] the shield, the sword and the weapons of war.[^5] Selah.[^6]

**5** You are radiant, majestic, more than the mountains of prey.[^7]

**6** The stouthearted have been plundered; they have sunk into their sleep; none of the warriors could lift their hands.[^8]

**7** At your rebuke, God of Jacob, both chariot and horse lay stunned.[^9]

**8** You, you are awesome! Who can stand before you once your anger is roused?

**9** From heaven you pronounced judgment; the earth feared and was still,

**10** when God rose up to judge, to save all the humble of the earth. Selah.

**11** Surely human wrath will praise you; with the remnant of wrath you will clothe yourself.[^10]

**12** Make vows to Yahweh[^11] your God, and fulfill them; let all around him bring gifts to the one who is to be feared.

**13** He cuts short the spirit of princes; he is feared by the kings of the earth.

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–13 are 76:1–12 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "his shelter", "his lair", as of a lion.
[^3]: Another name for Jerusalem (compare Genesis 14:18).
[^4]: Literally "the flames of the bow".
[^5]: Literally "and war".
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 10.
[^7]: The Septuagint reads "from the everlasting mountains".
[^8]: Literally "did not find their hands".
[^9]: Literally "fell into a deep sleep".
[^10]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "the remnant of wraths you will gird on". Or "the survivors of wrath will keep your festivals".
[^11]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
