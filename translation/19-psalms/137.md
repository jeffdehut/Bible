**1** By the rivers of Babylon,[^1] there we sat down and wept when we remembered Zion.

**2** On the poplars[^2] there we hung up our lyres.

**3** For there our captors asked us for songs, and our tormentors[^3] asked for joy: "Sing us one of the songs of Zion!"

**4** How can we sing the song of the LORD[^4] in a foreign land?

**5** If I forget you, Jerusalem, may my right hand forget its skill.[^5]

**6** May my tongue stick to the roof of my mouth if I do not remember you, if I do not set Jerusalem above my highest joy.

**7** Remember, LORD, against the Edomites the day of Jerusalem,[^6] when they said, "Tear it down! Tear it down to its foundations!"[^7]

**8** Daughter Babylon,[^8] doomed to destruction,[^9] happy[^10] is the one who pays you back for what you have done to us.

**9** Happy is the one who seizes your little ones and dashes them against the rock![^11]

[^1]: The rivers and canals of the land of Babylon, between the Tigris and the Euphrates, where the people of Judah lived in exile after Jerusalem fell in 587 or 586 BC (2 Kings 25).
[^2]: Hebrew *aravim*, traditionally "willows", probably the poplars that grow along the Euphrates.
[^3]: Hebrew *tolalenu*, a word found only here, so the meaning is uncertain. Perhaps "those who plundered us".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "may my right hand forget", that is, forget how to play the lyre. Some scholars read "may my right hand wither", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^6]: Literally "Remember, LORD, for the sons of Edom the day of Jerusalem", the day the city fell. The Edomites, Israel's neighbors to the south and descendants of Esau, rejoiced at its fall and joined in the plunder (Obadiah 10–14; Lamentations 4:21–22; Ezekiel 25:12).
[^7]: Literally "Lay it bare, lay it bare, down to the foundation in it."
[^8]: A poetic name for the city of Babylon and its people, pictured as a woman.
[^9]: Literally "devastated", that is, about to be devastated.
[^10]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed". Also in verse 9.
[^11]: Conquering armies of the time killed the children of a captured city in this way (2 Kings 8:12; Hosea 14:1; 13:16 in most English Bibles; Nahum 3:10), and the exiles had seen the children of Jerusalem die when the city fell (Lamentations 2:11, 19–21). The psalm asks that Babylon be paid back in kind (verse 8), as the prophets announced against it (Isaiah 13:16; Jeremiah 51:24, 56).
