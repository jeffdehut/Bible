**1** For the director. According to Jeduthun.[^1] A psalm of David.[^2]

**2** Truly[^3] my soul waits in silence for God;[^4] my salvation comes from him.

**3** Truly he is my rock and my salvation, my fortress; I will not be greatly shaken.

**4** How long will you all attack a man, to batter him down like a leaning wall, a tottering fence?[^5]

**5** Truly they plan to topple him from his high place; they delight in lies. With their mouths they bless, but inwardly they curse. Selah.[^6]

**6** Truly, my soul, wait in silence for God, for my hope comes from him.

**7** Truly he is my rock and my salvation, my fortress; I will not be shaken.

**8** My salvation and my glory rest on God; my strong rock, my refuge, is in God.

**9** Trust in him at all times, you people; pour out your hearts before him. God is a refuge for us. Selah.

**10** Truly, human beings are only a breath; people of rank are a lie.[^7] Placed on the scales, they go up; together they weigh less than a breath.

**11** Do not trust in extortion, or put empty hope in robbery; if wealth increases, do not set your heart on it.

**12** Once God has spoken; twice I have heard this: that power belongs to God,

**13** and faithful love[^8] belongs to you, Lord. For you repay each person according to what he has done.[^9]

[^1]: Hebrew *al-yedutun*. Jeduthun was one of David's chief musicians (1 Chronicles 16:41–42; 25:1–6); here perhaps the name of a tune or a style of music. Compare the titles of Psalms 39 and 77.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–13 are 62:1–12 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *akh*, "truly" or "only", which begins verses 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 10.
[^4]: Literally "Truly to God my soul (*nephesh*) is silence". Also in verse 6.
[^5]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Literally "How long will you rush upon a man? You will murder, all of you, like a leaning wall, a fence pushed in".
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 9.
[^7]: Literally "the sons of *adam* are a breath, the sons of *ish* are a lie", two words for "man", perhaps common people and people of rank (compare 49:3). *Breath*: Hebrew *hevel*, "vapor, breath", the word used throughout Ecclesiastes.
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^9]: Compare Job 34:11; Proverbs 24:12; Romans 2:6.
