**1** Praise the LORD![^1] Praise the LORD,[^2] my soul![^3]

**2** I will praise the LORD as long as I live; I will sing praises to my God while I have being.[^4]

**3** Do not put your trust in nobles, in a mortal,[^5] who cannot save.

**4** When his breath departs, he returns to his ground;[^6] on that very day his plans perish.

**5** Happy[^7] is the one whose help is the God of Jacob, whose hope is in the LORD his God,

**6** the maker of heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them, who keeps faith forever,[^8]

**7** who brings about justice for the oppressed, who gives food to the hungry. The LORD sets the prisoners free;

**8** the LORD opens the eyes of the blind; the LORD raises up those who are bowed down; the LORD loves the righteous.

**9** The LORD watches over the foreigners;[^9] he upholds the orphan and the widow, but he twists the way of the wicked.[^10]

**10** The LORD will reign forever, your God, Zion, from generation to generation. Praise the LORD!

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH. Also at the end of verse 10. Each of the last five psalms (146–150) begins and ends with this call.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. The psalmist speaks to his own soul, as in 103:1 and 104:1.
[^4]: Literally "while I am still".
[^5]: Literally "in a son of *adam* (humankind)".
[^6]: Hebrew *adamah*, "ground", which sounds like *adam*, "humankind" (compare Genesis 2:7; 3:19). *His breath*: or "his spirit". Hebrew *ruach*.
[^7]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^8]: Literally "who keeps faithfulness (*emet*) forever". Acts 4:24 and 14:15 echo the first line of this verse.
[^9]: Hebrew *gerim*, foreigners living in a land not their own, who like orphans and widows had no family to protect them (Deuteronomy 10:18).
[^10]: That is, he makes it lead to ruin.
