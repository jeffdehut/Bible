**1** For the director. According to "Do Not Destroy".[^1] Of David. A miktam.[^2]

**2** Do you indeed speak righteousness, you gods?[^3] Do you judge human beings fairly?

**3** No, in your hearts you devise wrongs; on the earth you weigh out the violence of your hands.

**4** The wicked go astray from the womb; liars wander off from birth.[^4]

**5** Their venom is like the venom of a snake, like a deaf cobra that stops up its ear,

**6** that will not listen to the voice of charmers, or of the most skillful caster of spells.

**7** God, break the teeth in their mouths; tear out the fangs of the young lions, Yahweh.[^5]

**8** Let them vanish like water that runs away; when he aims his arrows,[^6] let them be cut off.[^7]

**9** Let them be like a snail that melts away as it goes, like a woman's stillborn child, that never sees the sun.

**10** Before your pots can feel the heat of the thorns, whether green or burning, he will sweep them away.[^8]

**11** The righteous will rejoice when they see vengeance done; they will bathe their feet in the blood of the wicked.

**12** And people will say, "Surely there is a reward[^9] for the righteous; surely there is a God who judges on the earth."[^10]

[^1]: Hebrew *al-tashchet*, "do not destroy", probably the name of a tune (see the title of Psalm 57).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". *Miktam*: a term of uncertain meaning (see the note on the title of Psalm 16). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–12 are 58:1–11 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: The vowel points read *elem*, "silence": "Do you indeed speak righteousness in silence?" The same consonants can be read *elim*, "gods" or "mighty ones", the rulers addressed in this psalm (compare Psalm 82).
[^4]: Literally "from the belly".
[^5]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "his arrow" but read "his arrows".
[^7]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "he treads his arrows as they are cut off".
[^8]: The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult. Literally "Before your pots perceive the thornbush, like living, like burning, he will whirl it away".
[^9]: Literally "fruit".
[^10]: The verb "judges" is plural, as it sometimes is with *Elohim*, "God". Or "there are gods who judge".
