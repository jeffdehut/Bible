**1** Yahweh[^1] reigns![^2] Let the earth rejoice; let the many coastlands[^3] be glad.

**2** Clouds and thick darkness surround him;[^4] righteousness and justice are the foundation of his throne.

**3** Fire goes before him and burns up his adversaries all around.

**4** His lightnings light up the world; the earth sees and trembles.

**5** The mountains melt like wax before Yahweh, before the Lord of all the earth.

**6** The heavens proclaim his righteousness, and all the peoples see his glory.

**7** All who serve carved images are put to shame, those who boast in idols.[^5] Bow down to him, all you gods![^6]

**8** Zion hears and rejoices, and the daughters of Judah[^7] exult because of your judgments, Yahweh.

**9** For you, Yahweh, are the Most High[^8] over all the earth; you are exalted far above all gods.

**10** You who love Yahweh, hate evil! He guards the lives[^9] of his faithful ones;[^10] he rescues them from the hand of the wicked.

**11** Light is sown[^11] for the righteous, and joy for the upright in heart.

**12** Rejoice in Yahweh, you righteous, and give thanks to his holy name.[^12]

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Psalm 97 has no title in the Hebrew.
[^2]: Or "Yahweh has become king!" (see the note on 93:1).
[^3]: Or "islands", the distant lands across the sea.
[^4]: Compare God's coming at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:16–18; Deuteronomy 4:11).
[^5]: Hebrew *elilim*, "worthless things", which sounds like *elohim*, "gods".
[^6]: Hebrew *elohim*. The Septuagint reads "all his angels". Hebrews 1:6 quotes similar words, "Let all God's angels worship him", from the Septuagint of Deuteronomy 32:43 or of this verse.
[^7]: That is, the towns of Judah. Compare 48:12 (48:11 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^9]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Here it is plural.
[^10]: Hebrew *chasidim*, "faithful ones", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "Light has dawned", from a word that differs by one letter (*zarach* for *zarua*).
[^12]: Literally "to the remembrance of his holiness".
