**1** For the director. A psalm of David.[^1]

**2** Happy[^2] is the one who considers the poor; in the day of trouble Yahweh[^3] will rescue him.

**3** Yahweh will protect him and keep him alive; he will be called happy in the land. Do not hand him over to the will[^4] of his enemies!

**4** Yahweh will sustain him on his sickbed; in his illness you restore him to health.[^5]

**5** As for me, I said, "Yahweh, be gracious to me; heal me,[^6] for I have sinned against you."

**6** My enemies speak evil of me: "When will he die, and his name perish?"

**7** When one of them comes to see me, he speaks empty words, while his heart gathers up mischief; he goes out and tells it abroad.

**8** All who hate me whisper together against me; they imagine the worst for me:

**9** "A deadly thing[^7] has been poured into him; now that he lies down, he will not rise again."

**10** Even my close friend,[^8] whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted up his heel against me.[^9]

**11** But you, Yahweh, be gracious to me and raise me up, so that I may repay them.

**12** By this I know that you delight in me: that my enemy does not shout in triumph over me.

**13** As for me, you uphold me in my integrity, and set me in your presence forever.

**14** Blessed be Yahweh, the God of Israel, from everlasting to everlasting. Amen and Amen.[^10]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–14 are 41:1–13 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Literally "the soul (*nephesh*)", that is, the desire.
[^5]: Literally "all his bed you have turned in his sickness".
[^6]: Literally "heal my soul (*nephesh*)".
[^7]: Literally "a thing of *beliyya'al*", "worthlessness", here perhaps a deadly disease or a curse (compare 18:5, 18:4 in English).
[^8]: Literally "the man of my peace".
[^9]: That is, has turned against me. John 13:18 quotes this line.
[^10]: This blessing closes Book 1 of the Psalms (Psalms 1–41). Each of the next three books ends in a similar way (72:18–19; 89:53, 89:52 in English; 106:48); compare 1 Chronicles 16:36.
