**1** For the director. A maskil of David,[^1]

**2** when Doeg the Edomite came and told Saul, "David has come to the house of Ahimelech."[^2]

**3** Why do you boast of evil, you mighty man? The faithful love of God lasts all day long.[^3]

**4** Your tongue plots destruction like a sharpened razor, you worker of deceit.

**5** You love evil more than good, lying more than speaking what is right. Selah.[^4]

**6** You love all words that devour, you deceitful tongue.

**7** But God will break you down forever; he will snatch you up and tear you from your tent; he will uproot you from the land of the living. Selah.

**8** The righteous will see and fear; they will laugh at him, saying,

**9** "See, this is the man who would not make God his stronghold, but trusted in his great wealth, and grew strong by his destruction."[^5]

**10** But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God; I trust in the faithful love of God forever and ever.

**11** I will give thanks to you forever, because you have done it. I will hope in your name, for it is good, in the presence of your faithful ones.[^6]

[^1]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Maskil*: a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps "a skillful psalm" or "a psalm of instruction". *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verses 1–2, so verses 3–11 are 52:1–9 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: The events are told in 1 Samuel 21:2–10 and 22:6–23 (English 21:1–9; 22:6–23).
[^3]: Literally "The faithful love (*chesed*) of God all the day". The Septuagint reads "lawlessness all the day".
[^4]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 7.
[^5]: Hebrew *havvah*, "destruction", as in verse 4, or "craving".
[^6]: Hebrew *chasidim*, "the faithful, the loyal", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
