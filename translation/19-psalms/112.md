**1** Praise Yah![^1] Happy[^2] is the one[^3] who fears Yahweh,[^4] who greatly delights in his commands.[^5]

**2** His descendants will be mighty in the land; the generation of the upright will be blessed.

**3** Wealth and riches are in his house, and his righteousness endures forever.[^6]

**4** Light dawns in the darkness for the upright;[^7] he is gracious, compassionate and righteous.[^8]

**5** It goes well with the one who is generous and lends, who conducts his affairs with justice.[^9]

**6** For he will never be shaken; the righteous will be remembered forever.[^10]

**7** He is not afraid of bad news; his heart is steady, trusting in Yahweh.

**8** His heart is secure; he will not be afraid, until he looks in triumph on his adversaries.[^11]

**9** He has scattered his gifts; he has given to the needy. His righteousness endures forever;[^12] his horn[^13] will be lifted high in honor.

**10** The wicked will see it and be angry; he will grind his teeth and melt away. The desire of the wicked will perish.

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name Yahweh.
[^2]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^3]: Literally "the man".
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Psalm 112 is an acrostic, like Psalm 111. After the opening "Praise Yah", each line in the Hebrew begins with the next letter of the alphabet: twenty-two lines, two to a verse in verses 1–8 and three to a verse in verses 9–10.
[^6]: Psalm 111:3 says this of God's righteousness. Here it is said of the one who fears him (also in verse 9).
[^7]: Or "He dawns in the darkness as a light for the upright".
[^8]: Psalm 111:4 says that Yahweh is "gracious and compassionate". Here the words probably describe the one who fears him.
[^9]: Literally "Good (is) a man who is gracious and lends; he sustains his matters with justice".
[^10]: Literally "will be for a memorial forever".
[^11]: Literally "until he looks on his adversaries", that is, sees their defeat.
[^12]: 2 Corinthians 9:9 quotes the first half of this verse.
[^13]: A symbol of strength and dignity, like the horns of a wild ox.
