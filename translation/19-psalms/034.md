**1** Of David, when he changed his behavior[^1] before Abimelech, who drove him out, and he went away.[^2]

**2** I will bless the LORD[^3] at all times; his praise will always be in my mouth.[^4]

**3** My soul[^5] will boast in the LORD; let the humble hear and be glad.

**4** Magnify the LORD with me; let us exalt his name together.

**5** I sought the LORD, and he answered me; he rescued me from all my fears.

**6** Those who looked to him were radiant, and their faces will never be ashamed.[^6]

**7** This afflicted man called, and the LORD heard, and saved him from all his troubles.

**8** The angel of the LORD camps around those who fear him, and rescues them.

**9** Taste and see that the LORD is good.[^7] Happy[^8] is the one who takes refuge in him.

**10** Fear the LORD, you his holy ones, for those who fear him lack nothing.

**11** Young lions[^9] may go hungry and starve, but those who seek the LORD lack no good thing.

**12** Come, children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD.

**13** Who is the one who delights in life, and loves many days to see good?[^10]

**14** Keep your tongue from evil and your lips from speaking deceit.

**15** Turn away from evil and do good; seek peace and pursue it.

**16** The eyes of the LORD are on the righteous, and his ears are turned to their cry for help.

**17** The face of the LORD is against those who do evil, to cut off the memory of them from the earth.

**18** They cry out,[^11] and the LORD hears, and rescues them from all their troubles.

**19** The LORD is near to the brokenhearted, and saves those who are crushed in spirit.

**20** Many are the troubles of the righteous, but the LORD rescues him from them all.

**21** He guards all his bones; not one of them will be broken.[^12]

**22** Evil will kill the wicked, and those who hate the righteous will be condemned.

**23** The LORD redeems the life[^13] of his servants; none who take refuge in him will be condemned.

[^1]: Literally "changed his sense", that is, pretended to be insane. The story is told in 1 Samuel 21:11–16 (21:10–15 in English), where the king of Gath is called Achish. *Abimelech* may have been a title of Philistine kings (compare Genesis 20; 26).
[^2]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–23 are 34:1–22 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Psalm 34 is an acrostic: in the Hebrew, each verse from verse 2 to verse 22 begins with the next letter of the alphabet. There is no verse for *vav*, and verse 23, beginning with *pe*, stands outside the alphabet as a closing line, as in Psalm 25.
[^5]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^6]: The Septuagint has commands: "Come to him and be enlightened, and your faces will never be put to shame."
[^7]: 1 Peter 2:3 echoes this line.
[^8]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^9]: The Septuagint reads "The rich".
[^10]: 1 Peter 3:10–12 quotes verses 13–17.
[^11]: That is, the righteous of verse 16. The Septuagint reads "The righteous cry out".
[^12]: John 19:36 applies this verse, together with Exodus 12:46, to the death of Jesus.
[^13]: Hebrew *nephesh*.
