**1** A psalm. A song for the dedication of the house.[^1] Of David.[^2]

**2** I will exalt you, Yahweh,[^3] for you have drawn me up,[^4] and have not let my enemies rejoice over me.

**3** Yahweh my God, I cried to you for help, and you healed me.

**4** Yahweh, you brought up my life[^5] from Sheol;[^6] you kept me alive, so that I did not go down to the pit.[^7]

**5** Sing praises to Yahweh, you his faithful ones;[^8] give thanks to his holy name.[^9]

**6** For his anger lasts only a moment, but his favor for a lifetime.[^10] Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.

**7** As for me, I said when I was at ease, "I will never be shaken."

**8** Yahweh, by your favor you made my mountain stand strong.[^11] You hid your face, and I was terrified.

**9** To you, Yahweh, I called; to the Lord I pleaded for mercy:

**10** "What profit is there in my blood, if I go down to the pit?[^12] Will the dust praise you? Will it proclaim your faithfulness?

**11** Hear, Yahweh, and be gracious to me! Yahweh, be my helper!"

**12** You have turned my mourning into dancing; you have taken off my sackcloth and clothed me with joy,

**13** so that my whole being[^13] may sing praises to you and not be silent. Yahweh my God, I will give thanks to you forever.

[^1]: Probably the temple, though it is not named. The word for "dedication", *chanukkah*, later gave its name to the festival of Hanukkah.
[^2]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–13 are 30:1–12 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Like a bucket drawn up from a well.
[^5]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^6]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^7]: Hebrew *bor*, "pit": the grave. The Hebrew text is written "you kept me alive from among those who go down to the pit" but read "you kept me alive from going down to the pit".
[^8]: Hebrew *chasidim*, "faithful, loyal ones", related to *chesed*, "faithful love".
[^9]: Literally "to the remembrance of his holiness".
[^10]: Or "but in his favor there is life".
[^11]: The Hebrew of this line is difficult. Literally "you made strength stand for my mountain".
[^12]: Hebrew *shachat*, another word for the grave.
[^13]: Literally "glory (*kavod*)", here standing for the whole self (compare 16:9).
