**1** For the director. According to the Gittith.[^1] Of Asaph.[^2]

**2** Sing for joy to God our strength; shout aloud to the God of Jacob.

**3** Raise a song and sound the tambourine, the sweet lyre with the harp.

**4** Blow the ram's horn[^3] at the new moon, at the full moon,[^4] on the day of our festival.

**5** For this is a decree for Israel, a ruling of the God of Jacob.

**6** He made it a statute[^5] in Joseph[^6] when he went out against the land of Egypt.[^7] I heard a voice I did not know:[^8]

**7** "I removed the burden from his shoulder; his hands were set free from the basket.[^9]

**8** In distress you called, and I rescued you; I answered you from the hiding place of the thunder; I tested you at the waters of Meribah.[^10] Selah.[^11]

**9** Hear, my people, and I will warn you; Israel, if only you would listen to me!

**10** There shall be no strange god among you; you shall not bow down to a foreign god.

**11** I am Yahweh[^12] your God, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt.[^13] Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it.

**12** But my people did not listen to my voice, and Israel would have none of me.

**13** So I let them go their way in the stubbornness of their hearts, to follow their own plans.

**14** If only my people would listen to me, if only Israel would walk in my ways!

**15** I would soon subdue their enemies, and turn my hand against their adversaries.

**16** Those who hate Yahweh would come cringing to him,[^14] and their time would last forever.[^15]

**17** But he would feed you[^16] with the finest of the wheat, and with honey from the rock I would satisfy you."[^17]

[^1]: Hebrew *gittit*, a term of uncertain meaning, perhaps an instrument or tune from Gath, or a song for the winepress (*gat*). Also in the titles of Psalms 8 and 84.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of Asaph*: or "for Asaph", a Levite who led the temple singers (see the note on 50:1). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–17 are 81:1–16 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *shofar*.
[^4]: Hebrew *keseh*, probably the full moon, when the festivals of Passover and of Booths began (Leviticus 23:5–6, 34).
[^5]: Hebrew *edut*, traditionally "testimony": the solemn terms of the covenant.
[^6]: Here spelled *Yehoseph*, a longer form of the name Joseph, standing for the people of Israel.
[^7]: Or "when he went out over the land of Egypt". *He* may be God, who went out against Egypt (Exodus 11:4), or Joseph, who went out over Egypt as its governor (Genesis 41:45). The Septuagint reads "when he went out of the land of Egypt".
[^8]: Literally "A lip I did not know I heard." The speaker hears God's words, which follow in verses 7–17. Or "where I heard a language I did not know", that is, in Egypt.
[^9]: The baskets used for carrying clay and bricks in Egypt (compare Exodus 1:14).
[^10]: *Meribah* means "quarreling". The events are told in Exodus 17:1–7 and Numbers 20:1–13.
[^11]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^12]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^13]: Verses 10–11 echo the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:2–3; Deuteronomy 5:6–7).
[^14]: Or "would pretend to obey him".
[^15]: Literally "and their time would be forever", perhaps their time of punishment.
[^16]: Literally "he would feed him". The Hebrew moves between "he" and "I", and between "him" and "you".
[^17]: Compare Deuteronomy 32:13–14.
