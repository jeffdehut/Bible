**1** A psalm of David.[^1] To bring to remembrance.[^2]

**2** LORD,[^3] do not rebuke me in your anger, or discipline me in your wrath.[^4]

**3** For your arrows have sunk into me, and your hand has come down on me.

**4** There is no soundness in my flesh because of your indignation; there is no health[^5] in my bones because of my sin.

**5** For my guilt has gone over my head; like a heavy burden it is too heavy for me.

**6** My wounds stink and fester because of my foolishness.

**7** I am bent over and bowed down greatly; all day long I go about in mourning.

**8** For my sides[^6] are filled with burning, and there is no soundness in my flesh.

**9** I am numb and badly crushed; I groan[^7] because of the turmoil of my heart.

**10** Lord, all my longing is before you; my sighing is not hidden from you.

**11** My heart pounds, my strength fails me; even the light of my eyes has gone from me.

**12** My friends and companions stand aloof from my affliction;[^8] my neighbors stand far off.

**13** Those who seek my life[^9] lay snares; those who seek my harm speak of ruin and plot deceit all day long.

**14** But I am like a deaf man who does not hear, like a mute man who does not open his mouth.

**15** I have become like a man who does not hear, in whose mouth there are no arguments.

**16** For in you, LORD, I hope; you will answer, Lord my God.

**17** For I said, "Do not let them gloat over me, who exalt themselves over me when my foot slips."

**18** For I am ready to fall, and my pain is always before me.

**19** I confess my guilt; I am troubled by my sin.

**20** My enemies are full of life and strong;[^10] many are those who hate me wrongfully.

**21** Those who repay evil for good accuse me[^11] because I pursue good.

**22** Do not abandon me, LORD; my God, do not be far from me.

**23** Come quickly to help me, Lord, my salvation.

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–23 are 38:1–22 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *lehazkir*, "to cause to remember" or "to make a memorial". The psalm may have been sung with the memorial portion of an offering (Leviticus 2:2), or it may call on God to remember the one who prays. Also in the title of Psalm 70.
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: This verse is almost the same as 6:2 (6:1 in English).
[^5]: Hebrew *shalom*: peace, well-being, wholeness.
[^6]: Literally "my loins".
[^7]: Literally "I roar".
[^8]: Or "my disease". Hebrew *nega*, "a blow, a plague".
[^9]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^10]: Literally "My enemies are living, they are strong".
[^11]: Hebrew *yistenuni*, from the same root as *satan*, "adversary, accuser".
