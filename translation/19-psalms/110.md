**1** Of David. A psalm.[^1] The LORD[^2] says to my lord:[^3] "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet."[^4]

**2** The LORD will extend your mighty scepter[^5] from Zion. Rule in the midst of your enemies!

**3** Your people will offer themselves willingly[^6] on the day of your power,[^7] in holy splendor.[^8] From the womb of the dawn, the dew of your youth is yours.[^9]

**4** The LORD has sworn an oath and will not change his mind: "You are a priest forever, after the manner of Melchizedek."[^10]

**5** The Lord is at your right hand;[^11] he will shatter kings on the day of his wrath.

**6** He will judge among the nations, heaping up corpses;[^12] he will shatter heads across the wide earth.[^13]

**7** He will drink from the stream by the way; so he will lift up his head.[^14]

[^1]: *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles. In Mark 12:35–37 Jesus' argument takes David as the speaker of the psalm.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *Says*: literally "The declaration of the LORD", the word (*ne'um*) that marks a prophet's oracle ("declares the LORD").
[^3]: Hebrew *adoni*, "my lord", a title of respect for a superior, here the king. The vowel points distinguish it from *Adonai*, "the Lord", used of God (as in verse 5).
[^4]: The place at the right hand is the place of honor and shared rule; a conquered enemy was put under the victor's feet (compare Joshua 10:24). Jesus quotes this verse in Mark 12:36 (also Matthew 22:44; Luke 20:42–43). It is one of the Old Testament verses most often quoted or echoed in the New Testament: see Acts 2:34–35; 1 Corinthians 15:25; Hebrews 1:13; 10:12–13.
[^5]: Literally "the staff of your strength".
[^6]: Literally "Your people (are) freewill offerings". The Hebrew of this verse is very difficult, and each of its lines is uncertain.
[^7]: Or "of your army", the day the king leads out his forces.
[^8]: Or "in holy garments". Some Hebrew manuscripts read "on the holy mountains".
[^9]: Literally "From the womb of the dawn, to you (is) the dew of your youth": perhaps the king's youthful strength is renewed each day, like the dew at dawn. Or "your young men will come to you like the dew". The same consonants can be read "I have begotten you", as in 2:7. The Septuagint reads the verse "With you is rule on the day of your power, in the splendor of the holy ones; from the womb, before the morning star, I have begotten you."
[^10]: Hebrew *al-divrati malki-tsedeq*. *Al-divrati* probably means "according to the manner of"; it can also mean "on account of", giving "on my account, a righteous king". Melchizedek was king of Salem and "priest of God Most High" (Genesis 14:18–20); his name means "my king is righteous" or "king of righteousness". The Septuagint reads "according to the order of Melchizedek", the wording quoted in Hebrews 5:6, 10; 6:20, and Hebrews 7 explains the verse at length.
[^11]: In verse 1 the king sits at the LORD's right hand; here the Lord stands at the king's right hand, as his protector in battle. In verses 5–7 it is not always clear whether the subject is the Lord or the king.
[^12]: Literally "he judges among the nations; full of corpses". Or "he fills (the land) with corpses".
[^13]: Literally "he shatters head over a wide land". Or "he shatters the ruler of a vast land".
[^14]: That is, refreshed, he stands tall in victory.
