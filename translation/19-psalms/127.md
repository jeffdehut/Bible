**1** A song of ascents. Of Solomon.[^1] Unless the LORD[^2] builds the house, its builders labor over it in vain. Unless the LORD guards the city, the guard keeps watch in vain.

**2** It is in vain that you rise up early and go late to rest, eating the bread of anxious toil; for he gives sleep to the one he loves.[^3]

**3** Look, children[^4] are an inheritance from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward.

**4** Like arrows in the hand of a warrior are the children of one's youth.

**5** Happy[^5] is the man who has filled his quiver with them! They will not be put to shame when they speak with enemies at the gate.[^6]

[^1]: *A song of ascents*: Hebrew *shir ha-ma'alot* (see the note on 120:1). *Of Solomon*: or "for Solomon". Solomon built the house of the LORD (1 Kings 6), and he was also named *Jedidiah*, "loved by Yah" (2 Samuel 12:25; compare "the one he loves" in verse 2). In the Hebrew text the title is part of verse 1. In this psalm it shares the verse with the first line, so the verse numbers match English Bibles.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Or "for he gives to the one he loves while he sleeps". Hebrew *yedido*, "his beloved".
[^4]: Literally "sons". Also in verse 4.
[^5]: Hebrew *ashrei*: happy, fortunate; traditionally "blessed".
[^6]: The town gate, where legal cases were heard and business was done. A man with grown sons had others to stand with him.
