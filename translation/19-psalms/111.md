**1** Praise the LORD![^1] I will give thanks to the LORD[^2] with all my heart, in the council of the upright and in the assembly.[^3]

**2** Great are the works of the LORD, studied by all who delight in them.

**3** Splendor and majesty are his work, and his righteousness endures forever.

**4** He has made his wonders to be remembered;[^4] the LORD is gracious and compassionate.[^5]

**5** He gives food[^6] to those who fear him; he remembers his covenant forever.

**6** He has shown his people the power of his works, giving them the inheritance of the nations.

**7** The works of his hands are faithfulness and justice; all his precepts are trustworthy.

**8** They are established forever and ever, made in faithfulness and uprightness.

**9** He sent redemption to his people; he has commanded his covenant forever. Holy and awesome is his name.

**10** The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom;[^7] all who practice them have good understanding.[^8] His praise endures forever.

[^1]: Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah". *Yah* is a short form of the name YHWH.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Psalm 111 is an acrostic. After the opening "Praise Yah", each line in the Hebrew begins with the next letter of the alphabet: twenty-two lines, two to a verse in verses 1–8 and three to a verse in verses 9–10. Psalm 112 follows the same pattern, and the two psalms share several phrases (111:3 and 112:3, 9; 111:4 and 112:4).
[^4]: Literally "He has made a memorial for his wonders", perhaps the festivals that recall them (compare Exodus 12:14).
[^5]: Compare Exodus 34:6.
[^6]: Hebrew *teref*, usually "prey", the food of wild animals: perhaps here the food he gave in the wilderness.
[^7]: Or "the first principle of wisdom". Compare Proverbs 1:7; 9:10; Job 28:28.
[^8]: Literally "good understanding (is) to all who do them", that is, who keep his precepts (verse 7).
