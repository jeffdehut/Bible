**1** For the director. According to "Do Not Destroy".[^1] Of David. A miktam,[^2] when he fled from Saul, in the cave.[^3]

**2** Be gracious to me, God, be gracious to me, for in you my soul[^4] takes refuge; in the shadow of your wings I will take refuge until the destruction passes by.

**3** I cry to God Most High,[^5] to God who fulfills his purpose for me.[^6]

**4** He will send from heaven and save me; he will put to shame the one who hounds me.[^7] Selah.[^8] God will send out his faithful love[^9] and his faithfulness.

**5** My soul is in the midst of lions; I lie down among those who breathe fire,[^10] human beings whose teeth are spears and arrows, whose tongue is a sharp sword.

**6** Be exalted above the heavens, God; let your glory be over all the earth.

**7** They set a net for my steps; my soul was bowed down. They dug a pit in my path; they have fallen into it themselves. Selah.

**8** My heart is steadfast, God, my heart is steadfast;[^11] I will sing and make music.

**9** Wake up, my glory![^12] Wake up, harp and lyre! I will wake the dawn.

**10** I will give thanks to you among the peoples, Lord; I will sing praise to you among the nations.

**11** For your faithful love is great, reaching to the heavens, and your faithfulness to the clouds.

**12** Be exalted above the heavens, God; let your glory be over all the earth.

[^1]: Hebrew *al-tashchet*, "do not destroy", probably the name of a tune (compare Isaiah 65:8). Also in the titles of Psalms 58, 59 and 75.
[^2]: Hebrew *miktam*, a term of uncertain meaning (see the note on the title of Psalm 16).
[^3]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". For the cave, compare 1 Samuel 22:1 and 24:4 (24:3 in most English Bibles). In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–12 are 57:1–11 in most English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self. Also in verses 5 and 7.
[^5]: Hebrew *Elyon*, a title of God.
[^6]: Literally "to God who completes for me". The Septuagint reads "who does good to me".
[^7]: Or "when the one who hounds me taunts me".
[^8]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning. Also in verse 7.
[^9]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love. Also in verse 11.
[^10]: Or "who devour".
[^11]: Verses 8–12 appear again, with small differences, as 108:2–6 (108:1–5 in most English Bibles).
[^12]: Hebrew *kevodi*, "my glory", perhaps meaning "my soul", the inner self (compare 16:9).
