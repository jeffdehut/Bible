**1** For the director. According to "The Lily of the Testimony".[^1] A miktam of David, for teaching,[^2]

**2** when he fought against Aram-naharaim and Aram-zobah,[^3] and when Joab returned and struck down twelve thousand of Edom in the Valley of Salt.[^4]

**3** God, you have rejected us and broken us; you have been angry. Restore us!

**4** You have made the land quake; you have split it open. Heal its cracks, for it is tottering.

**5** You have made your people see hard times; you have given us wine to drink that makes us stagger.

**6** You have set up a banner for those who fear you, for them to rally to, out of range of the bow.[^5] Selah.[^6]

**7** So that those you love may be rescued, save by your right hand, and answer me.[^7]

**8** God has spoken in his holiness:[^8] "I will exult; I will divide up Shechem, and measure out the Valley of Succoth.

**9** Gilead is mine, and Manasseh is mine; Ephraim is the helmet on my head;[^9] Judah is my scepter.[^10]

**10** Moab is my washbasin; on Edom I throw my sandal.[^11] Philistia, shout aloud because of me!"[^12]

**11** Who will bring me to the fortified city? Who will lead me to Edom?[^13]

**12** Is it not you, God, who have rejected us? You no longer go out, God, with our armies.

**13** Give us help against the adversary, for human help is worthless.[^14]

**14** With God we will do valiantly; he will trample our adversaries.

[^1]: Hebrew *al-shushan edut*, probably the name of a tune (compare the titles of Psalms 45, 69 and 80).
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Miktam*: a term of uncertain meaning (see the note on the title of Psalm 16). *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verses 1–2, so verses 3–14 are 60:1–12 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *Aram-naharaim*, "Aram of the two rivers", was in northern Mesopotamia; *Aram-zobah* was an Aramean kingdom north of Damascus.
[^4]: The events are told in 2 Samuel 8:3–14 and 1 Chronicles 18:3–13. In 2 Samuel 8:13 David strikes down eighteen thousand of Aram in the Valley of Salt (the Septuagint and the Syriac read "Edom"); in 1 Chronicles 18:12 Abishai strikes down eighteen thousand Edomites there.
[^5]: Hebrew *qoshet*, probably a form of *qeshet*, "bow". Or "a banner to be raised because of the truth", taking *qoshet* as "truth", as in Aramaic.
[^6]: Hebrew *selah*, a musical or liturgical term of uncertain meaning.
[^7]: The Hebrew text is written "answer us" but read "answer me". Verses 7–14 appear again, with small differences, as 108:7–14 (English 108:6–13).
[^8]: Or "in his holy place".
[^9]: Literally "the stronghold of my head".
[^10]: Or "my ruler's staff" (compare Genesis 49:10).
[^11]: Throwing a sandal on land was perhaps a sign of taking possession of it (compare Ruth 4:7).
[^12]: Psalm 108:10 (English 108:9) has "over Philistia I shout in triumph".
[^13]: Literally "Who has led me to Edom?" Psalm 108:11 (English 108:10) has "Who will lead me to Edom?"
[^14]: Literally "the salvation of *adam* (humankind) is worthless".
