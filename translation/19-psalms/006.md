**1** For the director. With stringed instruments, according to the Sheminith.[^1] A psalm of David.[^2]

**2** Yahweh,[^3] do not rebuke me in your anger, or discipline me in your wrath.

**3** Be gracious to me, Yahweh, for I am wasting away; heal me, Yahweh, for my bones are shaking with terror.

**4** My soul[^4] is shaking with terror. But you, Yahweh, how long?

**5** Turn, Yahweh, rescue my life;[^5] save me for the sake of your faithful love.[^6]

**6** For in death no one remembers you; in Sheol[^7] who gives you thanks?

**7** I am worn out with my groaning. Every night I flood my bed;[^8] I drench my couch with my tears.

**8** My eye grows dim with grief; it grows weak because of all my adversaries.

**9** Depart from me, all you evildoers,[^9] for Yahweh has heard the sound of my weeping.

**10** Yahweh has heard my plea for mercy; Yahweh accepts my prayer.

**11** All my enemies will be ashamed and shake with terror; they will turn back and be ashamed in an instant.

[^1]: Hebrew *sheminit*, "the eighth", a term of uncertain meaning: perhaps an eight-stringed instrument, a lower octave, or a tune. Also in the title of Psalm 12 and in 1 Chronicles 15:21.
[^2]: *For the director*: Hebrew *lamenatseach*, probably the leader of the temple music. *Of David*: or "for David", "about David". In the Hebrew text the title is verse 1, so verses 2–11 are 6:1–10 in most English Bibles.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *nephesh*: soul, life, self.
[^5]: Hebrew *nephesh*.
[^6]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love.
[^7]: Hebrew *Sheol*, the realm of the dead.
[^8]: Literally "I make my bed swim".
[^9]: Jesus uses these words in Matthew 7:23 and Luke 13:27.
