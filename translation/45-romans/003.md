**1** Then what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the value of circumcision?

**2** Much, in every way. For first of all, they were entrusted with the words[^1] of God.

**3** What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify the faithfulness of God?[^2]

**4** By no means![^3] Let God be true, and every human being a liar,[^4] as it is written: "So that you may be proved right[^5] in your words, and prevail when you are judged."[^6]

**5** But if our unrighteousness shows God's righteousness, what shall we say? That God is unrighteous to inflict wrath? (I am speaking in a human way.)

**6** By no means! For then how could God judge the world?

**7** But[^7] if through my falsehood God's truthfulness overflows to his glory, why am I still being judged as a sinner?

**8** And why not say, as we are slanderously accused of saying, and as some claim that we say, "Let us do evil so that good may come"? Their condemnation is just.

**9** What then? Are we better off?[^8] Not at all. For we have already charged that all, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin,[^9]

**10** as it is written: "There is no one righteous, not even one.

**11** There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God.

**12** They have all turned aside; together they have become worthless. There is no one who does good, not even one.[^10]

**13** Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues.[^11] The venom of vipers is under their lips.[^12]

**14** Their mouth is full of curses and bitterness.[^13]

**15** Their feet are swift to shed blood;

**16** ruin and misery are in their paths,

**17** and the way of peace they have not known.[^14]

**18** There is no fear of God before their eyes."[^15]

**19** Now we know that whatever the Law says, it speaks to those who are under the Law,[^16] so that every mouth may be silenced and the whole world may be held accountable to God.

**20** For by works of the Law[^17] no human being[^18] will be declared righteous in his sight, for through the Law comes the knowledge of sin.

**21** But now, apart from the Law, the righteousness of God has been made known, attested by the Law and the Prophets:

**22** the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ[^19] for all who believe.[^20] For there is no distinction,

**23** for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God,

**24** and they are declared righteous freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

**25** God put him forward as a place of atonement[^21] through faith, by his blood,[^22] to demonstrate his righteousness, because he had passed over the sins committed beforehand[^23]

**26** in his forbearance. It was to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that he would be righteous and would declare righteous the person who has faith in Jesus.[^24]

**27** Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law?[^25] By the law of works? No, but by the law of faith.

**28** For[^26] we hold that a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the Law.

**29** Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also,

**30** since God is one,[^27] and he will declare righteous the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.

**31** Do we then nullify the Law through faith? By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the Law.

[^1]: Greek *logia*, "sayings, oracles".
[^2]: Or "If some did not believe, will their unbelief nullify the faithfulness of God?" The Greek words for *were unfaithful*, *unfaithfulness* and *faithfulness* are related (*apisteo*, *apistia*, *pistis*).
[^3]: Greek *me genoito*, "may it not be", a strong denial; also in verses 6 and 31.
[^4]: Compare Psalm 116:11.
[^5]: Greek *dikaioo*, here "be shown to be in the right". In verses 20–30 it is translated "declare righteous" (traditionally "justify").
[^6]: Psalm 51:6 (51:4 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew reads "so that you are right when you speak, and blameless when you judge". *When you are judged*: or "when you go to court".
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "For".
[^8]: Or "Are we at a disadvantage?" or "Are we making excuses?" The meaning of the Greek verb here is uncertain.
[^9]: Literally "under sin".
[^10]: Verses 10–12: Psalm 14:1–3 and 53:2–4 (53:1–3 in most English Bibles), quoted freely and mostly as in the Septuagint. The psalms have "There is no one who does good" where verse 10 has "There is no one righteous", and the Hebrew has "they have become corrupt" where Paul has "they have become worthless".
[^11]: Psalm 5:10 (5:9 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "they flatter with their tongue".
[^12]: Psalm 140:4 (140:3 in most English Bibles).
[^13]: Psalm 10:7, shortened, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "His mouth is full of curses, deceit and oppression".
[^14]: Verses 15–17: Isaiah 59:7–8, shortened and mostly as in the Septuagint.
[^15]: Psalm 36:2 (36:1 in most English Bibles). The psalm has "his eyes".
[^16]: Literally "in the Law", as in 2:12.
[^17]: That is, by doing what the Law requires.
[^18]: Literally "no flesh". Compare Psalm 143:2.
[^19]: Or "through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ". Literally "through faith of Jesus Christ".
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "to all and on all who believe".
[^21]: Greek *hilasterion*, the word the Septuagint uses for the *kapporet*, the atonement cover on the ark, where blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement (Exodus 25:17; Leviticus 16:14–15). Or "a sacrifice of atonement"; traditionally "propitiation".
[^22]: Or "through faith in his blood". Some take *faith* here as Christ's faithfulness (compare verse 22).
[^23]: Literally "because of the passing over of the sins committed beforehand".
[^24]: Or "the person who relies on the faithfulness of Jesus". Literally "the one from faith of Jesus".
[^25]: Or "By what kind of principle?" Greek *nomos*, the word translated "the Law" elsewhere in this chapter; also later in the verse.
[^26]: Many manuscripts read "So".
[^27]: Compare Deuteronomy 6:4.
