**1** Or do you not know, brothers and sisters[^1] (for I am speaking to people who know the law),[^2] that the law has authority over a person only as long as that person lives?

**2** For a married woman is bound by law to her husband while he lives; but if her husband dies, she is released from the law concerning her husband.

**3** So then, if she marries another man while her husband is alive, she will be called an adulteress. But if her husband dies, she is free from that law, so that she is not an adulteress if she marries another man.

**4** So, my brothers and sisters, you also died to the Law through the body of Christ, so that you might belong to another, to him who was raised from the dead, in order that we might bear fruit for God.

**5** For when we were in the flesh,[^3] the sinful passions that came through the Law[^4] were at work in the parts of our body[^5] to bear fruit for death.

**6** But now we have been released from the Law, having died to what held us captive, so that we serve in the new way of the Spirit and not in the old way of the written code.[^6]

**7** What then shall we say? Is the Law sin? By no means! Yet I[^7] would not have known sin except through the Law. For I would not have known what it is to covet if the Law had not said, "You must not covet."[^8]

**8** But sin, seizing the opportunity through the commandment, produced in me every kind of coveting. For apart from the Law, sin is dead.

**9** I was once alive apart from the Law, but when the commandment came, sin sprang to life,

**10** and I died. The very commandment that was meant to bring life proved to bring death to me.

**11** For sin, seizing the opportunity through the commandment, deceived me,[^9] and through it killed me.

**12** So the Law is holy, and the commandment is holy and righteous and good.

**13** Did what is good, then, become death to me? By no means! Rather, it was sin, so that it might be shown to be sin, producing death in me through what is good, so that through the commandment sin might become utterly sinful.

**14** For we know that the Law is spiritual, but I am made of flesh,[^10] sold as a slave to sin.[^11]

**15** For I do not understand what I do. For I do not do what I want, but I do what I hate.

**16** But if I do what I do not want, I agree with the Law that it is good.

**17** As it is, it is no longer I who do it, but sin that lives in me.

**18** For I know that nothing good lives in me, that is, in my flesh. For the desire to do what is good is present with me, but not the doing of it.[^12]

**19** For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I do.

**20** But if I do what I do not want, it is no longer I who do it, but sin that lives in me.

**21** So I find this law:[^13] when I want to do what is good, evil is present with me.

**22** For in my inner being[^14] I delight in the law of God,

**23** but I see another law in the parts of my body, waging war against the law of my mind and taking me captive to the law of sin that is in the parts of my body.

**24** Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?[^15]

**25** Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord![^16] So then, I myself with my mind serve the law of God, but with my flesh the law of sin.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 4.
[^2]: Or "the Law", meaning the law of Moses. The Greek word *nomos* can mean either; also in verses 1–3.
[^3]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God; also in verses 18 and 25.
[^4]: Literally "the passions of sins through the Law".
[^5]: Literally "in our members"; also in verse 23.
[^6]: Literally "in newness of Spirit and not in oldness of letter". Or "spirit".
[^7]: In verses 7–25 Paul speaks as "I". Interpreters differ on whom he describes: himself before he came to faith, or as a believer; Adam; Israel under the Law; or human beings in general.
[^8]: Exodus 20:17; Deuteronomy 5:21. *Covet*: Greek *epithymeo*, "desire, crave"; the noun *epithymia*, "desire, coveting", is used in verses 7–8.
[^9]: Compare Genesis 3:13.
[^10]: Greek *sarkinos*, "made of flesh, fleshly".
[^11]: Literally "sold under sin".
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "but how to do what is good I do not find".
[^13]: Or "principle". Greek *nomos*, which in this chapter elsewhere means the Law of Moses. Some take it so here: "So I find, with regard to the Law, that when I want to do what is good, evil is present with me". In verses 22–25 the word is used of "the law of God", "another law", "the law of my mind" and "the law of sin".
[^14]: Literally "according to the inner human being".
[^15]: Or "from the body of this death".
[^16]: The SBLGNT here follows Tregelles. Some manuscripts read "But thanks be to God"; many read "I thank God".
