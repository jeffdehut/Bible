**1** Therefore, since we have been declared righteous[^1] by faith, we have peace[^2] with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.

**2** Through him we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we boast[^3] in the hope of the glory of God.

**3** And not only that, but we also boast in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance,

**4** and endurance produces proven character, and proven character produces hope.

**5** And hope does not put us to shame, because the love of God[^4] has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.

**6** For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.[^5]

**7** For one will scarcely die for a righteous person, though for a good person someone might perhaps even dare to die.

**8** But God shows his own love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

**9** Much more, then, since we have now been declared righteous by his blood, will we be saved through him from wrath.

**10** For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, now that we have been reconciled, will we be saved by his life.

**11** And not only that, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.

**12** Therefore, just as sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all people, because all sinned[^6]—

**13** for sin was in the world before the Law,[^7] but sin is not counted when there is no law.

**14** Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam,[^8] who is a pattern[^9] of the one who was to come.

**15** But the gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died through the trespass of the one, much more have the grace of God and the gift given by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, overflowed to the many.

**16** And the gift is not like what came through the one who sinned. For the judgment following one trespass[^10] led to condemnation, but the gift following many trespasses led to being declared righteous.[^11]

**17** For if, by the trespass of the one, death reigned through that one, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ.

**18** So then, just as through one trespass condemnation came to all people, so also through one righteous act[^12] there comes to all people the declaration of righteousness that gives life.[^13]

**19** For just as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made[^14] sinners, so also through the obedience of the one the many will be made righteous.

**20** The Law came in alongside, so that the trespass would increase.[^15] But where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more,

**21** so that, just as sin reigned in death, so also grace would reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

[^1]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^2]: Some manuscripts read "let us have peace".
[^3]: Or "rejoice", "exult"; also in verses 3 and 11. Greek *kauchaomai*, the verb translated "boast" in 2:17, 23.
[^4]: That is, God's love for us. Or "our love for God".
[^5]: Some manuscripts read "If indeed Christ, while we were still weak, at the right time died for the ungodly".
[^6]: Greek *eph' ho*, usually understood as "because". Others take it as "in whom all sinned", referring to Adam, as the Latin Vulgate has it, or as "with the result that all sinned".
[^7]: Literally "until the Law", that is, before it was given through Moses.
[^8]: Literally "over those who had not sinned in the likeness of the transgression of Adam".
[^9]: Greek *typos*, "type, pattern, foreshadowing".
[^10]: Literally "from one". *One* may be one trespass or one person.
[^11]: Greek *dikaioma*, here "a verdict of acquittal". In verse 18 the same word means "a righteous act".
[^12]: Or "through the righteous act of one". Likewise *one trespass* may be "the trespass of one".
[^13]: Literally "to justification of life". Greek *dikaiosis*, as in 4:25.
[^14]: Or "were constituted"; also later in the verse.
[^15]: Or "with the result that the trespass increased".
