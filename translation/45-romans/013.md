**1** Let every person[^1] be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been put in place by God.

**2** Therefore whoever resists the authority opposes what God has appointed, and those who oppose it will bring judgment on themselves.

**3** For rulers are not a terror to good conduct but to bad. Do you want to have no fear of the authority? Then do what is good, and you will receive its approval,

**4** for it is God's servant for your good. But if you do what is wrong, be afraid, for it does not bear the sword for nothing. It is God's servant, an avenger who brings wrath[^2] on the one who does wrong.

**5** Therefore it is necessary to be subject, not only because of the wrath but also because of conscience.

**6** For this is also why you pay taxes, for they are God's servants,[^3] devoting themselves to this very thing.

**7** Pay to all what you owe them: tax to whom tax is owed, revenue to whom revenue, respect[^4] to whom respect, honor to whom honor.

**8** Owe no one anything except to love one another. For the one who loves another has fulfilled the Law.

**9** For the commandments, "You must not commit adultery," "You must not murder," "You must not steal," "You must not covet,"[^5] and any other commandment, are summed up in this word: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."[^6]

**10** Love does no wrong to a neighbor. So love is the fulfillment of the Law.

**11** And do this, knowing the time, that it is already the hour for you[^7] to wake from sleep. For salvation is nearer to us now than when we first believed.

**12** The night is far gone; the day is near. So let us throw off[^8] the works of darkness and put on the armor of light.

**13** Let us live decently, as in the daytime, not in carousing and drunkenness, not in sexual immorality[^9] and debauchery, not in quarreling and jealousy.

**14** Instead, put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh,[^10] to gratify its desires.

[^1]: Literally "every soul".
[^2]: Literally "an avenger for wrath". *Avenger* (Greek *ekdikos*) is related to "vengeance" in 12:19, where the same word "wrath" is used.
[^3]: Greek *leitourgoi*, "public servants", a word also used of those who serve in worship (15:16).
[^4]: Literally "fear"; also later in the verse.
[^5]: Exodus 20:13–17; Deuteronomy 5:17–21. Paul names adultery before murder.
[^6]: Leviticus 19:18.
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "for us".
[^8]: Greek *apobalometha*. The SBLGNT reads this word alone among the editions it compares; the others read *apothometha*, "let us put off", with much the same meaning.
[^9]: Literally "beds".
[^10]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God.
