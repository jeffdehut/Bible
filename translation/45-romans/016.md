**1** I commend to you our sister Phoebe, who is a servant[^1] of the church in Cenchreae,[^2]

**2** so that you may welcome her in the Lord in a way worthy of God's holy people,[^3] and help her in whatever she may need from you. For she herself has been a patron[^4] of many, and of me as well.

**3** Greet Prisca[^5] and Aquila, my co-workers in Christ Jesus,

**4** who risked their necks for my life. Not only I but also all the churches of the Gentiles give thanks for them.

**5** Greet also the church that meets in their house. Greet my dear friend Epaenetus, who was the first convert in Asia[^6] for Christ.

**6** Greet Mary, who has worked hard for you.[^7]

**7** Greet Andronicus and Junia,[^8] my fellow Jews[^9] and my fellow prisoners. They are well known among the apostles,[^10] and they were in Christ before I was.

**8** Greet Ampliatus, my dear friend in the Lord.

**9** Greet Urbanus, our co-worker in Christ, and my dear friend Stachys.

**10** Greet Apelles, who is approved in Christ. Greet those who belong to the household of Aristobulus.[^11]

**11** Greet Herodion, my fellow Jew. Greet those of the household of Narcissus who are in the Lord.

**12** Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa, who work hard in the Lord. Greet dear Persis, who has worked hard in the Lord.

**13** Greet Rufus,[^12] chosen in the Lord, and his mother, who has been a mother to me as well.[^13]

**14** Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermes, Patrobas, Hermas, and the brothers and sisters[^14] who are with them.

**15** Greet Philologus and Julia, Nereus and his sister, and Olympas, and all God's holy people who are with them.

**16** Greet one another with a holy kiss. All the churches of Christ greet you.

**17** I urge you, brothers and sisters, to watch out for those who cause divisions and put obstacles in your way, contrary to the teaching that you have learned. Keep away from them.

**18** For such people do not serve our Lord Christ[^15] but their own appetites.[^16] By smooth talk and flattery they deceive the hearts of the unsuspecting.

**19** For everyone has heard of your obedience, so I rejoice over you. But I want you to be wise about what is good and innocent about what is evil.

**20** The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet.[^17] The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ[^18] be with you.

**21** Timothy, my co-worker, greets you, and so do Lucius, Jason and Sosipater, my fellow Jews.

**22** I, Tertius, who wrote this letter, greet you in the Lord.[^19]

**23** Gaius, who is host to me and to the whole church, greets you. Erastus, the city treasurer, greets you, and so does our brother Quartus.[^20]

**24** The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.[^21]

[^1]: Or "a deacon". Greek *diakonos*, used of an office in Philippians 1:1 and 1 Timothy 3:8–13.
[^2]: The eastern port of Corinth.
[^3]: Literally "worthily of the holy ones"; traditionally "saints"; also in verse 15.
[^4]: Greek *prostatis*, "patron, protector": someone who supports others with their means and influence.
[^5]: Called Priscilla in Acts 18:2, 18, 26.
[^6]: Literally "the firstfruits of Asia", the Roman province in the west of what is now Turkey. Many manuscripts read "Achaia".
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "for us".
[^8]: Greek *Iounian*. The SBLGNT, with Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and the Byzantine text, accents it as the woman's name Junia, a common Roman name. One edition in the apparatus (marked NA28 there) accents it as a man's name, Junias, which is not otherwise known; the accent decides the gender, and early manuscripts have no accents.
[^9]: Greek *syngeneis*, "relatives, kinsfolk", here probably fellow Jews; also in verses 11 and 21.
[^10]: Or "well known to the apostles". The Greek can mean that they were themselves outstanding apostles, or that the apostles held them in high regard.
[^11]: Literally "those of Aristobulus"; likewise "those of Narcissus" in verse 11.
[^12]: Perhaps the Rufus of Mark 15:21.
[^13]: Literally "his mother and mine".
[^14]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 17. *Our brother* in verse 23 is the singular, *adelphos*.
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "our Lord Jesus Christ".
[^16]: Literally "their own belly".
[^17]: Compare Genesis 3:15.
[^18]: Some manuscripts leave out "Christ".
[^19]: Or "I, Tertius, who wrote this letter in the Lord, greet you".
[^20]: Literally "Quartus the brother".
[^21]: Verse 24 is in the SBLGNT, following the Byzantine text; many modern editions, including Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and NA28, leave it out. In its place they end the letter with verses 25–27: "Now to him who is able to strengthen you according to my good news and the proclamation of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but is now disclosed, and through the prophetic scriptures, by the command of the eternal God, has been made known to all the Gentiles to bring about the obedience of faith: to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, be the glory forever. Amen." The Byzantine text has these words after 14:23 instead. The SBLGNT prints them only in its apparatus, so this translation has no verses 25–27.
