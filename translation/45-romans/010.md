**1** Brothers and sisters,[^1] my heart's desire and my prayer to God for them[^2] is for their salvation.

**2** For I testify about them that they have a zeal for God, but it is not based on knowledge.

**3** For, not knowing the righteousness of God, and seeking to establish their own, they did not submit to the righteousness of God.

**4** For Christ is the end of the Law,[^3] so that there may be righteousness for everyone who believes.

**5** For Moses writes that the person who does the righteousness that comes from the Law will live by it.[^4]

**6** But the righteousness that comes from faith says this: "Do not say in your heart,[^5] 'Who will go up to heaven?'" (that is, to bring Christ down),

**7** or, "Who will go down into the abyss?" (that is, to bring Christ up from the dead).[^6]

**8** But what does it say? "The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart"[^7] (that is, the word of faith that we proclaim).

**9** For if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord,[^8] and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.

**10** For with the heart one believes, leading to righteousness, and with the mouth one confesses, leading to salvation.

**11** For the scripture says, "Everyone who believes in him will not be put to shame."[^9]

**12** For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek. The same Lord is Lord of all, and he is rich toward all who call on him.

**13** For "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved."[^10]

**14** How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? And how will they believe in him whom they have not heard?[^11] And how will they hear without someone to preach?

**15** And how will they preach unless they are sent? As it is written: "How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news of good things!"[^12]

**16** But not all have obeyed the good news. For Isaiah says, "Lord, who has believed our message?"[^13]

**17** So faith comes from hearing the message, and the message comes through the word of Christ.[^14]

**18** But I ask, have they not heard? Indeed they have: "Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world."[^15]

**19** But I ask, did Israel not understand? First Moses says, "I will make you jealous with what is not a nation; with a foolish nation I will provoke you."[^16]

**20** And Isaiah is bold enough to say, "I was found by those who did not seek me; I showed myself to those who did not ask for me."[^17]

**21** But concerning Israel he says, "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people."[^18]

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "for Israel".
[^3]: Or "the goal of the Law". Greek *telos*, "end, goal, fulfillment".
[^4]: Leviticus 18:5. The SBLGNT here follows Westcott and Hort. Other editions read "Moses writes about the righteousness that comes from the Law, that the person who does these things will live by them".
[^5]: Deuteronomy 9:4 (compare 8:17), where this translation has "do not say to yourself". Verses 6–8 then use Deuteronomy 30:12–14.
[^6]: Deuteronomy 30:12–13, adapted. Deuteronomy 30:12 has "Who will go up to heaven for us?", and 30:13 "Who will cross the sea for us?" *Abyss*: Greek *abyssos*, "the deep", here the realm of the dead.
[^7]: Deuteronomy 30:14, which has "very near".
[^8]: Some manuscripts read "if you confess with your mouth the word that Jesus is Lord".
[^9]: Isaiah 28:16, as in the Septuagint (see 9:33), with "everyone" added.
[^10]: Joel 3:5 (2:32 in most English Bibles). In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^11]: Or "of whom they have not heard".
[^12]: Isaiah 52:7, shortened. Isaiah has "on the mountains" and "the one who brings good news". Many manuscripts read "the feet of those who bring good news of peace, who bring good news of good things".
[^13]: Isaiah 53:1, as in the Septuagint, which adds "Lord". *Message*: Greek *akoe*, "hearing, what is heard", where this translation of Isaiah has "what we have heard"; also in verse 17.
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "the word of God".
[^15]: Psalm 19:5 (19:4 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint, which reads "their sound" where the Hebrew has "their line".
[^16]: Deuteronomy 32:21. The Hebrew and the Septuagint have "them" where Paul has "you". *What is not a nation*: so the Septuagint; the Hebrew has "what is no people".
[^17]: Isaiah 65:1, as in the Septuagint, with its two lines in the reverse order. The Hebrew has "I let myself be sought by those who did not ask for me; I let myself be found by those who did not seek me".
[^18]: Isaiah 65:2, as in the Septuagint, which has "disobedient and contrary" where the Hebrew has "rebellious".
