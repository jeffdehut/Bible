**1** What then shall we say? Shall we continue in sin so that grace may increase?

**2** By no means! How can we who died to sin still live in it?

**3** Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?

**4** So we were buried with him through baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too might live a new life.[^1]

**5** For if we have been united with him in the likeness of his death,[^2] we will certainly also be united with him in the likeness of his resurrection.

**6** We know that our old self[^3] was crucified with him, so that the body ruled by sin[^4] might be done away with, so that we would no longer be slaves[^5] to sin.

**7** For the one who has died has been set free[^6] from sin.

**8** Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with him,

**9** knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, will not die again; death no longer has mastery over him.

**10** For the death he died, he died to sin once for all; but the life he lives, he lives to God.

**11** So you also must count yourselves dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus.[^7]

**12** Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires.[^8]

**13** Do not offer the parts of your body[^9] to sin as instruments[^10] of unrighteousness, but offer yourselves to God as people brought from death to life, and the parts of your body to God as instruments of righteousness.

**14** For sin will not be master over you, because you are not under the Law but under grace.

**15** What then? Shall we sin because we are not under the Law but under grace? By no means!

**16** Do you not know that if you offer yourselves to someone as slaves to obey him, you are slaves of the one you obey, either of sin, which leads to death, or of obedience, which leads to righteousness?

**17** But thanks be to God that you, who were slaves of sin, have obeyed from the heart the pattern of teaching to which you were handed over,[^11]

**18** and, having been set free from sin, you have become slaves of righteousness.

**19** I am speaking in human terms because of your human weakness.[^12] For just as you once offered the parts of your body as slaves to impurity and to lawlessness, leading to more lawlessness, so now offer them as slaves to righteousness, leading to holiness.[^13]

**20** For when you were slaves of sin, you were free with regard to righteousness.

**21** So what fruit did you have then from the things of which you are now ashamed?[^14] For the end of those things is death.

**22** But now that you have been set free from sin and have become slaves of God, the fruit you have leads to holiness, and its end is eternal life.

**23** For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

[^1]: Literally "might walk in newness of life".
[^2]: Or "united with the likeness of his death"; also later in the verse. Literally "grown together".
[^3]: Literally "our old human being".
[^4]: Literally "the body of sin".
[^5]: Greek *douleuo*, "be a slave". Paul develops the picture of slavery in verses 16–22, where *doulos* is "slave".
[^6]: Literally "has been declared righteous". Greek *dikaioo*, elsewhere "declare righteous" (traditionally "justify"); with "from" it means to be cleared of, released from (compare Acts 13:38–39).
[^7]: Many manuscripts add "our Lord".
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "so that you obey it in its desires".
[^9]: Literally "your members"; also in verse 19.
[^10]: Or "weapons"; also later in the verse.
[^11]: Or "which was handed on to you".
[^12]: Literally "because of the weakness of your flesh".
[^13]: Or "sanctification"; also in verse 22.
[^14]: Or "So what fruit did you have then? Things of which you are now ashamed."
