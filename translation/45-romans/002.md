**1** Therefore you have no excuse, whoever you are,[^1] when you pass judgment. For in judging another, you condemn yourself, because you, the judge, do the same things.

**2** We know that God's judgment on those who do such things is in accordance with the truth.

**3** Do you suppose, you who judge those who do such things and yet do them yourself, that you will escape the judgment of God?

**4** Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is leading you to repentance?

**5** But because of your hardness and your unrepentant heart, you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath, when the righteous judgment of God is revealed.[^2]

**6** He will repay each person according to what he has done:[^3]

**7** to those who by perseverance in doing good seek glory and honor and immortality, eternal life;

**8** but for those who are self-seeking[^4] and disobey the truth but obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury.

**9** There will be trouble and distress for every human being[^5] who does evil, the Jew first and also the Greek,

**10** but glory and honor and peace for everyone who does good, the Jew first and also the Greek.

**11** For God shows no partiality.[^6]

**12** For all who have sinned without the Law will also perish without the Law, and all who have sinned under the Law[^7] will be judged by the Law.

**13** For it is not the hearers of the Law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the Law who will be declared righteous.[^8]

**14** For when Gentiles, who do not have the Law, do by nature what the Law requires,[^9] they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the Law.

**15** They show that the work of the Law[^10] is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their thoughts in turn accuse or even defend them,

**16** on the day when God judges the secrets of human beings according to my good news, through Christ Jesus.[^11]

**17** But if you call yourself a Jew,[^12] and rely on the Law, and boast in God,

**18** and know his will, and approve what is excellent[^13] because you are instructed from the Law,

**19** and are confident that you are a guide to the blind, a light to those in darkness,

**20** an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of children, because you have in the Law the embodiment of knowledge and truth—

**21** you, then, who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who preach that one must not steal, do you steal?

**22** You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples?

**23** You who boast in the Law, do you dishonor God by breaking the Law?

**24** For "the name of God is despised among the Gentiles because of you," as it is written.[^14]

**25** For circumcision is of value if you practice the Law; but if you break the Law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**26** So if an uncircumcised man keeps the requirements of the Law, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision?

**27** And the one who is physically uncircumcised but fulfills the Law will judge you, who, with the written code and circumcision, break the Law.[^15]

**28** For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is circumcision what is outward, in the flesh.[^16]

**29** No, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is circumcision of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the written code.[^17] That person's praise is not from people but from God.[^18]

[^1]: Literally "O man, everyone who judges". *O man* (Greek *o anthrope*) addresses an imagined listener, as in a debate; also in verse 3.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "the day of wrath and of revelation and of God's righteous judgment".
[^3]: Psalm 62:13 (62:12 in most English Bibles); Proverbs 24:12. Literally "according to his works".
[^4]: Greek *eritheia*, "selfish ambition, rivalry".
[^5]: Literally "every soul of a human being".
[^6]: Literally "there is no receiving of face with God", that is, no favoritism.
[^7]: Literally "in the Law", that is, having the Law.
[^8]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^9]: Or "For when Gentiles, who by nature do not have the Law, do what the Law requires". *By nature* may go with either part of the sentence.
[^10]: That is, what the Law requires. Compare "the works of the Law" in 3:20, and Jeremiah 31:33.
[^11]: Verse 16 may continue the thought of verses 12–13, with verses 14–15 as an aside.
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "Look, you call yourself a Jew".
[^13]: Or "discern what matters". Literally "test the things that differ".
[^14]: Isaiah 52:5, as in the Septuagint, which reads "Because of you my name is despised among the nations". The Hebrew has "all day long my name is continually despised". Compare Ezekiel 36:20–23. *Despised*: Greek *blasphemeo*, "slander, revile, blaspheme".
[^15]: Literally "who through letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the Law". *Letter* (Greek *gramma*) means the Law as written; also in verse 29.
[^16]: That is, physical. Greek *sarx*, "flesh", here the body.
[^17]: Or "in spirit, not in letter". Compare Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6; Jeremiah 4:4.
[^18]: There is a play on words. The name Judah, from which "Jew" comes, is explained in Genesis 29:35 by the Hebrew for "I will praise".
