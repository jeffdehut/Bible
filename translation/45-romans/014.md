**1** Welcome the one who is weak in faith, but not in order to quarrel over opinions.

**2** One person believes he may eat anything, while the one who is weak eats only vegetables.

**3** The one who eats must not despise the one who does not eat, and the one who does not eat must not judge the one who eats, for God has welcomed him.

**4** Who are you to judge someone else's servant? It is before his own master that he stands or falls. And he will stand, for the Lord[^1] is able to make him stand.

**5** One person[^2] regards one day as more important than another, while another regards every day alike. Each one must be fully convinced in his own mind.

**6** The one who observes the day observes it for the Lord.[^3] And the one who eats, eats for the Lord, since he gives thanks to God; and the one who does not eat abstains for the Lord, and he gives thanks to God.

**7** For none of us lives for himself, and none of us dies for himself.

**8** For if we live, we live for the Lord, and if we die, we die for the Lord. So whether we live or whether we die, we belong to the Lord.

**9** For this is why Christ died and lived again,[^4] so that he might be Lord of both the dead and the living.

**10** Why then do you judge your brother or sister?[^5] Or you, why do you despise your brother or sister? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God.[^6]

**11** For it is written: "As surely as I live, says the Lord, every knee will bow to me, and every tongue will confess to God."[^7]

**12** So then each of us will give an account of himself.[^8]

**13** So let us no longer judge one another. Instead, decide[^9] this: never to put a stumbling block or an obstacle in the way of a brother or sister.

**14** I know and am convinced in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean[^10] in itself; but if anyone considers something unclean, then for that person it is unclean.

**15** For[^11] if your brother or sister is distressed because of what you eat, you are no longer living by love. Do not by what you eat destroy that person for whom Christ died.

**16** So do not let what is good for you be spoken of as evil.

**17** For the kingdom of God is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.

**18** Whoever serves Christ in this way is pleasing to God and approved by people.

**19** So then let us pursue what makes for peace and for building one another up.

**20** Do not tear down the work of God for the sake of food. All things are indeed clean, but it is wrong for a person to eat in a way that makes others stumble.[^12]

**21** It is good not to eat meat or drink wine, or do anything that makes your brother or sister stumble, or fall away, or grow weak.[^13]

**22** The faith that you have,[^14] keep between yourself and God. Blessed is the one who does not condemn himself in what he approves.

**23** But whoever has doubts is condemned if he eats, because he does not act from faith; and everything that does not come from faith is sin.[^15]

[^1]: Many manuscripts read "God". *Master* and *Lord* translate the same Greek word, *kyrios*.
[^2]: Some manuscripts read "For one person".
[^3]: Many manuscripts add "and the one who does not observe the day does not observe it for the Lord".
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "Christ both died and rose and lived again".
[^5]: Greek *adelphos*, "brother", a fellow believer; also in verses 13, 15 and 21.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "the judgment seat of Christ".
[^7]: Isaiah 49:18 (and elsewhere), combined with Isaiah 45:23 as in the Septuagint, which has "every tongue will confess to God" where the Hebrew has "every tongue will swear allegiance". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh: "As surely as I live, declares Yahweh". *Confess to God*: or "praise God".
[^8]: Many manuscripts add "to God". The SBLGNT leaves it out, following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort.
[^9]: *Judge* and *decide* translate the same Greek verb, *krino*.
[^10]: Greek *koinos*, "common", that is, ritually unclean; also later in the verse.
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "But".
[^12]: Or "for the person who eats and so stumbles". Literally "for the person who eats through stumbling".
[^13]: Some manuscripts leave out "or fall away, or grow weak".
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "Do you have faith?"
[^15]: Many manuscripts add here, as verses 24–26, the words of praise that other editions print at the end of the letter as 16:25–27 (see the note on 16:24).
