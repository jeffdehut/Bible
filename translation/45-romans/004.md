**1** What then shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh,[^1] has found?[^2]

**2** For if Abraham was declared righteous[^3] by works, he has something to boast about, but not before God.

**3** For what does the scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness."[^4]

**4** Now to the one who works, the wages are not credited as a gift[^5] but as what is owed.

**5** But to the one who does not work but believes in him who declares the ungodly righteous, his faith is credited as righteousness,

**6** just as David also speaks of the happiness[^6] of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works:

**7** "Happy are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven, and whose sins are covered.

**8** Happy is the person whose sin the Lord will never count."[^7]

**9** Is this happiness, then, for the circumcised, or also for the uncircumcised? For we say, "Faith was credited to Abraham as righteousness."

**10** How then was it credited? When he was circumcised, or when he was uncircumcised? Not when he was circumcised, but when he was uncircumcised.

**11** He received the sign of circumcision[^8] as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while still uncircumcised. This was so that he would be the father of all who believe without being circumcised, so that righteousness would be credited to them,[^9]

**12** and the father of the circumcised who are not only circumcised but who also follow in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham had while he was still uncircumcised.[^10]

**13** For the promise to Abraham or to his offspring[^11] that he would inherit the world did not come through the Law but through the righteousness of faith.

**14** For if those who rely on the Law[^12] are heirs, faith is made empty and the promise is nullified.

**15** For the Law brings about wrath, but[^13] where there is no law, there is no transgression either.

**16** That is why it depends on faith, so that it may rest on grace,[^14] and so that the promise may be guaranteed to all his offspring, not only to those who are of the Law but also to those who are of the faith of Abraham (who is the father of us all,

**17** as it is written, "I have made you the father of many nations"),[^15] in the presence of the God in whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls into being things that do not exist.[^16]

**18** Hoping against hope, he believed that he would become the father of many nations, according to what had been said: "So will your offspring be."[^17]

**19** Without weakening in faith, he considered[^18] his own body, which was as good as dead[^19] (for he was about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah's womb.

**20** He did not waver in unbelief concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in faith, giving glory to God,

**21** and was fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.

**22** That is why[^20] it was credited to him as righteousness.

**23** But the words "it was credited to him" were not written for his sake alone,

**24** but for ours also. It will be credited to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

**25** who was handed over for our trespasses and raised so that we would be declared righteous.[^21]

[^1]: That is, by physical descent.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "What then shall we say that Abraham our father has found according to the flesh?" Some leave out "has found": "What then shall we say about Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh?"
[^3]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^4]: Genesis 15:6, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"), where Paul has "God", and reads "he credited it to him as righteousness". *Credited*: Greek *logizomai*, "count, reckon, credit", a word that runs through this chapter.
[^5]: Literally "according to grace".
[^6]: Greek *makarismos*, the declaring of someone "happy" or "blessed" (*makarios*, verses 7–8); also in verse 9.
[^7]: Psalm 32:1–2, as in the Septuagint. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). *Happy*: traditionally "Blessed". *Count*: Greek *logizomai*, the verb translated "credit" in verses 3–6.
[^8]: Compare Genesis 17:11.
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "credited to them also".
[^10]: Literally "and the father of circumcision to those not of circumcision only but also to those who walk in the footsteps of the faith in uncircumcision of our father Abraham".
[^11]: Literally "seed"; also in verses 16 and 18.
[^12]: Literally "those of the Law".
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "for".
[^14]: Literally "Because of this, from faith, so that according to grace". *It* is the promise and its inheritance.
[^15]: Genesis 17:5, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "a multitude of nations".
[^16]: Or "calls things that do not exist as though they did". Literally "calls the things that are not as being".
[^17]: Genesis 15:5.
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "he did not consider".
[^19]: Many manuscripts read "already as good as dead". The SBLGNT leaves out "already", following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort.
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "That is why also". The SBLGNT leaves out "also", following a marginal reading of Westcott and Hort. The words that follow are Genesis 15:6, as in verse 3.
[^21]: Literally "because of our justification" (Greek *dikaiosis*, from the same root as *dikaioo*). *For* in this verse also means "because of". Compare Isaiah 53:12.
