**1** So there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.[^1]

**2** For the law[^2] of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you[^3] free from the law of sin and of death.

**3** For what the Law could not do, because it was weak through the flesh,[^4] God did.[^5] Sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh[^6] and as a sin offering,[^7] he condemned sin in the flesh,

**4** so that the righteous requirement of the Law might be fulfilled in us, who live[^8] not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.

**5** For those who belong to the flesh[^9] set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who belong to the Spirit set their minds on the things of the Spirit.

**6** For the mindset of the flesh is death, but the mindset of the Spirit is life and peace,

**7** because the mindset of the flesh is hostile to God, for it does not submit to the law of God; indeed it cannot.

**8** Those who are in the flesh cannot please God.

**9** You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if indeed the Spirit of God lives in you. But anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him.

**10** But if Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but the Spirit is life because of righteousness.[^10]

**11** And if the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead lives in you, he who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies because of his Spirit who lives in you.[^11]

**12** So then, brothers and sisters,[^12] we are under obligation, not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh.

**13** For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.

**14** For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God.[^13]

**15** For you did not receive a spirit of slavery that leads you back into fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption,[^14] by whom we cry, "Abba,[^15] Father!"

**16** The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit that we are children of God.

**17** And if children, then heirs: heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with him so that we may also be glorified with him.

**18** For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is going to be revealed to us.

**19** For the creation waits with eager longing for the revealing of the sons of God.

**20** For the creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of the one who subjected it, in hope

**21** that[^16] the creation itself will also be set free from its slavery to decay into the freedom of the glory of the children of God.

**22** For we know that the whole creation has been groaning together and suffering the pains of childbirth together until now.

**23** And not only that, but we ourselves, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, also groan inwardly as we wait eagerly for adoption, the redemption of our bodies.

**24** For in this hope we were saved. But hope that is seen is not hope. For who hopes for what he sees?[^17]

**25** But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience.

**26** In the same way the Spirit also helps us in our weakness. For we do not know what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes[^18] with wordless groans.

**27** And he who searches hearts knows what the mind of the Spirit is, because he intercedes for God's holy people[^19] according to the will of God.[^20]

**28** And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good,[^21] for those who are called according to his purpose.

**29** For those whom he foreknew he also predestined[^22] to be conformed to the image of his Son, so that he would be the firstborn among many brothers and sisters.

**30** And those whom he predestined he also called; and those whom he called he also declared righteous;[^23] and those whom he declared righteous he also glorified.

**31** What then shall we say in response to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?

**32** He who did not spare his own Son but handed him over for us all,[^24] how will he not also, with him, graciously give us all things?

**33** Who will bring a charge against God's chosen ones? It is God who declares righteous.[^25]

**34** Who is to condemn? Christ[^26] is the one who died—more than that, who was raised—who is also at the right hand of God,[^27] who also intercedes for us.

**35** Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Will trouble, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or sword?

**36** As it is written: "For your sake we are being killed all day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter."[^28]

**37** No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us.

**38** For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor rulers,[^29] neither things present nor things to come, nor powers,

**39** neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

[^1]: Many manuscripts add "who do not live according to the flesh but according to the Spirit" (compare verse 4).
[^2]: Or "principle" (see 7:21); also later in the verse.
[^3]: *You* is singular in Greek. Many manuscripts read "me".
[^4]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". In Paul's moral sense, human nature in its weakness and opposition to God; so through verse 13.
[^5]: The Greek has no verb for this first clause. Literally "For the inability of the Law, in that it was weak through the flesh, God, sending …".
[^6]: Literally "flesh of sin".
[^7]: Or "and for sin". Greek *peri hamartias*, the phrase the Septuagint uses for the sin offering (for example Leviticus 5:6–7; Isaiah 53:10).
[^8]: Literally "walk".
[^9]: Literally "those who are according to the flesh"; also "according to the Spirit" later in the verse.
[^10]: Or "your spirit is alive because of righteousness".
[^11]: Some manuscripts read "through his Spirit who lives in you".
[^12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 29.
[^13]: Greek *huioi*, "sons", as in verse 19. Paul includes all believers, men and women, as heirs (verse 17); in verses 16–17 and 21 he uses *tekna*, "children".
[^14]: Or "a spirit of adoption". Greek *huiothesia*, "adoption as sons", the legal act that made someone a son and heir; also in verse 23.
[^15]: Aramaic for "father". Compare Mark 14:36; Galatians 4:6.
[^16]: Or "in hope, because".
[^17]: Many manuscripts read "For why does anyone still hope for what he sees?"
[^18]: Many manuscripts add "for us".
[^19]: Literally "holy ones"; traditionally "saints".
[^20]: Literally "according to God".
[^21]: Or "God works all things together for good", or "in all things God works for good". Some manuscripts read "God works all things together for good".
[^22]: Or "decided beforehand". Greek *proorizo*; also in verse 30.
[^23]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify"; also in verse 33.
[^24]: Compare Genesis 22:12, 16.
[^25]: Compare Isaiah 50:8–9. Verses 33–34 may also be read as questions: "Is it God, who declares righteous? … Is it Christ, who died …?"
[^26]: Some manuscripts read "Christ Jesus", and some add "from the dead" after "raised".
[^27]: Compare Psalm 110:1.
[^28]: Psalm 44:23 (44:22 in most English Bibles).
[^29]: Or "principalities", spiritual powers.
