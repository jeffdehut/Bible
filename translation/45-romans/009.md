**1** I am telling the truth in Christ, I am not lying; my conscience bears me witness in the Holy Spirit

**2** that I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart.

**3** For I could wish that I myself were accursed[^1] and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers and sisters,[^2] my own people according to the flesh.[^3]

**4** They are Israelites. To them belong the adoption,[^4] the glory, the covenants, the giving of the Law, the worship and the promises.

**5** To them belong the patriarchs, and from them, according to the flesh, comes the Messiah,[^5] who is over all, God blessed forever. Amen.[^6]

**6** But it is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all who are descended from Israel are Israel,

**7** and not all are children because they are Abraham's offspring,[^7] but "It is through Isaac that your offspring will be named."[^8]

**8** That is, it is not the children of the flesh[^9] who are children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as offspring.

**9** For this is what the promise said: "At this time I will come, and Sarah will have a son."[^10]

**10** And not only that, but Rebekah too, when she had conceived children by one man, our father Isaac —

**11** for though they were not yet born and had done nothing either good or bad, so that God's purpose in choosing might stand,

**12** not because of works but because of him who calls, she was told, "The older will serve the younger."[^11]

**13** As it is written: "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated."[^12]

**14** What then shall we say? Is there injustice[^13] with God? By no means!

**15** For he says to Moses, "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion."[^14]

**16** So then it depends not on human will or effort, but on God, who has mercy.[^15]

**17** For the scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very purpose I raised you up, to show my power in you, and so that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth."[^16]

**18** So then he has mercy on whom he wants, and he hardens whom he wants.

**19** You will say to me then, "Why does he still find fault? For who can resist his will?"

**20** On the contrary, who are you, a mere human being,[^17] to talk back to God? Will what is formed say to the one who formed it, "Why did you make me like this?"[^18]

**21** Or does the potter not have the right over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for an honorable use and another for a dishonorable use?[^19]

**22** What if God, wanting[^20] to show his wrath and to make his power known, has endured with much patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction,

**23** in order to make known the riches of his glory to vessels of mercy, which he prepared beforehand for glory,

**24** even us, whom he also called, not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles?

**25** As he also says in Hosea: "Those who were not my people I will call 'my people,' and her who was not loved I will call 'loved.'[^21]

**26** And in the place where it was said to them, 'You are not my people,' there they will be called children[^22] of the living God."[^23]

**27** And Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: "Though the number of the Israelites be like the sand of the sea, the remnant will be saved;

**28** for the Lord will carry out his word on the earth, completing it and cutting it short."[^24]

**29** And as Isaiah said beforehand: "If the Lord of Armies[^25] had not left us offspring, we would have been like Sodom; we would have resembled Gomorrah."[^26]

**30** What then shall we say? That Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, have obtained righteousness, the righteousness that comes from faith;

**31** but Israel, who pursued a law of righteousness, did not attain to that law.[^27]

**32** Why? Because they did not pursue it by faith, but as if it were by works.[^28] They stumbled over the stone that makes people stumble,

**33** as it is written: "Look, I am laying in Zion a stone that makes people stumble and a rock that makes them fall, and whoever believes in him[^29] will not be put to shame."[^30]

[^1]: Greek *anathema*, something devoted to God for destruction, and so under a curse.
[^2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", here Paul's fellow Jews.
[^3]: That is, by physical descent; also in verses 5 and 8.
[^4]: Greek *huiothesia*, "adoption as sons" (see 8:15). Compare Exodus 4:22.
[^5]: Greek *ho christos*, "the Christ", "the Anointed One".
[^6]: The Greek manuscripts have little punctuation, and the meaning depends on where a full stop is placed. The SBLGNT punctuates as translated here. Others read "the Messiah. God, who is over all, be blessed forever! Amen", or "the Messiah, who is over all. God be blessed forever! Amen".
[^7]: Literally "seed"; also in verses 7–8 and 29.
[^8]: Genesis 21:12. Literally "In Isaac seed will be called for you". The verb is *kaleo*, "call", as in verses 12 and 24–26.
[^9]: Literally "the children of the flesh", that is, those born by physical descent.
[^10]: Genesis 18:10, 14, quoted freely.
[^11]: Genesis 25:23.
[^12]: Malachi 1:2–3.
[^13]: Greek *adikia*, translated "unrighteousness" in 1:18 and 3:5.
[^14]: Exodus 33:19, as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and I will show compassion to whom I will show compassion".
[^15]: Literally "not of the one who wills nor of the one who runs, but of God who has mercy".
[^16]: Exodus 9:16. The Hebrew has "I have let you live" (literally "I have caused you to stand"), and "to show you my power".
[^17]: Literally "O man", as in 2:1.
[^18]: Compare Isaiah 29:16; 45:9.
[^19]: Literally "one vessel for honor and another for dishonor". Compare Isaiah 45:9; Jeremiah 18:6.
[^20]: Or "although he wanted", or "because he wanted".
[^21]: Hosea 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles), quoted freely. The Hebrew has "I will have compassion on Lo-ruhamah ('not shown compassion'), and I will say to Lo-ammi ('not my people'), 'You are my people'".
[^22]: Greek *huioi*, "sons". The Hebrew of Hosea has *bene*, "sons, children".
[^23]: Hosea 2:1 (1:10 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint, which has "they will be called" where the Hebrew has "it will be said to them".
[^24]: Isaiah 10:22–23, mostly as in the Septuagint; the first line also echoes Hosea 2:1 (1:10 in most English Bibles). The Hebrew reads "only a remnant of them will return. Destruction has been decreed, overflowing with righteousness. For the Lord GOD of Armies will carry out the destruction he has decreed in the middle of all the earth". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). *Completing it and cutting it short*: or "fully and swiftly"; literally "finishing and cutting short a word". Many manuscripts read "for he will finish the word and cut it short in righteousness, because the Lord will carry out a word cut short on the earth", as in the Septuagint.
[^25]: Greek *kyrios sabaoth*, from the Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, "the LORD of Armies", traditionally "the LORD of hosts". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD").
[^26]: Isaiah 1:9, as in the Septuagint, which has "offspring" (literally "seed") where the Hebrew has "a few survivors".
[^27]: Literally "pursuing a law of righteousness, did not arrive at law". Many manuscripts read "did not attain to the law of righteousness".
[^28]: Many manuscripts read "by works of the Law. For they stumbled".
[^29]: Or "in it". Many manuscripts read "everyone who believes in him".
[^30]: Isaiah 28:16 combined with 8:14. Paul follows the Septuagint of 28:16 in "will not be put to shame", where the Hebrew has "will not panic".
