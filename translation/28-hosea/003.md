**1** The LORD[^1] said to me, "Go again, love a woman who is loved by another man[^2] and is an adulteress, just as the LORD loves the Israelites, though they turn to other gods and love raisin cakes."[^3]

**2** So I bought her for myself for fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley.[^4]

**3** Then I said to her, "You are to live with me for many days. You must not be a prostitute or belong to any man, and I too will be faithful to you."[^5]

**4** For the Israelites will live for many days without king or official, without sacrifice or standing stone, without ephod or household gods.[^6]

**5** Afterward the Israelites will return and seek the LORD their God and David their king.[^7] They will come trembling to the LORD and to his goodness in the last days.[^8]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Or "who is loved by her husband". Hebrew *rea*, "friend, companion", which can also mean a lover or a husband (Jeremiah 3:20).
[^3]: Cakes of pressed raisins were eaten at festivals (2 Samuel 6:19), here probably festivals of other gods.
[^4]: A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so fifteen shekels were about 6 ounces (170 grams). A homer was probably about 6 bushels (220 liters). A *lethech* was perhaps half a homer.
[^5]: Literally "and also I to you". Or "and I will not come to you either".
[^6]: *Standing stones* (Hebrew *matsevot*) were set up as objects of worship. The *ephod* was a priestly garment, sometimes an object used to seek guidance from God (Judges 8:27; 17:5). *Household gods* is Hebrew *teraphim*, small images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19).
[^7]: That is, a king from the family of David.
[^8]: Literally "at the end of the days".
