**1** Hear this, you priests! Pay attention, house of Israel! Listen, house of the king! For the judgment is for you,[^1] because you have been a snare at Mizpah and a net spread out on Tabor.

**2** The rebels have gone deep into slaughter,[^2] but I will discipline them all.[^3]

**3** I know Ephraim, and Israel is not hidden from me. For now, Ephraim, you have prostituted yourself; Israel is defiled.

**4** Their deeds do not allow them to return to their God, for a spirit of prostitution is within them, and they do not know the LORD.[^4]

**5** Israel's pride testifies against him.[^5] Israel and Ephraim will stumble in their iniquity, and Judah also will stumble with them.

**6** With their flocks and their herds they will go to seek the LORD, but they will not find him; he has withdrawn from them.

**7** They have been unfaithful to the LORD, for they have borne children who are not his.[^6] Now the new moon[^7] will devour them along with their fields.

**8** Blow the horn in Gibeah, the trumpet in Ramah! Sound the alarm in Beth-aven![^8] Behind you, Benjamin![^9]

**9** Ephraim will become a desolation on the day of punishment. Among the tribes of Israel I make known what is certain.

**10** The officials of Judah have become like those who move a boundary stone.[^10] I will pour out my fury on them like water.

**11** Ephraim is oppressed, crushed in judgment,[^11] because he was determined to follow a command.[^12]

**12** I am like a moth[^13] to Ephraim, like rot to the house of Judah.

**13** When Ephraim saw his sickness and Judah his wound, Ephraim went to Assyria and sent to the great king.[^14] But he cannot heal you or cure you of your wound.

**14** For I will be like a lion to Ephraim, like a young lion to the house of Judah. I myself will tear them to pieces and go away; I will carry them off, and no one will rescue them.

**15** I will go back to my place until they admit their guilt[^15] and seek my face. In their distress they will earnestly seek me.

[^1]: Or "For you are responsible for justice". Hebrew *mishpat*.
[^2]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Some scholars read "they have dug deep the pit of Shittim", with small changes to the Hebrew, continuing the images of the snare and the net in verse 1.
[^3]: Literally "and I am discipline for all of them".
[^4]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "answers in his face".
[^6]: Literally "foreign children".
[^7]: Or "a month", that is, very soon. The new moon was a festival (2:13; 2:11 in most English Bibles).
[^8]: See 4:15.
[^9]: Perhaps a battle cry (compare Judges 5:14). Gibeah, Ramah and Bethel lay on the road north from Jerusalem, in the land of Benjamin.
[^10]: Moving a neighbor's boundary stone was a serious crime (Deuteronomy 19:14; 27:17).
[^11]: Or "crushed, deprived of justice".
[^12]: Hebrew *tsav*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "command" or a mocking syllable (compare Isaiah 28:10). The Septuagint reads "worthless things" (Hebrew *shav*).
[^13]: Or "like pus". Hebrew *ash*.
[^14]: The Hebrew as pointed reads *melekh yarev*, "King Jareb" or "a king who contends". The same consonants, divided differently, can be read *malki rav*, "the great king", a title of the kings of Assyria. Also in 10:6.
[^15]: Or "until they bear their punishment".
