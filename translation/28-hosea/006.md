**1** "Come, let us return to the LORD.[^1] For he has torn us, but he will heal us; he has struck us, but he will bind up our wounds.

**2** After two days he will revive us; on the third day he will raise us up, so that we may live before him.[^2]

**3** Let us know, let us press on to know the LORD. His coming is as sure as the dawn. He will come to us like the rain, like the spring rain that waters the earth."[^3]

**4** What shall I do with you, Ephraim? What shall I do with you, Judah? Your faithful love[^4] is like the morning mist, like the dew that goes away early.

**5** That is why I have cut them to pieces by the prophets; I have killed them by the words of my mouth, and my judgment goes out like light.[^5]

**6** For I desire faithful love, not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.[^6]

**7** But like Adam they have broken the covenant;[^7] there they have been unfaithful to me.

**8** Gilead is a city of evildoers, tracked with bloody footprints.

**9** As bands of robbers lie in wait for someone, so the company of priests murder on the road to Shechem.[^8] Indeed, they have committed shameful crimes.

**10** In the house of Israel I have seen a horrible thing. Ephraim's prostitution is there; Israel is defiled.

**11** For you also, Judah, a harvest is appointed, when I restore the fortunes of my people.[^9]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. Verses 1–3 give the words of the people who seek the LORD in their distress (5:15).
[^2]: That is, soon. Some Christian writers have connected this verse with the resurrection of Jesus "on the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:4).
[^3]: Hebrew *malqosh*, the spring rain of March and April, and *yoreh*, which can mean "the autumn rain" or "waters".
[^4]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love. Also in verse 6.
[^5]: The Hebrew as pointed reads "and your judgments, light goes out". The same consonants, divided differently, read "and my judgment goes out like light", as in the Septuagint.
[^6]: The first half of the verse is quoted by Jesus in Matthew 9:13 and 12:7, following the Septuagint: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice."
[^7]: Or "like human beings" or "at Adam". Adam was also a town in the Jordan valley (Joshua 3:16), which would explain "there".
[^8]: The Hebrew of the first part of the verse is uncertain.
[^9]: Many translations join this last clause to 7:1.
