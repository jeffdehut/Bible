**1** When Israel was a child, I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son.[^1]

**2** The more they were called, the more they went away from them.[^2] They kept sacrificing to the Baals and burning incense to carved images.

**3** Yet it was I who taught Ephraim to walk, taking them by their arms,[^3] but they did not know that I healed them.

**4** I led them with cords of human kindness,[^4] with bands of love. I was to them like those who lift a yoke from their jaws,[^5] and I bent down to them and fed them.

**5** He will not return to the land of Egypt,[^6] but Assyria will be his king, because they refused to return to me.[^7]

**6** The sword will whirl through his cities; it will destroy the bars of his gates[^8] and devour them because of their schemes.

**7** My people are bent on turning away from me. They call him upward, but he will not raise them up at all.[^9]

**8** How can I give you up, Ephraim? How can I hand you over, Israel? How can I make you like Admah? How can I treat you like Zeboiim?[^10] My heart is turned over within me; all my compassion is stirred.[^11]

**9** I will not carry out my burning anger; I will not again destroy Ephraim. For I am God and not a human being, the Holy One among you, and I will not come in wrath.[^12]

**10** They will follow the LORD;[^13] he will roar like a lion. When he roars, his children will come trembling from the west.[^14]

**11** They will come trembling like birds from Egypt, like doves from the land of Assyria, and I will settle them in their homes, declares the LORD.

[^1]: Quoted in Matthew 2:15, which follows the Hebrew. The Septuagint reads "and out of Egypt I called his children".
[^2]: Literally "They called to them; so they went from before them", perhaps meaning the prophets (verse 7; 12:11, 12:10 in most English Bibles). The Septuagint reads "The more I called them, the more they went away from me".
[^3]: Literally "he took them on his arms". The Septuagint and the Vulgate read "I took them in my arms".
[^4]: Literally "cords of a man".
[^5]: Or "like those who lift a little child to their cheek", reading the same consonants as *ul*, "infant", instead of *ol*, "yoke".
[^6]: Or "Will he not return to the land of Egypt?" Elsewhere Hosea says that Ephraim will return to Egypt (8:13; 9:3).
[^7]: "To me" is supplied. Literally "they refused to return".
[^8]: Literally "his bars". Some take the word to mean "his boasters" or "his false prophets".
[^9]: The Hebrew of this verse is uncertain. *Upward* (Hebrew *al*) may be a title of God, "the Most High" (compare 7:16).
[^10]: Admah and Zeboiim were destroyed with Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 14:2; Deuteronomy 29:22, 29:23 in most English Bibles).
[^11]: Literally "my compassions grow warm together".
[^12]: Or "I will not come into the city". The Hebrew *be-ir* usually means "into a city".
[^13]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^14]: Literally "from the sea".
