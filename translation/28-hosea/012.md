**1** Ephraim has surrounded me with lies, and the house of Israel with deceit.[^1] But Judah still walks with God and is faithful to the Holy One.[^2]

**2** Ephraim feeds on the wind and pursues the east wind all day long. He multiplies lies and violence. They make a covenant with Assyria, and olive oil is carried to Egypt.

**3** Yahweh[^3] has a case against Judah; he will punish Jacob according to his ways and repay him according to his deeds.

**4** In the womb he grasped his brother's heel,[^4] and in his manhood he struggled with God.[^5]

**5** He struggled with the angel and prevailed; he wept and pleaded with him. He found him at Bethel, and there he spoke with us.[^6]

**6** Yahweh, the God of Armies,[^7] Yahweh is his name![^8]

**7** As for you, return to your God with his help.[^9] Keep faithful love[^10] and justice, and wait for your God continually.

**8** A merchant,[^11] in whose hands are false scales, he loves to oppress.

**9** Ephraim says, "Surely I have become rich; I have found wealth for myself.[^12] In all my labor they will find in me no iniquity that is sin."

**10** But I am Yahweh your God from the land of Egypt.[^13] I will make you live in tents again, as in the days of the appointed festival.[^14]

**11** I spoke to the prophets; it was I who multiplied visions, and through the prophets I spoke in parables.[^15]

**12** If Gilead is wicked,[^16] surely they are worthless. In Gilgal they sacrifice bulls; their altars will be like heaps of stones[^17] in the furrows of a field.

**13** Jacob fled to the country of Aram; Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep.[^18]

**14** By a prophet Yahweh brought Israel up from Egypt, and by a prophet Israel was kept.[^19]

**15** Ephraim has provoked bitter anger, so his Lord will leave his bloodguilt on him and repay him for his contempt.

[^1]: This verse is 11:12 in most English Bibles, and verses 2–15 are 12:1–14.
[^2]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. It may also mean "and Judah is still unruly against God, against the faithful Holy One". *Holy One* is plural in Hebrew, probably a plural of majesty (compare Proverbs 9:10; 30:3), or "the holy ones".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: A play on words: *aqav*, "he grasped the heel", and *Ya'aqov*, "Jacob" (Genesis 25:26; 27:36).
[^5]: A play on words: *sarah*, "he struggled", and *Yisra'el*, "Israel" (Genesis 32:25–29, 32:24–28 in most English Bibles). Also in verse 5.
[^6]: The Septuagint and the Syriac read "with him". On Jacob at Bethel, see Genesis 28:10–22; 35:1–15.
[^7]: *Yahweh, the God of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH Elohei ha-tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD God of hosts". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^8]: Literally "Yahweh is his memorial", the name by which he is to be remembered (Exodus 3:15).
[^9]: Literally "by your God you will return".
[^10]: Hebrew *chesed*, loyal, covenant love.
[^11]: Hebrew *kena'an*, "Canaan", also used for "trader" (compare Zephaniah 1:11).
[^12]: A play on words: *on*, "wealth", and *avon*, "iniquity".
[^13]: That is, since the time of the exodus. Also in 13:4.
[^14]: Probably the Festival of Shelters, when the Israelites lived in shelters to remember the wilderness (Leviticus 23:42–43).
[^15]: Or "and through the prophets I will bring destruction". The Hebrew verb can have either meaning.
[^16]: Hebrew *aven*, "wickedness", the word used in the mocking name Beth-aven (4:15).
[^17]: A play on words: *Gilgal* and *gallim*, "heaps of stones".
[^18]: See Genesis 28:5; 29:15–30. *Kept* (Hebrew *shamar*) is the same verb as in verse 14.
[^19]: Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15; 34:10).
