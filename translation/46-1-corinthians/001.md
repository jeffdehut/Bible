**1** Paul, called to be an apostle of Christ Jesus[^1] by the will of God, and our brother Sosthenes,[^2]

**2** to the church of God in Corinth, to those made holy[^3] in Christ Jesus, called to be God's holy people,[^4] together with all those in every place who call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ,[^5] their Lord and ours:[^6]

**3** Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**4** I always thank my God for you, because of the grace of God that was given to you in Christ Jesus.

**5** For in him you have been made rich in every way, in all speech[^7] and all knowledge,

**6** just as the testimony about Christ[^8] was firmly established among you.

**7** So you do not lack any gift[^9] as you eagerly wait for the revealing[^10] of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**8** He[^11] will also keep you firmly established to the end, blameless on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**9** God is faithful, and by him you were called into fellowship[^12] with his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord.

**10** I appeal to you, brothers and sisters,[^13] by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree with one another,[^14] and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be united in the same mind and the same purpose.

**11** For it has been made clear to me about you, my brothers and sisters, by Chloe's people,[^15] that there are quarrels among you.

**12** What I mean is this: each of you says, "I belong to Paul,"[^16] or "I belong to Apollos," or "I belong to Cephas,"[^17] or "I belong to Christ."

**13** Has Christ been divided?[^18] Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name[^19] of Paul?

**14** I am thankful[^20] that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius,[^21]

**15** so that no one can say that you were baptized in my name.[^22]

**16** I also baptized the household of Stephanas. Beyond that, I do not know whether I baptized anyone else.

**17** For Christ did not send me to baptize but to proclaim the good news, and not with wise and clever speech,[^23] so that the cross of Christ would not be emptied of its power.[^24]

**18** For the message[^25] of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.

**19** For it is written: "I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the understanding of the discerning I will set aside."[^26]

**20** Where is the wise person? Where is the scribe?[^27] Where is the debater of this age? Hasn't God made the wisdom of the world foolish?

**21** For since, in the wisdom of God, the world did not know God through its wisdom, God was pleased, through the foolishness of what we proclaim,[^28] to save those who believe.

**22** For Jews ask for signs and Greeks look for wisdom,

**23** but we proclaim Christ crucified,[^29] a stumbling block[^30] to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles,[^31]

**24** but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God.

**25** For the foolishness of God is wiser than humans are, and the weakness of God is stronger than humans are.

**26** Consider your own calling, brothers and sisters: not many of you were wise by human standards,[^32] not many were powerful, not many were of noble birth.

**27** But God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise, and God chose what is weak in the world to shame the strong.

**28** God chose what is lowborn[^33] in the world and what is despised, things that are not, to bring to nothing[^34] things that are,

**29** so that no human being[^35] may boast before God.

**30** It is because of him that you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness and holiness and redemption,[^36]

**31** so that, as it is written, "Let the one who boasts boast in the Lord."[^37]

[^1]: *Christ*: Greek *Christos*, "anointed one", the Greek equivalent of "Messiah". In Paul's letters it usually works as a name, so this translation writes "Christ". Paul's order, "Christ Jesus" or "Jesus Christ", is kept as the Greek has it.
[^2]: Literally "Sosthenes the brother". A Sosthenes was leader of the synagogue in Corinth (Acts 18:17); whether he is the same man is not known.
[^3]: Or "sanctified", "set apart as holy".
[^4]: Literally "called holy ones", traditionally "called to be saints".
[^5]: To "call on the name" of the Lord is the Old Testament's expression for praying to and worshiping the LORD (Genesis 12:8; Joel 3:5, 2:32 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Or "in every place, theirs and ours".
[^7]: Greek *logos*, "word, speech".
[^8]: Literally "the testimony of Christ". *Among you*: or "in you".
[^9]: Greek *charisma*, "gift of grace", from *charis*, "grace".
[^10]: Greek *apokalypsis*, "revelation, unveiling".
[^11]: Greek "who", referring either to God (verse 4) or to Jesus Christ (verse 7).
[^12]: Greek *koinonia*, "fellowship, partnership, sharing".
[^13]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 11 and 26.
[^14]: Literally "that you all say the same thing".
[^15]: Literally "by those of Chloe", probably members of her household or people who worked for her.
[^16]: Literally "I am of Paul"; and so for each of the names.
[^17]: The Aramaic name of the apostle Peter (John 1:42).
[^18]: Or "Christ has been divided!"
[^19]: Literally "into the name"; also in verse 15.
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "I thank God".
[^21]: Crispus had been the leader of the synagogue in Corinth (Acts 18:8). A Gaius is Paul's host in Romans 16:23.
[^22]: Many manuscripts read "that I baptized in my own name".
[^23]: Literally "not in wisdom of word".
[^24]: Literally "would not be emptied".
[^25]: Greek *logos*, the word translated "speech" in verse 17.
[^26]: Isaiah 29:14, following the Septuagint, in which God says "I will destroy". The Hebrew reads "The wisdom of their wise will perish, and the understanding of their discerning will vanish." The Septuagint ends "I will hide"; Paul has "I will set aside".
[^27]: Or "the expert in the law". Compare Isaiah 19:12 and 33:18.
[^28]: Greek *kerygma*, "proclamation, preaching". Literally "the foolishness of the proclamation".
[^29]: Or "a crucified Messiah".
[^30]: Greek *skandalon*, something that trips people up, a cause of offense.
[^31]: Many manuscripts read "Greeks".
[^32]: Literally "according to the flesh".
[^33]: Literally "low-born", the opposite of "of noble birth" in verse 26.
[^34]: Greek *katargeo*, "make ineffective, abolish, bring to an end".
[^35]: Literally "no flesh".
[^36]: Or "who became for us wisdom from God, that is, righteousness, holiness and redemption". *Holiness*: or "sanctification". *Redemption*: release, as of a slave or captive, by payment of a price.
[^37]: A summary of Jeremiah 9:22–23 (9:23–24 in most English Bibles), where the one who boasts is to boast that he knows God, "that I am the LORD". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" here is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). Also in 2 Corinthians 10:17.
