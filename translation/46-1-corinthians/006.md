**1** When any of you has a dispute with another, does he dare to go to court before the unrighteous,[^1] and not before God's holy people?[^2]

**2** Or don't you know that God's holy people will judge the world? And if the world is to be judged by you, are you not competent to try the smallest cases?

**3** Don't you know that we will judge angels? How much more, then, the matters of this life!

**4** So if you have cases about the matters of this life, do you appoint as judges those who have no standing in the church?[^3]

**5** I say this to your shame. Is there really no one among you wise enough to decide between one brother or sister and another?[^4]

**6** Instead, brother or sister goes to court against brother or sister, and that before unbelievers!

**7** In fact, to have lawsuits with one another at all is already a defeat for you. Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be cheated?

**8** Instead, you yourselves do wrong and cheat, and you do it to your brothers and sisters.[^5]

**9** Or don't you know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: neither the sexually immoral,[^6] nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor soft men,[^7] nor men who lie with males,[^8]

**10** nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor slanderers, nor swindlers will inherit the kingdom of God.

**11** And that is what some of you were. But you were washed, you were made holy,[^9] you were declared righteous[^10] in the name of the Lord Jesus[^11] and by the Spirit of our God.

**12** "I have the right to do anything,"[^12] but not everything is beneficial. "I have the right to do anything," but I will not be brought under the power of anything.[^13]

**13** "Food is for the stomach and the stomach for food,"[^14] and God will bring both the one and the other to nothing.[^15] But the body is not for sexual immorality; it is for the Lord, and the Lord is for the body.

**14** And God raised the Lord, and he will also raise us by his power.

**15** Don't you know that your bodies are members[^16] of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them members of a prostitute? By no means![^17]

**16** Or don't you know that whoever joins himself to a prostitute is one body with her? For it says,[^18] "The two will become one flesh."[^19]

**17** But whoever joins himself to the Lord is one spirit with him.

**18** Flee from sexual immorality. Every sin a person commits is outside the body,[^20] but the one who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body.

**19** Or don't you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you,[^21] whom you have from God? You are not your own,

**20** for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.[^22]

[^1]: That is, before judges who are not believers.
[^2]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints"; also in verse 2.
[^3]: Or, as a command, "appoint as judges even those who have the lowest standing in the church!"
[^4]: Literally "between his brother", a shortened expression. The SBLGNT's editor notes a possible conjectural change, adding "and his brother". Greek *adelphos*, "brother", means any fellow believer; so also in verse 6.
[^5]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10).
[^6]: Greek *pornoi*, those who engage in *porneia*, sexual relations outside marriage (see 5:1).
[^7]: Greek *malakoi*, literally "soft ones". The word was used of men regarded as effeminate, self-indulgent or lacking self-control, and in sexual contexts especially of males who took the passive role in sex with men. Interpreters differ over how broadly or narrowly Paul uses it here.
[^8]: Greek *arsenokoitai*, a rare compound of *arsen*, "male", and *koite*, "bed", a common euphemism for sexual intercourse. The two words stand side by side in the Septuagint of Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13 ("lie with a male"), and many scholars think the term was formed from those verses. Its earliest known uses are here and in 1 Timothy 1:10. Whether it refers to sex between men generally or to particular practices, such as exploitation of boys or prostitution, is debated. Traditional renderings include "abusers of themselves with mankind", "sodomites" and "homosexuals".
[^9]: Or "sanctified".
[^10]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify".
[^11]: The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "the Lord Jesus Christ", and Westcott and Hort "our Lord Jesus Christ".
[^12]: Or "Everything is permitted for me". Most interpreters take this as a saying used by some in Corinth, which Paul quotes and then qualifies; the Greek has no quotation marks. Also in 10:23.
[^13]: "I have the right" and "be brought under the power" are a play on related Greek words, *exestin* and *exousiazo*.
[^14]: Most interpreters take this as another saying from Corinth. Some extend the quotation to "and God will bring both the one and the other to nothing".
[^15]: Greek *katargeo* (see 1:28).
[^16]: Or "parts of the body", "limbs"; also twice more in this verse.
[^17]: Greek *me genoito*, "may it not be", a strong denial.
[^18]: Or "he says", meaning God.
[^19]: Genesis 2:24, following the Septuagint, which adds "the two" (compare Mark 10:8).
[^20]: Many translations add "other": "Every other sin". Some interpreters take this sentence as another Corinthian saying, which Paul answers in the rest of the verse.
[^21]: "Body" is singular and "you" plural in Greek. *Temple*: Greek *naos*, the sanctuary itself (see 3:16).
[^22]: Many manuscripts add "and in your spirit, which belong to God".
