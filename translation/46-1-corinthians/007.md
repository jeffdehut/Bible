**1** Now concerning the matters you wrote about:[^1] "It is good for a man not to touch a woman."[^2]

**2** But because of cases of sexual immorality,[^3] each man should have his own wife, and each woman should have her own husband.[^4]

**3** The husband should give his wife what he owes her,[^5] and likewise the wife her husband.

**4** The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does. Likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does.

**5** Do not deprive one another, except perhaps by mutual agreement for a time, so that you may devote yourselves to prayer,[^6] and then come together again, so that Satan does not tempt you because of your lack of self-control.

**6** I say this[^7] as a concession, not as a command.

**7** Still, I wish[^8] that everyone were as I am myself. But each has his own gift[^9] from God, one of one kind and another of another.

**8** To the unmarried[^10] and to widows I say: it is good for them if they remain as I am.

**9** But if they are not exercising self-control, they should marry, for it is better to marry than to burn with passion.[^11]

**10** To the married I give this command, not I but the Lord:[^12] a wife is not to separate from her husband

**11** (but if she does separate, she must remain unmarried or else be reconciled to her husband), and a husband is not to divorce[^13] his wife.

**12** To the rest I say, I and not the Lord: if any brother has a wife who is an unbeliever, and she is willing to live with him, he must not divorce her.

**13** And if any woman has a husband who is an unbeliever, and he is willing to live with her, she must not divorce her husband.

**14** For the unbelieving husband is made holy[^14] through his wife, and the unbelieving wife is made holy through the brother.[^15] Otherwise your children would be unclean, but as it is, they are holy.

**15** But if the unbeliever separates, let him separate. The brother or the sister is not bound[^16] in such cases. But God has called us[^17] to live in peace.

**16** For how do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you will save your wife?[^18]

**17** Only, let each one live[^19] as the Lord has assigned to him, as God has called each one.[^20] This is what I command in all the churches.

**18** Was anyone already circumcised when he was called? He should not try to undo his circumcision.[^21] Has anyone been called while uncircumcised? He should not be circumcised.

**19** Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing; what counts is keeping the commandments of God.

**20** Each one should remain in the condition in which he was called.[^22]

**21** Were you a slave when you were called? Do not let it trouble you. But if you are able to become free, make use of it instead.[^23]

**22** For whoever was called in the Lord as a slave is the Lord's freedman.[^24] Likewise, whoever was free when he was called is a slave of Christ.

**23** You were bought with a price. Do not become slaves of human beings.

**24** Brothers and sisters,[^25] each one should remain with God in the condition in which he was called.

**25** Now concerning virgins:[^26] I have no command from the Lord, but I give my opinion as one who by the Lord's mercy is trustworthy.

**26** So I think this is good because of the present distress:[^27] it is good for a person to remain as he is.

**27** Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be released. Are you free from a wife?[^28] Do not seek a wife.

**28** But if you do marry, you have not sinned, and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. Yet such people will have trouble in this life,[^29] and I would spare you that.

**29** What I mean, brothers and sisters, is this: the time is short.[^30] From now on, those who have wives should be as though they had none,

**30** those who weep as though they were not weeping, those who rejoice as though they were not rejoicing, those who buy as though they had no possessions,

**31** and those who use the world as though they were not making full use of it.[^31] For the present form of this world is passing away.

**32** I want you to be free from concerns. The unmarried man is concerned about the things of the Lord, how to please the Lord.

**33** But the married man is concerned about the things of the world, how to please his wife,

**34** and his interests are divided. And the unmarried woman and the virgin are concerned about the things of the Lord, so as to be holy in both body and spirit. But the married woman is concerned about the things of the world, how to please her husband.[^32]

**35** I say this for your own benefit, not to put a restraint on you,[^33] but to promote what is fitting and undivided devotion to the Lord.[^34]

**36** If anyone thinks he is not behaving properly toward his virgin, if his passions are strong,[^35] and it has to be, let him do as he wishes. He is not sinning. Let them marry.

**37** But whoever stands firm in his heart, being under no necessity but having control over his own will, and has decided in his own heart to keep his virgin as she is, will do well.

**38** So then, he who marries his virgin[^36] does well, and he who does not marry her will do better.

**39** A wife is bound[^37] as long as her husband lives. But if her husband dies,[^38] she is free to be married to anyone she wishes, only in the Lord.

**40** But in my judgment she is happier[^39] if she remains as she is. And I think that I too have the Spirit of God.

[^1]: Paul now turns to questions raised in a letter from Corinth. "Now concerning" introduces such topics again in 7:25, 8:1, 12:1, 16:1 and 16:12.
[^2]: Many interpreters take these words as a saying quoted from the Corinthians' letter, which Paul then answers; others take them as Paul's own view, which he goes on to qualify. The Greek has no quotation marks. "To touch a woman" was a common euphemism for sexual relations. *Man* is Greek *anthropos*, "human being, man"; *woman* is *gyne*, "woman, wife".
[^3]: Literally "because of the immoralities". Greek *porneiai* (see 5:1).
[^4]: *Have* may mean "be married to" or "have sexual relations with".
[^5]: Literally "the debt", that is, sexual relations within marriage. Many manuscripts read "the goodwill he owes her".
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "to fasting and prayer".
[^7]: "This" may refer to the time apart in verse 5, or to all of verses 2–5.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "For I wish".
[^9]: Greek *charisma* (see 1:7).
[^10]: Greek *agamoi*, a masculine plural. It may mean the unmarried in general, or, beside "widows", widowers.
[^11]: Literally "to burn".
[^12]: Compare Mark 10:9–12.
[^13]: Greek *aphiemi*, "send away, leave"; also in verses 12 and 13.
[^14]: Or "sanctified"; also later in the verse.
[^15]: That is, her husband, who is a believer. Many manuscripts read "through her husband".
[^16]: Literally "enslaved".
[^17]: Some manuscripts read "you".
[^18]: The questions may express doubt, or hope: "Wife, how do you know? Perhaps you will save your husband."
[^19]: Literally "walk", a common biblical way of speaking of how one lives.
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "as God has assigned to him, as the Lord has called each one".
[^21]: Literally "He should not draw it over", a reference to an operation that disguised circumcision (compare 1 Maccabees 1:15).
[^22]: Literally "in the calling in which he was called".
[^23]: Or "Even if you are able to become free, make use of your present condition instead." The Greek does not say what to make use of: the opportunity to become free, or one's condition as a slave.
[^24]: Greek *apeleutheros*, a former slave who had been set free and still owed duties to his former master.
[^25]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10); also in verse 29.
[^26]: Greek *parthenoi*, unmarried people who have not had sexual relations, most often young women; also in verses 28 and 34. See the note on verse 36.
[^27]: Or "the impending distress". Greek *anagke*, "necessity, distress".
[^28]: Literally "Have you been released from a wife?" This may mean never married, or no longer married.
[^29]: Literally "affliction in the flesh".
[^30]: Literally "the appointed time has been compressed". The punctuation is uncertain: "the time remaining is short".
[^31]: Or "as though they were not misusing it".
[^32]: Many manuscripts read "(33) … how to please his wife. (34) There is a difference also between a wife and a virgin. The unmarried woman is concerned about …".
[^33]: Literally "not to throw a noose over you".
[^34]: Literally "to promote what is fitting and constant attendance on the Lord without distraction".
[^35]: The meaning of verses 36–38 is debated. "His virgin" may be the man's fiancée, or his daughter or ward, whom he may give in marriage. *If his passions are strong*: or "if she is past the usual age for marriage"; Greek *hyperakmos* can describe either.
[^36]: Or "he who gives his virgin in marriage"; also later in the verse. Greek *gamizo*, which usually means "give in marriage". Many manuscripts have *ekgamizo*, which has that meaning.
[^37]: Many manuscripts read "bound by law".
[^38]: Literally "falls asleep", a common way of speaking of death.
[^39]: Or "more blessed".
