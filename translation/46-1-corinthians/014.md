**1** Pursue love, and earnestly desire spiritual gifts,[^1] but especially that you may prophesy.[^2]

**2** For one who speaks in a tongue[^3] does not speak to people but to God, for no one understands him;[^4] he speaks mysteries in the Spirit.[^5]

**3** But one who prophesies speaks to people for their building up, encouragement and comfort.

**4** One who speaks in a tongue builds up himself, but one who prophesies builds up the church.

**5** I would like all of you to speak in tongues, but even more to prophesy. One who prophesies is greater than one who speaks in tongues, unless he interprets,[^6] so that the church may be built up.

**6** Now, brothers and sisters,[^7] if I come to you speaking in tongues, what good will I do you unless I speak to you with some revelation or knowledge or prophecy or teaching?

**7** In the same way, lifeless instruments that produce sound, such as the flute or the harp, if they do not give distinct notes, how will anyone know what is being played on the flute or the harp?

**8** And if the trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will get ready for battle?

**9** So it is with you: if with your tongue you do not speak intelligible words, how will anyone know what is being said? You will be speaking into the air.

**10** There are, it may be, so many kinds of languages[^8] in the world, and none is without meaning.

**11** If then I do not know the meaning of the language, I will be a foreigner[^9] to the speaker, and the speaker a foreigner to me.

**12** So it is with you: since you are eager for spirits,[^10] seek to excel in them for the building up of the church.

**13** Therefore one who speaks in a tongue should pray that he may interpret.

**14** For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays,[^11] but my mind is unproductive.[^12]

**15** What then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will also pray with the mind. I will sing praise with the spirit, and I will also sing praise with the mind.

**16** Otherwise, if you give a blessing with the spirit, how can anyone in the position of an outsider[^13] say "Amen"[^14] to your thanksgiving, since he does not know what you are saying?

**17** For you may be giving thanks well enough, but the other person is not built up.

**18** I thank God that I speak in tongues more than all of you.

**19** But in church I would rather speak five words with my mind, in order to instruct others also, than ten thousand words in a tongue.

**20** Brothers and sisters, do not be children in your thinking. Be infants in evil, but in your thinking be mature.

**21** In the Law it is written, "By people of other tongues and by the lips of foreigners I will speak to this people, and even then they will not listen to me, says the Lord."[^15]

**22** So then, tongues are a sign[^16] not for believers but for unbelievers, while prophecy is not for unbelievers but for believers.

**23** So if the whole church comes together in one place and all speak in tongues, and outsiders or unbelievers come in, will they not say that you are out of your minds?

**24** But if all prophesy, and an unbeliever or outsider comes in, he is convicted by all and examined by all,

**25** the secrets of his heart are laid bare, and so, falling on his face, he will worship God and declare, "God is really among you!"[^17]

**26** What then, brothers and sisters? When you come together, each one has a psalm,[^18] a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, an interpretation. Let everything be done for building up.

**27** If anyone speaks in a tongue, let there be two or at most three, each in turn, and let someone interpret.

**28** But if there is no interpreter, let each of them keep silent in the church and speak to himself and to God.

**29** Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said.[^19]

**30** If a revelation comes to someone else who is sitting there, the first should be silent.

**31** For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all may be encouraged

**32** (and the spirits of prophets are subject to prophets,

**33** for God is not a God of disorder but of peace), as in all the churches of God's holy people.[^20]

**34** The women[^21] should keep silent in the churches, for they are not permitted to speak, but should be in submission, as the Law also says.[^22]

**35** If they want to learn something, they should ask their own husbands at home, for it is shameful for a woman to speak in church.

**36** Or did the word of God originate with you? Or are you the only ones it has reached?

**37** If anyone thinks he is a prophet or spiritual, he should acknowledge that what I am writing to you is the Lord's.[^23]

**38** If anyone does not recognize this, he is not recognized.[^24]

**39** So, my brothers and sisters, earnestly desire to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues.

**40** But everything should be done decently and in order.

[^1]: Or "spiritual things". Greek *pneumatika* (see 12:1).
[^2]: Greek *propheteuo*, "speak as a prophet" (see 12:10); so throughout the chapter.
[^3]: Or "in a language". Greek *glossa* (see 12:10); so throughout the chapter.
[^4]: Literally "no one hears".
[^5]: Or "with his spirit" (compare verse 14).
[^6]: Or "unless someone interprets".
[^7]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10); also in verses 20, 26 and 39.
[^8]: Literally "voices" or "sounds", Greek *phonai*, not the word used for "tongues". *None is without meaning*: literally "none is voiceless".
[^9]: Greek *barbaros*, someone whose speech sounds like meaningless noise, a speaker of a foreign language.
[^10]: Literally "zealous for spirits", probably meaning spiritual gifts or manifestations of the Spirit.
[^11]: Or "the Spirit in me prays". So also "with the spirit" in verses 15 and 16.
[^12]: Literally "unfruitful".
[^13]: Greek *idiotes*, an ordinary person without special knowledge or skill; also in verses 23 and 24. Here perhaps someone who is enquiring, or a believer who does not understand tongues.
[^14]: A Hebrew word meaning "it is firm, it is true", said to affirm a prayer.
[^15]: Isaiah 28:11–12, in a form that differs from both the Hebrew and the Septuagint; the words "says the Lord" are not in Isaiah. In Isaiah, the LORD will speak to his people through the foreign speech of invaders, a people who "would not listen". Here "the Law" means the scriptures as a whole.
[^16]: The sense of "sign" here is debated. In the light of verse 21, tongues may be a sign of judgment on those who do not believe; verses 23–25 describe the effect of each gift on unbelievers.
[^17]: Compare Isaiah 45:14 and Zechariah 8:23.
[^18]: Or "a song of praise".
[^19]: Greek *diakrino*, "discern, evaluate" (compare 11:29 and 12:10). "The others" may be the other prophets or the whole church.
[^20]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints". The SBLGNT's punctuation joins "as in all the churches of God's holy people" to what comes before. Many translations begin verse 34 with it: "As in all the churches of the saints, the women should keep silent …"
[^21]: Or "wives". Greek *gynaikes*, "women, wives" (compare "their own husbands", verse 35). Many manuscripts read "Your women". Verses 34–35 are much discussed. In a few manuscripts, mainly of the Western text (the Greek-Latin Codices Claromontanus, Augiensis and Boernerianus, known as D, F and G, the minuscule 88 and some Latin witnesses), they stand after verse 40. The SBLGNT apparatus, which compares printed editions, does not record this, and all the editions it compares print the verses here. Some scholars think the verses began as a note in the margin that was copied into the text at two different places; others hold that Paul wrote them here. Since 11:5 assumes that women pray and prophesy, interpreters who take the verses as Paul's understand the silence in different ways: for example, as a limit on asking questions during worship, on speech that disrupts it, or on weighing the prophecies (verse 29). Some read verses 34–35 as a view held in Corinth, which Paul rejects in verse 36.
[^22]: The passage of the Law is not named. Genesis 3:16 is often suggested.
[^23]: The SBLGNT reads "is the Lord's". Most editions read "is the Lord's command", and many manuscripts "are the Lord's commands".
[^24]: That is, by God. Many manuscripts read "If anyone is ignorant of this, let him remain ignorant."
