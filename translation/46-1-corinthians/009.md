**1** Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?

**2** If I am not an apostle to others, at least I am to you, for you are the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.

**3** This is my defense[^1] to those who would examine me.

**4** Do we not have the right[^2] to eat and drink?

**5** Do we not have the right to take a believing wife[^3] along with us, as do the other apostles and the brothers of the Lord[^4] and Cephas?

**6** Or is it only Barnabas and I who do not have the right to stop working for a living?

**7** Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Who tends a flock and does not drink[^5] the milk of the flock?

**8** Am I saying these things only on human authority?[^6] Does not the Law say the same?

**9** For it is written in the Law of Moses, "Do not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain."[^7] Is it about oxen that God is concerned,

**10** or does he say it entirely for our sake? It was written for our sake, because the one who plows ought to plow in hope, and the one who threshes ought to thresh in hope of a share in the crop.[^8]

**11** If we have sown spiritual things among you, is it too much if we reap material things[^9] from you?

**12** If others share this right over you, do not we even more? But we have not made use of this right. Instead, we put up with anything so that we will not put any obstacle in the way of the good news of Christ.

**13** Don't you know that those who perform the temple service eat food from the temple, and those who serve at the altar share in what is offered on the altar?[^10]

**14** In the same way, the Lord commanded that those who proclaim the good news should get their living from the good news.[^11]

**15** But I have not made use of any of these rights. And I am not writing this so that it may be done for me. For I would rather die than—[^12] No one will make my boast empty!

**16** For if I proclaim the good news, that gives me no reason to boast, for I am under compulsion.[^13] Woe to me if I do not proclaim the good news!

**17** For if I do this willingly, I have a reward. But if not willingly, I have still been entrusted with a stewardship.[^14]

**18** What then is my reward? That in proclaiming the good news I may offer it free of charge, so as not to make full use of my right in the good news.

**19** For though I am free from all, I have made myself a slave to all, so that I may win as many as possible.

**20** To the Jews I became like a Jew, to win Jews. To those under the Law I became like one under the Law, though I myself am not under the Law,[^15] to win those under the Law.

**21** To those without the Law I became like one without the Law, though I am not without God's law but am under the law of Christ,[^16] to win those without the Law.

**22** To the weak I became weak, to win the weak. I have become all things to all people, so that by all means I may save some.

**23** I do it all for the sake of the good news, so that I may share in it with them.[^17]

**24** Don't you know that in a race all the runners run, but only one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may win it.

**25** Everyone who competes in the games exercises self-control in all things. They do it to receive a wreath[^18] that will wither, but we an imperishable one.

**26** So I do not run aimlessly; I do not box like someone beating the air.

**27** No, I treat my body roughly[^19] and make it my slave, so that after proclaiming to others I myself will not be disqualified.

[^1]: Greek *apologia*, a defense in court. *Examine* is *anakrino*, as in 4:3.
[^2]: Greek *exousia*, "right, authority" (see 8:9); also in verses 5, 6, 12 and 18.
[^3]: Literally "a sister, a wife", that is, a wife who is a fellow believer.
[^4]: Compare Mark 6:3.
[^5]: Literally "eat from".
[^6]: Literally "according to a human being".
[^7]: Deuteronomy 25:4.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "and the one who threshes in hope should share in what he hopes for". *Because*: or "that", introducing what was written.
[^9]: Literally "fleshly things", here meaning material support.
[^10]: Literally "share with the altar". Compare Numbers 18:8–19 and Deuteronomy 18:1–5.
[^11]: Compare Luke 10:7.
[^12]: The Greek sentence breaks off here. Many manuscripts read "For I would rather die than have anyone make my boast empty."
[^13]: Literally "necessity is laid on me".
[^14]: Greek *oikonomia*, the task of a household manager (see 4:1).
[^15]: Many manuscripts leave out "though I myself am not under the Law".
[^16]: Or "not outside God's law but within the law of Christ".
[^17]: Literally "so that I may become a fellow sharer of it".
[^18]: A wreath of leaves, given to the winner at the games.
[^19]: Literally "I strike my body under the eye", a boxing term.
