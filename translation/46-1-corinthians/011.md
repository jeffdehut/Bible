**1** Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ.

**2** I praise you because you remember me in everything and hold firmly to the traditions[^1] just as I handed them on to you.

**3** But I want you to understand that the head[^2] of every man is Christ, the head of a woman is the man,[^3] and the head of Christ is God.

**4** Every man who prays or prophesies[^4] with something on his head[^5] dishonors his head.[^6]

**5** But every woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered[^7] dishonors her head, for it is one and the same thing as having her head shaved.

**6** For if a woman will not cover her head, she should also cut off her hair. But if it is shameful for a woman to have her hair cut off or her head shaved, she should cover her head.

**7** For a man ought not to cover his head, since he is the image and glory of God.[^8] But the woman is the glory of the man.

**8** For man did not come from woman, but woman from man.[^9]

**9** Neither was man created for the sake of woman, but woman for the sake of man.[^10]

**10** That is why a woman ought to have authority over her head,[^11] because of the angels.[^12]

**11** Nevertheless, in the Lord woman is not independent of man, nor man of woman.

**12** For just as woman came from man, so man is born through woman. And all things come from God.

**13** Judge for yourselves: is it proper for a woman to pray to God with her head uncovered?

**14** Does not nature itself teach you that if a man has long hair, it is a disgrace to him,

**15** but if a woman has long hair, it is her glory? For long hair has been given as a covering.[^13]

**16** If anyone is inclined to be contentious, we have no such practice,[^14] nor do the churches of God.

**17** But in giving this instruction I do not praise you, because when you come together it does more harm than good.[^15]

**18** For, first of all, when you come together as a church,[^16] I hear that there are divisions among you, and I partly believe it.

**19** For there must indeed be factions among you, so that those who are genuine among you may be recognized.[^17]

**20** When you come together in one place, then, it is not the Lord's Supper that you eat.[^18]

**21** For when the time comes to eat, each of you goes ahead with his own supper,[^19] and one goes hungry while another gets drunk.

**22** What! Don't you have houses to eat and drink in? Or do you despise the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing? What shall I say to you? Shall I praise you? In this I do not praise you.

**23** For I received from the Lord what I also handed on to you:[^20] that the Lord Jesus, on the night when he was handed over,[^21] took bread,

**24** and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said,[^22] "This is my body, which is for you.[^23] Do this in remembrance of me."

**25** In the same way he took the cup also, after supper, saying, "This cup is the new covenant in my blood.[^24] Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of me."

**26** For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.

**27** So then, whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be answerable for the body and the blood of the Lord.[^25]

**28** Let each person test himself, and then eat of the bread and drink of the cup.

**29** For whoever eats and drinks without discerning the body[^26] eats and drinks judgment on himself.[^27]

**30** That is why many of you are weak and sick, and a number have fallen asleep.[^28]

**31** But if we judged ourselves rightly,[^29] we would not be judged.

**32** But when we are judged by the Lord, we are being disciplined, so that we will not be condemned along with the world.

**33** So then, my brothers and sisters,[^30] when you come together to eat, wait for one another.[^31]

**34** If anyone is hungry, he should eat at home, so that when you come together it will not be for judgment. The other matters I will set in order when I come.

[^1]: Greek *paradoseis*, "traditions", teachings handed on. The related verb *paradidomi*, "hand on", is used here and in verse 23.
[^2]: Greek *kephale*, "head"; also later in the verse. Used figuratively, the word has been understood here as "one with authority over", as "source, origin" (compare verses 8 and 12), or as "one who is prominent or first in honor". Scholars debate which sense, or combination of senses, Paul intends. In verses 4–7 the physical head is also in view, so the word may carry both its literal and figurative senses there.
[^3]: Or "the head of a wife is her husband". Greek *aner* and *gyne* mean "man" and "woman", or "husband" and "wife". Both senses have been proposed throughout verses 3–16.
[^4]: Greek *propheteuo*, "speak as a prophet", to speak a message from God (see chapter 14); also in verse 5.
[^5]: Literally "having down from the head", probably a covering hanging from the head. Some interpreters think instead of long hair (compare verse 14).
[^6]: "His head" may mean his own head, or Christ, his "head" in verse 3, or both. So also "her head" in verse 5.
[^7]: Greek *akatakalyptos*, "uncovered"; also in verse 13. Most interpreters think of a garment drawn over the head. Some think of hair let down loose rather than bound up, and take the "covering" of verses 6–7 in the same way.
[^8]: Compare Genesis 1:26–27.
[^9]: Compare Genesis 2:21–23.
[^10]: Compare Genesis 2:18.
[^11]: Or "on her head". Greek *exousia*, "authority, right, power", the word translated "right" in 8:9 and 9:4–18. Interpreters understand it here either as the woman's own authority (her right to decide about her head, or her authority to pray and prophesy), or as a sign of authority worn on the head, that is, a head covering. Many translations supply "a symbol of authority" or "a sign of authority". The Greek has no such words.
[^12]: The meaning is uncertain. Suggestions include angels thought to be present at worship and to watch over its order, and the "sons of God" of Genesis 6:1–4, understood in Jewish tradition as angels drawn by human women.
[^13]: Or "instead of a covering". Greek *anti*. The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "has been given to her".
[^14]: "No such practice" may mean women praying with their heads uncovered, or the habit of being contentious.
[^15]: Literally "you come together not for the better but for the worse".
[^16]: Or "in assembly". Greek *ekklesia*.
[^17]: Possibly said with irony: "so that it may become clear which of you are the approved ones". Greek *dokimoi*, "tested, approved".
[^18]: Or "it is not possible to eat the Lord's Supper".
[^19]: Or "each of you eats his own supper first".
[^20]: "Received" and "handed on" are the usual words for passing on a tradition (as in 15:3). Compare Mark 14:22–24, Matthew 26:26–28 and Luke 22:19–20.
[^21]: Or "betrayed". Greek *paradidomi*, the verb translated "handed on" earlier in the verse.
[^22]: Many manuscripts add "Take, eat".
[^23]: Many manuscripts read "which is broken for you".
[^24]: Compare Jeremiah 31:31 and Exodus 24:8.
[^25]: Literally "will be guilty of the body and the blood of the Lord".
[^26]: "The body" may be the Lord's body in the bread (verse 27), or the church as the body of Christ (10:17; 12:27), or both. Many manuscripts read "whoever eats and drinks in an unworthy manner" and "the body of the Lord".
[^27]: Verses 29–34 play on words formed from *krino*, "judge": *krima*, "judgment" (verses 29 and 34), *diakrino*, "discern" (verses 29 and 31), and *katakrino*, "condemn" (verse 32).
[^28]: That is, have died.
[^29]: Greek *diakrino*, the verb translated "discerning" in verse 29.
[^30]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10).
[^31]: Or "welcome one another".
