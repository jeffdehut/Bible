**1** This is how people should regard us: as servants[^1] of Christ and stewards[^2] of the mysteries of God.

**2** In this case, what is required of stewards is that each one be found faithful.

**3** But to me it is a very small thing that I should be examined[^3] by you or by any human court.[^4] I do not even examine myself.

**4** For I am not aware of anything against myself, but I am not declared righteous[^5] by that. It is the Lord who examines me.

**5** So do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes. He will bring to light what is hidden in darkness and will reveal the purposes of the heart.[^6] Then each one will receive his praise from God.

**6** Brothers and sisters,[^7] I have applied all this to myself and Apollos[^8] for your sake, so that you may learn from us what "Not beyond what is written"[^9] means, so that none of you will be puffed up[^10] in favor of one person against another.

**7** For who makes you different from anyone else?[^11] What do you have that you did not receive? And if you did receive it, why do you boast as if you had not received it?

**8** Already you have all you want! Already you have become rich! Without us you have become kings! And I wish you had become kings, so that we too might reign with you.

**9** For I think God has put us apostles on display at the very end, like people condemned to death,[^12] because we have become a spectacle[^13] to the world, both to angels and to human beings.

**10** We are fools for Christ's sake, but you are wise in Christ. We are weak, but you are strong. You are honored, but we are dishonored.

**11** To this very hour we go hungry and thirsty, we are poorly clothed,[^14] we are beaten, and we have no home.

**12** We work hard, laboring with our own hands. When we are insulted, we bless; when we are persecuted, we endure it;

**13** when we are slandered, we answer kindly.[^15] We have become like the scum of the world, the filth of everyone,[^16] to this day.

**14** I am not writing this to shame you, but to warn you as my dear children.

**15** For even if you have ten thousand guardians[^17] in Christ, you do not have many fathers. For in Christ Jesus I became your father[^18] through the good news.

**16** So I urge you, be imitators of me.

**17** That is why I have sent[^19] Timothy to you. He is my dear and faithful child in the Lord, and he will remind you of my ways in Christ Jesus, as I teach them everywhere in every church.

**18** Some of you have become puffed up, as though I were not coming to you.

**19** But I will come to you soon, if the Lord wills, and I will find out not the talk of these puffed-up people but their power.

**20** For the kingdom of God is not a matter of talk but of power.

**21** What do you want? Shall I come to you with a rod,[^20] or with love and a spirit of gentleness?

[^1]: Greek *hyperetai*, "assistants, attendants", not the word for "servants" in 3:5.
[^2]: Greek *oikonomoi*, household managers, often slaves, entrusted with their master's affairs; also in verse 2.
[^3]: Greek *anakrino*, "examine, judge" (see 2:14); also later in this verse and in verse 4.
[^4]: Literally "by a human day", in contrast with the Day of the Lord (3:13).
[^5]: Greek *dikaioo*, traditionally "justify". Here it may mean "acquitted".
[^6]: Literally "of the hearts".
[^7]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10).
[^8]: Or "I have applied these things in a figure to myself and Apollos". The Greek verb means to change the form of something.
[^9]: The meaning of this saying is uncertain. It may refer to the scriptures, perhaps those quoted in 1:19, 1:31 and 3:19–20, or it may be a slogan or proverb. Many manuscripts read "not to think beyond what is written".
[^10]: Greek *physioo*, "inflate, puff up": to be arrogant; also in verses 18 and 19.
[^11]: Or "Who regards you as superior?" "You" is singular in Greek in this verse.
[^12]: The picture is of condemned prisoners brought out at the end of a show in the arena.
[^13]: Greek *theatron*, "theater, spectacle".
[^14]: Literally "we are naked".
[^15]: Or "we appeal to them", "we speak gently". Greek *parakaleo*.
[^16]: Both Greek words mean what is scraped or swept off as rubbish. They were also used of people expelled or put to death to cleanse a community.
[^17]: Greek *paidagogoi*, slaves who looked after children and took them to school. They were not teachers.
[^18]: Literally "I fathered you".
[^19]: Or "I am sending".
[^20]: Or "a stick", for punishment.
