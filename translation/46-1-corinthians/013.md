**1** If I speak in the tongues[^1] of human beings and of angels, but do not have love,[^2] I have become a noisy gong[^3] or a clanging cymbal.

**2** And if I have prophecy and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to move mountains,[^4] but do not have love, I am nothing.

**3** And if I give away all my possessions to feed others,[^5] and if I hand over my body so that I may be burned,[^6] but do not have love, I gain nothing.

**4** Love is patient, love is kind. Love is not jealous, it does not brag, it is not puffed up,[^7]

**5** it does not behave improperly, it does not seek its own interests, it is not easily angered, it does not keep a record of wrongs,[^8]

**6** it does not rejoice in wrongdoing but rejoices with the truth.

**7** It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.[^9]

**8** Love never fails.[^10] But as for prophecies, they will be brought to an end;[^11] as for tongues, they will cease; as for knowledge, it will be brought to an end.

**9** For we know in part and we prophesy in part,

**10** but when what is complete[^12] comes, what is in part will be brought to an end.

**11** When I was a child, I spoke like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. When I became a man, I put an end to childish ways.

**12** For now we see in a mirror, dimly,[^13] but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I will know fully, even as I have been fully known.

**13** So now faith, hope and love remain, these three; but the greatest[^14] of these is love.

[^1]: Or "languages". Greek *glossai* (see 12:10).
[^2]: Greek *agape*, "love"; throughout the chapter.
[^3]: Literally "sounding bronze".
[^4]: Compare Mark 11:23.
[^5]: Or "If I dole out all my possessions". Greek *psomizo*, "feed by putting bits of food into the mouth".
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "so that I may boast".
[^7]: Greek *physioo* (see 4:6).
[^8]: Literally "it does not reckon the evil".
[^9]: Or "It always bears, always believes, always hopes, always endures." *Bears*: or "covers", "protects".
[^10]: Literally "never falls".
[^11]: Greek *katargeo* (see 1:28); also later in this verse and in verses 10 and 11 ("put an end to").
[^12]: Greek *teleion*, "complete, perfect, mature" (compare 2:6). What it refers to is debated. Most interpreters think of the end of the age, when believers will see "face to face" (verse 12); others think of a mature state of the church, or of the completed scriptures.
[^13]: Literally "through a mirror in a riddle". Mirrors were made of polished metal and gave an imperfect image. Compare Numbers 12:8, where the LORD speaks with Moses "face to face, plainly and not in riddles".
[^14]: Literally "the greater".
