**1** Now I want to remind you,[^1] brothers and sisters,[^2] of the good news that I proclaimed to you, which you also received, in which you also stand,

**2** through which you are also being saved, if you hold firmly to the message I proclaimed to you,[^3] unless you believed in vain.

**3** For I handed on to you as of first importance[^4] what I also received:[^5] that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the scriptures,[^6]

**4** that he was buried, that he was raised[^7] on the third day in accordance with the scriptures,

**5** and that he appeared[^8] to Cephas,[^9] then to the Twelve.

**6** Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers and sisters at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep.[^10]

**7** Then he appeared to James,[^11] then to all the apostles.

**8** Last of all, as to one untimely born,[^12] he appeared to me also.

**9** For I am the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God.

**10** But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace toward me was not in vain. On the contrary, I worked harder than any of them, though it was not I, but the grace of God that was with me.

**11** Whether, then, it was I or they, this is what we proclaim and this is what you believed.

**12** Now if Christ is proclaimed as raised from the dead, how can some of you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?

**13** If there is no resurrection of the dead, then not even Christ has been raised.

**14** And if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation is empty and your faith[^13] is empty too.

**15** We are even found to be false witnesses about God, because we testified about God[^14] that he raised Christ, whom he did not raise if in fact the dead are not raised.

**16** For if the dead are not raised, not even Christ has been raised.

**17** And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.

**18** Then those also who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished.

**19** If only for this life we have hoped in Christ,[^15] we are of all people most to be pitied.

**20** But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits[^16] of those who have fallen asleep.

**21** For since death came through a human being, the resurrection of the dead has also come through a human being.

**22** For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive.

**23** But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming[^17] those who belong to Christ.

**24** Then comes the end, when he hands over the kingdom to God the Father, after he has brought to nothing[^18] every ruler and every authority and power.

**25** For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet.[^19]

**26** The last enemy to be brought to nothing is death.

**27** For "he has put everything under his feet."[^20] But when it says,[^21] "Everything has been put under him," it is clear that this does not include the one who put everything under him.

**28** When everything has been put under him, then the Son himself will be put under[^22] the one who put everything under him, so that God may be all in all.

**29** Otherwise, what will those people do who are baptized on behalf of the dead?[^23] If the dead are not raised at all, why are people baptized on their behalf?

**30** And why are we in danger every hour?

**31** I die every day! That is as sure as my boasting about you,[^24] which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord.

**32** If, from a merely human point of view,[^25] I fought with wild beasts at Ephesus,[^26] what did I gain? If the dead are not raised, "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die."[^27]

**33** Do not be deceived: "Bad company corrupts good character."[^28]

**34** Wake up and be sober, as is right, and stop sinning. For some have no knowledge of God. I say this to your shame.

**35** But someone will ask, "How are the dead raised? With what kind of body do they come?"

**36** You fool! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies.

**37** And what you sow is not the body that is to be, but a bare seed, perhaps of wheat or of something else.

**38** But God gives it a body as he has chosen, and to each kind of seed its own body.

**39** Not all flesh is the same: there is one kind for human beings, another flesh for animals, another flesh for birds, and another for fish.

**40** There are also heavenly bodies and earthly bodies, but the glory[^29] of the heavenly is one kind and the glory of the earthly is another.

**41** There is one glory of the sun, another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars; for star differs from star in glory.

**42** So it is with the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in decay; it is raised imperishable.

**43** It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in glory. It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power.

**44** It is sown a natural body;[^30] it is raised a spiritual body. If there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual body.[^31]

**45** So also it is written, "The first man, Adam, became a living being";[^32] the last Adam became a life-giving spirit.

**46** But it is not the spiritual that comes first, but the natural, and then the spiritual.

**47** The first man was from the earth, made of dust;[^33] the second man is from heaven.[^34]

**48** As was the man of dust, so are those who are of the dust; and as is the man of heaven, so are those who are of heaven.

**49** And just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we will also bear[^35] the image of the man of heaven.

**50** What I mean, brothers and sisters, is this: flesh and blood[^36] cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does decay inherit what is imperishable.

**51** Listen, I tell you a mystery: we will not all fall asleep, but we will all be changed,

**52** in a moment, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed.

**53** For this perishable body must put on the imperishable, and this mortal body must put on immortality.[^37]

**54** When this perishable body puts on the imperishable, and this mortal body puts on immortality,[^38] then the saying that is written will come true: "Death has been swallowed up in victory."[^39]

**55** "Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting?"[^40]

**56** The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the Law.

**57** But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ!

**58** So, my dear brothers and sisters, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in the Lord.

[^1]: Literally "I make known to you".
[^2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10); also in verses 6, 50 and 58.
[^3]: The Greek is compressed; literally "with what word I proclaimed it to you, if you hold it fast".
[^4]: Or "first of all", "at the beginning".
[^5]: "Handed on" and "received" are the usual words for passing on a tradition (see 11:23). Many scholars think that in verses 3–5, and perhaps beyond, Paul is quoting an early summary of the faith that he had been taught, with its repeated "that". Where the quoted words end is debated; the list of appearances in verses 6–8 may be Paul's own addition, at least in part, and verse 6 has his comment "most of whom are still alive".
[^6]: No passage is named, here or in verse 4. Isaiah 53:5–12 is often suggested for Christ's death "for our sins", and Hosea 6:2 for "on the third day".
[^7]: The Greek perfect tense, "has been raised", which stresses that he remains raised. So throughout the chapter.
[^8]: Literally "was seen by"; so also in verses 6–8.
[^9]: Peter (see 1:12). Compare Luke 24:34.
[^10]: That is, have died; also in verses 18, 20 and 51.
[^11]: Probably James the brother of the Lord (Galatians 1:19; compare Mark 6:3).
[^12]: Greek *ektroma*, "a miscarriage", a child born dead or before its time. It may have been a term of abuse used against Paul, or a picture of his sudden and abnormal "birth" as an apostle.
[^13]: Some manuscripts read "our faith".
[^14]: Or "against God".
[^15]: Or "If in this life we have only hoped in Christ".
[^16]: The first part of the harvest, offered to God (Leviticus 23:10), a pledge of the rest to come; also in verse 23.
[^17]: Greek *parousia*, "presence, arrival", a word also used of the visit of a ruler.
[^18]: Greek *katargeo* (see 1:28); also in verse 26. *Ruler … authority … power*: these may be spiritual powers (compare 2:6).
[^19]: Compare Psalm 110:1.
[^20]: Psalm 8:7 (8:6 in most English Bibles).
[^21]: Or "when he says", meaning God.
[^22]: Or "will subject himself to". Greek *hypotasso*, the verb translated "put under" throughout verses 27–28. Other editions read "then the Son himself also".
[^23]: Baptism "on behalf of the dead" is not mentioned anywhere else in the New Testament or in other early Christian writings, and its meaning is unknown. Many explanations have been proposed: for example, that some believers were baptized in place of people who had died without being baptized; that people were baptized for the sake of believers who had died, hoping to be reunited with them; or that the phrase refers to being baptized in view of one's own death. Greek *hyper*, "for, on behalf of, for the sake of".
[^24]: Literally "by your boasting", an oath formula: Paul swears by his pride in them. Other editions add "brothers and sisters".
[^25]: Literally "according to a human being". It may mean "with merely human hopes", or "speaking in human terms".
[^26]: This may be a figure of speech for fierce opponents, or refer to a real danger in the arena.
[^27]: Isaiah 22:13.
[^28]: A line from the Greek comic poet Menander (4th century BC), in his play *Thais*, which had become a proverb.
[^29]: Greek *doxa*, "glory, splendor, brightness"; also in verse 41.
[^30]: Greek *psychikos* (see 2:14), from *psyche*, the word translated "being" in verse 45; also in verse 46.
[^31]: Many manuscripts read "There is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body."
[^32]: Genesis 2:7, with "first" and "Adam" added. *Living being*: Greek *psyche zosa*, translating the Hebrew *nephesh chayyah*.
[^33]: Compare Genesis 2:7, "from the dust of the ground".
[^34]: Many manuscripts read "the second man is the Lord from heaven".
[^35]: The SBLGNT here follows NA28; other editions read "let us also bear". The two Greek forms differ by one letter.
[^36]: That is, human beings in their present, mortal state.
[^37]: Literally "this perishable", "this mortal"; also in verse 54.
[^38]: Some manuscripts leave out "this perishable body puts on the imperishable, and".
[^39]: Isaiah 25:8. The Hebrew reads "He will swallow up death forever"; the word translated "forever", *lanetsach*, can also mean "in victory". Paul's wording differs from the Septuagint and agrees with the later Greek translation of Theodotion.
[^40]: Hosea 13:14, adapted from the Septuagint, which has "Where is your penalty, O death? Where is your sting, O Hades?" The Hebrew reads "Where, O death, are your plagues? Where, O Sheol, is your destruction?" Many manuscripts read "Where, O death, is your sting? Where, O Hades, is your victory?" *Hades*: the realm of the dead, like Sheol.
