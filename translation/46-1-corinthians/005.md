**1** It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality[^1] among you, and of a kind that is not found even among the Gentiles:[^2] a man has his father's wife.[^3]

**2** And you are puffed up![^4] Shouldn't you rather have mourned, so that the man who did this would be removed from among you?

**3** For I, though absent in body, am present in spirit, and as if I were present I have already passed judgment on the one who has done such a thing,

**4** in the name of our Lord Jesus.[^5] When you are assembled, and my spirit is with you, with the power of our Lord Jesus,

**5** you are to hand this man over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh,[^6] so that the spirit[^7] may be saved on the day of the Lord.

**6** Your boasting is not good. Don't you know that a little yeast leavens the whole batch of dough?

**7** Clean out the old yeast so that you may be a new batch, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover lamb,[^8] has been sacrificed.[^9]

**8** So let us celebrate the festival, not with the old yeast, the yeast of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.

**9** I wrote to you in my letter[^10] not to associate with sexually immoral people.

**10** I did not mean the sexually immoral people of this world at all, or the greedy and swindlers, or idolaters, since then you would have to leave the world.

**11** But now I am writing to you[^11] not to associate with anyone who bears the name of brother or sister[^12] if he is sexually immoral or greedy, or an idolater, a slanderer, a drunkard or a swindler. Do not even eat with such a person.

**12** For what business is it of mine to judge those outside? Is it not those inside whom you are to judge?

**13** God judges[^13] those outside. "Purge the evil person from among you."[^14]

[^1]: Greek *porneia*, sexual relations outside marriage, including prostitution and forbidden unions; also later in this verse. In verses 9–11, "sexually immoral people" is *pornoi*.
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "of a kind that is not even named among the Gentiles".
[^3]: That is, he is living with her as his wife. "His father's wife" probably means his stepmother. Compare Leviticus 18:8.
[^4]: Greek *physioo* (see 4:6). The sentence can also be read as a question: "And are you puffed up?"
[^5]: The phrases in verses 3–5 can be connected in several ways. "In the name of our Lord Jesus" may go with "passed judgment", with "assembled", or with "hand this man over" in verse 5.
[^6]: Greek *sarx*. This may mean the man's body, through suffering or death, or "the flesh" in Paul's moral sense, human nature in its opposition to God (see 3:1).
[^7]: Probably the man's spirit. Some take it as the spirit of the community.
[^8]: Greek *pascha*, "Passover", here the lamb sacrificed at Passover (Exodus 12:21). Yeast had to be removed from every house during the festival that followed (Exodus 12:15, 19).
[^9]: Many manuscripts add "for us".
[^10]: Literally "in the letter", probably an earlier letter that has not survived.
[^11]: Or "But in fact, I wrote to you".
[^12]: Greek *adelphos*, "brother", meaning any fellow believer.
[^13]: Other editions accent the verb as a future: "God will judge".
[^14]: Deuteronomy 17:7, and several times in Deuteronomy (for example 13:6, 13:5 in most English Bibles), where this translation has "purge the evil from among you". The Greek, like the Hebrew, can mean "the evil" or "the evil person".
