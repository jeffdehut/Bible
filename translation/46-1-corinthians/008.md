**1** Now concerning food sacrificed to idols:[^1] we know that "we all have knowledge."[^2] Knowledge puffs up,[^3] but love builds up.

**2** If anyone thinks he knows something, he does not yet know as he ought to know.

**3** But if anyone loves God, that person is known by him.

**4** So then, concerning the eating of food sacrificed to idols, we know that "an idol is nothing in the world,"[^4] and that "there is no God but one."

**5** For even if there are so-called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, as indeed there are many gods and many lords,

**6** yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom all things come and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things come and through whom we exist.[^5]

**7** But not everyone has this knowledge. Some, through being accustomed until now to the idol,[^6] eat the food as food sacrificed to an idol, and their conscience,[^7] being weak, is defiled.

**8** Food will not bring us closer to God.[^8] We are no better off if we eat, and no worse off if we do not eat.

**9** But take care that this right[^9] of yours does not become a cause of stumbling to the weak.

**10** For if anyone sees you, who have knowledge, reclining at table in an idol's temple, will not his conscience, since he is weak, be encouraged[^10] to eat food sacrificed to idols?

**11** So by your knowledge the weak person is destroyed, the brother or sister[^11] for whom Christ died.

**12** When you sin against your brothers and sisters[^12] in this way and wound their conscience when it is weak, you sin against Christ.

**13** Therefore, if food causes my brother or sister to stumble, I will never eat meat again, so that I will not cause my brother or sister to stumble.

[^1]: Greek *eidolothyta*, meat or other food that had been offered in sacrifice to an idol. It might be eaten at a meal in the temple (verse 10) or sold in the market (10:25).
[^2]: Most interpreters take "We all have knowledge" as a saying used in Corinth, which Paul quotes; so also "An idol is nothing in the world" and "There is no God but one" in verse 4. The Greek has no quotation marks.
[^3]: Greek *physioo* (see 4:6).
[^4]: Or "there is no idol in the world".
[^5]: The wording recalls Deuteronomy 6:4, the *Shema*: "The LORD our God, the LORD is one". The Septuagint there has *kyrios*, "Lord", for the name YHWH.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "with awareness of the idol until now", using *syneidesis*, the word translated "conscience" later in the verse.
[^7]: Greek *syneidesis*, "conscience, awareness"; also in verses 10 and 12.
[^8]: Literally "will not present us to God".
[^9]: Greek *exousia*, "right, authority, freedom". The same word is used of Paul's own rights in chapter 9.
[^10]: Literally "be built up", the word used in verse 1.
[^11]: Greek *adelphos*, "brother", meaning any fellow believer; also twice in verse 13.
[^12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike (see 1:10).
