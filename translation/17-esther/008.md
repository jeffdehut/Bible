**1** On that day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews. And Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told him what Mordecai was to her.

**2** The king took off his signet ring, which he had taken back from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. And Esther put Mordecai in charge of the house of Haman.

**3** Then Esther spoke again to the king. She fell at his feet and wept, and pleaded with him to avert the evil of Haman the Agagite and the plot that he had devised against the Jews.

**4** The king held out the gold scepter to Esther, and Esther got up and stood before the king.

**5** She said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favor with him, and if the thing seems right to the king, and I am pleasing in his eyes, let an order be written to revoke the letters devised by Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite,[^1] which he wrote to destroy the Jews who are in all the king's provinces.

**6** For how can I bear to see the disaster that is coming on my people? And how can I bear to see the destruction of my kindred?"

**7** King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew, "Look, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and they have hanged him on the pole[^2] because he reached out his hand against the Jews.

**8** Now you write as you see fit concerning the Jews, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's signet ring. For a document that is written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked."

**9** So the king's scribes were summoned at that time, on the twenty-third day of the third month, which is the month of Sivan.[^3] An order was written, just as Mordecai commanded, to the Jews, and to the satraps,[^4] the governors and the officials of the provinces from India to Cush, 127 provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and their own language.

**10** Mordecai wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed it with the king's signet ring, and he sent letters by couriers on horseback, riding swift horses from the royal service, bred from the royal stud.[^5]

**11** By these letters the king allowed the Jews in every city to gather and defend their lives, to destroy, kill and annihilate any armed force of any people or province that might attack them, with their children and women,[^6] and to plunder their goods,

**12** on one day throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar.

**13** A copy of the document was to be issued as law in every province and made known to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready on that day to take revenge on their enemies.

**14** The couriers, riding the swift horses from the royal service, went out in haste, urged on by the king's command. And the law was issued in the citadel of Susa.

**15** Mordecai went out from the king's presence in royal robes of violet and white, with a great gold crown and a cloak of fine linen and purple. And the city of Susa shouted for joy and rejoiced.

**16** For the Jews there was light and gladness, joy and honor.

**17** In every province and in every city, wherever the king's command and his law reached, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, a banquet and a holiday.[^7] And many of the peoples of the land became Jews,[^8] for fear of the Jews had fallen on them.

[^1]: Literally "the letters, the plot of Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite".
[^2]: Hebrew *ets*; see 2:23.
[^3]: Around May–June, two months after Haman's decree (3:12).
[^4]: Hebrew *achashdarpenim*: the satraps, who governed the large regions of the Persian Empire; see 3:12.
[^5]: The meaning of several of these Hebrew words is uncertain. *Achashteranim*, here "from the royal service", is a word borrowed from Persian. Also in verse 14.
[^6]: The Hebrew is ambiguous. The children and women may be those of the attackers, as in Haman's decree (3:13), or those of the Jews who are attacked.
[^7]: Literally "a good day".
[^8]: Or "professed to be Jews". Hebrew *mityahadim* is found only here.
