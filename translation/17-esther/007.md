**1** So the king and Haman went in to drink with Queen Esther.

**2** On the second day too, as they were drinking wine, the king said to Esther, "What is your petition, Queen Esther? It will be granted to you. And what is your request? Even if it is half the kingdom, it will be done."

**3** Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favor in your eyes, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given to me as my petition, and my people as my request.

**4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, killed and annihilated. If we had only been sold as male and female slaves, I would have kept silent, for that trouble would not be worth troubling the king."[^1]

**5** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to do this?"[^2]

**6** Esther said, "An adversary and an enemy: this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

**7** The king got up in his rage from the drinking of wine and went into the palace garden, but Haman stayed behind to beg Queen Esther for his life, for he saw that the king had determined to do him harm.

**8** When the king returned from the palace garden to the place where they had been drinking wine, Haman was falling on the couch where Esther was. The king said, "Will he even assault[^3] the queen while I am in the house?" As soon as the words left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**9** Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs[^4] in attendance on the king, said, "Look, the pole that Haman made for Mordecai, whose word saved the king,[^5] is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high."[^6] The king said, "Hang him on it!"

**10** So they hanged Haman on the pole that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's rage died down.

[^1]: The meaning of the last words is uncertain. Perhaps "for the distress would not have justified troubling the king", or "but the enemy could not make up for the king's loss".
[^2]: Literally "whose heart has filled him to do so".
[^3]: Hebrew *kavash*, "subdue, force".
[^4]: Hebrew *sarisim*; see 1:10.
[^5]: Literally "who spoke good concerning the king" (2:21–23).
[^6]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so fifty cubits was about 75 feet (23 meters).
