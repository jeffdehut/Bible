**1** After these things, when the anger of King Ahasuerus had died down, he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her.

**2** Then the king's young men who attended him said, "Let beautiful young virgins be sought for the king.

**3** Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom to gather every beautiful young virgin to the citadel of Susa, to the house of the women, into the care of Hegai, the king's eunuch,[^1] who is in charge of the women, and let their beauty treatments be given to them.

**4** And let the young woman who pleases the king become queen instead of Vashti." This pleased the king, and he did so.

**5** Now there was a Jewish man in the citadel of Susa whose name was Mordecai[^2] son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite.[^3]

**6** He[^4] had been taken into exile from Jerusalem with the exiles who were carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken into exile.[^5]

**7** He was bringing up Hadassah,[^6] that is, Esther,[^7] the daughter of his uncle, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was lovely in form and beautiful to look at, and when her father and her mother died, Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

**8** When the king's command and his law had been proclaimed, and many young women had been gathered to the citadel of Susa into the care of Hegai, Esther also was taken to the king's palace, into the care of Hegai, who was in charge of the women.

**9** The young woman pleased him and won his favor.[^8] He quickly provided her with her beauty treatments and her portions of food, and with seven young women chosen from the king's palace, and he moved her and her young women to the best place in the house of the women.

**10** Esther had not made known her people or her family background, because Mordecai had commanded her not to make it known.

**11** Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the court of the house of the women, to learn how Esther was[^9] and what was being done with her.

**12** Each young woman's turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus after she had been treated for twelve months according to the regulations for the women. For this was how the days of their beauty treatment were completed: six months with oil of myrrh, and six months with spices and the cosmetics for women.

**13** Then the young woman would go in to the king like this: whatever she asked for was given to her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's palace.

**14** In the evening she would go in, and in the morning she would return to the second house of the women,[^10] into the care of Shaashgaz, the king's eunuch, who was in charge of the concubines. She would not go in to the king again unless the king desired her and she was summoned by name.

**15** When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her as his own daughter, to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, who was in charge of the women, advised. And Esther won the favor of all who saw her.

**16** Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, to his royal palace, in the tenth month, which is the month of Tebeth,[^11] in the seventh year of his reign.[^12]

**17** The king loved Esther more than all the other women, and she won his grace and favor more than all the virgins. So he set the royal crown on her head and made her queen instead of Vashti.

**18** Then the king gave a great banquet for all his officials and his servants, Esther's banquet. He granted a holiday[^13] to the provinces and gave gifts with royal generosity.[^14]

**19** When the virgins were gathered a second time,[^15] Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate.[^16]

**20** Esther had still not made known her family background or her people, as Mordecai had commanded her, for Esther obeyed Mordecai's word, just as she had when she was being brought up by him.

**21** In those days, while Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold, became angry and plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus.

**22** The plot became known to Mordecai, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther told the king in Mordecai's name.

**23** When the matter was investigated and found to be true, both men were hanged on a pole.[^17] And it was recorded in the book of the chronicles[^18] in the king's presence.

[^1]: Hebrew *saris*; see 1:10. Also in verses 14–15 and 21.
[^2]: The name is related to that of Marduk, the chief god of Babylon.
[^3]: Literally "a man of Yemini". Saul's father was also Kish, a Benjaminite (1 Samuel 9:1), and Shimei a Benjaminite of Saul's family (2 Samuel 16:5).
[^4]: "He" may refer to Mordecai or to Kish, his great-grandfather.
[^5]: In 597 BC (2 Kings 24:10–16). Jeconiah is also called Jehoiachin.
[^6]: *Hadassah* means "myrtle".
[^7]: *Esther* may come from the Persian word for "star", or from the name of the Babylonian goddess Ishtar.
[^8]: Hebrew *chesed*, here "kindness, favor". Also in verse 17.
[^9]: Literally "to know the peace of Esther".
[^10]: Or "she would return to the house of the women a second time".
[^11]: Around December–January.
[^12]: 479 BC.
[^13]: Or "a remission of taxes".
[^14]: Literally "according to the hand of the king".
[^15]: The meaning is uncertain: perhaps a second gathering of young women after Esther became queen.
[^16]: The gate was where the king's officials did their business. Sitting there may mean that Mordecai held an office (compare 3:2–3).
[^17]: Hebrew *ets*, "tree, wood": a pole or stake. Traditionally "gallows". The Persians executed people by impaling them (compare Ezra 6:11).
[^18]: Literally "the book of the events of the days", the royal records (compare 6:1; 10:2).
