**1** This happened in the days of Ahasuerus,[^1] the Ahasuerus who reigned over 127 provinces,[^2] from India to Cush.[^3]

**2** In those days, when King Ahasuerus sat on his royal throne in the citadel of Susa,[^4]

**3** in the third year of his reign[^5] he gave a banquet for all his officials and his servants. The army[^6] of Persia and Media, the nobles[^7] and the officials of the provinces were in his presence,

**4** while he displayed the riches of his royal glory and the splendor and pomp of his greatness for many days, 180 days.

**5** When these days were over, the king gave a banquet lasting seven days for all the people who were present in the citadel of Susa, from the greatest to the least, in the court of the garden of the king's palace.[^8]

**6** There were hangings of white cotton and violet,[^9] fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and marble pillars. There were couches of gold and silver on a pavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl and precious stones.[^10]

**7** Drinks were served in gold vessels, each vessel different from the others, and there was royal wine in abundance, as befitted the king's generosity.[^11]

**8** The drinking was according to the law:[^12] no one was compelled. For the king had instructed all the officials of his household to do as each man wished.

**9** Queen Vashti also gave a banquet for the women in the royal palace that belonged to King Ahasuerus.

**10** On the seventh day, when the king's heart was merry with wine, he told Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha and Abagtha, Zethar and Carkas, the seven eunuchs[^13] who served King Ahasuerus,

**11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king wearing the royal crown, to show the peoples and the officials her beauty, for she was beautiful to look at.

**12** But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's command, which the eunuchs brought. The king became very angry, and his rage burned within him.

**13** Then the king spoke to the wise men who understood the times,[^14] for this was the king's practice with all who knew law and justice.[^15]

**14** Those closest to him were Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media who had access to the king[^16] and held the first place in the kingdom.

**15** He asked,[^17] "According to the law, what is to be done to Queen Vashti, because she has not obeyed the command of King Ahasuerus, which the eunuchs brought?"

**16** Memucan said in the presence of the king and the officials, "Queen Vashti has done wrong not only to the king, but also to all the officials and all the peoples in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**17** For what the queen has done will become known to all the women, and they will look on their husbands with contempt. They will say, 'King Ahasuerus commanded Queen Vashti to be brought before him, but she did not come.'

**18** This very day the noble ladies of Persia and Media who have heard what the queen has done will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be no end of contempt and anger.

**19** If it pleases the king, let a royal order go out from him, and let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media, so that it cannot be revoked,[^18] that Vashti is never again to come before King Ahasuerus. And let the king give her royal position to another woman who is better than she.

**20** When the king's edict[^19] that he makes is heard throughout his whole kingdom, vast as it is, all the women will give honor to their husbands, from the greatest to the least."

**21** The advice pleased the king and the officials, and the king did as Memucan proposed.

**22** He sent letters to all the king's provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, that every man should be master in his own house and should speak the language of his own people.[^20]

[^1]: Hebrew *Achashverosh*: Xerxes I, king of Persia from 486 to 465 BC.
[^2]: Hebrew *medinot*, provinces or districts: smaller divisions of the empire than the satrapies (3:12).
[^3]: Hebrew *Hoddu* and *Kush*: the Indus valley in the east and the land south of Egypt (ancient Nubia) in the west.
[^4]: Hebrew *Shushan ha-birah*. Susa, in what is now southwest Iran, was one of the royal cities of the Persian kings. The citadel was the fortified royal quarter of the city.
[^5]: 483 BC.
[^6]: Or "the powerful men".
[^7]: Hebrew *partemim*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^8]: Hebrew *bitan*, a word borrowed from Persian for a palace or pavilion.
[^9]: Hebrew *tekhelet*, a violet-blue.
[^10]: The precise meaning of several of these Hebrew words for fabrics and stones is uncertain.
[^11]: Literally "according to the hand of the king".
[^12]: Hebrew *dat*, "law, decree", a word borrowed from Persian. It is the usual word for law in Esther.
[^13]: Hebrew *sarisim*, court officials. In Esther they serve the king and keep the women of the palace (2:3, 14–15), so the word probably has its narrower sense, "eunuchs".
[^14]: Perhaps those skilled in reading the signs of the times, such as astrologers.
[^15]: Literally "for so was the king's word before all who knew law and judgment".
[^16]: Literally "who saw the king's face". Compare Ezra 7:14.
[^17]: "He asked" is not in the Hebrew; the king's question follows directly from verse 13.
[^18]: Literally "so that it will not pass away". Compare 8:8; Daniel 6:9, 13, 16 (6:8, 12, 15 in most English Bibles).
[^19]: Hebrew *pitgam*, a word borrowed from Persian.
[^20]: The meaning of the last words is uncertain. Literally "and speaking according to the language of his people".
