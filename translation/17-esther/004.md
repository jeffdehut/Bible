**1** When Mordecai learned all that had been done, he tore his clothes and put on sackcloth and ashes. He went out into the middle of the city and cried out with a loud and bitter cry.

**2** He went only as far as the front of the king's gate, for no one was allowed to enter the king's gate wearing sackcloth.

**3** In every province, wherever the king's command and his law reached, there was great mourning among the Jews, with fasting, weeping and lamenting, and many lay in sackcloth and ashes.[^1]

**4** When Esther's young women and her eunuchs[^2] came and told her, the queen was deeply distressed. She sent clothes for Mordecai to put on, so that he would take off his sackcloth, but he would not accept them.

**5** Then Esther called Hathach, one of the king's eunuchs, whom he had appointed to attend her, and ordered him to go to Mordecai to learn what this was and why it was happening.

**6** So Hathach went out to Mordecai in the city square in front of the king's gate.

**7** Mordecai told him all that had happened to him, and the exact amount of money that Haman had promised to pay into the king's treasuries for the destruction of the Jews.

**8** He also gave him a copy of the written law that had been issued in Susa for their destruction, to show Esther and explain to her, and to order her to go to the king to plead with him and make a request before him for her people.

**9** Hathach went and told Esther what Mordecai had said.

**10** Then Esther spoke to Hathach and ordered him to say to Mordecai,

**11** "All the king's servants and the people of the king's provinces know that for any man or woman who goes to the king in the inner court without being summoned, there is one law: to be put to death, unless the king holds out the gold scepter to him, so that he may live. And I have not been summoned to come to the king for these thirty days."

**12** When Esther's words were reported to Mordecai,

**13** Mordecai told them to reply to Esther, "Do not imagine that because you are in the king's palace you alone of all the Jews will escape.

**14** For if you keep silent at this time, relief and deliverance will come to the Jews from another place,[^3] but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether it was not for a time like this that you have come to the kingdom?"[^4]

**15** Then Esther told them to reply to Mordecai,

**16** "Go, gather all the Jews who are found in Susa, and fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day. My young women and I will also fast in the same way. Then I will go in to the king, though it is against the law. And if I perish, I perish."

**17** So Mordecai went away and did everything that Esther had commanded him.

[^1]: Literally "sackcloth and ashes were spread out for many".
[^2]: Hebrew *sarisim*; see 1:10. Also in verse 5.
[^3]: The Hebrew book of Esther never mentions God, and this translation does not add him. "Another place" is not explained: some take it as a veiled reference to God, others as help from some other quarter. The Greek version of Esther in the Septuagint adds long passages, including prayers, that speak of God.
[^4]: Or "to royal rank".
