**1** King Ahasuerus imposed forced labor[^1] on the land and on the coastlands of the sea.

**2** All his powerful and mighty acts, and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai, whom the king made great, are they not written in the book of the chronicles[^2] of the kings of Media and Persia?

**3** For Mordecai the Jew was second only to King Ahasuerus. He was great among the Jews and well liked by his many kindred, for he sought the good of his people and spoke for the welfare[^3] of all his descendants.[^4]

[^1]: Hebrew *mas*, forced labor or tribute.
[^2]: Literally "the book of the events of the days"; see 2:23.
[^3]: Hebrew *shalom*, "peace, well-being".
[^4]: Literally "all his seed", perhaps meaning all his people. The Septuagint continues the book with further passages not found in the Hebrew.
