**1** In the twelfth month, which is the month of Adar, on the thirteenth day of the month, when the king's command and his law were about to be carried out, on the very day when the enemies of the Jews had hoped to gain power over them, it was turned the other way:[^1] the Jews gained power over those who hated them.

**2** The Jews gathered in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hands on those who sought to harm them. No one could stand against them, for fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.

**3** All the officials of the provinces, the satraps,[^2] the governors and those who carried out the king's business supported the Jews, for fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.

**4** For Mordecai was great in the king's palace, and his fame was spreading through all the provinces, as the man Mordecai grew more and more powerful.

**5** The Jews struck down all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them, and they did as they pleased to those who hated them.

**6** In the citadel of Susa the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men.

**7** They also killed Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,

**8** Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,

**9** Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha,

**10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews.[^3] But they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**11** That same day the number of those killed in the citadel of Susa was reported to the king.

**12** The king said to Queen Esther, "In the citadel of Susa the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your petition? It will be granted to you. And what further request do you have? It will be done."

**13** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews who are in Susa be allowed tomorrow also to act according to today's law, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the pole."[^4]

**14** So the king commanded that this be done. A law was issued in Susa, and the ten sons of Haman were hanged.

**15** The Jews who were in Susa gathered again on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar, and they killed three hundred men in Susa. But they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**16** The rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces also gathered to defend their lives, and they gained rest from their enemies. They killed seventy-five thousand[^5] of those who hated them, but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**17** This was on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on the fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and joy.

**18** But the Jews who were in Susa gathered on the thirteenth day and on the fourteenth day, and on the fifteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and joy.

**19** That is why the Jews of the countryside, who live in the unwalled towns, keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of joy and feasting, a holiday,[^6] and a day for sending portions of food to one another.

**20** Mordecai recorded these things, and he sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, near and far,

**21** to require them to keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day every year,

**22** as the days on which the Jews gained rest from their enemies, and as the month that had been turned for them from sorrow into joy and from mourning into a holiday. They were to make them days of feasting and joy, of sending portions of food to one another and gifts to the poor.

**23** So the Jews accepted as a custom what they had begun to do, and what Mordecai had written to them.

**24** For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them. He had cast pur, that is, the lot, to throw them into confusion[^7] and destroy them.

**25** But when it came[^8] before the king, he commanded by letter that the evil plot that Haman had devised against the Jews should come back on his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the pole.

**26** That is why these days are called Purim,[^9] after the word pur. So, because of everything written in this letter, and because of what they had seen concerning this and what had happened to them,

**27** the Jews established it and took it on themselves,[^10] and on their descendants and on all who would join them, that without fail they would keep these two days every year, as it was written and at the appointed time,

**28** and that these days would be remembered and kept in every generation, in every family, every province and every city, and that these days of Purim would never cease among the Jews, nor would the memory of them come to an end among their descendants.

**29** Then Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter about Purim.[^11]

**30** Letters were sent to all the Jews, to the 127 provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, with words of peace and truth,

**31** to establish these days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had established for them, and as they had established for themselves and for their descendants, concerning their fasts and their lamenting.

**32** Esther's command confirmed these regulations about Purim, and it was recorded in writing.[^12]

[^1]: Literally "it was overturned".
[^2]: Hebrew *achashdarpenim*: the satraps, who governed the large regions of the Persian Empire; see 3:12.
[^3]: Literally "the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, they killed". In Hebrew the verb comes at the end of the list.
[^4]: They had already been killed (verses 7–10), so their bodies were to be displayed. Hebrew *ets*, "tree, wood"; see 2:23.
[^5]: The Septuagint reads "fifteen thousand".
[^6]: Literally "a good day". Also in verse 22.
[^7]: Hebrew *le-hummam*, "to throw them into confusion" or "to crush them", sounds like the name Haman.
[^8]: Or "when she came", that is, Esther.
[^9]: *Purim* is the plural of *pur*, "lot" (3:7).
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "and he took it on himself" but read "and they took it on themselves".
[^11]: Or "with all authority to confirm this second letter about Purim". The first letter is the one Mordecai sent in verses 20–22.
[^12]: Literally "it was written in the book".
