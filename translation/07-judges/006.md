**1** The Israelites did what was evil in the sight of the LORD,[^1] and the LORD handed them over to Midian for seven years.

**2** Midian's power was too strong for Israel.[^2] Because of Midian the Israelites made for themselves the hiding places[^3] in the mountains, the caves and the strongholds.

**3** Whenever Israel had sown their crops, Midian, Amalek and the people of the east[^4] would come up and attack them.

**4** They would camp against them and destroy the produce of the land all the way to Gaza. They left nothing to live on in Israel, no sheep, no ox and no donkey.

**5** For they would come up with their livestock and their tents, coming like swarms of locusts. They and their camels were too many to count, and they came into the land to ruin it.

**6** Israel was brought very low because of Midian, and the Israelites cried out to the LORD.

**7** When the Israelites cried out to the LORD because of Midian,

**8** the LORD sent a prophet to the Israelites. He said to them, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: 'I brought you up from Egypt and brought you out of the place of slavery.[^5]

**9** I rescued you from the power of Egypt and from the power of all who oppressed you. I drove them out before you and gave you their land.

**10** I said to you, "I am the LORD your God. Do not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you are living." But you have not obeyed me.'"[^6]

**11** Then the angel of the LORD[^7] came and sat under the oak at Ophrah that belonged to Joash the Abiezrite. His son Gideon was threshing wheat in the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.[^8]

**12** The angel of the LORD appeared to him and said to him, "The LORD is with you, mighty warrior."

**13** Gideon said to him, "Pardon me, my lord,[^9] but if the LORD is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders that our ancestors told us about, when they said, 'Didn't the LORD bring us up from Egypt?' But now the LORD has abandoned us and handed us over to Midian."[^10]

**14** The LORD turned to him[^11] and said, "Go in this strength of yours, and save Israel from the power of Midian. Am I not sending you?"

**15** He said to him, "Pardon me, Lord,[^12] but how can I save Israel? My clan[^13] is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family."

**16** The LORD said to him, "I will be with you,[^14] and you will strike down the Midianites as if they were one man."

**17** He said to him, "If I have found favor with you, give me a sign that it is you who are speaking with me.

**18** Please do not leave here until I come back to you and bring out my offering and set it before you." He said, "I will stay until you come back."

**19** So Gideon went in and prepared a young goat, and unleavened bread from an ephah[^15] of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, and he brought them out to him under the oak and presented them.

**20** The angel of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened bread, put them on this rock, and pour out the broth." And he did so.

**21** Then the angel of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened bread. Fire flared up from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened bread, and the angel of the LORD vanished from his sight.[^16]

**22** When Gideon saw that it was the angel of the LORD, Gideon said, "Oh no, Lord GOD! For I have seen the angel of the LORD face to face!"[^17]

**23** But the LORD said to him, "Peace be with you. Do not be afraid. You will not die."

**24** So Gideon built an altar there to the LORD and named it The LORD Is Peace.[^18] To this day it still stands at Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

**25** That same night the LORD said to him, "Take your father's young bull, the second bull, seven years old.[^19] Tear down the altar of Baal that belongs to your father, and cut down the Asherah pole[^20] beside it.

**26** Then build an altar to the LORD your God on the top of this stronghold, with the stones laid in proper order.[^21] Take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering, using the wood of the Asherah pole that you cut down."

**27** So Gideon took ten men from among his servants and did as the LORD had told him. But he was too afraid of his family and of the men of the town to do it by day, so he did it at night.

**28** When the men of the town got up early in the morning, there was the altar of Baal torn down, and the Asherah pole beside it cut down, and the second bull offered on the altar that had been built.

**29** They said to one another, "Who has done this?" They asked around and searched, and they were told, "Gideon son of Joash has done this."

**30** The men of the town said to Joash, "Bring out your son. He must die, because he has torn down the altar of Baal and cut down the Asherah pole beside it."

**31** But Joash said to all who stood against him, "Are you going to plead Baal's case? Are you going to save him? Whoever pleads his case will be put to death by morning. If he is a god, let him plead his own case, since someone has torn down his altar."

**32** So that day Gideon was named Jerubbaal,[^22] for people said, "Let Baal plead his case against him, because he has torn down his altar."

**33** Then all Midian, Amalek and the people of the east joined forces. They crossed over and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.

**34** But the Spirit of the LORD took possession of Gideon,[^23] and he blew the ram's horn,[^24] and the Abiezrites were called out to follow him.

**35** He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, and they too were called out to follow him. He also sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, and they went up to meet them.

**36** Then Gideon said to God, "If you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said,

**37** look, I am putting a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew on the fleece alone, and all the ground is dry, then I will know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said."

**38** And so it was. He got up early the next day and squeezed the fleece, and he wrung dew out of the fleece, a bowlful of water.

**39** Then Gideon said to God, "Do not be angry with me, but let me speak just once more. Please let me make just one more test with the fleece. Let the fleece alone be dry, and let there be dew on all the ground."

**40** God did so that night. Only the fleece was dry, and there was dew on all the ground.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "The hand of Midian prevailed over Israel."
[^3]: Hebrew *minharot*, a word found only here, perhaps "hollows" or "dens".
[^4]: Literally "the sons of the east", the desert peoples living east of the Jordan. Also in verse 33.
[^5]: Literally "out of the house of slaves".
[^6]: Literally "you have not listened to my voice".
[^7]: Or "the messenger of the LORD". Hebrew *mal'akh* means messenger. In this story the angel is also called "the LORD" (verses 14, 16 and 23).
[^8]: Grain was usually threshed on an open threshing floor. A winepress was a pit cut in the rock, where it could be hidden.
[^9]: The Hebrew vowel points read *adoni*, "my lord", a polite form of address to a man.
[^10]: Literally "into the palm of Midian"; also in verse 14.
[^11]: The Septuagint reads "The angel of the LORD turned to him".
[^12]: Here the Hebrew vowel points read *Adonai*, "Lord", the form used of God.
[^13]: Literally "my thousand", a clan within the tribe.
[^14]: The same words God spoke to Moses in Exodus 3:12.
[^15]: An ephah was probably about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel).
[^16]: Literally "went from his eyes".
[^17]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord YHWH". After "Lord", God's personal name is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles. Gideon fears death, because no one could see God and live (Exodus 33:20; compare Judges 13:22).
[^18]: Hebrew *YHWH shalom*.
[^19]: Literally "Take the young bull of the ox that belongs to your father, and the second bull, seven years old." It is not clear whether one bull is meant or two.
[^20]: Hebrew *asherah*: a wooden pole or tree that was a symbol of the goddess Asherah. Also in verses 26, 28 and 30.
[^21]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain. Literally "in the arrangement", or perhaps "in the battle line".
[^22]: *Jerubbaal* means "Let Baal plead his case", or "Let Baal contend".
[^23]: Literally "clothed itself with Gideon".
[^24]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn.
