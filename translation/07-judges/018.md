**1** In those days there was no king in Israel. And in those days the tribe of the Danites was looking for territory of its own to live in, because until then no inheritance had fallen to it among the tribes of Israel.

**2** So the Danites sent five men from their whole clan, capable men from Zorah and Eshtaol, to spy out the land and explore it. They said to them, "Go and explore the land." The men came to the hill country of Ephraim, to Micah's house, and spent the night there.

**3** When they were near Micah's house, they recognized the voice[^1] of the young Levite. So they turned aside there and said to him, "Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place? What is your business here?"

**4** He said to them, "Micah has done this and that for me. He has hired me, and I have become his priest."

**5** They said to him, "Please ask God, so that we may know whether the journey we are on will succeed."

**6** The priest said to them, "Go in peace. The journey you are on is under the eye of the LORD."[^2]

**7** So the five men went on and came to Laish. They saw that the people there were living in security, in the manner of the Sidonians, quiet and unsuspecting. There was no one in the land to do them any harm or to rule over them by force.[^3] They were far from the Sidonians and had no dealings with anyone.

**8** When they came back to their kinsmen at Zorah and Eshtaol, their kinsmen said to them, "What news?"[^4]

**9** They said, "Come on, let us go up against them! We have seen the land, and it is very good. Are you going to sit still? Don't hesitate to go and enter the land and take possession of it.

**10** When you go, you will come to an unsuspecting people and a spacious land.[^5] God has handed it over to you, a place where nothing on earth is lacking."

**11** So six hundred men of the clan of the Danites set out from there, from Zorah and Eshtaol, armed with weapons of war.

**12** They went up and camped at Kiriath-jearim in Judah. That is why that place is called Mahaneh-dan[^6] to this day. It is west of Kiriath-jearim.[^7]

**13** From there they passed on into the hill country of Ephraim and came to Micah's house.

**14** Then the five men who had gone to spy out the land of Laish said to their kinsmen, "Do you know that in these houses there are an ephod,[^8] household gods,[^9] a carved image and a cast idol?[^10] Now think about what you should do."

**15** So they turned aside there and came to the house of the young Levite, at Micah's house, and greeted him.[^11]

**16** The six hundred Danite men, armed with their weapons of war, stood at the entrance of the gate.

**17** The five men who had gone to spy out the land went up and went in there. They took the carved image, the ephod, the household gods and the cast idol, while the priest stood at the entrance of the gate with the six hundred men armed with weapons of war.

**18** When these men went into Micah's house and took the carved image,[^12] the ephod, the household gods and the cast idol, the priest said to them, "What are you doing?"

**19** They said to him, "Be quiet! Put your hand over your mouth and come with us, and be a father and a priest to us. Is it better for you to be priest to one man's household, or to be priest to a tribe and a clan in Israel?"

**20** The priest was glad.[^13] He took the ephod, the household gods and the carved image, and went along in the middle of the people.

**21** They turned and went on their way, putting the little children, the livestock and their goods in front of them.

**22** When they were some distance from Micah's house, the men who lived in the houses near Micah's house were called out, and they caught up with the Danites.

**23** They shouted at the Danites, who turned around and said to Micah, "What is the matter with you, that you have called out your men?"

**24** He said, "You have taken my gods that I made, and the priest, and gone away. What do I have left? How can you say to me, 'What is the matter with you?'"

**25** The Danites said to him, "Don't let your voice be heard among us, or some hot-tempered men[^14] will attack you, and you will lose your life and the lives of your household."

**26** Then the Danites went on their way. When Micah saw that they were too strong for him, he turned and went back home.

**27** They took what Micah had made, and the priest who had belonged to him, and they came to Laish, to a people quiet and unsuspecting. They put them to the sword[^15] and burned the city.

**28** There was no one to rescue them, because it was far from Sidon, and they had no dealings with anyone. It was in the valley that belongs to Beth-rehob. The Danites rebuilt the city and settled in it.

**29** They named the city Dan, after their ancestor Dan, who was born to Israel. But the city was formerly called Laish.

**30** The Danites set up the carved image for themselves. Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Manasseh,[^16] and his sons were priests for the tribe of the Danites until the time the land went into exile.

**31** They kept for themselves the carved image that Micah had made, all the time that the house of God was at Shiloh.

[^1]: Perhaps his accent.
[^2]: Literally "Your way in which you go is before the LORD." *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: The Hebrew of this sentence is difficult. Literally "and there was no one putting to shame in anything in the land, possessing restraint".
[^4]: Literally "What are you?"
[^5]: Literally "a land broad of hands", that is, wide on both sides.
[^6]: "The camp of Dan". Compare 13:25.
[^7]: Literally "behind Kiriath-jearim".
[^8]: See 8:27.
[^9]: Hebrew *teraphim*, small images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19). Also in verses 17, 18 and 20.
[^10]: See 17:3.
[^11]: Literally "asked him about his welfare".
[^12]: Literally "the carved image of the ephod".
[^13]: Literally "The heart of the priest was good."
[^14]: Literally "men bitter of soul".
[^15]: Literally "struck them by the mouth of the sword".
[^16]: In the Hebrew text the letter *n* (nun) of *Menasheh*, "Manasseh", is written raised above the line, a scribal mark. Without it the word reads *Mosheh*, "Moses". Gershom was the son of Moses (Exodus 2:22), and the raised letter is generally thought to have been added to keep Moses' name apart from this shrine. The Vulgate reads "Moses".
