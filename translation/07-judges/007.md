**1** Early in the morning Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, and all the people with him camped beside the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was to the north of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.

**2** Yahweh[^1] said to Gideon, "You have too many people with you for me to hand Midian over to them. Israel might boast against me, 'My own hand has saved me.'

**3** Now announce to the people,[^2] 'Whoever is afraid and trembling may turn back and leave Mount Gilead.'"[^3] So twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand remained.

**4** Yahweh said to Gideon, "There are still too many people. Take them down to the water, and I will sift them for you there. If I say to you, 'This one is to go with you,' he is to go with you. But anyone of whom I say to you, 'This one is not to go with you,' is not to go."

**5** So he took the people down to the water, and Yahweh said to Gideon, "Everyone who laps the water with his tongue, as a dog laps, you are to set apart by himself, and also everyone who kneels down to drink."[^4]

**6** The number of those who lapped, putting their hands to their mouths,[^5] was three hundred men. All the rest of the people knelt down to drink water.

**7** Yahweh said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men who lapped I will save you, and I will hand Midian over to you. Let all the rest of the people go home."[^6]

**8** So the people took provisions in their hands, and their rams' horns.[^7] Gideon sent all the other Israelites away, each to his own tent, but he kept the three hundred men. The camp of Midian lay below him in the valley.

**9** That night Yahweh said to him, "Get up and go down against the camp, for I have handed it over to you.

**10** But if you are afraid to go down, go down to the camp with Purah your servant.

**11** Listen to what they are saying, and afterward you will have the courage[^8] to go down against the camp." So he went down with Purah his servant to the outposts of the armed men[^9] in the camp.

**12** Midian, Amalek and all the people of the east[^10] lay spread out in the valley, as thick as locusts. Their camels were too many to count, as many as the sand on the seashore.

**13** When Gideon arrived, there was a man telling his friend a dream. He said, "Listen, I had a dream. A round loaf[^11] of barley bread came tumbling into the camp of Midian. It came to the tent and struck it, and it fell. It turned it upside down, and the tent collapsed."

**14** His friend answered, "This can be nothing other than the sword of Gideon son of Joash, the Israelite. God has handed Midian and the whole camp over to him."

**15** When Gideon heard the account of the dream and its interpretation, he bowed down in worship. He went back to the camp of Israel and said, "Get up, for Yahweh has handed the camp of Midian over to you."

**16** He divided the three hundred men into three companies, and he put rams' horns and empty jars into the hands of all of them, with torches inside the jars.

**17** He said to them, "Watch me and do the same. When I come to the edge of the camp, do just as I do.

**18** When I and all who are with me blow the ram's horn, then you too blow your rams' horns all around the camp, and shout, 'For Yahweh and for Gideon!'"

**19** Gideon and the hundred men who were with him came to the edge of the camp at about ten at night,[^12] just after the guards had been posted. They blew the rams' horns and smashed the jars that were in their hands.

**20** All three companies blew the rams' horns and broke the jars. They held the torches in their left hands and in their right hands the rams' horns to blow, and they shouted, "A sword for Yahweh and for Gideon!"

**21** Each man stood in his place around the camp, and the whole camp ran. They cried out and fled.[^13]

**22** When the three hundred blew the rams' horns, Yahweh set every man's sword against his comrade throughout the whole camp. The army fled as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, as far as the edge of Abel-meholah, near Tabbath.

**23** The men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, from Asher and from all Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.

**24** Gideon sent messengers throughout the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Come down against Midian, and seize the waters ahead of them as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan too." So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they seized the waters as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.

**25** They captured the two commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb.[^14] They killed Oreb at the Rock of Oreb, and they killed Zeeb at the Winepress of Zeeb. They pursued Midian, and they brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon on the other side of the Jordan.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "call in the ears of the people".
[^3]: The verb translated "leave" is uncertain; it may mean "hurry away". Mount Gilead is unexpected here, since Gideon was camped west of the Jordan.
[^4]: Literally "and everyone who kneels on his knees to drink", that is, set them apart in a second group.
[^5]: Literally "those who lapped with their hand to their mouth".
[^6]: Literally "each to his place".
[^7]: Or "So the three hundred took the people's provisions and their rams' horns." Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn.
[^8]: Literally "your hands will be strengthened".
[^9]: Hebrew *chamushim*, "armed" or "in battle order" (Exodus 13:18).
[^10]: Literally "the sons of the east", the desert peoples living east of the Jordan.
[^11]: Hebrew *tselil*, a rare word, probably a round flat loaf.
[^12]: Literally "at the beginning of the middle watch". The night was divided into three watches.
[^13]: The Hebrew text is written "and they put to flight" but read "and they fled".
[^14]: *Oreb* means "raven" and *Zeeb* means "wolf".
