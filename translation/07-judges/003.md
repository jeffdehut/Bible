**1** These are the nations that the LORD[^1] left in the land to test Israel by them, all those Israelites who had not known any of the wars of Canaan.

**2** It was only so that the generations of the Israelites would know war, to teach war to those who had not known it before.

**3** They were the five rulers[^2] of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived in the mountains of Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon to Lebo-hamath.[^3]

**4** They were there to test Israel by them, to find out whether they would obey the commands of the LORD, which he had given their ancestors through Moses.

**5** So the Israelites lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

**6** They took their daughters as wives for themselves and gave their own daughters to their sons, and they served their gods.

**7** The Israelites did what was evil in the sight of the LORD. They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asherahs.[^4]

**8** The LORD's anger burned against Israel, and he sold them into the power of Cushan-rishathaim[^5] king of Aram-naharaim.[^6] The Israelites served Cushan-rishathaim for eight years.

**9** Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for the Israelites, who saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.[^7]

**10** The Spirit of the LORD[^8] came on him, and he judged Israel.[^9] He went out to war, and the LORD handed Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram over to him, and he overpowered Cushan-rishathaim.

**11** Then the land had peace[^10] for forty years, and Othniel son of Kenaz died.

**12** Once again the Israelites did what was evil in the sight of the LORD, and the LORD made Eglon king of Moab strong against Israel, because they had done what was evil in the sight of the LORD.

**13** Eglon brought the Ammonites and the Amalekites together with him. He went and defeated Israel, and they took possession of the City of Palms.[^11]

**14** The Israelites served Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.

**15** Then the Israelites cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for them: Ehud son of Gera, a Benjaminite, a man who was left-handed.[^12] The Israelites sent tribute[^13] by him to Eglon king of Moab.

**16** Ehud made himself a sword with two edges, a *gomed*[^14] long, and he strapped it on his right thigh under his clothes.

**17** He presented the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Now Eglon was a very fat man.

**18** When Ehud had finished presenting the tribute, he sent away the people who had carried it.

**19** But he himself turned back at the carved images[^15] near Gilgal and said, "I have a secret message[^16] for you, Your Majesty." The king said, "Silence!" And all his attendants went out from his presence.

**20** Ehud came to him as he was sitting alone in his cool upper room.[^17] Ehud said, "I have a message from God for you." And the king rose from his seat.

**21** Ehud reached out with his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh, and plunged it into his belly.

**22** Even the hilt went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, because he did not pull the sword out of his belly. And the dung came out.[^18]

**23** Then Ehud went out into the porch.[^19] He shut the doors of the upper room behind him and locked them.

**24** After he had gone, the king's servants came. They saw that the doors of the upper room were locked, and they said, "He must be relieving himself[^20] in the inner room of the cool chamber."

**25** They waited until they were embarrassed,[^21] but still he did not open the doors of the upper room. So they took the key and opened them, and there was their master, fallen to the floor, dead.

**26** Ehud had escaped while they waited. He passed the carved images and escaped to Seirah.

**27** When he arrived, he blew the ram's horn[^22] in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went down with him from the hill country, with Ehud at their head.

**28** He said to them, "Follow me,[^23] for the LORD has handed your enemies, the Moabites, over to you." So they went down after him and seized the fords of the Jordan leading to Moab, and they did not let anyone cross.

**29** At that time they struck down about ten thousand Moabites, all of them sturdy[^24] and strong men. Not one escaped.

**30** So that day Moab was brought down under the power of Israel, and the land had peace for eighty years.

**31** After him came Shamgar son of Anath.[^25] He struck down six hundred Philistines with an ox goad,[^26] and he too saved Israel.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *seranim*, a title used only for the rulers of the five Philistine cities (Joshua 13:3).
[^3]: Or "to the entrance of Hamath".
[^4]: Hebrew *asherot*, plural of *Asherah*, a Canaanite goddess. The same word is used for the wooden poles that were her symbols (Deuteronomy 16:21).
[^5]: *Rishathaim* means "double wickedness", perhaps a mocking name.
[^6]: "Aram of the two rivers", in northern Mesopotamia.
[^7]: The Hebrew can also mean that Kenaz was Caleb's younger brother. See 1:13.
[^8]: Or "the spirit of the LORD". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^9]: Or "he led Israel", "he governed Israel". The verb *shaphat* is the root of *shofet*, "judge" (2:16).
[^10]: Literally "was quiet"; also in verse 30.
[^11]: Probably Jericho (1:16; Deuteronomy 34:3).
[^12]: Literally "restricted in his right hand". *Benjaminite* means "son of the right hand", an irony the story may intend.
[^13]: Hebrew *minchah*, "gift, offering", here the payment a subject people owed its overlord. Also in verses 17 and 18.
[^14]: A unit of length found only here. It was probably somewhat shorter than a cubit (about 18 inches, 45 cm).
[^15]: Hebrew *pesilim*, usually "idols". Here perhaps carved stones that marked a place. Also in verse 26.
[^16]: Literally "a secret word". Hebrew *davar* means both "word" and "thing"; also in verse 20.
[^17]: Literally "in the upper room of coolness", a roof chamber built to catch the breeze.
[^18]: Hebrew *parshedonah*, a word found only here. It is often taken as "the dung" or "the excrement". Others read "it came out at the back", or "he went out through the vestibule".
[^19]: Hebrew *misderonah*, another word found only here. Perhaps "colonnade", "vestibule", or the opening of a toilet.
[^20]: Literally "covering his feet", a polite way of speaking.
[^21]: Or "until they were at a loss".
[^22]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn.
[^23]: Literally "Pursue after me".
[^24]: Literally "fat", here meaning strong, well-fed men.
[^25]: *Anath* was also the name of a Canaanite goddess of war.
[^26]: A long pointed stick used to drive oxen.
