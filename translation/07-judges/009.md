**1** Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, to his mother's brothers, and he said to them and to the whole clan of his mother's family,[^1]

**2** "Please ask all the leaders of Shechem,[^2] 'Which is better for you: for seventy men, all the sons of Jerubbaal, to rule over you, or for one man to rule over you?' Remember that I am your own flesh and blood."[^3]

**3** His mother's brothers spoke all these words on his behalf to all the leaders of Shechem, and their hearts leaned toward Abimelech, for they said, "He is our brother."

**4** They gave him seventy pieces of silver[^4] from the temple of Baal-berith,[^5] and with it Abimelech hired worthless and reckless men, who followed him.

**5** He went to his father's house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, the sons of Jerubbaal, seventy men, on one stone. But Jotham, the youngest son of Jerubbaal, was left alive, because he hid.

**6** Then all the leaders of Shechem and all Beth-millo[^6] gathered together. They went and made Abimelech king beside the oak of the standing stone[^7] at Shechem.

**7** When Jotham was told about it, he went and stood on the top of Mount Gerizim and called out in a loud voice.[^8] He said to them, "Listen to me, leaders of Shechem, so that God may listen to you.

**8** Once the trees went out to anoint a king over themselves. They said to the olive tree, 'Reign over us.'

**9** But the olive tree said to them, 'Should I give up my rich oil, by which gods and people are honored,[^9] and go to sway over the trees?'

**10** Then the trees said to the fig tree, 'You come and reign over us.'

**11** But the fig tree said to them, 'Should I give up my sweetness and my good fruit, and go to sway over the trees?'

**12** Then the trees said to the vine, 'You come and reign over us.'

**13** But the vine said to them, 'Should I give up my wine, which cheers gods and people, and go to sway over the trees?'

**14** Then all the trees said to the thornbush,[^10] 'You come and reign over us.'

**15** The thornbush said to the trees, 'If you really mean to anoint me king over you, come and take shelter in my shade. But if not, let fire come out of the thornbush and consume the cedars of Lebanon.'

**16** Now then, did you act in good faith and with integrity when you made Abimelech king? Have you dealt well with Jerubbaal and his family, and done to him as his deeds deserved?[^11]

**17** My father fought for you. He risked his life[^12] and rescued you from the power of Midian.

**18** But today you have risen up against my father's family. You have killed his sons, seventy men, on one stone, and you have made Abimelech, the son of his slave woman, king over the leaders of Shechem, because he is your brother.

**19** So if you have acted in good faith and with integrity toward Jerubbaal and his family today, then may you find joy in Abimelech, and may he find joy in you.

**20** But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and consume the leaders of Shechem and Beth-millo, and let fire come out from the leaders of Shechem and from Beth-millo and consume Abimelech."

**21** Then Jotham ran away and escaped. He went to Beer and lived there, out of the reach of his brother Abimelech.

**22** Abimelech ruled over Israel for three years.

**23** Then God sent an evil spirit[^13] between Abimelech and the leaders of Shechem, and the leaders of Shechem betrayed Abimelech.

**24** This happened so that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal would come back, and their blood would be laid on their brother Abimelech, who killed them, and on the leaders of Shechem, who helped him[^14] kill his brothers.

**25** The leaders of Shechem set men to lie in wait against him on the mountaintops, and they robbed everyone who passed by them on the road. And Abimelech was told about it.

**26** Gaal son of Ebed came with his brothers, and they moved into Shechem, and the leaders of Shechem put their trust in him.

**27** They went out into the fields, gathered the grapes from their vineyards and trod them, and held a festival of praise.[^15] They went into the temple of their god, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.

**28** Gaal son of Ebed said, "Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem,[^16] that we should serve him? Isn't he the son of Jerubbaal, and isn't Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor, the father of Shechem![^17] Why should we serve him?

**29** If only this people were under my command![^18] Then I would get rid of Abimelech." And he said to Abimelech,[^19] "Muster your army and come out!"

**30** When Zebul, the governor of the city, heard what Gaal son of Ebed had said, he was furious.

**31** He sent messengers to Abimelech secretly,[^20] saying, "Gaal son of Ebed and his brothers have come to Shechem, and they are stirring up the city against you.

**32** Now get up during the night, you and the troops with you, and lie in wait in the fields.

**33** In the morning, as soon as the sun rises, get up early and rush against the city. When he and the people with him come out against you, do to him whatever you are able."[^21]

**34** So Abimelech and all the troops with him got up during the night and lay in wait against Shechem in four companies.

**35** Gaal son of Ebed went out and stood at the entrance of the city gate, and Abimelech and the troops with him rose from their hiding place.

**36** When Gaal saw the troops, he said to Zebul, "Look, people are coming down from the mountaintops!" Zebul said to him, "You are seeing the shadows of the mountains as if they were men."

**37** Gaal spoke again and said, "Look, people are coming down from the center of the land,[^22] and one company is coming by way of the Diviners' Oak."[^23]

**38** Zebul said to him, "Where is your big talk now,[^24] you who said, 'Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?' Aren't these the people you despised? Go out now and fight them!"

**39** So Gaal went out at the head of the leaders of Shechem[^25] and fought against Abimelech.

**40** Abimelech chased him, and he fled before him. Many fell wounded, all the way to the entrance of the gate.

**41** Abimelech stayed at Arumah, and Zebul drove out Gaal and his brothers, so that they could not stay in Shechem.

**42** The next day the people went out into the fields, and Abimelech was told about it.

**43** He took his troops, divided them into three companies, and lay in wait in the fields. When he saw the people coming out of the city, he rose against them and struck them down.

**44** Abimelech and the company[^26] with him rushed forward and took their stand at the entrance of the city gate, while the two other companies rushed on all who were in the fields and struck them down.

**45** Abimelech fought against the city all that day. He captured the city and killed the people in it. Then he tore down the city and sowed it with salt.[^27]

**46** When all the leaders of the Tower of Shechem heard of it, they went into the stronghold[^28] of the temple of El-berith.[^29]

**47** Abimelech was told that all the leaders of the Tower of Shechem had gathered together.

**48** So Abimelech went up Mount Zalmon, he and all the troops with him. Abimelech took an axe[^30] in his hand and cut a branch from the trees. He lifted it up and put it on his shoulder, and he said to the troops with him, "What you have seen me do, hurry and do the same."

**49** So all the troops each cut down a branch and followed Abimelech. They piled them against the stronghold and set the stronghold on fire over the people inside. So all the people of the Tower of Shechem also died, about a thousand men and women.

**50** Then Abimelech went to Thebez. He camped against Thebez and captured it.

**51** But there was a strong tower inside the city, and all the men and women, all the leaders of the city, fled there. They shut themselves in and went up on the roof of the tower.

**52** Abimelech came to the tower and attacked it. He came up to the entrance of the tower to set it on fire.

**53** But a woman threw an upper millstone[^31] down on Abimelech's head and cracked his skull.

**54** He quickly called the young man who carried his weapons and said to him, "Draw your sword and kill me, so that people will not say of me, 'A woman killed him.'" So his young man ran him through, and he died.

**55** When the men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, they all went home.[^32]

**56** So God paid back Abimelech for the evil he had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers.

**57** God also brought all the evil of the men of Shechem back on their own heads, and the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.

[^1]: Literally "the whole clan of the house of his mother's father".
[^2]: Literally "Please speak in the ears of all the lords of Shechem". Hebrew *ba'alei Shekhem*, "the lords of Shechem", were its leading citizens or landowners. Also in the rest of the chapter.
[^3]: Literally "your bone and your flesh".
[^4]: Literally "seventy of silver", probably seventy shekels, about 28 ounces (800 grams).
[^5]: "Baal of the covenant". See 8:33.
[^6]: Literally "the house of Millo". *Millo* means "filling", probably an earth-filled terrace or fortress. Also in verse 20.
[^7]: Or "the oak of the pillar". Compare Joshua 24:26.
[^8]: Literally "lifted up his voice and called".
[^9]: Or "by which God and people are honored". Also in verse 13.
[^10]: Hebrew *atad*, a thorny shrub. Also in verse 15.
[^11]: Literally "And now, if you have acted in truth and integrity and made Abimelech king, and if you have done good with Jerubbaal and with his house, and if you have done to him as the deeds of his hands deserved …". The sentence is interrupted in verses 17–18 and taken up again in verse 19.
[^12]: Literally "he threw his life out in front".
[^13]: Or "a spirit of ill will". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath".
[^14]: Literally "who strengthened his hands".
[^15]: Hebrew *hillulim*, a festival of praise at the harvest (Leviticus 19:24).
[^16]: The meaning is uncertain. Perhaps "who is Shechem's ruler".
[^17]: Hamor was the founder of Shechem in Genesis 33:19; 34:2.
[^18]: Literally "Who will give this people into my hand?"
[^19]: The Septuagint reads "And I would say to Abimelech".
[^20]: Or "at Tormah". The Hebrew word *betormah* is uncertain. It may be a place name, perhaps the Arumah of verse 41.
[^21]: Literally "as your hand finds".
[^22]: Literally "the navel of the land", perhaps the name of a high place near Shechem.
[^23]: Hebrew *elon me'onenim*, "the oak of those who practice divination".
[^24]: Literally "Where is your mouth now".
[^25]: Or "in full view of the leaders of Shechem".
[^26]: Literally "the companies".
[^27]: Probably a symbolic act, marking the place as cursed and barren.
[^28]: Hebrew *tseriach*, a rare word, perhaps a vault or an inner chamber. Also in verse 49.
[^29]: "God of the covenant", probably the same god as Baal-berith (verse 4).
[^30]: Literally "the axes".
[^31]: The upper, movable stone of a hand mill, small enough to be carried.
[^32]: Literally "went each to his place".
