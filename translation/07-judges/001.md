**1** After the death of Joshua, the Israelites asked Yahweh,[^1] "Which of us is to go up first against the Canaanites, to fight against them?"

**2** Yahweh said, "Judah is to go up. I have handed the land over to him."[^2]

**3** Judah said to his brother Simeon, "Come up with me into my allotted territory,[^3] and let us fight against the Canaanites. Then I too will go with you into your allotted territory." So Simeon went with him.

**4** Judah went up, and Yahweh handed the Canaanites and the Perizzites over to them. They defeated ten thousand men at Bezek.

**5** At Bezek they found Adoni-bezek[^4] and fought against him, and they defeated the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

**6** Adoni-bezek fled, but they chased him, caught him, and cut off his thumbs and his big toes.[^5]

**7** Adoni-bezek said, "Seventy kings with their thumbs and big toes cut off used to pick up scraps under my table. As I have done, so God has paid me back." They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

**8** The people of Judah[^6] fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They put it to the sword[^7] and set the city on fire.

**9** Afterward the people of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites who lived in the hill country, in the Negev[^8] and in the western foothills.[^9]

**10** Judah went against the Canaanites who lived in Hebron (Hebron was formerly called Kiriath-arba),[^10] and they defeated Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai.[^11]

**11** From there Judah went against the people living in Debir. (Debir was formerly called Kiriath-sepher.)[^12]

**12** Caleb said, "Whoever attacks Kiriath-sepher and captures it, I will give him my daughter Achsah as his wife."

**13** Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother,[^13] captured it, and Caleb gave him his daughter Achsah as his wife.

**14** When she came to him, she urged him[^14] to ask her father for a field. She got down from her donkey,[^15] and Caleb said to her, "What do you want?"

**15** She said to him, "Give me a blessing.[^16] Since you have given me land in the Negev,[^17] give me springs of water too." So Caleb gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.[^18]

**16** The descendants of the Kenite,[^19] Moses' father-in-law, went up with the people of Judah from the City of Palms[^20] into the wilderness of Judah, which is in the Negev near Arad. They went and settled among the people.[^21]

**17** Judah went with his brother Simeon, and they defeated the Canaanites who lived in Zephath. They devoted the city to destruction,[^22] and so it was named Hormah.[^23]

**18** Judah also captured Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, and Ekron and its territory.[^24]

**19** Yahweh was with Judah, and Judah took possession of the hill country. But they could not drive out the people living in the plain,[^25] because they had iron chariots.[^26]

**20** Hebron was given to Caleb, as Moses had promised,[^27] and he drove out from there the three sons of Anak.[^28]

**21** But the Benjaminites did not drive out the Jebusites who lived in Jerusalem. So the Jebusites have lived with the Benjaminites in Jerusalem to this day.

**22** The house of Joseph[^29] also went up against Bethel, and Yahweh was with them.

**23** The house of Joseph sent men to spy out Bethel. (The city was formerly called Luz.)

**24** The men keeping watch saw a man coming out of the city, and they said to him, "Please show us the way into the city, and we will treat you kindly."[^30]

**25** He showed them the way into the city, and they put the city to the sword, but they let the man and his whole family go.

**26** The man went to the land of the Hittites and built a city. He named it Luz, and that is its name to this day.

**27** But Manasseh did not drive out Beth-shean and its villages,[^31] or Taanach and its villages, or the people living in Dor[^32] and its villages, or the people living in Ibleam and its villages, or the people living in Megiddo and its villages. The Canaanites were determined to stay in that land.

**28** When Israel grew strong, they put the Canaanites to forced labor,[^33] but they did not drive them out completely.

**29** Ephraim did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer, so the Canaanites lived among them in Gezer.

**30** Zebulun did not drive out the people living in Kitron or the people living in Nahalol. So the Canaanites lived among them, and they became forced laborers.

**31** Asher did not drive out the people living in Acco or the people living in Sidon, or Ahlab, Achzib, Helbah, Aphik or Rehob.

**32** So the Asherites lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land, because they did not drive them out.

**33** Naphtali did not drive out the people living in Beth-shemesh or the people living in Beth-anath. So they lived among the Canaanites, the people of the land, but the people of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became forced laborers for them.

**34** The Amorites pressed the Danites back into the hill country. They did not let them come down into the plain.

**35** The Amorites were determined to stay in Har-heres,[^34] in Aijalon and in Shaalbim. But the house of Joseph gained the upper hand,[^35] and they became forced laborers.

**36** The border of the Amorites ran from the Ascent of Akrabbim,[^36] from Sela,[^37] and upward.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. To "ask Yahweh" was to seek a ruling from him, probably by the sacred lot (Numbers 27:21; 1 Samuel 14:37–42).
[^2]: Literally "Look, I have given the land into his hand." Judah is spoken of as one person, the tribe's ancestor; also in verse 3.
[^3]: Literally "into my lot": the territory assigned to each tribe by lot (Joshua 15; 19:1–9).
[^4]: *Adoni-bezek* means "lord of Bezek".
[^5]: Literally "the thumbs of his hands and of his feet"; also in verse 7.
[^6]: Literally "the sons of Judah"; also in verse 9.
[^7]: Literally "struck it by the mouth of the sword"; also in verse 25.
[^8]: The dry country in the south of the land. Also in verses 15 and 16.
[^9]: Hebrew *shephelah*: the low hills between the central hill country and the coastal plain.
[^10]: *Kiriath-arba* means "town of Arba" or "town of four".
[^11]: The descendants of Anak, a people of great size (Numbers 13:22; Joshua 15:14).
[^12]: *Kiriath-sepher* means "town of the book" or "town of the scribe".
[^13]: The Hebrew can also mean that Kenaz, not Othniel, was Caleb's younger brother.
[^14]: That is, Othniel. The Septuagint reads "he urged her". Compare Joshua 15:18.
[^15]: The Hebrew verb is rare and its meaning uncertain. Traditionally "she alighted from her donkey".
[^16]: That is, a gift.
[^17]: Or "Since you have sent me off to the land of the Negev". Literally "For you have given me the land of the Negev".
[^18]: Hebrew *gullot*, perhaps "pools" or "basins". It may also be a place name: "Upper Gulloth and Lower Gulloth".
[^19]: The Kenites were a clan who lived in the south of the land. Moses' father-in-law is called a Kenite here and in 4:11.
[^20]: Probably Jericho (Deuteronomy 34:3).
[^21]: The people are not identified: the people of Judah, or the people of the region.
[^22]: Hebrew *charam*: to give something over completely to God, which in war meant destroying it and keeping nothing for oneself.
[^23]: *Hormah* sounds like *charam*, "devote to destruction". Compare Numbers 21:3.
[^24]: The Septuagint reads "Judah did not take Gaza …", in line with verse 19 and 3:3.
[^25]: Literally "for not to drive out", a shortened expression. Hebrew *emeq*, "valley", here the lower, flatter land. Also in verse 34.
[^26]: Probably chariots fitted with iron, not made entirely of iron.
[^27]: Numbers 14:24; Joshua 14:9–14.
[^28]: Or "the three Anakite clans". See verse 10.
[^29]: The tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh, descended from Joseph. Also in verses 23 and 35.
[^30]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal kindness, here a promise to keep faith with him. Compare Joshua 2:12–14.
[^31]: Literally "daughters", the smaller places that depended on a town. Also later in the verse.
[^32]: The Hebrew text is written "the one living in Dor" but read "the people living in Dor".
[^33]: Hebrew *mas*: work gangs of conscripted laborers. Also in verses 30, 33 and 35.
[^34]: *Har-heres* means "mountain of the sun".
[^35]: Literally "the hand of the house of Joseph was heavy".
[^36]: *Akrabbim* means "scorpions". The Ascent of Akrabbim was a pass south of the Dead Sea (Numbers 34:4; Joshua 15:3).
[^37]: Or "from the rock". *Sela* means "rock".
