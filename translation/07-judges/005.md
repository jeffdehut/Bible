**1** On that day Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang this song:[^1]

**2** "When the leaders took the lead in Israel,[^2] when the people offered themselves willingly, bless the LORD![^3]

**3** Hear, you kings! Listen, you rulers! I, to the LORD, I will sing; I will make music to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**4** LORD, when you went out from Seir, when you marched from the land of Edom, the earth shook, and the heavens poured, and the clouds poured down water.

**5** The mountains quaked[^4] before the LORD, the One of Sinai,[^5] before the LORD, the God of Israel.

**6** In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the roads were deserted,[^6] and travelers went by winding paths.

**7** The villagers ceased in Israel,[^7] they ceased, until I, Deborah, arose,[^8] until I arose, a mother in Israel.

**8** They chose new gods; then there was war in the gates.[^9] Was a shield or a spear to be seen among forty thousand in Israel?

**9** My heart is with the commanders of Israel, who offered themselves willingly among the people. Bless the LORD!

**10** You who ride on white donkeys,[^10] you who sit on saddle blankets,[^11] and you who walk along the road, tell of it!

**11** At the sound of those who divide the flocks[^12] at the watering places, there they recount the victories[^13] of the LORD, the victories of his villagers in Israel. Then the people of the LORD went down to the gates.

**12** Wake up, wake up, Deborah! Wake up, wake up, sing a song! Rise up, Barak! Lead away your captives, son of Abinoam!

**13** Then the survivors came down to the nobles; the people of the LORD came down to me against the warriors.[^14]

**14** From Ephraim came those whose roots are in Amalek;[^15] after you, Benjamin, with your people. From Machir came down commanders, and from Zebulun those who carry the officer's staff.[^16]

**15** The princes of Issachar[^17] were with Deborah, and Issachar was true to Barak;[^18] into the valley they rushed at his heels. Among the clans of Reuben[^19] there were great resolves of heart.[^20]

**16** Why did you stay among the sheepfolds,[^21] to hear the shepherds piping for the flocks? Among the clans of Reuben there was great searching of heart.

**17** Gilead stayed on the far side of the Jordan. And Dan, why did he linger by the ships?[^22] Asher sat still at the seashore and stayed by his landing places.

**18** Zebulun is a people who risked their lives to the death,[^23] Naphtali too, on the heights of the field.

**19** Kings came, they fought; then the kings of Canaan fought, at Taanach, by the waters of Megiddo. They carried off no plunder of silver.

**20** From heaven they fought; the stars from their courses fought against Sisera.

**21** The Kishon River swept them away, the ancient river,[^24] the Kishon River. March on, my soul, with strength![^25]

**22** Then the horses' hooves hammered with the galloping, the galloping of his stallions.[^26]

**23** 'Curse Meroz,' said the angel of the LORD,[^27] 'bitterly curse its people, because they did not come to help the LORD, to help the LORD against the warriors.'[^28]

**24** Most blessed of women is Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, most blessed of women who live in tents.

**25** He asked for water; she gave him milk. In a bowl fit for nobles she brought him curds.

**26** She reached out her hand for the tent peg, her right hand for the workman's hammer. She hammered Sisera, she crushed his head, she shattered and pierced his temple.

**27** Between her feet he sank down, he fell, he lay still. Between her feet he sank down, he fell. Where he sank down, there he fell, destroyed.[^29]

**28** Through the window she looked out; Sisera's mother cried out[^30] through the lattice: 'Why is his chariot so slow to come? Why are the hoofbeats of his chariots so late?'

**29** The wisest of her ladies answer her; and she answers herself again and again:[^31]

**30** 'Aren't they finding and dividing the plunder? A girl, two girls[^32] for every warrior; plunder of dyed cloth for Sisera, plunder of dyed cloth, embroidered, dyed cloth, two pieces of embroidery for the necks of the plunderer.'[^33]

**31** So may all your enemies perish, LORD! But may those who love him be like the sun when it rises in its strength."[^34] And the land had peace[^35] for forty years.

[^1]: Literally "sang on that day, saying". The verb is feminine singular, which puts Deborah first. The Song of Deborah is one of the oldest poems in the Bible. Its language is ancient, and in many lines the meaning is uncertain.
[^2]: The meaning of this line is uncertain. It may instead mean "When locks of hair hung loose in Israel", referring to warriors who left their hair uncut under a vow (compare Numbers 6:5).
[^3]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^4]: The Hebrew vowel points read "flowed". The same consonants can be read "quaked", as the Septuagint understood them.
[^5]: Literally "this Sinai", perhaps an old way of saying "the One of Sinai". It may also mean "even Sinai", or "this is Sinai". Compare Psalm 68:9 (68:8 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Or "the caravans ceased". Literally "the roads ceased".
[^7]: Hebrew *perazon*, a rare word, also in verse 11. It probably means the people of the open villages, or village life. Others take it as "leaders" or "warriors".
[^8]: Or "until you, Deborah, arose, until you arose". The old verb form can be read either way.
[^9]: The Hebrew of this verse is very uncertain. The first line may instead mean "God chose new leaders" or "When they chose new gods". The word translated "war" is found only here.
[^10]: Or "tawny donkeys". The rare word probably describes a light or reddish-gray color.
[^11]: Hebrew *middin*, a rare word, perhaps "rich carpets" or "saddle blankets". Some take it as "judgment seats".
[^12]: Hebrew *mechatsetsim*, a word of uncertain meaning. Others take it as "archers" or "musicians" or "singers".
[^13]: Hebrew *tsidqot*, literally "righteous acts": the acts by which the LORD set things right for his people. Also later in the verse.
[^14]: The Hebrew of this verse is very uncertain. The vowel points seem to mean "Then he made a survivor rule over the nobles of the people; the LORD made me rule over the mighty."
[^15]: The meaning is uncertain. Part of Ephraim's land may once have belonged to the Amalekites (12:15).
[^16]: Or "the scribe's staff". The *sofer*, "scribe", may here be the officer who mustered the troops.
[^17]: The Hebrew vowel points read "My princes in Issachar".
[^18]: Literally "and Issachar, so Barak".
[^19]: Hebrew *peluggot*, perhaps "divisions" or "streams". Also in verse 16.
[^20]: In verse 16 the phrase is "searchings of heart", which differs by one letter in Hebrew. Many think the two lines were once the same.
[^21]: Or "the saddlebags" or "the campfires". The Hebrew word is rare and its meaning uncertain.
[^22]: Or "Why did Dan stay on as a guest at the ships?"
[^23]: Literally "who scorned their life to die".
[^24]: Or "the onrushing river". The Hebrew word *qedumim* is uncertain.
[^25]: Or "My soul, you trampled down the strong."
[^26]: Or "his mighty ones", that is, his warriors.
[^27]: Or "the messenger of the LORD". Hebrew *mal'akh* means messenger.
[^28]: Or "with the warriors".
[^29]: Or "dead".
[^30]: The Hebrew verb is found only here; it probably means to cry out in a high, wailing voice.
[^31]: Literally "even she returns her words to herself".
[^32]: Literally "a womb, two wombs", a crude word for captive women.
[^33]: Literally "for the necks of plunder". Some take it as "for my neck as plunder".
[^34]: The Hebrew changes from "your enemies" to "those who love him".
[^35]: Literally "was quiet".
