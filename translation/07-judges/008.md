**1** Then the men of Ephraim said to him, "What is this you have done to us, not calling us when you went to fight against Midian?" And they quarreled with him fiercely.

**2** He said to them, "What have I done now compared with you? Isn't the gleaning of Ephraim's grapes better than the whole grape harvest of Abiezer?[^1]

**3** God handed the commanders of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb, over to you. What was I able to do compared with you?" When he said this, their anger against him died down.[^2]

**4** Gideon came to the Jordan and crossed over, he and the three hundred men with him, exhausted but still pursuing.

**5** He said to the men of Succoth, "Please give some loaves of bread to the people who are following me,[^3] for they are exhausted, and I am pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian."

**6** But the officials of Succoth said, "Do you already have the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna in your hand,[^4] that we should give bread to your army?"

**7** Gideon said, "Because of that, when the LORD[^5] has handed Zebah and Zalmunna over to me, I will thresh your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers."

**8** From there he went up to Penuel and spoke to them in the same way, and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered.

**9** So he said to the men of Penuel too, "When I come back safely,[^6] I will tear down this tower."

**10** Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor with their army, about 15,000 men, all who were left of the whole army of the people of the east.[^7] Those who had fallen were 120,000 men who carried the sword.

**11** Gideon went up by the route of the tent-dwellers, east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and he attacked the army while it felt secure.

**12** Zebah and Zalmunna fled, but he pursued them and captured the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and he threw the whole army into panic.

**13** Then Gideon son of Joash returned from the battle by the Ascent of Heres.[^8]

**14** He caught a young man from Succoth and questioned him, and the young man wrote down for him the officials and elders of Succoth, seventy-seven men.

**15** Then he came to the men of Succoth and said, "Here are Zebah and Zalmunna, about whom you taunted me, saying, 'Do you already have the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna in your hand, that we should give bread to your exhausted men?'"

**16** He took the elders of the city, and thorns of the wilderness and briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth a lesson.[^9]

**17** He also tore down the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city.

**18** Then he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, "What about the men you killed at Tabor?"[^10] They said, "They were like you, every one of them, like the sons of a king."[^11]

**19** He said, "They were my brothers, my mother's sons. As surely as the LORD lives, if you had let them live, I would not kill you."

**20** He said to Jether, his firstborn, "Get up and kill them." But the boy did not draw his sword, because he was afraid, for he was still only a boy.

**21** Zebah and Zalmunna said, "Get up yourself and strike us down, for as the man is, so is his strength." So Gideon got up and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and he took the crescent ornaments that were on their camels' necks.

**22** The men of Israel said to Gideon, "Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson too, for you have saved us from the power of Midian."

**23** But Gideon said to them, "I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you. The LORD will rule over you."

**24** Then Gideon said to them, "I have one request to make of you. Each of you give me an earring[^12] from his plunder." (The enemy had gold earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.)[^13]

**25** They said, "We will gladly give them."[^14] So they spread out a cloak, and each man threw on it an earring from his plunder.

**26** The weight of the gold earrings that he asked for was 1,700 shekels of gold,[^15] besides the crescent ornaments, the pendants and the purple garments worn by the kings of Midian, and besides the chains on their camels' necks.

**27** Gideon made it into an ephod[^16] and set it up in his town, in Ophrah. All Israel prostituted themselves to it there,[^17] and it became a trap for Gideon and his family.

**28** So Midian was brought low before the Israelites, and they did not raise their heads again. The land had peace[^18] for forty years in the days of Gideon.

**29** Jerubbaal son of Joash went back and lived in his own house.

**30** Gideon had seventy sons, his own offspring,[^19] for he had many wives.

**31** His concubine[^20] who lived in Shechem also bore him a son, and he named him Abimelech.[^21]

**32** Gideon son of Joash died at a good old age and was buried in the tomb of his father Joash, in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

**33** As soon as Gideon was dead, the Israelites again prostituted themselves to the Baals,[^22] and they made Baal-berith[^23] their god.

**34** The Israelites did not remember the LORD their God, who had rescued them from the power of all their enemies all around.

**35** And they did not show faithful kindness[^24] to the family of Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, in return for all the good he had done for Israel.

[^1]: The gleaning is what is left after the harvest. Gideon means that the little Ephraim did at the end was worth more than all that his own clan, Abiezer, had done.
[^2]: Literally "their spirit toward him relaxed".
[^3]: Literally "who are at my feet".
[^4]: Literally "Is the palm of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your hand?" Perhaps referring to the practice of cutting off the hands of dead enemies to count them. Also in verse 15.
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: Literally "in peace".
[^7]: Literally "the sons of the east", the desert peoples living east of the Jordan.
[^8]: Or "before the sun was up". *Heres* means "sun".
[^9]: Literally "he made known with them". The Septuagint reads "he threshed", as in verse 7.
[^10]: Literally "Where are the men …?"
[^11]: Literally "Like you, so were they, each one like the form of the sons of the king."
[^12]: Hebrew *nezem*, an earring or a nose ring. Also in verses 25 and 26.
[^13]: *Ishmaelites* here seems to be a general name for the desert traders. Compare Genesis 37:25–28, where Ishmaelites and Midianites appear together.
[^14]: Literally "We will surely give."
[^15]: Literally "a thousand and seven hundred of gold". A shekel is about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so 1,700 shekels is about 43 pounds (19.5 kg).
[^16]: Hebrew *ephod*. In Exodus 28:6–14 the ephod is a garment worn by the high priest. Here it seems to be an object used in worship, perhaps to seek guidance from God.
[^17]: Literally "went whoring after it there", an image of unfaithfulness to the LORD.
[^18]: Literally "was quiet".
[^19]: Literally "who came out of his thigh".
[^20]: Hebrew *pilegesh*: a wife of secondary rank.
[^21]: *Abimelech* means "my father is king".
[^22]: *Baal*, "lord, master, owner", was the name of the Canaanite storm god. The plural refers to his local forms, or to other gods in general.
[^23]: "Baal of the covenant". Compare 9:4, 46.
[^24]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love or kindness.
