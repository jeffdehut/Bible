**1** After Ehud died, the Israelites again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD.[^1]

**2** So the LORD sold them into the power of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. The commander of his army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth-ha-goiim.[^2]

**3** The Israelites cried out to the LORD, because Jabin had nine hundred iron chariots,[^3] and he had cruelly oppressed the Israelites for twenty years.

**4** Now Deborah, a prophet, the wife of Lappidoth,[^4] was judging Israel at that time.

**5** She used to sit under the Palm of Deborah, between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the Israelites went up to her to have their disputes decided.[^5]

**6** She sent for Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali and said to him, "The LORD, the God of Israel, has commanded you,[^6] 'Go, gather your men at Mount Tabor,[^7] and take with you ten thousand men from Naphtali and Zebulun.

**7** I will draw Sisera, the commander of Jabin's army, out to meet you at the Kishon River,[^8] with his chariots and his troops,[^9] and I will hand him over to you.'"

**8** Barak said to her, "If you go with me, I will go. But if you don't go with me, I won't go."

**9** She said, "I will certainly go with you. But the road you are taking will not bring you honor,[^10] for the LORD will sell Sisera into the power of a woman." Then Deborah got up and went with Barak to Kedesh.

**10** Barak called out Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh. Ten thousand men went up under his command,[^11] and Deborah went up with him.

**11** Now Heber the Kenite had separated from the Kenites,[^12] the descendants of Hobab, Moses' father-in-law,[^13] and had pitched his tent as far away as the oak[^14] at Zaanannim,[^15] near Kedesh.

**12** Sisera was told that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor.

**13** So Sisera called out all his chariots, nine hundred iron chariots, and all the troops who were with him, from Harosheth-ha-goiim to the Kishon River.

**14** Deborah said to Barak, "Get up! This is the day the LORD has handed Sisera over to you. Hasn't the LORD gone out ahead of you?" So Barak went down from Mount Tabor, with ten thousand men following him.

**15** Before Barak, the LORD threw Sisera, all his chariots and his whole army into panic and put them to the sword.[^16] Sisera got down from his chariot and fled on foot.

**16** Barak chased the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth-ha-goiim, and Sisera's whole army fell by the sword. Not a single man was left.

**17** But Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the family of Heber the Kenite.

**18** Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, "Come in, my lord. Come in here with me. Don't be afraid." So he went into her tent, and she covered him with a rug.[^17]

**19** He said to her, "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty." She opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink, and covered him again.

**20** He said to her, "Stand at the entrance of the tent. If anyone comes and asks you, 'Is there a man here?' say, 'No.'"

**21** But Jael, Heber's wife, took a tent peg and picked up a hammer. She went to him quietly and drove the peg through his temple, and it went down into the ground. He was fast asleep and exhausted,[^18] and he died.

**22** Just then Barak came by, chasing Sisera. Jael went out to meet him and said to him, "Come, I will show you the man you are looking for." He went in with her, and there was Sisera lying dead, with the tent peg in his temple.

**23** So on that day God brought Jabin king of Canaan low before the Israelites.

**24** The Israelites pressed harder and harder on Jabin king of Canaan,[^19] until they had destroyed Jabin king of Canaan.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: *Harosheth-ha-goiim* means "Harosheth of the nations". Also in verses 13 and 16.
[^3]: Probably chariots fitted with iron. Also in verse 13.
[^4]: Literally "Deborah, a woman, a prophetess, wife of Lappidoth". It can also be read "a woman of torches", that is, a fiery woman. *Lappidoth* means "torches", and *Deborah* means "bee".
[^5]: Literally "for judgment".
[^6]: Literally "Has not the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded …?", a question that expects the answer yes.
[^7]: Literally "Go and draw at Mount Tabor", perhaps "draw out your troops" or "march".
[^8]: Hebrew *nachal Qishon*. A *nachal* is a stream bed that often runs dry in summer. Also in verse 13.
[^9]: Literally "his multitude".
[^10]: Literally "your honor will not be on the road on which you are going".
[^11]: Literally "at his feet".
[^12]: Hebrew *Qayin*, the name of the clan's ancestor.
[^13]: Hebrew *choten*, a relative by marriage, usually a father-in-law. Compare Numbers 10:29, where Hobab is the son of Moses' father-in-law.
[^14]: Or "the great tree".
[^15]: The Hebrew text is written "Zaanaim" but read "Zaanannim".
[^16]: Literally "The LORD threw Sisera and all the chariots and all the army into confusion by the mouth of the sword before Barak."
[^17]: The Hebrew word is found only here. Also in verse 19.
[^18]: Or "He was fast asleep. He went limp and died." The verb translated "exhausted" may instead mean "fainted" or "twitched".
[^19]: Literally "The hand of the Israelites went on, going on and growing harder, against Jabin king of Canaan".
