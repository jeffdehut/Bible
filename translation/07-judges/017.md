**1** There was a man from the hill country of Ephraim named Micah.[^1]

**2** He said to his mother, "The 1,100 pieces of silver[^2] that were taken from you, about which you pronounced a curse, and said it in my hearing too: look, the silver is with me. I took it." His mother said, "May my son be blessed by Yahweh!"[^3]

**3** He gave the 1,100 pieces of silver back to his mother, and his mother said, "I solemnly dedicate the silver to Yahweh from my hand for my son, to make a carved image and a cast idol.[^4] So now I will give it back to you."

**4** So he gave the silver back to his mother, and his mother took two hundred pieces of silver[^5] and gave them to a silversmith, who made them into a carved image and a cast idol. And it was in Micah's house.

**5** This man Micah had a shrine.[^6] He made an ephod[^7] and household gods,[^8] and he installed[^9] one of his sons, who became his priest.

**6** In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes.

**7** There was a young man from Bethlehem in Judah, of the clan of Judah. He was a Levite, and he had been living there as a foreigner.[^10]

**8** The man left the town, Bethlehem in Judah, to live wherever he could find a place. On his journey he came to the hill country of Ephraim, to Micah's house.

**9** Micah said to him, "Where do you come from?" He said to him, "I am a Levite from Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to live wherever I can find a place."

**10** Micah said to him, "Stay with me and be a father and a priest to me. I will give you ten pieces of silver[^11] a year, a set of clothes, and your food." So the Levite went in.[^12]

**11** The Levite agreed to live with the man, and the young man became like one of his sons.

**12** Micah installed the Levite, and the young man became his priest and lived in Micah's house.

**13** Then Micah said, "Now I know that Yahweh will make me prosper, because I have a Levite as my priest."

[^1]: Here and in verse 4 the Hebrew has the longer form of the name, *Mikhayehu*; elsewhere in chapters 17–18 it is *Mikhah*.
[^2]: Literally "a thousand and a hundred of silver", probably shekels: about 28 pounds (12.7 kg). Also in verse 3.
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *pesel u-massekhah*. The two words may describe a single image: a carved figure covered with cast metal. Also in verse 4.
[^5]: Literally "two hundred of silver", about 5 pounds (2.3 kg).
[^6]: Literally "a house of God" or "a house of gods".
[^7]: See 8:27.
[^8]: Hebrew *teraphim*, small images of gods kept in the home (Genesis 31:19).
[^9]: Literally "filled the hand of", an expression for ordaining a priest (Exodus 28:41). Also in verse 12.
[^10]: Hebrew *ger*: someone living away from his own people.
[^11]: Literally "ten of silver", about 4 ounces (115 grams).
[^12]: Literally "And the Levite went."
