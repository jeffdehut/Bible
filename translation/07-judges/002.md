**1** The angel of Yahweh[^1] went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, "I brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that I promised on oath to your ancestors. I said, 'I will never break my covenant with you,

**2** and you must not make a covenant with the people who live in this land. You must tear down their altars.' But you have not obeyed me.[^2] What is this you have done?

**3** So I also say this:[^3] I will not drive them out before you. They will become thorns in your sides,[^4] and their gods will be a trap for you."

**4** When the angel of Yahweh had spoken these words to all the Israelites, the people wept aloud.[^5]

**5** They named that place Bochim,[^6] and they offered sacrifices there to Yahweh.

**6** When Joshua had sent the people away, the Israelites went, each to his own inheritance, to take possession of the land.[^7]

**7** The people served Yahweh all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who had seen all the great work that Yahweh had done for Israel.

**8** Joshua son of Nun, the servant of Yahweh, died at the age of 110.

**9** They buried him in the territory he had inherited, at Timnath-heres[^8] in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

**10** That whole generation also was gathered to its ancestors, and after them another generation grew up who did not know Yahweh or the work he had done for Israel.

**11** Then the Israelites did what was evil in the sight of Yahweh and served the Baals.[^9]

**12** They abandoned Yahweh, the God of their ancestors, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They followed other gods, gods of the peoples around them, and bowed down to them, and they provoked Yahweh to anger.

**13** They abandoned Yahweh and served Baal and the Ashtoreths.[^10]

**14** Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he handed them over to raiders, who plundered them. He sold them into the power[^11] of their enemies all around, and they could no longer stand up to their enemies.

**15** Whenever they went out to fight, the hand of Yahweh was against them to bring them disaster, just as Yahweh had said and as Yahweh had sworn to them. They were in great distress.

**16** Then Yahweh raised up judges,[^12] and they saved them from the power of those who plundered them.

**17** But they would not listen even to their judges. They prostituted themselves to other gods[^13] and bowed down to them. They were quick to turn aside from the way their ancestors had walked in obedience to Yahweh's commands. They did not do as their ancestors did.

**18** Whenever Yahweh raised up judges for them, Yahweh was with the judge and saved them from the power of their enemies as long as the judge lived. For Yahweh was moved to pity[^14] by their groaning because of those who oppressed them and crushed them.

**19** But when the judge died, they would turn back and act more corruptly than their ancestors, following other gods to serve them and bow down to them. They did not give up any of their practices or their stubborn ways.[^15]

**20** So Yahweh's anger burned against Israel, and he said, "This nation has broken[^16] my covenant, which I commanded their ancestors, and has not obeyed me.

**21** So I in turn will no longer drive out before them any of the nations that Joshua left when he died.

**22** I will use them to test Israel, to see whether they will keep the way of Yahweh and walk in it, as their ancestors kept it, or not."

**23** So Yahweh left those nations in the land. He did not drive them out quickly, and he did not hand them over to Joshua.

[^1]: Or "the messenger of Yahweh". Hebrew *mal'akh* means messenger. Here the angel speaks in Yahweh's name, as in Genesis 16:7–13. *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "listened to my voice"; also in verse 20.
[^3]: Or "And I also said". Compare Numbers 33:55; Joshua 23:13.
[^4]: Literally "They will be sides to you." The phrase is probably short for "thorns in your sides", as in Numbers 33:55.
[^5]: Literally "lifted up their voice and wept".
[^6]: *Bochim* means "people weeping".
[^7]: Verses 6–9 repeat, with small differences, Joshua 24:28–31.
[^8]: In Joshua 19:50 and 24:30 the name is Timnath-serah.
[^9]: Hebrew *ha-be'alim*. *Baal*, "lord, master, owner", was the name of the Canaanite storm god. The plural refers to his local forms, or to other gods in general.
[^10]: Hebrew *ashtarot*, plural of *Ashtoreth* (Astarte), a Canaanite goddess. The plural refers to her local forms, or to goddesses in general.
[^11]: Literally "into the hand"; also in verses 16 and 18.
[^12]: Hebrew *shofetim*: leaders who rescued Israel and governed it. The word means more than a judge in a court. Also in verses 17–19.
[^13]: Literally "they went whoring after other gods", an image of unfaithfulness to Yahweh, as of a wife to her husband.
[^14]: Or "relented". Hebrew *nicham*, "be sorry, change one's mind, feel compassion".
[^15]: Literally "They did not let anything fall from their deeds and from their hard way."
[^16]: Literally "passed over", that is, transgressed.
