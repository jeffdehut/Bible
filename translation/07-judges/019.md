**1** In those days, when there was no king in Israel, there was a Levite living in the far parts of the hill country of Ephraim. He took for himself a concubine[^1] from Bethlehem in Judah.

**2** But his concubine was unfaithful to him,[^2] and she left him and went to her father's house in Bethlehem in Judah. She was there for four months.

**3** Then her husband got up and went after her to speak tenderly to her[^3] and bring her back.[^4] He had his servant with him and a pair of donkeys. She brought him into her father's house, and when the young woman's father saw him, he was glad to meet him.

**4** His father-in-law, the young woman's father, made him stay, and he stayed with him three days. They ate and drank and spent the nights there.

**5** On the fourth day they got up early in the morning, and he got ready to leave. But the young woman's father said to his son-in-law, "Have a bite to eat to refresh yourself,[^5] and then you can go."

**6** So the two of them sat down and ate and drank together. Then the young woman's father said to the man, "Please agree to stay the night, and enjoy yourself."[^6]

**7** The man got up to leave, but his father-in-law urged him, so he stayed there again that night.

**8** On the fifth day he got up early in the morning to leave, and the young woman's father said, "Please refresh yourself, and wait until the afternoon."[^7] So the two of them ate.

**9** When the man got up to leave, with his concubine and his servant, his father-in-law, the young woman's father, said to him, "Look, the day is drawing toward evening. Please spend the night. The day is coming to an end. Stay here and enjoy yourself. Tomorrow you can get up early for your journey and go home."[^8]

**10** But the man was not willing to spend the night. He got up and left, and he came opposite Jebus, that is, Jerusalem. He had with him a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.

**11** When they were near Jebus, the day was almost gone, and the servant said to his master, "Come, let's turn aside into this city of the Jebusites and spend the night in it."

**12** His master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a city of foreigners, who are not Israelites. We will go on to Gibeah."

**13** He said to his servant, "Come, let's try to reach one of these places and spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah."

**14** So they passed on and went their way, and the sun set on them near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**15** They turned aside there to go in and spend the night in Gibeah. He went in and sat down in the city square, but no one took them into his house for the night.

**16** Then in the evening an old man came in from his work in the fields. He was from the hill country of Ephraim, and he was living as a foreigner in Gibeah. The men of the place were Benjaminites.

**17** When he looked up and saw the traveler in the city square, the old man said, "Where are you going? Where do you come from?"

**18** He said to him, "We are traveling from Bethlehem in Judah to the far parts of the hill country of Ephraim. That is where I am from. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to the house of the LORD.[^9] But no one will take me into his house.

**19** We have both straw and fodder for our donkeys, and there is also bread and wine for me, for your servant woman, and for the young man with your servants.[^10] We don't need anything."

**20** The old man said, "Peace be with you. Let me take care of all your needs. Only don't spend the night in the square."

**21** So he brought him into his house and gave the donkeys fodder. They washed their feet and ate and drank.

**22** While they were enjoying themselves, the men of the city, worthless men,[^11] surrounded the house and pounded on the door. They said to the old man, the owner of the house, "Bring out the man who came to your house, so that we can have sex with him."[^12]

**23** The man, the owner of the house, went out to them and said to them, "No, my brothers, please don't do such an evil thing. Since this man has come into my house, don't do this outrageous thing.[^13]

**24** Here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. Let me bring them out now. Violate them[^14] and do to them whatever you like. But don't do such an outrageous thing to this man."

**25** But the men would not listen to him. So the man took hold of his concubine and pushed her outside to them. They raped her[^15] and abused her all night until morning, and they let her go as dawn was breaking.

**26** At daybreak the woman came and fell down at the entrance of the man's house where her master was, and she lay there until it was light.

**27** In the morning her master got up, opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way. And there was the woman, his concubine, lying at the entrance of the house with her hands on the threshold.

**28** He said to her, "Get up. Let's go." But there was no answer.[^16] Then he put her on the donkey, and the man set out and went home.

**29** When he reached his house, he took the knife,[^17] took hold of his concubine, and cut her up limb by limb into twelve pieces. And he sent her throughout the territory of Israel.

**30** Everyone who saw it said, "Nothing like this has ever happened or been seen from the day the Israelites came up out of the land of Egypt until this day. Think about it! Take counsel, and speak!"[^18]

[^1]: Hebrew *pilegesh*: a wife of secondary rank. Literally "a woman, a concubine".
[^2]: Literally "played the prostitute against him". The Septuagint reads "became angry with him".
[^3]: Literally "to speak to her heart".
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "to bring it back" (perhaps "her heart") but read "to bring her back".
[^5]: Literally "Sustain your heart with a piece of bread"; also in verse 8.
[^6]: Literally "let your heart be good"; also in verses 9 and 22.
[^7]: Literally "until the day declines".
[^8]: Literally "go to your tent".
[^9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. The Septuagint reads "and I am going to my home".
[^10]: Polite ways for the Levite to refer to his concubine and his servant, and to himself as the old man's servant.
[^11]: Literally "sons of *beliyya'al*", "sons of worthlessness": scoundrels.
[^12]: Literally "so that we may know him". Compare Genesis 19:5.
[^13]: Hebrew *nevalah*, a shameful act that violates what is sacred or right. Also in verse 24.
[^14]: Hebrew *innah*, "humble, mistreat", here of sexual violence.
[^15]: Literally "knew her".
[^16]: The Septuagint adds "for she was dead".
[^17]: Hebrew *ma'akhelet*, the same word used for the knife Abraham took in Genesis 22:6, 10.
[^18]: The Septuagint has a longer text, in which the Levite tells the men he sends out what they are to say to the Israelites.
