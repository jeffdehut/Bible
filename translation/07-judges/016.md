**1** Samson went to Gaza. There he saw a prostitute, and he slept with her.[^1]

**2** The people of Gaza were told,[^2] "Samson has come here!" So they surrounded the place and lay in wait for him all night at the city gate. All night they kept quiet, saying, "At dawn we will kill him."

**3** But Samson lay there until midnight. At midnight he got up, took hold of the doors of the city gate and the two gateposts, and pulled them up, bar and all. He put them on his shoulders and carried them up to the top of the hill that faces Hebron.

**4** After this he fell in love with a woman in the Sorek Valley,[^3] whose name was Delilah.

**5** The rulers[^4] of the Philistines went up to her and said to her, "Coax him, and find out what makes his strength so great, and how we can overpower him, so that we can tie him up and subdue him. Each of us will give you 1,100 pieces of silver."[^5]

**6** So Delilah said to Samson, "Please tell me what makes your strength so great, and how you could be tied up and subdued."

**7** Samson said to her, "If they tie me with seven fresh bowstrings[^6] that have not been dried, I will become weak and be like any other man."

**8** So the rulers of the Philistines brought her seven fresh bowstrings that had not been dried, and she tied him with them.

**9** She had men lying in wait in an inner room. She said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" But he snapped the bowstrings as a strand of yarn snaps when it touches fire.[^7] So the secret of his strength was not discovered.

**10** Then Delilah said to Samson, "You have made a fool of me and told me lies. Now please tell me how you could be tied up."

**11** He said to her, "If they tie me tightly with new ropes that have never been used, I will become weak and be like any other man."

**12** So Delilah took new ropes and tied him with them, and she said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" The men lying in wait were in the inner room. But he snapped the ropes off his arms like thread.

**13** Delilah said to Samson, "Until now you have made a fool of me and told me lies. Tell me how you could be tied up." He said to her, "If you weave the seven braids of my hair into the fabric on the loom …"[^8]

**14** She fastened it with the pin and said to him, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" He woke up from his sleep and pulled out the pin of the loom, and the fabric.

**15** Then she said to him, "How can you say, 'I love you,' when your heart is not with me? Three times now you have made a fool of me, and you have not told me what makes your strength so great."

**16** Day after day she pressed him with her words and nagged him, until he was worn out to death.[^9]

**17** So he told her everything[^10] and said to her, "No razor has ever touched my head, for I have been a Nazirite[^11] dedicated to God from my mother's womb. If I were shaved, my strength would leave me, and I would become weak and be like any other man."

**18** When Delilah saw that he had told her everything, she sent word to the rulers of the Philistines: "Come up this once, for he has told me everything."[^12] So the rulers of the Philistines came up to her, and they brought the silver with them.

**19** She lulled him to sleep on her knees and called a man, and she had the seven braids of his hair shaved off.[^13] Then she began to subdue him,[^14] and his strength left him.

**20** She said, "The Philistines are upon you, Samson!" He woke up from his sleep and thought, "I will go out as at other times and shake myself free." But he did not know that the LORD[^15] had left him.

**21** The Philistines seized him and gouged out his eyes. They took him down to Gaza and bound him with bronze chains, and he was made to grind grain in the prison.

**22** But the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved.

**23** Now the rulers of the Philistines gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon[^16] their god and to celebrate. They said, "Our god has handed over to us Samson our enemy."

**24** When the people saw him, they praised their god, for they said, "Our god has handed over to us our enemy, who laid waste our land and killed so many of us."

**25** When they were in high spirits,[^17] they said, "Call Samson to entertain us." So they called Samson from the prison, and he entertained them.[^18] They made him stand between the pillars.

**26** Samson said to the young man who was leading him by the hand, "Let me feel the pillars that hold up the temple,[^19] so that I can lean against them."

**27** Now the temple was full of men and women. All the rulers of the Philistines were there, and on the roof there were about three thousand men and women watching Samson entertain them.

**28** Then Samson called out to the LORD, "Lord GOD, please remember me. Please give me strength just this once, O God, so that with one act of revenge I may pay back the Philistines for my two eyes."[^20]

**29** Samson took hold of the two central pillars that held up the temple, and he braced himself against them, one with his right hand and the other with his left.

**30** Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines!" He pushed with all his strength, and the temple fell on the rulers and on all the people in it. So those he killed at his death were more than those he had killed during his life.

**31** His brothers and all his father's family came down and took him. They brought him up and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of his father Manoah. He had judged Israel for twenty years.

[^1]: Literally "went in to her", a Hebrew way of speaking of sexual relations.
[^2]: The Hebrew has no verb here: literally "To the people of Gaza, saying". The Septuagint reads "It was told to the people of Gaza".
[^3]: Hebrew *nachal Soreq*, a stream bed that often runs dry in summer.
[^4]: Hebrew *seranim*, a title used only for the rulers of the five Philistine cities (3:3). Also in verses 8, 18, 23, 27 and 30.
[^5]: Literally "a thousand and a hundred of silver", probably shekels: about 28 pounds (12.7 kg).
[^6]: Or "cords", probably made from animal sinew. Also in verses 8 and 9.
[^7]: Literally "as a thread of tow snaps when it smells fire". Tow is the coarse, broken fiber of flax.
[^8]: The Hebrew sentence breaks off here. The Septuagint continues: "… and fasten it with the pin into the wall, I will become weak and be like any other man." In verse 14 it then begins: "While he was asleep, Delilah took the seven braids of his hair and wove them into the fabric."
[^9]: Literally "his soul became short, to death".
[^10]: Literally "all his heart"; also in verse 18.
[^11]: Hebrew *nazir*, "one set apart, dedicated". See 13:5.
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written "he has told her everything" but read "he has told me everything".
[^13]: Literally "she called to the man, and she shaved the seven braids of his head".
[^14]: The Septuagint reads "he began to grow weak".
[^15]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^16]: A god worshiped by the Philistines, probably a god of grain (Hebrew *dagan*, "grain").
[^17]: Literally "When their heart was good".
[^18]: Or "he performed for them", or "they made sport of him". The verb can mean to laugh, to play, or to make fun of. Also in verse 27.
[^19]: Literally "the house"; also in verses 27, 29 and 30.
[^20]: Or "for one of my two eyes".
