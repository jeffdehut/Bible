**1** At once, early in the morning, the chief priests with the elders, the scribes, and the whole council[^1] reached a decision. They bound Jesus, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate.

**2** Pilate asked him, "Are you the king of the Jews?" He answered him, "You say so."[^2]

**3** The chief priests accused him of many things.

**4** So Pilate asked him again, "Have you no answer? See how many charges they are bringing against you."

**5** But Jesus made no further answer, so that Pilate was amazed.

**6** At the festival he used to release for them one prisoner, whomever they asked for.

**7** A man called Barabbas was in prison with the rebels who had committed murder during the uprising.

**8** The crowd came up and began asking Pilate to do for them what he usually did.

**9** Pilate answered them, "Do you want me to release for you the king of the Jews?"

**10** For he knew it was out of envy that the chief priests had handed Jesus over.

**11** But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to have him release Barabbas for them instead.

**12** Pilate answered them again, "Then what do you want me to do with the one you call the king of the Jews?"

**13** They shouted back, "Crucify him!"

**14** Pilate said to them, "Why? What wrong has he done?" But they shouted all the louder, "Crucify him!"

**15** Wanting to satisfy the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas for them. He had Jesus flogged, and handed him over to be crucified.

**16** The soldiers led Jesus away into the courtyard, that is, the governor's headquarters,[^3] and they called together the whole cohort.[^4]

**17** They dressed him in purple, twisted together a crown of thorns, and put it on him.

**18** Then they began to salute him: "Hail, king of the Jews!"

**19** They kept striking his head with a reed and spitting on him, and they knelt down and paid homage to him.

**20** When they had finished mocking him, they took off the purple robe and put his own clothes back on him. Then they led him out to crucify him.

**21** A passerby, Simon of Cyrene, the father of Alexander and Rufus, was coming in from the country, and they forced him to carry Jesus' cross.

**22** They brought Jesus to the place called Golgotha, which means "Place of a Skull".

**23** They offered him wine mixed with myrrh, but he did not take it.

**24** They crucified him and divided his clothes among them, casting lots to decide what each would take.[^5]

**25** It was nine in the morning[^6] when they crucified him.

**26** The written notice of the charge against him read: "The King of the Jews."

**27** With him they crucified two rebels,[^7] one on his right and one on his left.[^8]

**29** Those who passed by insulted him, shaking their heads[^9] and saying, "Aha! You who would destroy the temple and build it in three days,

**30** save yourself and come down from the cross!"

**31** In the same way the chief priests, with the scribes, mocked him among themselves: "He saved others, but he cannot save himself!

**32** Let the Messiah, the king of Israel, come down now from the cross, so that we may see and believe." Those crucified with him also taunted him.

**33** At noon[^10] darkness came over the whole land[^11] until three in the afternoon.

**34** At three in the afternoon Jesus cried out with a loud voice, "Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?" which means, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"[^12]

**35** When some of those standing nearby heard this, they said, "Listen, he is calling Elijah."

**36** Someone ran, filled a sponge with sour wine,[^13] put it on a reed, and gave it to him to drink, saying, "Wait, let us see whether Elijah comes to take him down."

**37** Then Jesus let out a loud cry and breathed his last.

**38** The veil[^14] of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom.

**39** When the centurion who stood facing him saw how he breathed his last,[^15] he said, "Truly this man was God's Son!"[^16]

**40** Some women were also watching from a distance. Among them were Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the younger and of Joses, and Salome.

**41** When he was in Galilee, these women used to follow him and look after his needs.[^17] Many other women who had come up with him to Jerusalem were there too.

**42** Evening had already come. Since it was the day of Preparation, that is, the day before the Sabbath,

**43** Joseph of Arimathea, a respected member of the council,[^18] who was himself waiting for the kingdom of God, came and went boldly to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus.

**44** Pilate was surprised that he was already dead. He summoned the centurion and asked him whether he had been dead for long.

**45** When he learned from the centurion that it was so, he granted the corpse to Joseph.

**46** Joseph bought a linen cloth, took him down, wrapped him in the linen, and laid him in a tomb cut out of the rock. Then he rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb.

**47** Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses saw where he was laid.

[^1]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin.
[^2]: Or "You have said it." The reply neither simply affirms nor denies.
[^3]: Greek *praitorion*, the Praetorium, the Roman governor's residence.
[^4]: A Roman military unit, nominally 600 soldiers.
[^5]: See Psalm 22:19 (22:18 in most English Bibles).
[^6]: Literally "the third hour", counted from sunrise.
[^7]: Or "robbers", "bandits". Greek *lestai*.
[^8]: Many manuscripts add verse 28: "And the scripture was fulfilled that says, 'He was counted among the lawless'" (Isaiah 53:12). The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave it out, so this translation has no verse 28.
[^9]: See Psalm 22:8 (22:7 in most English Bibles).
[^10]: Literally "the sixth hour"; "three in the afternoon" is literally "the ninth hour".
[^11]: Or "the whole earth".
[^12]: Psalm 22:2 (22:1 in most English Bibles), quoted in Aramaic.
[^13]: Cheap wine vinegar, a common drink of soldiers and laborers. See Psalm 69:22 (69:21 in most English Bibles).
[^14]: Greek *katapetasma*, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for the veil that screened the most holy place (Exodus 26:31–33).
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "saw that he cried out and breathed his last".
[^16]: Or "a son of God". The Greek has no article.
[^17]: Or "serve him".
[^18]: Greek *bouleutes*, a member of the Sanhedrin.
