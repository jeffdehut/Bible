**1** The beginning of the good news about Jesus the Messiah.[^1]

**2** It is written in the prophet Isaiah:[^2] "Look, I am sending my messenger ahead of you. He will prepare your way.

**3** A voice is shouting in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight.'"[^3]

**4** So John the Baptizer appeared in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance[^4] for the forgiveness of sins.

**5** The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem went out to him. They confessed their sins, and he baptized them in the Jordan River.

**6** John wore clothing made of camel's hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.

**7** He proclaimed, "After me comes someone more powerful than I am. I am not worthy to bend down and untie the strap of his sandals.

**8** I have baptized you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

**9** At that time Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.

**10** As soon as[^5] he came up out of the water, he saw the heavens torn open and the Spirit coming down on him like a dove.

**11** And a voice came from the heavens: "You are my Son, whom I love. I am delighted with you."[^6]

**12** At once the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness.

**13** He was in the wilderness forty days, being tested[^7] by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels took care of him.

**14** After John was arrested,[^8] Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the good news of God.

**15** He said, "The time has come, and the kingdom of God has come near. Repent, and believe the good news."

**16** As Jesus was going along beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Simon's brother Andrew casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**17** Jesus said to them, "Come, follow me, and I will make you fish for people."

**18** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**19** Going on a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John. They were in their boat, mending the nets.

**20** At once he called them. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and went after him.

**21** They went to Capernaum. As soon as the Sabbath came, he began teaching in the synagogue.[^9]

**22** The people were amazed at his teaching, because he taught them like someone with authority, not like the scribes.[^10]

**23** Just then, in their synagogue, there was a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

**24** "What do you want with us,[^11] Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are: the Holy One of God!"

**25** Jesus rebuked him: "Be quiet, and come out of him!"

**26** The unclean spirit threw the man into convulsions, shrieked with a loud voice, and came out of him.

**27** Everyone was so astonished that they asked one another, "What is this? A new teaching, and with authority! He even gives orders to the unclean spirits, and they obey him."[^12]

**28** At once the news about him spread everywhere, throughout the whole region around Galilee.

**29** As soon as they left the synagogue, they went with James and John to the house of Simon and Andrew.

**30** Simon's mother-in-law was lying in bed with a fever, and they told Jesus about her at once.

**31** He went to her, took her by the hand, and helped her up. The fever left her, and she began to serve them.

**32** That evening, after the sun had set, people brought to him all who were sick and those who were possessed by demons.

**33** The whole town gathered at the door.

**34** He healed many who were sick with various diseases and drove out many demons. But he would not let the demons speak, because they knew who he was.

**35** Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up and went out to a deserted place, and there he prayed.

**36** Simon and those with him went looking for him.

**37** When they found him, they said to him, "Everyone is looking for you."

**38** He answered them, "Let us go somewhere else, to the nearby towns, so that I can proclaim the message there too. That is why I came out."

**39** So he went throughout Galilee, proclaiming the message in their synagogues and driving out demons.

**40** A man with a skin disease[^13] came to him. Kneeling down, he begged him, "If you are willing, you can make me clean."

**41** Moved with anger,[^14] Jesus reached out his hand and touched him. "I am willing," he said. "Be made clean!"

**42** At once the skin disease left him, and he was made clean.

**43** Jesus sent him away at once with a stern warning:

**44** "See that you say nothing to anyone. But go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as evidence for them."[^15]

**45** But the man went out and began to talk about it freely, spreading the news, so that Jesus could no longer go into a town openly. He stayed out in deserted places, and people kept coming to him from everywhere.

[^1]: Greek *christos*, "anointed one". The SBLGNT and some early manuscripts end the verse here. Many manuscripts add "the Son of God".
[^2]: Many later manuscripts read "in the prophets". The quotation combines Malachi 3:1 (and Exodus 23:20) with Isaiah 40:3.
[^3]: Isaiah 40:3. In Isaiah's Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^4]: A baptism expressing a change of heart and life. Greek *metanoia*, a turning around of mind and life.
[^5]: Greek *euthys*, "immediately", one of Mark's favorite words. It is rendered "at once", "as soon as" or "just then", as the sentence requires.
[^6]: Or "You are my beloved Son; I take great pleasure in you."
[^7]: Or "tempted". The Greek word means both.
[^8]: Literally "handed over".
[^9]: Most manuscripts read "On the Sabbath he went into the synagogue and began to teach."
[^10]: Experts in the Law of Moses.
[^11]: Literally "What to us and to you?", an idiom: "What do we have to do with you?"
[^12]: Or "What is this? A new teaching with authority! He even gives orders …"
[^13]: Greek *lepros*, traditionally "leper". The word covered various skin diseases, not only what is now called leprosy.
[^14]: The SBLGNT reads "becoming angry", with one early manuscript and some Latin manuscripts. Most manuscripts read "moved with compassion". Scholars debate which is original: the harder reading, "angry", may have been softened by copyists.
[^15]: Or "as a testimony against them".
