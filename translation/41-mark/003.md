**1** Another time Jesus went into a synagogue, and a man was there whose hand was withered.

**2** Some people were watching Jesus closely to see whether he would heal him on the Sabbath, so that they could accuse him.

**3** He said to the man with the withered hand, "Get up and stand in the middle."

**4** Then he asked them, "Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do harm, to save a life or to kill?" But they said nothing.

**5** He looked around at them with anger, deeply grieved at their stubborn hearts,[^1] and said to the man, "Stretch out your hand." He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.

**6** The Pharisees went out and at once began plotting with the Herodians[^2] against Jesus, how they might destroy him.

**7** Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a large crowd from Galilee followed. People also came from Judea,

**8** from Jerusalem, from Idumea, from beyond the Jordan, and from around Tyre and Sidon. A great crowd came to him when they heard about all he was doing.

**9** He told his disciples to keep a small boat ready for him because of the crowd, so that they would not crush him.

**10** For he had healed many, so that all who had diseases[^3] were pushing forward to touch him.

**11** Whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell down before him and shouted, "You are the Son of God!"

**12** But he strictly ordered them not to make him known.

**13** Jesus went up the mountain and called to him those he wanted, and they came to him.

**14** He appointed twelve[^4] to be with him, so that he could send them out to proclaim the message

**15** and to have authority to drive out demons.

**16** So he appointed the Twelve:[^5] Simon, to whom he gave the name Peter;

**17** James son of Zebedee and John the brother of James, to whom he gave the name Boanerges, which means "Sons of Thunder";

**18** Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,[^6]

**19** and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

**20** Then Jesus went home,[^7] and again such a crowd gathered that they could not even eat.

**21** When his family[^8] heard about it, they went out to take charge of him, for they were saying, "He is out of his mind."

**22** And the scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, "He has Beelzebul,"[^9] and, "He drives out demons by the ruler of the demons."

**23** So Jesus called them over and spoke to them in parables: "How can Satan drive out Satan?

**24** If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.

**25** If a household is divided against itself, that household will not be able to stand.

**26** And if Satan has risen up against himself and is divided, he cannot stand; his end has come.

**27** But no one can go into a strong man's house and carry off his possessions without first tying up the strong man. Then he can plunder his house.

**28** "Truly I tell you, people[^10] will be forgiven everything: their sins and whatever blasphemies they speak.

**29** But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness, but is guilty of an eternal sin."[^11]

**30** He said this because they were saying, "He has an unclean spirit."

**31** Then his mother and his brothers came. Standing outside, they sent word to him, calling for him.

**32** A crowd was sitting around him, and they told him, "Look, your mother and your brothers[^12] are outside looking for you."

**33** He answered them, "Who are my mother and my brothers?"

**34** Looking at those sitting in a circle around him, he said, "Here are my mother and my brothers!

**35** Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother."

[^1]: Literally "the hardness of their heart".
[^2]: Supporters of the family of Herod.
[^3]: Literally "scourges", a word for painful afflictions.
[^4]: Many manuscripts add "whom he also named apostles".
[^5]: Some manuscripts leave out "So he appointed the Twelve".
[^6]: From an Aramaic word meaning "zealous"; Luke calls him "Simon the Zealot" (Luke 6:15).
[^7]: Literally "into a house".
[^8]: Literally "those from him", that is, his relatives or associates.
[^9]: A name for the prince of demons. In the Hebrew Bible, Baal-zebub ("lord of flies") was a god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2).
[^10]: Literally "the sons of men".
[^11]: Most later manuscripts read "eternal judgment".
[^12]: Many manuscripts add "and your sisters".
