**1** When the day of Pentecost[^1] arrived, they were all together in one place.[^2]

**2** Suddenly a sound came from heaven like the rush of a violent wind,[^3] and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.

**3** Tongues like fire appeared to them, divided among them, and one came to rest on each of them.

**4** All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages,[^4] as the Spirit gave them the words to speak out.

**5** Now there were Jews living in Jerusalem, devout men from every nation under heaven.

**6** When this sound[^5] came, the crowd gathered, and they were bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in his own language.

**7** They were amazed and astonished, and they said,[^6] "Look, aren't all these who are speaking Galileans?

**8** Then how is it that each of us hears them in our own native language?

**9** Parthians, Medes, and Elamites; people who live in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,[^7]

**10** Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene; and visitors from Rome,

**11** both Jews and converts to Judaism;[^8] Cretans and Arabs: we hear them speaking in our own languages[^9] about the mighty works of God!"

**12** They were all amazed and perplexed, and they said to one another, "What does this mean?"

**13** But others mocked them and said, "They are full of new wine!"

**14** Then Peter stood up with the Eleven, raised his voice, and declared to them, "Fellow Jews[^10] and all of you who live in Jerusalem, let this be known to you, and listen carefully to my words.

**15** These people are not drunk, as you suppose, for it is only nine in the morning.[^11]

**16** No, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:

**17** 'In the last days,[^12] God says, I will pour out my Spirit[^13] on all people.[^14] Your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your young men will see visions, and your old men will dream dreams.

**18** Even on my male and female servants[^15] in those days I will pour out my Spirit, and they will prophesy.

**19** I will show wonders in the heavens above and signs on the earth below:[^16] blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.

**20** The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and glorious day of the Lord comes.[^17]

**21** And everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.'

**22** "Fellow Israelites,[^18] listen to these words. Jesus of Nazareth was a man commended to you by God through mighty works, wonders, and signs, which God did through him among you, as you yourselves know.

**23** This man was handed over by God's set plan and foreknowledge, and you killed him, nailing him to a cross[^19] by the hands of lawless men.

**24** But God raised him up, freeing him from the pains of death,[^20] because it was not possible for death to keep its hold on him.

**25** For David says about him: 'I saw the Lord[^21] always before me, for he is at my right hand, so that I will not be shaken.

**26** So my heart was glad, and my tongue rejoiced; my body too will live in hope.[^22]

**27** For you will not abandon my life[^23] to Hades;[^24] you will not let your faithful one[^25] see decay.[^26]

**28** You have made known to me the paths of life; you will fill me with joy in your presence.'[^27]

**29** "Brothers,[^28] I can tell you confidently that the patriarch David died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this day.

**30** But he was a prophet, and he knew that God had sworn an oath to him that he would set one of his descendants on his throne.[^29]

**31** He foresaw this and spoke about the resurrection of the Messiah,[^30] that he was not abandoned to Hades,[^31] and his body did not see decay.

**32** God raised up this Jesus, and we are all witnesses of it.

**33** So he has been exalted to the right hand of God,[^32] and he has received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit and has poured out what you see and hear.

**34** For David did not go up into heaven, but he himself says, 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand

**35** until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet."'[^33]

**36** So let the whole house of Israel know for certain that God has made him both Lord and Messiah, this Jesus whom you crucified."

**37** When they heard this, they were cut to the heart, and they said to Peter and the rest of the apostles, "Brothers, what should we do?"

**38** Peter said to them, "Repent,[^34] and each of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

**39** For the promise is for you and for your children, and for all who are far away, as many as the Lord our God will call to himself."[^35]

**40** With many other words he testified and urged them, "Save yourselves[^36] from this crooked generation."

**41** So those who accepted his message[^37] were baptized, and about three thousand people[^38] were added that day.

**42** They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to the fellowship,[^39] to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.

**43** Awe came over everyone, and many wonders and signs were done through the apostles.

**44** All the believers were together and had everything in common.

**45** They sold their property and possessions and distributed the money to all, as anyone had need.

**46** Every day they continued to meet together in the temple with one mind. They broke bread in their homes and shared their food with glad and sincere hearts,

**47** praising God and having the goodwill of all the people. And every day the Lord added to their number[^40] those who were being saved.

[^1]: Greek *pentekoste*, "fiftieth": the Jewish Festival of Weeks, fifty days after Passover (Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12).
[^2]: Many manuscripts read "they were all together with one mind in one place".
[^3]: Greek *pnoe*, "wind, breath", a word related to *pneuma*, "spirit, wind".
[^4]: Literally "in other tongues". Greek *glossa* means both the tongue and a language; verse 3 uses it for the tongues like fire. In verses 6 and 8 the listeners call these languages their own *dialektos*, "language, speech". Verse 11 uses *glossa* again.
[^5]: Greek *phone*, "sound" or "voice": either the sound from heaven (verse 2) or the sound of their speaking.
[^6]: Some manuscripts read "They were all amazed"; many add "to one another".
[^7]: The Roman province of Asia, in the west of what is now Turkey.
[^8]: Greek *proselytoi*, traditionally "proselytes": Gentiles who had converted to Judaism.
[^9]: Greek *glossai*, "tongues", as in verse 4.
[^10]: Literally "Men, Jews". Greek *Ioudaioi* can also mean "Judeans", people of Judea.
[^11]: Literally "the third hour of the day", counting from sunrise.
[^12]: Joel 3:1–5 (2:28–32 in most English Bibles), quoted in verses 17–21 mostly as in the Septuagint. Joel has "After this" where Acts has "In the last days, God says", and Joel puts the old men's dreams before the young men's visions.
[^13]: Literally "from my Spirit", the wording of the Septuagint; also in verse 18.
[^14]: Literally "all flesh".
[^15]: Or "slaves". Joel has "the male and female servants", without "my", and does not have "and they will prophesy".
[^16]: Joel has "in the heavens and on the earth"; "above", "signs" and "below" are added in Acts.
[^17]: Joel has "the great and terrible day". Acts follows the Septuagint, which reads *epiphanes*, "glorious, manifest". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" in verses 20–21 is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^18]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^19]: Literally "fastening (him)", that is, to a cross. *Lawless men*: or "men without the Law", that is, Gentiles.
[^20]: Greek *odines*, "pains", especially the pains of childbirth. The phrase "pains of death" is found in the Septuagint of Psalms 18:5 (18:4 in most English Bibles) and 116:3, where the Hebrew has "the cords of death".
[^21]: Verses 25–28 quote Psalm 16:8–11 from the Septuagint. Here the Hebrew reads "I have set Yahweh always before me". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^22]: The Hebrew has "my whole being rejoices" (literally "my glory") and "will rest secure"; "my tongue" and "in hope" are the Septuagint's wording. *My body*: literally "my flesh", as in verse 31.
[^23]: Greek *psyche*, "soul, life", for Hebrew *nephesh*.
[^24]: Greek *hades*, the realm of the dead, like Sheol. The Hebrew psalm has *Sheol*.
[^25]: Greek *hosios*, "holy, devout", traditionally "your Holy One". The Hebrew has *chasid*, "faithful, loyal".
[^26]: Greek *diaphthora*, "decay, corruption", as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "the pit", a name for the grave.
[^27]: The Hebrew has "the path of life" and "in your presence there is fullness of joy". The psalm continues "at your right hand there are pleasures forever".
[^28]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly; also in verse 37.
[^29]: Literally "to seat (one) from the fruit of his loins on his throne". See Psalm 132:11; 2 Samuel 7:12–16. Many manuscripts read "that from the fruit of his loins, according to the flesh, he would raise up the Messiah to sit on his throne".
[^30]: Greek *christos*, "anointed one".
[^31]: Many manuscripts read "that his soul was not abandoned to Hades".
[^32]: Or "exalted by the right hand of God".
[^33]: Psalm 110:1. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name: "Yahweh says to my lord".
[^34]: Greek *metanoeo*, to turn around in mind and life.
[^35]: Compare Isaiah 57:19 ("to the far and to the near") and Joel 3:5 (2:32 in most English Bibles: "those whom Yahweh calls").
[^36]: Literally "Be saved". *Crooked generation*: compare Deuteronomy 32:5.
[^37]: Literally "his word". Many manuscripts read "those who gladly accepted his message".
[^38]: Literally "souls".
[^39]: Greek *koinonia*, "fellowship, sharing".
[^40]: Literally "added together". Many manuscripts read "added to the church".
