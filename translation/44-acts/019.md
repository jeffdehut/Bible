**1** While Apollos was in Corinth, Paul passed through the inland regions[^1] and came to Ephesus. There he found some disciples

**2** and asked them, "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?" They replied, "No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit."[^2]

**3** He asked, "Then into what were you baptized?" They answered, "Into John's baptism."

**4** Paul said, "John baptized with a baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was coming after him, that is, in Jesus."[^3]

**5** When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.

**6** When Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came on them, and they began to speak in tongues[^4] and to prophesy.

**7** There were about twelve men in all.

**8** Paul went into the synagogue, and for three months he spoke boldly there, reasoning with the people and persuading them about the kingdom of God.

**9** But some became stubborn and refused to believe, and they spoke evil of the Way[^5] in front of the gathering. So he left them and took the disciples with him, and he reasoned every day in the lecture hall of Tyrannus.

**10** This went on for two years, so that all who lived in Asia,[^6] both Jews and Greeks, heard the word of the Lord.

**11** God was doing extraordinary miracles through Paul's hands,

**12** so that even handkerchiefs or aprons[^7] that had touched his skin were carried away to the sick, and their diseases left them, and the evil spirits went out of them.

**13** Some of the Jewish exorcists who went from place to place also tried to use the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits. They would say, "I order you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims!"

**14** Seven sons of a Jewish high priest named Sceva[^8] were doing this.

**15** But the evil spirit answered them, "Jesus I know, and Paul I know about,[^9] but who are you?"

**16** Then the man who had the evil spirit leaped on them, overpowered them all,[^10] and was so much stronger than they were that they fled out of that house naked and wounded.

**17** This became known to everyone who lived in Ephesus, both Jews and Greeks. Fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was held in high honor.

**18** Many of those who had become believers kept coming, confessing and disclosing their practices.[^11]

**19** A good number of those who had practiced magic brought their books together and burned them in front of everyone. They added up their value and found it came to fifty thousand pieces of silver.[^12]

**20** So the word of the Lord grew mightily and prevailed.[^13]

**21** After these things had happened, Paul resolved in the Spirit[^14] to go through Macedonia and Achaia and then on to Jerusalem. "After I have been there," he said, "I must also see Rome."

**22** He sent two of his helpers, Timothy and Erastus, to Macedonia, while he himself stayed on for a while in Asia.

**23** About that time there arose no small disturbance about the Way.

**24** A silversmith named Demetrius, who made silver shrines of Artemis,[^15] brought a great deal of business to the craftsmen.

**25** He called them together, along with the workers in related trades, and said, "Men, you know that our prosperity comes from this business.

**26** And you see and hear that, not only in Ephesus but in almost the whole of Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away a large number of people. He says that gods made by human hands are not gods at all.

**27** There is danger not only that this trade of ours will fall into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be regarded as nothing, and that she whom all Asia and the whole world worship will even be stripped of her majesty."

**28** When they heard this, they were filled with rage and began to shout, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

**29** The city was filled with confusion, and the people rushed together into the theater,[^16] dragging along Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonians who were Paul's traveling companions.

**30** Paul wanted to go in among the people, but the disciples would not let him.

**31** Even some of the Asiarchs,[^17] who were his friends, sent word to him urging him not to venture into the theater.

**32** Meanwhile some were shouting one thing and some another, for the assembly[^18] was in confusion, and most of them did not know why they had come together.

**33** Some of the crowd prompted Alexander,[^19] whom the Jews had pushed forward. Alexander motioned with his hand, wanting to make a defense before the people.

**34** But when they recognized that he was a Jew, they all shouted with one voice for about two hours, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

**35** When the town clerk[^20] had quieted the crowd, he said, "Men of Ephesus, who in the world does not know that the city of the Ephesians is the temple keeper[^21] of the great Artemis and of the image that fell from heaven?[^22]

**36** Since these things cannot be denied, you ought to keep calm and do nothing rash.

**37** For you have brought these men here, though they are neither temple robbers nor blasphemers of our goddess.[^23]

**38** So if Demetrius and the craftsmen with him have a complaint against anyone, the courts are in session, and there are proconsuls.[^24] Let them bring charges against one another.

**39** But if you want anything more, it will be settled in the lawful assembly.

**40** For we are in danger of being charged with rioting because of today's events, since there is no reason we can give to account for this disorderly gathering." When he had said this, he dismissed the assembly.[^25]

[^1]: Literally "the upper parts", the highlands of the interior of Asia Minor.
[^2]: Or "that the Holy Spirit has been given".
[^3]: Many manuscripts read "in Christ Jesus".
[^4]: Or "in other languages".
[^5]: A name for the movement of Jesus' followers, as in 9:2; also in verse 23.
[^6]: The Roman province of Asia, with Ephesus as its chief city; also in verses 22, 26, 27 and 31.
[^7]: Greek *soudaria* and *simikinthia*, words borrowed from Latin: cloths for wiping off sweat, and aprons worn at work.
[^8]: No Jewish high priest of this name is known from other sources.
[^9]: Two different Greek verbs for "know".
[^10]: Literally "both of them". The word usually means "both", but in later Greek it could mean "all". Many manuscripts read "overpowered them".
[^11]: Or "their spells". The Greek word was used of magical practices.
[^12]: Probably drachmas, silver coins each worth about a day's wage for a laborer.
[^13]: Or "So by the power of the Lord the word grew and prevailed."
[^14]: Or "resolved in his spirit", that is, decided.
[^15]: The great goddess of Ephesus, whose temple there was one of the wonders of the ancient world. The Greeks identified her with their goddess Artemis; the Romans called her Diana. The shrines were probably small models of her temple or of a shrine with her image, sold to worshipers.
[^16]: The great open-air theater of Ephesus, where the citizens' assembly met; also in verse 31.
[^17]: Greek *Asiarchai*, leading men of the province of Asia, drawn from its wealthiest families, who held office in the league of its cities and in the worship of the emperor and of Rome.
[^18]: Greek *ekklesia*, the word translated "church" elsewhere, here in its ordinary sense of a citizens' assembly; also in verses 39 and 40.
[^19]: The meaning of the Greek verb here is uncertain: perhaps "instructed", "prompted", or "concluded that it was about Alexander". Many manuscripts read "Some brought Alexander forward out of the crowd".
[^20]: Greek *grammateus*, "secretary", the chief civic official of Ephesus, who drafted the decrees of the assembly and dealt with the Roman authorities.
[^21]: Greek *neokoros*, "temple warden", an honorary title given to a city that kept a great temple.
[^22]: Literally "and of the thing that fell from Zeus" (or "from the sky"): perhaps a meteorite, or an ancient image believed to have come from heaven.
[^23]: Many manuscripts read "your goddess".
[^24]: The plural may be general: there are proconsuls to hear such cases. See 18:12.
[^25]: In most English Bibles, this sentence is verse 41.
