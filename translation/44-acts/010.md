**1** At Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius, a centurion[^1] of what was called the Italian Cohort.[^2]

**2** He was devout and feared God,[^3] along with all his household. He gave generously to the people and prayed to God continually.

**3** At about three in the afternoon[^4] he saw clearly in a vision an angel of God coming to him and saying, "Cornelius."

**4** He stared at him in terror and said, "What is it, Lord?"[^5] The angel said to him, "Your prayers and your acts of charity have gone up as a memorial[^6] before God.

**5** Now send men to Joppa and bring back a man named Simon, who is called Peter.

**6** He is staying with a tanner named Simon, whose house is by the sea."

**7** When the angel who had spoken to him had left, Cornelius called two of his household servants and a devout soldier from among those who attended him.

**8** He explained everything to them and sent them to Joppa.

**9** The next day, as they were on their way and approaching the city, Peter went up on the roof about noon[^7] to pray.

**10** He became hungry and wanted something to eat. While it was being prepared, he fell into a trance.

**11** He saw heaven opened and something like a large sheet coming down, being let down to the earth by its four corners.

**12** In it were all kinds of four-footed animals and creatures that crawl on the earth,[^8] and birds of the sky.

**13** Then a voice said to him, "Get up, Peter, kill and eat."

**14** But Peter said, "Certainly not, Lord! I have never eaten anything common or unclean."[^9]

**15** The voice spoke to him again, a second time: "What God has made clean, you must not call common."

**16** This happened three times, and at once the object was taken up into heaven.[^10]

**17** While Peter was still puzzling over what the vision he had seen might mean, the men sent by Cornelius, who had asked their way to Simon's house, arrived at the gate.

**18** They called out and asked whether Simon, who was called Peter, was staying there.

**19** While Peter was still thinking about the vision, the Spirit said to him, "Look, men[^11] are looking for you.

**20** Get up, go down, and go with them without hesitation,[^12] for I have sent them."

**21** So Peter went down to the men and said, "I am the one you are looking for. Why have you come?"

**22** They said, "Cornelius the centurion, a righteous and God-fearing man who is well spoken of by the whole Jewish nation, was directed by a holy angel to send for you to come to his house and to hear what you have to say."

**23** So Peter invited them in to be his guests. The next day he got up and went with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa went along with him.

**24** The following day he arrived in Caesarea. Cornelius was expecting them and had called together his relatives and close friends.

**25** When Peter arrived, Cornelius met him, fell at his feet, and worshiped him.[^13]

**26** But Peter raised him up and said, "Stand up; I myself am only a human being."

**27** As he talked with him, he went in and found many people gathered.

**28** He said to them, "You yourselves know that it is not lawful for a Jewish man to associate with a foreigner or visit him. But God has shown me that I should not call anyone common or unclean.

**29** That is why I came without objection when I was sent for. So I ask, why did you send for me?"

**30** Cornelius said, "Four days ago, at this very hour, three in the afternoon, I was praying in my house.[^14] Suddenly a man in shining clothes stood before me

**31** and said, 'Cornelius, your prayer has been heard, and your acts of charity have been remembered before God.

**32** So send to Joppa and invite Simon, who is called Peter. He is staying in the house of Simon the tanner, by the sea.'[^15]

**33** So I sent for you at once, and you have been kind enough to come. Now we are all here in the presence of God to listen to everything the Lord[^16] has commanded you to say."

**34** Then Peter began to speak:[^17] "Now I truly understand that God does not show partiality,[^18]

**35** but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.

**36** As for the message he sent to the people of Israel, proclaiming the good news of peace through Jesus Christ (he is Lord of all),[^19]

**37** you yourselves know what happened throughout all Judea, beginning in Galilee after the baptism that John proclaimed:

**38** how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power,[^20] and how he went around doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, because God was with him.

**39** We are witnesses of all that he did in the land of the Jews and in Jerusalem. They killed him by hanging him on a tree,[^21]

**40** but God raised him on the third day and caused him to be seen,

**41** not by all the people, but by us, the witnesses chosen beforehand by God, who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.

**42** He commanded us to proclaim to the people and to testify that he is the one appointed by God to be judge of the living and the dead.

**43** All the prophets testify about him, that everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name."

**44** While Peter was still saying these things, the Holy Spirit fell on all who were listening to the message.

**45** The circumcised believers[^22] who had come with Peter were amazed that the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out even on the Gentiles.

**46** For they heard them speaking in tongues[^23] and praising God. Then Peter said,

**47** "Can anyone withhold the water for baptizing these people, who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?"

**48** So he ordered that they be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.[^24] Then they asked him to stay for some days.

[^1]: A Roman officer in command of about a hundred soldiers.
[^2]: Greek *speira*, a cohort, a unit of several hundred soldiers.
[^3]: Or "was a God-fearer", probably meaning a Gentile who worshiped the God of Israel without becoming a full convert to Judaism; also in verse 22.
[^4]: Literally "about the ninth hour of the day", counting from sunrise; also in verse 30.
[^5]: Or "sir". Greek *kyrios*.
[^6]: Greek *mnemosynon*, the word the Septuagint uses for the memorial portion of an offering, the part burned on the altar (Leviticus 2:2).
[^7]: Literally "about the sixth hour".
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, creatures that crawl".
[^9]: Greek *koinos*, "common", meaning ritually impure; also in verses 15 and 28. *Unclean*: forbidden as food by the Law of Moses (Leviticus 11).
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "and the object was taken up again into heaven".
[^11]: The SBLGNT, following the Byzantine majority text, gives no number. Many modern editions read "three men", and some manuscripts read "two men".
[^12]: Or "without making any distinction".
[^13]: Or "bowed down before him".
[^14]: Literally "From the fourth day until this hour I was praying at the ninth hour in my house". Many manuscripts read "Four days ago I was fasting until this hour, and at the ninth hour I was praying".
[^15]: Many manuscripts add "When he comes, he will speak to you."
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "God".
[^17]: Literally "Opening his mouth, Peter said".
[^18]: Literally "is not one who receives faces". Compare Deuteronomy 10:17.
[^19]: The Greek of verses 36–38 is difficult. Literally "The word that he sent to the sons of Israel, proclaiming good news of peace through Jesus Christ: this one is Lord of all." Some manuscripts leave out "that", giving "He sent the word to the sons of Israel …". *Good news of peace*: compare Isaiah 52:7.
[^20]: Compare Isaiah 61:1.
[^21]: See 5:30.
[^22]: Literally "the believers from the circumcision", that is, Jewish believers.
[^23]: Or "in other languages". Greek *glossai*, "tongues"; compare 2:4, 11.
[^24]: Many manuscripts read "in the name of the Lord".
