**1** Meanwhile Saul, still breathing threats and murder against the Lord's disciples, went to the high priest

**2** and asked him for letters to the synagogues in Damascus, so that if he found any who belonged to the Way,[^1] whether men or women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem.

**3** As he was traveling and approaching Damascus, suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him.

**4** He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, "Saul,[^2] Saul, why are you persecuting me?"

**5** He said, "Who are you, Lord?"[^3] He replied, "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.[^4]

**6** But get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

**7** The men traveling with him stood speechless, hearing the voice but seeing no one.[^5]

**8** Saul got up from the ground, but when he opened his eyes he could see nothing. So they led him by the hand into Damascus.

**9** For three days he was unable to see, and he neither ate nor drank.

**10** There was a disciple in Damascus named Ananias. The Lord said to him in a vision, "Ananias." "Here I am, Lord," he answered.

**11** The Lord said to him, "Get up and go to the street called Straight, and at the house of Judas ask for a man from Tarsus named Saul. He is praying,

**12** and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias come in and lay his hands on him so that he may see again."

**13** Ananias answered, "Lord, I have heard from many people about this man, how much harm he has done to your holy people[^6] in Jerusalem.

**14** And here he has authority from the chief priests to arrest all who call on your name."

**15** But the Lord said to him, "Go, for this man is my chosen instrument[^7] to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the people of Israel.

**16** For I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name."

**17** So Ananias went and entered the house. He laid his hands on him and said, "Brother Saul, the Lord has sent me, Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you were coming, so that you may see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

**18** At once something like scales fell from his eyes, and he could see again. He got up and was baptized,

**19** and after he had eaten, he regained his strength. He stayed for some days with the disciples in Damascus,

**20** and at once he began to proclaim Jesus[^8] in the synagogues, saying that he is the Son of God.

**21** All who heard him were amazed and said, "Isn't this the man who in Jerusalem was destroying those who call on this name? And hasn't he come here for this very purpose, to bring them bound to the chief priests?"

**22** But Saul grew more and more powerful, and he confounded the Jews who lived in Damascus by proving that Jesus[^9] is the Messiah.

**23** After many days had passed, the Jews plotted to kill him,

**24** but Saul learned of their plot. They were watching the gates day and night so that they could kill him.

**25** But his disciples[^10] took him by night and let him down through an opening in the wall,[^11] lowering him in a basket.

**26** When he arrived in Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples, but they were all afraid of him, because they did not believe that he was a disciple.

**27** But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles. He told them how Saul had seen the Lord on the road and the Lord had spoken to him, and how in Damascus he had spoken boldly in the name of Jesus.

**28** So he stayed with them, moving about freely in Jerusalem,[^12] speaking boldly in the name of the Lord.

**29** He spoke and debated with the Greek-speaking Jews,[^13] but they were trying to kill him.

**30** When the brothers learned of this, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus.

**31** So the church[^14] throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was being built up. Living in the fear of the Lord and with the encouragement of the Holy Spirit, it continued to grow in numbers.

**32** As Peter was traveling through all those parts, he came down also to God's holy people who lived in Lydda.

**33** There he found a man named Aeneas, who had been lying in bed for eight years,[^15] because he was paralyzed.

**34** Peter said to him, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your bed."[^16] At once he got up.

**35** All who lived in Lydda and Sharon[^17] saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

**36** In Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha, which translated means Dorcas.[^18] She was always doing good works and acts of charity.

**37** At that time she became sick and died. After washing her body, they laid her in an upstairs room.

**38** Since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples heard that Peter was there and sent two men to him, urging him, "Please come to us without delay."

**39** So Peter got up and went with them. When he arrived, they took him up to the upstairs room. All the widows stood around him, weeping and showing him the tunics and cloaks that Dorcas had made while she was with them.

**40** Peter sent them all outside, knelt down, and prayed. Then he turned to the body and said, "Tabitha, get up." She opened her eyes, and when she saw Peter, she sat up.

**41** He gave her his hand and helped her up. Then he called God's holy people and the widows and presented her to them alive.

**42** This became known throughout Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.

**43** Peter stayed for many days in Joppa with a tanner named Simon.

[^1]: A name for the followers of Jesus and their teaching; also in 19:9, 23; 22:4; 24:14, 22.
[^2]: Greek *Saoul*, the Hebrew form of the name, also in verse 17. Elsewhere Acts uses the Greek form *Saulos*.
[^3]: Or "Sir". Greek *kyrios*.
[^4]: The Textus Receptus (followed by the King James Version) adds "It is hard for you to kick against the goads" (compare 26:14), and begins verse 6 "Trembling and astonished, he said, 'Lord, what do you want me to do?' And the Lord said to him".
[^5]: Compare 22:9 and 26:13–14.
[^6]: Literally "your holy ones", traditionally "saints"; also in verses 32 and 41.
[^7]: Literally "a vessel of choice".
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "proclaim the Messiah".
[^9]: Literally "this one".
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "But the disciples took him".
[^11]: Literally "through the wall". Compare 2 Corinthians 11:32–33.
[^12]: Literally "going in and going out in Jerusalem".
[^13]: Greek *Hellenistai*; see 6:1.
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "So the churches … had peace", with the rest of the verse in the plural.
[^15]: Or "since he was eight years old".
[^16]: Literally "spread for yourself": perhaps "make your bed" or "prepare a meal for yourself".
[^17]: The coastal plain north of Lydda.
[^18]: Both names mean "gazelle", *Tabitha* in Aramaic and *Dorcas* in Greek.
