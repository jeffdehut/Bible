**1** About that time King Herod[^1] laid violent hands on some who belonged to the church.

**2** He killed James, the brother of John, with the sword.

**3** When he saw that this pleased the Jews, he went on to arrest Peter too. This was during the days of Unleavened Bread.[^2]

**4** After seizing him, he put him in prison and handed him over to four squads of four soldiers each to guard him, intending to bring him out to the people after the Passover.

**5** So Peter was kept in prison, but the church was praying earnestly to God for him.

**6** The night before Herod was going to bring him out, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, and guards in front of the door were watching the prison.

**7** Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood there, and a light shone in the cell. He struck Peter on the side and woke him, saying, "Get up quickly." And the chains fell off his hands.

**8** The angel said to him, "Put on your belt and your sandals." He did so. Then he said to him, "Put on your cloak and follow me."

**9** Peter went out and followed him. He did not know that what the angel was doing was really happening; he thought he was seeing a vision.

**10** They passed the first guard post and the second, and came to the iron gate leading into the city. It opened for them by itself, and they went out and walked along one street, and at once the angel left him.

**11** Then Peter came to himself and said, "Now I know for certain that the Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all that the Jewish people were expecting."

**12** When he realized this, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John, who was called Mark, where many had gathered and were praying.

**13** When he knocked at the door of the gateway, a servant girl[^3] named Rhoda[^4] came to answer.

**14** When she recognized Peter's voice, she was so overjoyed that she did not open the gate, but ran in and announced that Peter was standing at the gate.

**15** They said to her, "You're out of your mind!" But she kept insisting that it was so. They said, "It is his angel."

**16** But Peter kept on knocking. When they opened the gate and saw him, they were amazed.

**17** He motioned to them with his hand to be quiet and described to them how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. He said, "Tell these things to James[^5] and the brothers and sisters."[^6] Then he left and went to another place.

**18** When day came, there was no small commotion among the soldiers over what had become of Peter.

**19** Herod searched for him and could not find him. After questioning the guards, he ordered them to be led away.[^7] Then he went down from Judea to Caesarea and stayed there.

**20** Herod had been very angry with the people of Tyre and Sidon. They came to him together, and after winning over Blastus, the king's chamberlain, they asked for peace, because their country depended on the king's country for its food.

**21** On an appointed day Herod put on his royal robes, took his seat on the platform, and made a speech to them.

**22** The people kept shouting, "The voice of a god, and not of a man!"

**23** Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give the glory to God. He was eaten by worms and died.[^8]

**24** But the word of God[^9] continued to grow and spread.

**25** Barnabas and Saul returned when they had completed their service to Jerusalem,[^10] and they took with them John, who was called Mark.

[^1]: Herod Agrippa I, a grandson of Herod the Great, king over Judea from AD 41 to 44.
[^2]: The Festival of Unleavened Bread, the seven days that followed the Passover meal (Exodus 12:15–20; Leviticus 23:5–8).
[^3]: Or "slave girl".
[^4]: The name means "rose".
[^5]: Probably James the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3; Galatians 1:19), who later led the church in Jerusalem (15:13; 21:18), not the James of verse 2.
[^6]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", the believers.
[^7]: That is, to execution.
[^8]: The historian Josephus also tells of Agrippa's sudden death at Caesarea in AD 44, after a crowd hailed him as a god.
[^9]: Some manuscripts read "the word of the Lord".
[^10]: The SBLGNT's punctuation joins "to Jerusalem" with "completed their service" (see 11:29–30). The words can also be read "returned to Jerusalem". Some manuscripts read "from Jerusalem".
