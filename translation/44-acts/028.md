**1** Once we were safely ashore, we learned that the island was called Malta.[^1]

**2** The local people[^2] showed us unusual kindness. They lit a fire and welcomed us all, because it had begun to rain and it was cold.

**3** Paul gathered a bundle of brushwood, and as he was putting it on the fire, a viper came out because of the heat and fastened itself on his hand.

**4** When the local people saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "This man must be a murderer. He has escaped from the sea, but Justice[^3] has not allowed him to live."

**5** But Paul shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm.

**6** They were expecting him to swell up[^4] or suddenly fall down dead. But after they had waited a long time and seen that nothing unusual happened to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.

**7** In the area around that place there were estates belonging to the chief man of the island,[^5] whose name was Publius. He welcomed us and entertained us kindly for three days.

**8** Publius' father happened to be lying sick in bed with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to see him, prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him.

**9** After this had happened, the rest of the people on the island who had diseases also came and were cured.

**10** They honored us in many ways,[^6] and when we were about to sail, they put on board everything we needed.

**11** After three months we set sail in a ship that had spent the winter at the island, an Alexandrian ship with the Twin Brothers[^7] as its emblem.

**12** We put in at Syracuse and stayed there three days.

**13** From there we cast off[^8] and reached Rhegium. After one day a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

**14** There we found brothers and sisters,[^9] and they invited us to stay with them seven days. And so we came to Rome.

**15** The brothers and sisters there had heard about us, and they came as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns[^10] to meet us. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.

**16** When we came into Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself,[^11] with the soldier who guarded him.

**17** Three days later he called together the leading men of the Jews. When they had gathered, he said to them, "Brothers,[^12] though I had done nothing against our people or the customs of our ancestors, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans.

**18** They examined me and wanted to release me, because there was no reason for the death penalty in my case.

**19** But when the Jews objected, I was forced to appeal to Caesar, though I had no charge to bring against my own nation.

**20** That is why I have asked to see you and speak with you, for it is because of the hope of Israel that I am wearing this chain."

**21** They said to him, "We have received no letters from Judea about you, and none of the brothers[^13] who have come here has reported or said anything bad about you.

**22** But we would like to hear from you what you think, for we know that everywhere people are speaking against this sect."[^14]

**23** They arranged a day with him, and they came to him at his lodging in greater numbers. From morning until evening he explained the matter to them, testifying to the kingdom of God and trying to convince them about Jesus from both the law of Moses and the prophets.

**24** Some were convinced by what he said, but others did not believe.

**25** They disagreed among themselves, and they began to leave after Paul had made one statement: "The Holy Spirit was right when he spoke to your ancestors[^15] through Isaiah the prophet,

**26** saying, 'Go to this people and say, "You will keep on hearing, but you will never understand, and you will keep on seeing, but you will never perceive."

**27** For the heart of this people has grown dull, their ears are hard of hearing, and they have shut their eyes. Otherwise they might see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.'[^16]

**28** So let it be known to you that this salvation of God[^17] has been sent to the Gentiles. They will listen!"[^18]

**30** He stayed there two whole years in his own rented lodging,[^19] and he welcomed all who came to see him.

**31** He proclaimed the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance.

[^1]: Greek *Melite*, the island of Malta, south of Sicily. Some manuscripts read *Melitene*.
[^2]: Greek *barbaroi*, people who did not speak Greek; also in verse 4. The people of Malta spoke a form of Punic (Phoenician).
[^3]: Greek *Dike*, "Justice", thought of here as a goddess who punishes wrongdoers.
[^4]: Or "to burn with fever".
[^5]: Literally "the first man of the island", a title known from inscriptions on Malta for its chief official.
[^6]: Or "They gave us many gifts".
[^7]: Greek *Dioskouroi*, "sons of Zeus": Castor and Pollux, twin gods believed to protect sailors. Their figures were carved or painted on the ship.
[^8]: Literally "having taken away", probably the anchors. Many manuscripts read "we sailed around" (or "we followed the coast").
[^9]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 15.
[^10]: Towns on the Appian Way, the road from the south into Rome: the Forum of Appius was about 43 miles (69 km) from Rome, and Three Taverns about 33 miles (53 km).
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "the centurion handed the prisoners over to the commander of the camp, but Paul was allowed to live by himself". With this verse the passage that says "we" ends (see 27:1).
[^12]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly.
[^13]: That is, fellow Jews.
[^14]: Greek *hairesis*, "party, school" (see 24:5).
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "our ancestors".
[^16]: Isaiah 6:9–10, quoted from the Greek Septuagint, which turns the Hebrew commands into a description. The Hebrew reads "Keep on hearing, but do not understand; keep on seeing, but do not perceive. Make the heart of this people dull, make their ears heavy and shut their eyes, so that they may not see with their eyes …"
[^17]: The same expression as in Luke 3:6, which quotes the Septuagint of Isaiah 40:5.
[^18]: Many manuscripts add verse 29: "When he had said this, the Jews left, arguing vigorously among themselves." The SBLGNT does not include it, so this translation has no verse 29.
[^19]: Or "at his own expense".
