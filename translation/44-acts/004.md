**1** While they were speaking to the people, the priests,[^1] the captain of the temple guard,[^2] and the Sadducees[^3] came up to them,

**2** greatly annoyed because they were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.[^4]

**3** They arrested them and put them in custody until the next day, because it was already evening.

**4** But many of those who had heard the message believed, and the number of the men came to about five thousand.

**5** The next day their rulers, elders, and scribes[^5] gathered in Jerusalem,

**6** with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas,[^6] John, Alexander, and all who were of the high priest's family.

**7** They made Peter and John stand in the middle and asked them, "By what power or by what name did you do this?"

**8** Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, "Rulers of the people and elders,[^7]

**9** if we are being questioned today about a good deed done to a sick man, and how this man has been made well,[^8]

**10** then let it be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel: by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead, by him this man stands before you healthy.

**11** He is the stone that you, the builders, treated with contempt, which has become the cornerstone.[^9]

**12** Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven[^10] given among people by which we must be saved."

**13** When they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and realized that they were uneducated, ordinary men,[^11] they were amazed. They recognized that they had been with Jesus.

**14** And seeing the man who had been healed standing with them, they had nothing to say in reply.

**15** So they ordered them to leave the council[^12] and conferred with one another.

**16** "What should we do with these men?" they said. "It is plain to everyone living in Jerusalem that a remarkable sign has been done through them, and we cannot deny it.

**17** But to keep it from spreading any further among the people, let us warn them to speak no more to anyone in this name."

**18** So they called them in and ordered them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus.

**19** But Peter and John answered them, "Judge for yourselves whether it is right in God's sight to listen to you rather than to God.

**20** For we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard."

**21** After threatening them further, they let them go. They could find no way to punish them, because of the people, for everyone was praising God for what had happened.

**22** For the man on whom this sign of healing had been done was more than forty years old.

**23** When they had been released, they went to their own people and reported all that the chief priests and the elders had said to them.

**24** When they heard it, they raised their voices to God with one mind and said, "Master,[^13] you made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them.[^14]

**25** You said by the Holy Spirit, through the mouth of our ancestor David, your servant,[^15] 'Why do the nations rage, and the peoples plot in vain?

**26** The kings of the earth take their stand, and the rulers gather together against the Lord and against his anointed one.'[^16]

**27** For truly in this city[^17] Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles[^18] and the peoples of Israel, gathered together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed,

**28** to do whatever your hand and your plan had decided beforehand should happen.

**29** And now, Lord, look at their threats, and enable your servants[^19] to speak your word with all boldness,

**30** while you stretch out your hand to heal, and signs and wonders are done through the name of your holy servant Jesus."

**31** When they had prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the word of God with boldness.

**32** The whole group of those who believed was one in heart and soul. No one claimed that any of his possessions was his own, but they had everything in common.

**33** With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was on them all.

**34** There was no one in need among them, for all who owned land or houses sold them, brought the money from what was sold,

**35** and laid it at the apostles' feet. It was distributed to each as anyone had need.

**36** Joseph,[^20] whom the apostles called Barnabas (which means "son of encouragement"),[^21] was a Levite, a native of Cyprus.

**37** He sold a field that he owned, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles' feet.

[^1]: Some manuscripts read "the chief priests".
[^2]: Literally "the captain of the temple", the priest in command of the temple police, second in rank to the high priest.
[^3]: A party of the leading priestly families, who did not believe in the resurrection (Mark 12:18).
[^4]: Or "proclaiming the resurrection from the dead in the case of Jesus".
[^5]: Experts in the Law of Moses.
[^6]: Annas had been high priest (AD 6–15) and was still honored with the title. Caiaphas, his son-in-law (John 18:13), was high priest at this time (about AD 18–36).
[^7]: Many manuscripts add "of Israel".
[^8]: Greek *sozo*, "save, heal", related to *soteria*, "salvation", in verse 12.
[^9]: Literally "the head of the corner". Psalm 118:22, applied to Peter's hearers. The Septuagint and Mark 12:10 have "rejected"; Acts has *exoutheneo*, "treat as nothing".
[^10]: Many manuscripts leave out "under heaven".
[^11]: Literally "unlettered men and laymen", that is, without formal training in the Law.
[^12]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^13]: Greek *despotes*, "master, sovereign ruler". Many manuscripts read "Master, you are the God who made".
[^14]: See Exodus 20:11; Psalm 146:6.
[^15]: The Greek of this sentence is difficult. Literally "who, of our father, through the Holy Spirit, of the mouth of David your servant, said". *Servant*: Greek *pais*, "servant" or "child", as in verses 27 and 30. Many manuscripts read "who said through the mouth of your servant David".
[^16]: Psalm 2:1–2, as in the Septuagint, which has "gather together" where the Hebrew has "conspire together". In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh. *His anointed one*: Greek *christos*, "anointed one, Messiah"; compare "whom you anointed" in verse 27.
[^17]: Many manuscripts leave out "in this city".
[^18]: Greek *ethne*, "nations" or "Gentiles", the word translated "nations" in verse 25.
[^19]: Or "slaves".
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "Joses".
[^21]: Greek *paraklesis*: encouragement, comfort, appeal. How the Aramaic name *Barnabas* has this meaning is uncertain.
