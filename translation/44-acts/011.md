**1** The apostles and the brothers and sisters[^1] throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles too had accepted the word of God.

**2** So when Peter went up to Jerusalem, the circumcised believers[^2] criticized him,

**3** saying, "You went to uncircumcised men and ate with them."[^3]

**4** Peter began to explain it to them step by step, saying,

**5** "I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: something like a large sheet coming down, being let down from heaven by its four corners, and it came right to me.

**6** I looked into it closely and studied it, and I saw the four-footed animals of the earth, the wild animals, the creatures that crawl, and the birds of the sky.

**7** I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Get up, Peter, kill and eat.'

**8** But I said, 'Certainly not, Lord! Nothing common or unclean[^4] has ever entered my mouth.'

**9** But the voice answered a second time from heaven, 'What God has made clean, you must not call common.'

**10** This happened three times, and then everything was pulled up again into heaven.

**11** At that very moment three men who had been sent to me from Caesarea arrived at the house where we were staying.

**12** The Spirit told me to go with them without making any distinction.[^5] These six brothers also went with me, and we entered the man's house.

**13** He told us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying, 'Send to Joppa and bring back Simon, who is called Peter.

**14** He will speak words to you by which you and all your household will be saved.'

**15** As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them, just as on us at the beginning.

**16** And I remembered the word of the Lord, how he said, 'John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.'[^6]

**17** If, then, God gave them the same gift that he gave us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I to be able to stand in God's way?"

**18** When they heard this, they fell silent. Then they praised God, saying, "So God has given even to the Gentiles the repentance that leads to life."

**19** Now those who had been scattered by the persecution that broke out over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jews.

**20** But some of them, men from Cyprus and Cyrene, came to Antioch and began speaking to the Greek speakers[^7] too, proclaiming the good news of the Lord Jesus.

**21** The hand of the Lord was with them, and a large number believed and turned to the Lord.

**22** News of this reached the ears of the church in Jerusalem, and they sent Barnabas to Antioch.

**23** When he arrived and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced, and he encouraged them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast hearts,

**24** for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a large number of people were brought to the Lord.

**25** Then Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul,

**26** and when he found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they met with the church and taught a large number of people. It was in Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians.[^8]

**27** During this time some prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.

**28** One of them, named Agabus, stood up and indicated through the Spirit that there would be a severe famine over the whole world.[^9] This took place in the reign of Claudius.[^10]

**29** The disciples decided that each of them, as he was able, would send relief to the brothers and sisters living in Judea.

**30** This they did, sending it to the elders[^11] by the hand of Barnabas and Saul.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", here the believers as a whole; also in verse 29.
[^2]: Literally "those from the circumcision", that is, Jewish believers (compare 10:45).
[^3]: Some manuscripts read "He went to uncircumcised men and ate with them".
[^4]: See 10:14.
[^5]: Or "without hesitation", as in 10:20.
[^6]: See 1:5.
[^7]: Greek *Hellenistai*, "Hellenists", the word used for Greek-speaking Jews in 6:1 and 9:29. Here, after "no one except Jews" in verse 19, it appears to mean Greek-speaking Gentiles. Some manuscripts read *Hellenas*, "Greeks".
[^8]: Greek *Christianoi*, "followers of Christ", "people of Christ".
[^9]: Greek *oikoumene*, "the inhabited world", often meaning the Roman Empire.
[^10]: Roman emperor from AD 41 to 54. Ancient historians report food shortages in several regions during his reign.
[^11]: Greek *presbyteroi*, here the leaders of the church in Jerusalem.
