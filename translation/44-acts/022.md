**1** "Brothers and fathers,[^1] listen now to the defense I make before you."

**2** When they heard him addressing them in Hebrew,[^2] they became even quieter. Then he said:

**3** "I am a Jew, born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel.[^3] I was educated strictly in the law of our ancestors, and I was zealous for God, just as all of you are today.

**4** I persecuted this Way[^4] to the death, binding both men and women and putting them in prison,

**5** as the high priest and the whole council of elders can testify about me. From them I even received letters to our brothers[^5] in Damascus, and I went there to bring those who were there to Jerusalem in chains to be punished.

**6** As I was on my way and approaching Damascus, about noon a bright light from heaven suddenly flashed around me.

**7** I fell to the ground and heard a voice say to me, 'Saul,[^6] Saul, why are you persecuting me?'

**8** I answered, 'Who are you, Lord?'[^7] He said to me, 'I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you are persecuting.'

**9** Those who were with me saw the light,[^8] but they did not hear the voice of the one who was speaking to me.[^9]

**10** I asked, 'What shall I do, Lord?' The Lord said to me, 'Get up and go into Damascus. There you will be told about everything that has been appointed for you to do.'

**11** Since I could not see because of the brightness of that light, those who were with me led me by the hand, and I came into Damascus.

**12** A man named Ananias, who was devout in keeping the law and was well spoken of by all the Jews who lived there,

**13** came to me. He stood beside me and said, 'Brother Saul, receive your sight!' And at that very moment I looked up and saw him.[^10]

**14** Then he said, 'The God of our ancestors has chosen you to know his will, to see the Righteous One and to hear his own voice,[^11]

**15** for you will be his witness to all people of what you have seen and heard.

**16** And now why are you waiting? Get up, be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling on his name.'

**17** When I had returned to Jerusalem and was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance

**18** and saw him saying to me, 'Hurry and leave Jerusalem quickly, because they will not accept your testimony about me.'

**19** And I said, 'Lord, they themselves know that in synagogue after synagogue I imprisoned and beat those who believed in you.

**20** And when the blood of Stephen your witness[^12] was being shed, I myself was standing by, giving my approval[^13] and guarding the cloaks of those who were killing him.'

**21** Then he said to me, 'Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles.'"

**22** Up to this point they listened to him. Then they raised their voices and shouted, "Away with such a man from the earth! He is not fit to live!"

**23** As they were shouting, throwing off their cloaks and tossing dust into the air,

**24** the tribune[^14] ordered Paul to be brought into the barracks. He told them to question him under the lash,[^15] to find out why the people were shouting against him like this.

**25** But when they had stretched him out for the straps,[^16] Paul said to the centurion who was standing there, "Is it lawful for you to flog a man who is a Roman citizen,[^17] without a trial?"

**26** When the centurion heard this, he went to the tribune and reported it. "What are you about to do?" he said. "This man is a Roman citizen."

**27** The tribune came and said to Paul, "Tell me, are you a Roman citizen?" He said, "Yes."

**28** The tribune answered, "I bought this citizenship for a large sum of money." Paul said, "But I was born a citizen."

**29** At once those who were about to question him drew back from him, and the tribune too was afraid when he realized that Paul was a Roman citizen and that he had put him in chains.

**30** The next day, since he wanted to know for certain why Paul was being accused by the Jews, he released him and ordered the chief priests and the whole council[^18] to meet. Then he brought Paul down and had him stand before them.

[^1]: Literally "Men, brothers and fathers", a formal way of addressing an assembly, as in 7:2.
[^2]: Or "in Aramaic". See 21:40.
[^3]: That is, as his pupil. Gamaliel was a leading Pharisee and teacher of the law (5:34).
[^4]: A name for the movement of Jesus' followers (see 19:9).
[^5]: That is, the Jews of Damascus.
[^6]: Greek *Saoul*, the Hebrew form of the name, as in 9:4; also in verse 13.
[^7]: Or "Sir". Greek *kyrios*.
[^8]: Many manuscripts add "and were afraid".
[^9]: Or "did not understand the voice". Compare 9:7.
[^10]: The Greek verb means both "look up" and "see again", as in Ananias' words "receive your sight".
[^11]: Literally "a voice from his mouth".
[^12]: Greek *martys*, "witness", the source of the word "martyr".
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "giving my approval to his death".
[^14]: Greek *chiliarchos*, "commander of a thousand", the Roman commander of the garrison in Jerusalem (see 21:31); also in verses 26, 27, 28 and 29.
[^15]: Literally "to be examined with whips", a way of questioning slaves and others who were not Roman citizens.
[^16]: Or "had tied him up with straps".
[^17]: Literally "a man who is a Roman"; also in verses 26, 27 and 29. Roman law forbade flogging a citizen, above all one who had not been tried and condemned.
[^18]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
