**1** And Saul approved of his killing. On that day a severe persecution broke out against the church in Jerusalem, and all except the apostles were scattered throughout the regions of Judea and Samaria.

**2** Devout men buried Stephen and mourned loudly for him.

**3** But Saul began to ravage the church. Going from house to house, he dragged off men and women and handed them over to prison.

**4** Those who had been scattered went from place to place, proclaiming the good news of the word.

**5** Philip went down to the city of Samaria[^1] and proclaimed the Messiah to them.

**6** The crowds with one mind paid attention to what Philip said, as they heard him and saw the signs he was doing.

**7** For unclean spirits came out of many who had them, shrieking with a loud voice,[^2] and many who were paralyzed or lame were healed.

**8** So there was great joy in that city.

**9** A man named Simon had for some time been practicing magic in the city and amazing the people of Samaria, claiming to be someone great.

**10** All of them, from the least to the greatest, paid attention to him, saying, "This man is the power of God that is called Great."[^3]

**11** They paid attention to him because for a long time he had amazed them with his magic.

**12** But when they believed Philip as he proclaimed the good news about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women.

**13** Even Simon himself believed. After he was baptized, he stayed close to Philip, and he was amazed when he saw the signs and great works of power that were taking place.

**14** When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had accepted the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them.

**15** They went down and prayed for them, that they might receive the Holy Spirit.

**16** For the Spirit had not yet fallen on any of them; they had only been baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.[^4]

**17** Then they laid their hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit.

**18** When Simon saw that the Spirit was given through the laying on of the apostles' hands, he offered them money,

**19** saying, "Give me this authority too, so that anyone I lay my hands on may receive the Holy Spirit."

**20** But Peter said to him, "May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money!

**21** You have no part or share in this matter,[^5] because your heart is not right before God.

**22** So repent of this wickedness of yours, and pray to the Lord[^6] that, if possible, the intent of your heart may be forgiven you.

**23** For I see that you are full of bitter poison and bound by wrongdoing."[^7]

**24** Simon answered, "Pray to the Lord for me yourselves, so that nothing you have said may happen to me."

**25** After they had testified and spoken the word of the Lord, they returned to Jerusalem, proclaiming the good news in many villages of the Samaritans.

**26** An angel of the Lord said to Philip, "Get up and go south[^8] to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza." (This is a desert road.)[^9]

**27** So he got up and went. And there was an Ethiopian[^10] man, a eunuch,[^11] an official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians,[^12] who was in charge of all her treasury. He had come to Jerusalem to worship,

**28** and he was on his way back, sitting in his chariot and reading the prophet Isaiah.

**29** The Spirit said to Philip, "Go up to this chariot and stay near it."

**30** So Philip ran up and heard him reading the prophet Isaiah. He asked, "Do you understand what you are reading?"

**31** He said, "How can I, unless someone guides me?" And he invited Philip to come up and sit with him.

**32** The passage of scripture he was reading was this: "Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer is silent, so he does not open his mouth.

**33** In his humiliation justice was denied him. Who can describe his generation? For his life is taken away from the earth."[^13]

**34** The eunuch said to Philip, "Please tell me, who is the prophet speaking about, himself or someone else?"

**35** Then Philip began to speak,[^14] and starting from this scripture he proclaimed to him the good news about Jesus.

**36** As they went along the road, they came to some water, and the eunuch said, "Look, here is water. What prevents me from being baptized?"[^15]

**38** He ordered the chariot to stop, and both Philip and the eunuch went down into the water, and Philip baptized him.

**39** When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord carried Philip away, and the eunuch did not see him again, for he went on his way rejoicing.

**40** But Philip found himself at Azotus,[^16] and as he passed through, he proclaimed the good news in all the towns until he came to Caesarea.

[^1]: Some manuscripts read "a city of Samaria".
[^2]: The Greek is loosely put together. Literally "For many of those who had unclean spirits, shrieking with a loud voice, came out".
[^3]: Many manuscripts read "This man is the great power of God".
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "in the name of Christ Jesus".
[^5]: Or "in this message". Greek *logos*, "word, matter".
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "pray to God".
[^7]: Literally "in the gall of bitterness and the bond of unrighteousness". Compare Deuteronomy 29:17 (29:18 in most English Bibles) and Isaiah 58:6.
[^8]: Or "go at noon".
[^9]: Or "This is deserted", which may describe the road or Gaza. The words may also be part of what the angel said.
[^10]: Ancient Ethiopia was the kingdom of Kush on the upper Nile, south of Egypt (in what is now Sudan), not modern Ethiopia.
[^11]: Greek *eunouchos*, a castrated man. The word was also used for a high official at a royal court.
[^12]: Greek *Kandake*, the title of the queens of Kush rather than a personal name.
[^13]: Isaiah 53:7–8, quoted from the Septuagint. In the Hebrew, verse 8 begins "By oppression and judgment he was taken away. And who of his generation considered that he was cut off from the land of the living …?"
[^14]: Literally "opened his mouth".
[^15]: The Textus Receptus (followed by the King James Version) adds verse 37: "Philip said, 'If you believe with all your heart, you may.' He answered, 'I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.'" It is not in the SBLGNT or in the other editions it compares, including the Byzantine majority text, so this translation has no verse 37.
[^16]: The Greek name of Ashdod, near the coast.
