**1** In my first book,[^1] Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began to do and to teach,

**2** until the day he was taken up, after he had given instructions through the Holy Spirit to the apostles he had chosen.[^2]

**3** After his suffering he showed himself to them alive, with many convincing proofs. Over a period of forty days he appeared to them and spoke about the kingdom of God.

**4** While he was staying with them,[^3] he ordered them not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for what the Father had promised, which, he said,[^4] "you heard about from me.

**5** For John baptized with water, but not many days from now you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit."

**6** So when they had come together, they asked him, "Lord, is this the time when you will restore the kingdom to Israel?"

**7** He said to them, "It is not for you to know the times or seasons that the Father has set by his own authority.

**8** But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."[^5]

**9** When he had said this, he was lifted up while they were watching, and a cloud took him out of their sight.

**10** While they were staring into the sky[^6] as he went, suddenly two men dressed in white stood beside them.

**11** They said, "Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into the sky? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven."

**12** Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mountain called Olivet,[^7] which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath day's walk away.[^8]

**13** When they arrived, they went up to the upstairs room where they were staying: Peter and John, James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot,[^9] and Judas son of James.[^10]

**14** All of these were devoting themselves with one mind to prayer,[^11] together with some women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers.

**15** In those days Peter stood up among the brothers and sisters[^12] (a crowd of about 120 people[^13] was gathered together) and said,

**16** "Brothers,[^14] the scripture had to be fulfilled that the Holy Spirit spoke long ago through the mouth of David about Judas, who became a guide for those who arrested Jesus.

**17** For he was counted as one of us, and he received his share[^15] in this ministry.

**18** (With the reward he got for his wickedness, this man acquired a field.[^16] Falling headlong,[^17] he burst open in the middle, and all his intestines spilled out.

**19** This became known to everyone living in Jerusalem, so that the field came to be called in their own language[^18] Hakeldamach, that is, Field of Blood.)

**20** For it is written in the book of Psalms: 'Let his camp be desolate, and let no one live in it,'[^19] and, 'May another take his office.'[^20]

**21** So one of the men who have been with us the whole time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,[^21]

**22** beginning from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up from us, one of these must become a witness with us to his resurrection."

**23** So they put forward two men: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also known as Justus, and Matthias.

**24** Then they prayed, "Lord, you know the hearts of all. Show which one of these two you have chosen

**25** to take the place[^22] in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place."

**26** Then they cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was counted with the eleven apostles.

[^1]: Literally "The first account I made". The Gospel of Luke is also addressed to Theophilus (Luke 1:3).
[^2]: Or "to the apostles whom he had chosen through the Holy Spirit".
[^3]: Or "While he was eating with them" or "As he was gathering them together". The meaning of the Greek word is uncertain.
[^4]: "He said" is supplied. The Greek moves from reported speech into Jesus' own words.
[^5]: Literally "to the end of the earth", the phrase used in Isaiah 49:6, which is quoted in 13:47.
[^6]: Greek *ouranos*, which means both "sky" and "heaven", as in verse 11.
[^7]: Greek *Elaion*, "olive grove": the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem.
[^8]: The distance Jewish teaching allowed a person to walk on the Sabbath, about 2,000 cubits, a little over half a mile (about 1 km).
[^9]: Greek *zelotes*, "zealous one". Mark 3:18 calls him "Simon the Cananaean", from an Aramaic word meaning "zealous".
[^10]: Literally "Judas of James", probably meaning "son of James" (also Luke 6:16).
[^11]: Many manuscripts add "and supplication".
[^12]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers". The group included women (verse 14). Many manuscripts read "among the disciples".
[^13]: Literally "names".
[^14]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly.
[^15]: Literally "lot", the word used for casting lots in verse 26.
[^16]: Verses 18–19 are an aside by the narrator, set within Peter's speech. Matthew 27:3–10 tells of Judas' death differently: Judas hanged himself, and the chief priests bought a field with the money he had thrown into the temple.
[^17]: Literally "becoming prone". Some take the Greek word to mean "swelling up".
[^18]: Aramaic, the everyday language of Jerusalem. *Hakeldamach*: traditionally spelled "Akeldama".
[^19]: Psalm 69:26 (69:25 in most English Bibles), applied to one person: the psalm reads "their camp" and "their tents".
[^20]: Psalm 109:8. Greek *episkope*, "office, position of oversight", related to *episkopos*, "overseer".
[^21]: Literally "went in and went out over us", an idiom for living and working among people.
[^22]: Many manuscripts read "to take the share" (Greek *kleros*, "lot", as in verses 17 and 26).
