**1** He came to Derbe and then to Lystra. A disciple named Timothy was there, the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek.

**2** The brothers and sisters[^1] in Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him.

**3** Paul wanted Timothy to go on with him, so he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who lived in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek.

**4** As they went from town to town, they passed on to the people there the decisions reached by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem, for them to observe.

**5** So the churches were strengthened in the faith, and they grew in number every day.

**6** They went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia,[^2] because the Holy Spirit had prevented them from speaking the word in Asia.[^3]

**7** When they came to the border of Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them.[^4]

**8** So they passed by Mysia[^5] and went down to Troas.[^6]

**9** During the night Paul had a vision. A man of Macedonia was standing there, urging him, "Come over to Macedonia and help us!"

**10** As soon as he had seen the vision, we[^7] tried to leave for Macedonia, concluding that God[^8] had called us to proclaim the good news to them.

**11** So we put out to sea from Troas and sailed straight to Samothrace, and the next day to Neapolis,

**12** and from there to Philippi, a leading city of the district of Macedonia[^9] and a Roman colony.[^10] We stayed in this city for some days.

**13** On the Sabbath day we went outside the city gate to the riverside, where we thought there was a place of prayer.[^11] We sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered there.

**14** One of those listening was a woman named Lydia, a dealer in purple cloth from the city of Thyatira,[^12] who worshiped God.[^13] The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what Paul was saying.

**15** When she and her household had been baptized, she urged us, "If you have judged me to be a believer in the Lord, come and stay at my house." And she persuaded us.

**16** One day, as we were going to the place of prayer, we were met by a slave girl who had a spirit of divination.[^14] She brought her owners a great deal of money by telling fortunes.

**17** She followed Paul and the rest of us, crying out, "These men are servants[^15] of the Most High God! They are proclaiming to you[^16] a way of salvation!"

**18** She kept doing this for many days. Paul became greatly annoyed. He turned around and said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her!" And it came out that very moment.

**19** When her owners saw that their hope of making money was gone,[^17] they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the authorities.

**20** They brought them before the magistrates[^18] and said, "These men are throwing our city into an uproar. They are Jews,

**21** and they are advocating customs that we are not permitted to accept or practice, since we are Romans."

**22** The crowd joined in the attack on them, and the magistrates had their clothes torn off them and ordered them to be beaten with rods.

**23** After they had given them many blows, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to guard them securely.

**24** Having received such an order, he put them into the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.

**25** About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.

**26** Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so strong that the foundations of the prison were shaken. At once all the doors flew open, and everyone's chains came loose.

**27** When the jailer woke up and saw the prison doors open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself, because he thought the prisoners had escaped.

**28** But Paul shouted in a loud voice, "Don't harm yourself! We are all here!"

**29** The jailer called for lights, rushed in, and fell down trembling before Paul and Silas.

**30** Then he brought them outside and said, "Sirs,[^19] what must I do to be saved?"

**31** They said, "Believe in the Lord Jesus,[^20] and you will be saved, you and your household."

**32** Then they spoke the word of the Lord[^21] to him and to everyone in his house.

**33** At that hour of the night he took them and washed their wounds, and at once he and all his family were baptized.

**34** He brought them up into his house and set a meal before them, and he was overjoyed, with his whole household, that he had come to believe in God.

**35** When it was day, the magistrates sent the officers[^22] with the message, "Release those men."

**36** The jailer reported these words to Paul: "The magistrates have sent word to release you. So come out now and go in peace."

**37** But Paul said to them, "They beat us in public without a trial, men who are Roman citizens,[^23] and threw us into prison. And now are they throwing us out secretly? Certainly not! Let them come themselves and escort us out."

**38** The officers reported these words to the magistrates. They were afraid when they heard that the men were Roman citizens,

**39** and they came and appealed to them.[^24] They escorted them out and asked them to leave the city.

**40** So Paul and Silas came out of the prison and went to Lydia's house. There they saw the brothers and sisters and encouraged them, and then they left.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 40.
[^2]: Or "the Phrygian and Galatian region".
[^3]: The Roman province of Asia, in the west of what is now Turkey, with Ephesus as its chief city.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "the Spirit".
[^5]: Or "went through Mysia".
[^6]: Alexandria Troas, a port on the Aegean coast near the site of ancient Troy.
[^7]: Here the narrative begins to say "we", and the narrator is now one of Paul's companions. Without explaining the change, Acts speaks this way in 16:10–17, 20:5–21:18 and 27:1–28:16.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord".
[^9]: Or "the first city of the district of Macedonia". Some editions read, by a conjectural change to the Greek, "a city of the first district of Macedonia": the Romans had divided Macedonia into four districts, and Philippi lay in the first.
[^10]: Greek *kolonia*, from Latin *colonia*: a city settled by Roman citizens, often veterans of the army, and governed under Roman law.
[^11]: Greek *proseuche*, "prayer", also used of a Jewish place of prayer or synagogue; also in verse 16. Many manuscripts read "where prayer was customarily made".
[^12]: A city in the province of Asia, known for its dyeing trade. Purple cloth was costly.
[^13]: That is, a Gentile who worshiped the God of Israel without becoming a Jew.
[^14]: Literally "a spirit, a python". The Python was the serpent killed by Apollo at Delphi, the site of his oracle, and the word came to be used of people believed to be inspired to foretell the future.
[^15]: Or "slaves". Greek *douloi*.
[^16]: Many manuscripts read "to us".
[^17]: Literally "had gone out", the same word used of the spirit in verse 18.
[^18]: Greek *strategoi*, the Greek title for the two chief magistrates of a Roman colony (in Latin *duoviri*, also called *praetores*); also in verses 22, 35, 36 and 38.
[^19]: Greek *kyrioi*, "lords, masters", here a respectful form of address.
[^20]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord Jesus Christ".
[^21]: Some manuscripts read "the word of God".
[^22]: Greek *rhabdouchoi*, "rod-bearers": the lictors, attendants of Roman magistrates who carried bundles of rods as a sign of their power to punish; also in verse 38.
[^23]: Literally "men who are Romans"; also in verse 38. Roman law forbade beating a Roman citizen with rods, above all without a trial.
[^24]: Or "apologized to them".
