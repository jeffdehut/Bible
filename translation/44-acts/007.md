**1** Then the high priest asked, "Are these things so?"

**2** Stephen said, "Brothers and fathers,[^1] listen. The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran,[^2]

**3** and said to him, 'Go from your land and from your relatives, and come to the land that I will show you.'[^3]

**4** Then he left the land of the Chaldeans and settled in Haran. From there, after his father died,[^4] God moved him to this land where you are now living.

**5** He gave him no inheritance in it, not even enough to put his foot on, but he promised to give it to him as a possession, and to his offspring after him, even though he had no child.[^5]

**6** God spoke in this way: that his offspring would be foreigners in a land belonging to others, and that they would be enslaved and oppressed for four hundred years.[^6]

**7** 'But I will judge the nation they serve,' God said, 'and after that they will come out and worship me in this place.'[^7]

**8** Then he gave him the covenant of circumcision. And so Abraham became the father of Isaac and circumcised him on the eighth day, and Isaac became the father of Jacob, and Jacob of the twelve patriarchs.[^8]

**9** The patriarchs were jealous of Joseph and sold him into Egypt. But God was with him[^9]

**10** and rescued him from all his troubles. He gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh king of Egypt, who made him governor over Egypt and over all his household.[^10]

**11** Then a famine came over all Egypt and Canaan, with great suffering, and our ancestors could find no food.[^11]

**12** When Jacob heard that there was grain in Egypt, he sent our ancestors there the first time.

**13** On their second visit Joseph made himself known to his brothers, and Joseph's family became known to Pharaoh.[^12]

**14** Then Joseph sent for his father Jacob and all his relatives, seventy-five people in all.[^13]

**15** So Jacob went down to Egypt, and there he died, and so did our ancestors.

**16** They were carried back to Shechem and placed in the tomb that Abraham had bought for a sum of silver from the sons of Hamor in Shechem.[^14]

**17** As the time drew near for the fulfillment of the promise that God had made to Abraham,[^15] the people grew and multiplied in Egypt,

**18** until a different king came to power over Egypt, one who did not know about Joseph.[^16]

**19** He dealt shrewdly with our people and oppressed our ancestors, forcing them to abandon their babies so that they would not survive.[^17]

**20** At that time Moses was born, and he was a fine child in God's sight.[^18] He was cared for three months in his father's house.

**21** When he was abandoned, Pharaoh's daughter took him and brought him up as her own son.

**22** Moses was trained in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and he was powerful in his words and deeds.[^19]

**23** When he was forty years old,[^20] he decided[^21] to visit his brothers, the Israelites.

**24** When he saw one of them being treated unjustly, he defended him and avenged the man who was being mistreated by striking down the Egyptian.

**25** He assumed his brothers would understand that God was rescuing them through him,[^22] but they did not understand.

**26** The next day he came upon two of them as they were fighting, and he tried to reconcile them, saying, 'Men, you are brothers. Why are you wronging each other?'

**27** But the man who was wronging his neighbor pushed Moses aside and said, 'Who made you a ruler and judge over us?

**28** Are you planning to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?'[^23]

**29** When he heard this, Moses fled and became a foreigner in the land of Midian, where he became the father of two sons.[^24]

**30** After forty years had passed, an angel[^25] appeared to him in the wilderness of Mount Sinai, in a flame of fire in a bush.

**31** When Moses saw it, he was amazed at the sight. As he went closer to look, the voice of the Lord[^26] came:

**32** 'I am the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.'[^27] Moses trembled and did not dare to look.

**33** Then the Lord said to him, 'Take your sandals off your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy ground.

**34** I have seen, I have truly seen, the misery of my people in Egypt. I have heard their groaning, and I have come down to rescue them. And now come, I will send you to Egypt.'[^28]

**35** This is the Moses they disowned when they said, 'Who made you a ruler and judge?' God sent him as both ruler and redeemer, by the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the bush.

**36** He led them out, doing wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, at the Red Sea,[^29] and in the wilderness for forty years.

**37** This is the Moses who said to the Israelites, 'God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your own people.'[^30]

**38** He is the one who was in the assembly[^31] in the wilderness, with the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai and with our ancestors. He received living words to give to us.[^32]

**39** Our ancestors were unwilling to obey him. Instead they pushed him aside, and in their hearts they turned back to Egypt.

**40** They said to Aaron, 'Make us gods who will go ahead of us. As for this Moses, who brought us out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has happened to him.'[^33]

**41** That was when they made a calf. They brought a sacrifice to the idol and celebrated what their own hands had made.

**42** But God turned away and gave them over to worship the host of heaven,[^34] as it is written in the book of the Prophets:[^35] 'Did you bring me slaughtered animals and sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness, house of Israel?[^36]

**43** You took up the tent of Moloch and the star of the god Rephan,[^37] the images you made to worship. So I will send you into exile beyond Babylon.'[^38]

**44** Our ancestors had the tent of the testimony[^39] in the wilderness. It was made just as the one who spoke to Moses directed him, according to the pattern he had seen.[^40]

**45** Our ancestors received it in turn and brought it in with Joshua[^41] when they took the land from the nations that God drove out before them. So it was until the time of David.

**46** David found favor with God and asked to find a dwelling place for the God of Jacob.[^42]

**47** But it was Solomon who built him a house.

**48** Yet the Most High does not live in houses made by human hands.[^43] As the prophet says,

**49** 'Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool. What kind of house will you build for me, says the Lord, or what is the place of my rest?

**50** Did my hand not make all these things?'[^44]

**51** You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in heart and ears![^45] You always resist the Holy Spirit. As your ancestors did, so do you.

**52** Which of the prophets did your ancestors not persecute? They killed those who announced in advance the coming of the Righteous One, and now you have become his betrayers and murderers.

**53** You received the Law as ordained by angels,[^46] and yet you have not kept it."

**54** When they heard these things, they were furious[^47] and ground their teeth at him.

**55** But Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked intently into heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God.

**56** "Look," he said, "I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God."

**57** But they shouted at the top of their voices, covered their ears, and rushed at him together.

**58** They drove him out of the city and began to stone him. The witnesses laid their coats at the feet of a young man named Saul.

**59** As they were stoning Stephen, he prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit."[^48]

**60** Then he knelt down and cried out with a loud voice, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them."[^49] When he had said this, he fell asleep.[^50]

[^1]: Literally "Men, brothers and fathers".
[^2]: In Genesis 11:31–12:4 God's call to Abraham comes after the family has settled in Haran. Genesis 15:7 and Nehemiah 9:7 say that God brought him out of Ur of the Chaldeans.
[^3]: Genesis 12:1, without "your father's household".
[^4]: In Genesis, Terah was 70 when Abram was born (11:26) and died at 205 (11:32), and Abram was 75 when he left Haran (12:4). On those figures Terah was still living when Abram left. The Samaritan Pentateuch gives Terah's age at death as 145.
[^5]: *Not even enough to put his foot on*: literally "not even the step of a foot", as in Deuteronomy 2:5. The promise: Genesis 12:7; 13:15; 17:8.
[^6]: Genesis 15:13. Exodus 12:40 gives 430 years.
[^7]: Genesis 15:14, with words from Exodus 3:12, where God says "on this mountain".
[^8]: Genesis 17:9–14; 21:2–4; 25:26; 35:22–26.
[^9]: Genesis 37:11, 28; 39:2, 21.
[^10]: Genesis 41:37–44.
[^11]: Genesis 41:54–57; 42:1–5.
[^12]: Genesis 45:1–4, 16.
[^13]: Literally "seventy-five souls". The Hebrew text of Genesis 46:27, Exodus 1:5 and Deuteronomy 10:22 gives seventy. The Septuagint of Genesis 46:27 and Exodus 1:5, and a Dead Sea Scroll of Exodus (4QExod-b), give seventy-five; in Genesis 46:20 the Septuagint also lists more descendants of Joseph.
[^14]: Genesis tells that Abraham bought the cave of Machpelah near Hebron from Ephron the Hittite (Genesis 23), and that Jacob was buried there (50:13). It was Jacob who bought land at Shechem from the sons of Hamor (33:19), and Joseph's bones were buried there (Joshua 24:32). *Hamor*: Greek *Emmor*.
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "the promise that God had sworn to Abraham".
[^16]: Exodus 1:7–8, as in the Septuagint, which has "a different king" where the Hebrew has "a new king".
[^17]: Exodus 1:10–11, 22.
[^18]: Literally "fine to God", perhaps meaning "a very fine child". Exodus 2:2 in the Septuagint uses the same word, *asteios*.
[^19]: Exodus does not speak of Moses' education. Compare Exodus 4:10.
[^20]: Exodus 2:11 does not give his age. Exodus 7:7 says he was eighty when he spoke to Pharaoh, and Deuteronomy 34:7 that he died at 120. Verses 23, 30 and 36 divide his life into three periods of forty years.
[^21]: Literally "it came up on his heart".
[^22]: Literally "was giving them salvation by his hand".
[^23]: Exodus 2:13–14, as in the Septuagint, which adds "yesterday".
[^24]: Exodus 2:15, 22; 18:3–4.
[^25]: Many manuscripts read "an angel of the Lord", as in Exodus 3:2. Verses 30–34 retell Exodus 3:1–10, where the mountain is called Horeb.
[^26]: Here and in verse 33, "the Lord" stands for God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"), as in Exodus 3:4, 7.
[^27]: Exodus 3:6, which reads "the God of your father". Many manuscripts read "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob".
[^28]: Exodus 3:5, 7–8, 10, shortened. *Groaning* is the word of Exodus 2:24.
[^29]: Greek *Erythra Thalassa*, the Septuagint's name for the Hebrew *Yam Suph*, "Sea of Reeds".
[^30]: Literally "from your brothers". Deuteronomy 18:15; see 3:22. Many manuscripts read "The Lord our God will raise up".
[^31]: Greek *ekklesia*, translated "church" elsewhere in Acts. The Septuagint uses it for Israel gathered at Mount Sinai, "the day of the assembly" (Deuteronomy 9:10; 18:16).
[^32]: Some manuscripts read "to give to you".
[^33]: Exodus 32:1, 23.
[^34]: That is, the sun, moon and stars, worshiped as gods (Deuteronomy 4:19; 17:3).
[^35]: The twelve shorter prophetic books, Hosea to Malachi, which were written on one scroll.
[^36]: Verses 42–43 quote Amos 5:25–27 from the Septuagint. Amos has "sacrifices and grain offerings".
[^37]: The Hebrew of Amos 5:26 reads "You will carry Sikkuth your king and Kiyyun your images, the star of your god". *Rephan*: Greek *Rhaiphan*, a name the manuscripts spell in several ways (*Rhompha*, *Rhephan*, *Rhemphan*). Many manuscripts read "your god Rephan", as in the Septuagint.
[^38]: Amos 5:27 has "beyond Damascus", in the Septuagint as in the Hebrew.
[^39]: Greek *he skene tou martyriou*, the Septuagint's name for the tabernacle, which the Hebrew calls "the tabernacle of the testimony" (Exodus 38:21) and "the tent of meeting".
[^40]: That is, as God directed. Exodus 25:9, 40.
[^41]: Greek *Iesous*, the Greek form of the name Joshua (Hebrew *Yehoshua*). Joshua 3:14–17; 18:1.
[^42]: Some manuscripts read "for the house of Jacob". Compare Psalm 132:5 ("a dwelling place for the Mighty One of Jacob") and 2 Samuel 7:1–16.
[^43]: Literally "in things made by hands". Many manuscripts read "in temples made by hands".
[^44]: Isaiah 66:1–2. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, YHWH ("the LORD"). The Hebrew of verse 2 is a statement: "My hand made all these things."
[^45]: *Stiff-necked*: literally "hard of neck", that is, stubborn, as in Exodus 33:3, 5. *Uncircumcised in heart and ears*: closed to God and unwilling to listen; compare Leviticus 26:41; Jeremiah 6:10; 9:25 (9:26 in most English Bibles). *Resist the Holy Spirit*: compare Isaiah 63:10.
[^46]: Literally "into ordinances of angels". Compare Deuteronomy 33:2, where the Septuagint has angels at God's right hand at Sinai; Galatians 3:19; Hebrews 2:2.
[^47]: Literally "they were sawn through in their hearts"; see 5:33.
[^48]: Compare Luke 23:46.
[^49]: Compare Luke 23:34.
[^50]: A common way of speaking of death.
