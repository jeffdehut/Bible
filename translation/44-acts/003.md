**1** Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three in the afternoon.[^1]

**2** A man who had been lame from birth was being carried there. Every day he was put at the gate of the temple called the Beautiful Gate, to beg for charity from those going into the temple.

**3** When he saw Peter and John about to go into the temple, he asked them for charity.

**4** Peter looked straight at him, as did John, and said, "Look at us."

**5** So the man gave them his attention, expecting to get something from them.

**6** But Peter said, "I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk!"[^2]

**7** Then he took him by the right hand and raised him up. At once the man's feet and ankles became strong.

**8** He jumped up, stood, and began to walk. Then he went with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God.

**9** All the people saw him walking and praising God.

**10** They recognized him as the man who used to sit begging at the Beautiful Gate of the temple, and they were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.

**11** While he was holding on to Peter and John,[^3] all the people ran together to them in the colonnade called Solomon's,[^4] utterly astonished.

**12** When Peter saw this, he said to the people, "Fellow Israelites,[^5] why are you amazed at this? Why do you stare at us, as if we had made him walk by our own power or godliness?

**13** The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God of our ancestors,[^6] has glorified his servant[^7] Jesus. You handed him over and disowned him before Pilate, when Pilate had decided to release him.

**14** You disowned the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be released to you.

**15** You killed the Author[^8] of life, whom God raised from the dead. We are witnesses of this.

**16** It is his name, by faith in his name, that has made this man strong, whom you see and know.[^9] The faith that comes through him has given him this complete health in front of you all.

**17** "Now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your rulers.

**18** But this is how God fulfilled what he had announced beforehand through the mouth of all the prophets, that his Messiah would suffer.

**19** So repent and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,

**20** so that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and that he may send the Messiah appointed for you, Jesus.

**21** Heaven must receive him until the times when all things are restored, which God spoke about long ago through the mouth of his holy prophets.

**22** Moses said,[^10] 'The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your own people.[^11] You must listen to him in everything he tells you.

**23** Everyone who does not listen to that prophet will be cut off from the people and destroyed.'[^12]

**24** And all the prophets who have spoken, from Samuel and those after him, also announced these days.

**25** You are the heirs[^13] of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with your ancestors, when he said to Abraham, 'And in your offspring all the families of the earth will be blessed.'[^14]

**26** God raised up his servant and sent him to you first, to bless you by turning each of you from your wicked ways."[^15]

[^1]: Literally "the ninth hour", counting from sunrise. Many manuscripts begin the verse "Together", taking the word from the end of 2:47.
[^2]: Some manuscripts read "get up and walk".
[^3]: Many manuscripts read "While the lame man who had been healed was holding on to Peter and John".
[^4]: Greek *stoa*, a long porch with a roof held up by columns, along the edge of the temple courts.
[^5]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^6]: Literally "fathers". Exodus 3:6, 15. Some manuscripts read "The God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob", as in Exodus.
[^7]: Greek *pais*, "servant" or "child"; also in verse 26. *Has glorified his servant* echoes Isaiah 52:13 in the Septuagint ("my servant … will be glorified").
[^8]: Or "Leader", "Founder", "Pioneer". Greek *archegos*.
[^9]: The Greek of this sentence is awkward. Literally "And on the faith of his name, this one whom you see and know, his name made strong".
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "Moses said to the ancestors".
[^11]: Literally "from your brothers". Verses 22–23 quote Deuteronomy 18:15, 18–19, with words from Leviticus 23:29. In the Hebrew, "the Lord" is God's personal name, Yahweh. Some manuscripts read "the Lord your God", as in Deuteronomy, and many read "the Lord our God".
[^12]: Greek *exolethreuo*, "destroy utterly". Deuteronomy 18:19 reads "I myself will hold him accountable"; the words "cut off from the people" are from Leviticus 23:29, where the Septuagint uses this verb.
[^13]: Literally "sons".
[^14]: Genesis 22:18; 26:4; 28:14 (compare 12:3). Genesis 22:18 and 26:4 have "nations"; 12:3 and 28:14 have "families".
[^15]: Or "to bless you as each of you turns from your wicked ways".
