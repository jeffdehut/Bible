**1** In those days, as the number of disciples was growing, the Greek-speaking disciples[^1] complained against the Hebrew-speaking disciples,[^2] because their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution of food.[^3]

**2** So the Twelve called together the whole group of disciples and said, "It is not right for us to neglect the word of God in order to serve tables.[^4]

**3** So, brothers and sisters,[^5] choose seven men from among you who are well spoken of, full of the Spirit[^6] and of wisdom, and we will appoint them to this task.

**4** But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word."

**5** What they said pleased the whole group. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, as well as Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert to Judaism[^7] from Antioch.

**6** They presented these men to the apostles, and they prayed and laid their hands on them.

**7** The word of God continued to spread. The number of disciples in Jerusalem grew greatly, and a large number of priests became obedient to the faith.

**8** Stephen, full of grace[^8] and power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people.

**9** But some men from the synagogue called the Synagogue of the Freedmen,[^9] and from the Cyrenians and the Alexandrians, and from those of Cilicia and Asia, rose up and argued with Stephen.

**10** But they could not stand up against the wisdom and the Spirit with which he spoke.

**11** Then they secretly put up some men to say, "We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and against God."

**12** They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes. Then they came upon him, seized him, and brought him before the council.[^10]

**13** They brought forward false witnesses, who said, "This man never stops speaking[^11] against the holy place[^12] and the Law.

**14** For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place and change the customs that Moses handed down to us."[^13]

**15** All who were sitting in the council stared at him, and they saw that his face was like the face of an angel.

[^1]: Greek *Hellenistai*, "Hellenists": here Jewish disciples whose language was Greek, many of them from outside the land of Israel.
[^2]: Greek *Hebraioi*, "Hebrews": here Jewish disciples whose language was Hebrew or Aramaic.
[^3]: Literally "in the daily service" (Greek *diakonia*).
[^4]: Greek *diakoneo*, "serve", related to *diakonia*, "service", in verses 1 and 4. The tables may be tables for meals or tables for money.
[^5]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", addressing the whole community.
[^6]: Many manuscripts read "the Holy Spirit".
[^7]: Greek *proselytos*; see 2:11.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "faith".
[^9]: Greek *Libertinoi*, from Latin *libertini*, "freedmen": Jews who had been slaves, or whose parents had been slaves, and had been set free. The Greek may describe one synagogue or several.
[^10]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "never stops speaking blasphemous words".
[^12]: That is, the temple.
[^13]: Compare Mark 14:58.
