**1** Paul looked straight at the council[^1] and said, "Brothers,[^2] I have lived my life[^3] before God with a completely clear conscience up to this day."

**2** At this the high priest Ananias[^4] ordered those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth.

**3** Then Paul said to him, "God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall![^5] You sit there to judge me according to the law, and yet you break the law by ordering me to be struck?"

**4** Those standing near him said, "Do you insult God's high priest?"

**5** Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he was the high priest. For it is written, 'You must not speak evil of a leader of your people.'"[^6]

**6** Paul knew that one part of them were Sadducees[^7] and the other Pharisees, so he called out in the council, "Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees.[^8] I am on trial concerning the hope of the resurrection of the dead!"[^9]

**7** When he said this, a dispute broke out between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the gathering was divided.

**8** For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, and no angel or spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge them all.[^10]

**9** There was a great uproar, and some of the scribes[^11] who belonged to the Pharisees' party stood up and argued fiercely, "We find nothing wrong with this man. What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?"[^12]

**10** The dispute became so violent that the tribune[^13] was afraid that Paul would be torn to pieces by them. He ordered the troops to go down, take him away from them by force, and bring him into the barracks.

**11** The following night the Lord stood beside Paul and said, "Take courage! For just as you have testified about me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome."

**12** When day came, the Jews[^14] formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse[^15] not to eat or drink until they had killed Paul.

**13** More than forty of them were in this plot.

**14** They went to the chief priests and the elders and said, "We have bound ourselves under a solemn curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul.

**15** So now you and the council must notify the tribune to bring him down to you, as if you intended to examine his case more thoroughly. And we are ready to kill him before he gets near."

**16** But the son of Paul's sister heard about the ambush. He went and entered the barracks and told Paul.

**17** Paul called one of the centurions[^16] and said, "Take this young man to the tribune, for he has something to report to him."

**18** So the centurion took him and brought him to the tribune and said, "The prisoner Paul called me and asked me to bring this young man to you. He has something to tell you."

**19** The tribune took him by the hand, drew him aside privately and asked, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

**20** He said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though it intended[^17] to inquire more thoroughly about him.

**21** But do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty of their men are lying in wait for him. They have bound themselves under a curse not to eat or drink until they have killed him, and now they are ready, waiting for your consent."

**22** So the tribune sent the young man away and ordered him, "Tell no one that you have informed me of this."

**23** Then he called two of the centurions and said, "Get two hundred soldiers ready to go to Caesarea, with seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen,[^18] by nine o'clock tonight.[^19]

**24** Provide mounts too, to put Paul on, and bring him safely to Felix the governor."[^20]

**25** He wrote a letter to this effect:

**26** "Claudius Lysias, to His Excellency[^21] the governor Felix: Greetings.

**27** This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them, but I came with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he was a Roman citizen.[^22]

**28** I wanted to know the charge they were bringing against him, so I brought him down to their council.

**29** I found that he was accused over questions about their law, but was charged with nothing that deserved death or imprisonment.

**30** When I was informed that there would be a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once, and I have also ordered his accusers to state their case against him before you."[^23]

**31** So the soldiers, following their orders, took Paul and brought him during the night to Antipatris.[^24]

**32** The next day they let the horsemen go on with him, and they returned to the barracks.

**33** When the horsemen reached Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor and handed Paul over to him as well.

**34** The governor read the letter and asked what province Paul was from. When he learned that he was from Cilicia,

**35** he said, "I will hear your case when your accusers have also arrived." Then he ordered him to be kept under guard in Herod's praetorium.[^25]

[^1]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council; also in verses 6, 15, 20 and 28.
[^2]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly; also in verse 6.
[^3]: Literally "I have lived as a citizen", that is, conducted my life.
[^4]: Ananias son of Nedebaeus, high priest from about AD 47 to 59.
[^5]: A wall plastered over to hide its weakness. Compare Ezekiel 13:10–16; Matthew 23:27.
[^6]: Exodus 22:27 (22:28 in most English Bibles), as in the Septuagint. The Hebrew has "curse".
[^7]: A party of the leading priestly families, who did not believe in the resurrection (4:1–2; Mark 12:18).
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "the son of a Pharisee".
[^9]: Literally "concerning hope and the resurrection of the dead".
[^10]: Literally "both", that is, the resurrection, and angels and spirits.
[^11]: Experts in the Law of Moses.
[^12]: The Greek sentence breaks off: "But if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him …" Many manuscripts add "let us not fight against God".
[^13]: Greek *chiliarchos*, "commander of a thousand", the Roman commander of the garrison in Jerusalem (see 21:31); also in verses 15, 17, 18, 19 and 22. His name, Claudius Lysias, is given in verse 26.
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "some of the Jews".
[^15]: Greek *anathematizo*, "put under a curse": they called down a curse on themselves if they failed; also in verses 14 and 21.
[^16]: Roman officers each in command of about a hundred soldiers; also in verse 23.
[^17]: Some manuscripts read "as though you intended".
[^18]: Greek *dexiolaboi*, a rare word of uncertain meaning, literally "those who take (in) the right hand": perhaps spearmen, slingers or other lightly armed troops, or perhaps spare horses led by the hand.
[^19]: Literally "from the third hour of the night", counting from sunset.
[^20]: Greek *hegemon*, "governor", here the Roman procurator of Judea, who lived at Caesarea; also in verses 26 and 33. Antonius Felix governed Judea from about AD 52 to about 60.
[^21]: Greek *kratistos*, "most excellent", a title for high Roman officials (also Luke 1:3).
[^22]: Literally "that he was a Roman". Compare 21:31–34 and 22:25–29.
[^23]: Many manuscripts add "Farewell".
[^24]: A town on the road from Jerusalem to Caesarea, some 35 to 40 miles (about 60 km) from Jerusalem.
[^25]: Greek *praitorion*, the official residence of the Roman governor, here a palace built by Herod the Great.
