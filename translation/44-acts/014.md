**1** In Iconium they went together[^1] into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a great number of both Jews and Greeks believed.

**2** But the Jews who refused to believe stirred up the Gentiles and poisoned their minds against the brothers and sisters.[^2]

**3** So they stayed there a considerable time, speaking boldly in reliance on the Lord, who confirmed the message of his grace by granting signs and wonders to be done through their hands.

**4** The people of the city were divided: some sided with the Jews, and some with the apostles.[^3]

**5** When the Gentiles and Jews, together with their rulers, made a move to mistreat them and stone them,

**6** they found out about it and fled to the Lycaonian cities of Lystra and Derbe and the surrounding region,

**7** and there they continued to proclaim the good news.

**8** In Lystra there sat a man who had no strength in his feet. He had been lame from birth and had never walked.

**9** He listened to Paul as he spoke. Paul looked straight at him and saw that he had faith to be healed,[^4]

**10** and said in a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet!" And the man sprang up and began to walk.

**11** When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they shouted in the Lycaonian language, "The gods have come down to us in human form!"

**12** They called Barnabas Zeus, and Paul they called Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.[^5]

**13** The priest of Zeus, whose temple was just outside the city,[^6] brought bulls and garlands to the gates, and he and the crowds wanted to offer sacrifice.

**14** But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their clothes and rushed out into the crowd, shouting,

**15** "Men, why are you doing this? We too are human beings, with the same nature as you. We are bringing you the good news that you should turn from these worthless things[^7] to the living God, who made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them.[^8]

**16** In past generations he allowed all the nations to go their own ways.

**17** Yet he did not leave himself without a witness. He did good, giving you rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, and filling your hearts with food and gladness."

**18** Even with these words, they could hardly keep the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.

**19** But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium and won over the crowds. They stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, thinking he was dead.

**20** But when the disciples gathered around him, he got up and went back into the city. The next day he left with Barnabas for Derbe.

**21** They proclaimed the good news in that city and made many disciples. Then they returned to Lystra, to Iconium, and to Antioch,

**22** strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain true to the faith, and saying that we must go through many hardships to enter the kingdom of God.

**23** In each church they appointed[^9] elders for them, and with prayer and fasting they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

**24** Then they passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.

**25** After speaking the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.

**26** From there they sailed back to Antioch, where they had been commended to the grace of God for the work they had now completed.

**27** When they arrived, they gathered the church together and reported all that God had done with them, and how he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

**28** And they stayed a long time with the disciples.

[^1]: Or "in the same way" (as at Antioch, 13:14–48).
[^2]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", the believers.
[^3]: Here and in verse 14, Barnabas and Paul are called apostles, "those sent out" (compare 13:2–4).
[^4]: Greek *sozo*, "save, heal"; compare 4:9.
[^5]: Zeus was the chief of the Greek gods, and Hermes was the messenger of the gods and the god of speech.
[^6]: Literally "of Zeus, who is before the city", perhaps the name of the local temple or cult.
[^7]: Greek *mataia*, a word often used of idols in the Greek Old Testament.
[^8]: Compare Exodus 20:11; Psalm 146:6; see 4:24.
[^9]: Greek *cheirotoneo*, originally "choose by raising hands", here "appoint".
