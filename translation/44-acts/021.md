**1** After we had torn ourselves away from them, we put out to sea and sailed straight to Cos, the next day to Rhodes, and from there to Patara.

**2** We found a ship crossing over to Phoenicia, went on board and set sail.

**3** We came in sight of Cyprus and, leaving it on our left,[^1] sailed on to Syria. We landed at Tyre, because the ship was to unload its cargo there.

**4** We looked up the disciples there and stayed seven days. Through the Spirit they told Paul not to set foot in Jerusalem.

**5** But when our days there were over, we left and went on our way. All of them, with their wives and children, escorted us until we were outside the city. We knelt down on the beach and prayed,

**6** and we said goodbye to one another. Then we went on board the ship, and they returned home.

**7** We completed[^2] the voyage from Tyre and arrived at Ptolemais. There we greeted the brothers and sisters[^3] and stayed with them for one day.

**8** The next day we left and came to Caesarea.[^4] We went into the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the Seven,[^5] and stayed with him.

**9** He had four unmarried daughters[^6] who prophesied.

**10** While we were staying there for a number of days, a prophet named Agabus[^7] came down from Judea.

**11** He came to us, took Paul's belt, and tied his own feet and hands with it. Then he said, "This is what the Holy Spirit says: In this way the Jews in Jerusalem will bind the man who owns this belt, and they will hand him over to the Gentiles."

**12** When we heard this, both we and the people there begged Paul not to go up to Jerusalem.

**13** Then Paul answered, "What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? For I am ready not only to be bound but even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus."

**14** Since he would not be persuaded, we said no more except, "The Lord's will be done."

**15** After these days we got ready and started up to Jerusalem.

**16** Some of the disciples from Caesarea also went with us and brought us to Mnason of Cyprus, a disciple from the early days, with whom we were to stay.[^8]

**17** When we arrived in Jerusalem, the brothers and sisters welcomed us gladly.

**18** The next day Paul went with us to see James, and all the elders were there.

**19** After greeting them, Paul reported one by one the things that God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry.

**20** When they heard it, they praised God.[^9] Then they said to him, "You see, brother, how many thousands[^10] of believers there are among the Jews, and they are all zealous for the law.

**21** They have been told about you that you teach all the Jews who live among the Gentiles to abandon Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children or live by our customs.

**22** What then is to be done? They will certainly hear that you have come.[^11]

**23** So do what we tell you. We have four men who are under a vow.

**24** Take them, purify yourself along with them, and pay their expenses so that they can have their heads shaved.[^12] Then everyone will know that there is nothing in what they have been told about you, but that you yourself also live in observance of the law.

**25** As for the Gentiles who have believed, we have sent word of our decision that they should keep themselves[^13] from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality."[^14]

**26** Then Paul took the men, and the next day he purified himself along with them and went into the temple. There he gave notice of when the days of purification would be completed and the offering would be made for each one of them.

**27** When the seven days were almost over, the Jews from Asia saw him in the temple. They stirred up the whole crowd and seized him,

**28** shouting, "Fellow Israelites,[^15] help! This is the man who teaches everyone everywhere against our people, the law and this place. What is more, he has even brought Greeks into the temple[^16] and has defiled this holy place."

**29** For they had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian in the city with him, and they supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple.

**30** The whole city was stirred up, and the people came running together. They seized Paul and dragged him out of the temple, and at once the gates were shut.

**31** While they were trying to kill him, word reached the tribune[^17] of the cohort[^18] that all Jerusalem was in an uproar.

**32** At once he took soldiers and centurions[^19] and ran down to them. When the people saw the tribune and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.

**33** Then the tribune came up, arrested him, and ordered him to be bound with two chains. He asked who he was and what he had done.

**34** Some in the crowd shouted one thing and some another. Since the tribune could not find out the facts because of the uproar, he ordered Paul to be taken into the barracks.[^20]

**35** When Paul reached the steps, he actually had to be carried by the soldiers because of the violence of the crowd,

**36** for the mass of people kept following and shouting, "Away with him!"

**37** As Paul was about to be brought into the barracks, he said to the tribune, "May I say something to you?" The tribune replied, "Do you know Greek?

**38** Then aren't you the Egyptian who stirred up a revolt some time ago and led the four thousand men of the Assassins[^21] out into the wilderness?"

**39** Paul answered, "I am a Jew, from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no ordinary city. Please let me speak to the people."

**40** When the tribune gave him permission, Paul stood on the steps and motioned with his hand to the people. When they had become completely silent, he addressed them in Hebrew,[^22] saying:

[^1]: That is, they passed to the south of the island.
[^2]: Or "continued".
[^3]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 17.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "The next day those who were with Paul left and came to Caesarea."
[^5]: The seven men appointed in 6:1–6, of whom Philip was one (6:5; 8:5–40).
[^6]: Literally "four virgin daughters".
[^7]: See 11:28.
[^8]: Or "and brought with them Mnason of Cyprus, a disciple from the early days, with whom we were to stay".
[^9]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord".
[^10]: Literally "how many myriads", that is, tens of thousands.
[^11]: Many manuscripts read "A crowd will certainly gather, for they will hear that you have come."
[^12]: The vow was probably a Nazirite vow, which ended with offerings in the temple and the shaving of the head (Numbers 6:13–21).
[^13]: Many manuscripts read "that they need observe nothing of this kind, except to keep themselves".
[^14]: See 15:20, 29.
[^15]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^16]: Gentiles were forbidden, on pain of death, to pass the barrier into the inner courts of the temple. Stone notices giving this warning in Greek have been found in Jerusalem.
[^17]: Greek *chiliarchos*, "commander of a thousand": a Roman military tribune, here the commander of the garrison in Jerusalem; also in verses 32, 33 and 37. His name is given in 23:26.
[^18]: Greek *speira*, a Roman military unit, nominally 600 soldiers.
[^19]: Roman officers each in command of about a hundred soldiers.
[^20]: The barracks of the Roman garrison, probably in the Antonia fortress at the northwest corner of the temple courts, which was reached by steps from the courts; also in verse 37.
[^21]: Greek *sikarioi*, from Latin *sicarii*, "dagger-men": Jewish rebels who murdered their opponents with daggers hidden under their clothes. The Jewish historian Josephus also tells of an Egyptian false prophet who gathered a large following near Jerusalem when Felix was governor.
[^22]: Or "in Aramaic". Literally "in the Hebrew dialect". Aramaic was the everyday language of the Jews of Judea and closely related to Hebrew.
