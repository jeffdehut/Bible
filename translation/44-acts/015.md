**1** Some men came down from Judea and began teaching the brothers and sisters,[^1] "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved."

**2** Paul and Barnabas had no small dispute and debate with them, so they[^2] appointed Paul and Barnabas and some others of them to go up to Jerusalem, to the apostles and the elders, about this question.

**3** So they were sent on their way by the church, and they passed through both Phoenicia and Samaria, telling in detail how the Gentiles had turned to God. They brought great joy to all the brothers and sisters.

**4** When they arrived in Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the church and the apostles and the elders, and they reported all that God had done with them.

**5** But some of the believers who belonged to the party[^3] of the Pharisees stood up and said, "They must be circumcised and ordered to keep the law of Moses."

**6** The apostles and the elders met together to look into this matter.

**7** After much debate, Peter stood up and said to them, "Brothers,[^4] you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that the Gentiles would hear the message of the good news through my mouth and believe.[^5]

**8** And God, who knows the heart, testified for them by giving them the Holy Spirit, just as he did to us.

**9** He made no distinction between us and them, for he cleansed their hearts by faith.

**10** So now, why are you putting God to the test by placing on the neck of the disciples a yoke that neither our ancestors nor we have been able to bear?

**11** No, we believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in the same way as they are."

**12** The whole gathering fell silent, and they listened to Barnabas and Paul as they described all the signs and wonders that God had done among the Gentiles through them.

**13** When they had finished, James[^6] responded, "Brothers, listen to me.

**14** Simeon[^7] has described how God first showed his care for the Gentiles by taking from among them a people for his name.

**15** The words of the prophets agree with this, as it is written:

**16** 'After this I will return,[^8] and I will rebuild the fallen shelter[^9] of David. I will rebuild its ruins, and I will set it up again,

**17** so that the rest of humankind may seek the Lord, and all the nations[^10] who are called by my name,[^11] says the Lord,[^12] who makes these things

**18** known from long ago.'[^13]

**19** Therefore my judgment is that we should not make things difficult for those from among the Gentiles who are turning to God,

**20** but that we should write to them to abstain from things polluted by idols,[^14] from sexual immorality,[^15] from what has been strangled,[^16] and from blood.

**21** For from ancient generations Moses has had people in every town who proclaim him, since he is read aloud in the synagogues every Sabbath."

**22** Then the apostles and the elders, with the whole church, decided to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas. They chose Judas, called Barsabbas, and Silas, men who were leaders among the brothers and sisters,

**23** and they sent this letter with them:[^17] "The apostles and the elders, your brothers,[^18] to the brothers and sisters of Gentile birth in Antioch, Syria and Cilicia: Greetings.

**24** We have heard that some who went out from us, without any instructions from us, have troubled you with what they said, unsettling your minds.[^19]

**25** So we have decided unanimously to choose men and send them to you with our dear friends Barnabas and Paul,

**26** men who have risked their lives[^20] for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**27** We have therefore sent Judas and Silas, who will tell you the same things by word of mouth.

**28** For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay on you no greater burden than these necessary things:

**29** that you abstain from food sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality. If you keep yourselves from these, you will do well. Farewell."

**30** So when they had been sent off, they went down to Antioch. They gathered the whole community together and delivered the letter.

**31** When they had read it, they rejoiced at its encouragement.

**32** Judas and Silas, who were prophets themselves, encouraged the brothers and sisters with many words and strengthened them.

**33** After they had spent some time there, they were sent off in peace by the brothers and sisters to those who had sent them.[^21]

**35** But Paul and Barnabas stayed on in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the good news of the word of the Lord, along with many others.

**36** After some days Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us go back and visit the brothers and sisters in every town where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, and see how they are doing."

**37** Barnabas wanted to take John, who was called Mark, with them too.

**38** But Paul thought it best not to take with them someone who had deserted them in Pamphylia and had not gone on with them to the work.[^22]

**39** The disagreement became so sharp that they parted from one another. Barnabas took Mark and sailed away to Cyprus,

**40** but Paul chose Silas and set out, after the brothers and sisters had commended him to the grace of the Lord.[^23]

**41** He went through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.

[^1]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verses 3, 22, 23, 32, 33, 36 and 40.
[^2]: That is, the believers in Antioch (14:26–28).
[^3]: Greek *hairesis*, "party, school of thought".
[^4]: Literally "Men, brothers", a formal way of addressing an assembly; also in verse 13.
[^5]: Peter refers to the conversion of Cornelius and his household (10:1–11:18).
[^6]: James the brother of Jesus, leader of the church in Jerusalem (12:17; Galatians 1:19).
[^7]: That is, Simon Peter. *Simeon* is the Hebrew form of the name.
[^8]: Verses 16–18 quote Amos 9:11–12 from the Greek Septuagint. The Hebrew of Amos 9:11 begins "On that day I will raise up the fallen shelter of David"; the opening "After this I will return" may echo Jeremiah 12:15.
[^9]: Greek *skene*, "tent, booth", the Septuagint's word for Hebrew *sukkah*, "shelter, hut". The royal house of David is pictured as a shelter that has fallen down.
[^10]: Or "all the Gentiles". The Greek word is the same.
[^11]: Literally "over whom my name has been called", that is, who belong to me. The Hebrew of Amos 9:12 reads "so that they may take possession of what is left of Edom and of all the nations that are called by my name". The Septuagint follows a Hebrew text that read *adam*, "humankind", for *edom*, "Edom", and *yidreshu*, "they will seek", for *yirshu*, "they will take possession". Most manuscripts of the Septuagint have no object after "seek"; Acts has "seek the Lord".
[^12]: In the Hebrew of Amos, "says the Lord" is "declares Yahweh", with God's personal name, Yahweh.
[^13]: These words are not in Amos; compare Isaiah 45:21. Many manuscripts read "who does all these things. Known to God from long ago are all his works."
[^14]: That is, food sacrificed to idols (verse 29).
[^15]: Greek *porneia*, sexual immorality of any kind.
[^16]: That is, the meat of animals killed without draining their blood (Leviticus 17:13–14).
[^17]: Literally "writing by their hand".
[^18]: Or "The apostles and the elder brothers". Many manuscripts read "The apostles, the elders and the brothers".
[^19]: Many manuscripts add "saying that you must be circumcised and keep the law".
[^20]: Literally "who have handed over their lives". Or "who have devoted their lives".
[^21]: Many manuscripts read "to the apostles". The Textus Receptus (followed by the King James Version) adds verse 34: "But Silas decided to stay there." It is not in the SBLGNT or in the Byzantine majority text, so this translation has no verse 34.
[^22]: See 13:13.
[^23]: Many manuscripts read "the grace of God".
