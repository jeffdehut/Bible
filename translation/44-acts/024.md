**1** Five days later the high priest Ananias came down with some elders and an advocate[^1] named Tertullus, and they brought their charges against Paul before the governor.[^2]

**2** When Paul had been called in, Tertullus began to accuse him. He said: "Through you we enjoy great peace, and by your foresight reforms are being made for this nation.

**3** In every way and everywhere, most excellent Felix,[^3] we acknowledge this with all gratitude.

**4** But so as not to take up any more of your time, I ask you in your kindness to hear us briefly.

**5** For we have found this man to be a pest. He stirs up riots among all the Jews throughout the world, and he is a ringleader of the sect[^4] of the Nazarenes.[^5]

**6** He even tried to desecrate the temple, and so we seized him.[^6]

**8** By examining him yourself, you will be able to learn from him[^7] about all these things of which we accuse him."

**9** The Jews also joined in the attack, asserting that these things were so.

**10** When the governor motioned to him to speak, Paul answered: "I know that for many years you have been a judge over this nation, so I gladly make my defense.

**11** You can find out for yourself that it is not more than twelve days since I went up to worship in Jerusalem.

**12** They did not find me arguing with anyone in the temple or stirring up a crowd, either in the synagogues or anywhere in the city,

**13** and they cannot prove to you the charges they are now bringing against me.

**14** But this I admit to you: I worship the God of our ancestors according to the Way,[^8] which they call a sect. I believe everything that is in accordance with the Law and that is written in the Prophets,

**15** and I have a hope in God, which these men themselves also look for, that there will be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous.

**16** So I too always do my best to have a clear conscience before God and before people.

**17** After several years I came to bring gifts for the poor to my nation, and to present offerings.[^9]

**18** While I was doing this, they found me in the temple, after I had been purified, with no crowd and no disturbance.

**19** But some Jews from Asia were there,[^10] and they are the ones who ought to be here before you to bring charges, if they have anything against me.

**20** Or else let these men themselves say what crime they found when I stood before the council,[^11]

**21** unless it is this one thing that I shouted as I stood among them: 'It is about the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial before you today.'"

**22** But Felix, who knew the facts about the Way rather well, adjourned their case. "When Lysias the tribune[^12] comes down," he said, "I will decide your case."

**23** He ordered the centurion to keep Paul in custody, but to give him some freedom and not to prevent any of his friends from taking care of his needs.[^13]

**24** Some days later Felix came with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish.[^14] He sent for Paul and listened to him speak about faith in Christ Jesus.

**25** As Paul talked about righteousness, self-control and the coming judgment, Felix became frightened and said, "That is enough for now. You may go. When I find the time, I will send for you."

**26** At the same time he was hoping that Paul would give him money,[^15] so he sent for him often and talked with him.

**27** When two years had passed, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus.[^16] And because Felix wanted to do the Jews a favor, he left Paul in prison.

[^1]: Greek *rhetor*, "orator", here a professional advocate.
[^2]: Greek *hegemon*, the Roman governor of Judea, Felix (see 23:24); also in verse 10.
[^3]: Greek *kratistos*, "most excellent", a title for high Roman officials (see 23:26).
[^4]: Greek *hairesis*, "party, school", the word used of the Pharisees in 15:5 and 26:5; also in verse 14.
[^5]: That is, the followers of Jesus of Nazareth.
[^6]: In the Greek, verses 5–6 form one sentence without a main verb. The Textus Receptus (followed by the King James Version) adds: "and we wanted to judge him according to our law. (7) But Lysias the tribune came and with great force took him away out of our hands, (8) ordering his accusers to come before you." These words are not in the SBLGNT or in the Byzantine majority text, so this translation has no verse 7, and verse 8 begins with the words that follow them.
[^7]: That is, from Paul. With the words added in the Textus Receptus (see the previous note), "him" would refer to Lysias.
[^8]: A name for the movement of Jesus' followers (see 19:9); also in verse 22.
[^9]: Compare Romans 15:25–28.
[^10]: The Greek sentence is incomplete. Literally "But some Jews from Asia, who ought to be here before you …"
[^11]: Greek *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council.
[^12]: Greek *chiliarchos*, "commander of a thousand", the Roman commander of the garrison in Jerusalem (see 21:31; 23:26).
[^13]: Many manuscripts add "or visiting him".
[^14]: Drusilla was a daughter of Herod Agrippa I (12:1) and a sister of Agrippa II and Bernice (25:13).
[^15]: Many manuscripts add "so that he would release him".
[^16]: Porcius Festus governed Judea from about AD 60 to 62.
