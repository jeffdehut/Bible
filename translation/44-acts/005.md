**1** But a man named Ananias, with his wife Sapphira, sold a piece of property.

**2** With his wife's knowledge he kept back some of the money for himself,[^1] and he brought only part of it and laid it at the apostles' feet.

**3** Peter said, "Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart, so that you lied to the Holy Spirit and kept back some of the money from the land?

**4** While it remained unsold, didn't it remain yours? And after it was sold, wasn't the money at your disposal? Why have you planned this deed in your heart? You have not lied to people but to God."

**5** When Ananias heard these words, he fell down and died. And great fear came on all who heard of it.

**6** The young men got up, wrapped up his body, carried him out, and buried him.

**7** About three hours later his wife came in, not knowing what had happened.

**8** Peter said to her, "Tell me, did you sell the land for this much?" "Yes," she said, "for that much."

**9** Then Peter said to her, "How could you agree together to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out."

**10** At once she fell down at his feet and died. When the young men came in, they found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her beside her husband.

**11** Great fear came on the whole church[^2] and on all who heard about these things.

**12** Many signs and wonders were done among the people through the hands of the apostles. And they were all together with one mind in Solomon's Colonnade.[^3]

**13** None of the others dared to join them, but the people held them in high honor.

**14** More and more people believed in the Lord[^4] and were added to them, crowds of both men and women.

**15** As a result, they even carried the sick out into the streets and laid them on cots and mats, so that when Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall on some of them.

**16** Crowds also gathered from the towns around Jerusalem, bringing people who were sick or tormented by unclean spirits, and they were all healed.

**17** Then the high priest and all who were with him, that is, the party of the Sadducees, took action, filled with jealousy.

**18** They arrested the apostles and put them in the public jail.

**19** But during the night an angel of the Lord opened the doors of the prison, brought them out, and said,

**20** "Go, stand in the temple, and tell the people the whole message of this life."[^5]

**21** When they heard this, they went into the temple at daybreak and began to teach. When the high priest and those with him arrived, they called together the council, the full assembly of the elders of Israel,[^6] and sent to the jail to have the apostles brought.

**22** But when the officers got there, they did not find them in the prison. So they came back and reported,

**23** "We found the jail securely locked and the guards standing at the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside."

**24** When the captain of the temple guard and the chief priests[^7] heard this report, they were perplexed about them, wondering what would come of this.

**25** Then someone came and told them, "Look, the men you put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people."

**26** Then the captain went with the officers and brought them, but without force, because they were afraid of being stoned by the people.

**27** They brought them in and made them stand before the council. The high priest questioned them,

**28** saying, "We gave you strict orders[^8] not to teach in this name. Yet here you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and you are determined to bring this man's blood on us."[^9]

**29** But Peter and the apostles answered, "We must obey God rather than people.

**30** The God of our ancestors raised up Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree.[^10]

**31** God exalted him to his right hand[^11] as Leader[^12] and Savior, to give repentance and forgiveness of sins to Israel.

**32** We are witnesses of these things, and so is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him."

**33** When they heard this, they were furious[^13] and wanted to kill them.

**34** But a Pharisee[^14] in the council named Gamaliel, a teacher of the Law respected by all the people, stood up and ordered the men[^15] to be put outside for a short time.

**35** Then he said to them, "Fellow Israelites,[^16] be careful about what you intend to do to these men.

**36** For some time ago Theudas rose up, claiming to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was killed, and all who followed him were scattered and came to nothing.[^17]

**37** After him, Judas the Galilean rose up in the days of the census[^18] and drew people away to follow him. He too perished, and all who followed him were scattered.

**38** So in this case I tell you, keep away from these men and leave them alone. For if this plan or this work is of human origin, it will fail.

**39** But if it is from God, you will not be able to overthrow them. You might even find yourselves fighting against God." They were persuaded by him.

**40** They called the apostles in and had them flogged. Then they ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go.

**41** So they left the council, rejoicing that they had been counted worthy to suffer disgrace for the Name.[^19]

**42** Every day, in the temple and from house to house, they did not stop teaching and proclaiming the good news that the Messiah is Jesus.[^20]

[^1]: Greek *nosphizo*, "set aside for oneself", the verb the Septuagint uses for Achan's theft in Joshua 7:1; also in verse 3.
[^2]: Greek *ekklesia*, "assembly", the community of believers. This is the first time Acts uses the word for them.
[^3]: See 3:11.
[^4]: Or "were added to the Lord".
[^5]: Literally "all the words of this life".
[^6]: Literally "the council and all the senate of the sons of Israel". The council is the *synedrion*, the Sanhedrin, the supreme Jewish council. The second phrase (Greek *gerousia*, "senate, council of elders") probably describes the council itself, but it may mean a wider body.
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "the priest, the captain of the temple guard, and the chief priests".
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "Did we not give you strict orders …?"
[^9]: That is, to hold us guilty of his death.
[^10]: Literally "wood", meaning the cross. The words recall Deuteronomy 21:22–23.
[^11]: Or "exalted him by his right hand".
[^12]: Greek *archegos*, as in 3:15.
[^13]: Literally "they were sawn through".
[^14]: The Pharisees were a Jewish party known for strict keeping of the Law and its traditions. Gamaliel is later named as Paul's teacher (22:3).
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "the apostles".
[^16]: Literally "Men, Israelites".
[^17]: The historian Josephus tells of a Theudas who led a movement when Fadus was governor of Judea (about AD 44–46), some years after this speech. Gamaliel may mean that man or another of the same name.
[^18]: Probably the Roman census of AD 6, when Judea came under direct Roman rule. Josephus tells of a revolt against it led by Judas the Galilean.
[^19]: That is, the name of Jesus. Many manuscripts read "for the name of Jesus".
[^20]: Or "proclaiming the good news of the Messiah, Jesus".
