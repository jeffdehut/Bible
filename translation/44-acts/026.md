**1** Agrippa said to Paul, "You have permission to speak for yourself." Then Paul stretched out his hand and began his defense:

**2** "King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate that it is before you that I am to make my defense today against all the accusations of the Jews,

**3** especially because you are an expert in all the customs and questions of the Jews. So I beg you to listen to me patiently.

**4** All the Jews know the way I have lived from my youth, a life spent from the beginning among my own people and in Jerusalem.

**5** They have known me for a long time, and they could testify, if they were willing, that I lived as a Pharisee, according to the strictest party[^1] of our religion.

**6** And now I stand here on trial because of my hope in the promise God made to our ancestors,

**7** the promise that our twelve tribes hope to see fulfilled as they earnestly worship God night and day. It is because of this hope, Your Majesty, that I am accused by Jews!

**8** Why do any of you think it incredible that God raises the dead?

**9** I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things to oppose the name of Jesus of Nazareth.

**10** And that is what I did in Jerusalem. With authority I had received from the chief priests, I locked up many of God's holy people[^2] in prison, and when they were being put to death, I cast my vote[^3] against them.

**11** In all the synagogues I often punished them and tried to force[^4] them to blaspheme. In my furious rage against them, I pursued them even to foreign cities.

**12** On one of these journeys I was traveling to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests.

**13** At midday, Your Majesty, as I was on the road, I saw a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those who were traveling with me.

**14** We all fell to the ground, and I heard a voice saying to me in Hebrew,[^5] 'Saul,[^6] Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'[^7]

**15** I said, 'Who are you, Lord?'[^8] And the Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**16** But get up and stand on your feet.[^9] For I have appeared to you for this purpose: to appoint you as a servant and a witness of what you have seen of me and of the ways in which I will appear to you.

**17** I will rescue you[^10] from your own people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you

**18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God,[^11] and so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are made holy by faith in me.'

**19** So then, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the vision from heaven.

**20** I declared first to those in Damascus, and then to those in Jerusalem and throughout the whole region of Judea, and to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God and do deeds that show their repentance.

**21** That is why the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.

**22** But I have had help from God to this very day, and so I stand here testifying to small and great alike. I say nothing beyond what the prophets and Moses said would happen:

**23** that the Messiah would suffer, and that, as the first to rise from the dead, he would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles."

**24** While Paul was saying this in his defense, Festus said in a loud voice, "You are out of your mind, Paul! Your great learning is driving you mad!"

**25** But Paul said, "I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus.[^12] What I am saying is true and sensible.

**26** For the king knows about these things, and I speak to him freely. I am sure that none of these things has escaped his notice, for this was not done in a corner.

**27** King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe."

**28** Agrippa said to Paul, "In so short a time are you persuading me to become a Christian?"[^13]

**29** Paul said, "Whether in a short time or a long time, I pray to God that not only you but also all who are listening to me today may become what I am, except for these chains."

**30** Then the king got up, and with him the governor and Bernice and those who were sitting with them.

**31** As they were leaving, they said to one another, "This man is doing nothing that deserves death or imprisonment."

**32** Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar."

[^1]: Greek *hairesis*, "party, school", as in 15:5 and 24:5.
[^2]: Literally "the holy ones", traditionally "saints".
[^3]: Literally "I cast my pebble", the small stone used in voting.
[^4]: Or "I forced".
[^5]: Or "in Aramaic". See 21:40.
[^6]: Greek *Saoul*, the Hebrew form of the name, as in 9:4.
[^7]: A proverb found in Greek writers. A goad was a pointed stick for driving oxen; an ox that kicks against it only hurts itself.
[^8]: Or "Sir". Greek *kyrios*.
[^9]: Compare Ezekiel 2:1.
[^10]: Or "I will choose you out". Compare Jeremiah 1:7–8.
[^11]: Compare Isaiah 42:7, 16.
[^12]: Greek *kratistos*, "most excellent", a title for high Roman officials (see 23:26).
[^13]: The Greek is difficult. Literally "In a little you are persuading me to make (or 'play') a Christian." Or "With so little you are trying to persuade me to become a Christian!" Many manuscripts read "to become a Christian", the basis of the traditional rendering "Almost you persuade me to become a Christian." The name *Christian* is used also in 11:26 and 1 Peter 4:16.
