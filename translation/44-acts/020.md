**1** When the uproar had ended, Paul sent for the disciples and encouraged them. Then he said goodbye and set out to go to Macedonia.

**2** He went through those regions and encouraged the people there with many words, and then he came to Greece.[^1]

**3** He spent three months there. As he was about to set sail for Syria, the Jews made a plot against him, so he decided to go back through Macedonia.

**4** He was accompanied[^2] by Sopater son of Pyrrhus,[^3] from Berea; by Aristarchus and Secundus, from Thessalonica; by Gaius of Derbe, and Timothy; and by Tychicus and Trophimus, from Asia.

**5** These men came[^4] and waited for us[^5] at Troas.

**6** But we sailed from Philippi after the days of Unleavened Bread,[^6] and five days later we joined them at Troas, where we stayed seven days.

**7** On the first day of the week, when we had gathered to break bread, Paul was talking with them. Since he intended to leave the next day, he kept on speaking until midnight.

**8** There were many lamps in the upstairs room where we were gathered.

**9** A young man named Eutychus,[^7] who was sitting in the window, was sinking into a deep sleep as Paul talked on and on. Overcome by sleep, he fell from the third floor and was picked up dead.

**10** But Paul went down, threw himself on him, took him in his arms and said, "Don't be alarmed, for his life is still in him."

**11** Then he went back upstairs, broke the bread and ate. He went on talking with them for a long time, until daybreak, and then he left.

**12** They took the boy away alive, and they were greatly comforted.

**13** We went on ahead to the ship and set sail for Assos, intending to take Paul on board there. He had arranged it this way, because he himself was going to go by land.

**14** When he met us at Assos, we took him on board and went on to Mitylene.

**15** We sailed from there and the next day arrived off Chios. The day after that we crossed over to Samos,[^8] and on the following day we came to Miletus.

**16** For Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus, so that he would not have to spend time in Asia, because he was hurrying to be in Jerusalem, if possible, by the day of Pentecost.[^9]

**17** From Miletus he sent a message to Ephesus and called for the elders of the church.

**18** When they came to him, he said to them, "You know how I lived among you the whole time, from the first day I set foot in Asia,

**19** serving the Lord[^10] with all humility, with tears, and in the trials that came to me through the plots of the Jews.

**20** You know that I did not hold back from telling you anything that was helpful, or from teaching you in public and from house to house,

**21** as I testified to both Jews and Greeks about turning to God in repentance and about faith in our Lord Jesus.

**22** And now, bound by the Spirit,[^11] I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there,

**23** except that in town after town the Holy Spirit testifies to me that chains and hardships are waiting for me.

**24** But I do not count my life of any value to myself, if only I may finish my race[^12] and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the good news of God's grace.

**25** And now I know that none of you, among whom I have gone about proclaiming the kingdom, will ever see my face again.

**26** Therefore I declare to you this day that I am innocent of the blood of everyone,[^13]

**27** for I did not hold back from declaring to you the whole purpose of God.

**28** Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers,[^14] to shepherd the church of God,[^15] which he obtained with his own blood.[^16]

**29** I know that after I leave, savage wolves will come in among you, and they will not spare the flock.

**30** Even from among your own number men will arise and say twisted things, to draw the disciples away after them.

**31** So be on your guard, remembering that for three years, night and day, I did not stop warning each one of you with tears.

**32** And now I commit you to God[^17] and to the word of his grace, which is able[^18] to build you up and to give you the inheritance among all those who have been made holy.

**33** I have not desired anyone's silver or gold or clothing.

**34** You yourselves know that these hands of mine provided for my needs and for those who were with me.

**35** In every way I showed you that by working hard like this we must help the weak, remembering the words of the Lord Jesus, that he himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"[^19]

**36** When he had said this, he knelt down with all of them and prayed.

**37** They all wept aloud, and they threw their arms around Paul's neck and kissed him,

**38** grieving most of all over what he had said, that they would never see his face again. Then they escorted him to the ship.

[^1]: Greek *Hellas*, probably the province of Achaia (18:12).
[^2]: Many manuscripts add "as far as Asia".
[^3]: Many manuscripts leave out "son of Pyrrhus".
[^4]: Some manuscripts read "These men went on ahead".
[^5]: Here the narrative again says "we" (see 16:10), and does so until 21:18.
[^6]: The Festival of Unleavened Bread, the seven days that follow the Passover (Exodus 12:15–20).
[^7]: The name means "fortunate".
[^8]: Many manuscripts add "and after staying at Trogyllium".
[^9]: The Festival of Weeks, fifty days after Passover (Leviticus 23:15–21). The Greek name means "fiftieth".
[^10]: Or "serving the Lord as a slave". Greek *douleuo*.
[^11]: Or "bound in my spirit".
[^12]: Many manuscripts read "But I take no account of any of this, nor do I count my life dear to myself, if only I may finish my race with joy".
[^13]: That is, not responsible for anyone's ruin. Compare 18:6; Ezekiel 33:1–9.
[^14]: Greek *episkopoi*, "overseers", traditionally "bishops". The same men are called "elders" in verse 17.
[^15]: Some manuscripts read "the church of the Lord". Many read "the church of the Lord and God".
[^16]: Or "with the blood of his own one", that is, of his own Son.
[^17]: Some manuscripts read "to the Lord".
[^18]: Or "who is able", referring to God.
[^19]: These words of Jesus are not recorded in the Gospels.
