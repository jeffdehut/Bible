**1** After this he left Athens and went to Corinth.

**2** There he found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus,[^1] who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius[^2] had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome. Paul went to see them,

**3** and because he had the same trade, he stayed with them and worked, for they were tentmakers[^3] by trade.

**4** Every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue and tried to persuade both Jews and Greeks.

**5** When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself entirely to the word,[^4] testifying to the Jews that Jesus was the Messiah.

**6** But when they opposed him and insulted him, he shook out his clothes[^5] and said to them, "Your blood be on your own heads![^6] I am innocent. From now on I will go to the Gentiles."

**7** Then he left there and went to the house of a man named Titius Justus,[^7] who worshiped God.[^8] His house was next door to the synagogue.

**8** Crispus, the synagogue leader, believed in the Lord, together with his whole household, and many of the Corinthians who heard Paul believed and were baptized.

**9** One night the Lord said to Paul in a vision, "Do not be afraid. Keep on speaking, and do not be silent,

**10** for I am with you, and no one will lay a hand on you to harm you, for I have many people in this city."

**11** So he stayed there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

**12** But while Gallio was proconsul[^9] of Achaia,[^10] the Jews made a united attack on Paul and brought him before the judgment seat.[^11]

**13** They said, "This man is persuading people to worship God in ways that are contrary to the law."[^12]

**14** Just as Paul was about to speak,[^13] Gallio said to the Jews, "If this were a matter of some wrongdoing or serious crime, you Jews, it would be reasonable for me to hear your complaint.

**15** But since these are questions about words and names and your own law, see to it yourselves. I do not wish to be a judge of such things."

**16** And he drove them away from the judgment seat.

**17** Then they all[^14] seized Sosthenes, the synagogue leader, and beat him in front of the judgment seat. But Gallio paid no attention to any of this.

**18** Paul stayed on for a good many days. Then he said goodbye to the brothers and sisters[^15] and sailed for Syria, and with him went Priscilla and Aquila. At Cenchreae[^16] he had his hair cut off, because he had taken a vow.[^17]

**19** They arrived at Ephesus, and he left them there. He himself went into the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews.

**20** When they asked him to stay longer, he declined.

**21** But as he said goodbye, he told them,[^18] "I will come back to you again, if God wills." Then he set sail from Ephesus.

**22** When he had landed at Caesarea, he went up[^19] and greeted the church, and then went down to Antioch.

**23** After spending some time there, he left and went from place to place through the region of Galatia and Phrygia,[^20] strengthening all the disciples.

**24** Meanwhile a Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria,[^21] came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man,[^22] and powerful in his use of the Scriptures.

**25** He had been instructed in the way of the Lord, and with burning zeal[^23] he spoke and taught accurately the things about Jesus,[^24] though he knew only the baptism of John.

**26** He began to speak boldly in the synagogue. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the way of God to him more accurately.

**27** When he wanted to cross over to Achaia, the brothers and sisters encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, he was a great help to those who through grace had believed.[^25]

**28** For he vigorously refuted the Jews in public, showing from the Scriptures that Jesus was the Messiah.

[^1]: A region on the southern coast of the Black Sea.
[^2]: The Roman emperor Claudius (AD 41–54). The Roman historian Suetonius also mentions this expulsion of the Jews from Rome, which is usually dated about AD 49.
[^3]: Or "leatherworkers". Greek *skenopoioi*.
[^4]: Many manuscripts read "Paul was pressed in spirit".
[^5]: A gesture of protest and of breaking off contact (compare 13:51).
[^6]: That is, you are responsible for what happens to you. Compare Ezekiel 33:4.
[^7]: Many manuscripts read "Justus".
[^8]: That is, a Gentile who worshiped the God of Israel without becoming a Jew.
[^9]: Greek *anthypatos*, "proconsul", the governor of a Roman province under the authority of the Senate. Gallio, the brother of the philosopher Seneca, was proconsul of Achaia in AD 51–52, a date fixed by an inscription found at Delphi. It is one of the few firm dates for Paul's life.
[^10]: The Roman province of southern Greece, with Corinth as its capital; also in verse 27.
[^11]: Greek *bema*, the raised platform from which a Roman official heard cases; also in verses 16 and 17.
[^12]: Either the Jewish law or Roman law may be meant.
[^13]: Literally "to open his mouth".
[^14]: Many manuscripts read "all the Greeks".
[^15]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike; also in verse 27.
[^16]: The eastern port of Corinth.
[^17]: The Greek can also mean that Aquila had his hair cut. The vow may have been a Nazirite vow or one like it (Numbers 6:1–21).
[^18]: Many manuscripts add "I must by all means keep the coming festival in Jerusalem, but".
[^19]: Probably to Jerusalem. "Go up" is the usual expression for going to Jerusalem.
[^20]: Or "the Galatian region and Phrygia". Compare 16:6.
[^21]: The great city of Egypt, on the Mediterranean coast, with a large Jewish population.
[^22]: Or "a learned man".
[^23]: Literally "boiling in spirit". Or "burning with the Spirit".
[^24]: Many manuscripts read "the Lord".
[^25]: Or "through grace he was a great help to those who had believed".
