**1** All the people gathered as one man in the square in front of the Water Gate. They asked Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the Law of Moses, which Yahweh[^1] had commanded Israel.

**2** So on the first day of the seventh month Ezra the priest brought the Law before the assembly, men and women and all who could understand what they heard.

**3** He read from it in the square in front of the Water Gate from daybreak until midday,[^2] in the presence of the men and the women and those who could understand. And all the people listened closely to the book of the Law.[^3]

**4** Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform[^4] that had been made for the purpose. Beside him stood Mattithiah, Shema, Anaiah, Uriah, Hilkiah and Maaseiah on his right, and Pedaiah, Mishael, Malchijah, Hashum, Hashbaddanah, Zechariah and Meshullam on his left.

**5** Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people, for he was above all the people, and as he opened it, all the people stood up.

**6** Ezra blessed Yahweh, the great God, and all the people answered, "Amen! Amen!"[^5] lifting up their hands. Then they bowed down and worshiped Yahweh with their faces to the ground.

**7** Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, Jamin, Akkub, Shabbethai, Hodiah, Maaseiah, Kelita, Azariah, Jozabad, Hanan, Pelaiah and the Levites[^6] helped the people understand the Law, while the people stayed in their places.

**8** They read from the book, from the Law of God, clearly,[^7] and they gave the sense, so that the people understood what was read.

**9** Then Nehemiah, who was the governor,[^8] and Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who were teaching the people said to all the people, "This day is holy to Yahweh your God. Do not mourn or weep." For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the Law.

**10** He said to them, "Go, eat rich food and drink sweet drinks, and send portions to anyone who has nothing ready, for this day is holy to our Lord. Do not grieve, for the joy of Yahweh is your strength."[^9]

**11** The Levites calmed all the people, saying, "Be still, for the day is holy. Do not grieve."

**12** Then all the people went away to eat and drink, to send portions and to celebrate with great joy, because they had understood the words that had been made known to them.

**13** On the second day the heads of the ancestral families[^10] of all the people, the priests and the Levites gathered to Ezra the scribe to study the words of the Law.

**14** They found written in the Law, which Yahweh had commanded through Moses, that the Israelites were to live in shelters[^11] during the festival in the seventh month,

**15** and that they were to announce it and send a proclamation through all their towns and in Jerusalem: "Go out to the hills and bring branches of olive, of wild olive,[^12] of myrtle, of palm and of leafy trees, to make shelters, as it is written."[^13]

**16** So the people went out and brought them, and they made shelters for themselves, each on his roof, and in their courtyards, in the courts of the house of God, in the square at the Water Gate and in the square at the Gate of Ephraim.

**17** The whole assembly of those who had returned from the captivity made shelters and lived in the shelters. From the days of Joshua[^14] son of Nun to that day the Israelites had not done so. And there was very great joy.

**18** Day after day, from the first day to the last day, Ezra read from the book of the Law of God. They kept the festival for seven days, and on the eighth day there was a closing assembly,[^15] as prescribed.

[^1]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "from the light until the middle of the day".
[^3]: Literally "the ears of all the people were to the book of the Law".
[^4]: Literally "a tower of wood".
[^5]: *Amen* means "So be it", "It is true".
[^6]: The names may be those of the Levites themselves: "Pelaiah, the Levites".
[^7]: Or "with interpretation" or "section by section". Jewish tradition understood it as translating into Aramaic.
[^8]: Hebrew *tirshata*, a Persian title of a high official; see 7:65.
[^9]: Or "your refuge". "The joy of Yahweh" may be the joy that Yahweh gives, or joy in Yahweh.
[^10]: Literally "the heads of the fathers", that is, of the fathers' houses: extended families descended from a common ancestor.
[^11]: Hebrew *sukkot*, "booths, shelters": huts made of branches. See Leviticus 23:39–43. Traditionally the festival is called "Tabernacles".
[^12]: Literally "oil tree": perhaps wild olive, or pine.
[^13]: Leviticus 23:40 names "the fruit of majestic trees, palm fronds, branches of leafy trees and willows from the stream".
[^14]: Hebrew *Yeshua*, a later form of the name Joshua.
[^15]: Hebrew *atseret*, a solemn gathering that closes the festival (Leviticus 23:36; Numbers 29:35).
