**1** When Sanballat, Tobiah, the Arabs, the Ammonites and the people of Ashdod heard that the repair of the walls of Jerusalem was going ahead,[^1] and that the gaps were beginning to be closed, they were very angry.

**2** They all plotted together to come and fight against Jerusalem and to cause confusion in it.

**3** But we prayed to our God, and we set a guard against them day and night because of them.

**4** Meanwhile Judah said, "The strength of the burden carriers is failing, and there is so much rubble. We cannot build the wall."

**5** And our enemies said, "They will not know or see anything until we come into their midst and kill them and put a stop to the work."

**6** When the Jews who lived near them came, they told us ten times over,[^2] "From every place where you turn, they will be upon us."[^3]

**7** So I stationed people in the lowest parts of the space behind the wall, in the open places. I stationed the people by families, with their swords, their spears and their bows.

**8** I looked things over, then stood up and said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "Do not be afraid of them. Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and fight for your brothers, your sons and your daughters, your wives and your homes."

**9** When our enemies heard that we knew of it, and that God had frustrated their plan, we all returned[^4] to the wall, each to his own work.

**10** From that day on, half of my servants did the work, while the other half held the spears, the shields, the bows and the coats of armor. The officers stood behind the whole house of Judah.

**11** Those who were building the wall, and those who carried the loads as they loaded them,[^5] each did the work with one hand and held a weapon[^6] with the other.

**12** Each of the builders had his sword strapped at his side as he built. The man who blew the horn[^7] stayed beside me.

**13** I said to the nobles, the officials and the rest of the people, "The work is great and spread out, and we are separated along the wall, far from one another.

**14** Wherever you hear the sound of the horn, gather to us there. Our God will fight for us."

**15** So we did the work, while half of them held spears, from the break of dawn until the stars came out.

**16** At that time I also said to the people, "Let every man and his servant spend the night inside Jerusalem, so that they can guard us by night and work by day."

**17** So neither I nor my brothers nor my servants nor the men of the guard who followed me took off our clothes. Each had his weapon, even at the water.[^8]

[^1]: Literally "that new flesh had grown on the walls of Jerusalem", the image of a wound healing. Verses 1–17 are 4:7–23 in most English Bibles.
[^2]: That is, again and again.
[^3]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "from all the places that you will return to us". It may be an urgent plea, "You must come back to us", or a warning that the enemy would attack from every side.
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "and we will return" but read "and we returned".
[^5]: The meaning of the Hebrew is uncertain.
[^6]: Hebrew *shelach*, a weapon, perhaps a spear or javelin.
[^7]: Hebrew *shofar*, a ram's horn, used to give signals. Also in verse 14.
[^8]: The Hebrew is obscure. Literally "each his weapon, the water". It may mean that each man kept his weapon with him even when he went to get water.
