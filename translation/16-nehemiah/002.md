**1** In the month of Nisan,[^1] in the twentieth year of King Artaxerxes, when wine was set before him, I took up the wine and gave it to the king. I had not been sad in his presence before.[^2]

**2** So the king said to me, "Why is your face sad, when you are not ill? This can only be sadness of heart." Then I was very much afraid.

**3** I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why should my face not be sad, when the city where my ancestors are buried lies in ruins, and its gates have been destroyed by fire?"[^3]

**4** The king said to me, "What is it you are asking for?" So I prayed to the God of heaven,

**5** and I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor with you, send me to Judah, to the city where my ancestors are buried, so that I may rebuild it."

**6** Then the king, with the queen[^4] sitting beside him, said to me, "How long will your journey take, and when will you return?" So it pleased the king to send me, and I gave him a set time.

**7** I also said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given to me for the governors of the province Beyond the River,[^5] so that they will let me pass through until I come to Judah,

**8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the king's forest,[^6] so that he will give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress that belongs to the temple,[^7] for the wall of the city and for the house that I will live in." And the king granted me these, because the good hand of my God was on me.

**9** Then I came to the governors of the province Beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. The king had sent army officers and horsemen with me.

**10** When Sanballat the Horonite[^8] and Tobiah the Ammonite official[^9] heard of this, it displeased them greatly[^10] that someone had come to seek the good of the Israelites.

**11** So I came to Jerusalem, and I was there for three days.

**12** Then I got up in the night, I and a few men with me. I had not told anyone what my God was putting in my heart to do for Jerusalem. There was no animal with me except the animal I was riding.

**13** I went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon Spring[^11] and to the Dung Gate, and I inspected the walls of Jerusalem, which had been broken down, and its gates, which had been destroyed by fire.

**14** Then I went on to the Fountain Gate and to the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.

**15** So I went up through the valley[^12] by night and inspected the wall. Then I turned back and came in through the Valley Gate, and so I returned.

**16** The officials[^13] did not know where I had gone or what I was doing. I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles,[^14] the officials or the rest who would do the work.

**17** Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in: Jerusalem lies in ruins, and its gates have been burned with fire. Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem, so that we will no longer be a disgrace."

**18** I told them how the hand of my God had been good to me, and also what the king had said to me. They said, "Let us get up and build!" So they strengthened their hands for the good work.[^15]

**19** But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite official and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and despised us. They said, "What is this thing you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**20** I answered them, "The God of heaven will give us success, and we his servants will get up and build. But you have no share, no claim and no memorial in Jerusalem."[^16]

[^1]: The first month, around March–April, four months after the events of 1:1.
[^2]: Or "I had never been sad in his presence".
[^3]: Literally "have been eaten by fire". Also in verse 13.
[^4]: Hebrew *shegal*, the king's consort.
[^5]: Hebrew *ever ha-nahar*, "beyond the river": the Persian province west of the Euphrates, which included Syria, Phoenicia and Judah. Also in verse 9.
[^6]: Hebrew *pardes*, a word borrowed from Persian for a walled park or royal forest. The English word "paradise" comes from it.
[^7]: Literally "the house". Hebrew *birah*: a fortress or citadel, here guarding the temple (compare 7:2).
[^8]: Probably from Beth-horon, northwest of Jerusalem. Documents from Elephantine in Egypt show that Sanballat was governor of Samaria.
[^9]: Literally "the servant", probably a title for an official of the Persian king. Also in verse 19.
[^10]: Literally "it was evil to them, a great evil".
[^11]: Or "the Serpent's Spring". Hebrew *Ein ha-tannin*.
[^12]: Hebrew *nachal*, a valley with a stream that runs in the rainy season: here the Kidron valley, east of the city.
[^13]: Hebrew *seganim*, a title borrowed from Akkadian: local officials or magistrates. Also later in the verse.
[^14]: Hebrew *chorim*, literally "free men": the leading families.
[^15]: Literally "for the good".
[^16]: Hebrew *tsedaqah*, "righteousness", here a legal right. Their "memorial" would be a place in the city's memory.
