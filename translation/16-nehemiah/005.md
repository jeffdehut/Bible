**1** Now there was a great outcry from the people and their wives against their fellow Jews.[^1]

**2** Some were saying, "We, our sons and our daughters, are many. Let us get grain, so that we can eat and stay alive."

**3** Others were saying, "We are mortgaging our fields, our vineyards and our houses to get grain because of the famine."

**4** Still others were saying, "We have borrowed money for the king's tax on our fields and our vineyards.[^2]

**5** Now our flesh is the same as the flesh of our brothers, and our children are the same as their children. Yet here we are, forcing our sons and our daughters to become slaves. Some of our daughters have already been forced into it, and we are powerless,[^3] because our fields and our vineyards belong to others."

**6** I was very angry when I heard their outcry and these words.

**7** I thought it over,[^4] and then I brought charges against the nobles and the officials. I said to them, "You are lending at interest,[^5] each to his own brother!" And I called a great assembly against them.

**8** I said to them, "As far as we were able, we have bought back our fellow Jews who had been sold to the nations. But now you are selling your own brothers, so that they can be sold to us!" They were silent and could not find a word to say.

**9** I also said,[^6] "What you are doing is not right. Should you not walk in the fear of our God, to avoid the taunts of the nations, our enemies?

**10** I too, and my brothers and my servants, have been lending them money and grain. Let us give up this lending at interest.

**11** Give back to them this very day their fields, their vineyards, their olive groves and their houses, and the hundredth part[^7] of the money, the grain, the new wine and the olive oil that you have been exacting from them."

**12** They said, "We will give it back, and we will demand nothing from them. We will do just as you say." Then I called the priests and made them swear to do as they had promised.[^8]

**13** I also shook out the fold of my robe and said, "So may God shake out of his house and out of the fruit of his labor every man who does not keep this promise. So may he be shaken out and emptied!" And the whole assembly said, "Amen,"[^9] and they praised Yahweh.[^10] And the people did as they had promised.

**14** Moreover, from the day I was appointed to be their governor in the land of Judah, from the twentieth year to the thirty-second year of King Artaxerxes,[^11] twelve years, neither I nor my brothers ate the food allowance of the governor.

**15** The former governors who were before me laid heavy burdens on the people and took from them food and wine, besides forty shekels[^12] of silver. Even their servants lorded it over the people. But I did not do so, because of the fear of God.

**16** I also devoted myself to the work on this wall, and we did not acquire any land.[^13] All my servants were gathered there for the work.

**17** Moreover, there were at my table the Jews and the officials, 150 men, besides those who came to us from the nations around us.

**18** What was prepared for each day was one ox and six choice sheep, and birds were prepared for me, and once every ten days plenty of wine of every kind. Yet with all this I did not demand the governor's food allowance, because the work weighed heavily on this people.

**19** Remember me for good, my God, for all that I have done for this people.

[^1]: Literally "against their brothers the Jews".
[^2]: Or "We have borrowed money against our fields and our vineyards to pay the king's tax".
[^3]: Literally "there is no power in our hand".
[^4]: Literally "My heart took counsel within me".
[^5]: Or "taking pledges". The meaning of Hebrew *masha* is uncertain: a loan, the interest on it, or a pledge given for it. Also in verse 10.
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "he said" but read "I said".
[^7]: Probably the interest charged, one percent a month.
[^8]: Literally "made them swear to do according to this word", probably the nobles and officials. Also "promise" in verse 13.
[^9]: *Amen* means "So be it", "It is true".
[^10]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^11]: From 445 to 433 BC.
[^12]: A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so forty shekels was about 1 pound (460 grams). The meaning may be "forty shekels of silver a day".
[^13]: Literally "a field". That is, he did not take over the land of those in debt.
