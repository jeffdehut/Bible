**1** The company of prophets[^1] said to Elisha, "Look, the place where we live under your leadership[^2] is too small for us.

**2** Let us go to the Jordan, and let each of us get a beam from there, and let us make ourselves a place to live there." He said, "Go."

**3** Then one of them said, "Please be willing to go with your servants." He said, "I will go."

**4** So he went with them. When they came to the Jordan, they began cutting down trees.

**5** As one of them was felling a beam, the iron axe head[^3] fell into the water. He cried out, "Oh no, my lord! It was borrowed!"

**6** The man of God said, "Where did it fall?" When he showed him the place, Elisha cut off a stick, threw it in there and made the iron float.

**7** He said, "Pick it up." So the man reached out his hand and took it.

**8** Now the king of Aram was at war with Israel. He consulted with his officers and said, "My camp will be at such and such a place."

**9** But the man of God sent word to the king of Israel: "Be careful not to pass that place, because the Arameans are going down there."[^4]

**10** So the king of Israel sent word to the place the man of God had told him about. Elisha would warn him,[^5] and the king would be on his guard there. This happened more than once or twice.

**11** The king of Aram was enraged[^6] by this. He called his officers and said to them, "Won't you tell me which of us is on the side of the king of Israel?"

**12** One of his officers said, "No one, my lord the king. It is Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel. He tells the king of Israel the words you speak in your bedroom."

**13** The king said, "Go and find out where he is, so that I can send men and seize him." He was told, "He is in Dothan."

**14** So he sent horses and chariots and a strong force there. They came by night and surrounded the city.

**15** The attendant of the man of God got up early and went out, and there was an army surrounding the city with horses and chariots. His servant said to him, "Oh no, my lord! What shall we do?"

**16** He said, "Do not be afraid, for there are more with us than with them."

**17** Then Elisha prayed and said, "LORD,[^7] please open his eyes so that he may see." The LORD opened the servant's eyes, and he saw that the hill was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha.

**18** As the Arameans came down toward him, Elisha prayed to the LORD and said, "Please strike this nation with blindness."[^8] So he struck them with blindness, as Elisha had asked.

**19** Elisha said to them, "This is not the road, and this is not the city. Follow me, and I will lead you to the man you are looking for." And he led them to Samaria.

**20** As soon as they had come into Samaria, Elisha said, "LORD, open the eyes of these men so that they may see." The LORD opened their eyes, and they saw that they were inside Samaria.

**21** When the king of Israel saw them, he said to Elisha, "Shall I strike them down, my father? Shall I strike them down?"

**22** He said, "Do not strike them down. Would you strike down those you have taken captive with your own sword and bow?[^9] Set food and water before them, so that they may eat and drink and go back to their master."

**23** So he prepared a great feast for them, and they ate and drank. Then he sent them away, and they went back to their master. And the raiding bands of Aram did not come into the land of Israel again.

**24** Some time later Ben-hadad king of Aram gathered his whole army, marched up and laid siege to Samaria.

**25** There was a great famine in Samaria. They besieged it until a donkey's head sold for eighty shekels[^10] of silver, and a quarter of a kab[^11] of dove's dung[^12] for five shekels of silver.

**26** As the king of Israel was walking along the wall, a woman cried out to him, "Help, my lord the king!"

**27** He said, "If the LORD does not help you, how can I help you?[^13] With grain from the threshing floor, or with wine from the winepress?"

**28** Then the king asked her, "What is the matter?" She said, "This woman said to me, 'Give up your son, so that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow.'

**29** So we cooked my son and ate him. The next day I said to her, 'Give up your son, so that we may eat him.' But she has hidden her son."

**30** When the king heard the woman's words, he tore his clothes. As he was walking along the wall, the people looked, and there was sackcloth underneath, on his body.

**31** He said, "May God do this to me, and more,[^14] if the head of Elisha son of Shaphat stays on his shoulders today!"

**32** Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. The king sent a man ahead of him, but before the messenger reached him, Elisha said to the elders, "Do you see how this son of a murderer[^15] has sent someone to take off my head? Look, when the messenger comes, shut the door and hold the door shut against him. Isn't the sound of his master's footsteps behind him?"

**33** While he was still speaking with them, the messenger came down to him. And he said,[^16] "This disaster is from the LORD. Why should I wait for the LORD any longer?"

[^1]: See 2:3.
[^2]: Literally "where we sit before you".
[^3]: Literally "the iron". Also in verse 6.
[^4]: Hebrew *nechittim*, a rare form, perhaps "going down" or "lying in wait".
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "warned it" but read "warned him".
[^6]: Literally "The heart of the king of Aram was storm-tossed".
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *sanverim*, a sudden blinding or confusion of sight. The word is used elsewhere only in Genesis 19:11.
[^9]: That is, prisoners of war were not killed, even those taken in battle.
[^10]: Literally "eighty of silver", the unit being understood; also "five of silver" later in the verse. A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams), so eighty shekels was about 2 pounds (0.9 kg).
[^11]: A kab was about 1¼ quarts (1.2 liters), so a quarter of a kab was about a cup (0.3 liter).
[^12]: The Hebrew text is written *chare-yonim*, "dove's dung", but read *divyonim*, a more polite word with the same meaning. It may have been a popular name for some cheap food, such as wild seeds or bulbs.
[^13]: Or "No! Let the LORD help you. How can I help you?"
[^14]: Literally "So may God do to me, and so may he add", a solemn oath.
[^15]: Or "this murderer". In Hebrew "son of" can describe a person's character.
[^16]: The speaker is not named. It may be the king, who was following his messenger (verse 32; compare 7:2, 17).
