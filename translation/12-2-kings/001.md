**1** After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel.[^1]

**2** Ahaziah[^2] had fallen through the lattice[^3] of his upper room in Samaria and was injured.[^4] So he sent messengers and told them, "Go and consult[^5] Baal-zebub,[^6] the god of Ekron,[^7] whether I will recover from this injury."

**3** But the angel[^8] of the LORD[^9] said to Elijah the Tishbite, "Get up, go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria, and say to them, 'Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron?

**4** Therefore this is what the LORD says: You will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die.'" So Elijah went.

**5** The messengers went back to the king,[^10] and he said to them, "Why have you come back?"

**6** They said to him, "A man came up to meet us and said to us, 'Go back to the king who sent you and say to him, "This is what the LORD says: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron? Therefore you will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die."'"

**7** He asked them, "What was the man like[^11] who came up to meet you and told you these things?"

**8** They said to him, "He was a hairy man,[^12] with a leather belt fastened around his waist." He said, "It was Elijah the Tishbite."

**9** Then the king sent a captain of fifty[^13] with his fifty men to Elijah. The captain went up to him, and there he was, sitting on top of the hill. He said to him, "Man of God, the king says: Come down!"

**10** Elijah answered the captain of fifty, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty!"[^14] Then fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.

**11** Again the king sent another captain of fifty with his fifty men to him. He spoke to Elijah and said, "Man of God, this is what the king says: Come down at once!"

**12** Elijah answered them, "If I am a man of God, let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty!" Then the fire of God[^15] came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.

**13** Again the king sent a third captain of fifty with his fifty men. The third captain of fifty went up, came and fell on his knees in front of Elijah, and begged him, "Man of God, please let my life and the lives of these fifty servants of yours be precious in your sight.

**14** Look, fire came down from heaven and consumed the first two captains of fifty and their fifties. But now let my life be precious in your sight."

**15** Then the angel of the LORD said to Elijah, "Go down with him. Do not be afraid of him." So he got up and went down with him to the king.

**16** He said to the king, "This is what the LORD says: You sent messengers to consult Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, as if there were no God in Israel whose word you could consult.[^16] Therefore you will not come down from the bed you have gone up to. You will certainly die."

**17** So Ahaziah died, as the LORD had said through Elijah.[^17] Because he had no son, Jehoram[^18] became king in his place, in the second year of Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.[^19]

**18** As for the rest of the acts of Ahaziah, and what he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?[^20]

[^1]: Moab had been subject to Israel (3:4–5). In the Hebrew tradition 1 and 2 Kings are a single book, and the account of Ahaziah's reign begins in 1 Kings 22:52–54 (22:51–53 in most English Bibles).
[^2]: Ahaziah son of Ahab, king of Israel.
[^3]: Hebrew *sevakhah*, a latticework screen over a window or a balcony.
[^4]: Literally "became sick". The same word is used for his "injury" or "illness" later in the verse.
[^5]: Hebrew *darash be-*, "inquire of": to seek a god's answer through a prophet, priest or oracle. Also in verses 3, 6 and 16.
[^6]: The name means "lord of the flies". It is probably a mocking change of *Baal-zebul*, "Baal the prince", a title of the Canaanite god Baal.
[^7]: One of the five main Philistine cities, west of Judah.
[^8]: Or "messenger". Hebrew *mal'akh* means both. It is the same word as the king's "messengers". Also in verse 15.
[^9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^10]: Literally "to him".
[^11]: Literally "What was the manner of the man".
[^12]: Literally "a man, a lord of hair". This may describe his hair or a garment of hair (compare Zechariah 13:4).
[^13]: An officer in command of fifty soldiers. Also in verses 10–14.
[^14]: Hebrew *ish*, "man", and *esh*, "fire", sound alike. Also in verse 12.
[^15]: Or "a tremendous fire".
[^16]: Literally "Because you sent messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, is it because there is no God in Israel to inquire of his word?"
[^17]: Literally "according to the word of the LORD that Elijah had spoken".
[^18]: Ahaziah's brother, another son of Ahab (3:1). He is also called Joram (8:16; 9:14–24).
[^19]: The king of Judah with the same name is also called Joram (8:21–24). 3:1 dates the Israelite Jehoram's accession instead to the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat.
[^20]: Literally "the book of the matters of the days of the kings of Israel", an official royal record that has not survived. It is not the biblical book of Chronicles.
