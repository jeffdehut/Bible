**1** Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram,[^1] was a great man in his master's eyes and highly honored,[^2] because through him Yahweh[^3] had given victory to Aram. He was a mighty warrior, but he had a skin disease.[^4]

**2** Raiding bands from Aram had gone out and taken a young girl captive from the land of Israel, and she served Naaman's wife.

**3** She said to her mistress, "If only my master were with the prophet who is in Samaria! Then he would cure him of his skin disease."[^5]

**4** Naaman went and told his master, "This is what the girl from the land of Israel said."[^6]

**5** The king of Aram said, "Go now, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel." So he went, taking with him ten talents[^7] of silver, six thousand shekels[^8] of gold and ten changes of clothes.

**6** He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It said, "Now, with this letter,[^9] I am sending my servant Naaman to you, so that you may cure him of his skin disease."

**7** When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his clothes and said, "Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man sends word to me to cure a man of his skin disease? Just look and see how he is trying to pick a quarrel with me!"

**8** When Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes, he sent this message to the king: "Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come to me, and he will know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**9** So Naaman came with his horses[^10] and his chariots and stopped at the door of Elisha's house.

**10** Elisha sent a messenger to him to say, "Go and wash seven times in the Jordan. Your flesh will be restored, and you will be clean."

**11** But Naaman was angry and went away. He said, "I thought he would surely come out to me, stand and call on the name of Yahweh his God, wave his hand over the place and cure the skin disease.

**12** Aren't the Amana[^11] and the Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Couldn't I wash in them and be clean?" So he turned and went away in a rage.

**13** But his servants came up to him and said, "My father,[^12] if the prophet had told you to do something great, wouldn't you have done it? How much more, then, when he tells you, 'Wash and be clean'!"

**14** So he went down and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times, as the man of God had said. His flesh was restored and became like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.

**15** Then he went back to the man of God, he and all his company. He came and stood before him and said, "Now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. So please accept a gift[^13] from your servant."

**16** But Elisha said, "As Yahweh lives, whom I serve,[^14] I will not accept anything." Naaman urged him to accept it, but he refused.

**17** Naaman said, "If not, then please let your servant be given as much earth as a pair of mules can carry.[^15] For your servant will never again offer burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but only to Yahweh.

**18** But may Yahweh pardon your servant for this one thing: when my master goes into the temple of Rimmon[^16] to bow down there, and he leans on my arm, and I bow down in the temple of Rimmon, when I bow down in the temple of Rimmon, may Yahweh pardon your servant for this."[^17]

**19** Elisha said to him, "Go in peace." So Naaman went away from him some distance.

**20** Then Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said to himself, "Look, my master has let this Aramean Naaman off without accepting from him what he brought. As Yahweh lives, I will run after him and take something from him."

**21** So Gehazi hurried after Naaman. When Naaman saw someone running after him, he got down from the chariot to meet him and said, "Is everything all right?"

**22** He said, "Everything is all right. My master has sent me to say, 'Just now two young men from the company of prophets[^18] have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of clothes.'"

**23** Naaman said, "Please, take two talents." He urged him to take them, and he tied up two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of clothes. He gave them to two of his servants, and they carried them ahead of Gehazi.

**24** When Gehazi came to the hill,[^19] he took the things from them and put them away in the house. Then he sent the men away, and they left.

**25** He went in and stood before his master, and Elisha said to him, "Where have you been, Gehazi?" He said, "Your servant hasn't gone anywhere."

**26** But Elisha said to him, "Wasn't my heart there when a man turned from his chariot to meet you? Is this the time to accept money and to accept clothes, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and cattle, male and female servants?

**27** Naaman's skin disease will cling to you and to your descendants forever." And Gehazi went out from his presence with a skin disease, white as snow.[^20]

[^1]: The Aramean kingdom, whose capital was Damascus, in what is now Syria. Traditionally "Syria".
[^2]: Literally "lifted up of face".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^4]: Hebrew *metsora*, having *tsara'at*, traditionally "leprosy". It was not leprosy (Hansen's disease) as known today, but a range of skin conditions that made a person ritually unclean (Leviticus 13). Also in verses 3, 6, 7, 11 and 27.
[^5]: Literally "Then he would gather him from his skin disease." The same verb is used in verses 6, 7 and 11.
[^6]: Literally "Like this and like this the girl … said."
[^7]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg). Ten talents was about 750 pounds (340 kg).
[^8]: Literally "six thousand", the unit being understood. A shekel was about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams). Six thousand shekels was about 150 pounds (69 kg).
[^9]: Literally "And now, when this letter comes to you, look,". "And now" marks the start of the letter's message after its greeting.
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "his horse" but read "his horses".
[^11]: The Hebrew text is written "Abanah" but read "Amanah". Most English Bibles follow the written form, "Abana".
[^12]: A respectful way of addressing a master.
[^13]: Literally "a blessing".
[^14]: Literally "before whom I stand".
[^15]: He wanted soil from Israel to take home, so that he could worship Yahweh on the soil of Yahweh's land.
[^16]: The Aramean storm god, also called Hadad. *Rimmon* means "pomegranate" in Hebrew; it is probably a mocking form of the god's title Rammanu, "the Thunderer".
[^17]: The Hebrew text adds "please" after "may Yahweh", written but not read.
[^18]: See 2:3.
[^19]: Hebrew *ha-ophel*, "the mound": a hill or a fortified part of the city.
[^20]: Literally "diseased, like snow". The comparison is probably to the white, flaking skin.
