**1** In the third year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, Hezekiah son of Ahaz king of Judah became king.

**2** He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Abi[^1] daughter of Zechariah.

**3** He did what was right in the eyes of Yahweh,[^2] just as his ancestor[^3] David had done.

**4** He removed the high places,[^4] smashed the standing stones[^5] and cut down the Asherah pole.[^6] He broke into pieces the bronze snake that Moses had made, for until those days the Israelites had been burning incense to it. It was called Nehushtan.[^7]

**5** He trusted in Yahweh, the God of Israel. There was no one like him among all the kings of Judah after him, or among those who were before him.

**6** He held fast to Yahweh and did not turn away from following him. He kept the commands that Yahweh had commanded Moses.

**7** Yahweh was with him, and he succeeded in everything he undertook.[^8] He rebelled against the king of Assyria and would not serve him.

**8** He defeated the Philistines as far as Gaza and its territory, from watchtower to fortified city.

**9** In the fourth year of King Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah king of Israel, Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against Samaria and besieged it.

**10** At the end of three years they captured it. In the sixth year of Hezekiah, which was the ninth year of Hoshea king of Israel, Samaria was captured.

**11** The king of Assyria deported Israel to Assyria and settled them in Halah, on the Habor, the river of Gozan, and in the towns of the Medes,

**12** because they had not obeyed Yahweh their God, but had broken his covenant, all that Moses the servant of Yahweh had commanded. They would neither listen nor obey.

**13** In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the fortified cities of Judah and captured them.[^9]

**14** Then Hezekiah king of Judah sent this message to the king of Assyria at Lachish: "I have done wrong. Withdraw from me, and I will pay whatever you demand of me." The king of Assyria required of Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.[^10]

**15** Hezekiah gave him all the silver that was found in the house of Yahweh and in the treasuries of the king's palace.

**16** At that time Hezekiah stripped the doors of the temple of Yahweh, and the doorposts that Hezekiah king of Judah had overlaid,[^11] and gave the gold to the king of Assyria.

**17** The king of Assyria sent the Tartan,[^12] the Rab-saris[^13] and the Rabshakeh[^14] with a large army from Lachish to King Hezekiah at Jerusalem. They went up and came to Jerusalem. They went up and came and stood by the conduit of the Upper Pool, which is on the road to the Washerman's Field.[^15]

**18** They called for the king, and Eliakim son of Hilkiah, the palace administrator,[^16] Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder,[^17] went out to them.

**19** The Rabshakeh said to them, "Tell Hezekiah, 'This is what the great king, the king of Assyria, says: What is this confidence of yours? What are you trusting in?

**20** You think[^18] that mere words are strategy and strength for war. Now, on whom are you relying, that you have rebelled against me?

**21** Look, you are relying on Egypt, that broken reed of a staff, which goes into the hand of anyone who leans on it and pierces it. That is what Pharaoh king of Egypt is to all who rely on him.

**22** But if you say to me, "We are trusting in Yahweh our God," isn't he the one whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed, saying to Judah and Jerusalem, "You must worship before this altar in Jerusalem"?

**23** So now, make a wager with my master, the king of Assyria: I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able to put riders on them!

**24** How then can you drive back even one officer,[^19] one of the least of my master's servants, when you rely on Egypt for chariots and horsemen?

**25** And besides, have I come up against this place to destroy it without Yahweh? Yahweh himself said to me, "Go up against this land and destroy it."'"

**26** Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, Shebna and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, "Please speak to your servants in Aramaic,[^20] for we understand it. Do not speak to us in the language of Judah[^21] in the hearing of the people on the wall."

**27** But the Rabshakeh said to them, "Was it to your master and to you that my master sent me to speak these words? Wasn't it to the men sitting on the wall, who, like you, will have to eat their own dung and drink their own urine?"[^22]

**28** Then the Rabshakeh stood and called out in a loud voice in the language of Judah. He said, "Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria!

**29** This is what the king says: Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to rescue you from his hand.[^23]

**30** Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in Yahweh by saying, 'Yahweh will surely rescue us, and this city will not be handed over to the king of Assyria.'

**31** Do not listen to Hezekiah, for this is what the king of Assyria says: Make peace with me[^24] and come out to me. Then each of you will eat from his own vine and his own fig tree, and each of you will drink water from his own cistern,

**32** until I come and take you to a land like your own land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of olive trees and honey, so that you may live and not die. Do not listen to Hezekiah, for he is misleading you when he says, 'Yahweh will rescue us.'

**33** Has any of the gods of the nations ever rescued his land from the hand of the king of Assyria?

**34** Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena and Ivvah? Did they rescue Samaria from my hand?

**35** Who among all the gods of the lands has rescued his land from my hand, that Yahweh should rescue Jerusalem from my hand?"

**36** But the people kept silent and did not answer him a word, for the king had commanded, "Do not answer him."

**37** Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, the palace administrator, Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph, the recorder, came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn, and they told him the words of the Rabshakeh.

[^1]: Called Abijah in 2 Chronicles 29:1.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple.
[^3]: Literally "his father".
[^4]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops. Also in verse 22.
[^5]: Hebrew *matsevot*: standing stones set up as objects of worship.
[^6]: Hebrew *asherah*: a wooden pole or tree that was a symbol of the goddess Asherah.
[^7]: Or "He called it Nehushtan". The name sounds like the Hebrew words for "bronze" (*nechoshet*) and "snake" (*nachash*). On the bronze snake, see Numbers 21:8–9.
[^8]: Literally "in everything to which he went out".
[^9]: In 701 BC. 18:13–20:19 is also found, with small differences, in Isaiah 36:1–39:8. Verses 14–16 are not in Isaiah, and verse 13 is Isaiah 36:1.
[^10]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg). Three hundred talents was about 11 tons (10 metric tons), and thirty talents about 1 ton (1 metric ton).
[^11]: Literally "cut off the doors of the temple of Yahweh and the doorposts that Hezekiah … had overlaid", that is, he stripped off the gold that covered them.
[^12]: Hebrew *tartan*, from the Assyrian title *turtanu*: the commander-in-chief of the army, second only to the king. Isaiah 36:2 names only the Rabshakeh.
[^13]: Hebrew *rav-saris*, "chief official" or "chief eunuch", a senior officer of the Assyrian court.
[^14]: Hebrew *rav-shaqeh*, from the Assyrian title *rab shaqe*, "chief cupbearer", a high official who here speaks for the king. Also in verses 19, 26–28 and 37.
[^15]: A field outside the city where cloth was washed and bleached.
[^16]: Literally "who was over the house": see 10:5. Also in verse 37.
[^17]: Hebrew *mazkir*, "one who brings to remembrance": a senior royal official, perhaps the king's herald or the keeper of records. Also in verse 37.
[^18]: Literally "You say". Isaiah 36:5 reads "I say". Literally "a word of lips: strategy and strength for war".
[^19]: Hebrew *pechah*, "governor".
[^20]: The international language of diplomacy and trade in the Assyrian empire.
[^21]: Literally "in Judean", that is, Hebrew. Also in verse 28.
[^22]: The Hebrew text is written with coarse words for "dung" and "urine", but read with more polite ones, literally "excrement" and "the water of their feet".
[^23]: That is, from the hand of the king of Assyria. Isaiah 36:14 does not have "from his hand".
[^24]: Literally "Make a blessing with me", that is, surrender on terms.
