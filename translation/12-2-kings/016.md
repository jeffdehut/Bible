**1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham king of Judah became king.

**2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD[^1] his God, as his ancestor[^2] David had done.

**3** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel. He even made his son pass through the fire,[^3] following the detestable practices of the nations whom the LORD had driven out before the Israelites.

**4** He sacrificed and burned incense at the high places,[^4] on the hills and under every green tree.

**5** Then Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to make war. They besieged Ahaz but could not overcome him.[^5]

**6** At that time Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram[^6] and drove the people of Judah[^7] out of Elath. Then the Edomites[^8] came to Elath, and they have lived there to this day.

**7** Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria to say, "I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the power[^9] of the king of Aram and the king of Israel, who are attacking me."

**8** Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king's palace, and sent it as a payment[^10] to the king of Assyria.

**9** The king of Assyria listened to him. The king of Assyria went up against Damascus and captured it. He deported its people to Kir,[^11] and he killed Rezin.

**10** When King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, he saw the altar that was in Damascus. King Ahaz sent Uriah the priest a model of the altar and a plan of it, with all the details of its construction.

**11** Uriah the priest built the altar. He made it according to everything King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, and Uriah the priest had it finished by the time King Ahaz came back from Damascus.

**12** When the king came back from Damascus, the king saw the altar. The king approached the altar and went up on it.[^12]

**13** He burned his burnt offering and his grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and splashed the blood of his fellowship offerings[^13] against the altar.

**14** As for the bronze altar that stood before the LORD, he moved it from the front of the house, from between the new altar[^14] and the house of the LORD, and put it on the north side of the new altar.

**15** King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, "On the great altar burn the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering, the king's burnt offering and his grain offering, and the burnt offering of all the people of the land, their grain offering and their drink offerings. Splash against it all the blood of the burnt offerings and all the blood of the sacrifices. But the bronze altar will be for me to inquire by."[^15]

**16** Uriah the priest did everything King Ahaz commanded.

**17** King Ahaz cut off the frames of the stands and removed the basins from them. He took down the Sea from the bronze oxen that were under it and put it on a stone pavement.[^16]

**18** Because of the king of Assyria, he removed from the house of the LORD the covered way for the Sabbath that had been built in the house, and the king's outer entrance.[^17]

**19** As for the rest of the acts of Ahaz, what he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Judah?

**20** Ahaz slept with his ancestors and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. And his son Hezekiah became king in his place.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. "The house of the LORD" is the temple.
[^2]: Literally "his father".
[^3]: That is, he burned him as a sacrifice (17:17, 31; Deuteronomy 18:10).
[^4]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^5]: Literally "could not fight". Compare Isaiah 7:1–9.
[^6]: In Hebrew the names Aram and Edom differ by one letter, and the two letters (*r* and *d*) look much alike.
[^7]: Hebrew *ha-yehudim*, "the Judeans". This is the first time the word appears in the Bible. It later came to mean "the Jews".
[^8]: The Hebrew text is written "Arameans" but read "Edomites".
[^9]: Literally "palm".
[^10]: Hebrew *shochad*, a gift given to win favor, often a bribe.
[^11]: A place in Mesopotamia from which the Arameans had come (Amos 1:5; 9:7).
[^12]: Or "and offered sacrifices on it".
[^13]: Hebrew *shelamim*, traditionally "peace offerings": see Leviticus 3.
[^14]: Literally "the altar". Also later in the verse.
[^15]: Hebrew *levaqqer*, "to inquire" or "to examine", perhaps by looking at the organs of sacrificed animals for omens. The meaning is uncertain.
[^16]: On the stands, the basins and the Sea, see 1 Kings 7:23–39.
[^17]: The meaning of this verse is uncertain. Literally "And the covering of the Sabbath which they had built in the house, and the outer entrance of the king, he turned (in) the house of the LORD, because of the king of Assyria."
