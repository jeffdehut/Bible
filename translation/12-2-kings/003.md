**1** Jehoram son of Ahab became king over Israel in Samaria in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned twelve years.[^1]

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD,[^2] but not like his father and his mother. He removed the standing stone[^3] of Baal that his father had made.

**3** But he held on to the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, which he had caused Israel to commit.[^4] He did not turn away from them.[^5]

**4** Now Mesha king of Moab was a sheep breeder,[^6] and he used to pay the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and the wool of a hundred thousand rams.

**5** But when Ahab died, the king of Moab rebelled against the king of Israel.

**6** So at that time[^7] King Jehoram set out from Samaria and mustered all Israel.

**7** He went and sent this message to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: "The king of Moab has rebelled against me. Will you go with me to war against Moab?" Jehoshaphat said, "I will go up. I am as you are, my people as your people, my horses as your horses."

**8** He asked, "Which way shall we go up?" He answered, "By the road through the wilderness of Edom."

**9** So the king of Israel set out, with the king of Judah and the king of Edom.[^8] They marched around for seven days, and there was no water for the army or for the animals that followed them.[^9]

**10** The king of Israel said, "Oh no! The LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab!"

**11** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there no prophet of the LORD here, so that we may consult the LORD through him?" One of the officers[^10] of the king of Israel answered, "Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who used to pour water on Elijah's hands."[^11]

**12** Jehoshaphat said, "The word of the LORD is with him." So the king of Israel, Jehoshaphat and the king of Edom went down to him.

**13** Elisha said to the king of Israel, "What do you want with me?[^12] Go to your father's prophets and your mother's prophets." But the king of Israel said to him, "No, for the LORD has called these three kings together to hand them over to Moab."

**14** Elisha said, "As surely as the LORD of Armies[^13] lives, whom I serve,[^14] if I did not have respect for Jehoshaphat king of Judah,[^15] I would not look at you or even notice you.

**15** But now bring me a musician." And while the musician was playing, the hand of the LORD[^16] came on Elisha.

**16** He said, "This is what the LORD says: Dig this valley[^17] full of ditches.[^18]

**17** For this is what the LORD says: You will see no wind and you will see no rain, yet this valley will be filled with water, and you will drink, you and your livestock and your other animals.

**18** This is a small thing in the eyes of the LORD. He will also hand Moab over to you.

**19** You will strike down every fortified city and every choice city. You will cut down every good tree, stop up all the springs of water and ruin every good field with stones."

**20** In the morning, at the time when the grain offering is presented,[^19] water suddenly came flowing from the direction of Edom, and the land was filled with water.

**21** Now all Moab had heard that the kings had come up to fight against them. So every man old enough to bear arms was called out,[^20] and they took their stand at the border.

**22** When they got up early in the morning, the sun was shining on the water, and the Moabites saw the water across from them, red as blood.[^21]

**23** They said, "This is blood! The kings must have fought each other and killed one another. Now, Moab, to the plunder!"

**24** But when they came to the camp of Israel, the Israelites rose up and struck down the Moabites, and they fled before them. Then the Israelites struck at the land,[^22] slaughtering Moab.

**25** They tore down the cities, and on every good field each man threw his stone, until they had filled it. They stopped up every spring of water and cut down every good tree. Only in Kir-hareseth[^23] were its stones left standing,[^24] but the slingers surrounded it and attacked it.

**26** When the king of Moab saw that the battle was going against him,[^25] he took seven hundred swordsmen with him to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not.

**27** Then he took his firstborn son, who was to become king in his place, and offered him as a burnt offering on the wall. And there was great anger against Israel,[^26] so they withdrew from him and returned to their own land.

[^1]: On this king's name, see 1:17.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *matsevah*, a stone pillar set up as an object of worship.
[^4]: Jeroboam, the first king of the northern kingdom, set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan (1 Kings 12:26–33).
[^5]: Literally "from it".
[^6]: Hebrew *noqed*, the word used of the prophet Amos (Amos 1:1). Mesha's own account of his revolt against Israel survives in a Moabite inscription of the 9th century BC, the Mesha Stele.
[^7]: Literally "on that day".
[^8]: Edom was then ruled by a deputy, not a king of its own line (1 Kings 22:48; 22:47 in most English Bibles).
[^9]: Literally "the animals that were at their feet".
[^10]: Or "servants". Hebrew *avadim* means servants of any rank, including the king's officials.
[^11]: That is, he had been Elijah's personal attendant.
[^12]: Literally "What is there between me and you?"
[^13]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^14]: Literally "before whom I stand".
[^15]: Literally "if I were not lifting up the face of Jehoshaphat king of Judah".
[^16]: A way of speaking of God's power coming on a prophet.
[^17]: Hebrew *nachal*, a wadi: a valley with a stream that is dry except in the rainy season. Also in verse 17.
[^18]: Literally "Making this wadi ditches, ditches." Or "I will make this valley full of pools."
[^19]: Literally "at the going up of the offering": the regular morning sacrifice (Exodus 29:38–41).
[^20]: Literally "they were called out, from everyone who girds on a belt and upward".
[^21]: Hebrew *adom*, "red", and *dam*, "blood", sound like *Edom*, the direction the water came from (verse 20).
[^22]: The Hebrew text is written "they went into it" but read "they struck it". "It" is the land of Moab.
[^23]: The capital of Moab, also called Kir-heres (Isaiah 16:7, 11).
[^24]: Literally "until he left its stones in Kir-hareseth". The Hebrew is difficult.
[^25]: Literally "that the battle was too strong for him".
[^26]: The Hebrew does not say whose anger this was.
