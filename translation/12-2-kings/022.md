**1** Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem.[^1] His mother's name was Jedidah daughter of Adaiah, from Bozkath.

**2** He did what was right in the eyes of Yahweh[^2] and walked in all the way of his ancestor[^3] David. He did not turn aside to the right or to the left.

**3** In the eighteenth year of King Josiah, the king sent Shaphan son of Azaliah son of Meshullam, the secretary, to the house of Yahweh, saying,

**4** "Go up to Hilkiah the high priest and have him total up[^4] the money that has been brought into the house of Yahweh, which the doorkeepers[^5] have collected from the people.

**5** Let them hand it over[^6] to the men doing the work, who have been put in charge of the house of Yahweh, and let them pay it to the workers in the house of Yahweh to repair the damage to the house,

**6** to the carpenters, the builders and the masons, and for buying timber and dressed stone to repair the house.

**7** But no accounting is to be required of them for the money handed over to them, for they deal honestly."

**8** Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the secretary, "I have found the book of the Law[^7] in the house of Yahweh." Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan, and he read it.

**9** Then Shaphan the secretary came to the king and reported to him: "Your servants have emptied out[^8] the money that was found in the house and have handed it over to the men doing the work, who have been put in charge of the house of Yahweh."

**10** Shaphan the secretary also told the king, "Hilkiah the priest has given me a book." And Shaphan read it aloud before the king.

**11** When the king heard the words of the book of the Law, he tore his clothes.

**12** The king commanded Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Achbor son of Micaiah, Shaphan the secretary and Asaiah the king's servant,[^9]

**13** "Go and inquire of Yahweh for me, for the people and for all Judah, about the words of this book that has been found. For great is the wrath of Yahweh that has been kindled against us, because our ancestors did not listen to the words of this book, to do all that is written concerning us."

**14** So Hilkiah the priest, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan and Asaiah went to Huldah the prophet, the wife of Shallum son of Tikvah son of Harhas,[^10] the keeper of the wardrobe. She lived in Jerusalem, in the Second Quarter.[^11] They spoke with her,

**15** and she said to them, "This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: Tell the man who sent you to me,

**16** 'This is what Yahweh says: I am going to bring disaster on this place and on its inhabitants, all the words of the book that the king of Judah has read.

**17** Because they have abandoned me and burned incense to other gods, provoking me to anger with all the work of their hands, my wrath will be kindled against this place, and it will not be quenched.'

**18** But to the king of Judah, who sent you to inquire of Yahweh, say this: 'This is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, says: As for the words you have heard,

**19** because your heart was tender and you humbled yourself before Yahweh when you heard what I spoke against this place and against its inhabitants, that they would become a desolation and a curse, and because you tore your clothes and wept before me, I too have heard you, declares Yahweh.

**20** Therefore I will gather you to your ancestors, and you will be gathered to your grave in peace. Your eyes will not see all the disaster I am going to bring on this place.'" And they brought back word to the king.

[^1]: The account of Josiah's reign in 22:1–23:30 is parallel to 2 Chronicles 34–35.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple.
[^3]: Literally "his father".
[^4]: Literally "complete". Perhaps "melt down" (compare verse 9).
[^5]: Literally "the keepers of the threshold".
[^6]: The Hebrew text is written "Let him hand it over" but read "Let them hand it over".
[^7]: Hebrew *sefer ha-torah*, "the book of the instruction", here the law given through Moses. It is often thought to be a form of Deuteronomy (compare Deuteronomy 28:58–61; 31:24–26).
[^8]: Or "melted down".
[^9]: The title of a senior royal official.
[^10]: Called Hasrah in 2 Chronicles 34:22.
[^11]: Hebrew *ha-mishneh*, "the second", a newer district of Jerusalem (Zephaniah 1:10).
