**1** In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah, Hoshea son of Elah became king over Israel in Samaria, and he reigned nine years.[^1]

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh,[^2] but not like the kings of Israel who were before him.

**3** Shalmaneser[^3] king of Assyria came up against him, and Hoshea became his vassal[^4] and paid him tribute.

**4** But the king of Assyria discovered that Hoshea was conspiring against him, for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt[^5] and had not paid tribute to the king of Assyria as he had done year by year. So the king of Assyria arrested him and put him in prison.

**5** The king of Assyria invaded the whole land. He came up to Samaria and besieged it for three years.

**6** In the ninth year of Hoshea, the king of Assyria captured Samaria.[^6] He deported Israel to Assyria and settled them in Halah, on the Habor, the river of Gozan,[^7] and in the towns of the Medes.

**7** This happened because the Israelites had sinned against Yahweh their God, who had brought them up out of the land of Egypt, from under the power of Pharaoh king of Egypt. They worshiped[^8] other gods.

**8** They followed the customs[^9] of the nations whom Yahweh had driven out before the Israelites, and the customs that the kings of Israel had introduced.[^10]

**9** The Israelites secretly did things against Yahweh their God that were not right.[^11] They built themselves high places[^12] in all their towns, from watchtower to fortified city.

**10** They set up standing stones[^13] and Asherah poles[^14] for themselves on every high hill and under every green tree.

**11** There they burned incense at all the high places, like the nations whom Yahweh had driven into exile before them. They did evil things, provoking Yahweh to anger.

**12** They served idols,[^15] though Yahweh had said to them, "You must not do this."

**13** Yet Yahweh warned Israel and Judah through every prophet and every seer:[^16] "Turn from your evil ways, and keep my commands and my decrees, in keeping with all the law that I commanded your ancestors and sent to you through my servants the prophets."

**14** But they would not listen. They were as stubborn as their ancestors,[^17] who did not trust in Yahweh their God.

**15** They rejected his decrees and his covenant that he had made with their ancestors, and the statutes[^18] with which he had warned them. They went after worthless things and became worthless themselves.[^19] They followed the nations around them, although Yahweh had commanded them not to do as they did.

**16** They abandoned all the commands of Yahweh their God and made themselves images of cast metal,[^20] two calves. They made an Asherah pole, bowed down to all the host of heaven[^21] and served Baal.

**17** They made their sons and their daughters pass through the fire.[^22] They practiced divination and looked for omens, and they sold themselves to do what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, provoking him to anger.

**18** So Yahweh was very angry with Israel and removed them from his presence. No one was left but the tribe of Judah alone.

**19** Judah too did not keep the commands of Yahweh their God, but followed the customs that Israel had introduced.

**20** So Yahweh rejected all the descendants of Israel. He afflicted them and handed them over to plunderers, until he had cast them out of his presence.

**21** When he tore Israel away from the house of David,[^23] they made Jeroboam son of Nebat king. Jeroboam drove Israel away from following Yahweh and made them commit a great sin.

**22** The Israelites walked in all the sins that Jeroboam had committed. They did not turn away from them,

**23** until Yahweh removed Israel from his presence, as he had said through all his servants the prophets. So Israel went into exile from their own land to Assyria, and they are there to this day.

**24** The king of Assyria brought people from Babylon, Cuthah, Avva, Hamath and Sepharvaim and settled them in the towns of Samaria in place of the Israelites. They took possession of Samaria and lived in its towns.

**25** When they first lived there, they did not worship Yahweh, so Yahweh sent lions among them, and they killed some of them.

**26** So the king of Assyria was told, "The nations you deported and settled in the towns of Samaria do not know what the god of the land requires.[^24] So he has sent lions among them, and they are killing them, because they do not know what the god of the land requires."

**27** Then the king of Assyria gave this command: "Send back there one of the priests you deported from there. Let him go and live there,[^25] and let him teach them what the god of the land requires."

**28** So one of the priests whom they had deported from Samaria came and lived in Bethel, and he taught them how they should worship Yahweh.

**29** But each nation made its own gods and set them up in the shrines of the high places that the people of Samaria[^26] had made, each nation in the towns where they lived.

**30** The people of Babylon made Succoth-benoth, the people of Cuth made Nergal, and the people of Hamath made Ashima.[^27]

**31** The Avvites made Nibhaz and Tartak, and the Sepharvites burned their children in the fire to Adrammelech and Anammelech, the gods of Sepharvaim.

**32** They also worshiped Yahweh, and they appointed all sorts of people[^28] from among themselves as priests for the high places, who served for them in the shrines of the high places.

**33** They worshiped Yahweh, but they also served their own gods, following the custom of the nations from which they had been deported.

**34** To this day they follow their former customs. They do not worship Yahweh, and they do not follow the decrees and rulings given to them,[^29] or the law and the command that Yahweh gave to the descendants of Jacob, whom he named Israel.[^30]

**35** Yahweh had made a covenant with them and commanded them, "Do not worship other gods. Do not bow down to them, serve them or sacrifice to them.

**36** But Yahweh, who brought you up from the land of Egypt with great power and an outstretched arm, is the one you must worship. To him you must bow down, and to him you must sacrifice.

**37** The decrees and the rulings, the law and the command that he wrote for you, you must always be careful to keep. Do not worship other gods.

**38** Do not forget the covenant I have made with you, and do not worship other gods.

**39** But worship Yahweh your God, and he will rescue you from the power of all your enemies."

**40** But they would not listen. Instead they went on following their former custom.

**41** So these nations worshiped Yahweh, but they also served their carved images. Their children and their grandchildren have done as their ancestors did, to this day.

[^1]: Compare 15:30.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Shalmaneser V, king of Assyria from 727 to 722 BC.
[^4]: Literally "his servant".
[^5]: This king is not known by this name from other sources. He may be Osorkon IV of Egypt, or "So" may be the Egyptian city of Sais.
[^6]: In 722 BC.
[^7]: The Habor (Khabur) river flows into the Euphrates in northern Mesopotamia. Gozan was a city on its banks.
[^8]: Literally "feared". In this chapter "fear" means to revere and worship. Also in verses 25, 28 and 32–41.
[^9]: Literally "decrees". Also in verse 19.
[^10]: Literally "and of the kings of Israel, which they made". The Hebrew is awkward.
[^11]: The meaning of the Hebrew verb, found only here, is uncertain: perhaps "did secretly" or "spoke".
[^12]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^13]: Hebrew *matsevot*: standing stones set up as objects of worship.
[^14]: Hebrew *asherim*: wooden poles or trees that were symbols of the goddess Asherah. Also in verse 16.
[^15]: Hebrew *gillulim*, a contemptuous word for idols, perhaps "dung pellets".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "through all his prophets, every seer" but read "through all the prophets of every seer".
[^17]: Literally "They hardened their neck like the neck of their ancestors."
[^18]: Hebrew *edot*, traditionally "testimonies": the solemn terms of the covenant.
[^19]: Hebrew *hevel*, "breath, vapor, emptiness", often used of idols. Literally "They went after emptiness and became empty."
[^20]: Hebrew *massekhah*, an image made of molten metal (1 Kings 12:28–29).
[^21]: The sun, moon and stars, worshiped as gods.
[^22]: That is, they burned them as sacrifices (verse 31; 16:3).
[^23]: Or "When Israel tore itself away from the house of David". See 1 Kings 11:29–39; 12:16–20.
[^24]: Hebrew *mishpat*, "ruling, custom": here the right way to worship the god of the land. Also in verse 27.
[^25]: Literally "let them go and live there".
[^26]: Hebrew *ha-shomeronim*, the only time this word occurs in the Hebrew Bible. It means the earlier, Israelite inhabitants of Samaria.
[^27]: Nergal was the god of the underworld, worshiped at Cuthah in Babylonia. The other gods named in verses 30–31 are not certainly known from other sources.
[^28]: Literally "from their ends", that is, from every rank of people (compare 1 Kings 12:31).
[^29]: Literally "their decrees and their ruling".
[^30]: Genesis 32:29 (32:28 in most English Bibles); 35:10.
