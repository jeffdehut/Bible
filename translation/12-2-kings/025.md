**1** So in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month,[^1] Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came against Jerusalem with his whole army. He camped against it, and they built siege works all around it.

**2** The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**3** On the ninth day of the month[^2] the famine in the city was so severe that there was no food for the people of the land.

**4** Then the city wall was broken through, and all the soldiers fled[^3] by night by way of the gate between the two walls, near the king's garden, though the Babylonians[^4] were all around the city. The king[^5] went off in the direction of the Arabah.[^6]

**5** But the Babylonian army pursued the king and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**6** They captured the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, and they passed sentence on him.

**7** They slaughtered Zedekiah's sons before his eyes. Then the king of Babylon put out Zedekiah's eyes, bound him in bronze shackles and brought him to Babylon.

**8** In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month,[^7] which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard,[^8] an officer[^9] of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**9** He burned the house of the LORD,[^10] the king's palace and all the houses of Jerusalem. Every great house he burned with fire.

**10** The whole Babylonian army that was with the commander of the guard tore down the walls around Jerusalem.

**11** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard deported the rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the population.

**12** But the commander of the guard left some of the poorest people of the land to work the vineyards and the fields.

**13** The Babylonians broke up the bronze pillars that were in the house of the LORD, and the stands and the bronze Sea that were in the house of the LORD, and they carried the bronze to Babylon.

**14** They also took away the pots, the shovels, the wick trimmers, the dishes and all the bronze articles used in the temple service.

**15** The commander of the guard took away the firepans and the sprinkling bowls, whatever was made of gold and whatever was made of silver.

**16** As for the two pillars, the one Sea and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD, the bronze of all these articles was beyond weighing.

**17** Each pillar was eighteen cubits[^11] high, and the bronze capital on top of it was three cubits high,[^12] with a network and pomegranates all around the capital, all of bronze. The second pillar, with its network, was the same.

**18** The commander of the guard took Seraiah the chief priest, Zephaniah the second priest and the three doorkeepers.[^13]

**19** From the city he took an official who was in charge of the soldiers, and five of the king's advisers[^14] who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army,[^15] who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.

**20** Nebuzaradan the commander of the guard took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**21** The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah went into exile from its land.

**22** As for the people who were left in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam son of Shaphan over them.[^16]

**23** When all the commanders of the armed forces and their men heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite, and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, they and their men.

**24** Gedaliah swore an oath to them and their men, and he said to them, "Do not be afraid of the Babylonian officials.[^17] Live in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you."

**25** But in the seventh month, Ishmael son of Nethaniah son of Elishama, who was of royal descent, came with ten men, and they struck down Gedaliah, and he died. They also struck down the people of Judah[^18] and the Babylonians who were with him at Mizpah.

**26** Then all the people, from the least to the greatest, and the commanders of the armed forces set out and went to Egypt, for they were afraid of the Babylonians.

**27** In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month,[^19] Evil-merodach[^20] king of Babylon, in the year he became king, released Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison.[^21]

**28** He spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the other kings who were with him in Babylon.

**29** So Jehoiachin took off his prison clothes, and for the rest of his life he regularly ate at the king's table.

**30** As for his allowance, a regular allowance was given him by the king, a portion for each day, for as long as he lived.[^22]

[^1]: In January 588 BC. 25:1–12 is also told in Jeremiah 39:1–10, and 24:18–25:30 in Jeremiah 52.
[^2]: Jeremiah 52:6 reads "In the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month".
[^3]: The Hebrew has no verb here. "Fled" is supplied from Jeremiah 52:7, which reads "all the soldiers fled and went out of the city by night".
[^4]: Hebrew *Kasdim*, traditionally "Chaldeans": the people of southern Mesopotamia whose kings then ruled Babylon. Also in verses 5, 10, 13 and 24–26.
[^5]: Literally "he". Jeremiah 52:7 reads "they".
[^6]: The Jordan Valley.
[^7]: In the summer of 587 or 586 BC. Jeremiah 52:12 reads "on the tenth day of the month".
[^8]: Hebrew *rav-tabbachim*, literally "chief of the butchers" or "chief of the cooks", a title of the commander of the royal bodyguard. Also in verses 10–12, 15, 18 and 20.
[^9]: Literally "servant".
[^10]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. "The house of the LORD" is the temple.
[^11]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm). Eighteen cubits was about 27 feet (8.1 meters).
[^12]: About 4½ feet (1.4 meters). Jeremiah 52:22 and 1 Kings 7:16 read "five cubits".
[^13]: Literally "the keepers of the threshold".
[^14]: Literally "of those who saw the king's face". Jeremiah 52:25 reads "seven".
[^15]: Or "the secretary, the commander of the army".
[^16]: The fuller account is in Jeremiah 40:7–41:18.
[^17]: Literally "the servants of the Babylonians". Jeremiah 40:9 reads "Do not be afraid to serve the Babylonians."
[^18]: Hebrew *ha-yehudim*, "the Judeans": see 16:6.
[^19]: In 561 BC. Jeremiah 52:31 reads "on the twenty-fifth day of the month".
[^20]: Amel-Marduk, son of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon from 562 to 560 BC.
[^21]: Literally "lifted up the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah from the prison house", that is, showed him favor (compare Genesis 40:13, 20).
[^22]: Literally "all the days of his life". Jeremiah 52:34 reads "until the day of his death, all the days of his life".
