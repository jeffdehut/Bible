**1** In the second year of Joash son of Joahaz[^1] king of Israel, Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah became king.

**2** He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jehoaddan,[^2] from Jerusalem.

**3** He did what was right in the eyes of Yahweh,[^3] but not like his ancestor[^4] David. He did everything his father Joash had done.

**4** But the high places[^5] were not removed. The people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.

**5** As soon as the kingdom was firmly in his hand, he struck down his officials who had struck down his father the king.

**6** But he did not put to death the sons of the murderers, in keeping with what is written in the book of the Law of Moses, where Yahweh commanded, "Fathers are not to be put to death for their sons, and sons are not to be put to death for their fathers. Each is to be put to death for his own sin."[^6]

**7** He struck down ten thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt, and he captured Sela[^7] in battle and called it Joktheel, the name it has to this day.

**8** Then Amaziah sent messengers to Jehoash son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, "Come, let us face each other."[^8]

**9** Jehoash king of Israel sent this reply to Amaziah king of Judah: "A thornbush in Lebanon sent word to a cedar in Lebanon, 'Give your daughter to my son as his wife.' Then a wild animal in Lebanon passed by and trampled the thornbush.

**10** You have indeed struck down Edom, and your heart has made you proud. Enjoy your glory, and stay at home. Why stir up trouble, so that you fall, you and Judah with you?"

**11** But Amaziah would not listen. So Jehoash king of Israel went up, and he and Amaziah king of Judah faced each other at Beth-shemesh, which belongs to Judah.

**12** Judah was defeated by Israel, and every man fled to his home.[^9]

**13** Jehoash king of Israel captured Amaziah king of Judah, son of Jehoash son of Ahaziah, at Beth-shemesh. Then he came[^10] to Jerusalem and broke down the wall of Jerusalem from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate, four hundred cubits.[^11]

**14** He took all the gold and silver and all the articles that were found in the house of Yahweh and in the treasuries of the king's palace, as well as hostages, and he returned to Samaria.

**15** As for the rest of the acts of Jehoash, what he did and his mighty deeds, and how he fought against Amaziah king of Judah, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?

**16** Jehoash slept with his ancestors and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. And his son Jeroboam became king in his place.

**17** Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah lived fifteen years after the death of Jehoash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel.

**18** As for the rest of the acts of Amaziah, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Judah?

**19** They formed a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish. But they sent men after him to Lachish and killed him there.

**20** They brought him back on horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem with his ancestors in the City of David.

**21** Then all the people of Judah took Azariah,[^12] who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.

**22** He was the one who rebuilt Elath[^13] and restored it to Judah, after the king slept with his ancestors.[^14]

**23** In the fifteenth year of Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah, Jeroboam son of Joash king of Israel became king in Samaria, and he reigned forty-one years.

**24** He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh. He did not turn away from all the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, which he had caused Israel to commit.

**25** He restored the border of Israel from Lebo-hamath[^15] to the Sea of the Arabah,[^16] according to the word of Yahweh, the God of Israel, which he spoke through his servant Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet from Gath-hepher.[^17]

**26** For Yahweh saw that the suffering of Israel was very bitter. There was no one left, slave or free,[^18] and there was no one to help Israel.

**27** Yahweh had not said that he would blot out the name of Israel from under heaven, so he saved them through Jeroboam son of Joash.

**28** As for the rest of the acts of Jeroboam, all that he did and his mighty deeds, how he fought, and how he recovered for Israel Damascus and Hamath, which had belonged to Judah,[^19] are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Israel?

**29** Jeroboam slept with his ancestors, with the kings of Israel. And his son Zechariah became king in his place.

[^1]: *Joahaz* is a shorter form of *Jehoahaz* (13:1). In this chapter the Hebrew also uses both *Joash* and *Jehoash* for the kings of Israel and of Judah who bore that name.
[^2]: The Hebrew text is written "Jehoaddin" but read "Jehoaddan".
[^3]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. "The house of Yahweh" is the temple.
[^4]: Literally "his father".
[^5]: Hebrew *bamot*: places of worship, often on hilltops.
[^6]: Deuteronomy 24:16. The Hebrew text is written "will die" but read "will be put to death".
[^7]: Or "the Rock". The place is in Edom; its exact location is uncertain.
[^8]: Literally "let us look one another in the face", a challenge to battle. Also in verse 11.
[^9]: Literally "to his tent".
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "they came" but read "he came".
[^11]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm). Four hundred cubits was about 600 feet (180 meters).
[^12]: Also called Uzziah (15:13, 30–34; 2 Chronicles 26).
[^13]: A port on the Gulf of Aqaba, near Ezion-geber (1 Kings 9:26).
[^14]: That is, after the death of his father Amaziah.
[^15]: Or "the entrance to Hamath", at the northern limit of Israel's territory.
[^16]: The Dead Sea.
[^17]: The prophet of the book of Jonah (Jonah 1:1).
[^18]: See 9:8.
[^19]: Literally "how he restored Damascus and Hamath to Judah in Israel". The Hebrew is difficult. It may refer to lands once ruled by David and Solomon (2 Samuel 8:5–10; 1 Kings 8:65).
