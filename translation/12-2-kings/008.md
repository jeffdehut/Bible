**1** Now Elisha had said to the woman whose son he had brought back to life,[^1] "Get up and go, you and your household, and live wherever you can, for Yahweh[^2] has called for a famine, and it will come on the land for seven years."

**2** The woman got up and did as the man of God had said. She went with her household and lived in the land of the Philistines for seven years.

**3** At the end of the seven years, the woman returned from the land of the Philistines and went to appeal to the king for her house and her land.

**4** Now the king was talking with Gehazi, the servant of the man of God, and saying, "Tell me all the great things Elisha has done."

**5** Just as Gehazi was telling the king how Elisha had brought the dead back to life, the woman whose son he had brought back to life came to appeal to the king for her house and her land. Gehazi said, "My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha brought back to life."

**6** The king questioned the woman, and she told him about it. Then the king assigned an official[^3] to her and said, "Give back everything that was hers, along with all the produce of her land from the day she left the country until now."

**7** Elisha came to Damascus. Ben-hadad king of Aram was sick, and he was told, "The man of God has come here."

**8** The king said to Hazael, "Take a gift with you and go to meet the man of God. Consult Yahweh through him and ask, 'Will I recover from this illness?'"

**9** So Hazael went to meet him, taking with him a gift of every good thing of Damascus, forty camel loads. He came and stood before him and said, "Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to you to ask, 'Will I recover from this illness?'"

**10** Elisha said to him, "Go and tell him, 'You will certainly recover.'[^4] But Yahweh has shown me that he will certainly die."

**11** He kept his face fixed and stared until the other was ashamed.[^5] Then the man of God wept.

**12** Hazael said, "Why is my lord weeping?" He said, "Because I know the harm you will do to the Israelites. You will set their fortresses on fire, kill their young men with the sword, dash their little children to pieces and rip open their pregnant women."

**13** Hazael said, "But what is your servant, a mere dog, that he should do this great thing?" Elisha said, "Yahweh has shown me that you will be king over Aram."

**14** Then Hazael left Elisha and went back to his master, who said to him, "What did Elisha say to you?" He said, "He told me you would certainly recover."

**15** But the next day he took the blanket,[^6] dipped it in water and spread it over his face, and he died. And Hazael became king in his place.

**16** In the fifth year of Joram[^7] son of Ahab king of Israel, when Jehoshaphat was king of Judah,[^8] Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah became king.

**17** He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**18** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for a daughter of Ahab was his wife. He did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh.

**19** But Yahweh was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of his servant David, since he had promised to give a lamp[^9] to him and to his descendants forever.

**20** In his days Edom rebelled against Judah's rule[^10] and set up a king of their own.

**21** So Joram[^11] crossed over to Zair[^12] with all his chariots. He rose by night and struck the Edomites who had surrounded him and the commanders of the chariots, and the army fled to their homes.[^13]

**22** So Edom has been in rebellion against Judah's rule to this day. Libnah also rebelled at that time.

**23** As for the rest of the acts of Joram, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the annals of the kings of Judah?[^14]

**24** Joram slept with his ancestors[^15] and was buried with his ancestors in the City of David. And his son Ahaziah became king in his place.

**25** In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah became king.[^16]

**26** Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king,[^17] and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Athaliah daughter of Omri king of Israel.[^18]

**27** He walked in the way of the house of Ahab and did what was evil in the eyes of Yahweh, as the house of Ahab had done, for he was related by marriage to the house of Ahab.

**28** He went with Joram son of Ahab to war against Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead, and the Arameans wounded Joram.

**29** King Joram went back to Jezreel to recover from the wounds the Arameans had given him at Ramah,[^19] when he fought against Hazael king of Aram. And Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to Jezreel to see Joram son of Ahab, because he was wounded.

[^1]: See 4:8–37.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *saris*, a court official. The word can also mean a eunuch.
[^4]: The Hebrew text is written "Go, say, 'You will not live'", but read "Go, say to him, 'You will certainly live.'" The words "not" and "to him" sound the same in Hebrew (*lo*).
[^5]: Literally "He made his face stand and set it until shame." The Hebrew does not say who stared and who was ashamed. Probably Elisha stared at Hazael.
[^6]: Hebrew *makhber*, a word found only here: a cloth or blanket. The Hebrew does not name the one who did this. It was probably Hazael, and the one who died Ben-hadad.
[^7]: The king called Jehoram in 1:17 and 3:1. *Joram* is a shorter form of the same name. Also in verses 25, 28 and 29.
[^8]: Literally "and Jehoshaphat king of Judah". These words are missing in some Septuagint manuscripts and in the Syriac.
[^9]: That is, a descendant on the throne to continue his line (1 Kings 11:36; 15:4).
[^10]: Literally "from under the hand of Judah". Also in verse 22.
[^11]: Jehoram king of Judah (verse 16). *Joram* is a shorter form of the same name. Also in verses 23–24.
[^12]: A place not otherwise known.
[^13]: Literally "to their tents". The Hebrew does not make clear whose army this was, Joram's or Edom's.
[^14]: Literally "the book of the matters of the days of the kings of Judah", an official royal record that has not survived. It is not the biblical book of Chronicles.
[^15]: Literally "lay down with his fathers", a way of speaking of death.
[^16]: 9:29 dates Ahaziah's accession to the eleventh year of Joram.
[^17]: 2 Chronicles 22:2 gives his age as forty-two.
[^18]: In Hebrew "daughter" can mean any female descendant. Verse 18 calls Jehoram's wife a daughter of Ahab, Omri's son.
[^19]: That is, Ramoth-gilead (verse 28).
