**1** Elisha said, "Hear the word of the LORD.[^1] This is what the LORD says: About this time tomorrow, a seah[^2] of fine flour will sell for a shekel,[^3] and two seahs of barley for a shekel, at the gate of Samaria."

**2** The officer[^4] on whose arm the king leaned answered the man of God, "Look, even if the LORD were to make windows in the sky,[^5] could this happen?" Elisha said, "You will see it with your own eyes, but you will not eat any of it."

**3** Now there were four men with a skin disease[^6] at the entrance to the gate. They said to one another, "Why are we sitting here until we die?

**4** If we say, 'Let's go into the city,' the famine is in the city, and we will die there. And if we stay here, we will die. So come, let's go over to the camp of the Arameans.[^7] If they let us live, we will live, and if they kill us, we will die."

**5** So they got up at dusk to go to the Aramean camp. When they came to the edge of the Aramean camp, there was no one there.

**6** For the Lord[^8] had made the Aramean army hear the sound of chariots and the sound of horses, the sound of a great army. They had said to one another, "Look, the king of Israel has hired the kings of the Hittites[^9] and the kings of Egypt to attack us."

**7** So they had got up and fled at dusk. They had abandoned their tents, their horses and their donkeys, the camp just as it was, and fled for their lives.

**8** When these men with skin disease came to the edge of the camp, they went into one tent and ate and drank. They carried off silver, gold and clothes from it, and went and hid them. Then they came back and went into another tent, carried things off from it, and went and hid them too.

**9** Then they said to one another, "We are not doing right. This is a day of good news, and we are keeping quiet. If we wait until morning light, we will be punished.[^10] So come, let's go and tell the king's household."

**10** So they went and called out to the gatekeepers of the city and told them, "We went to the Aramean camp, and there was no one there, not a human voice. There were only the horses tied up, the donkeys tied up, and the tents just as they were."

**11** The gatekeepers shouted the news, and it was reported inside the king's palace.

**12** The king got up in the night and said to his officers, "Let me tell you what the Arameans have done to us. They know that we are starving, so they have left the camp to hide in the open country. They are thinking, 'When they come out of the city, we will take them alive and get into the city.'"

**13** One of his officers answered, "Let some men take five of the horses that are left, the ones still left in the city. They are like the whole crowd of Israel who are left in it; they are like the whole crowd of Israel who have already died.[^11] Let us send them and see."

**14** So they took two chariots with their horses, and the king sent them after the Aramean army, saying, "Go and see."

**15** They followed them as far as the Jordan, and the whole road was strewn with clothes and equipment that the Arameans had thrown away in their haste. The messengers returned and told the king.

**16** Then the people went out and plundered the Aramean camp. So a seah of fine flour sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, as the LORD had said.

**17** Now the king had put the officer on whose arm he leaned in charge of the gate. The people trampled him in the gate, and he died, just as the man of God had said when the king came down to him.[^12]

**18** For when the man of God had said to the king, "Two seahs of barley will sell for a shekel, and a seah of fine flour for a shekel, about this time tomorrow at the gate of Samaria,"

**19** the officer had answered the man of God, "Look, even if the LORD were to make windows in the sky, could such a thing happen?" And Elisha had said, "You will see it with your own eyes, but you will not eat any of it."

**20** And that is what happened to him. The people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: A seah was about 7 quarts (7 liters).
[^3]: A shekel was a weight of silver, about ⅖ ounce (11.5 grams).
[^4]: Hebrew *shalish*, literally "third", probably first the third man in a chariot and then a high-ranking officer or aide. Also in verses 17 and 19.
[^5]: Openings in the sky through which rain falls (Genesis 7:11). Also in verse 19.
[^6]: Hebrew *metsora'im*: on this disease, see 5:1. Also in verse 8.
[^7]: Literally "let's fall to the camp of Aram", that is, surrender to them.
[^8]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name YHWH.
[^9]: The kings of the small states in northern Syria that were heirs of the Hittite empire.
[^10]: Literally "guilt will find us".
[^11]: That is, whatever happens to them, they are no worse off than everyone else in the city.
[^12]: See 6:32–33.
