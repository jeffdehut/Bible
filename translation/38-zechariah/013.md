**1** On that day a fountain will be opened for the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, to cleanse them from sin and impurity.[^1]

**2** On that day, declares the LORD of Armies,[^2] I will cut off the names of the idols from the land, and they will be remembered no more. I will also remove from the land the prophets and the spirit of impurity.

**3** If anyone still prophesies, his father and mother who bore him will say to him, "You will not live, for you have spoken lies in the name of the LORD." And his father and mother who bore him will pierce him[^3] when he prophesies.

**4** On that day every prophet will be ashamed of his vision when he prophesies. He will not put on a hairy cloak[^4] in order to deceive.

**5** He will say, "I am not a prophet. I am a man who works the soil, for the land has been my livelihood since my youth."[^5]

**6** And if someone asks him, "What are these wounds on your chest?"[^6] he will say, "I was wounded in the house of my friends."[^7]

**7** Awake, sword, against my shepherd, against the man who stands next to me,[^8] declares the LORD of Armies. Strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered,[^9] and I will turn my hand against the little ones.

**8** In the whole land, declares the LORD, two thirds will be cut off and die, and a third will be left in it.

**9** I will bring this third into the fire. I will refine them as silver is refined and test them as gold is tested. They will call on my name, and I will answer them. I will say, "They are my people," and they will say, "The LORD is my God."[^10]

[^1]: Hebrew *niddah*, the impurity of menstruation, and the word used for the water that cleansed from impurity (Numbers 19:9).
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles. *The LORD of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares the LORD* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verses 7 and 8.
[^3]: The same verb, *daqar*, as *pierced* in 12:10. The law required a false prophet to be put to death, even by his own family (Deuteronomy 13:7–12, 13:6–11 in most English Bibles; 18:20).
[^4]: The garment worn by prophets such as Elijah (2 Kings 1:8; 2:13).
[^5]: The Hebrew is pointed *adam hiqnani*, "a man sold me" or "a man made me his servant". The same letters, divided into words differently, can be read *adamah qinyani*, "the land has been my livelihood". Compare Amos 7:14.
[^6]: Literally "between your hands", that is, on the chest or the back. Prophets of other gods sometimes cut themselves (1 Kings 18:28).
[^7]: Or "of those who love me".
[^8]: Literally "against the man who is my companion".
[^9]: Jesus quotes these words in Mark 14:27 and Matthew 26:31, of himself and his disciples, in the form "I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered."
[^10]: Compare Hosea 2:25 (2:23 in most English Bibles).
