**1** Look, a day is coming for the LORD,[^1] when the plunder taken from you[^2] will be divided in your midst.

**2** For I will gather all the nations against Jerusalem for battle. The city will be captured, the houses plundered and the women raped.[^3] Half of the city will go into exile, but the rest of the people will not be cut off from the city.

**3** Then the LORD will go out and fight against those nations, as he fights on a day of battle.

**4** On that day his feet will stand on the Mount of Olives,[^4] which lies east of Jerusalem, and the Mount of Olives will be split in two from east to west by a very wide valley. Half of the mountain will move north, and half of it south.

**5** You will flee by the valley of my mountains,[^5] for the valley of the mountains will reach as far as Azel.[^6] You will flee as you fled from the earthquake in the days of Uzziah king of Judah.[^7] Then the LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with him.[^8]

**6** On that day there will be no light, neither brightness nor frost.[^9]

**7** It will be one unbroken day,[^10] known to the LORD, neither day nor night, and at evening time there will be light.

**8** On that day living water will flow out from Jerusalem, half of it to the eastern sea and half of it to the western sea.[^11] It will flow in summer and in winter.

**9** The LORD will be king over all the earth. On that day the LORD will be one, and his name one.[^12]

**10** The whole land will be changed into a plain like the Arabah,[^13] from Geba to Rimmon, south of Jerusalem.[^14] But Jerusalem will be raised up and will remain in its place, from the Benjamin Gate to the place of the First Gate and to the Corner Gate, and from the Tower of Hananel to the king's winepresses.[^15]

**11** People will live in it, and there will never again be a curse of destruction.[^16] Jerusalem will live in security.

**12** This will be the plague with which the LORD will strike all the peoples who have made war on Jerusalem: their flesh will rot while they are still standing on their feet, their eyes will rot in their sockets, and their tongues will rot in their mouths.

**13** On that day a great panic from the LORD will fall on them. Each will seize the hand of his neighbor, and they will raise their hands against each other.

**14** Judah too will fight at Jerusalem.[^17] The wealth of all the surrounding nations will be gathered: gold, silver and clothing in great abundance.

**15** A plague like this will also strike the horses, the mules, the camels, the donkeys and all the animals in those camps.

**16** Then all who are left of all the nations that came against Jerusalem will go up year after year to worship the King, the LORD of Armies,[^18] and to celebrate the Festival of Booths.[^19]

**17** If any of the clans of the earth do not go up to Jerusalem to worship the King, the LORD of Armies, there will be no rain on them.

**18** If the clan of Egypt does not go up and come in, they will have no rain.[^20] There will be the plague with which the LORD strikes the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Booths.

**19** This will be the punishment[^21] of Egypt and the punishment of all the nations that do not go up to celebrate the Festival of Booths.

**20** On that day the bells of the horses will bear the words "Holy to the LORD",[^22] and the cooking pots in the house of the LORD will be like the sprinkling bowls in front of the altar.

**21** Every cooking pot in Jerusalem and in Judah will be holy to the LORD of Armies, and all who offer sacrifices will come and take some of them and cook in them. And on that day there will no longer be a trader[^23] in the house of the LORD of Armies.

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Literally "your plunder". The Hebrew is feminine, addressing Jerusalem.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written with a coarse word for sexual violation but read with a milder one, "lain with".
[^4]: The ridge east of Jerusalem, across the Kidron Valley. Only here in the Old Testament is it called by this name (compare 2 Samuel 15:30; Acts 1:11–12).
[^5]: The same consonants can be read "The valley of my mountains will be blocked", as the Septuagint reads. Also later in the verse.
[^6]: The place is unknown.
[^7]: The earthquake in the eighth century BC, which Amos 1:1 also mentions.
[^8]: Hebrew "with you". The Septuagint, the Syriac and the Vulgate read "with him". The holy ones are the angels of the LORD's heavenly army (Deuteronomy 33:2–3; Psalm 89:6, 89:5 in most English Bibles). Compare 1 Thessalonians 3:13.
[^9]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "there will be no light, precious things and congealing". The Hebrew text is written "the precious things will congeal". The Septuagint reads "there will be no light, and cold and frost".
[^10]: Literally "one day", perhaps a day like no other (compare Genesis 1:5).
[^11]: The Dead Sea and the Mediterranean. Compare Ezekiel 47:1–12; Joel 4:18 (3:18 in most English Bibles).
[^12]: Compare Deuteronomy 6:4. This verse ends the *Aleinu* prayer of Jewish daily worship.
[^13]: The deep valley of the Jordan and the Dead Sea.
[^14]: Geba was at the northern border of Judah (2 Kings 23:8), and Rimmon in the south (Joshua 15:32).
[^15]: The gates and the tower were landmarks at the edges of the city (Jeremiah 31:38; 37:13; Nehemiah 3:1).
[^16]: Hebrew *cherem*, the ban by which a city was devoted to destruction (Joshua 6:17). Compare Revelation 22:3.
[^17]: Or "against Jerusalem".
[^18]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 17 and 21.
[^19]: The autumn festival at the end of the harvest, when the people lived in booths (Leviticus 23:33–43). It came just before the rainy season.
[^20]: Literally "and not on them". *Rain* is supplied from verse 17. Egypt depends on the Nile rather than on rain. The Septuagint and the Syriac do not have "not": "on them will come the plague".
[^21]: Hebrew *chatta't*, "sin", here the punishment for sin.
[^22]: The words engraved on the gold plate on the high priest's turban (Exodus 28:36).
[^23]: Literally "a Canaanite", a word also used for a merchant (Zephaniah 1:11; Proverbs 31:24). Compare John 2:16.
