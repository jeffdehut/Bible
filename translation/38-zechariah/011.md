**1** Open your doors, Lebanon, so that fire may devour your cedars!

**2** Wail, you cypress, for the cedar has fallen, for the mighty trees are ruined! Wail, you oaks of Bashan, for the dense forest[^1] has come down!

**3** Listen! The wailing of the shepherds, for their glory[^2] is ruined! Listen! The roaring of young lions, for the thickets of the Jordan[^3] are ruined!

**4** This is what Yahweh[^4] my God said: "Shepherd the flock marked for slaughter.[^5]

**5** Those who buy them kill them and go unpunished. Those who sell them say, 'Blessed be Yahweh, for I am rich!' And their own shepherds have no pity on them.

**6** For I will no longer have pity on the people of the land, declares Yahweh.[^6] Look, I am going to hand over everyone to his neighbor[^7] and to his king. They will crush the land, and I will not rescue anyone from their hand."

**7** So I shepherded the flock marked for slaughter, the most afflicted of the flock.[^8] I took two staffs. One I called Favor and the other I called Union,[^9] and I shepherded the flock.

**8** In one month I got rid of the three shepherds.[^10] I lost patience with them,[^11] and they also detested me.

**9** Then I said, "I will not be your shepherd. Let the dying die, and let the perishing perish, and let those who are left devour one another's flesh."

**10** Then I took my staff Favor and broke it, to revoke the covenant I had made with all the peoples.

**11** So it was revoked on that day, and the afflicted of the flock,[^12] who were watching me, knew that it was the word of Yahweh.

**12** Then I said to them, "If it seems good to you, give me my wages; if not, keep them." So they weighed out my wages, thirty pieces of silver.[^13]

**13** Then Yahweh said to me, "Throw it to the potter,[^14] that magnificent price at which they valued me!"[^15] So I took the thirty pieces of silver and threw them into the house of Yahweh, to the potter.

**14** Then I broke my second staff, Union, to revoke the brotherhood between Judah and Israel.

**15** Then Yahweh said to me, "Take once more the equipment of a foolish shepherd.[^16]

**16** For look, I am going to raise up a shepherd in the land who will not care for those that are perishing or seek the young[^17] or heal the injured or feed the healthy, but will eat the flesh of the fat ones and tear off their hooves.[^18]

**17** Woe to[^19] my worthless shepherd, who abandons the flock! May the sword strike his arm and his right eye! May his arm wither completely, and his right eye go totally blind!"

[^1]: The Hebrew text is written *batsur*, "inaccessible", but read *batsir*, a word that usually means "grape harvest". Both are probably meant to describe a thick forest.
[^2]: Hebrew *adderet*, "glory", also the word for a rich robe. Perhaps their rich pastures are meant.
[^3]: Literally "the pride of the Jordan", the thick growth along the river where lions lived (Jeremiah 12:5; 49:19).
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^5]: Literally "the flock of slaughter". Also in verse 7. The meaning of verses 4–17 is much debated. The prophet acts out the part of a shepherd, but who the shepherds and the flock stand for is uncertain.
[^6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement.
[^7]: The same consonants can be read "to his shepherd".
[^8]: Literally "therefore the afflicted of the flock". The same letters, divided into words differently, can be read "for the sheep traders", and the Septuagint reads it in this way, as a reference to Canaan or Canaanites (traders). Also in verse 11.
[^9]: Hebrew *No'am*, "pleasantness, favor", and *Chovelim*, "bonds", that is, the bonds that join Judah and Israel (verse 14).
[^10]: The three shepherds have never been identified with certainty.
[^11]: Literally "my *nephesh* became short with them".
[^12]: See verse 7.
[^13]: Literally "thirty of silver", probably thirty shekels, about 12 ounces (345 grams) of silver: the price paid for a slave who had been gored to death (Exodus 21:32). Matthew 26:15 and 27:3–10 tell how Judas was paid thirty pieces of silver and the money was used to buy a potter's field. Matthew 27:9–10 quotes verses 12–13 as spoken by "Jeremiah the prophet", combining them with words from Jeremiah (compare Jeremiah 18:2–3; 32:6–9).
[^14]: Hebrew *ha-yotser*, "the potter". Also later in the verse. The Syriac reads "the treasury" (*ha-otsar*), a word that differs by one letter.
[^15]: Spoken with scorn. Literally "the glory of the price at which I was valued by them".
[^16]: Hebrew *evili*, "foolish", a word for one who is morally worthless.
[^17]: Hebrew *ha-na'ar*, "the youth". Or "the scattered", with a small change to the Hebrew. Compare the bad shepherds of Ezekiel 34:2–4.
[^18]: Perhaps by driving the sheep over rough ground, or by tearing the carcass apart to eat it.
[^19]: Hebrew *hoy*, here the cry "Woe to" (see 2:10). *Worthless* is Hebrew *ha-elil*, a word often used of idols.
