**1** An oracle.[^1] The word of Yahweh[^2] is against the land of Hadrach,[^3] and Damascus is where it comes to rest. For the eyes of humankind and of all the tribes of Israel are on Yahweh.[^4]

**2** It is against Hamath too, which borders on it, and against Tyre and Sidon, though they are very wise.

**3** Tyre has built herself a fortress[^5] and heaped up silver like dust and gold like the mud of the streets.

**4** But look, the Lord[^6] will take possession of her and strike down her power on the sea,[^7] and she will be devoured by fire.

**5** Ashkelon will see it and be afraid; Gaza too will writhe in anguish, and Ekron, for her hope will be put to shame. Gaza will lose her king, and Ashkelon will be uninhabited.

**6** A people of mixed race[^8] will live in Ashdod, and I will cut off the pride of the Philistines.

**7** I will take the blood out of his mouth and the detestable food from between his teeth.[^9] He too will be a remnant for our God. He will be like a clan[^10] in Judah, and Ekron like the Jebusites.[^11]

**8** I will camp around my house as a guard,[^12] against those who march back and forth. No oppressor will overrun them again, for now I have seen with my own eyes.

**9** Rejoice greatly, Daughter Zion![^13] Shout, Daughter Jerusalem! Look, your king is coming to you, righteous and victorious,[^14] humble[^15] and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.[^16]

**10** I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim and the war horse from Jerusalem, and the battle bow will be cut off. He will proclaim peace to the nations. His rule will be from sea to sea, and from the River to the ends of the earth.[^17]

**11** As for you, because of the blood of my covenant with you,[^18] I will set your prisoners free from the waterless pit.[^19]

**12** Return to the stronghold, you prisoners of hope. Even today I declare that I will give back to you double.[^20]

**13** For I will bend Judah as my bow; I will fill it with Ephraim as its arrow.[^21] I will rouse your sons, Zion, against your sons, Greece,[^22] and make you like a warrior's sword.

**14** Then Yahweh will appear over them, and his arrow will flash like lightning. The Lord Yahweh[^23] will blow the ram's horn[^24] and march out in the storms of the south.[^25]

**15** Yahweh of Armies[^26] will shield them. They will devour and trample the sling stones;[^27] they will drink and roar as if with wine. They will be full like a bowl, like the corners of the altar.[^28]

**16** On that day Yahweh their God will save them, as the flock of his people. For like the jewels of a crown they will sparkle over his land.

**17** How good and how beautiful it will be![^29] Grain will make the young men flourish, and new wine the young women.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*, from the verb "to lift up, carry": a message "lifted up" by the prophet, or a "burden" laid on a nation. Traditionally "the burden". The same heading begins chapters 12–14 and the book of Malachi. Chapters 9–14 have no dates and no visions, and many scholars think they come from a later time than chapters 1–8.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: A city-state in northern Syria, known from Assyrian records as Hatarikka.
[^4]: Or "For Yahweh has an eye on humankind, as on all the tribes of Israel". Literally "For to Yahweh is the eye of humankind". Some scholars read "the eye of Aram", with a small change to the Hebrew.
[^5]: A play on words: *Tsor*, "Tyre", and *matsor*, "fortress". Tyre stood on an island off the coast.
[^6]: Hebrew *Adonai*, "the Lord", not the name Yahweh.
[^7]: Or "strike her wealth into the sea" or "her rampart into the sea".
[^8]: Hebrew *mamzer*, a person of forbidden or mixed birth (Deuteronomy 23:3, 23:2 in most English Bibles).
[^9]: Meat eaten with its blood, and other food that was unclean to Israel (Leviticus 11; 17:10–14). The Philistines will be cleansed and become part of Yahweh's people.
[^10]: Hebrew *alluf*, a clan or a clan chief.
[^11]: The people of Jerusalem before David took it, who lived on among the people of Judah (2 Samuel 5:6–9; 24:18–24).
[^12]: Hebrew *mitsavah*, perhaps "against an army". The meaning is uncertain.
[^13]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. So also *Daughter Jerusalem*.
[^14]: Literally "saved", that is, given victory by Yahweh. The Septuagint reads "saving".
[^15]: Hebrew *ani*, "poor, afflicted, humble". The Septuagint reads "gentle".
[^16]: Literally "on a male donkey, on a colt, the son of she-donkeys". The two phrases describe one animal, as Hebrew poetry often says one thing twice. Matthew 21:5 and John 12:15 quote this verse of Jesus' entry into Jerusalem, which Mark 11:1–10 also tells, and Matthew speaks of a donkey and a colt.
[^17]: The same words are found in Psalm 72:8. *The River* is the Euphrates.
[^18]: Literally "the blood of your covenant", the covenant sealed with the blood of sacrifice (Exodus 24:8). The Hebrew is feminine, addressing Zion.
[^19]: Hebrew *bor*, a pit or empty cistern, used as a prison (Genesis 37:24; Jeremiah 38:6).
[^20]: Compare Isaiah 61:7.
[^21]: Literally "I will fill the bow with Ephraim".
[^22]: Hebrew *Yavan*, Ionia, the Greeks of the Aegean coasts (Ezekiel 27:13; Joel 4:6, 3:6 in most English Bibles).
[^23]: Hebrew *Adonai YHWH*, "my Lord Yahweh", traditionally "the Lord GOD".
[^24]: Hebrew *shofar*, a trumpet made from a ram's horn, sounded as an alarm in war.
[^25]: Hebrew *teman*, the south, also the region of Teman in Edom, from which Yahweh comes in Habakkuk 3:3.
[^26]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^27]: The Hebrew of this verse is difficult. Or "They will devour, and the sling stones will conquer".
[^28]: The bowls held the blood of the sacrifices, which was splashed on the corners of the altar (Exodus 27:3; Leviticus 4:7).
[^29]: Literally "How great is his goodness, and how great his beauty!" *His* may be Yahweh or the people.
