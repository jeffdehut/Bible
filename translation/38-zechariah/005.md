**1** Again I looked up, and I saw a flying scroll.

**2** He asked me, "What do you see?" I said, "I see a flying scroll, twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide."[^1]

**3** He said to me, "This is the curse that is going out over the whole land. For according to what is written on one side, every thief will be swept away,[^2] and according to what is written on the other side, everyone who swears falsely[^3] will be swept away.

**4** I will send it out, declares Yahweh of Armies,[^4] and it will enter the house of the thief and the house of the one who swears falsely by my name. It will stay inside his house and destroy it, both its timbers and its stones."[^5]

**5** Then the angel who was speaking with me came forward and said to me, "Look up and see what this is that is going out."

**6** I asked, "What is it?" He said, "This is the ephah basket[^6] that is going out." And he said, "This is their iniquity[^7] in all the land."

**7** Then a round cover of lead[^8] was lifted, and there was a woman sitting inside the basket.

**8** He said, "This is Wickedness." He thrust her back into the basket and pushed the lead weight down over its mouth.

**9** Then I looked up and saw two women coming out, with the wind[^9] in their wings. They had wings like the wings of a stork, and they lifted up the basket between earth and heaven.

**10** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "Where are they taking the basket?"

**11** He said to me, "To build a house[^10] for it in the land of Shinar.[^11] When the house is ready, the basket will be set down there on its stand."

[^1]: A cubit was about 18 inches (45 cm), so the scroll was about 30 feet (9 m) long and 15 feet (4.5 m) wide, the size of the porch of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:3).
[^2]: Or "has gone unpunished". The Hebrew verb *niqqah* can mean "be cleaned out" or "be held innocent". Also later in the verse.
[^3]: Literally "everyone who swears". *Falsely* is supplied from verse 4. Stealing and swearing falsely by Yahweh's name break two of the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:7, 15).
[^4]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. *Yahweh of Armies* (Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*) is traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. *Declares Yahweh* is Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement.
[^5]: Compare the house struck by mold in Leviticus 14:45.
[^6]: Literally "the ephah", a dry measure of about 22 liters (two-thirds of a bushel), here a basket or barrel of that size. Also in verses 7–11.
[^7]: Hebrew *einam*, "their eye", perhaps "their appearance". The Septuagint and the Syriac read *avonam*, "their iniquity", which differs by one letter.
[^8]: Literally "a talent of lead", a round, flat piece of lead of a set weight.
[^9]: Hebrew *ruach*, "wind" or "spirit".
[^10]: That is, a temple, where the basket would be set on a stand, as an idol is.
[^11]: An old name for Babylonia (Genesis 11:2; Daniel 1:2), the place of exile and of the tower of Babel.
