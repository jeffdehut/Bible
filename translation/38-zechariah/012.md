**1** An oracle.[^1] The word of the LORD[^2] concerning Israel. Declares the LORD,[^3] who stretches out the heavens, lays the foundation of the earth, and forms the human spirit within a person:

**2** Look, I am going to make Jerusalem a cup that makes all the surrounding peoples reel.[^4] Judah too will be under siege, as Jerusalem is.[^5]

**3** On that day I will make Jerusalem a heavy stone for all the peoples. All who lift it will be badly injured, and all the nations of the earth will gather against her.

**4** On that day, declares the LORD, I will strike every horse with panic and its rider with madness.[^6] But I will keep my eyes open over the house of Judah, when I strike every horse of the peoples with blindness.

**5** Then the clans[^7] of Judah will say in their hearts, "The people who live in Jerusalem are our strength, through the LORD of Armies,[^8] their God."

**6** On that day I will make the clans of Judah like a fire pot in a pile of wood, like a flaming torch among sheaves. They will devour all the surrounding peoples, right and left, while Jerusalem will remain in its place, in Jerusalem.[^9]

**7** The LORD will save the tents of Judah first, so that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem will not be raised above Judah.

**8** On that day the LORD will shield the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so that the feeblest among them on that day will be like David, and the house of David will be like God,[^10] like the angel of the LORD going before them.

**9** On that day I will set out to destroy all the nations that come against Jerusalem.

**10** I will pour out on the house of David and on the inhabitants of Jerusalem a spirit of grace and of pleading.[^11] They will look on me, the one they have pierced,[^12] and they will mourn for him as one mourns for an only child, and weep bitterly over him as one weeps over a firstborn.

**11** On that day the mourning in Jerusalem will be as great as the mourning for Hadad-rimmon in the plain of Megiddo.[^13]

**12** The land will mourn, each clan by itself: the clan of the house of David by itself and their wives by themselves; the clan of the house of Nathan[^14] by itself and their wives by themselves;

**13** the clan of the house of Levi by itself and their wives by themselves; the clan of the Shimeites[^15] by itself and their wives by themselves;

**14** and all the clans that are left, each clan by itself and their wives by themselves.

[^1]: Hebrew *massa*. See 9:1.
[^2]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of the LORD", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verse 4.
[^4]: Literally "a basin of reeling". Compare the cup of the LORD's anger in Isaiah 51:17, 22.
[^5]: The Hebrew is difficult. Literally "and also against Judah it will be in the siege against Jerusalem".
[^6]: Panic, madness and blindness are the three curses of Deuteronomy 28:28.
[^7]: Hebrew *allufim*, clans or clan chiefs (9:7). Also in verse 6.
[^8]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^9]: Literally "Jerusalem will again dwell in its place, in Jerusalem".
[^10]: Hebrew *elohim*, "God", or "a divine being".
[^11]: Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit, wind, breath". *Grace and pleading* may mean a spirit that receives favor and prays for it.
[^12]: Literally "They will look to me, whom they have pierced". The Hebrew then changes to "him". Some Hebrew manuscripts read "to him". The Septuagint reads "They will look to me, because they danced in triumph", reading a Hebrew word that differs by one letter. John 19:37 quotes this line as "They will look on the one whom they have pierced", of the death of Jesus, and Revelation 1:7 echoes it.
[^13]: Perhaps the mourning for King Josiah, who was killed at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29–30; 2 Chronicles 35:22–25), if Hadad-rimmon is a place nearby. Or Hadad-rimmon may be a storm god whose death was mourned each year.
[^14]: A son of David (2 Samuel 5:14; Luke 3:31).
[^15]: Shimei was a grandson of Levi (Numbers 3:17–18, 21).
