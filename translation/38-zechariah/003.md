**1** Then he showed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD,[^1] and the Accuser[^2] standing at his right hand to accuse him.

**2** The LORD said to the Accuser, "The LORD rebuke you, Accuser! The LORD, who has chosen Jerusalem, rebuke you![^3] Is not this man a burning stick snatched from the fire?"[^4]

**3** Now Joshua was dressed in filthy clothes[^5] as he stood before the angel.

**4** The angel said to those who were standing before him, "Take the filthy clothes off him." And to Joshua he said, "See, I have taken your guilt away from you, and I will clothe you in festal robes."

**5** Then I said, "Let them put a clean turban[^6] on his head." So they put a clean turban on his head and clothed him with garments, while the angel of the LORD stood by.

**6** Then the angel of the LORD solemnly charged Joshua:

**7** "This is what the LORD of Armies[^7] says: If you walk in my ways and keep my charge,[^8] then you will govern my house and have charge of my courts, and I will give you the right to come and go[^9] among these who are standing here.

**8** Listen, Joshua the high priest, you and your colleagues who sit before you, for they are men who are a sign:[^10] I am going to bring my servant the Branch.[^11]

**9** For look, the stone that I have set before Joshua: on one stone there are seven eyes.[^12] I am going to engrave an inscription on it, declares the LORD of Armies,[^13] and I will remove the guilt of this land in a single day.

**10** On that day, declares the LORD of Armies, each of you will invite his neighbor to sit under his vine and under his fig tree."[^14]

[^1]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^2]: Hebrew *ha-satan*, "the adversary, the accuser", with the article: a title rather than a name. Traditionally "Satan". *To accuse him* is *le-sitno*, from the same root. The accuser stood at the right hand of the accused in a court (Psalm 109:6). Also in verse 2.
[^3]: Jude 9 echoes these words: "The Lord rebuke you."
[^4]: Joshua and his people have been rescued from the fire of the exile (compare Amos 4:11).
[^5]: Hebrew *tso'im*, "soiled with excrement", the filthiest of words. Also in verse 4.
[^6]: Hebrew *tsanif*, a word related to *mitsnefet*, the turban of the high priest (Exodus 28:4, 36–39).
[^7]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel.
[^8]: Hebrew *mishmeret*, the duties of the priests in the sanctuary (Leviticus 8:35; Numbers 3:7).
[^9]: Literally "places to walk", perhaps access to the council of heaven (compare Jeremiah 23:18, 22).
[^10]: Hebrew *mofet*, "sign, portent", a sign of things to come (Isaiah 8:18).
[^11]: Hebrew *tsemach*, "sprout, growth", a title of a coming king of David's line (Jeremiah 23:5; 33:15; Zechariah 6:12). The Aramaic Targum reads "my servant the anointed one (Messiah)".
[^12]: The meaning of the stone is uncertain. It may be a stone for the temple, or a gem for the high priest's turban, which was engraved "Holy to the LORD" (Exodus 28:36–38). *Eyes* may mean "facets" or "springs". Compare 4:10.
[^13]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH tseva'ot*, "the declaration of the LORD of Armies", a formula that marks a prophet's words as the LORD's own solemn statement. Also in verse 10.
[^14]: A picture of peace and safety (1 Kings 5:5, 4:25 in most English Bibles; Micah 4:4).
