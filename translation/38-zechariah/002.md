**1** Then I looked up and saw four horns.[^1]

**2** I asked the angel who was speaking with me, "What are these?" He said to me, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, Israel and Jerusalem."

**3** Then Yahweh[^2] showed me four craftsmen.[^3]

**4** I asked, "What are these coming to do?" He said, "These are the horns that scattered Judah, so that no one could lift up his head. But these craftsmen have come to terrify them, to throw down the horns of the nations that lifted up their horn against the land of Judah to scatter it."

**5** Then I looked up and saw a man with a measuring line in his hand.

**6** I asked, "Where are you going?" He said to me, "To measure Jerusalem, to see how wide it is and how long."

**7** Then the angel who was speaking with me came forward, and another angel came out to meet him.

**8** He said to him, "Run, tell that young man:[^4] 'Jerusalem will be inhabited like open villages without walls,[^5] because of the great number of people and animals in it.

**9** And I myself will be a wall of fire around it, declares Yahweh,[^6] and I will be the glory within it.'"

**10** Come! Come![^7] Flee from the land of the north, declares Yahweh, for I have scattered you like the four winds of the heavens,[^8] declares Yahweh.

**11** Come, Zion! Escape, you who live with Daughter Babylon!

**12** For this is what Yahweh of Armies[^9] says, he who sent me after glory[^10] to the nations that plundered you: Whoever touches you touches the pupil of his eye.[^11]

**13** For look, I am going to wave my hand against them,[^12] and they will become plunder for those who served them. Then you will know that Yahweh of Armies has sent me.

**14** Sing and rejoice, Daughter Zion,[^13] for look, I am coming, and I will live among you,[^14] declares Yahweh.

**15** Many nations will join themselves to Yahweh on that day and become my people. I will live among you, and you will know that Yahweh of Armies has sent me to you.

**16** Yahweh will take possession of Judah as his portion in the holy land,[^15] and he will again choose Jerusalem.

**17** Be silent, all humankind,[^16] before Yahweh, for he has roused himself from his holy dwelling.

[^1]: Verses 1–4 are 1:18–21 in most English Bibles, and verses 5–17 are 2:1–13. Horns are a picture of strength, here of the nations that conquered Israel and Judah.
[^2]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles.
[^3]: Hebrew *charashim*, skilled workers in metal, wood or stone, who would cut the horns down.
[^4]: Probably the man with the measuring line (verse 5), or Zechariah himself.
[^5]: Hebrew *perazot*, "open country, unwalled villages". The city will be too large for walls.
[^6]: Hebrew *ne'um YHWH*, "the declaration of Yahweh", a formula that marks a prophet's words as Yahweh's own solemn statement. Also in verses 10 and 14.
[^7]: Hebrew *hoy*, usually "Woe to" (Amos 5:18). Here, and in verse 11, it is a call for attention. Verses 10–17 call the exiles home from Babylon, the "land of the north", from which invaders came to Israel.
[^8]: The Septuagint reads "I will gather you from the four winds of the heavens".
[^9]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. Also in verses 13 and 15.
[^10]: The meaning is uncertain. Literally "after glory he sent me". Perhaps "for the sake of his glory", or "after the Glory sent me". The speaker may be the prophet or the angel.
[^11]: Literally "the gate of his eye", the pupil, which is guarded with great care (Deuteronomy 32:10). This is one of the *tiqqune sopherim*, the rabbinic "corrections of the scribes": the tradition says that the text once read "my eye" and was changed out of reverence for God.
[^12]: A gesture of threat (Isaiah 11:15; 19:16).
[^13]: A poetic name for Jerusalem and its people, pictured as a young woman. Compare Zephaniah 3:14.
[^14]: Hebrew *shakhan*, the verb used of Yahweh's dwelling among his people in the tabernacle (Exodus 25:8). Also in verse 15.
[^15]: The only place in the Bible where the land is called "holy".
[^16]: Literally "all flesh". Compare Habakkuk 2:20; Zephaniah 1:7.
