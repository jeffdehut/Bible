# Translation review, October 2026

A read of the whole translation against TRANSLATION.md: a script for the rules it can test
(`scripts/consistency.py`, `translation/CONSISTENCY.md`) and a careful read of every verse and note,
book by book, for the rest. 147 clear breaches with a certain fix were corrected (see the commit); the 259 findings below wait for a person: first the questions of policy the read raised, then the findings by confidence.

## Questions of policy

**Decided, 30 October 2026.** The owner accepted the proposed answer to each question below;
they are recorded in TRANSLATION.md (the checklist, and the table "Decided in the October 2026
review") and applied throughout the translation, with the findings they settled. In Numbers,
*chuqqim u-mishpatim* follows the rest of the Torah and the glossary: "decrees and rulings". The
findings further down that the decisions did not settle are still open.

1. **The messenger formula.** TRANSLATION.md says "This is what the LORD says: …" inside a message takes a colon and no quotation marks of its own. The drafts are split: some books follow it, others open single quotes after the colon, especially where a prophet speaks directly (1 Samuel 2:27, 10:18, 15:2; 1 Kings 13:2) and for a king's message ("This is what Ben-hadad says"). Decide whether the rule covers direct speech and human messengers; about 35 findings (Exodus, Joshua, Judges, 1–2 Kings) wait on it.
2. **Measurements.** The checklist says every measurement in a passage of measurements is in digits (Exodus 26, Ezekiel 40–48), but Genesis 6:15, Exodus 25–27 and 36–38, 1 Kings 6–7 and Ezekiel 40–47 are in words; only Ezekiel 48 (and now 45, to match it) uses digits. Change the rule (digits only where some number needs them) or convert the passages.
3. **The unit of the numbers rule.** "A list is the paragraph its numbers belong to" splits some tallies (Judges 20, 1 Kings 5:27–30, 1 Chronicles 25). Decide whether a tally can span paragraphs.
4. **Quotation marks for songs and the LORD's words in poems.** Deuteronomy 32–33 are unquoted, Judges 5 is quoted; the Psalms quote God's words throughout (46:11, 75:3–6, 87:4, 89:4–5, 91:14–16); Hosea 6:1–3 quotes an unintroduced speech. Decide how the rule applies to songs and to the Psalms.
5. **Terms used two ways:** "holy assembly" / "sacred assembly" (*miqra qodesh*); "bread made without yeast" / "unleavened bread" (*matsot*); "firepan" / "censer" (*machtah*); "beer" / "strong drink" / "fermented drink" (*shekhar*); "fellowship offering" / "sacrifice"; "decrees, rulings" against "statutes, regulations, rules" in Numbers; "Shephelah" / "western foothills"; "iniquity" in Lamentations against "guilt" in Jeremiah; "Alas" (Jeremiah) against the glossary's "Ah" for a mourning *hoy*; "As surely as the LORD lives" / "As the LORD lives"; "inquired of" / "asked" the LORD.
6. **Capitals:** "Daughter Zion" / "daughter Zion" (and Babylon, Egypt, Sidon); "King of the Jews" in John against Mark and Luke.
7. **Ketiv/qere in names** that differ only by a vowel letter (Doeg, Abigail, Naioth, Luhith, Memucan, Ahasuerus): note them or treat them as spelling. Adding a note renumbers the chapter's notes.
8. **Following the Hebrew or the versions:** Zechariah 14:5 ("with him" from the versions against the Hebrew "with you"); 2 Chronicles 1:13 ("from" against the Hebrew "to").
9. **Supplied subjects:** Revelation writes "the angel" where the Greek has "he" (19:9, 22:1), and Hebrews 10:5 "Christ says". The checklist keeps the pronoun and puts the noun in a note.
10. **New Testament variant wording** not covered by the rules: words the SBLGNT leaves out following the Byzantine text or Westcott and Hort's margin; whether "The SBLGNT and other critical editions leave it out" needs qualifying where Tregelles includes the verse (Mark 7:16, 9:44, 46); whether short Byzantine additions (John 1:27, 1:51, 7:46, 10:14) should be noted; an English verse number inside an existing parenthesis ("Psalm 89:11, 89:10 in most English Bibles").
11. **Smaller choices:** the shekel's weight written "⅖ ounce" or "2/5 ounce"; Hebrews' articled *Christos* as "the Messiah" every time; *imrah* outside Psalm 119 (Psalm 138:2, 147:15); "brothers" in Acts 3:17, 9:30, 1 Timothy 6:2.

Proposed: “to the family of his bride (Exodus 22:15–16 (22:16–17 in most English Bibles)).” → “to the family of his bride: Exodus 22:15–16 (22:16–17 in most English Bibles).”.
- Acts 19, note 25: The checklist writes English verse numbers in the form "(89:52 in most English Bibles)". This note uses a different wording. It is a minor point of form. Proposed: “[^25]: In most English Bibles, this sentence is verse 41.” → “[^25]: This sentence is 19:41 in most English Bibles.”.
- 2 Corinthians 13, note 9: This verse-numbering note does not use the standard form "(13:14 in most English Bibles)". It also sits on verse 12, though the verse whose number differs is verse 13. It is a minor point of form.

**Text: supplied subject kept minimal (Mark 10:6)**

- Revelation 19:9: The Greek has καὶ λέγει μοι, 'he said to me', and note 12 says so. The text supplies 'the angel' where the checklist would have 'he', with the angel in the note. The same thing happens at 22:1 and Hebrews 10:5, so the owner may want one policy for all of them.
- Revelation 22:1: The Greek has ἔδειξέν μοι, 'he showed me', and note 1 says 'Literally "Then he showed me", the angel of 21:9'. Here too a noun is supplied in the text instead of in the note.
- 1 John 5:16: The Greek has δώσει αὐτῷ ζωήν, 'he will give him life'. The text supplies 'God', and note 13 admits the subject may instead be the one who prays. Under the checklist the text would say 'he', with God in the note. This is a judgment call, because 'he' is ambiguous here.

**Notes: accuracy**

- Leviticus 6, note 6: Verse 15 renders חָק־עוֹלָם "a permanent decree", so "Also in verse 15" wrongly suggests "share" there. Proposed: “"a fixed portion, a rule". Also in verse 15.” → “"a fixed portion, a rule". In verse 15 the same word is translated "decree".”.
- Leviticus 14, note 8: The note quotes wording that is not in verse 30 ("from whatever he can afford") or verse 31 ("From what he can afford"). Proposed: “The words "whichever he can afford" end verse 30” → “The words "from whatever he can afford" end verse 30”.

**Consistency: parallel passage**

- Judges 1:15: Judges 1:12–15 repeats Joshua 15:16–19 word for word in Hebrew here (וְנָתַתָּה לִי גֻּלֹּת מָיִם). Joshua 15:19 reads "give me springs of water as well"; the parallel should match. Proposed: “give me springs of water too.” → “give me springs of water as well.”.
- Judges 2:9: The Hebrew (וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אוֹתוֹ בִּגְבוּל נַחֲלָתוֹ) is the same as in Joshua 24:30, which reads "They buried him within the territory of his inheritance". Judges 2:6–9 repeats Joshua 24:28–31 (note 7). Proposed: “They buried him in the territory he had inherited,” → “They buried him within the territory of his inheritance,”.

**Notes: standard forms**

- Deuteronomy 32, note 30: This sentence is the standard wording for New Testament notes on quotations. In an Old Testament note, where the text already reads "the LORD", it is confusing. It could be cut, or reworded as "Hebrews follows the Septuagint, which has 'Lord'".
- Romans 12, note 3: The text of 12:2 already reads "this age", so a "Literally" note giving the same words says nothing. Either the text was meant to say something else ("this world") or the note should say something else. A person should decide; removing the note would mean renumbering the notes.

**Glossary: servant/slave**

- 1 Chronicles 2:34: Hebrew eved: Jarha is the property of Sheshan, who gives him a wife, so 'slave' fits as well as 'servant'. The glossary asks for an 'Or "slave"' note where either fits, and the batch has none. Adding one would mean adding a note marker.
- Ezra 2:65: Hebrew avdehem ve-amhotehem are household slaves, counted apart from the free assembly, so either rendering fits and the glossary calls for an 'Or "slave"' note. The same applies to Nehemiah 7:67.

**Notes: a reading with different vowels is given as "The same consonants can be read …"**

- Job 5, note 5: The reading changes only the vowels (yolid for yullad). The checklist sets the wording 'The same consonants can be read …' for this. Proposed: “With a slight change of vowels, the first line can be read "human beings give birth to misery".” → “The same consonants can be read "human beings give birth to misery" in the first line.”.
- Job 21, note 12: This reading changes only the vowels of חלב, and the checklist sets the wording 'The same consonants can be read …' for that. Proposed: “with different vowels, "full of fat" (*chelev*) instead of "full of milk" (*chalav*)” → “the same consonants can be read "full of fat" (*chelev*) instead of "full of milk" (*chalav*)”.

**hallelu-yah noted at its first occurrence in each psalm**

- Psalms 105, note 23: The glossary gives the wording "Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, 'praise Yah', the source of the word 'Hallelujah'"; this note replaces it with a cross-reference. Proposed: “Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah" (see the note on 104:35).” → “Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah" (see the note on 104:35).”.
- Psalms 106, note 1: Same as 105:45: the glossary wording is shortened to a cross-reference. Proposed: “Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah" (see the note on 104:35).” → “Hebrew *hallelu-yah*, "praise Yah", the source of the word "Hallelujah" (see the note on 104:35).”.

**Servant/slave: "Or 'slave'" where either fits**

- Proverbs 17:2: Proverbs notes "Or 'slave'" at 22:7 only. Several other sayings about an *eved* plainly have the household slave in view and have no note: 17:2 (the slave who inherits with the sons), 29:19, 29:21, 30:10 and 30:22. A person should decide which of these need the note, keeping the numbering in order.
- Isaiah 14:2: לַעֲבָדִים וְלִשְׁפָחוֹת: peoples taken as possessions are slaves in the institutional sense. The text could have "slaves", or keep "servants" with an "Or 'slaves'" note.

**New Testament variant wording**

- Mark 1:34: Apparatus: αὐτόν Treg NA28 RP ] + χριστὸν εἶναι WH. A reading of WH that adds words ('because they knew he was the Messiah') changes the meaning but has no note ('Some manuscripts read …'). The fix would add a note.
- Mark 10:29: Apparatus: μητέρα ἢ πατέρα WH Treg NA28 ] πατέρα ἢ μητέρα ἢ γυναῖκα RP. RP adds 'or wife', which changes the meaning, but there is no note; the parallel Matthew 19:29 notes the same words. The fix would add a note ('Many manuscripts add "or wife".').

**Numbers**

- Genesis 47:28: Numbers counted together in one sentence form a list; since 147 needs digits, 17 should be digits too. Worth a look: the two figures are a span and an age. Proposed: “seventeen years, and the length” → “17 years, and the length”.

**Consistency within the batch (words for God's law)**

- Genesis 26:5: מִצְוֹת and חֻקּוֹת are "commands" and "decrees" in Exodus (15:26; 16:28; 18:16; 20:6), as the glossary sets them; "statutes" is reserved for עֵדוֹת. Proposed: “my commandments, my statutes and my laws” → “my commands, my decrees and my laws”.

**Numbers: a census uses digits throughout**

- Numbers 3, note 10: Follows the change in verse 39 and matches "22,300" earlier in the same note. Proposed: “verse 39 gives a total of twenty-two thousand” → “verse 39 gives a total of 22,000”.

**Numbers: a calculation uses digits throughout**

- Numbers 3:47: Verses 46–50 are a calculation (273 × 5 = 1,365 shekels); strictly all its numbers are digits. Worth a look rather than a certain fix. Proposed: “collect five shekels” → “collect 5 shekels”.

**Glossary: shelamim = "fellowship offering"**

- Numbers 7:17: זֶבַח הַשְּׁלָמִים is "fellowship offering" in Leviticus 3:1 and elsewhere; Numbers 6:17–18, 7:17–88 and 10:10 use "fellowship sacrifice(s)", while 15:8 and 29:39 use "fellowship offering(s)".

**Numbers: battle tally in digits**

- Judges 20:2, v15–17, v21, v25, v34: The checklist names Judges 20 as its example of a battle tally set in digits throughout. The draft uses digits only in vv35–47 and words for the earlier tallies (400,000; 26,000 and 700; 22,000; 18,000; 10,000), so the chapter gives 18,000 as "eighteen thousand" in v25 and "18,000" in v44. The owner should decide whether the tally is the whole chapter or only the paragraph.

**Messenger formula / consistency within batch**

- Joshua 22:16: "This is what the whole community of the LORD says:" is the messenger formula (koh amru). Judges 11:15 sets the same human-sender formula without marks; this passage puts the message in single quotes through v20. If changed, v20 ends `wrongdoing."` instead of `wrongdoing.'"`. Proposed: “says: 'What is this unfaithfulness” → “says: What is this unfaithfulness”.

**Quotation conventions: songs introduced as speech**

- Deuteronomy 33:2: The Blessing of Moses (33:2–29) and the Song of Moses (32:1–43, introduced at 31:30) are introduced as speech but have no quotation marks. The Song of Deborah (Judges 5:2–31) has them. The checklist both quotes "a song … the text introduces as speech" and cites Deuteronomy 32 as unmarked, so the owner should decide which treatment the batch follows.

**Ketiv/qere: give the ketiv where the English would differ**

- 1 Samuel 20:2: The Hebrew is written לו עשה, "if my father had done", but read לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה, "does not do". The English would differ, so a note seems needed (for example in note 1: "The Hebrew text is written 'If my father had done' but read 'My father does not do'.").

**Quotation conventions: messenger formula**

- 2 Kings 1:9: Hebrew הַמֶּלֶךְ דִּבֶּר רֵדָה, 'the king has spoken: Come down!', is the same kind of formula as v11 (כֹּה אָמַר הַמֶּלֶךְ). Setting both verses the same way keeps them consistent. Proposed: “the king says, 'Come down!'"” → “the king says: Come down!"”.

**Notes: standard forms (Literally …)**

- 2 Kings 23, note 19: The note explains a 'right' that the reader cannot see in the text ('south of'). It should give the literal wording first, as the batch does elsewhere (1 Kings 6:8 note 16). Proposed: “On a person facing east, "right" is south.” → “"South of" is literally "to the right of": facing east, the right hand is south.”.

**Notes: no claim of evidence not sure of (OT variants)**

- 2 Kings 10, note 1: The Vulgate does have 'principes civitatis'. But the main Septuagint text (Vaticanus) reads 'the rulers of Samaria'; 'of the city' is the Lucianic reading. The note should be checked against an apparatus, and may need to say 'some Septuagint manuscripts' or name the Vulgate alone.

**Numbers: passage of measurements**

- 2 Chronicles 3:3: 2 Chronicles 3:3-17 is a passage of temple measurements, and the checklist says every measurement in such a passage is in digits. Note that 1 Kings 6 and Exodus 26 also keep words where no number needs digits, so the owner should decide whether that rule means all measurements or only those in a list with digits. Proposed: “was sixty cubits, and the width twenty cubits.” → “was 60 cubits, and the width 20 cubits.”.

**Notes: conjectural emendations worded as such**

- 2 Chronicles 20, note 3: Reading Edom for Aram is an emendation. The checklist sets the wording 'Some scholars read …, with a small change to the Hebrew'. Proposed: “Many interpreters read *Edom*, which differs from *Aram* by one letter in Hebrew.” → “Some scholars read *Edom*, with a small change to the Hebrew (*Edom* and *Aram* differ by one letter).”.

**Glossary: plural avot = ancestors**

- Ezra 4:15: Aramaic avahatakh means the king's predecessors, not his literal fathers. The glossary renders the plural as 'ancestors'; 'predecessors' would also do. Proposed: “in the record books of your fathers.” → “in the record books of your ancestors.”.

**Numbers: words for one to one hundred**

- 1 Chronicles 25:10: No number in the list of lots in verses 9-31 needs digits, so 'twelve' would be the rule unless the list is treated together with the total of 288 in verse 7. The owner should decide. The phrase recurs in every verse from 10 to 31.

**Notes: wording of conjectural emendations**

- Job 3, note 8: Reading ים for יום drops a consonant, so this is an emendation, not a vowel reading. The checklist says to word it 'Some scholars read …, with a small change to the Hebrew'. Proposed: “Some interpreters read *yam*, "the Sea", for *yom*, "day", in the first line, as a parallel to Leviathan.” → “Some scholars read *yam*, "the Sea", for *yom*, "day", in the first line, with a small change to the Hebrew, as a parallel to Leviathan.”.

**Glossary: servant/slave, "Or 'slave'" where either fits**

- Job 19:16: The Hebrew is עַבְדִּי, a household servant who could equally be a slave, and the maidservants of v15 (אַמְהֹתַי) are the same case. The parallel in 31:13 ('my male or female servant') has the note "Or 'slave'"; here there is none.

**Words for God's law: imrah is "word" outside Psalm 119**

- Psalms 147:15: *Imrato* is rendered "his command", the English reserved for *mitsvah*; the glossary says *imrah* is "word" outside Psalm 119. Probably chosen to avoid "his word ... his word" (v15b has *devaro*); note 11 explains. Owner to decide whether to keep.

**Consistency: transliteration**

- Psalms 136, note 4: 106:7 (note 7) has *yam suf*; 136:13 has *Yam Suph*. The checklist asks for simple transliteration, kept the same. Proposed: “Hebrew *Yam Suph*, literally "Sea of Reeds".” → “Hebrew *yam suf*, literally "Sea of Reeds".”.

**Divine name: GOD next to Lord**

- Isaiah 1, note 29: 1:24 is the first "the Lord GOD" in the book (הָאָדוֹן יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת), but nothing here or anywhere before 61:1 tells the reader why YHWH is written GOD rather than LORD. Proposed: “Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name YHWH.” → “Hebrew *ha-adon*, "the Lord", a title, not the name YHWH. After "Lord", God's personal name YHWH is written GOD in capitals, as in most English Bibles.”.

**Consistency: names spelled the same way**

- Jeremiah 46:11, v19; also 48:18, 50:42: Jeremiah writes "virgin daughter Egypt", "daughter Egypt" (46:19), "daughter Dibon" (48:18) and "daughter Babylon" (50:42) in lowercase. Lamentations writes "Virgin Daughter Judah", "Daughter Jerusalem" and "Daughter Edom". The owner should pick one style for this title across the batch.

**Glossary: prophetic formulas (hoy)**

- Jeremiah 22:18 (also v18 second line, 34:5, 47:6): The glossary says that where hoy is not a cry of disaster it is "Ah", with a note. Jeremiah renders the mourning hoy "Alas" every time (22:18, 34:5, 47:6). The book is consistent and noted, but it differs from the glossary wording. Either the text or the glossary should change.

**Ketiv/qere noted where the English differs**

- Jeremiah 8:6: The ketiv במרצותם can be read as plural ("their courses") and the qere בִּמְרוּצָתָם as singular. The checklist notes ketiv differences even in number only. This is a borderline case.

**Quotation conventions: a whole oracle in the LORD's voice is not quoted**

- Ezekiel 37:16-28: Everywhere else in Ezekiel an oracle introduced by "The word of the LORD came to me:" is set without quotation marks (6:2, 12:2, 13:2, 14:3, 17:2, 21:2 ...). 37:15-28, the oracle of the two sticks, is the one exception: it opens a quotation at verse 16 and closes it at verse 28. (3:17 and 11:15 are also quoted, but they are dialogue inside a vision narrative.) To match, remove the outer double marks in verses 16 and 28 and turn the inner single marks into double ones.

**Text: nothing added**

- Hosea 11:10: The Hebrew is וְיֶחֶרְדוּ בָנִים מִיָּם, 'children/sons will come trembling from the sea', with no possessive. 'His' is a small addition, though a defensible one. Proposed: “his children will come trembling from the west.” → “children will come trembling from the west.”.

**Units and money noted at first occurrence in each chapter**

- Matthew 27:3: The money unit's value is given in 26:15 note 4 but not at its first occurrence in chapter 27 (verse 3; also verses 6 and 9). A short note ('See 26:15') would meet the rule. The fix would add a note and renumber.

**NT variants: a different word that changes the meaning is noted**

- John 16:27: Apparatus at 16:27: θεοῦ NA28 RP ] πατρὸς WH Treg. 'From God' and 'from the Father' are a different word. A note such as 'Some manuscripts read "from the Father"' would follow the rule (it could be added to note 8, in the same verse). Low priority.

**Consistency within batch (and with Mark)**

- John 18:33: The Greek (Σὺ εἶ ὁ βασιλεὺς τῶν Ἰουδαίων;) is identical in Luke 23:3, which has 'king of the Jews', as Mark 15:2 does. Luke and Mark capitalize only the title on the inscription. John capitalizes it in speech too (also 18:39; 19:3; 19:21 "I am King of the Jews"). If adopted, make the same change in those verses and keep the capital in 19:19 and the inscription quoted in 19:21. Proposed: “"Are you the King of the Jews?"” → “"Are you the king of the Jews?"”.

**Notes: marker after the word or phrase it explains**

- John 7:53: Here the marker comes before the text. Mark 16:8 puts the note on bracketed text after the passage. The same applies to John 8:1 '⟦[^1]But Jesus went' → '⟦But Jesus went to the Mount of Olives.[^1]'. Note order is unaffected in both chapters. Proposed: “**53** ⟦[^19]Then each of them went home.” → “**53** ⟦Then each of them went home.[^19]”.

**Glossary: ὁ χριστός = the Messiah (as a title); consistency within a book**

- Hebrews 9:14: Hebrews has the article with Christos six times. It renders four as 'the Messiah' (3:14; 5:5; 6:1; 11:26) but 9:14 and 9:28 as 'Christ'. Note 19 explains that the article refers back to verse 11, so the choice is deliberate, but the book is not consistent and the owner may want to settle it.
