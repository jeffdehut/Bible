**1** After this, Nahash king of the Ammonites died,[^1] and his son became king in his place.

**2** David said, "I will show faithful love[^2] to Hanun son of Nahash, because his father showed faithful love to me." So David sent messengers to console him about his father. When David's servants came to the land of the Ammonites, to Hanun, to console him,

**3** the Ammonite commanders said to Hanun, "Do you think David is honoring your father because he has sent you comforters? Isn't it to explore, to overthrow and to spy out the land that his servants have come to you?"

**4** So Hanun took David's servants, shaved them,[^3] cut off their garments in the middle, as far as their buttocks, and sent them away.

**5** Some people went and told David about the men, and he sent people to meet them, for the men were deeply humiliated. The king said, "Stay in Jericho until your beards have grown, and then come back."

**6** When the Ammonites saw that they had made themselves a stench to David,[^4] Hanun and the Ammonites sent a thousand talents[^5] of silver to hire chariots and horsemen from Aram-naharaim,[^6] from Aram-maacah and from Zobah.

**7** They hired thirty-two thousand chariots, and the king of Maacah with his army, who came and camped before Medeba.[^7] The Ammonites gathered from their towns and came out to battle.

**8** When David heard of it, he sent Joab with the whole army of warriors.[^8]

**9** The Ammonites came out and drew up in battle formation at the entrance of the city, while the kings who had come were by themselves in the open country.

**10** When Joab saw that the battle was set against him both in front and behind, he chose some of the best men of Israel and drew them up against the Arameans.

**11** The rest of the troops he put under the command of his brother Abishai, and they drew up against the Ammonites.

**12** He said, "If the Arameans are too strong for me, then you are to come and rescue me. But if the Ammonites are too strong for you, I will rescue you.

**13** Be strong, and let us show ourselves strong for the sake of our people and for the cities of our God. And may the LORD[^9] do what seems good to him."

**14** So Joab and the troops who were with him advanced to fight against the Arameans, and they fled before him.

**15** When the Ammonites saw that the Arameans had fled, they too fled before his brother Abishai and went into the city. Then Joab came to Jerusalem.

**16** When the Arameans saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they sent messengers and brought out the Arameans who were beyond the River,[^10] with Shophach[^11] the commander of Hadadezer's army at their head.

**17** When David was told, he gathered all Israel, crossed the Jordan, came to them and drew up in battle formation against them.[^12] David drew up in battle formation to meet the Arameans, and they fought with him.

**18** But the Arameans fled before Israel, and David killed seven thousand of the Aramean charioteers[^13] and forty thousand foot soldiers. He also put to death Shophach the commander of the army.

**19** When Hadadezer's servants[^14] saw that they had been defeated by Israel, they made peace with David and became subject to him. So the Arameans were no longer willing to help the Ammonites.

[^1]: Chapter 19 parallels 2 Samuel 10.
[^2]: Hebrew *chesed*: loyal, faithful love, here the loyalty owed between allies.
[^3]: 2 Samuel 10:4 reads "shaved off half of each man's beard".
[^4]: That is, they had made David their bitter enemy.
[^5]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg), so a thousand talents was about 37 tons (34 metric tons).
[^6]: That is, "Aram of the two rivers", in northern Mesopotamia.
[^7]: 2 Samuel 10:6 says instead that the Ammonites hired twenty thousand foot soldiers from Beth-rehob and Zobah, the king of Maacah with a thousand men, and twelve thousand men of Tob.
[^8]: Literally "all the army, the mighty men".
[^9]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^10]: That is, the Euphrates.
[^11]: 2 Samuel 10:16 has Shobach.
[^12]: 2 Samuel 10:17 reads "and came to Helam".
[^13]: Literally "seven thousand chariots", meaning the men in them. 2 Samuel 10:18 has "seven hundred" charioteers and "forty thousand horsemen".
[^14]: 2 Samuel 10:19 has "all the kings who were subject to Hadadezer".
