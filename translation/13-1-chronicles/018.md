**1** After this, David defeated the Philistines and subdued them, and he took Gath and its towns[^1] out of the hand of the Philistines.

**2** He also defeated Moab, and the Moabites became subject[^2] to David and brought him tribute.[^3]

**3** David also defeated Hadadezer king of Zobah, near Hamath, when he went to set up his control[^4] at the River Euphrates.

**4** David captured from him a thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen and twenty thousand foot soldiers.[^5] David hamstrung all the chariot horses, but he kept back a hundred of them for chariots.

**5** When the Arameans of Damascus came to help Hadadezer king of Zobah, David struck down twenty-two thousand of the Arameans.

**6** Then David put garrisons[^6] in Aram of Damascus, and the Arameans became subject to David and brought him tribute. The LORD[^7] gave David victory wherever he went.

**7** David took the gold shields[^8] that belonged to the officers of Hadadezer and brought them to Jerusalem.

**8** And from Tibhath and Cun, towns of Hadadezer, David took a very large amount of bronze. With it Solomon made the bronze Sea,[^9] the pillars and the bronze utensils.

**9** When Tou[^10] king of Hamath heard that David had defeated the whole army of Hadadezer king of Zobah,

**10** he sent his son Hadoram[^11] to King David to greet him[^12] and to congratulate him because he had fought against Hadadezer and defeated him, for Hadadezer had often been at war with Tou.[^13] He also sent all kinds of articles of gold, silver and bronze.

**11** King David dedicated these also to the LORD, along with the silver and gold that he had carried off from all the nations: from Edom, from Moab, from the Ammonites, from the Philistines and from Amalek.

**12** Abishai son of Zeruiah struck down eighteen thousand Edomites in the Valley of Salt.[^14]

**13** He put garrisons in Edom, and all the Edomites became subject to David. The LORD gave David victory wherever he went.

**14** David reigned over all Israel, and he did what was just and right[^15] for all his people.

**15** Joab son of Zeruiah was over the army. Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder.[^16]

**16** Zadok son of Ahitub and Abimelech[^17] son of Abiathar were priests. Shavsha was secretary.[^18]

**17** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites.[^19] And David's sons were the chief officials at the king's side.[^20]

[^1]: Literally "daughters", the smaller settlements around a town. Chapter 18 parallels 2 Samuel 8, which has "Metheg-ammah" here.
[^2]: Literally "became servants". Also in verses 6 and 13.
[^3]: 2 Samuel 8:2 also says that David put two-thirds of the Moabites to death.
[^4]: Literally "his hand", perhaps meaning a monument marking his rule. It is not clear whether "he" is David or Hadadezer.
[^5]: 2 Samuel 8:4 has "1,700 horsemen and 20,000 foot soldiers".
[^6]: Or "governors". The Hebrew lacks the word here; it is supplied from 2 Samuel 8:6. Hebrew *netsivim* is used in verse 13.
[^7]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^8]: Hebrew *shiltei*, a word of uncertain meaning: shields, or perhaps quivers.
[^9]: The great bronze basin in the courtyard of the temple (1 Kings 7:23–26). 2 Samuel 8:8 has "Betah and Berothai" for Tibhath and Cun, and does not mention Solomon.
[^10]: 2 Samuel 8:9 has Toi.
[^11]: 2 Samuel 8:10 has Joram.
[^12]: Literally "to ask him about his well-being".
[^13]: Literally "for Hadadezer was a man of wars of Tou".
[^14]: 2 Samuel 8:13 reads "David made a name for himself when he returned from striking down Aram in the Valley of Salt, eighteen thousand of them."
[^15]: Literally "was doing justice and righteousness".
[^16]: Hebrew *mazkir*, "one who causes to remember": a high official, perhaps the king's herald or keeper of records.
[^17]: 2 Samuel 8:17 has Ahimelech. Elsewhere Abiathar is the son of Ahimelech (1 Samuel 22:20).
[^18]: Hebrew *sopher*, "scribe", the royal secretary. 2 Samuel 8:17 has Seraiah, and 2 Samuel 20:25 has Sheva.
[^19]: Foreign soldiers who served as David's personal guard. The Cherethites were probably from Crete, and the Pelethites may have been Philistines.
[^20]: Literally "the first at the hand of the king". 2 Samuel 8:18 reads "And David's sons were priests."
