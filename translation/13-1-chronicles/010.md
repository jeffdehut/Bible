**1** Now the Philistines fought against Israel.[^1] The men of Israel fled before the Philistines, and many fell dead[^2] on Mount Gilboa.

**2** The Philistines pursued Saul and his sons closely, and the Philistines struck down Jonathan, Abinadab and Malchi-shua, the sons of Saul.

**3** The battle pressed hard against Saul. The archers found him, and he was wounded by the archers.[^3]

**4** Saul said to his armor-bearer, "Draw your sword and run me through with it, or these uncircumcised men will come and abuse me."[^4] But his armor-bearer would not, for he was very much afraid. So Saul took the sword and fell on it.

**5** When his armor-bearer saw that Saul was dead, he too fell on the sword and died.

**6** So Saul died, and his three sons, and all his house died together.[^5]

**7** When all the men of Israel who were in the valley saw that they had fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they abandoned their towns and fled. And the Philistines came and lived in them.

**8** The next day, when the Philistines came to strip the dead, they found Saul and his sons lying dead[^6] on Mount Gilboa.

**9** They stripped him and took his head and his armor, and they sent messengers throughout the land of the Philistines to spread the good news to their idols and to the people.

**10** They put his armor in the temple of their gods, and they fastened his skull in the temple of Dagon.[^7]

**11** When all Jabesh-gilead heard everything that the Philistines had done to Saul,

**12** all the valiant men set out. They took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons and brought them to Jabesh. They buried their bones under the oak[^8] at Jabesh, and they fasted for seven days.[^9]

**13** So Saul died because of his unfaithfulness to Yahweh.[^10] He did not keep the word of Yahweh, and he even consulted a medium[^11] for guidance

**14** instead of seeking guidance from Yahweh. So he put him to death and turned the kingdom over to David son of Jesse.

[^1]: Verses 1–12 parallel 1 Samuel 31:1–13.
[^2]: Literally "fell slain".
[^3]: Or "he was terrified of the archers".
[^4]: Hebrew *hit'allel*, "deal ruthlessly with, make sport of". 1 Samuel 31:4 reads "will come and run me through and abuse me".
[^5]: 1 Samuel 31:6 reads "So Saul, his three sons, his armor-bearer and all his men died together on that day."
[^6]: Literally "fallen".
[^7]: Literally "the house of their gods … the house of Dagon". 1 Samuel 31:10 reads "They put his armor in the temple of the Ashtoreths, and they fastened his body to the wall of Beth-shan." Dagon was a god of the Philistines (1 Samuel 5:2).
[^8]: Hebrew *elah*, a large tree, perhaps a terebinth. 1 Samuel 31:13 has "the tamarisk tree".
[^9]: 1 Samuel 31:12 adds that they traveled all night to take the bodies from the wall of Beth-shan, and that they burned them at Jabesh.
[^10]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Compare 1 Samuel 13:13–14; 15:22–23; 28:6–19.
[^11]: Hebrew *ov*: a spirit of the dead, or a person who claimed to call one up and consult it (1 Samuel 28:7).
