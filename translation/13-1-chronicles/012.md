**1** These are the men who came to David at Ziklag while he was still kept away from Saul son of Kish.[^1] They were among the mighty warriors who helped him in battle.

**2** They were armed with bows, and they could use both the right hand and the left to sling stones and to shoot arrows with the bow. They were Saul's kinsmen[^2] from Benjamin.

**3** The chief was Ahiezer, then Joash, the sons of Shemaah the Gibeathite; Jeziel[^3] and Pelet, the sons of Azmaveth; Beracah; Jehu the Anathothite;

**4** Ishmaiah the Gibeonite, a mighty warrior among the Thirty and over the Thirty;

**5** Jeremiah,[^4] Jahaziel, Johanan, Jozabad the Gederathite;

**6** Eluzai, Jerimoth, Bealiah, Shemariah, Shephatiah the Haruphite;[^5]

**7** Elkanah, Isshiah, Azarel, Joezer and Jashobeam, the Korahites;

**8** and Joelah and Zebadiah, the sons of Jeroham of Gedor.

**9** From the Gadites some went over to David at the stronghold in the wilderness. They were mighty warriors, men trained for war, armed with shield and spear. Their faces were like the faces of lions, and they were as swift as gazelles on the mountains.

**10** Ezer was the chief, Obadiah the second, Eliab the third,

**11** Mishmannah the fourth, Jeremiah the fifth,

**12** Attai the sixth, Eliel the seventh,

**13** Johanan the eighth, Elzabad the ninth,

**14** Jeremiah the tenth, Machbannai the eleventh.

**15** These Gadites were commanders of the army. The least was a match for a hundred, and the greatest for a thousand.[^6]

**16** These are the men who crossed the Jordan in the first month, when it was overflowing all its banks, and put to flight all those in the valleys, to the east and to the west.

**17** Some of the Benjaminites and men of Judah also came to David at the stronghold.

**18** David went out to meet them and said to them, "If you have come to me in peace, to help me, my heart will be united with you. But if you have come to betray me to my enemies, though there is no violence in my hands, may the God of our ancestors[^7] see it and judge."

**19** Then the Spirit took possession of[^8] Amasai, the chief of the officers,[^9] and he said, "We are yours, David! We are with you, son of Jesse![^10] Peace, peace to you, and peace to those who help you, for your God helps you." So David received them and made them officers of his troops.

**20** Some from Manasseh deserted to David when he came with the Philistines to fight against Saul. But they did not help them,[^11] because the rulers[^12] of the Philistines consulted together and sent him away, saying, "At the cost of our heads he will desert to his master Saul."

**21** As he went to Ziklag, these men of Manasseh deserted to him: Adnah, Jozabad, Jediael, Michael, Jozabad, Elihu and Zillethai, commanders of thousands in Manasseh.

**22** They helped David against the raiding band,[^13] for they were all mighty warriors, and they became commanders in the army.

**23** Day after day men came to David to help him, until there was a great army, like the army of God.[^14]

**24** These are the numbers of the divisions[^15] armed for war, who came to David at Hebron to turn the kingdom of Saul over to him, according to the word of Yahweh:[^16]

**25** Of the people of Judah, carrying shield and spear: 6,800 armed for war.

**26** Of the Simeonites, mighty warriors for war: 7,100.

**27** Of the Levites: 4,600.

**28** Jehoiada, the leader of the house of Aaron,[^17] and with him 3,700.

**29** Zadok, a young man, a mighty warrior, and 22 commanders from his ancestral family.[^18]

**30** Of the Benjaminites, Saul's kinsmen: 3,000. Until then most of them had kept their allegiance to the house of Saul.

**31** Of the Ephraimites: 20,800 mighty warriors, famous men in their ancestral families.

**32** Of the half-tribe of Manasseh: 18,000, who were designated by name to come and make David king.

**33** Of the Issacharites, men who understood the times and knew what Israel should do: 200 chiefs, with all their kinsmen under their command.[^19]

**34** Of Zebulun: 50,000 ready for military service, prepared for battle with every kind of weapon, to help David with undivided loyalty.[^20]

**35** Of Naphtali: 1,000 commanders, and with them 37,000 men with shield and spear.

**36** Of the Danites: 28,600 prepared for battle.

**37** Of Asher: 40,000 ready for military service, prepared for battle.

**38** From beyond the Jordan, of the Reubenites, the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh: 120,000, with every kind of military weapon.

**39** All these warriors, arrayed in battle order,[^21] came to Hebron with a whole heart to make David king over all Israel. All the rest of Israel too were of one mind to make David king.

**40** They were there with David for three days, eating and drinking, for their kinsmen had made preparations for them.

**41** Their neighbors too, from as far away as Issachar, Zebulun and Naphtali, came bringing food on donkeys, camels, mules and oxen: abundant supplies of flour, fig cakes, clusters of raisins, wine, oil, cattle and sheep, for there was joy in Israel.

[^1]: Literally "while he was still restrained because of Saul son of Kish". See 1 Samuel 27.
[^2]: Literally "brothers"; also "kinsmen" in verses 30, 33 and 40.
[^3]: The Hebrew text is written "Jezuel" but read "Jeziel".
[^4]: Verses 5–41 are 12:4–40 in most English Bibles.
[^5]: The Hebrew text is written "the Hariphite" but read "the Haruphite".
[^6]: Or "The least was over a hundred, and the greatest over a thousand", that is, as their commanders.
[^7]: Literally "fathers".
[^8]: Literally "clothed itself with". Hebrew *ruach*, "spirit", here without the article.
[^9]: The Hebrew text is written "the Thirty" but read *ha-shalishim*, perhaps "the officers"; see 11:11.
[^10]: Literally "To you, David, and with you, son of Jesse."
[^11]: That is, David and his men did not help the Philistines (1 Samuel 29).
[^12]: Hebrew *seranim*, a word used only of the rulers of the five Philistine cities.
[^13]: The Amalekites who raided Ziklag (1 Samuel 30).
[^14]: Literally "like the camp of God", perhaps meaning "a very great army".
[^15]: Literally "the heads".
[^16]: *Yahweh*: God's personal name (Hebrew YHWH), traditionally rendered "the LORD" in English Bibles. Literally "according to the mouth of Yahweh".
[^17]: Literally "the leader of Aaron".
[^18]: Literally "and the house of his father, 22 commanders".
[^19]: Literally "at their mouth".
[^20]: Literally "without a heart and a heart". The meaning of the verb translated "help" is uncertain.
[^21]: Hebrew *oderei ma'arakhah*. The verb is the one translated "help" in verse 34, and its meaning is uncertain.
