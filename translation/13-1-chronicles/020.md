**1** In the spring of the year,[^1] at the time when kings go out to battle, Joab led out the army and ravaged the land of the Ammonites. He came and besieged Rabbah, while David stayed in Jerusalem. Joab struck Rabbah and overthrew it.[^2]

**2** David took the crown of their king[^3] from his head. He found that it weighed a talent[^4] of gold, and it held a precious stone. It was placed on David's head. He also brought out a very large amount of plunder from the city.

**3** He brought out the people who were in it and cut them with saws,[^5] with iron picks and with saws.[^6] David did the same to all the towns of the Ammonites. Then David and all the troops returned to Jerusalem.

**4** After this, war broke out with the Philistines at Gezer.[^7] At that time Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai,[^8] one of the descendants of the Rephaim,[^9] and they were subdued.

**5** There was another battle with the Philistines, and Elhanan son of Jair[^10] struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite,[^11] the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam.[^12]

**6** There was yet another battle, at Gath, where there was a man of great size whose fingers and toes numbered six and six, twenty-four in all.[^13] He too was descended from the Raphah.

**7** When he taunted Israel, Jonathan son of Shimea, David's brother, struck him down.

**8** These were descended from the Raphah in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

[^1]: Literally "at the time of the return of the year". Verse 1 parallels 2 Samuel 11:1, and verses 2–3 parallel 2 Samuel 12:30–31. In between, 2 Samuel tells of David, Bathsheba and Uriah, and in 12:26–29 Joab sends for David to capture the city.
[^2]: Or "destroyed it".
[^3]: Hebrew *malkam*, "their king", which may also be read as Milcom, the god of the Ammonites (1 Kings 11:5).
[^4]: A talent was about 75 pounds (34 kg).
[^5]: Hebrew *vayyasar*, "he sawed". 2 Samuel 12:31 has *vayyasem*, "he set them (to work)", a difference of one letter.
[^6]: The Hebrew repeats "saws". 2 Samuel 12:31 has "iron axes".
[^7]: Verses 4–8 parallel 2 Samuel 21:18–22, which has Gob here.
[^8]: 2 Samuel 21:18 has Saph.
[^9]: Hebrew *ha-repha'im*, a people remembered as giants (Deuteronomy 2:11, 20). In verses 6 and 8 the singular *ha-rapha*, "the Raphah", perhaps their ancestor, is used.
[^10]: The Hebrew text is written "Jaur" but read "Jair".
[^11]: 2 Samuel 21:19 reads "Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite struck down Goliath the Gittite". In 1 Samuel 17 David kills Goliath.
[^12]: The heavy rod of a loom.
[^13]: Literally "and his digits were six and six, twenty-four". 2 Samuel 21:20 has "six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot".
