**1** All Israel gathered to David at Hebron[^1] and said, "Look, we are your own flesh and blood.[^2]

**2** For some time now,[^3] even while Saul was king, it was you who led Israel out to battle and brought it back.[^4] And the LORD[^5] your God said to you, 'You will shepherd my people Israel, and you will be leader[^6] over my people Israel.'"

**3** All the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel, according to the word of the LORD through Samuel.[^7]

**4** David and all Israel went to Jerusalem, that is, Jebus. The Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land, were there.

**5** The inhabitants of Jebus said to David, "You will not get in here."[^8] But David captured the stronghold of Zion, that is, the City of David.

**6** David said, "Whoever is first to strike down the Jebusites will become chief and commander." Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and he became chief.[^9]

**7** David lived in the stronghold. That is why it was called the City of David.

**8** He built the city all around, from the Millo[^10] to the surrounding area,[^11] and Joab restored[^12] the rest of the city.

**9** David became greater and greater, for the LORD of Armies[^13] was with him.

**10** These are the chiefs of David's mighty warriors,[^14] who, together with all Israel, gave him strong support in his kingdom, to make him king according to the word of the LORD concerning Israel.

**11** This is the list of David's mighty warriors: Jashobeam son of Hachmoni,[^15] the chief of the officers.[^16] He wielded his spear against three hundred,[^17] whom he killed at one time.

**12** Next to him was Eleazar son of Dodo, the Ahohite. He was one of the three mighty warriors.

**13** He was with David at Pas-dammim when the Philistines gathered there for battle.[^18] There was a plot of ground full of barley,[^19] and the troops fled from the Philistines.

**14** But they took their stand in the middle of the plot, defended it and struck down the Philistines. And the LORD saved them with a great victory.

**15** Three of the Thirty,[^20] the chiefs, went down to the rock,[^21] to David at the cave of Adullam, while an army of Philistines was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.

**16** At that time David was in the stronghold, and a Philistine garrison was then at Bethlehem.

**17** David felt a craving and said, "If only someone would give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate!"

**18** So the Three broke through the Philistine camp, drew water from the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate, and carried it back to David. But David would not drink it. He poured it out to the LORD

**19** and said, "Far be it from me before my God that I should do this! Shall I drink the blood of these men at the risk of their lives? For at the risk of their lives they brought it."[^22] So he would not drink it. These were the deeds of the three mighty warriors.

**20** Abishai, the brother of Joab, was chief of the Three. He wielded his spear against three hundred men and killed them, and he won a name[^23] among the Three.

**21** Of the Three he was doubly honored,[^24] and he became their commander, but he did not attain to the Three.

**22** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was the son of a valiant man from Kabzeel, a man of great deeds. He struck down the two champions[^25] of Moab. He also went down and killed a lion inside a pit on a snowy day.

**23** He also struck down an Egyptian, a man of great height, five cubits tall.[^26] The Egyptian had a spear in his hand like a weaver's beam,[^27] but Benaiah went down to him with a club, snatched the spear out of the Egyptian's hand and killed him with his own spear.

**24** These were the deeds of Benaiah son of Jehoiada, and he won a name among the three mighty warriors.

**25** He was more honored than the Thirty, but he did not attain to the Three. And David put him in charge of his bodyguard.[^28]

**26** The mighty warriors of the army were Asahel the brother of Joab; Elhanan son of Dodo, of Bethlehem;

**27** Shammoth the Harorite;[^29] Helez the Pelonite;[^30]

**28** Ira son of Ikkesh, the Tekoite; Abiezer the Anathothite;

**29** Sibbecai the Hushathite; Ilai the Ahohite;[^31]

**30** Maharai the Netophathite; Heled[^32] son of Baanah, the Netophathite;

**31** Ithai[^33] son of Ribai, from Gibeah of the Benjaminites; Benaiah the Pirathonite;

**32** Hurai of the ravines of Gaash; Abiel the Arbathite;[^34]

**33** Azmaveth the Baharumite;[^35] Eliahba the Shaalbonite;

**34** the sons of Hashem the Gizonite; Jonathan son of Shagee the Hararite;[^36]

**35** Ahiam son of Sachar the Hararite; Eliphal son of Ur;[^37]

**36** Hepher the Mecherathite; Ahijah the Pelonite;[^38]

**37** Hezro the Carmelite; Naarai son of Ezbai;[^39]

**38** Joel the brother of Nathan; Mibhar son of Hagri;[^40]

**39** Zelek the Ammonite; Naharai the Berothite,[^41] the armor-bearer of Joab son of Zeruiah;

**40** Ira the Ithrite; Gareb the Ithrite;

**41** Uriah the Hittite; Zabad son of Ahlai;[^42]

**42** Adina son of Shiza the Reubenite, a chief of the Reubenites, and thirty with him;[^43]

**43** Hanan son of Maacah; Joshaphat the Mithnite;

**44** Uzzia the Ashterathite; Shama and Jeiel,[^44] the sons of Hotham the Aroerite;

**45** Jediael son of Shimri, and Joha his brother, the Tizite;

**46** Eliel the Mahavite;[^45] Jeribai and Joshaviah, the sons of Elnaam; Ithmah the Moabite;

**47** Eliel, Obed, and Jaasiel the Mezobaite.

[^1]: Verses 1–9 parallel 2 Samuel 5:1–3 and 5:6–10.
[^2]: Literally "your bone and your flesh".
[^3]: Literally "both yesterday and the day before".
[^4]: Literally "you were the one who led out and brought in Israel".
[^5]: *LORD*: God's personal name, Hebrew YHWH, written "the LORD" in capitals as in most English Bibles.
[^6]: Hebrew *nagid*, a leader or ruler designated by God.
[^7]: Literally "by the hand of Samuel". See 1 Samuel 16:1–13. Here 2 Samuel 5:4–5 adds David's age and the length of his reign; compare 3:4 and 29:27 in this book.
[^8]: 2 Samuel 5:6 adds "Even the blind and the lame will turn you away."
[^9]: 2 Samuel 5:8 has a different saying about the water shaft, and does not mention Joab.
[^10]: Hebrew *ha-millo*, "the filling": probably a terraced structure of stone and earth on the slope of the city.
[^11]: Literally "to the surrounding". 2 Samuel 5:9 has "inward".
[^12]: Literally "revived".
[^13]: Hebrew *YHWH tseva'ot*, traditionally "the LORD of hosts" or "the LORD Almighty". *Tseva'ot* means armies: the heavenly hosts and the armies of Israel. 2 Samuel 5:10 has "the LORD, the God of Armies".
[^14]: Verses 10–41 parallel the list in 2 Samuel 23:8–39. Verses 41–47 continue the list beyond it.
[^15]: 2 Samuel 23:8 has "Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite".
[^16]: The Hebrew text is written "the Thirty" but read *ha-shalishim*, perhaps "the officers" (literally "third men"). See 2 Samuel 23:8.
[^17]: 2 Samuel 23:8 has "eight hundred".
[^18]: *Pas-dammim* is probably Ephes-dammim (1 Samuel 17:1). At this point 2 Samuel 23:9–11 has a longer text, which ends the story of Eleazar and tells of Shammah son of Agee. Verses 13b–14 here match what 2 Samuel tells of Shammah.
[^19]: 2 Samuel 23:11 has "lentils".
[^20]: David's elite company of warriors, whose names are listed in verses 26–47.
[^21]: 2 Samuel 23:13 has "at harvest time".
[^22]: Literally "with their *nephesh* … with their *nephesh*". 2 Samuel 23:17 reads "Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?"
[^23]: The Hebrew text is written "and he had no name" but read "and he had a name".
[^24]: Literally "Of the Three, among the two he was honored". 2 Samuel 23:19 reads "Was he not the most honored of the Three?" Since he did not attain to the Three, many think "the Thirty" is meant, as in verse 25.
[^25]: Hebrew *ari'el*, a word of uncertain meaning, perhaps "champion".
[^26]: A cubit is about 18 inches (45 cm), so five cubits is about 7 feet 6 inches (2.3 m). 2 Samuel 23:21 has "a man of impressive appearance", and does not mention the weaver's beam.
[^27]: A heavy wooden bar on a loom; compare Goliath's spear (1 Samuel 17:7).
[^28]: Hebrew *mishma'ato*, literally "those who obey him", perhaps his personal guard or council.
[^29]: 2 Samuel 23:25 has "Shammah the Harodite", followed by "Elika the Harodite", who is not in this list.
[^30]: 2 Samuel 23:26 has "the Paltite".
[^31]: 2 Samuel 23:27–28 has Mebunnai for Sibbecai and Zalmon for Ilai.
[^32]: 2 Samuel 23:29 has "Heleb".
[^33]: 2 Samuel 23:29 has "Ittai".
[^34]: 2 Samuel 23:30–31 has "Hiddai" for Hurai and "Abi-albon" for Abiel.
[^35]: 2 Samuel 23:31 has "the Barhumite".
[^36]: 2 Samuel 23:32–33 has "the sons of Jashen; Jonathan; Shammah the Hararite".
[^37]: 2 Samuel 23:33–34 has "Ahiam son of Sharar the Hararite; Eliphelet son of Ahasbai".
[^38]: 2 Samuel 23:34 has "the son of the Maacathite; Eliam son of Ahithophel, the Gilonite".
[^39]: 2 Samuel 23:35 has "Hezrai the Carmelite; Paarai the Arbite".
[^40]: Or "son of the Hagrite". 2 Samuel 23:36 has "Igal son of Nathan, from Zobah; Bani the Gadite".
[^41]: 2 Samuel 23:37 has "the Beerothite".
[^42]: The list in 2 Samuel 23 ends with Uriah the Hittite.
[^43]: Literally "and over him thirty".
[^44]: The Hebrew text is written "Jeuel" but read "Jeiel".
[^45]: Hebrew plural, "the Mahavites".
