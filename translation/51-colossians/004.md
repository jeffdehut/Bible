**1** Masters,[^1] give your slaves what is right and fair,[^2] knowing that you also have a Master[^3] in heaven.

**2** Devote yourselves to prayer, staying alert in it with thanksgiving.

**3** At the same time pray for us too, that God may open a door for the word for us, to speak the mystery[^4] of Christ, for which I am in chains,

**4** so that I may make it clear, as I ought to speak.

**5** Live wisely toward outsiders,[^5] making the most of the time.[^6]

**6** Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person.

**7** Tychicus, the beloved brother, faithful servant[^7] and fellow servant[^8] in the Lord, will tell you everything about my situation.

**8** I am sending[^9] him to you for this very purpose, so that you may know how we are[^10] and so that he may encourage your hearts,

**9** with Onesimus,[^11] the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will tell you everything that has happened here.

**10** Aristarchus my fellow prisoner greets you, as does Mark the cousin of Barnabas (about whom you have received instructions: if he comes to you, welcome him),

**11** and Jesus who is called Justus. These are the only ones of the circumcision[^12] among my fellow workers for the kingdom of God, and they have been a comfort to me.

**12** Epaphras, who is one of you, a servant[^13] of Christ,[^14] greets you. He is always struggling for you in his prayers, that you may stand mature and fully assured[^15] in all the will of God.

**13** For I testify about him that he has worked hard[^16] for you and for those in Laodicea and those in Hierapolis.

**14** Luke the beloved physician greets you, as does Demas.

**15** Greet the brothers and sisters[^17] in Laodicea, and Nympha and the church in her house.[^18]

**16** And when this letter has been read among you, have it read also in the church of the Laodiceans, and see that you also read the letter from Laodicea.

**17** And say to Archippus, "See that you complete the ministry[^19] you have received in the Lord."

**18** I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand.[^20] Remember my chains. Grace be with you.[^21]

[^1]: Greek *kyrioi*, "lords, masters, owners". This verse ends the instructions to households that begin in 3:18.
[^2]: Or "what is right and equal". Greek *isotes*, "equality, fairness".
[^3]: Or "Lord". Greek *kyrios*, the same word as "masters" earlier in the verse.
[^4]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it.
[^5]: Literally "Walk in wisdom toward those outside", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^6]: Literally "buying up the time".
[^7]: Greek *diakonos*, "servant", traditionally "minister".
[^8]: Greek *syndoulos*, "fellow slave".
[^9]: Literally "I sent", written from the point of view of the readers, who will receive the letter when Tychicus arrives.
[^10]: Many manuscripts read "so that he may know how you are".
[^11]: Compare Philemon 10–16, where Paul writes about a slave named Onesimus.
[^12]: That is, Jewish believers.
[^13]: Or "slave". Greek *doulos*.
[^14]: The SBLGNT here follows the Byzantine text; other editions read "Christ Jesus".
[^15]: Many manuscripts read "complete".
[^16]: Literally "he has much toil". Many manuscripts read "he has great zeal".
[^17]: Greek *adelphoi*, "brothers", used of the believers, men and women alike.
[^18]: Many manuscripts read "Nymphas and the church in his house", with a man's name; some read "Nymphas and the church in their house".
[^19]: Greek *diakonia*, "service, ministry".
[^20]: Literally "The greeting with my own hand, of Paul". Paul usually dictated his letters to a scribe and wrote the last words himself (compare 2 Thessalonians 3:17).
[^21]: Many manuscripts add "Amen".
