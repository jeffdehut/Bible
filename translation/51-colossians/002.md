**1** For I want you to know how great a struggle I have for you and for those in Laodicea and for all who have not seen me face to face,[^1]

**2** so that their hearts may be encouraged, as they are united[^2] in love, and so that they may have all the riches of full assurance of understanding and the knowledge of God's mystery,[^3] which is Christ.

**3** In him[^4] all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.

**4** I say this so that no one may deceive you with persuasive arguments.

**5** For though I am absent in body,[^5] yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ.

**6** So then, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord,[^6] live[^7] in him,

**7** rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, overflowing with thanksgiving.[^8]

**8** See to it that no one takes you captive through philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elements of the world,[^9] and not according to Christ.

**9** For in him the whole fullness of deity[^10] dwells bodily,

**10** and you have been filled in him, who is the head of every ruler and authority.

**11** In him you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, by putting off the body of the flesh,[^11] in the circumcision of Christ,[^12]

**12** having been buried with him in baptism, in which[^13] you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead.

**13** And you, who were dead in your wrongdoings and in the uncircumcision of your flesh, he[^14] made alive together with him, having forgiven us all our wrongdoings,

**14** by canceling the record of debt[^15] that stood against us with its legal demands. He has taken it out of the way, nailing it to the cross.

**15** He disarmed[^16] the rulers and the authorities and made a public example of them, leading them in triumph[^17] in it.[^18]

**16** So let no one judge you in matters of food or drink, or with regard to a festival or a new moon or Sabbaths.

**17** These are a shadow of the things to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.[^19]

**18** Let no one disqualify you,[^20] who delights in humility[^21] and the worship of angels,[^22] going on at length about what he has seen,[^23] puffed up without reason by his fleshly mind,[^24]

**19** and not holding fast to the head, from whom the whole body, supplied and held together by its joints and ligaments, grows with a growth that is from God.

**20** If with Christ you died to the elements of the world, why, as if you were still living in the world, do you submit to regulations,

**21** "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch"

**22** (things that all perish as they are used), according to human commands and teachings?[^25]

**23** These have indeed a reputation for wisdom, with their self-made religion and humility and severe treatment of the body, but they are of no value against the indulgence of the flesh.[^26]

[^1]: Literally "who have not seen my face in the flesh".
[^2]: Or "instructed". Greek *symbibazo*, "bring together, unite", also "teach"; the same verb is translated "held together" in verse 19.
[^3]: Greek *mysterion*, a secret plan that was hidden until God revealed it. Many manuscripts read "the mystery of God and the Father and of Christ".
[^4]: Or "In it", that is, in the mystery.
[^5]: Literally "in the flesh".
[^6]: Or "as you received the Christ, Jesus the Lord".
[^7]: Literally "walk", a common biblical picture for a way of life.
[^8]: Many manuscripts read "overflowing in it with thanksgiving".
[^9]: Greek *stoicheia*, "elements": the basic components of the world, its elementary principles, or spiritual powers thought to be linked with them; also in verse 20.
[^10]: Greek *theotes*, "deity, divine nature".
[^11]: Greek *sarx*, "flesh". "The body of the flesh" may mean the physical body (as in 1:22), or human nature in its weakness and opposition to God, "the flesh" in Paul's moral sense, as in verses 18 and 23. Many manuscripts read "the body of the sins of the flesh".
[^12]: That is, either the circumcision that Christ performs, or Christ's own death, pictured as a circumcision.
[^13]: Or "in whom", that is, in Christ.
[^14]: That is, God.
[^15]: Greek *cheirographon*, a handwritten document, especially a signed record of a debt.
[^16]: Or "He stripped". Greek *apekdyomai*, "take off, strip"; compare "putting off" in verse 11.
[^17]: Greek *thriambeuo*, used of a Roman general leading his defeated enemies in a triumphal procession.
[^18]: That is, in the cross (verse 14). Or "in him".
[^19]: Literally "but the body is of Christ".
[^20]: Greek *katabrabeuo*, "decide against, rob of a prize".
[^21]: Or "self-abasement"; also in verse 23. Greek *tapeinophrosyne*, translated "humility" in 3:12; here it may mean practices of self-denial such as fasting. *Who delights in*: literally "wishing in", which may also mean "insisting on".
[^22]: Or "the worship that angels offer".
[^23]: Greek *embateuo*, "enter, set foot on", also "investigate". Its sense here is uncertain. Many manuscripts read "what he has not seen".
[^24]: Literally "by the mind of his flesh".
[^25]: Compare Isaiah 29:13 in the Septuagint, which speaks of "the commands and teachings of people".
[^26]: The Greek of this clause is difficult. Literally "not in any honor toward the satisfying of the flesh". It may mean that these rules do nothing to restrain the flesh, or that they are of no value and serve only to satisfy the flesh.
